## Notable Gaps and Points of Divergence

**Largest gap — UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (Transcript 42 vs. Minutes 58, gap = 16)**: The minutes use explicit "considerable uncertainty" language applied to multiple structural issues (fiscal policy, inventory correction magnitude, inflation trajectory). The transcript shows individual expressions of uncertainty but the aggregate impression scored lower, reflecting that multiple speakers also expressed views with confidence about the cyclical nature of the slowdown.

**Moderate gap — LABOR_TIGHTNESS (Transcript 58 vs. Minutes 48, gap = 10)**: The transcript captures detailed district-level reports of very tight labor markets (Ohio at 4% unemployment, Chicago at 15-year lows), which drive the transcript score higher. The minutes synthesize the picture as "easing of pressures on labor," reflecting the aggregate national unemployment rate of 5.8% and subdued compensation data rather than the district-level tightness.

**Staff vs. Discussion gap — OUTPUT_GAP (Staff 45 vs. Discussion 52)**: Staff explicitly forecasts growth below potential ("less than the rate of increase in the economy's potential output"), placing the score below 50. Discussion participants — using language of "at or slightly above" potential — score just above 50. This reflects staff's more forward-looking framing versus participants' current-conditions assessment.

**Staff vs. Discussion gap — LABOR_TIGHTNESS (Staff 40 vs. Discussion Transcript 58, Discussion Minutes 48)**: Staff anchors on the national unemployment rate (5.8%, rising) and stalling payrolls; discussion participants — especially in the transcript — emphasize district-level tightness and sub-3% unemployment in many metro areas.
