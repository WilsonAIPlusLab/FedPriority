### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 63 ±8 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Headline inflation clearly elevated (4%+ annualized recently) but underlying/core near 1%. Majority sees this as transitory; hawkish minority sees real pass-through risk. The committee perceives CURRENT inflation above target (headline) with substantial upside risk, offset by still-low underlying measures.

Representative quotes:
- MR. FISHER: "What was previously a faint theme is now a very loud, uniform drumbeat on two fronts... the first is that cost inflation is pushing forward price increases in all sectors beyond energy and basic commodities... Every CEO I talk to, large or small, public or private, is now budgeting for and managing to inflation."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "The staff estimate of the 12-month change in core PCE for this month is still about 0.9; market-based core is 0.9. The Dallas trimmed mean PCE is 1.0, and the core CPI is 1.2."
- MS. YELLEN: "measures of underlying inflation remain well below the 2 percent level I consider consistent with our dual mandate... the market-based core PCE price index has risen less than 1 percent over the past year."
- MR. LACKER: "Price growth picked up in both our manufacturing and services surveys this time, and is at or approaching the all-time highs for those series that they reached in 2008."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "inflation risks seem to the upside."

Minority view (transcript): Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, Bullard see more persistent and broader inflation threat — would push toward 70+.

---

**Minutes — Score: 58 ±8 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Headline inflation clearly elevated from commodity prices with rising concern about pass-through, but core still subdued and most view the rise as transitory.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants discussed the recent rise in inflation, which had been driven largely by significant increases in energy and, to a somewhat lesser extent, other commodity prices."
- "Many participants had become more concerned about the upside risks to the inflation outlook."
- "Core inflation and other indicators of underlying inflation over the medium term had increased modestly in recent months, but their levels remained subdued."
- "One participant suggested that excess liquidity might be leading to speculation in commodity markets."

Minority view (minutes): A few saw inflation risks as warranting sooner tightening.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Unambiguous — output gap approximately –5%, well below potential. Recovery is under way but far from closing the gap. The weak first quarter (~1.7%) tilts slightly toward the lower end of the 2010–14 anchor range of 35–42.

Representative quotes:
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (own remarks): "total hours of work, which is a good summary of labor input... is still about 6 percent below pre-recession levels, not even taking into account any trend increases in labor supply."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (Taylor rule calculation): "output gap of 5.1 percent [negative] in the fourth quarter of this year, which is essentially the same as where the staff is. They estimate 5.0 percent."
- MS. YELLEN: "I expect the output gap and unemployment to decline, but slowly, so both will be substantially higher than normal levels at the end of the forecast horizon."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "GDP growth over the past four quarters has averaged only 2.8 percent, just slightly above potential."

Minority view (transcript): No participant argued economy was at or above potential.

---

**Minutes — Score: 37 ±5 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Economy clearly below potential — moderate recovery with substantial remaining slack in labor and product markets.

Representative quotes:
- "The economic recovery was proceeding at a moderate pace, albeit somewhat slower than had been anticipated."
- "Low rates of resource utilization" cited as reason for extended period language.
- Unemployment at 8.8%, "remained elevated."
- "Some noted the rapid decline in unemployment 'suggested the possibility of stronger-than-anticipated economic growth.'"

Minority view (minutes): Some noted the rapid unemployment decline as a possible positive signal.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±6 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 8.8%, substantial slack acknowledged by virtually all participants. Rapid improvement from trough but still well below full employment. A few sectoral pockets of tightness (tech, manufacturing) noted but insufficient to shift center.

Representative quotes:
- MR. ROSENGREN: "The very low quit rate also suggests that considerable slack remains in the labor market... we need much more improvement in labor markets than we have seen to date."
- MS. PIANALTO: "there is still a lot of slack in labor markets, and therefore, I don't see wage pressures picking up any time soon."
- MR. TARULLO: "suggests that there may be even more slack in the labor markets than the unemployment numbers would suggest."
- MR. BULLARD: "There is something of a boom in certain technology labor markets. Competition for talent is fierce. The comment I got from a major technology firm was, 'It's like 1998 when it comes to hunting for talent in this area.'" [outlier/sector-specific]
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "hiring is weak, though firms may be forced into the labor market as they reach the end of productivity gains."

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±5 | High confidence**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving but still quite slack — unemployment elevated, wage growth muted, considerable hidden slack.

Representative quotes:
- "Overall conditions in labor markets had continued to improve, albeit gradually."
- "Increases in wages have been subdued."

- Participants discussed whether the unemployment rate drop "might be overstating the degree of improvement."

Minority view (minutes): "A few participants noted that firms may be poised to accelerate their pace of hiring because they have exhausted potential productivity gains."

Representative quotes:

Banks are seeing better credit quality but are concerned about top-line revenues. Loans to small businesses are up, and there is substantial competition among banks to make C&I loans."
