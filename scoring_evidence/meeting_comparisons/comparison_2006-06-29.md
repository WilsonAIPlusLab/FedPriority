# FOMC Meeting 2006-06-29: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 68 ±6 [High confidence] | 68 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Bies: "In the past three months, core CPI prices and core PCE prices have grown, respectively, at 3.8 percent and 3.4 percent, twice their rate of growth in the previous three months."
- Pianalto: "The core inflation numbers have been drifting up... this explanation is becoming increasingly difficult for me to defend."
- Bernanke: "if you slice core PCE in any other way... you get a similar pattern in terms of the three-month, six-month, and twelve-month averages, which suggests a broad-based acceleration."
- Bernanke (summary): "Everyone considered these recent developments to be unwelcome... there certainly was a sense that it's a risk to the economy."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants uniformly view inflation as clearly above target with broad-based elevation; debate is about whether it is persistent or transitory, not about whether it is elevated.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "All participants found the elevated readings on core inflation of recent months to be of concern and, if sustained, inconsistent with the maintenance of price stability."
- "The pickup in price increases had been broadly based rather than being limited to a few specific sectors."
- "Most" see inflation as "likely to edge down" but "several factors were cited as potentially sustaining upward pressure on inflation."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: All participants see inflation as elevated and concerning, with broadly based increases. Expectations of moderation temper the score, but the current readings and breadth of pressures place this clearly above target.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 48 ±5 [High confidence] | 54 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Geithner: "We expect real GDP growth to follow a path pretty close to potential in the balance of '06 and in '07."
- Yellen: "in view of the possibility that labor and product markets may have moved a bit beyond full utilization… a period of growth a bit below potential could be seen as necessary."
- Bernanke (summary): "output is slowing to something close to potential. Some felt growth would be stronger than the Greenbook suggested; others thought it would be falling somewhat below potential."
- Minehan: "small to nonexistent output gap we see currently reflected in the low unemployment rate."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Economy is seen at or very slightly below potential, with some seeing above (Warsh, Fisher) and some below (Yellen, Hoenig). Central tendency is near 48–50.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "Most participants expected output to advance over the next year and a half at a pace close to that which the economy can sustain over time."
- "Resource utilization was currently tight" and "relatively taut resource markets."
- "Economic growth as having moderated in the second quarter from its robust pace in the first quarter."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential — resource utilization is "tight/taut" but growth is moderating toward sustainable rates.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 60 ±6 [High confidence] | 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Bernanke (summary): "The view of the labor market is that it remains reasonably healthy, that it's difficult to find skilled workers, but there are still few signs of wage pressures in the economy."
- Poole: J.B. Hunt has "dire shortage of drivers — worse than he has ever seen it" yet wages rising only 3–4% annually.
- Minehan: "Many continue to note how hard it is to find the skilled labor they need."
- Kohn: "The decline in the unemployment rate to noticeably below 5 percent occurred only at the beginning of this year, but the behavior of compensation last year and this year suggests to me that the NAIRU is more likely to be under than to be over 5 percent."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight — low unemployment, difficulty finding skilled workers — but wage acceleration remains modest. Placed in the 53–65 "tight" range.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "Resource utilization was currently tight."
- "Some participants noted that businesses in their Districts were experiencing difficulty hiring certain types of skilled workers, suggesting that increased wage pressures might emerge."
- Unemployment forecast central tendency: 4.75–5%.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight but not severely so; skill-specific shortages rather than broad-based tightness.

|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 49 ±7 [Moderate confidence] | 48 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Poole: Wal-Mart "several hundred basis points below plan since Easter" — pronounced paycheck cycle for lower-income consumers.
- Guynn: "Consumption appears to be less strong than it was in the early spring."
- Warsh: "core retail sales rose 0.4 percent in May, up about 6.9 percent year over year"; "M&A pipelines are more robust than ever"; capital expenditures appear "above plan."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is softening especially at lower-income levels, but business investment remains robust and other consumption is near-trend. Net score is approximately at-trend with slight downside skew from housing and lower-income consumer weakness.

**Minutes evidence**:

- "Business investment would remain a source of strength going forward."
- "Businesses were now directing their capital expenditures toward expanding capacity rather than increasing efficiency."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Consumer weakness is offset by strong business investment. Net position is roughly at trend, with consumer softening pulling slightly below.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence] | 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Bies: "we all agree that spreads are still very narrow by historical standards… banks loosening lending standards. There's plenty of liquidity out there. Credit spreads are still very, very minimal."
- Warsh: "fixed-income markets and high-yield markets certainly have moved higher in terms of spreads but, again, not a lot by historical perspectives. Liquidity is plentiful."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are loose — historically narrow spreads, abundant liquidity, banks loosening lending standards. Conditions have tightened marginally from peak ease but remain in the "loose/accommodative" range.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "Several participants observed that the continued ready availability of credit would support business expenditures."
- "Others, however, noted that the pullback from risk-taking that had been observed in some financial markets over the preceding few months could intensify."
- Higher interest rates affecting housing and home equity borrowing.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain on the loose side despite tightening monetary policy; credit is readily available though some risk repricing is occurring.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 72 ±5 [High confidence] | 65 ±8 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Near-universal support for 25 bp hike (Kohn, Poole, Lacker, Yellen, Minehan, Guynn, Stern, Warsh, Bies, Bernanke, Fisher, Moskow, Pianalto all support).
- Bernanke: "I, too, think that we should raise 25 basis points at this meeting. I think it's justified by the basic economic situation. The greater risk seems to be from inflation."
- Lacker: "we may need to raise rates beyond today because it seems clear to me that we're not pleased with recent inflation."
- Hoenig (non-voter): would prefer to hold at 5.00% — sole dissenting voice.

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus for immediate tightening. Forward lean is also hawkish but deliberately moderated — most want to keep August open rather than signal another hike.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "All Committee members agreed that raising the target for the federal funds rate 25 basis points... was appropriate given the recent readings on inflation."
- "Members recognized the value of accumulating more information for determining what, if any, additional policy action would be needed."
- "One indicated that the decision to raise the target federal funds rate at this meeting was a close call."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for the hike driven by inflation concerns, but significant conditionality about the path forward. Moderate hawkish with increasing data dependence.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Mild tightening bias in forward guidance — "additional firming... may be needed" — but heavily conditional on data. A step down from committed tightening toward data dependence.
