### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- Rosengren: "My forecast for inflation is for a very slow return to our 2 percent goal. I expect core PCE inflation to remain below 2 percent into 2016."
- Powell: "headline PCE inflation has come up to about 1.8 percent, and core is about 1.6 percent…the threat of inflation to remain persistently low has clearly diminished."
- Kocherlakota: "I expect PCE inflation and core PCE inflation to be around 1.6 percent over the course of 2014…gets back to target by around 2018."
- Bullard: "inflation is just a few ticks from target."
- Chair Yellen summary: "inflation has moved higher but is still running below our 2 percent objective."

Center of gravity: Participants broadly see inflation in the 1.6–1.8% range — below target but firming meaningfully. Not alarm about deflation; concern is staying below target, not overshooting. The typical 2013–14 below-target characterization (~38–42) applies here, biased toward the upper end given observed firming.

Minority views: Plosser and Fisher see upside risk and near-target convergence sooner; Kocherlakota sees inflation persistently below target through 2018.

**Minutes — Score: 44 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Inflation firmed in recent months, and most participants anticipated that it would continue to move up toward the Committee's 2 percent objective."
- "Participants noted that inflation had moved somewhat closer to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective and generally saw the risks of inflation running persistently below their objective as having diminished somewhat."
- "A few participants continued to see inflation as likely to persist below the Committee's objective over the medium term."

Center of gravity: Inflation is below 2% target but firming, with most expecting a gradual return to target. Downside risks diminished but not eliminated.

Minority views: A few see persistent below-target inflation; a few others argue tightening labor market increases upside risks.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- Mester: "The current Tealbook now sees the output gap being eliminated by next summer. This is three quarters earlier than in the June Tealbook."
- Plosser: "The economy appears to be bouncing back from a weak first quarter and is poised to grow at a pace somewhat above trend."
- Kocherlakota: "I would assign more of the weak real GDP number in the first quarter to higher slack as opposed to lower potential."
- Brainard: "stark anomaly suggests that either potential is much weaker than we have hoped or the labor market data are substantially overstating the take-up in slack."

Center of gravity: The output gap is negative (below potential) but closing rapidly. The Tealbook sees elimination by mid-2015. Participants broadly agree the economy is operating below potential but approaching it — fitting the 35–42 range for 2010–14 recovery phase.

Minority views: Kocherlakota assigns more weight to the Q1 weakness implying a larger negative gap; Plosser/Fisher see the economy as near-trend and approaching above-trend growth.

**Minutes — Score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Meeting participants generally viewed the rebound in real GDP in the second quarter and the ongoing improvement in labor market conditions as supporting their expectations for continued moderate economic expansion."
- "Many participants continued to see a larger gap between current labor market conditions and those consistent with their assessments of normal levels of labor utilization."
- "Resource slack diminished."

Center of gravity: Economy below potential but gap narrowing notably; moderate expansion with slack still present.

Minority views: A few see the unemployment rate alone as adequate (suggesting less slack); many emphasize broader slack measures.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- Rosengren: "I still view labor markets as having substantial slack…The U-3 measure of unemployment, at 6.1 percent, is well above my measure of full employment at 5¼ percent."
- George: "job growth has been strong…Not only has job growth been strong over the past six months, but average payroll growth this year has exceeded the average of the top 10 most optimistic forecasts."
- Yellen summary: "labor market conditions do seem to be improving somewhat more rapidly than I had expected."
- Tarullo: "it still does not seem as though there's any evidence of generalized wage increases."
- Lacker: "this nonemployment index turns out to be right in line with what's usually associated with the current level of the standard unemployment rate" (suggesting U-3 is a good summary statistic — less slack than dovish views suggest).

Center of gravity: Unemployment at 6.1% with substantial debate about residual slack from part-timers, participation rate, and long-term unemployed. The dominant position: still below full employment but improving rapidly.

Minority views: Lacker/Bullard argue U-3 is close to sufficient statistic (less slack → score might be 42–44); Rosengren/Kocherlakota argue "substantial slack" (score might be 35–38).

**Minutes — Score: 39 ±6 [High confidence]**

- "Many participants continued to see a larger gap between current labor market conditions and those consistent with their assessments of normal levels of labor utilization."

Center of gravity: Labor market improving faster than expected but still below full employment, with multiple slack indicators cited.

Minority views: Several note wage pressures in specific regions/occupations; a few focus only on the unemployment rate (suggesting less slack).

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- Rosengren: "I would make no change in our current policy…I will be reluctant to alter that guidance unless there is strong evidence that inflation is coming in significantly higher than we currently anticipate."
- Plosser (hawkish outlier): "I can no longer support the language in alternative B or C…maintaining a near-zero federal funds rate well into next year is likely to be inappropriate."

- Kocherlakota (dovish outlier): "I would see no reason for us to be raising rates as long as the inflation outlook remained below 2 percent."

Center of gravity: The vote is unanimous to taper another $10B. The modal view maintains "considerable time" forward guidance while acknowledging that liftoff is approaching. The majority support alternative B as written — a hold with accommodative guidance. The hawkish minority (Plosser, Fisher, George, Mester, Bullard) want to alter language or hasten the exit.

Minority views: Plosser is clearly hawkish (~65–70 range individually); Kocherlakota is clearly dovish (~25–30 individually).
