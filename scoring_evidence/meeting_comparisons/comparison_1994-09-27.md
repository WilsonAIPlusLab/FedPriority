## Part A: Discussion — Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 58 ±7 | Minutes: 62 ±6**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "We have inflation edging higher until at least the middle of 1996... the current pressure on prices is due to aggregate demand growing more quickly than aggregate supply."
- MR. MELZER: "consumer prices accelerated to an annual rate of 4.1 percent and producer prices to an annual rate of more than 6 percent. Inflation is certainly headed in the wrong direction."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I did read your comments on inflation as implying slightly less upward pressure on prices. There was surprisingly little talk of more price pressures around the room."
- Multiple speakers note rising input prices but limited pass-through to final goods due to competitive pressures (Forrestal, Boehne, Moskow, Jordan).

Transcript center-of-gravity: Inflation modestly above ~3% level; input price pressures building but final goods prices still somewhat contained by competition and productivity. Upside risk broadly acknowledged.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "some measures of inflation had picked up recently and that many private forecasters anticipated higher inflation in 1995 than in 1994"
- "the risks of some rise in inflation rates probably had increased"
- "the potential for additional inflation remained substantial"
- "the pass-through of the higher costs of materials to the prices of final goods had been muted"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Members perceive inflation as modestly above target with significant upside risk. Pipeline pressures building but final goods prices remain contained for now. Language of "picked up" and "substantial potential" places this clearly above neutral.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 58 ±5 | Minutes: 60 ±5**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "Labor and product markets currently appear to be tight. This impression was reinforced by the sizable increase in capacity utilization rates."
- MR. MOSKOW: "We concur with the Greenbook assessment that little slack remains in the economy."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "most of the slack in labor and product markets is used up"
- MR. BLINDER: Growth concentrated in 25% of GDP (11.3% growth) while rest grew 1.6%, tempering the overheating narrative.

Transcript center-of-gravity: Economy operating modestly above potential, with capacity utilization elevated and labor markets tight. Growth above potential rate but not dramatically overheating.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "output near maximum sustainable levels"
- "growth in overall demand at a pace above the economy's long-run potential"
- "increasing capacity constraints" and "capacity utilization moved up further from already high levels"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Members clearly see the economy as operating above potential, with demand growth exceeding sustainable rates. Language "near maximum sustainable levels" and "above the economy's long-run potential" is unambiguous.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 60 ±6 | Minutes: 57 ±5**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "Labor markets generally are characterized as tight throughout the District... In some communities, people associated with retail or fast food operations actually complain about a shortage of teenagers."
- MR. MELZER: "unemployment rate hovers near its lowest level since August 1974. Reports of labor shortages in certain trades and geographic areas are increasing."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Demand for temporary workers in our region is very strong, with these firms having to pay wages that are 5 to 10 percent higher than last year to attract workers."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Wage pressures remain subdued."

Transcript center-of-gravity: Labor markets tight with widespread reports of shortages in specific trades and regions, but broad-based wage pressure has not yet materialized.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "many of them referred to increasing reports of scarcities of specific types of labor resources"
- "increasingly tight labor markets in many parts of the country had not resulted in higher overall wage inflation, but members reported some upward pressure on the wages of certain categories of workers in strong demand"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Labor markets tight and tightening further, with specific skill shortages emerging. Broad wage inflation has not materialized, keeping this below the "very tight" threshold.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Transcript: 61 ±6 | Minutes: 62 ±5**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. BLINDER: "Those three sectors [BFI, consumer durables, housing] over the last four quarters registered an 11.3 percent rate of growth."
- MR. JORDAN: "Motor vehicle production essentially is at capacity... Retail sales were quite good."
- MR. BROADDUS: "The strong recent growth in investment activity, both fixed and inventory investment, is certainly not suggestive of a business expansion that is about to run out of steam."
- Housing slowing noted by multiple speakers; some consumer moderation reported.

Transcript center-of-gravity: Business investment and consumer durables are well above trend. Housing is softening. Net spending position is above trend but with emerging moderation on the consumer/housing side.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "consumer spending had been more buoyant than expected over recent months"
- "business fixed investment was viewed as likely to rise substantially further over the next several quarters"
- "fairly brisk retail sales in many areas recently"
- "the rate of growth had been moderating this year" for business equipment (moderating factor)

Minutes center-of-gravity: Both consumption and investment are above trend. Consumer spending surprised to the upside. Business investment growth has moderated from the extremely rapid 1993 pace but remains strong.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 50 ±7 | Minutes: 30 ±7**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "credit is a good deal more readily available today to small and medium-size businesses than it was a year ago or even less. It does not appear that our moves toward higher interest rates have had any restraining effect in that sector."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Banks are reporting increasing loan volumes... There also are reports of a fairly general slippage in underwriting standards."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the capital markets are producing a cost of capital that is clearly supportive of investment and expansion."
- MR. BROADDUS: "The bond rate is now at its highest level since back in the 1990-1991 recession."

Transcript center-of-gravity: Despite 175bp of rate hikes in 1994, financial conditions remain roughly neutral to slightly loose. Credit is freely available, banks are lending aggressively, and capital markets support investment.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "many members stressed the ready availability of financing from increasingly aggressive bank lenders"
- "despite higher interest rates, capital markets were providing continued support to a wide variety of borrowers"
- "a financial climate that might even be providing an extra impetus to spending"
- "the ready availability of bank credit and the receptivity of financial markets more generally argued that many borrowers...currently had access to ample financing"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Despite the policy tightening actions of 1994, actual financial conditions remain loose. Members repeatedly emphasize that credit is freely available and bank lenders are aggressive. This is clearly on the accommodative side.

Note: The 20-point gap between transcript (50) and minutes (30) is the largest divergence in Part A. The transcript scores this as roughly neutral due to offset between high bond rates and loose credit; the minutes scores this as clearly accommodative based on the repeated emphasis on aggressive bank lending and the "extra impetus to spending" formulation.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 65 ±7 | Minutes: 65 ±6**

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I suspect we are going to need to move further somewhere along the line... If we do move then [November], I think that would probably require us to move another 50 basis points and probably move the discount rate as well."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would prefer to move. My choice would be alternative D" (50bp hike now).
- MR. MELZER: "I'd favor 'D' combined with a discount rate increase; I'd do the 50 basis points now."
- MR. BLINDER: "'B' asymmetric is the right policy."

Transcript center-of-gravity: Strong majority supports holding today but with a clear tightening bias. Nearly all participants expect further tightening; debate is about timing (now vs. November), not direction. Three participants wanted immediate 50bp action.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "most of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining a steady policy course, at least for the near term"
- "for many members this suggested that additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time"
- "the members generally supported a shift from the symmetry in the August directive to asymmetry toward restraint"
- "should incoming information point to a greater likelihood that price pressures would intensify, the Committee would need to act promptly and forcefully"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Committee on hold but with a clear hawkish lean. Shifted to asymmetric directive toward tightening; multiple members expect further tightening will be needed.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 74 ±6 | Minutes: 68 ±5**

**Transcript evidence:**

**Minutes evidence:**

Minutes center-of-gravity: Forward signal is clearly hawkish. Committee has adopted an asymmetric tilt toward tightening and explicitly signals willingness to act "promptly and forcefully" if inflation pressures intensify.

Note: The 6-point gap (74 vs. 68) reflects the transcript capturing more explicit and specific forward guidance — Greenspan's direct statement of "50 basis points and probably move the discount rate" and McDonough's specific intermeeting language — while the minutes renders the forward signal in more generic conditional language.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Transcript: 42 ±7 | Minutes: 47 ±6**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "it strikes me pretty forcefully that it's simply too early to determine whether we should be worried or not."
- MR. BOEHNE: "The major uncertainty, however, is what lies beyond the next quarter or two when some of the slackening of pent-up demand and lagged effects of previous interest rate hikes will be felt more fully."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of competing analytical frameworks (Phillips curve vs. monetary); "we are approaching a point where we will get interesting tests" of these models.
- MR. STERN: warns about "our ability to analyze the price data and come up with all these fine reasons why something unusual is going on and the rise in prices won't last."

Transcript center-of-gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Participants are confident about current conditions but uncertain about the near-term trajectory — whether growth will slow enough, whether inflation is a blip or persistent, and how much restraint is already in the pipeline.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "how large this rise might be or when it might be reversed was very difficult to predict at this point"
- "the prospects for inventory investment remained a key uncertainty in the outlook"
- "it was extremely difficult to evaluate whether the earlier tightening moves were exerting a lesser effect than usual or it simply was more delayed, or whether the members might have misjudged the underlying strength of the expansion"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty on several fronts: the transmission lag of prior tightening, inventory dynamics, and the inflation path. Multiple uses of "very difficult to predict" and "extremely difficult to evaluate" indicate genuinely elevated uncertainty.
