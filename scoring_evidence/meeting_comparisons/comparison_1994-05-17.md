# FOMC Meeting Comparison: 1994-05-17

## Meeting Context
May 1994. The Fed had already raised rates three times in 1994 (February, March, April) and was in the midst of a tightening cycle. This meeting produced a 50bp increase in the federal funds rate (to 4.25%) combined with a 50bp discount rate increase — the first "aggressive" move after three 25bp increments. Unemployment was approximately 6.4%.

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 58 ±8 [High] | Minutes: 53 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript evidence**:

- MR. BROADDUS: "We polled them on their inflation expectations and the vast majority expect some worsening in that situation."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Inflation running around 3% core; participants see building pressures — not yet elevated but clearly trending upward with upside risks widely acknowledged. Level of concern exceeds what measured data alone would warrant, reflecting forward-looking worry.

- "an increasing number of contacts were reporting that business firms were taking advantage of opportunities to adjust prices upward"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Members see current inflation as subdued but perceive clearly emerging upward pressures from capacity constraints, raw material costs, and diminishing discounting. The score sits just above neutral reflecting the tension between benign current readings and growing forward concern.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 72 ±5 [High] | Minutes: 72 ±4 [High]**

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Proposes 50bp increase; "we have the capability...to move more strongly than we usually do without the risk of cracking the system."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus. Unanimous support for a 50bp hike (doubling from prior 25bp moves). Several participants believe more is needed. Active, consensus tightening with forward tightening bias.

**Minutes evidence**:

- "all the members agreed that a further tightening action was needed at this point; and... the adjustment should fully reflect the 1/2 percentage point increase in the discount rate"
- "members were concerned that continued policy accommodation could be expected to lead before long to growing pressures on production resources and to a fresh outbreak of inflation"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for a substantial 50bp tightening move, explicitly motivated by inflation prevention. Clearly in the "strong hawkish" range — consensus for continued tightening with a larger step size than previous moves.

## Cross-Track Observations

### Discussion vs. Staff: INFLATION_PRESSURE
Discussion (transcript 58, minutes 53) scores notably higher than staff (transcript 45, minutes 42). Discussion participants emphasize forward-looking concern, rising business contacts' inflation expectations, and the talk of inflation at the table. Staff emphasizes the favorable ECI surprise and subdued broad measures of inflation, giving more weight to current data than participants' risk assessments.

### Discussion vs. Staff: OUTPUT_GAP
Both tracks agree the economy is above potential, with scores clustered in the 53-57 range. The convergence is strong.

### Discussion vs. Staff: LABOR_TIGHTNESS
Discussion participants score labor tightness at 53 (both transcript and minutes), while staff scores range lower at 43-48. Staff emphasizes the 6.4% unemployment rate and moderating ECI; discussion participants emphasize anecdotal reports of skilled labor shortages and first-since-1985 wage pressure mentions.

### Discussion vs. Staff: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
Both tracks agree spending is well above trend, with all four sources in the 62-66 range.

### Within-Discussion: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
Transcript (40) scores this dimension substantially higher than minutes (28). Greenspan's and Phillips's extended analysis of the bond market "bubble," portfolio unwinding, and capital market fragility in the transcript is compressed to brief contextual references in the synthesized minutes.

### Within-Discussion: DISSENT_INTENSITY
Transcript (25) scores higher than minutes (15). The transcript records individual participants' differing emphases and Jordan's desire for even more aggressive action; the minutes record only the formal vote outcome and one member's directive preference.

### Within-Staff: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
The largest staff-track divergence. Transcript (40) vs. minutes (52). Both describe conditions tightening from accommodative, but the transcript's emphasis on the funds rate being "still historically low" yields a below-neutral reading, while the minutes' emphasis on the magnitude of market rate increases and equity declines yields an approximately neutral reading.
