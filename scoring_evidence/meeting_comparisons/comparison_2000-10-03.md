## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores**: Disc-T 58 | Disc-M 58 | Staff-T 58 | Staff-M 58

**Disc-T evidence**:
- "core inflation hasn't accelerated in recent months despite higher oil prices" but "core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent this year and slightly more next year" and "the balance of risks for inflation going forward still is on the up side" (Parry)
- "the balance of risks is nevertheless toward rising core inflation. And this risk not only persists throughout the forecast period; it will likely remain for a considerable period of time thereafter" (Meyer)
- "when one decomposes the current inflation path into the energy component and other components, there remains an upward tilt in core prices that should not be discounted" (Guynn)

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "although forecasts of more moderate growth in aggregate demand at a pace around potential output had substantially reduced the odds on rising inflation, the risks still were pointed in that direction on balance"
- "any increase in inflation would occur from a level that in the view of many members was already on the high side of an acceptable range"
- "Although inflation had picked up, a decline in energy prices, should it materialize...clearly would have favorable implications"

**Staff-T evidence**:
- "two key influences about which there are considerable uncertainties — the tightness of resource utilization and the pace of structural productivity growth" (Stockton)
- Staff acknowledges "an updrift" in core inflation has occurred over the past year but does not expect it to extend further
- "inflation expectations appear to be well contained" (Stockton)

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Rising energy prices had boosted overall price inflation considerably, but core measures of consumer inflation had increased substantially less"
- "core consumer prices increased somewhat more [on a year-over-year basis]"
- Staff forecast: "Core consumer price inflation was projected to rise a little over the forecast horizon"

**Observation**: All four sources produce identical scores (58). The cross-document language consistently conveys: headline inflation elevated from energy, core modestly above comfort level but not accelerating, upside risks acknowledged.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores**: Disc-T 56 | Disc-M 52 | Staff-T 49 | Staff-M 51

**Disc-T evidence**:
- "the economy appears to be making a transition from above-trend to below-trend growth" and "the prevailing unemployment rate is below the short-run NAIRU" (Meyer)
- "our growth forecast has the economy expanding below potential, which we put at 4-1/2 percent per annum" (McDonough)
- "nothing terribly significant going on in the economy excluding energy" (Greenspan)

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "Several commented that growth of aggregate demand now appeared to be closer to, and perhaps slightly below, the rate of expansion in the nation's output potential"
- "growth in aggregate demand had moderated appreciably to a pace that improved the prospects for containing pressures on resources"
- Continued references to "substantial pressures on labor resources" suggesting the level is still near potential

**Staff-T evidence**:
- "the economy is easing onto a path of below-trend growth" (Stockton)
- "with the growth of GDP continuing to run below that of potential output over the forecast interval, we expect to see the unemployment rate rise to 4-1/2 percent by the end of 2002"
- "A continuation of below-trend growth in GDP seems the most likely outcome as we move into 2001"

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Economic activity was expanding at a more moderate pace than in the first half of the year"
- Staff forecast: "the economic expansion...would be sustained at a rate a little below the staff's current estimate of the economy's potential output"
- "The rate of capacity utilization in manufacturing edged down to a level slightly below its long-term average"

**Observation**: A 7-point spread across sources (49–56). Staff sources (49, 51) score below the midpoint, responding to "below-trend" and "below potential" language for the forecast period. Discussion sources (52, 56) are higher, reflecting policymakers' emphasis that the economy has been running above potential and labor markets remain very tight. The divergence tracks a genuine difference in framing: staff oriented toward the forward trajectory; participants oriented toward current resource utilization levels.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores**: Disc-T 70 | Disc-M 67 | Staff-T 68 | Staff-M 67

**Disc-T evidence**:
- "labor markets are still very tight in our area" — anecdote of company giving 10% across-the-board wage increase to stop "poaching" (Broaddus)
- "continued tight labor markets" with cattlemen unable to find communities to build in due to labor shortages (Moskow)
- "It is easier to get a new customer than it is to get a new employee!" (Jordan, quoting a contact)

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "conditions in labor markets were likely to remain relatively tight"
- "substantial pressures on labor resources"
- "low rates of unemployment that in the past had been associated with increasing inflation"

**Staff-T evidence**:
- Monthly payroll gains "had averaged nearly 200,000 in the twelve months preceding March, have dropped off to an average of about 150,000 per month since then" (Stockton)
- "the tightness of resource utilization" cited as one of two key factors in the inflation outlook
- Unemployment projected to "rise to 4-1/2 percent by the end of 2002" — implies current rate well below 4.5%

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "The civilian unemployment rate edged up to 4.1 percent in August"
- Previous meeting characterized markets as "unusually taut"
- "The pace of private job gains in the July-August period fell considerably from the rate for the first half of the year"

**Observation**: High cross-source consistency (67–70). All sources agree on very tight labor markets. The 3-point gap between Disc-T (70) and the minutes/staff (67) reflects the anecdotal richness of the transcript — individual accounts of severe local shortages push the estimate slightly higher.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores**: Disc-T 53 | Disc-M 53 | Staff-T 48 | Staff-M 55

**Disc-T evidence**:
- "reporting a significant slowing in consumer spending or at least disappointing sales over the last several months" (Stern)
- "considerably more softening in our region than the data suggest for the nation" (Jordan)
- "the vast majority of our contacts continue to report a slowing in activity" but "home-related items like furniture and appliances remain strong" (Moskow)

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "a recent softening in retail sales, and some industry contacts indicated that they were marking down their forecasts of retail sales"
- "expansion in business spending for equipment and software might be moderating from the extraordinary pace of recent years, though growth in such expenditures probably would remain robust"
- "continuing gains in consumer incomes and a high level of consumer confidence could be expected to foster sustained growth"

**Staff-T evidence**:
- "consumer spending figures also have picked up noticeably of late" but "our projected growth of 4-3/4 percent in real PCE in the third quarter would be the slowest in a year and a half" (Stockton)
- "Spending for high-tech equipment also appears to have slowed some in the third quarter" and "Other capital spending also has slowed of late"
- "mortgage rates still up nearly 75 basis points from the middle of last year...we believe that there are solid underpinnings to our forecast that household spending is likely to remain more subdued"

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Consumer spending picked up somewhat in July and August"
- "Real personal consumption expenditures on durable goods surged"
- "Business investment in durable equipment and software increased substantially further in the third quarter" but "information on orders...pointed to further slowing in the pace of spending increases"

**Observation**: The largest within-group divergence is Staff-T (48) vs Staff-M (55) — a 7-point gap. Staff-T emphasizes deceleration signals (slowest PCE growth in 18 months, investment slowing), landing slightly below neutral. Staff-M reads the incoming data more positively ("surged," "increased substantially further"), landing modestly above trend. Discussion sources converge at 53. The divergence between staff scorings may reflect which data vintage is more prominent in each document.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Scores**: Disc-T 58 | Disc-M 60 | Staff-T 58 | Staff-M 57

**Disc-T evidence**:
- "larger banks report that loan demand has declined noticeably from year-ago levels. And there is evidence that financing terms are now more stringent, especially for second-tier borrowers" (Guynn)
- "proposals are now being turned down that would have been accepted a year ago. Bankers also tell us that not only have their interest margins narrowed considerably this year" (Jordan)
- "credit markets seem to be distinguishing among borrowers, with spreads rising for lower quality credit. Tighter financial conditions are likely a healthy development" (Minehan)

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "less favorable financial conditions, notably weakness in the equity prices of numerous 'new economy' firms and tightening credit availability for business firms that did not enjoy investment-grade credit ratings"
- "the tightening that had occurred in financial conditions through the spring"
- "A flat and volatile stock market and the rise in energy costs appeared to be key factors"

**Staff-T evidence**:
- "the less favorable financing environment that we are expecting over the next two years" cited as headwind to business investment (Stockton)
- Forward rates show "markets are pricing in expectations that the Committee's next move will be an easing" — confirming current stance is restrictive (Fisher)
- Two-year Treasury yields declined ~18 basis points; 10- and 30-year yields "backed up"

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Most short- and intermediate-term interest rates moved lower over the interval"
- Staff forecast notes "higher interest rates" as restraining demand
- "tightening credit availability for business firms that did not enjoy investment-grade credit ratings"

**Observation**: Very tight cross-source clustering (57–60). All four sources characterize conditions as modestly tighter than neutral: elevated policy rate, equity weakness, credit tightening for lower-rated borrowers. No source describes crisis conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Scores**: Disc-T 60 | Disc-M 57

*(Staff scoring uses different taxonomy; not directly comparable.)*

**Disc-T evidence**:

**Observation**: Disc-T (60) and Disc-M (57) are close. The 3-point gap is consistent with the transcript capturing Hoenig's explicit push for balanced language, which attenuates the median dovish-hawkish reading slightly less in the minutes' aggregate phrasing.

### 12. CREDIT_SPREADS (Staff only)
**Scores**: Staff-T 38 | Staff-M 55

**Staff-T evidence**:
- Fisher: "the rather significant closing of the spreads in 30-year swaps versus Treasuries"
- Fisher: "heavy pace of private corporate offerings" — robust corporate issuance
- No discussion of elevated high-yield spreads or flight to quality

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Tightening credit availability for business firms that did not enjoy investment-grade credit ratings or favorable earnings prospects"
- "Growth of domestic nonfinancial debt slowed somewhat...some moderation in the brisk pace of private borrowing"

**Observation**: The largest divergence in the comparison (38 vs 55). Staff-T draws primarily on Fisher's financial briefing, which describes 30-year swap spread compression as the dominant credit spread development. Staff-M draws on minutes language about sub-investment-grade credit tightening, interpreted as spread widening for lower-quality borrowers. The two documents are describing different segments of the credit market: investment-grade/swap spreads (compressed) vs. high-yield/lower-rated credit (tightening).

Staff-M scores 45, reflecting the "outsized earlier gains" framing, which implies stocks were previously stretched and are now correcting toward fair value rather than signaling distress. The divergence turns on whether current equity levels represent stress (above 50) or a correction from overvaluation (below 50).

## Cross-Cutting Observations

**Where sources converge strongly** (all within 5 points):
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58/58/58/58 — perfect agreement
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70/67/68/67 — 3-point spread
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS/CONDITIONS: 58/60/58/57 — 3-point spread
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60/57 — 3-point spread
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20/18 — 2-point spread
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60/60 — identical

**Where sources diverge** (spread >5 points):
- OUTPUT_GAP: 56 (Disc-T) vs 49 (Staff-T) — 7-point spread driven by whether "below-trend" language is treated as describing the current level or the forward trajectory
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 (Staff-T) vs 55 (Staff-M) — 7-point intra-staff gap; Staff-T weights deceleration signals, Staff-M weights still-positive incoming data
- CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 (Staff-T) vs 55 (Staff-M) — 17-point intra-staff gap; different market segments described (swap spreads vs. high-yield credit availability)
- EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 (Staff-T) vs 45 (Staff-M) — 10-point intra-staff gap; Staff-T interprets as mild concern, Staff-M interprets as correction from overvaluation

**Document-format effects**: The transcript (Disc-T) produces higher INFLATION_PRESSURE and LABOR_TIGHTNESS than the minutes (Disc-M), consistent with individual anecdotes from district presidents amplifying the perceived intensity of conditions. The minutes' aggregate language ("relatively tight," "somewhat more") tends to moderate extreme readings. For staff sources, the transcript preserves the full nuance of Fisher's and Stockton's differentiated market commentary; the minutes compress this into summary phrases that can shift scores in either direction depending on which details are retained.
