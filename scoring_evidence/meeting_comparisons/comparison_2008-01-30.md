### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 66 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly perceive inflation as clearly above the 2% target, driven by energy and food pass-through plus some core acceleration, with the majority worried it may not self-correct quickly. A minority (Mishkin, Geithner, Rosengren) believe slack will bring it down, but even they acknowledge the current readings are elevated.

Quotes:
- MR. PLOSSER: "Both the core CPI and the core PCE accelerated in the second half of '07, compared with the first half. The core CPI advanced at a 2.6 percent rate in the second half of '07... the core PCE was up at a 2.4 percent rate in the second half compared with 1.9 in the first half."
- MR. LACKER: "If you look back over the past four years, the overall PCE price index has averaged 2¾ percent. On a 12-month basis, the index has been below 2 percent only four months in that span."
- MR. FISHER: "every measure of inflation has turned up... 83 percent of the items therein experienced a price upswing"; Frito-Lay raising prices 6%, Burlington Northern rail factor up 11% ("highest rate in history"), Wal-Mart budgeting 6-7% food price increases.
- MR. MISHKIN (minority): inflation risks are "balanced and actually somewhat to the downside." VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER: "inflation outlook for the reasons laid out in the Greenbook is better than it was."

---

**Minutes: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see current inflation as modestly above target with disappointing recent readings, but expect moderation from slack and commodity stabilization.

Quotes:
- "Participants agreed that the inflation data that were received since the December meeting had been disappointing."
- "Headline inflation had been generally above 2 percent over the past four years, and participants noted that such persistently elevated readings could ultimately affect inflation expectations."
- "Many believed that the slow growth in economic activity anticipated for the first half of this year and the associated slack in resource utilization would contribute to an easing of price pressures."
- "Some participants cautioned that commodity prices had remained stubbornly high" and firms were passing on costs.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-uniform agreement that the economy is below potential, with most forecasting Q1-Q2 growth of 0–1.5% against trend of ~2.5–2.75%. Recession probability cited at 45–70%+ by multiple speakers. The economy is distinctly below potential but has not yet entered the catastrophic contraction of late 2008/2009.

Quotes:
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Growth well below potential for the first half of this year results in additional slack in labor markets."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "the economy is at a risk of recession; in any case, it is likely to grow slowly for the first half of the year."
- MR. PLOSSER: "growth in the first half of the year to be quite weak, probably around 1 percent"; expects return "toward trend, which I estimate to be about 2¾ percent."
- MR. FISHER (minority): "none of the 30 CEOs... see the economy trending into negative territory." MR. WARSH: "trends in the real economy may be a bit more positive coming into the first quarter from the fourth quarter than the Greenbook projections."

---

**Minutes: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy clearly operating below potential with intensifying weakness across housing, manufacturing, and investment. Significant downside risks. Expected pickup in H2 2008 from fiscal stimulus and easier policy provides slight upward pull on the band.

Quotes:
- "Information received since the December meeting had been decidedly downbeat on balance."
- "Participants expected economic growth to remain weak in the first half of this year."
- "Several participants noted that the risks of a downturn in the economy were significant."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor is clearly softening — unemployment rose, payroll trimming has begun — but not yet at deep recession levels. Some pockets of tightness remain (engineers, Northern Virginia). Consistent with a labor market beginning to deteriorate, sitting below full employment but not yet in substantial slack territory.

Quotes:

- MR. LACKER: "Labor market conditions in the Fifth District have deteriorated in the last month. In a noticeable shift from previous surveys, numerous contacts told us they had begun to trim payrolls."
- MR. STERN: "the labor market may be a bit better than the December household and payroll surveys depicted, given the low level of initial claims."

---

**Minutes: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market loosening with emerging weakness, but not yet deep slack. Some pockets of tightness remain.

Quotes:

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is contracting or at best deeply below trend. Retail contacts universally report weakness; business investment also softening. Export strength provides a partial offset. Housing investment is in freefall.

Quotes:
- MS. PIANALTO: "The CFO of one of the nation's major department store chains told me last week that her company's January sales are shaping up to be the worst that they have seen in the past twenty years."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "Consumer spending has slowed. I think there's little doubt about that at this point... households... denied access to home equity... greater uncertainty in the economy and the labor market... natural tendency would be to be much more conservative in their spending."
- MR. LACKER: "Consumer spending appears to have slowed somewhat at the end of last year... carrying less momentum into this quarter... I am a bit less hopeful about business investment, particularly structures."
- MR. FISHER (minority): MasterCard December retail sales ex-auto, ex-gas up 5%; UPS up 2% YoY.

---

**Minutes: 28 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Broad-based weakness across all spending categories — consumer, business investment, and especially housing. Clearly contracting.

Quotes:

- "The outlook for business investment had turned weaker... Several participants reported that firms in their districts were reducing capital expenditures."
- "Housing activity had continued to deteriorate sharply... several participants noted the absence of signs of stabilization."
- "A few participants reported more upbeat attitudes among firms in the technology and energy sectors."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 74 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are significantly tighter than normal, with credit standards tightening across C&I, consumer, and commercial real estate. Monoline stress adds systemic risk. Spreads have widened substantially. Not yet the acute freeze of September 2008 — some improvement in short-term funding noted (TAF effective) — but clearly elevated.

Quotes:
- MR. KOHN: "investors and lenders seem increasingly concerned about the broader economic weakness... demanding much greater compensation for taking risk in nearly every sector... the spreading out from the housing sector of lending caution to other sectors... A substantial portion of banks reported tightening terms and standards for C&I loans... most banks—like 80 percent—tightened up on commercial real estate credit."
- MS. PIANALTO: "I'm now detecting the first signals of a credit crunch. Bankers in my District tell me that they're finding it much more difficult to issue debt... Deals that bankers would have done for creditworthy borrowers not long ago are simply not being done today."
- MR. WARSH: "financial institutions... are undercapitalized, even with the recent capital infusions... depleted credit availability and significantly tighter lending standards."
- VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER (minority): "some sign that the process of repair is starting... substantial early equity raising by major firms; pretty substantial improvement in market functioning; easing of liquidity pressure."

---

**Minutes: 72 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions significantly tighter with credit constraints spreading across markets. Not yet frozen/panic but clearly restrictive with worsening trajectory. Short-term funding markets improved (TAF); some banks raising capital — these partial offsets prevent a higher score.

Quotes:
- "Broader financial conditions had tightened significantly, on balance, in the weeks leading up to the meeting."
- "The availability of credit to consumers and businesses appeared to be tightening, likely adding to restraint on economic growth."
- "Some noted the especially worrisome possibility of an adverse feedback loop, that is, a situation in which a tightening of credit conditions could depress investment and consumer spending."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 18 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus for 50 bp cut (strong dovish action), with 1 formal dissent from Fisher (who wanted to hold) and 1-2 pushing for 75 bp. Firmly in the "strong dovish: majority favors easing or maintaining max accommodation" range. The 225 bp cumulative easing since August and the forward signal of further cuts if needed confirm the strong dovish posture.

Quotes:
- Overwhelming majority support Alternative B: 50 bp cut (Pianalto, Lockhart, Rosengren, Kohn, Yellen, Evans, Stern, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Geithner, Bernanke).
- MR. MISHKIN: "I would actually advocate a 75 basis point cut at this meeting because I do think there is a need to take out insurance."
- VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER: "we're going to have to get them lower even with another 50 basis points tomorrow."
- MR. FISHER (sole dissenter): "I truly believe we have it right at 3½ percent right now."

---

**Minutes: 18 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish consensus for significant further easing. Growth risks dominate, though inflation concerns provide a mild restraint on the most extreme dovish scores. No explicit dissent against easing is mentioned. Document is truncated before full policy discussion details.

Quotes:
- "Most members believed that a further significant easing in policy was warranted at this meeting."
- "Participants expected economic growth to remain weak in the first half... risks to economic growth in the near term appeared to be weighted to the downside."
- Context: This meeting followed an intermeeting 75bp emergency cut just days earlier, indicating strong dovish urgency.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent (Fisher), 2-3 strong reservations (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker) from the hawkish end, and 1-2 preferences for more easing (Mishkin, Rosengren). The statement language produced real debate — two camps on how dovish the forward guidance should be. Despite the disagreement, there is near-consensus on the 50 bp action itself; the dissent is mainly about degree and communications.

Quotes:

- MR. PLOSSER: "we are on the verge of overshooting, and I worry about the broad range of consequences for our credibility." Pushes for more hawkish statement language, disagreeing with majority on communications.
- MR. MISHKIN: would prefer 75 bp but accepts 50; views inflation risks as "balanced and somewhat to the downside."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "I now understand that several of you will be quite unhappy with the nature of that statement."

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is arguably the central organizing concern of this meeting. The economic assessment go-around is deeply intertwined with financial stability analysis. Just below 85-95 range because it has not yet reached the acute panic-level emphasis of Sept 2008.

Quotes:
