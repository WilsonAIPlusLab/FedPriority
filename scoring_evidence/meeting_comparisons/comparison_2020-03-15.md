# FOMC Meeting 2020-03-15: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

**Meeting type**: Emergency intermeeting videoconference (Sunday, March 15, 2020)
**Context**: COVID-19 pandemic onset; 100bp cut to ELB, QE launch ($500B Treasuries + $200B MBS), discount window and swap line actions

---

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 22 ±6 [High confidence] | 23 ±5 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes**:
- Clarida: "the net effect of the virus is likely to be disinflationary, not inflationary...my baseline view is that we will end the year further away from our price-stability objective than we began."
- Daly: "we face considerable disinflationary pressures coming from weaker demand, higher uncertainty, a stronger dollar, and lower oil prices. So this means that even when the pandemic abates, inflation is going to be an ongoing concern."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly see inflation as already below target and moving substantially further below target due to the demand collapse, with explicit disinflation language from multiple speakers — placing conditions well into the subdued/deflation-risk range.

**Minutes — representative quotes**:

- "a stronger dollar, weaker demand, and lower oil prices were factors likely to put downward pressure on inflation in the period ahead"

- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had declined"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants saw inflation as already below target and expected further downward pressure from multiple forces (dollar, demand, oil). Not yet deflationary alarm, but meaningful below-target concern.

**Minority views — Transcript**: Bullard briefly argues oil price decline "acts like a tax cut for consumers" and has a positive aspect, but framed as a minor note.
