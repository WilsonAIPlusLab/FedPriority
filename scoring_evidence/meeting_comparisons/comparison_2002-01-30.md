### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Disc. Transcript — Score: 24 ±6 [High]**
- Mr. Parry: "slack in labor and product markets holding the increase in the core PCE index to just over 1¼ percent in 2002."
- Ms. Bies: "I continue to be quite surprised by how little evidence there is of price increases. The fact that we actually had a fall in nominal GDP in the quarter is unusual."
- Ms. Minehan: "inflation as measured by the PCE at 1.3 percent…unemployment rate is well above…the level at which labor markets can function without inflation."
- Mr. Kohn: inflation on a "downward trajectory through the forecast interval," noting the risk of the zero nominal bound.

**Disc. Minutes — Score: 27 ±5 [High]**
- "Inflation was likely to remain quite subdued. Indeed, core inflation could well edge lower."
- "The low rate of resource utilization anticipated over the year ahead, rising productivity, and highly competitive market pricing could be expected to moderate price pressures."
- PCE forecasts "narrowly clustered around 1-1/2 percent."
- No member expressed concern about inflation pressures.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 30 ±5 [High]**
- Core inflation moderating; ZLB scenarios highlight deflation risks; inflation trajectory "more pronounced" downward in adverse scenarios.
- Core CPI still around 2% in baseline; deflation risk is scenario-based, not the central forecast.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 28 ±5 [High]**
- "Consumer price inflation was quite low at year-end."
- "Both the CPI and the PCE chain-type price index edged down on balance in November and December."
- "Core consumer price inflation was held down late in the year by widespread discounting of goods."
- Staff forecast: "Sub-par expansion...was expected to foster an appreciable further easing of pressures on resources and some moderation in core consumer price inflation."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Disc. Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 [High]**
- Mr. Ferguson: "in the staff projection the output gap is persistent and positive during the entire forecast period…output gap for the next eight quarters that goes from about 1.8 percent of GDP down to about 1 percent of GDP."
- Ms. Minehan: "After about two years of growth below potential, inflation is quite low."
- Mr. Kohn: "the pickup will be moderate, consistent with some further slackening in resource utilization in the near term. In the staff forecast, the output gap closes slowly in the second half of 2002 and in 2003."

**Disc. Minutes — Score: 34 ±5 [High]**
- "A variety of factors could well keep the pace of expansion below the rate of growth of potential for a while."
- "The low rate of resource utilization anticipated over the year ahead."
- "Economic activity was bottoming out and a recovery might already be under way."
- GDP growth forecast of 2.5-3% suggests gradual closing of the gap but not there yet.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 [High]**
- Unemployment projected to rise "through 2003"; economy just emerging from recession; significant slack.
- Recovery beginning but below potential; ZLB special briefing underscores depth of slack concern.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 30 ±5 [High]**
- "Economic activity probably steadied in the fourth quarter after a sizable drop in the summer."
- "The rate of utilization of total manufacturing capacity...was at its lowest quarterly level since 1983."
- Staff forecast: "economic activity likely would start to turn up early in 2002...and would gather strength only gradually" with "sub-par expansion over the next few quarters."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Disc. Transcript — Score: 30 ±6 [High]**
- Mr. Hoenig: "Labor markets continue to be fairly easy in terms of the ability to hire workers. It's still rather easy to find new employees, and there are no real signs of wage pressures."
- Mr. Ferguson: "unemployment that appears likely to be at or near 6 percent for as long as the forecast allows us to see the future. That is above anyone's estimate of the NAIRU."
- Mr. Parry: "slack in labor and product markets."

**Disc. Minutes — Score: 30 ±5 [High]**

- "Cyclical downturn in employment" and "further anticipated increases in unemployment" cited as risks.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 [High]**
- Employment still declining; unemployment rising; payrolls weak.
- Firms "responded swiftly and aggressively" to downturn by cutting payrolls; significant slack persists.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 33 ±4 [High]**

- "Private nonfarm payrolls fell considerably, with manufacturing again experiencing the largest job losses."
- Staff forecast: unemployment would "begin to edge down" only later.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Disc. Transcript — Score: 40 ±7 [High]**
- Mr. Moskow: "we have not heard any reports that suggest firms are increasing investment or plan to do so soon. Businesses seem to be continuing to delay even productivity-enhancing capital expenditures."
- Multiple speakers: holiday retail sales "slightly better than expected," housing "held up quite well," auto sales strong with incentives — but with "heavy discounting."
- Mr. Kohn: "household spending has been so well maintained, the release of pent-up demand for durables…are not likely to be present this time around."

**Disc. Minutes — Score: 40 ±7 [High]**
- Consumer spending "held up remarkably well" and "moderate growth was a reasonable expectation."
- "Anecdotal reports suggested that business executives were still notably cautious."
- "The capital investment sector seemed likely to retard the overall advance in economic activity during the quarters immediately ahead."
- "Some members" cited potential negatives for consumer spending; others noted capital spending rebound could exceed forecasts.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 42 ±6 [Moderate]**

- Business investment still contracting; mixed picture dominated by ZLB briefing focus.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 38 ±6 [High]**
