### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 35 ±6 | Moderate confidence**
**Minutes score: 35 ±5 | High confidence**

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly see inflation as clearly below the 2% target (~1.3–1.4% core PCE), with most expecting a gradual return driven by transitory oil/dollar effects fading, but a significant minority expressing persistent downside risk.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly agreed inflation was running below the 2% objective with transitory factors depressing it, and expected a gradual return to target. The score reflects below-target inflation with limited near-term concern but no deflation alarm.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Rosengren: "core PCE inflation is still well below our target, recent price data do seem consistent with some firming since earlier this year"
- Kocherlakota: "Inflation is well below target. Tealbook A projects that inflation will get back to target in 2019, four years from now"
- Brainard: "The 12-month change in core PCE is estimated to have been 1.4 percent in September...data suggest an underlying trend in inflation that is between 1¼ and perhaps 1¾ percent"
- Evans: "Our full suite of models still forecasts 2018 inflation in the 1¼ to 1¾ percent range...I have difficulty feeling confident that inflation is headed back to 2 percent any time soon"

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and prices of non-energy imports."
- "Core PCE inflation also remained low, but some other measures of inflation, such as the trimmed mean PCE and trimmed mean CPI measures, continued to run at higher levels than core PCE inflation and had recently moved up modestly."
- "Several participants, however, cited downside risks to inflation, pointing, for example, to declines in market-based measures of inflation compensation."
- "A few participants noted that the September CPI data appeared consistent with some firming in inflation."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: The economy was expanding moderately and approaching potential, but the center of gravity still saw some remaining slack, placing the output gap slightly below potential.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: The labor market had improved significantly but the center of gravity saw remaining slack, with subdued wage growth reinforcing the view that full employment had not yet been reached.

**Transcript quotes:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment were both characterized as "solid," with housing recovering. Net exports were a drag but domestic demand was the clear driver. This is modestly above trend.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 61 ±7 | High confidence**
**Minutes score: 55 ±7 | High confidence**

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The overwhelming majority (roughly 12 of 17 participants) lean toward liftoff in December at minimum, with 2 (Williams, Lacker) wanting to hike at this meeting; Alternative B passes with only 1 formal dissent (Lacker); one extreme dove (Kocherlakota) advocates easing. The center of gravity is lean-to-moderately hawkish — holding but strongly signaling December.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: The committee held steady but with a clear tightening bias — most anticipated conditions for liftoff could be met by December. This is a lean-hawkish hold with strong forward tightening intent.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Williams: "I continue to prefer to take the first step in policy normalization at this time as described in alternative C"
- Lacker: "I continue to see a rate increase as warranted, and I support alternative C"
- Mester: "I continue to believe there's a good case for moving policy off the zero lower bound. In my view, the criteria we've set out for liftoff have been met"
- George: "I continue to see a strong case for raising the funds rate. Final private domestic demand is growing at a healthy pace; unemployment looks to be at or near its natural rate"
- Kocherlakota (outlier): "I would recommend that the FOMC initiate a new round of asset purchases and publicly announce its intention to explore the feasibility of negative interest rates"
- Evans: "I'm not eager to support a December rate liftoff. I continue to believe current conditions and my outlook indicate that would be premature"

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Some participants thought that the conditions for beginning the policy normalization process had already been met. Most participants anticipated that... these conditions could well be met by the time of the next meeting."
