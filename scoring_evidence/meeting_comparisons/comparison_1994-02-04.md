### A1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 56 +/-7 [High] | 55 +/-7 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "Growth in the employment cost index... has been stuck at 3-1/2 percent for quite a while. At current interest rates... we would not be able to expect any further progress in bringing inflation down in 1994 and clearly not in 1995."
- MR. KELLEY: "that fourth-quarter CPI of 3.4 percent, which looks a bit ominous... just about all of the commodity price indexes... seem to have a rather persistent upward drift... 'There's dust on the horizon!'"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "we're now seeing some major risks on the inflation front... commodity price increases working their way into the PPI and CPI."
- MR. MELZER: "CRB futures price index is up by nearly 13 percent from its level a year ago."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants see current inflation as low but with the disinflationary trend ending and upward pressures emerging from labor tightness, commodity prices, and strong demand. The debate is not about current readings but about anticipated acceleration.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The members generally expressed concern about a buildup in inflationary pressures during the year ahead, especially if what they currently viewed as a very accommodative monetary policy were maintained."
- "Broad measures of prices and wages had displayed mixed patterns over recent months and that on the whole they did not yet point to any clear change in inflation trends."
- "Some members noted that a number of commodity prices had turned up in recent months, and they referred to still scattered but increasing anecdotal reports that some business firms were paying slightly higher prices."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Members see inflation as modestly concerning — broad measures are stable but anticipatory indicators (commodities, anecdotal input prices, diminishing slack) point to building pressure ahead.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The economy is growing above potential with slack diminishing to modest levels. Not yet overheating, but clearly above the neutral line.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor market is approaching full employment but not yet there. Scattered skilled-worker shortages emerging, but overall slack remains modest.

### A6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 67 +/-7 [High] | 65 +/-7 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "There's a time to talk, a time for speculation, and a time to act. We've talked, we've had speculation, and I think now is the time to act."
- MR. LINDSEY: "the staff... said that to maintain not price stability but constant inflation we have to raise interest rates 150 basis points by mid-1995."
- MR. MELZER: "I favor 50 basis points rather than a more modest amount... we are late in acting; too small a policy shift may not convince markets of our resolve."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if this economy continues to move... I would be strongly supportive of much more rapid and, frankly, larger moves."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unanimous agreement to tighten policy. Roughly half the committee preferred 50bp over 25bp. The debate was entirely about how much to tighten, not whether. Greenspan advocated 25bp due to market impact concerns, not economic disagreement.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The members favored an adjustment toward a less accommodative policy stance."
- "A number of members endorsed a policy move that would involve only a slight adjustment" while "other members indicated a preference for a somewhat greater firming action."
- "Many observed that additional firming probably would be desirable later."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unanimous support for tightening (the first in 5 years), with debate only about magnitude. The center favors a cautious initial move but expects more to come.

**Transcript evidence:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Broad agreement with minor tactical differences about magnitude of the first move. Zero dissents. The disagreement about pace is compressed into a brief phrase; the intensity of the debate is not conveyed.

|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 72 +/-6 [High] | 62 +/-7 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The forward signal leans hawkish — many members expect more tightening — but the formal directive is symmetric/neutral. The combination suggests a mild tightening bias going forward, data-dependent. Less hawkish in tone than what the transcript conveys.
