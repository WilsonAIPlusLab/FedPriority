# FOMC Meeting 2011-11-02: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 40 ±6 [High confidence] | 35 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Williams: "with considerable slack, slow growth, and fairly low inflation, the central tendency of these policy rules is completely consistent with the current very accommodative stance"
- Rosengren: "inflation being at 1½ percent, roughly where the Tealbook was, by mid-2013"
- Yellen: "inflation running below the 2 percent level that most Committee members consider consistent with our dual mandate"
- Lacker: "I'm very pleased with [inflation forecast]... I worry that a forecast of moderating inflation relies overly much on the effect of slack"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The large majority of participants perceive inflation at or slightly below the 2% target, with medium-term forecasts of ~1½–2%; the center of gravity is modestly below target but not at deflation levels.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Participants generally agreed that measures of total inflation appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year as prices of energy and some commodities declined from their peaks."
- "Measures of core inflation also seemed to have declined in recent months, and longer-term inflation expectations remained well anchored."
- "Some participants noted that core inflation had not come down as quickly or by as much as they had expected."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation is below target and moderating, held down by declining commodity prices, slack, and subdued wages, though core has been stickier than expected.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 33 ±5 [High confidence] | 35 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Evans: "output far below its potential, the more we risk actually eroding that potential"
- Bernanke: "unemployment well above what most people consider to be the long-run equilibrium level"
- Williams: "considerable slack, slow growth" — policy rules "consistent with the current very accommodative stance"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Near-universal agreement that the economy remains well below potential, with substantial slack persisting years after the recession; recovery is underway but the gap is large.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The most probable outcome was a moderate pace of economic growth over the medium run with only a gradual decline in the unemployment rate."
- "Significant slack in resource utilization."
- "The recovery was still subject to significant downside risks."
- "A few participants... mentioned the possibility that economic growth could be more rapid than currently expected."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy is meaningfully below potential with numerous headwinds cited, though the third quarter showed some improvement from temporary factor reversal.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 32 ±5 [High confidence] | 33 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Evans: "very high unemployment"; multiple participants reference unemployment "remaining stubbornly high"
- Plosser: "unemployment rates remain stubbornly high, and we're all deeply concerned about that"
- Tarullo: "we have now had a several-year pattern of growth that has little spurts and then reverts back to a very tepid pace" — attributing unemployment to aggregate demand shortfall
- Lacker: "the natural rate... is fairly high and fairly close to the unemployment rate we have" (minority view)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Virtually all participants characterize the labor market as far from full employment, with unemployment near 9% and limited prospect of rapid improvement.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Overall labor market conditions remained weak, and the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "Unemployment would decline only gradually from levels significantly above those that the Committee would expect to prevail in the longer run."
- "Some participants again warned that the exceptionally high level of long-term unemployment could ultimately lead to permanent negative effects."
- "Some participants suggested that the persistently high level of unemployment reflected the impact of structural factors" (minority view).

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor market is clearly slack with elevated unemployment; structural vs. cyclical debate but all agree the market is weak.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 37 ±6 [High confidence] | 40 ±6 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Tarullo: "a several-year pattern of growth that has little spurts and then reverts back to a very tepid pace"
- Williams: "normal channels of monetary transmission remain unusually clogged. Many businesses and households are unable to take on new debt or want to shed existing debt"
- George: "GDP growth has been resilient... thanks to strong business investment" (noting Q3 beat, slight upside surprise)
- Bullard: businesses are "profitable and cash rich," with growth strategies centered in Asia

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Spending and investment are below trend — the recovery is proceeding but sluggishly, with household deleveraging, housing dysfunction, and debt overhang holding back consumption; business investment provided a partial bright spot in Q3.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Final demand from consumers and businesses was stronger than had been expected at the time of the September FOMC meeting."
- "Incoming data on retail sales were somewhat stronger than expected."
- "The recent pickup in consumer spending outpaced growth in after-tax incomes... raising doubts about its sustainability."
- "Business contacts... remained reluctant to hire or expand capacity."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Below-trend spending with recent positive surprises, but sustainability questioned. Housing depressed, business cautious.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence] | 60 ±8 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Dudley: "very rapid failure of MF Global underscores the fact that the broker-dealers in the United States do not have a credible lender-of-last-resort backstop... we may have to do something very quickly on the liquidity front"
- Tarullo: "the risks of a significant adverse event in Europe are as high as they have been since May 2010"
- Yellen: "dysfunction in mortgage markets is attenuating the effectiveness of monetary policy accommodation"
- Fisher: tail scenario concern about S&P 500 falling to 600

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Current financial conditions are broadly accommodative given policy at ZLB and OT in place, but meaningful stress is present from European contagion risk, MF Global fallout, and persistent mortgage market dysfunction — producing a near-neutral-to-slightly-tight reading.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Financial markets continued to be particularly volatile during the intermeeting period."
- "Liquidity in many markets worsened."
- "Still-tight credit conditions for many households and small businesses."
- "Credit costs were low" and "U.S. banks continued to be willing to extend loans to creditworthy borrowers."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are somewhat tight, with elevated volatility and European spillover risks tightening conditions, though corporate credit is relatively healthy.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 28 ±7 [High confidence] | 22 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Evans (dissent): "I support further actions by the Committee to provide additional significant monetary accommodation... I cannot support alternative B today"
- Majority supports Alternative B (hold): Williams, Kocherlakota, Lockhart, Pianalto, Fisher, Bullard, Plosser, Lacker, Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin, Dudley, Bernanke
- George (lean hawkish): "My preference is to take no further action... I favor no further action"
- Plosser implicitly mentions preference for Alternative C

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The Committee firmly holds with ongoing QE-era accommodation (ZLB + OT), with one explicit dissent for more easing and multiple participants expressing dovish sympathy; no one advocates raising rates.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "All Committee members agreed to continue the program of extending the average maturity of the Federal Reserve's holdings."
- "The Committee agreed to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent and to reiterate its expectation that economic conditions... are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- "A few members expressed interest in using language specifying a period of time... rather than a calendar date."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Clearly dovish — maintaining zero rates, Operation Twist, and extended forward guidance. No hawkish dissents visible. A few members interested in potentially doing more.

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 58 ±8 [High confidence] | 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Evans formally dissents: "I expect that my policy position is simply too far outside the consensus, and I cannot support alternative B today"
- Tarullo: "very much tempted to join President Evans in dissenting"
- Rosengren argues current policy is inadequate in the medium term
- Three identifiable camps: strong dovish (Evans, Rosengren, Yellen, Tarullo, Duke, Raskin), centrist hold (Williams, Lockhart, Pianalto, Kocherlakota, Dudley), hawkish-cautious (Fisher, George, Plosser, Lacker, Bullard)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** One formal dissent (Evans) for more easing, near-dissent from Tarullo, and meaningful disagreement extending to economic assessment (structural vs. cyclical unemployment, inflation risk, efficacy of QE); three identifiable camps create notable division.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "All Committee members agreed to continue the program."
- "Members generally preferred to retain the existing forward guidance."
- "A few members expressed interest in using language specifying a period of time... rather than a calendar date."
- Disagreements noted on inflation risk balance and structural vs. cyclical unemployment framing.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Broad agreement on policy action with minor disagreements on guidance language and economic assessment. No formal dissents mentioned in available text.

### Dimension 8: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence] | 55 ±6 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Dudley: extensive discussion of MF Global failure, broker-dealer lender-of-last-resort gap, European contagion risk, need for liquidity backstop

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial stability receives moderate attention from several participants (Dudley, Tarullo, Yellen, Duke, Fisher), primarily focused on European tail risk and housing/mortgage market dysfunction.

**Minutes evidence:**

- "Participants also discussed the events surrounding the bankruptcy filing of MF Global Holdings Ltd. and saw the financial stability implications of this development as limited to date."
- "It was also noted that the extended period of highly accommodative monetary policy could eventually lead to a buildup of financial imbalances."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial stability received prominent attention — European crisis, market volatility, MF Global, banking sector health, and low-rate risks all discussed at length.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 12 ±5 [High confidence] | 10 ±4 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Yellen: "The phrase 'at least' conveys that policy firming might not commence until significantly later than mid-2013"
- Evans favors threshold guidance with unemployment floor of 7% — even more dovish
- Williams: liftoff not until "around mid-2014" on his own forecast; discussion of extending to 2015 under some frameworks

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is firmly "low for long," maintaining commitment to near-zero rates through at least mid-2013 (with many seeing 2014+); active debate pushes toward even more extended/conditioned accommodation.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The Committee agreed... to reiterate its expectation that economic conditions... are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- "A few members expressed interest in using language specifying a period of time during which the federal funds rate was expected to remain exceptionally low."
- No discussion of normalization.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Strong easing forward guidance with date-based commitment to near-zero rates through mid-2013 and ongoing Operation Twist. Some debate about form of guidance (date vs. time-contingent) but all on the dovish side.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### Staff Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Staff Transcript | Staff Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence] | 38 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Staff transcript evidence:**
- Core inflation had been surprising to upside in recent months, but expected to moderate.
- Commodity prices easing.
- Core PCE ~1.5–1.7%.
- Still below target on forward basis.

**Staff transcript center-of-gravity:** Below neutral but closer to it.

**Staff minutes evidence:**
- "Overall consumer price inflation was more moderate than earlier in the year, as prices of energy and some commodities declined from their recent peaks."
- "The staff continued to expect prices to rise at a subdued pace in 2012 and 2013."
- "Consumer prices excluding food and energy rose a bit less than in the preceding quarter."
- "Longer-term inflation expectations remained stable."

**Staff minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation moderating from a commodity-driven spike, running below target on a forward basis, with subdued wages and stable expectations.
