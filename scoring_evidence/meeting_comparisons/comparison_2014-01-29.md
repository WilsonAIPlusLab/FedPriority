## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 36 ±6 [Moderate confidence] | 33 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. Evans: "very low inflation outlook...wage growth has been modest to meager...inflation could remain low for some time. This poses a substantial risk to achieving our inflation objective of 2 percent within a reasonable time frame."
2. Williams: "overall and core PCE price measures both increased about 1 percent last year, and inflation appears likely to remain below our target for some time."
3. Yellen: "inflation has been disconcertingly low over the past year, and, at this stage, reversion of inflation toward our 2 percent objective remains only a forecast."
4. Bernanke (summary): "Inflation remains below target, though relatively stable at that level. Firms don't have much pricing power, and cost pressures are low."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Near-unanimous participant view that inflation is stuck around 1% PCE, well below the 2% target, with transitory factors (low import prices, medical costs) playing a role; anchored long-run expectations are the primary mechanism expected to bring inflation back, but this is viewed with uncertainty.

**Minutes — representative quotes:**

1. "Inflation remained below the Committee's longer-run objective over the intermeeting period."
2. "Several factors that cast doubt on this outcome were also mentioned, including slow growth in labor costs, the lack of pricing power reported by business contacts in various parts of the country."
3. "Inflation persistently below the Committee's objective would pose risks to economic performance."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Inflation is running below 2% target with multiple factors suggesting it could stay low; participants expect gradual return to target but acknowledge downside risks. No participants argued inflation was at or above target.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. Williams: "while the economy is improving, considerable slack remains."

**Minutes — representative quotes:**

**Minutes center of gravity:** Economy recovering and closing the gap but still operating below potential; moderate growth expected going forward. No participants significantly divergent on this assessment.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 37 ±7 [High confidence] | 34 ±6 [High confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. Kocherlakota: "a falling unemployment rate over the past four years has been associated with little increase in wage and compensation pressures. Indeed, in the first three quarters of 2013, average hourly compensation in the nonfarm business sector was essentially flat."
2. Tarullo: "wage gains have been pretty muted across a variety of industries, including those in the service sector...it's hard to find much evidence of industry-specific wage pressures by looking at aggregate data."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Unemployment at 6.7% and falling, but labor market broadly described as still having significant slack — low wages, high underemployment, depressed participation. **Minutes — representative quotes:**

1. "Broader concepts of the unemployment rate...remained well above the official unemployment rate, suggesting that considerable labor market slack remained."
2. "A number of participants saw the low rates of increase in most measures of wages as consistent with continued labor market slack."
3. "Still-elevated levels of underemployment and long-term unemployment, and the apparent absence of wage pressures."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Labor market improving but substantial slack persists, confirmed by broad measures of underemployment and weak wage growth. "A few participants noted worker shortages in specific regions and occupations" — clearly described as localized.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 28 ±6 [High confidence] | 43 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. All participants support Alternative B — continued tapering at $10B/meeting pace; unanimous (or near-unanimous) vote.
2. Evans: "maintaining our current accommodative monetary policy seems important to counteract the low-inflation-risk scenario."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Ongoing large-scale asset purchases (still $65B/month after this meeting's taper), near-zero rate commitment well into 2015, strong emphasis on patience and high bar for accelerating. Policy stance is firmly accommodative; marginal taper represents very gradual normalization rather than any pivot toward tightening.

**Minutes — representative quotes:**

1. "Most participants judged that the incoming information about the economy was broadly in line with their expectations and that a further modest step down in the pace of purchases was appropriate."
2. "All members agreed that the cumulative improvement in labor market conditions...indicated that it would be appropriate to make a further measured reduction."
3. "Maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6-1/2 percent."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Unanimous support for continued gradual taper, but maintaining highly accommodative stance via near-zero rates and continued (though reduced) purchases. Lean dovish overall. "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon" (hawkish outliers). "A couple" questioned taper pace given low inflation (dovish outliers).

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 12 ±5 [High confidence] | 18 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. Bernanke: guidance has "three prongs" — "well past" 6.5%, inflation criterion, taper timeline — all pointing to "somewhere in 2015" for liftoff.
2. Evans: "Communications do seem to indicate that the funds rate will most likely stay at zero into 2015...a slight overshoot [of inflation] without a lot of dithering about it could help reinforce that [2% is not a ceiling]."
3. Kocherlakota: advocates committing to keep rates low "at least until the unemployment rate falls below 5.5 percent" — more dovish than the status quo.
4. Yellen: "we intend to keep policy highly accommodative for quite some time and that we'll be patient in removing accommodation when the time comes to do so."

**Transcript center of gravity:** The forward signal is strongly dovish — rates at zero well into 2015, liftoff only expected after asset purchases end (with "considerable period" after that), and multiple participants want even more dovish quantitative guidance. No changes made at this meeting, but the status quo forward signal is explicitly dovish.

**Minutes — representative quotes:**

1. "Appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6-1/2 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's longer-run objective."
2. "Several participants argued that the forward guidance should give greater emphasis to the Committee's willingness to keep rates low if inflation were to remain persistently below."
3. "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon" and cited standard policy rules.

**Minutes center of gravity:** Forward guidance is clearly dovish — low for long, well past the unemployment threshold, especially with low inflation. "A few participants" who raised the possibility of earlier rate increases were a clear minority.

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 45 ±8 [High confidence] | 30 ±5 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript — representative quotes:**

1. Dudley: "we don't have a good read on three important factors: what productivity growth will be in 2014...how participation rates will change if the labor market tightens...and the level of the natural rate."
2. Evans: extended discussion of uncertainty in inflation modeling — whether anchored long-run expectations will continue to drive inflation back, or whether Japan-like dynamics could prevail.
3. Powell: "the whole framework for analyzing wage and price increases seems to me to be in question...we also don't know whether inflation will react as predicted in the textbooks."
4. Rosengren: "we have not always been able to distinguish well between what is transitory and what is a trend...we should be cautious about our inferences."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Multiple distinct dimensions of genuine uncertainty: inflation equation reliability, labor market slack measurement, participation rate decomposition, EME spillover, and the possibility of being closer to potential than baseline suggests. This goes beyond rhetorical hedging to genuine model uncertainty acknowledged by many speakers.

**Minutes — representative quotes:**

1. "Most participants still viewed the risks to the outlook for the economy and the labor market as having become more nearly balanced in recent months."
2. "Developments in several emerging market economies, if they continued, could pose downside risks."
3. Debate about reliability of unemployment rate as an indicator.

**Minutes center of gravity:** Risks seen as more nearly balanced, suggesting normal-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty. Not especially heightened. Some concern about emerging market contagion and interpretation of labor market signals added modest uncertainty.
