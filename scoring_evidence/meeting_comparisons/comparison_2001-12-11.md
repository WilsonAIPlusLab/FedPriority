### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores**: Disc-T 25 | Disc-M 25 | Staff-T 32 | Staff-M 30

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MR. MCTEER: "On the inflation front, I concur with the respondents to the University of Michigan survey who seem to have bought into our rhetoric a few years ago that we were only one recession away from price stability. We now seem to be getting the disinflation we wished for."
- MR. BROADDUS: "The Greenbook estimates that core PCE is currently running at a rate of about 1-1/2 percent and the staff is forecasting that it's going to come down 1/2 point to 1 percent by 2003."
- MR. PARRY: "slack in labor and product markets holding the increase in the core PCE price index to just over 1-1/4 percent in 2002."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the ability of firms to pass through increasing costs is virtually nonexistent... it is very difficult to pass on cost increases to customers. Profit margins have come down very sharply."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "Underlying inflation might well edge lower from its currently modest levels."
- "Labor compensation appeared to be on a decelerating trend."
- "The ability of most businesses to raise prices was likely to remain quite limited or even nonexistent."
- "Some also noted that the risks of a deflationary spiral seemed very limited."

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Stockton: "we have made another small downward adjustment to our forecast for core PCE prices. The indirect effects of lower projected energy prices, continued favorable news on inflation expectations, and a small upward revision to structural productivity led us to mark down our forecast of core PCE inflation by 1/4 percentage point in both 2002 and 2003. We now expect core prices to decelerate from about a 1-1/2 percent pace this year to a bit above 1 percent in 2003."
- Stockton: "we see the environment [for inflation] as one in which measures are more likely to be going down than up...there will be little upward pressure—and more likely downward [pressure]."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "Core consumer price inflation remained at a relatively subdued pace in September and October."
- "the core personal consumption expenditure (PCE) chain-type price index also indicated that consumer inflation was significantly lower during the year ended in October."
- "growth of average hourly earnings of production or nonsupervisory workers slowed."

**Note**: All four sources agree inflation is below target and declining. The 7-point gap between discussion sources (25) and staff sources (30–32) may reflect participants emphasizing deflationary risk language more emphatically than the staff's data-reporting register.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores**: Disc-T 32 | Disc-M 23 | Staff-T 22 | Staff-M 27

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MR. MOSKOW: "The economy is clearly in recession and the November employment report indicates that activity has not bottomed out yet."
- MR. PARRY: "Our forecast shows a drop in real GDP of 1-3/4 percent in the current quarter."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the very considerable and continuing overhang of deflationary forces... the playout of deflationary forces, at least looking at profit expectations, is not anywhere near complete."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "The economy clearly was continuing to contract."
- "Aggregate demand would remain well below the economy's potential output over the next several quarters."
- "Subpar economic performance seemed likely to persist, at least for a time."

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Stockton: "Overall activity appears likely to have contracted at a moderate pace in the fourth quarter, led once again by a sizable liquidation of inventories and ongoing declines in capital spending."
- Stockton: "our forecast for the growth of real GDP only a bit—from a drop of 2.4 percent in the last Greenbook to a decline of 2.1 percent in our current forecast."
- Stockton: "through 2003, we saw an economy that was still running with a considerable degree of slack in resource utilization and one in which inflation was still headed lower."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "economic activity had continued to decline into the fourth quarter."
- "Subpar expansion in the next few quarters was expected to foster an appreciable further easing of pressures on resources."
- "aggregate demand would remain well below the economy's potential output over the next several quarters."

**Note**: Staff-T (22) and Disc-M (23) are tightly aligned; Staff-M (27) and Disc-T (32) sit moderately higher. Disc-T's higher score reflects participants' discussion of recovery prospects as a partial offset, while the minutes' language of "well below potential" produces a lower score in both Disc-M and Staff-T/M.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores**: Disc-T 30 | Disc-M 26 | Staff-T 28 | Staff-M 30

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MR. BROADDUS: "the rise in the unemployment rate over the last three months has been the fastest increase in a long time — since 1982... the drop in manufacturing jobs, at 163,000, was the biggest decline so far in this cycle."
- MR. HOENIG: "only a quarter of the firms reported any difficulty hiring or retaining workers, and that's the lowest percentage in our survey's history."
- MR. POOLE: "the softening in the labor market has made it easier for Wal-Mart to hire sales associates."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "Substantial further deterioration in labor market conditions, which was widely anticipated."
- "Slack in labor and product markets."
- "The potentially adverse effect on consumer confidence and spending of additional deterioration in labor market conditions."

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Stockton: "The unemployment rate rose to 5.7 percent last month, a steeper rise than we had anticipated. And, after declining nearly 490,000 in October, private payrolls tumbled an additional 325,000 in November."
- Stockton: "In labor markets, employment has been falling at a rapid clip in recent months, and the unemployment rate is on a steep upward trajectory."
- Stockton: "Manufacturers shed more than 160,000 workers last month, while help-supply jobs dropped nearly 90,000 and wholesale trade employment declined 25,000."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "The labor market deteriorated substantially in October and November. Nonfarm payroll employment fell significantly in both months."
- "The heavy job losses boosted the unemployment rate to 5.7 percent in November."

**Note**: All four sources cluster in the 26–30 range — the tightest cross-source agreement of any dimension. All convey rapid deterioration to 5.7% unemployment with broad sectoral job losses.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores**: Disc-T 30 | Disc-M 32 | Staff-T 36 | Staff-M 34

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MS. MINEHAN: "real income fell sharply and the only recently elevated saving rate collapsed. Consumer borrowing may not be growing at the same pace... debt service as a share of disposable personal income is rising to levels that are historically quite high."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Sales so far in the holiday shopping season, however, have been disappointing for most retailers we've talked with."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "motor vehicle sales slumped very sharply in the first 10 days of December... so motor vehicles no longer seem to be a positive force."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- Consumer: "Consumer expenditures appeared to have been relatively well sustained."
- Business: "Business capital spending appeared to be continuing to decline at a rapid pace."
- "New construction of nonresidential structures had declined sharply."
- Housing: "New home construction and sales had displayed considerable resilience."

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Stockton: "Sales of new motor vehicles remained much stronger in November than we had anticipated, even making allowance for the extension of the recent incentive programs. Retail sales outside of autos bounced back more sharply in October than expected, as did the consumption of services. New home construction and sales have also exhibited greater resilience than we had expected."
- Stockton: "we are hearing few reports yet of a significant change in business sentiment regarding capital spending" and "ongoing declines in capital spending."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "Personal consumption expenditures are estimated to have rebounded in October... slightly above the third-quarter average."
- "Purchases of motor vehicles surged in response to aggressive zero-rate financing packages."
- "the downward trend in business spending on durable equipment and software might be moderating somewhat."

**Note**: Staff sources score this 6 points higher than Disc-T (36 vs. 30). Staff emphasize consumer spending resilience and upside surprises; discussion transcript participants emphasize the reversal of auto incentives, disappointing holiday sales, and rising debt service — a more forward-looking negative read on consumer conditions as of meeting date.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Scores**: Disc-T 38 | Disc-M 32 | Staff-T 30 | Staff-M 35

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MR. BROADDUS: "The stock market is up, Treasury bond rates are up, credit spreads have narrowed, and the federal funds futures market suggests that market participants expect policy to be tightened more aggressively next year than was expected earlier."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The 10-year Treasury yield... has gone up almost a full percentage point... that's pretty much what has happened to mortgage rates."
- MR. JORDAN: "since September 11, over $3 billion of commercial construction has been either cancelled or suspended because the lenders cannot get insurance or its cost is prohibitive."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "The stance of policy was already quite accommodative."
- "Low interest rates, including zero rates on many motor vehicle loans."
- "The recent rise in mortgage interest rates" noted as a concern.
- "Widespread expectations of some further easing at this meeting to be followed by a policy turnaround next year."

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Stockton: "With the stance of monetary policy remaining quite accommodative, faster sales growth might also lead firms to retrieve capital spending plans that had earlier been shelved."
- Kos (context): "22 out of 24 primary dealers expect an easing at this meeting—but then an aggressive tightening beginning sometime in the second half of 2002."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "short-term interest rates declined a little."
- "rates on speculative-grade bonds fell sharply, and major indexes of equity prices moved significantly higher."
- Monetary policy stance described as "quite accommodative."

**Note**: All sources agree conditions are accommodative (all scores below 50). The 8-point spread (30–38) reflects a distinction in emphasis: staff sources lead with the "quite accommodative" characterization; Disc-T acknowledges accommodative policy but also cites rising mortgage rates and commercial construction financing stress, producing a slightly higher (tighter) score.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Scores**: Disc-T 25 | Disc-M 30 | Staff: N/A

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "reduce the rate because... if we move the rate down 25 basis points and that turns out to be wrong, I cannot conceive of the deflationary forces moving away sufficiently quickly to prevent us from moving the rate back up well before inflationary pressures rise."
- MR. PARRY: "I strongly support your recommendation of a 25 basis point reduction in the funds rate as some insurance against downside risks."
- MR. BROADDUS: "a persuasive case can still be made for another 50 basis point reduction today."
- MR. STERN (minority): "I would prefer to leave policy unchanged today."
- MR. HOENIG (minority): "A 2 percent fed funds rate is stimulative. We've cut the rate 450 basis points this year."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "All but one member indicated their support of a proposal to ease reserve conditions slightly further, consistent with a 25 basis point reduction."
- "Several members viewed an easing action as a measure of insurance."
- "Several saw a decision to ease as a close call."
- "The Committee's easing cycle was likely to be approaching its completion."
- Minority (one member): "In the view of one member, policy was already sufficiently stimulative."

**Note**: Both sources place this in the dovish half, but the transcript scores more dovish (25 vs. 30). The transcript captures a three-way split (hold / 25 bp / 50 bp) with BROADDUS advocating 50 bp, which shifts the center of gravity lower. The minutes summary counts only one formal dissent and multiple "close call" members, producing a modestly less dovish reading.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Scores**: Disc-T 22 | Disc-M 32

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T)**
- MR. POOLE: "our task at this point is to start an orderly process toward a changed policy stance... the message that I personally would like to send... there may be a two-thirds chance that we will not change the funds rate at the next meeting."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I'm also hopeful that the next time we meet policy will be stable and the risks will be balanced."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it's too abrupt an adjustment to change to a balance of risks statement that says it is just as likely that at the next meeting we will raise rates as lower them... the chances are probably two to one that we will not be changing the funds rate at our next meeting."

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M)**
- "The Committee's easing cycle was likely to be approaching its completion, and several suggested the desirability of signaling that view to the public."
- "Risks continued to be weighted mainly toward conditions that could foster economic weakness."
- "Such a statement was not intended to convey the impression that the Committee necessarily contemplated further easing actions."

**Note**: 10-point gap. The transcript captures explicit probability statements by Greenspan and Poole (two-thirds chance of no change at next meeting) alongside strong tapering signals, producing a lower (more easing-biased-but-tapering) score. The minutes' language more directly highlights the retained weakness-risk language and does not reproduce the specific probability framing, yielding a somewhat higher (more balanced) score.

### TREASURY_STRESS
**Scores**: Staff-T 57 | Staff-M 42

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T)**
- Kos: "The volatility of the 2-year note was as high as 7 basis points; that level had been approached briefly a couple of times in the mid-1990s and was exceeded only in the late 1980s."
- Kos: "That may have been the case last Friday when the weak labor market report was followed by higher, not lower, yields." [counterintuitive price behavior]
- Kos: "the dealer community absorbed this supply but only with a large price adjustment...a shift in perceptions, a position overhang, a price insensitive group of hedgers, and a risk-averse dealer community came together at a delicate point in time."

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M)**
- "intermediate- and long-term Treasury rates rose substantially" — contextualized as reflecting "better-than-expected incoming economic data."

**Note**: 15-point gap — the largest cross-source divergence for staff dimensions. The transcript preserves Kos's full diagnosis of technical dislocations (historically elevated 2-year volatility, counterintuitive yield response to weak labor data, dealer positioning overhang, MBS hedging feedback loop). The minutes reduce this to a single sentence on yield movements attributed to improving economic data, with no dysfunction language, producing a near-neutral stress score.
