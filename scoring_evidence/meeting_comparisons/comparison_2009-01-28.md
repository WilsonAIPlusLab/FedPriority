### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 18 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The overwhelming majority of participants see meaningful deflation risk and a sub-1% inflation trajectory. Even the less alarmed acknowledge near-term disinflation.

Evidence:
- Yellen: "I expect core PCE price inflation to come in at about 1 percent this year and then edge down a bit more in 2010 and 2011...it is not hard to imagine a situation with excessive declines in inflation and a long-lasting deflationary trap."
- Bullard: "I am very concerned about deflationary expectations possibly taking hold...the key near-term goal is just to avoid that outcome."
- Fisher: "significantly higher deflationary risks...the 800-pound blue-footed booby in the room."
- Bernanke: "extent of slack and declining commodity prices...will lead to declining inflation...disinflation is a concern."
- Hoenig (minority/less alarmed): "I don't consider [deflation] the highest risk we have" — inflation will "remain low for some time" but not necessarily deflationary.

Minority views: Plosser and Lacker hold that inflation expectations are anchored enough to prevent sustained deflation; Hoenig downplays deflation risk.

---

**Minutes: 15 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The consensus sees inflation well below target with risk of a protracted period of excessively low inflation; deflation is a minority but real concern. A few worry about potential future inflation from balance sheet expansion, but this is a forward risk, not a current perception of pressure.

Evidence:
- "Participants agreed that inflation pressures had diminished appreciably in recent quarters, and they expected significantly lower headline and core inflation during the next few years."
- "Many participants noted some risk of a protracted period of excessively low inflation... and a few even saw some risk of deflation."
- "Several others, however, anticipated that longer-run inflation expectations would remain well anchored... and therefore thought it unlikely that inflation would decline below levels they saw as consistent with the dual goals."

Minority views: "Some noted a risk that expected inflation might actually increase to an undesirably high level" — this widens the uncertainty band upward.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 13 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is in catastrophic contraction. Capacity utilization at record lows, freefall in output across all sectors. Score of 13 is within the 1–15 "catastrophic contraction" range.

Evidence:
- Bernanke: "very sharp contraction, both domestically and abroad...likely to be the worst postwar recession."
- Warsh: "the economy is in terrible shape. Activity is falling fast with little indication of stabilization even at new lower levels."
- Kohn: "Consumption, business equipment spending, and housing are all falling rapidly, and commercial construction is on the cusp of pulling back in a major way. There is really no sign that the pace of decline in final demand is slowing."
- Rosengren: "more than five years to return to full employment and an inflation rate of 2 percent or more."
- Fisher (on capacity): "TI is running its factories at 33½ percent of capacity. The Eastman chemical plant...running at the lowest capacity utilization in over 100 years."

Minority views: Plosser is "a little more optimistic than a lot of people" and Stern sees "reasonable probability" of Greenbook recovery — but even these optimists agree the current moment is deeply below potential.

---

**Minutes: 15 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is in catastrophic contraction far below potential, with participants expecting substantial slack for years to come and seeing overwhelming downside risk.

Evidence:
- "The economy had weakened further going into 2009... a sharp and widespread economic contraction both domestically and abroad."
- "Considerable economic slack" expected for "several years."
- "All but a few saw the risks to growth as tilted to the downside; ... they saw a significant risk that the economic recovery would be both delayed and initially quite weak."

Minority views: None visible — near-universal agreement on severe contraction.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 14 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Universal reports of large-scale layoffs, hiring freezes, wage freezes, and rising unemployment. Forecasts of unemployment above 8% (many at 8.5%). Score of 14 falls in the 1–15 "depression-level" range.

Evidence:
- Evans: "all CEOs I spoke with indicated that they are in the process of large-scale layoffs...I did not find a left drawer either."
- Fisher: CEO described three drawers of job cuts — "I asked about the drawers to the left, and he said, 'There are none.'"
- Yellen: "anticipated severe hemorrhaging in jobs and a sizable jump in the unemployment rate."
- Plosser: "Unemployment rates will clearly rise this year above 8 percent."
- Kohn: "Employment and production have been cut back especially sharply...mounting layoffs are increasing the fear of job loss."

Minority views: No meaningful dissent on labor market weakness.

---

**Minutes: 16 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is in severe deterioration — aggressive payroll cuts, wage freezes, and sharply declining employment with no signs of stabilization.

Evidence:
- "Consumers were continuing to cut back expenditures in response to sharply declining employment."
- "Business contacts had indicated that firms were reducing payrolls aggressively and also freezing wages and salaries."
- "Participants indicated they had been surprised by the speed and magnitude of the slowdown."

Minority views: None — unanimous view of severe labor market weakness.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 14 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer and business spending both in freefall; investment plans being slashed across the board. Housing essentially at zero. Score of 14 is in the 1–15 "collapsing/freefall" range.

Evidence:
- Lockhart: "consumer retrenchment has significantly affected restaurants, the travel industry, and a wide variety of personal services...investment decisions are being indefinitely postponed."
- Pianalto: "steep decline of just over 20 percent [in business fixed investment] in the second quarter...those who had already lowered their investment plans seem to be accelerating their reductions."
- Fisher: "Not a single CEO I spoke to, from Disney to the large manufacturers, is doing anything but [cutting capex]. The least amount of cutback that I heard was 12 percent, and the average...is up to a third."
- Kohn: "Final demand is also continuing to contract. Consumption, business equipment spending, and housing are all falling rapidly."
- Yellen: director "reported that his company posted its worst season since it was founded in 1901."

---

**Minutes: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending, business investment, and housing are all in freefall simultaneously. Commercial real estate faces potential sharp deterioration. This is a collapsing-level assessment across all spending categories.

Evidence:
- "Consumers were continuing to cut back expenditures in response to sharply declining employment, further declines in wealth, and tighter credit conditions."
- "Equipment investment had declined particularly sharply... business contacts had reported sizable cutbacks in their planned capital expenditures for 2009."
- "No indication that the housing sector was beginning to stabilize... new home sales, housing starts, and permits all continued to decline steeply."

Minority views: Some hope from anticipated fiscal stimulus, but participants had "questions about the details" and the magnitude was "far from clear."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 83 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Conditions are at near-extreme tightness. Securitization markets virtually shut, credit available only to top-tier borrowers at very high rates, bank equity near single digits, CDS spreads at Depression-era levels. Some marginal improvements in money and mortgage markets (from Fed programs) prevent a score of 90+.

Evidence:
- Yellen: "credit spreads remain breathtakingly large...rated BB...the best they could come up with was a loan rate of 18 percent."
- Kohn: "Outside the GSEs, securitization markets remain virtually shut down. Credit to any but high-quality borrowers is still very expensive...yield on BBB corporate bonds...about 2 percentage points higher than it was in the first half of September."
- Warsh: "expectations of defaults that are near Depression levels...large and increasing number of financial institutions...that are perceived by markets to be effectively insolvent or nearly so."
- Rosengren: "both the securitization market has broken down and bank balance sheets are quite constrained, which is why we have seen spreads that are very, very large."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "we are not where your model wants to describe" — massive deleveraging across nonbank sector.

Minority views: Some participants note modest improvements in specific markets (interbank, mortgage after Fed purchases).

---

**Minutes: 82 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Crisis-level financial tightness — credit conditions are "extremely tight" and the banking sector is under "substantial stress," with only modest improvement in Fed-supported markets.

Evidence:
- "Participants generally saw credit conditions as extremely tight, with financial markets fragile and some parts of the banking sector under substantial stress."
- "Modest signs of improvement were evident in some financial markets — particularly those that were receiving support from Federal Reserve liquidity facilities."
- "Most participants saw the renewed deterioration in the banking sector's financial condition as posing a significant downside risk."

Minority views: The "modest signs of improvement" in supported markets slightly temper the score from the absolute crisis peak.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Universal desire to maintain/expand accommodation. No voice for tightening. Debate was purely about which additional easing tools to deploy and when. The adopted alternative B maintains 0–25 bp, implements TALF, and keeps MBS/Treasury purchase options open.

Evidence:
- Rosengren: "I prefer alternative A...I see little harm in expanding our program of purchases of mortgage-backed securities and GSE securities to lower mortgage rates further."
- Yellen: "I favor alternative B...I am certainly open to the idea of buying longer-term Treasuries...also very open to the idea of expanding our purchases of mortgage-backed securities."
- Bullard: "I am going to counsel essentially a wait-and-see attitude...but I think the wait-and-see attitude would echo a lot of what President Yellen said." Also: "we could be much more aggressive if we want to avoid this deflationary outcome."
- Lacker (dissent): Wanted to expand balance sheet via Treasuries rather than TALF — disagreement on tool, not on dovish direction.
- Bernanke: "I think we ought to recognize that we could be at zero for quite a long time."

Minority views: Hoenig and Plosser preferred B (wait and see) over A (immediate further expansion), but both still firmly in dovish territory. Lacker dissented on form of easing, not direction.

---

**Minutes: 8 ±4 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Maximum dovish accommodation — zero rates, unconventional tools deployed, discussion centers on whether/how to expand further, not whether to tighten.

Evidence:
- "Committee members agreed that keeping the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to ¼ percent would be appropriate."
- "They also agreed to continue using liquidity and asset-purchase programs to support the functioning of financial markets and stimulate the economy."
- "Members noted that it may be necessary to expand these programs."

Minority views: "Somewhat different views about the best way" of expanding — one member preferred Treasuries over targeted programs — but this is tactical, not directional.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 35 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent, a contested straw vote, multiple named camps with distinct prescriptions (A vs. B), and a day-long foundational debate about the appropriate policy tools. However, there is broad agreement on the dovish direction and urgency.

Evidence:
- Formal dissent: Lacker voted against TALF authorization, preferring Treasury purchases.
- Straw vote on inflation language: 9 in favor vs. 6 opposed — contested split.
- Clear camps: Rosengren/Evans/Lockhart favored alternative A (more aggressive now); Hoenig/Plosser/Stern/Warsh/Bullard/Kohn favored B (wait and see).
- Extended substantive debate on Day 1: Lacker vs. Dudley on TALF market failure, Lacker vs. majority on Treasury purchases vs. targeted credit.
- Bernanke: "I personally think we could go either way" on inflation language — reflects genuine uncertainty about consensus.

Minority views: Some might score this higher due to the Lacker/Dudley debate intensity; others might score lower noting the ultimate broad alignment on maintaining easing.

---

**Minutes: 15 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Broad consensus on the dovish direction and key policy actions. Disagreements are about operational modalities (Treasury purchases vs. targeted programs; quantitative targets), not about direction.

Evidence:
- "Committee members agreed that keeping the target range... would be appropriate. They also agreed to continue using liquidity and asset-purchase programs."
- "Had somewhat different views about the best way of doing so." "One member expressed the view that it would be best to expand holdings of U.S. Treasury securities rather than to expand targeted liquidity programs."
- "Several participants indicated that they thought the FOMC should explore establishing quantitative guidelines... Others were skeptical."

Minority views: One member prefers a different composition of asset purchases; some debate on quantitative targets.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 82 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability dominates both days of the meeting. The Day 1 balance-sheet discussion, plus the financial sector content of virtually every go-round participant, makes this a meeting where financial stability rivals or co-dominates the economic assessment.

Evidence:
- Day 1 session (approximately 40% of total discussion length): entirely devoted to Fed credit facilities, TALF, balance sheet programs, and banking system stabilization.
- Duke (entire go-round): deep discussion of bank fourth-quarter earnings, loan-loss provisions, goodwill impairment, TARP dynamics.
- Warsh: "the most precarious state in financial markets that we have been at, at least in a regularly scheduled FOMC meeting."
- Kohn: "bank losses for the fourth quarter were greater than markets anticipated, and many institutions remain under substantial stress."
- Bernanke (summary): "The most important news on the financial side since the last meeting...has been the worse-than-expected results in the banking system."
- Multiple participants: commercial real estate as "next shoe to drop" threatening bank solvency.

---

**Minutes: 78 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a dominant theme throughout the document — the crisis is understood primarily through a financial lens, with banking stress, credit tightness, and market fragility permeating virtually every topic discussed.

Evidence:
- "Intensification of the financial crisis and the interaction between deteriorating economic and financial conditions" — framing the crisis as financially driven.
- "Credit conditions as extremely tight, with financial markets fragile and some parts of the banking sector under substantial stress."
- "Most participants saw the renewed deterioration in the banking sector's financial condition as posing a significant downside risk to the economic outlook absent additional initiatives to stabilize the banking system."

Minority views: None — financial stability concerns are universally shared.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is one of extended zero-rate accommodation plus potential further asset purchase expansion. No exit discussion for the near term.

Evidence:
- Bernanke: "we could be at zero for quite a long time...even if you take the central tendencies of this Committee for 2011 and stick them into a Taylor rule, you still get minus interest rates."
- Bullard: Greenbook baseline "the federal funds rate remains at zero into 2013."
- Lacker: "I am thinking liftoff is likely sometime in 2010 rather than in 2012" — the most hawkish forward signal in the room and still 12+ months away.
- Adopted statement (Alternative B): maintains readiness to expand MBS purchases and "is prepared to purchase longer-term Treasury securities if evolving circumstances indicate that such transactions would be particularly effective."
- Rosengren: "it will take more than five years to return to full employment...without more-significant actions."

---

**Minutes: 8 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is firmly in the direction of continued or expanded easing. There is no discussion of eventual normalization or exit strategy. The focus is on whether to do more, not whether to do less.

Evidence:
- "Members noted that it may be necessary to expand these programs."
- "Participants anticipated that a gradual recovery in U.S. economic activity would begin during the third or fourth quarter of this year" — but even the recovery is expected to be slow and weak.
- Programs designed "to support the functioning of financial markets and stimulate the economy" — forward orientation is entirely toward continued/expanded easing.

Minority views: The document is truncated, potentially cutting off additional forward guidance language, but the available text is uniformly dovish in forward orientation.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 83 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-universal expressions of deep, genuine uncertainty — about the economy, about the efficacy of new tools, about the banking system trajectory, about whether deflation or inflation is the greater risk, and about whether any model is useful.

Evidence:
- Lockhart: "widespread frustration over the inability to make longer-term plans in such a highly uncertain environment."
- Pianalto: "We don't know how to write down the policy rule for this environment, and we surely don't have any empirical estimates to guide our decisions."
- Bullard: "there is a lot of additional uncertainty that may not be properly captured in those confidence bounds."
- Bernanke: "a lot of what we are doing is requiring our good sense and historical knowledge and our intuition...we do not really have the historical basis for all of the things that we are having to deal with here."
- Evans: "Uncertainty and risk aversion are inhibiting economic and financial activity."
- Warsh: "quite uncertain about the timing of improvement in financial conditions...we are well outside the range of normal experience."
- Dudley: "model-based forecasts probably should be viewed with somewhat greater skepticism."

---

**Minutes: 78 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Very high uncertainty — participants explicitly describe themselves as "quite uncertain," face major unknowns about fiscal stimulus effectiveness, banking system trajectory, and the depth/duration of the downturn.

Evidence:
- "Participants were, however, quite uncertain about the outlook."
- "They saw a significant risk that the economic recovery would be both delayed and initially quite weak."
- "Substantial uncertainty about future economic conditions and government policies."

Minority views: Uncertainty is broadly shared; the range of scenarios itself is the evidence.
