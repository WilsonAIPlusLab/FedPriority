## Part A: Discussion — Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: N/A | Minutes: 62 ±6**

**Transcript evidence:**
- No participant discusses current inflation, price pressures, or inflation expectations in this transcript segment.
- The only price-related reference is a historical anecdote about Nixon-era wage/price controls (MR. JORDAN).

Transcript center-of-gravity: Topic not discussed. The entire transcript segment is confined to foreign exchange intervention philosophy and swap line renewal.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "members saw considerable potential for higher inflation if growth in demand and output continued at an unsustainable pace"
- "They noted indications of greater inflation pressures, especially in the rising prices of many materials used in the production process and the increasing number of reports of labor shortages"
- "at least some modest worsening of inflation seemed quite likely over the quarters immediately ahead"
- "inflationary expectations might be in the process of worsening"

Minutes center-of-gravity: Participants see current finished goods inflation as not yet elevated, but strong pipeline pressures — materials prices, labor shortages — and forward-looking concern about worsening expectations place this clearly above the midpoint. One member suggested a cyclical rise "would not necessarily augur a permanent uptick," a slightly less alarmed view, but the preponderance of language is of building risk.

**Transcript: 48 ±15 [Low] | Minutes: 28 ±6 [High]**

**Minutes evidence:**

Minutes center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose and accommodative despite the rate hiking cycle. Members explicitly noted this as a puzzle and as a reason for further tightening. The "ample availability of funds" and "increasingly accommodative loan policies" language places this well below the midpoint.

Note: The 20-point gap between transcript (48) and minutes (28) reflects a fundamental content gap rather than a genuine interpretive divergence. ### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 65 ±12 [Low] | Minutes: 78 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. LINDSEY: "we stated it in our asymmetric directive. It was quite clear what this Committee wanted to do... my precise recommendation at the last meeting was that... we go asymmetric and have a 50 basis point move as our explicit price for intervention."
- MR. LINDSEY references having gone to "50 instead of 75" in the context of linking intervention to rate changes, suggesting active tightening was the prevailing orientation.

Transcript center-of-gravity: The only monetary policy references in this segment indicate the Committee had an asymmetric (tightening) directive in place and was in a tightening posture. No direct policy debate on the funds rate is present. Score of 65 carries Low confidence due to the indirect and tangential basis.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "all the members agreed that the current stance of monetary policy presented unacceptable risks of embedding higher inflation in the economy"
- "A majority of the members believed that an unusually sizable firming of monetary policy was desirable at this time"
- "they endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions in line with a 3/4 percentage point increase in the discount rate"
- Vote: 12-0 unanimous

Minutes center-of-gravity: Unanimous agreement that more restriction was needed, with a majority favoring an aggressive 75bp move. Even those preferring 50bp acquiesced to the majority view. "Unacceptable risks" language and an unusually large unanimous action place this firmly in the hawkish range. Minority view: "Other members" preferred 50bp, citing risk of "overly restrictive course," but they accepted the majority position.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Transcript: 38 ±10 [Moderate] | Minutes: 28 ±5 [Moderate]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS votes No on swap line renewal; advocates full withdrawal from intervention.
- MR. LINDSEY wants intervention coupled with rate changes, a distinct minority position.
- MR. JORDAN expresses deep reservations about intervention policy.
- MR. LAWARE, MR. KELLEY, VICE CHAIRMAN McDONOUGH strongly support continued participation.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN sympathizes with skeptics but supports participation on practical grounds.

Transcript center-of-gravity: Moderate disagreement on intervention philosophy with one formal dissent (Broaddus) on swap line renewal. The range of views runs from full withdrawal to enthusiastic participation. This disagreement concerns a procedural/philosophical matter about FX intervention, not the core monetary policy decision.

**Minutes evidence:**
- Vote: 12-0, unanimous on the policy directive
- "Views differed to some extent, however, regarding the degree of additional restraint"
- "Other members indicated that they preferred a less forceful policy move... Despite their reservations, these members indicated that they could accept the degree of restraint preferred by the majority"
- One member wanted asymmetric directive toward restraint

Minutes center-of-gravity: Unanimous vote with acknowledged differences about magnitude (75bp vs. 50bp) and directive type (symmetric vs. asymmetric). All agreed on the direction of tightening. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences about pace and degree.

Note: The transcript's higher dissent score (38 vs. 28) captures the real disagreement on the swap line vote (11-1) and the divergent views on intervention philosophy. The minutes reflects the underlying monetary policy decision, which was unanimous.

## Notable Cross-Source Observations

1. **Discussion transcript anomaly — structural gap**: The discussion transcript for this meeting covers only the FX intervention and swap line segment. Six of ten core dimensions are N/A in the transcript. All substantive economic and policy evidence for the discussion track comes from the minutes. Scores from the transcript should not be used for cross-method convergence analysis on this meeting.

2. **Unanimous 75bp hike — extreme hawkishness in minutes**: The minutes discussion dimension scores (HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 78, OUTPUT_GAP: 65, INFLATION_PRESSURE: 62, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65) form a coherent picture of a Committee delivering a forceful, unanimous action against a backdrop of clear above-potential growth. This meeting is an anchor-calibration data point for the 1994 tightening cycle.

3. **Staff cross-source alignment**: Despite coming from different documents (transcript vs. minutes), staff scores are tightly aligned across all dimensions where both sources have data. The largest divergence is DOLLAR_STRENGTH (47 vs. 35), which reflects the different emphasis on stabilization versus the full intermeeting weakness narrative. All other gaps are within or near the stated confidence bands.

4. **Financial conditions paradox — consistent across tracks**: Both staff sources describe moderately tight conditions via the rate channel, while the discussion minutes records participants explicitly noting accommodative lending conditions despite higher rates. This apparent paradox — tighter rates but loose credit — is coherently documented in both staff and discussion tracks and is a recognized feature of the 1994 tightening cycle.

5. **Financial stability emphasis divergence (Transcript 35 vs. Minutes 18)**: The transcript's higher score reflects the concentration of financial stability language in the FX intervention segment, which constitutes the entire transcript content. The minutes score of 18 reflects the correct proportional weight of financial stability discussion within the full meeting context. The minutes score is the more representative figure.
