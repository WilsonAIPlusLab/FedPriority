### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores: Disc-T 45 | Disc-M 38 | Staff-T 52 | Staff-M 53**

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 45:**
- "With inflation quiescent and quite likely to stay so for some time, I just don't see any necessity to keep such a tight hold." (Kelley)
- "recent inflation developments have been relatively modest, a fact we certainly recognize." (Hoenig)
- "while not yet a major concern, it's important for us to keep in mind that inflation measures also have come in on the high side of expectations." (Moskow)
- "Core PCE inflation is projected to come in at 2 percent this year and just a bit lower next year." (Parry)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 38:**
- "apart from energy prices and medical costs, inflation was still relatively quiescent"
- "With the growth in output likely to remain below the expansion of the economy's potential for a while, members anticipated that inflation would remain subdued"
- "some recent measures of increases in core prices had fluctuated on the high side of earlier expectations"

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T) — Score 52:**
- "incoming data have generally been on the high side of our expectations, especially for core CPI and PCE" (Stockton)
- Both core CPI and PCE showing "about 1/2 percentage point acceleration from the same period a year ago"
- Staff expects "core inflation to tail off in 2002" as slack develops

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M) — Score 53:**
- "Price inflation had picked up a little but, abstracting from energy, had remained relatively subdued."
- "Compensation per hour in the nonfarm business sector advanced appreciably more rapidly in the fourth quarter of 2000 and for the year as a whole."
- Staff forecast: "period of sub-par expansion was expected to foster... some moderation in core price inflation."

**Observation:** The staff sources (52, 53) score inflation modestly above neutral, noting the recent pickup in core CPI/PCE and labor cost acceleration. The discussion sources score it below neutral (45, 38), with participants emphasizing "quiescent" conditions and the growth slowdown as a containing force. The divergence reflects a difference in framing: staff report the data pickup; participants emphasize the forward outlook under slowing growth.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores: Disc-T 35 | Disc-M 35 | Staff-T 47 | Staff-M 38**

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 35:**
- "output growth in our forecast remains below potential throughout the forecast horizon" (Parry)
- "even in the more optimistic forecasts, the unemployment rate rises more than I think is necessary to control inflation... we should still worry about the growing output gap" (Gramlich)
- "growth has stabilized at a low rate... around 1 percent plus or minus" (Stern)
- "a lot of capital investment plans being placed on the shelf" (Greenspan)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 35:**
- "evolving business conditions as consistent on the whole with a continued softness in economic activity"
- "overall business activity currently appeared to be growing at a sluggish pace in most regions"
- "growth in output likely to remain below the expansion of the economy's potential for a while"

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T) — Score 47:**
- "sharp slowing in final demands" with manufacturing falling 3.5% (Stockton)
- Payrolls still averaging 130K in Jan/Feb; housing "OK"
- "Not in recession, but not far from it" — gap closing rapidly

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M) — Score 38:**
- "economic activity continued to expand very slowly in the first quarter"
- "The rate of capacity utilization in manufacturing dropped noticeably in January and February to a level further below its long-run average."
- Staff forecast: "period of sub-par expansion" expected to foster "appreciable easing of pressures on resources."

**Observation:** Discussion sources converge tightly at 35. Staff-M scores 38, close to discussion sources. Staff-T scores 47 — the outlier — reflecting that the transcript-based staff assessment noted partially offsetting positives (payrolls, housing) and stopped short of calling a recession, placing the economy near but not clearly below potential. Staff-M (with more complete data review) moves the staff assessment closer to the discussion consensus.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores: Disc-T 48 | Disc-M 52 | Staff-T 55 | Staff-M 55**

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 48:**
- "contacts continue to note tight labor markets as a particular concern, though high-tech employees may now be easier to find" (Minehan)
- "Labor markets in our District are still characterized as tight. The unemployment rate in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Delaware stood at 4 percent" (Santomero)
- "our labor markets eased considerably early this year. The unemployment rate for our five states moved above 4 percent for the first time since mid-1997" (Moskow)
- "The supply of labor may not be as tight as it was earlier, but labor also does not appear to be in excess supply" (Hoenig)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 52:**
- "labor markets in general remained tight throughout the nation"
- "anecdotal reports of less scarce labor resources were becoming more frequent in some areas or occupations"
- "prospects for an extended period of growth in demand at a pace below the economy's potential should ease pressures on labor"

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T) — Score 55:**
- Payrolls averaged 130K in Jan/Feb — slowing but still positive
- Unemployment rising but from very low levels
- Some participants note UE still around 4%

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M) — Score 55:**
- "Overall employment gains were relatively well maintained, and labor markets were still tight though showing signs of softening."
- "The unemployment rate increased to 4.2 percent in February."
- Payroll employment rose but "considerably below the pace of the first three quarters of 2000."

**Observation:** All four sources agree labor markets remain near full employment with easing at the margin. The gradient runs from Disc-T (48) through Disc-M (52) to Staff-T/M (both 55), reflecting that staff sources anchor more directly to the 4.2% unemployment figure and "still tight" characterization, while discussion participants across regions present a wider spread of tightening-vs-loosening views.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores: Disc-T 35 | Disc-M 33 | Staff-T 40 | Staff-M 40**

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 35:**
- "consumer spending seems likely to grow only moderately... investment spending on equipment will rebound more moderately and less quickly than I had thought earlier" (Guynn)
- "we are also getting some slowing in expenditures from the wealth effect... a lot of capital investment plans being placed on the shelf" (Greenspan)
- "the capital goods overhang is greater than the Greenbook assumes and that investment, therefore, will slow more significantly and for a longer period" (Ferguson)
- "Auto spending... contracted sharply but has turned back up... Housing construction and overall consumption spending have held up fairly well" (Gramlich)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 33:**
- "Spending for equipment and software declined in the fourth quarter... weakness during the first half of this year"
- "a substantial volume of planned investment was being postponed, if not cancelled"
- "overall consumer spending had remained relatively strong" but "likely at a relatively sluggish pace" going forward

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T) — Score 40:**
- "Consumer confidence and household balance sheets having deteriorated markedly" (Stockton)
- "the 'high-tech recession' is now our baseline forecast" for capital spending
- Auto sales holding at 17M (incentive-driven); housing OK
- "Very mixed" picture overall

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M) — Score 40:**
- "consumer spending evidently grew only moderately on balance in January and February" amid "slowing income gains and a sizable pullback in consumer sentiment."
- Business equipment spending "increasing sluggishly after a fourth-quarter decline."
- "nonresidential construction activity continued its robust rise."

**Observation:** Discussion sources (33–35) score more negatively than staff sources (both 40). The staff data review explicitly notes partial offsets — nonresidential construction "robust," housing "relatively firm," auto sales at 17M — whereas discussion participants placed more weight on the investment collapse and wealth-effect drag on consumers.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Scores: Disc-T 60 | Disc-M 62 | Staff-T 60 | Staff-M 62**

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 60:**
- "broader financial conditions have not eased—at least not appreciably—despite the decline in the funds rate... the decline in equity prices and strengthening of the dollar have significantly offset the stimulus from lower short-term interest rates" (Meyer)
- "it has become more difficult to obtain credit... further tightening of credit standards and a widening of spreads" (Moskow)
- "while credit market conditions are tighter, I don't hear people telling me that credit is not available" (Broaddus)
- "access to credit is not a problem confronting this economy... the explosive growth of the various measures of money and credit" (Jordan)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 62:**
- "less accommodative financial conditions associated with weaker equity markets and tighter credit terms and conditions imposed by banking institutions"
- "more stringent financing terms for many business borrowers, and a stronger dollar, all of which would be holding down domestic spending and production"
- "the amount of financial stimulus was much smaller than might otherwise be expected from policy easing... because it had been accompanied by further declines in stock market prices"

**Staff Transcript (Staff-T) — Score 60:**
- Stock market declined significantly; wealth effect "exerting considerable restraint" (Stockton)
- Fed cutting but dollar strong, equity prices down
- Despite lower policy rate, broader financial conditions remain tight

**Staff Minutes (Staff-M) — Score 62:**
- "rates on high-yield private debt obligations fell only a little, and banks further tightened standards and terms on business loans."
- Staff forecast cites "relatively stringent terms and conditions on some types of loans by financial institutions" and "decline in household net worth."
- "Yields on Treasury securities, both short- and long-term, moved appreciably lower" — but offset by equity declines and credit tightening.

**Observation:** The four sources converge tightly (60–62). All sources characterize financial conditions as tighter than neutral despite policy rate cuts, with equity declines, tighter bank lending standards, and dollar strength partially offsetting the easing stimulus. This is the dimension with the strongest cross-source agreement.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Scores: Disc-T 27 | Disc-M 25**
*(Staff sources do not score this dimension)*

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 27:**
- "I would propose, therefore, that we reduce the rate by 50 basis points and construct language for the press statement that leaves the door wide open on an intermeeting move" (Greenspan)
- "I believe the arguments for front-loading policy easing are just as valid today as they were at the last meeting... We need to do more rather than less, sooner rather than later" (McTeer)
- "I favor easing monetary policy today... even in the more optimistic forecasts, the unemployment rate rises more than I think is necessary to control inflation" (Gramlich)
- "our policy focus should be on the last half of 2001 and into 2002... I would be very cautious about taking more than a modest step because I believe there is a lot of stimulus in the economy" (Guynn)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 25:**
- "most of the members preferred and all could support a further easing... consistent with a 50 basis point reduction"
- "many of the members generally believed that additional policy easing might well prove to be necessary at some time"
- "A few members expressed a preference for a 75 basis point reduction"

**Observation:** Both sources align closely (25–27), capturing a strong dovish consensus with unanimous support for 50bp and a forward lean toward additional easing. The transcript registers fractionally higher because individual voices urging caution (Guynn, Hoenig, Jordan) are more audible; the minutes aggregate to a cleaner dovish signal.

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 45:**
- "less accommodative financial conditions associated with weaker equity markets and tighter credit terms and conditions imposed by banking institutions"

**Observation:** This is the largest gap in the comparison (70 vs. 45). The transcript records extensive first-person engagement with financial markets — Greenspan's extended stock market analogy, McDonough's international financial fragility tour, Meyer's equilibrium real rate analysis, and McTeer's direct causation claim. The minutes reduce these rich discussions to instrumental mentions of financial conditions as a transmission channel, substantially compressing the emphasis recorded in the transcript.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Scores: Disc-T 65 | Disc-M 58**
*(Staff sources do not score this dimension)*

**Discussion Transcript (Disc-T) — Score 65:**
- "we cannot know or be overly confident that an upturn is in train. It is still possible that the various negative forces existing today will drag us into further weakness. Our policy choice must be made in the face of this very fundamental uncertainty." (Kelley)
- "false dawn" concern — "it surprises us, and we get a renewed wave of weakness"; "the uncertainties surrounding the negative wealth effect... make me nervous about the outlook" (Greenspan)
- "we are dealing with both an uncertain real economy and unpredictable psychology among consumers and businesses" (Ferguson)
- "I also recognize that there's a great deal of uncertainty in the near term and some downside risks as well" (Hoenig)

**Discussion Minutes (Disc-M) — Score 58:**
- "considerable uncertainty surrounded this outlook"
- "a capital overhang of unknown dimensions"
- "rapidly changing and highly uncertain circumstances"
- "How this divergence might eventually be resolved was a significant source of uncertainty and downside risk"

**Observation:** Both sources convey elevated uncertainty; the transcript scores higher (65 vs. 58) as individual participants articulate the specific sources and character of uncertainty in first-person terms — Greenspan's "false dawn," Ferguson's "unpredictable psychology," Kelley's explicit framing of the policy decision as made under "fundamental uncertainty." The minutes compress these to summary phrases like "considerable uncertainty" and "unknown dimensions."
