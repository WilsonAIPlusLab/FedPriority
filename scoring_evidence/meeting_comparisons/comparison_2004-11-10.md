## Cross-Track Observations (Factual)

1. **Economic conditions dimensions (INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS) converge tightly** across both tracks and both document types. All four sources place inflation at 40-42, the output gap at 42-45, and labor tightness at 40-42. These are the most stable dimensions in this meeting.

2. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT shows a cross-track gap**: Discussion track scores 52-53; staff track scores 55-58. Staff sources weigh the "rapid pace" of equipment investment and "strong" consumer rebound more heavily, while discussion track participants modulate with Wal-Mart weak demand, Pianalto subdued Fourth District, and investment disappointment relative to cash flows.

3. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN diverges within the discussion track (70 vs. 62)**. The transcript captures Lacker's and Hoenig's explicit hawkish statements and Greenspan's push to "get the funds rate up as fast as we can this year," which are not separately attributable in the minutes.

4. **DISSENT_INTENSITY diverges most sharply within discussion track (32 vs. 20)**. Greenspan's explicit acknowledgment of "increased variance of opinion" is a uniquely transcript-preserved signal absent from the minutes.

5. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS diverges most sharply across the discussion track (30 vs. 12)**. Stern's housing meeting readout, Geithner's current account vulnerability discussion, and Gramlich's fiscal/dollar warnings do not surface in the minutes text.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS diverges within the staff track (43 vs. 28)**. Stockton's briefing qualifies accommodation with fiscal drag and tightening underway; the minutes staff data section presents a plainer "accommodative" characterization.

7. **The staff track shows greater cross-document consistency on economic data dimensions** than the discussion track, reflecting that staff briefings distill to the same data whether read in transcript or minutes form.
