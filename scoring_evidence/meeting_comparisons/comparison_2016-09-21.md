### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 40 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Brainard: "core inflation has not yet convincingly broken out of the 1.4 to 1.6 percent range it has occupied for most of the past three years. The underperformance of core inflation relative to our target has been extremely persistent."
- Kashkari: "On the inflation front, from our perspective, we've made little progress toward our inflation objective. Market- and survey-based measures of expected inflation are near record lows."
- Fischer (most optimistic): "the core rate of PCE inflation has been increasing since the middle of last year…and is now close to our 2 percent target. I believe we are close to meeting our inflation target."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The overwhelming majority see core PCE at 1.6%, persistently below target, with modest upward trajectory but no near-term threat. A minority (Fischer, Mester, George) see it as close to or approaching target using broader measures (core CPI, median CPI).

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Many members remarked that there were few signs of emerging inflationary pressures or that progress on inflation had been slow."
- "12-month core PCE price inflation had been running at a steady rate below 2 percent."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Inflation persistently below target with limited pressure, but expected to gradually return to 2%. Not alarming but not at target. Minority: "A couple of other members pointed to recent readings on core CPI inflation as suggesting that PCE price inflation was close to meeting the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 51 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Williams: "real GDP growth will be about 1¾ percent on a national basis this year, which is faster than my estimate of potential…With output and unemployment gaps now closed and substantial monetary accommodation still in place, I expect that the economy will overshoot potential next year."
- Lockhart: "moderate GDP growth around 2 percent…This marginally exceeds potential but is sufficient to absorb any remaining resource slack."
- Bullard (minority): "unlike the Tealbook, we do not regard 2 percent real GDP growth as being meaningfully above trend…we also expect relatively little change in the unemployment rate."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The majority view is that the economy is at or very slightly above potential. Yellen notes "some slack still remains." Not "running hot" but "approaching or just reaching potential." Minority: Bullard argues for literal at-potential; Brainard/Evans see below-potential with slack remaining.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "Growth of economic activity had picked up from the modest pace seen in the first half of the year."
- "Some slack likely remaining in the labor market."
- "Some other participants believed that the economy was at or near full employment."
- "The economy was growing only modestly above trend."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential with slight remaining slack. A substantial debate exists between those seeing full employment and those seeing remaining slack. Both directions well-represented.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 52 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 49 ±6 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Mester: "The unemployment rate has been roughly stable around my estimate of its longer-run rate. It and most of the other labor market indicators suggest to me that we are basically at full employment from the standpoint of what monetary policy can effectively address."
- Yellen: "the unemployment rate is still a couple of tenths above my estimate of its longer-run value, and involuntary part-time employment is more than 1 percentage point above its average pre-crisis level."
- Brainard: "The labor market has continued to improve, with few indications of overheating and some signs that continued slack remains."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 4.9%, near most estimates of the natural rate (4.8–5.0%). Labor force participation rising unexpectedly, suggesting marginal additional slack. Most see the market as "basically at" or "close to" full employment with residual uncertainty. Minority views: Mester/Williams/George/Lacker see fully at or beyond full employment; Kashkari/Brainard/Tarullo see more slack than consensus.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "Participants generally agreed that labor market conditions had improved appreciably over the course of the year, with monthly payroll gains averaging about 180,000."
- "Some participants...judged that the labor market had little or no remaining slack."
- "Some others noted that still-muted wage growth, a level of involuntary part-time employment that remained elevated...indicated that slack remained."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment but with genuine debate about remaining slack. The split between "some" saying no slack and "some others" saying slack remains places the center near 49. Wider uncertainty band reflects the active debate.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Transcript: 47 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Brainard: "recent data on equipment investment continue to be quite tepid, manufacturing output remains flat, and real exports are a little more than 1 percent below their level a year ago. Corporate profits fell close to 5 percent over the four quarters ending in the second quarter."
- Evans (on CEO sentiment): "I haven't spoken with anyone who talks about robust expansion plans in the United States." Manpower CEO: "most of his customers expect to be in cost-cutting mode next year."
- George: "Consumption growth was strong in the second quarter, with another robust gain projected in the current quarter…I expect consumption growth to be supported by strong labor market conditions."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is solid. Business investment is the consistent weak point — capex cautious, weak manufacturing, exports declining. H1 GDP was very weak (~1% annualized) but expected to bounce to ~2.5–3% in H2. Net: slightly below trend but improving. Minority: Lockhart, Powell, Harker are more optimistic about the H2 bounce.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "Household spending had been growing strongly but business fixed investment had remained soft."
- "Growth in consumer spending appeared to have moderated somewhat in the third quarter from its rapid second-quarter pace."
- "Manufacturing activity remained flat."
- "Sluggishness in the housing sector appeared to have continued."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending offset by weak business investment, flat manufacturing, and sluggish housing. Net picture is roughly at trend. Consumer side clearly above trend; investment side clearly below.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 30 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Powell: "Financial conditions have eased materially since December. [With] lower long-term rates, higher stock prices, a weaker dollar, and narrower credit spreads."
- Williams: "Financial conditions are easier than in June…there's been a notable decrease in the spread between high- and low-grade corporate debt."
- Mester: "accommodative financial conditions" cited as a supportive factor for growth.

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Near universal agreement that financial conditions are accommodative. Credit spreads narrow, stock prices up, dollar somewhat weaker, rates lower than expected. Dudley explicitly addresses the LIBOR uptick and dismisses it as economically insignificant. Kaplan's financial stability concerns (carry trade, leverage) represent a warning about the degree of looseness, but do not imply tightness.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "U.S. financial conditions were broadly accommodative."
- "Financial conditions remained accommodative."
- "Global financial conditions had improved somewhat in recent months."
- "A few participants expressed concern that the protracted period of very low interest rates might be encouraging excessive borrowing" — consistent with the low score.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Conditions clearly accommodative/loose. Well below neutral tightness.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 57 ±8 [High confidence] | Minutes: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Three formal dissents for C (tightening now): Williams, Rosengren, Lacker, Mester, George (~5 total support C).
- Yellen (recommending B): "my recommendation for today is not to move…teeing up a likely move in December if things move along their current course."
- Dudley: "while I think there are several good reasons not to tighten monetary policy at this meeting…the case for tightening monetary policy has strengthened in recent months."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The Committee voted to hold with clear forward lean to December. Roughly one-third of participants preferred to hike immediately; most others accepted a hold while signaling December. Kashkari was the only participant leaning toward more dovish stance (A). Minority: Kashkari (lean A, wanted evidence of inflation first); Brainard/Evans also express reservations about the forward lean.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "Members generally agreed that the case for an increase in the policy rate had strengthened."
- "A majority of members judged that the Committee should, for the time being, await further evidence."
- "Three members preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting."
- "Several stated that the decision at this meeting was a close call."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Lean hawkish — case for hiking acknowledged by all, but majority wants to wait a bit more. Close call for many. Minority: Three members wanted to hike now (~65–70); some others wanted more convincing evidence on inflation before hiking (~45–48).

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Transcript: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence] | Minutes: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:
- Kaplan: Extended discussion of carry trade, short investment horizons, leveraged yield-seeking, shareholder activism pressuring leverage. "I'm particularly and increasingly concerned about a growing trend toward use of what I will loosely call the carry trade."
- George: "nonfinancial corporate debt as a share of GDP is approaching its highest level on record going back to 1959…debt-to-earnings…at multidecade highs."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a secondary but present theme raised by 3–4 participants with some depth (Kaplan, George, Dudley, and lightly Fischer, Brainard on European banking). It is a supporting consideration for the policy debate but does not dominate the meeting.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "A few participants expressed concern that the protracted period of very low interest rates might be encouraging excessive borrowing and increased leverage in the nonfinancial corporate sector."
- "One participant expressed the view that prolonged periods of low interest rates could encourage pension funds, endowments, and investors...to save more."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Limited discussion — a few participants raised financial stability concerns briefly. Not a major topic. The few who raised it were concerned about low-rate risks, but it was not taken up broadly.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 67 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 62 ±6 [High confidence]**

**TRANSCRIPT evidence**:

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly toward one more hike in the near term (December), with "for the time being" language explicitly calendar-referencing December. A mild-to-moderate tightening signal — not aggressive multi-hike guidance, but a clear lean toward imminent tightening. Minority: Kashkari and Evans would prefer no such signal; Bullard warns it may over-commit the Committee.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "The case for increasing the target range for the federal funds rate had strengthened in recent months."
- "Some participants believed that it would be appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate relatively soon if the labor market continued to improve."
- "Participants generally expected that, with gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy, economic activity would expand at a moderate pace."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Clear forward lean toward tightening. The signal is that a hike is coming soon, consistent with gradual normalization path. Minority: "Some others preferred to wait for more convincing evidence that inflation was moving toward the Committee's 2 percent objective."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated and pervasive — debates about r*, u*, productivity trend, participation rate forecast, Phillips curve validity, global spillovers, and framework applicability. Multiple participants explicitly use "uncertain" or "uncertainty" regarding key structural parameters. Minority: Mester and Lacker express higher confidence; Bullard has a specific "regime" framework that gives him confidence within that model.

**MINUTES evidence**:
- "A substantial majority now viewed the near-term risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced."
- "Several of them indicating the risks from Brexit had receded."
- "Several participants indicated that there was uncertainty as to how long the low level of r* would persist."
- "The longer-run normal rate of unemployment was uncertain and could be lower than current estimates."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Risks roughly balanced, Brexit fears receded. Some structural uncertainty about r-star and natural rate of unemployment, but overall a moderate-to-somewhat-elevated level. Minority: "A few still judged that overall risks were weighted to the downside."
