### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 22 ±5 | Minutes: 26 ±6**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Essentially all participants see inflation significantly below the ~2% target, with most characterizing the trajectory as disinflation and several explicitly flagging deflation as a credible risk; this places the score firmly in the 11-25 range (meaningful deflation risk, "subdued," "muted").

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation clearly running below mandate-consistent levels with little pricing power and declining market-based expectations, but participants saw "only small odds of deflation," placing this in the disinflation zone rather than deflation alarm.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Rosengren: "We continue to see evidence of disinflation. The core CPI is currently just below 1 percent and is expected to remain far below 2 percent through the forecast period of 2012."
- Bullard: "Inflation measured from one year ago continues to be about 1 percent by a variety of measures. Inflation expectations measured by market-based TIPS break-even inflation rates seem to be moving somewhat lower."
- Yellen: "Inflation is running below desired levels" and "the probability distribution of future inflation outcomes is too low."
- Bernanke (summary): "Inflation remains low, with measures of underlying inflation having fallen since the start of the recession."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Most participants indicated that underlying inflation was at levels somewhat below those that they judged to be consistent with the Committee's dual mandate for maximum employment and price stability."
- "Many business contacts reported that they currently had little pricing power and that they anticipated limited, if any, increases in labor costs."
- "TIPS-based inflation compensation had declined, on balance, in recent quarters."
- Participants saw "only small odds of deflation."

**Transcript minority views:** Lacker views current 1.5% inflation as acceptable and not alarming; Plosser sees "little evidence to suggest that sustained deflation is a serious risk."

**Minutes minority views:** Some commodity and import price increases noted, but these were not shifting the center of gravity.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 37 ±5 | Minutes: 32 ±5**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** All participants agree the output gap is substantial and persistent (anchor zone 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery); no one claims economy is near potential, but positive growth distinguishes this from the 2009 trough (15-22).

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy well below potential with substantial and persistent slack. Recovery continuing but at a disappointing pace.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Yellen: "there is about 3 percentage points of excess unemployment, considerably more than at this stage of the recoveries in 1975 and 1983... there will be considerable slack remaining for years."
- Bernanke: "Economic growth has slowed moderately further... At this pace of growth, progress in reducing the unemployment rate is likely to be quite slow."
- Evans: "the economy continues to grow, but the pace of expansion is inadequate to meaningfully reduce the rolls of the unemployed at anything but a glacial pace."
- Tarullo: "nothing that has happened in the intermeeting period has changed my view that the only open question is how much the economy will underperform for the foreseeable future."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Output and employment were increasing only slowly and at rates well below those recorded earlier in the year."
- "The sluggish pace of growth and continued high levels of slack left the economy exposed to potential negative shocks."
- "Resource slack was anticipated to still remain elevated at the end of 2012."
- "A few" noted recoveries are typically slow after financial crises.

**Transcript minority views:** Hoenig takes a relatively sanguine view ("we are really on a fairly good path"), and Lacker sees "modest recovery in place." Warsh raises supply-side (lower potential GDP) as a mitigant of measured gap.

**Minutes minority views:** "A few" noted recoveries are typically slow after financial crises, providing context but not disagreement on the slack assessment.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 30 ±5 | Minutes: 28 ±5**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unemployment near 9.6%, widespread sectoral job losses, worker abundance rather than scarcity — firmly in the 16-30 range. Scored at 30 rather than lower because private payrolls have been positive since January and recovery is underway.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Very loose labor market with high unemployment, slow job creation, and businesses reluctant to hire.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Rosengren: "Only 10 percent [of small businesses] said yes [to hard-to-fill jobs], which remains near the 25-year low for this survey question."
- Yellen: "with an abundance of qualified applicants and a lack of sales, firms may well see some option value in holding vacancies open longer as they search for the really exceptional candidate."
- Bernanke (summary): "The labor market remains weak, having started from a deep hole. Employment in many sectors has fallen significantly... Wage gains remain very limited."
- Evans: businesses "have no intention of doing any serious hiring. At best, they are recalling some laid-off workers."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "The current sluggish pace of employment growth was insufficient to reduce unemployment at a satisfactory pace."
- "Many participants saw evidence that the current unemployment rate was considerably above levels that could be explained by structural factors alone."
- "Several participants reported feedback from business contacts who were delaying hiring until the economic and regulatory outlook became more certain."

**Transcript minority views:** Kocherlakota raises structural factors that might imply a higher effective NAIRU (perhaps 8%). Plosser also emphasizes structural/supply factors.

**Minutes minority views:** Some participants attributed more of unemployment to structural factors, but even they did not argue the labor market was tight.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 36 ±6 | Minutes: 34 ±6**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The net picture across consumer spending (modest), business investment (mixed, with housing severely depressed), and overall demand is clearly below trend — "sluggish" is the dominant characterization.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Below trend. Consumer spending mixed with some positive retail data but household deleveraging. Business investment held back despite cash hoards. Housing deeply depressed.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Lacker: "Consumer spending has been rising pretty steadily at an annual rate of about a 2 percent. It is likely to continue to expand at that pace."
- Pianalto: "lack of demand as the single most important problem facing the respondents [small businesses]... not expecting more than a gradual pickup in demand."
- Duke: "residential construction falling at an annual rate of 60 percent in the second quarter... construction and land development loans continue to deteriorate."
- Bernanke (summary): "moderate increases in consumer spending and modest job creation... Housing has continued to be very weak."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Indicators of spending by businesses and households were mixed."
- "Many businesses had built up large reserves of cash... but were refraining from adding workers or expanding plants and equipment."
- "Households were continuing efforts to repair their balance sheets by saving more and paying down debt." Housing "continued to be very weak."
- Retail sales "a bit stronger than expected" and "continued strong foreign demand" provide modest upside offset.

**Transcript minority views:** Fisher reports a surprising pickup in retail activity in September; Lacker notes business investment in equipment/software is "expanding briskly."

**Minutes minority views:** Retail sales "a bit stronger than expected" and "continued strong foreign demand" provide modest upside offset.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 38 ±7 | Minutes: 53 ±7**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** For the corporate/financial sector credit is quite loose (low rates, narrow spreads, abundant liquidity); for households and housing it is tight. The overall picture leans slightly loose to neutral (below 50), consistent with post-QE1 conditions at the zero lower bound.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Conditions somewhat tight but improving. Large firms have access; small firms and CRE remain stressed. Not crisis-level but not fully normalized.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Fisher: "my interlocutors report their companies have even better access to credit than before... enjoy abundant liquidity." Hoenig: "We have more deposits than we can deploy. Liquidity is not an issue for us."
- Warsh: "Credit spreads continue to be narrow, and corporate issuance continues to be impressive."
- Duke: "loans for expansion or to cover cash flow shortfalls hard to get"; housing and CRE credit severely restricted; "weak loan demand."
- Dudley: "significant constraints on credit availability remain in place" for households.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Banks remained generally cautious and uncertain about the regulatory outlook."
- "Credit remained readily available for larger corporations with access to financial markets, and there were some signs that credit conditions had begun to improve for smaller firms."
- "Credit problems more broadly appeared to have mostly peaked, although banks continued to report elevated losses on commercial real estate loans."
- Improving consumer loan performance and easing for small firms suggest conditions moving toward neutral.

**Transcript minority views:** Hoenig raises concern that easy monetary conditions are fueling risk-taking in farmland and duration-extension.

**Minutes minority views:** Improving consumer loan performance and easing for small firms suggest conditions moving toward neutral.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 22 ±6 | Minutes: 22 ±7**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The Committee majority — including the Chair — is actively preparing QE2 and firmly in the 15-25 QE-era dovish range. The 22 score reflects that actual QE action was not taken today but is clearly imminent.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Strong dovish lean with active discussion of QE2 and other easing tools. Current action is a hold, but the majority is oriented toward additional accommodation. Decision to wait for more information, not a decision against easing.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Evans: "I wholeheartedly support your suggestion that we make progress on developing a framework to deliver more accommodative monetary policy... I believe the current situation should be analyzed as an economy that's stuck in a bona fide liquidity trap."
- Dudley: "I believe we should provide additional monetary policy accommodation. The economy is too weak, inflation is too low, and we are too far away from our full employment objective."
- Yellen: "The economic case for one of the A alternatives is compelling... this dire situation requires us to act."
- Bernanke: "my view, then, is that we need to consider seriously taking measures that will move inflation up from 1 percent or a little lower."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Many participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow to make satisfactory progress toward reducing the unemployment rate or if inflation continued to come in below levels consistent with the FOMC's dual mandate, it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."
- "Participants discussed several possible approaches to providing additional accommodation but focused primarily on further purchases of longer-term Treasury securities and on possible steps to affect inflation expectations."
- "However, others thought that additional accommodation would be warranted only if the outlook worsened and the odds of deflation increased materially."

**Transcript minority views:** Hoenig (wants to remove "extended period," opposes balance sheet expansion), Plosser (not in favor of LSAP or signaling it), Lacker (does not favor moving), Fisher (skeptical of transmission mechanism).

**Minutes minority views:** "Others" set a higher bar for additional easing; "some" members preferred accumulating more information first.

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 57 ±8 | Minutes: 40 ±8**

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** There is clear ideological division — hawks (Hoenig, Plosser, Lacker, Fisher) vs. mainstream doves (Yellen, Evans, Rosengren, Dudley) vs. centrists (Bullard, Kocherlakota, Lockhart, Pianalto, Tarullo, Duke, Warsh) — but the majority coalesces on Alternative B.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Meaningful disagreement about the threshold for and likely effectiveness of additional accommodation, but broad agreement on current action (hold) and that risks are to the downside.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Hoenig opposes Alternative B entirely and wants to remove "extended period" language; his dissent represents a fundamental disagreement about the direction of policy.
- Plosser: "I am not in favor of either starting a large-scale LSAP program of Treasury securities or of signaling with the statement that the Committee is preparing to do so in the imminent future."
- Lacker: "I don't favor moving now, and I don't think I'm going to favor expanding our balance sheet in November."
- Yellen argues Alternative A is "compelling" — wants stronger action than the majority supports today.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Many" favor additional accommodation vs. "others" who would only ease if deflation odds rose materially.
- "Some members saw merit in accumulating further information" vs. those ready to act.
- "Some noting that the economic benefits could be small in current circumstances."
- Participants "differed in their assessments" of structural unemployment extent.

**Transcript minority views:** The structural unemployment debate: Kocherlakota and Plosser emphasize structural factors; Yellen and Evans argue strongly for cyclical/demand interpretation.

**Minutes minority views:** The "others" camp represents a significant minority skeptical of QE benefits.
