# FOMC Meeting 2008-03-18: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

**Meeting context**: Bear Stearns collapse/JPMorgan rescue occurring in real time. 75bp rate cut delivered. Two formal dissents (Hoenig and Fisher from transcript; Plosser and Fisher noted in minutes scoring). Economy entering recession. Oil above $110. Dollar at post-Bretton Woods lows.

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 65 ±8 [High confidence] | 62 ±8 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Hoenig: "core PCE inflation has averaged about 2.1 percent, considerably above the numbers that this Committee has put forward in its long-term projections"; "rapidly rising commodity prices worldwide and a weaker dollar" as upside risks.
- Lacker: "overall CPI is still up over 4 percent year over year"; "substantial rise in the five-year, five-year-ahead inflation compensation"; "inflation expectations no longer qualify as well anchored."
- Plosser: "prices-paid and prices-received indexes over the past three months are at very high levels relative to the last twenty years"; "future-prices-paid index is at its highest level since the early 1980s."
- Yellen (moderating view): raised core PCE forecast "about ¼ percent in 2008, to 2¼ percent" but "prospects for core inflation this year have worsened a bit."

**Transcript center of gravity**: Inflation is clearly above target, driven heavily by commodity, energy, and food price pass-throughs, with broadening cost-plus pricing behavior and some upward drift in inflation expectations. The majority view is that it will moderate as slack builds and commodities level off, but it is not dismissed. Minority (Lacker, Plosser) argue credibility is at risk, pushing upper uncertainty band toward 73.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Recent readings on inflation had generally been elevated" and "energy prices had risen sharply"
- "Several participants stated that business contacts had emphasized that their input costs were rising and that they were seeking to pass on higher costs to their customers"
- "Most participants still expected inflation to moderate later this year and in 2009"
- "Some participants, however, expressed the view that emerging economic slack would limit the extent to which firms could pass on their higher costs"

**Minutes center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as clearly elevated with cost pass-through pressures, though most expect moderation ahead. The current assessment is above target with upside risks.
