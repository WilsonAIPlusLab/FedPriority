### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 33 ±6 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Nearly all participants see inflation clearly below the 2% target, running around 1% for 2020, with the outlook for return to target requiring years of sustained accommodation; the center of gravity is in the 30–35 range, modestly above pure deflation concerns but well below target.

Representative quotes:
- Daly: "inflation is likely to remain soft as these sectors are held back by containment measures for COVID...Returning inflation to 2 percent, on average, will take sustained policy accommodation to moderately overshoot the target, and I don't see that occurring in the forecast horizon."
- Brainard: "the staff estimates that total PCE inflation has been 1.1 percent this year"; "prices on services have remained subdued."
- Quarles: "Price inflation will end the year near 1 percent, and even the staff's more elevated forecast alternatives see it remaining below 2 percent for the foreseeable future."
- Powell: "Inflation remains below our 2 percent objective...the risks to inflation seem to me to remain weighted to the downside."

Minority views: George notes large price declines in COVID sectors are "masking the inflationary pressures being felt by many households" and that food prices have risen. Bullard and George both flag potential for rapid price recovery post-vaccine.

---

**Minutes — Score: 28 ±6 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly viewed inflation as soft and below target, held down by pandemic-related slack and depressed services prices, though they expected downward pressures to start abating next year with vaccine deployment.

Representative quotes:
- "Increases in consumer prices had been soft of late, as prices of products in those categories most affected by social distancing -- such as hotel accommodations and air travel -- continued to be depressed and increases in rents remained low."
- "These patterns were expected to continue in the near term as pandemic concerns intensified over the winter."
- "A couple of participants suggested that, as a result of ongoing technology-enabled disruption to business models and practices or lasting pandemic-induced restraint on firms' pricing power, downward pressure on inflation could persist."

Minority views: Seven participants (up from two in September) expected inflation above 2% by 2023, suggesting some see medium-term upside. A few noted upside risk from supply constraints coupled with strong recovery.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 31 ±6 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is clearly below potential — GDP down ~2% for 2020, employment ~10 million below February — placing it in the 28–35 range; Bullard's optimism and the Q4 GDP tracking (~5–11%) prevent a lower score, but the dominant view is that a significant gap remains and will persist for years.

Representative quotes:
- Powell: "the economy remains a long, long way from our goals...payroll employment is still almost 10 million below its February level."
- Brainard: "the economy is still in a deep hole...payroll employment is currently nearly 10 million jobs lower than in February."
- Clarida: "decline in the level of GDP this year will come in at roughly minus 2 percent" and GDP "will have recouped entirely the catastrophic collapse" by fall 2021.
- Bullard (more optimistic): "if this growth rate should turn out to be correct, then real GDP will have recovered essentially all the way to the average level for 2019."

Minority views: Bullard and Quarles are more optimistic; Bullard sees near-recovery to 2019 GDP levels possibly in Q4, which could put OUTPUT_GAP closer to 40.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy remained well below potential despite a stronger-than-expected recovery from the Q2 2020 trough. The near-term outlook was for further slowing due to the pandemic surge, while the medium-term outlook improved with vaccine news.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity and employment had continued to recover but remained well below their levels at the beginning of the year."
- "The economy... was still far from having attained conditions consistent with the Committee's dual mandate."
- "The economic recovery thus far had been stronger than anticipated... but viewed the more recent indicators as signaling that the pace of recovery had slowed."

Minority views: Limited divergence on this assessment; broad agreement the economy was far below potential.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 27 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is substantially below full employment — official unemployment at 6.7%, broader measure approaching 10%, bottom quartile above 20% unemployment — placing the score clearly in the 25–30 range consistent with the 2011–14 recovery era anchors.

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "Taking into account the additional 5½ million workers who have left the labor force...boosts unemployment from the current headline 6.7 percent U-3 measure to 9.5 percent...unemployment is likely still above 20 percent for workers in the bottom wage quartile."
- Daly: "a 1 percentage point decline each year" in unemployment going forward; "permanent job losses have become the norm."
- Mester: "Employment remains more than 6 percent, or almost 10 million jobs, below its level in February."
- Powell: "payroll employment is still almost 10 million below its February level. The pain is not spread evenly."

Minority views: Bullard notes "labor market recovery is about four years ahead of where it was after the Global Financial Crisis" and Williams notes the high share of temporary layoffs suggests faster recovery.

---

**Minutes — Score: 25 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market had significant remaining slack, with roughly half of pandemic job losses still unrecovered, participation depressed, and disproportionate harm to low-wage and minority workers. Several noted permanent layoffs were rising.

Representative quotes:
- "Labor market conditions generally had continued to improve, but they were still a long way from those consistent with the Committee's maximum employment goal."
- "A little more than half of the 22 million jobs lost over March and April having been regained."
- "Several participants assessed that improvements in the labor market were lagging that of economic activity."

Minority views: Some were more concerned about lasting scarring than others, but all agreed substantial slack remained.

Center-of-gravity: Spending is bifurcated — goods/housing/online retail above pre-pandemic levels, while contact-intensive services remain severely depressed; the net position is below trend but improving, with near-term deceleration risk heading into winter, placing the score in the 35–42 range.

Representative quotes:

- George: "High-frequency measures of activity have begun to turn down as virus cases rise...Spending is likely to face ongoing constraints in the current public health environment."
- Barkin: "I presume this is elevated savings funding gifts...I perceive less caution by most consumers."

Minority views: Bullard and Quarles are more optimistic about consumer spending resilience (elevated savings as pent-up demand, online retail booming).

---

**Minutes — Score: 40 ±7 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: A sharply bifurcated picture: goods consumption, durables, housing, and business equipment were strong, but services spending was depressed by the pandemic surge. Small businesses were struggling. The net assessment is below trend but not deeply contracting.

Representative quotes:
- "Household spending on goods, especially durables, had been strong... residential investment and home sales remained robust."
- "Business equipment investment had picked up further, with strong readings registered on new orders and shipments."

Minority views: Various participants emphasized the vulnerability of low-income households and small businesses, while others noted strong goods and housing sectors.

Minority views: Rosengren and Brainard highlight tight conditions for small/mid-size businesses and stress in CMBS/credit markets for hard-hit sectors. Kaplan notes "speculative conditions" / SPACs / excessive risk-taking.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 17 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The Committee is deeply dovish — unanimous support for full QE continuation + ELB + new threshold-based forward guidance; the only policy debate is whether to do even more (Rosengren, Evans, Brainard) versus maintaining current pace (majority); no one advocates tightening, placing the score firmly in the 15–20 range consistent with sustained QE-era settings.

Representative quotes:
- Nearly all speakers support Alt-B: maintaining $120B/month in asset purchases + 0–25bp funds rate. "Monetary policy will need to remain highly accommodative over the forecast horizon. Patience will prove to be a virtue." (Mester)
- Rosengren prefers Alt-A (extend weighted average maturity — even MORE accommodative): "I would prefer the language in alternative A...focusing our purchases on longer maturities."
- Powell: "We remain a long way from our goals, and it will very likely take several years to closely approach them. Our focus should be on sustaining highly accommodative financial conditions."
- Kashkari: "I would support doing more, and I would welcome any analysis by the staff of such options."

Minority views: Kaplan, George, Quarles, and Harker express mild concerns about side effects of extended QE or hope for tapering by end of 2021, but all support Alt-B.

**Minutes — Score: 15 ±4 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous strong dovish consensus: all participants supported maintaining maximum accommodation and enhancing forward guidance on QE. Some even discussed the possibility of increasing purchases. The discussion was entirely about how to calibrate ongoing accommodation, not whether to ease.

Representative quotes:
- "All participants judged that maintaining an accommodative stance of monetary policy was essential to foster economic recovery and to achieve an average inflation rate of 2 percent over time."
- "All participants supported enhancing the Committee's guidance on asset purchases... adopting qualitative, outcome-based guidance indicating that increases in asset holdings would continue."
- "Some participants noted that the Committee could consider future adjustments to its asset purchases -- such as increasing the pace of securities purchases."

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 10 ±4 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward guidance is explicitly outcome-based and tied to both legs of the dual mandate; the combination of QE threshold guidance + funds rate ELB guidance through at least 2023 + calls for patience and overshoot represents a strong dovish forward signal, placing the score in the 8–12 range consistent with other QE-era forward guidance commitments (Evans Rule etc.).

Representative quotes:

Minority views: Barkin and Quarles both hope tapering can begin in Q4 2021 under favorable scenarios. Quarles notes the forward guidance "may be setting us up to begin tapering as soon as the fourth quarter of 2021."

**Minutes — Score: 8 ±4 [High]**

Representative quotes:
