## 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**D-T score: 42 ±7 [Low confidence]**
**D-M score: 46 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
**S-T score: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**
**S-M score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Ferguson: "inflation is relatively low but it might be picking up, though we aren't really sure and so we have to give ourselves a little more time."
- Gramlich: accommodation conditioned on "inflation being low and resource use being slack."
- Broaddus: "I believe that we still have credibility for low inflation. This is not late 1993 or early 1994."
- Statement language (read by Secretary Bernard): "risks to the goal of price stability have moved into balance."

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "Many members indicated that the surprisingly large advances had substantially reduced the odds of further disinflation and also had increased their uncertainty about prospective price trends."
- "Still, most members saw low inflation as the most likely outcome."
- "The members agreed that, all things considered, the risks to the goal of price stability had moved into balance."
- Minority: "Some members saw underlying inflation as relatively stable and put low odds on the possibility that prices now were accelerating." Others "were less confident about the degree of restraint on prices."

### Staff — Transcript (S-T)
- Stockton: core PCE revised up 0.25–0.5 percentage point. "Breadth of the surprises... suggested to us that recent wage and price developments could not prudently be ascribed to special factors alone."
- Staff still projecting core PCE flat at 1.25% going forward; risks shifted — "no longer about deflation."
- NAIRU uncertainty raised as an alternative scenario.

### Staff — Minutes (S-M)
- "The staff anticipated a transitory rise in the pace of core inflation in the near term. However, it was expected that the remaining slack in resource utilization and strong productivity growth would keep core inflation at a low level over the forecast period."
- "core consumer price index advanced at a faster rate in the first quarter than it had in the fourth quarter, reflecting the pass-through of higher energy prices and a leveling off of goods prices after sizable declines last year."

**Observation**: D-T and D-M diverge by 4 points (42 vs. 46). S-T is notably lower (35) because staff emphasized the "transitory" qualifier and the continued low-inflation projection, while the discussion sources reflect member concern about the surprise breadth. S-M and D-T converge at 42. All four sources agree inflation is below 50 and that risks have shifted from deflation to balance; the gap is a matter of weighting the transitory-vs-persistent interpretation.

## 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**D-T score: 39 ±8 [Low confidence]**
**D-M score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**
**S-T score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**
**S-M score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Kohn: "the output gap is disappearing but is disappearing slowly."
- Ferguson: "economy that is still working hard to get rid of slack."
- Greenspan: mentions the downside risk of "more slack in this economy than we now anticipate" — treating it as a low-probability scenario, not the base.
- Gramlich: conditions for measured removal include "resource use being slack" — confirming slack remains.

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "Most likely at a pace that would be adequate to make appreciable headway in narrowing margins of unutilized resources."
- "With inflation low and resource use slack, the Committee saw a continuation of its existing policy stance as providing a degree of support."
- "The expansion had continued to broaden and had become more firmly established."

### Staff — Transcript (S-T)
- Stockton: GDP miss in Q1 on inventories but final sales strong; Q2 boosted to 5%.
- "Firing on all cylinders" — tax cuts, government spending, brisk final sales; gap narrowing but still present.
- Participation rate effects complicate gap measurement.

### Staff — Minutes (S-M)
- "The remaining slack in resource utilization and strong productivity growth would keep core inflation at a low level."
- "Manufacturing capacity utilization rose for the second consecutive quarter, but to a rate well below its long-run average."
- "the economy expanded at a rapid pace in the first quarter."

**Observation**: Near-consensus. D-T is 3 points lower (39 vs. 42), which is within uncertainty bands and plausibly reflects the transcript's heavier emphasis on "slowly disappearing" language (Kohn) versus the minutes' more positive framing of broadening expansion. S-T and S-M converge exactly at 42. All sources satisfy the bright-line rule: slack remains, score < 50.

## 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**D-T score: 37 ±8 [Low confidence]**
**D-M score: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**
**S-T score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**
**S-M score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Ferguson: economy "still working hard to get rid of slack" — encompasses labor market.
- Gramlich: statement conditioned on "resource use being slack" — labor slack included.
- Minehan: "will depend largely on the strength of the incoming data, even in the next week" — implies labor data are the key watchvariable, not yet conclusive.

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "Considerable slack seemed to remain in the labor market overall, and wage gains on the whole were moderate."
- "After a protracted period of meager gains in employment, conditions in the U.S. labor market evidently were improving in recent weeks."
- "A number of members cited reports of difficulties in hiring within certain job families in which specialized skills were in short supply."

### Staff — Transcript (S-T)
- Stockton: "Private payrolls rose 160,000 in March." "Long-awaited jobs recovery looked to be getting under way."
- "Should be viewed as encouraging but not definitive."
- Some employers reporting difficulty finding workers with specialized skills.

### Staff — Minutes (S-M)
- "The unemployment rate ticked up to 5.7 percent in March, and the labor force participation rate remained low."
- "Growth in payroll employment during March pushed the average monthly gain for the first quarter as a whole well above that of the fourth quarter."
- "The labor market showed renewed vigor during the first quarter."

**Observation**: Tight convergence across all four sources (35–37). All characterize labor as improving from a weak base but still with considerable slack. The specific unemployment anchor of 5.7% (S-M) is consistent with the 31–45 range used. D-T is slightly higher (37) than D-M and S-T (35) but well within uncertainty bands.

## 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**D-T score: 55 ±10 [Low confidence]**
**D-M score: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**
**S-T score: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**
**S-M score: 63 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Minehan: "I think we may need to move sooner than the markets now expect" — reflects strong incoming data.
- Guynn: "I wish we were in a position to move today" — implies current economic momentum justifies tightening.
- Greenspan: discusses scenario where "economy gets stronger" and faster tightening needed — treating robust demand as a plausible base case.

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "Consumer spending was rising at a brisk rate, housing activity remained at a high level, and business fixed investment was growing vigorously."
- "Committee members generally perceived overall business fixed investment as accelerating considerably, especially for equipment and software."
- "Consumer spending was rising at a solid pace... housing activity had stayed strong across the nation."

### Staff — Transcript (S-T)
- Stockton: consumer spending "rose 4 percent in the first quarter."
- Equipment and software "up about 12 percent."
- Housing "remained extremely strong" — near record starts. GDP projected 5% Q2.

### Staff — Minutes (S-M)
- "Consumer spending and the housing market continued to exhibit strength. Business fixed investment grew smartly."
- "Outlays for equipment and software expanded at a vigorous pace in the first quarter."
- "Retail sales rose briskly."

**Observation**: The largest divergence in this comparison. D-T is 7 points below the other three sources (55 vs. 62–63). This is flagged in the D-T scoring document as a low-confidence estimate — the transcript excerpt contains only the policy/language discussion, not the economic go-around, so the 55 is inferred from participants' hawkish urgency rather than direct economic characterizations. D-M, S-T, and S-M converge tightly (62–63) with explicit spending/investment data.

## 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**D-T score: 28 ±8 [Low confidence]** (labeled FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS — higher = tighter)
**D-M score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]** (same convention)
**S-T score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]** (labeled FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — higher = tighter)
**S-M score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]** (same convention)

*Note: All four sources use the same polarity convention (higher = tighter / less accommodative).*

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Rate at 1% — historically accommodative.
- Kohn: concerned about markets "build[ing] in a 1994-type tightening" — worry that markets will tighten conditions too fast from a loose baseline.
- Ferguson: market expectations are for "a modest tightening probably starting sometime in August."
- Greenspan: "if the market is likely to respond to this statement by tightening... it's going to tighten more if we don't use 'measured'" — confirms starting point is loose.

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "Accommodative financial conditions" cited as supporting household spending.
- "The appreciable rise in real long-term interest rates over the intermeeting period implied that financial market conditions had already tightened on balance."
- Mortgage rate backup noted; some risk that further rise in longer-term rates would restrain activity.

### Staff — Transcript (S-T)
- Reinhart: "financial conditions tightened appreciably over the intermeeting period." Treasury yields up 75–90 basis points.
- Some narrowing of corporate risk spreads. Dollar appreciated slightly. Equity prices showed small net change.
- Still accommodative overall (funds rate at 1%) but less so than before.

### Staff — Minutes (S-M)
- "Financial market conditions had already tightened on balance" over the intermeeting period.
- "Accommodative financial conditions" supporting household spending.
- Treasury yields "climbed significantly"; federal funds futures suggest tightening sooner.

**Observation**: Staff sources score higher (35–40) than discussion sources (28–30). Staff accounts give more weight to the intermeeting tightening in long-term rates ("climbed significantly," "tightened appreciably"), while discussion sources anchor more heavily on the 1% policy rate as the baseline. This is a genuine framing difference: discussion participants emphasize how loose conditions are; staff presenters report the change in conditions over the intermeeting period. All four agree conditions remain below 50 (accommodative).

## 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (Discussion sources only)

**D-T score: 54 ±6 [High confidence]**
**D-M score: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Rate held at 1% — no action constrains score from going far above 50.
- "Patience" dropped; "measured pace" language added.
- Gramlich: "positioning ourselves for a move of 25 basis points in June is fine."
- Kohn: "our best guess is that the firming, when it comes, will be gradual" — confirms firming is coming.
- Guynn: "I wish we were in a position to move today."
- 12-0 unanimous vote.

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "All of the members favored maintenance of the existing target of 1 percent for the federal funds rate."
- "The time at which policy firming appropriately would commence might be closer than previously had seemed most probable."
- Committee drops "patient" language; adopts "measured" pace for future tightening.
- "A number of policymakers were concerned that such an assertion could unduly constrain future adjustments."

**Observation**: Near-identical scores (54 vs. 55), well within uncertainty bands. Both sources record the same fundamental shift: unanimous hold but unambiguous tightening signal. The minor difference (1 point) is within noise.

## 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY (Discussion sources only)

**D-T score: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**
**D-M score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

### Discussion — Transcript (D-T)
- Guynn: "I'm among those who have a strong preference not to introduce another word such as 'measured.'"
- Moskow: "I'd prefer a different path. I would prefer to drop the word 'patience' and not substitute the word 'measured.'"
- Hoenig: "I think we would be better served not to have this last sentence... in the statement."
- Bies: "I, too, am still in the camp that would prefer not to have a commitment in the statement."
- McTeer: "I also would prefer just to drop 'patience' without substituting anything else."

### Discussion — Minutes (D-M)
- "All of the members favored maintenance of the existing target of 1 percent."
- "All the members agreed that they could accept" the measured language.
- "A number of policymakers were concerned... Others, however, saw substantial benefits."
- No formal dissenting votes recorded.

**Observation**: The largest divergence in the comparison: 40 (D-T) vs. 22 (D-M), an 18-point gap. The transcript captures the full go-around debate — five named members explicitly stating language preferences contrary to the majority recommendation. The minutes record the outcome ("all agreed") and summarizes the debate as a "number of policymakers were concerned," which substantially compresses the apparent intensity of disagreement. This is a known transcript-vs.-minutes structural difference: transcripts preserve the individual argumentation; minutes record the final consensus.
