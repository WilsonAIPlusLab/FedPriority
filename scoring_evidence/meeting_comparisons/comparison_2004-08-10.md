## DISCUSSION TRACK — Side-by-Side

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 52 ±7 [High] | Minutes: 53 ±8 [High]**

| Transcript Evidence | Minutes Evidence |
|---|---|
| Moskow: "data suggest that core inflation remains well contained" — yet notes cost pressures (steel, energy, construction materials), "taking longer to resolve." | "incoming data over the intermeeting period had shown a slowing in core inflation from the high levels posted earlier in the year, consistent with the Committee's view that a portion of the earlier increase had reflected transitory factors" |
| Pianalto: "I have not been hearing anything that would lead me to believe that substantial inflationary pressures are building across a wide range of final goods prices" — but warns about contract expirations and potential pass-through. | "a number of firms had been able to pass on at least some of their higher energy and other costs to customers, but few signs of more widespread price increases were apparent" |
| Guynn: "I am less willing than some to conclude that most of the price pressures we have seen...are going to turn out to be transitory" — cites trucking price increases of 5–6%, UPS fuel surcharges. | "longer-term inflation expectations remained well contained and slack in resource markets was seen as persisting, leading the Committee to expect underlying inflation to be relatively low" |

**Score gap: 1 point. Both sources converge on modestly above-neutral inflation with cost pressures present but core contained.**

### Key Cross-Track Observations

**INFLATION_PRESSURE — Discussion vs. Staff gap: ~12 points.**
Discussion participants scored 52–53 (slightly above neutral); staff scored 40–42 (slightly below neutral). Staff characterized core inflation as "moderated substantially" with a projection to "remain low"; participants acknowledged moderation but retained more residual concern about cost pass-through and uncertain transitory vs. persistent split.

**OUTPUT_GAP — Convergence.**
All four sources score 38–43, all below 50. Agreement that slack persists. The tightest cross-track convergence of any dimension.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS — Convergence.**
All four sources score 35–40. Agreement on below-full-employment conditions.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT — Staff transcript diverges.**
Staff transcript (52) vs. the other three sources (42–45). The transcript captured Wilcox's emphasis on export and manufacturing strength that offset consumer weakness; the other three sources weight the consumer spending shortfall more heavily.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — Notable structural difference.**
Discussion track (both sources): 25. Staff minutes: 30. Staff transcript: 42. The staff transcript adopted a more measured framing incorporating the rate hike cycle already underway and rising long rates; the discussion track and staff minutes use the "accommodative" characterization from the Greenbook forecast paragraphs. The rubric polarity is the same (higher = tighter) but the starting frame differs.

**PRODUCTIVITY — Discussion transcript highest (72).**
Discussion participants (especially Bernanke and Greenspan) devoted more explicit attention to productivity as a structural explanatory factor than is captured in the staff presentations or the minutes. Both staff sources converge around 62–65.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS — Discussion track divergence.**
Transcript: 25 vs. Minutes: 12. Individual participant remarks on equity declines, external financing tail risks (Geithner), and consumer credit extension (Olson) were not synthesized into the minutes, producing the largest within-discussion-track gap.
