### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The strong majority of participants see inflation as currently at or slightly below 2% and trending lower, with the risk of persistent disinflation; the minority (Lacker, Plosser, George) view the dip as temporary and not yet cause for alarm.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "the drop in oil and other commodity prices and the absence of wage pressures have shifted the outlook for inflation lower. Thus, we will be falling short on both employment and inflation for years and missing from the same direction each part of our dual mandate."
- Yellen: "Absent further policy action, inflation seems likely to come in somewhat below our 2 percent objective."
- Stein: "in essential agreement with the staff's forecast, which... sees inflation as subdued, likely coming below our 2 percent target."
- Pianalto: "there is a risk that inflation could fall below our inflation objective over the medium term... 10-year inflation expectations have been declining."
- Lacker (minority): "the dip in inflation will be temporary... measures of inflation expectations have been well behaved."
- Plosser (minority): "if inflation were to take a serious plunge downward, we also may have to react to defend our inflation target. But none of those events have materialized yet."

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The strong majority sees inflation at or below target with subdued cost pressures; recent commodity price declines have further reduced near-term inflation. This places the reading meaningfully below the 50 midpoint.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation had slowed somewhat, mainly reflecting the decline in the prices of crude oil and gasoline in recent months, and longer-term inflation expectations remained stable."
- "Several participants noted that they saw little if any evidence of price pressures, commenting that increases in labor costs continued to be subdued and that non-energy commodity prices had declined of late."
- "Almost all participants continued to anticipate that inflation over the medium-term would run at or below the 2 percent rate."
- Minority view: "A few saw inflation risks as tilted to the upside; they generally were skeptical of models that rely on economic slack to forecast inflation and were concerned that maintaining the current highly accommodative stance of monetary policy over the medium run risked eroding the stability of inflation expectations."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The consensus among participants is that the economy remains meaningfully below potential — a persisting output gap in the 2012 recovery phase — though a minority (Bullard, Plosser) question whether the gap is as large as estimated.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke (summary): "unemployment is above everybody's estimate of the long-run natural rate and is coming down very slowly at the same time that inflation is less than our target."
- Williams: "progress toward closing the large unemployment gap over the next year appears to have stalled."
- Powell: "I am somebody who does see a substantial output gap, although I'm mindful of the history and of the dangers of relying on contemporaneous estimates of it."
- Yellen: "economic growth is likely to be insufficient to achieve any meaningful progress in lowering unemployment."
- Tarullo: "subpar, perhaps even subtrend, economic growth."
- Bullard (minority/skeptical): extensive argument that "slack is a very poor basis for making monetary policy" due to measurement uncertainty; argues potential output may be lower than estimated.

**Minutes — Score: 36 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy expanding moderately but well below potential, with multiple headwinds and downward revisions to growth projections. Consistent with a persistent negative output gap during the slow recovery phase.

Representative quotes:
- "The economy had continued to expand moderately, though many noted that a variety of indicators showed smaller gains than had been anticipated."
- "Most participants saw the incoming information as indicating somewhat slower growth in total demand, output, and employment over coming quarters than they had projected in April."
- "A number of factors continued to be seen as likely to limit the economic expansion to a moderate pace in the near term."
- Minority view: "Some participants judged that the recent weakness in a variety of economic indicators was more likely to prove transitory, and thought that the outlook beyond this year was essentially unchanged."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is clearly below full employment; unemployment is elevated, progress is slow, and absence of wage pressure is cited; consistent with the 2011–14 anchor range of 35–40.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke: "agonizing" pace of labor market improvement.
- Williams: "the drop in oil and other commodity prices and the absence of wage pressures have shifted the outlook for inflation lower" — absence of wage pressure signals labor slack.
- Kocherlakota: "all other FOMC participants project that under appropriate monetary policy, headline inflation will be 2 percent or less in 2014, even though unemployment will be above 6.3 percent."
- Lacker: "labor market clearly has been disappointing."
- Note: No participant claims the labor market is tight or near full employment; only Bullard cautions against over-reliance on labor slack measures for policy.

**Minutes — Score: 34 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market has significant slack with elevated unemployment and slowing job growth. The structural-vs-cyclical debate creates uncertainty about the degree of slack but not about the direction — all agree the labor market is loose.

Representative quotes:
- "Growth in employment, in particular, appeared to have slowed in recent months, and the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "The pace of improvement in labor market conditions diminished in recent months; in particular, growth in employment slowed."
- Debate: "Some participants judged that the unemployment rate was being substantially boosted by structural factors" vs. others who "said that, in their view, slack remained high and weak aggregate demand was the major reason."
- Minority view: The structural unemployment camp implies less slack than the headline number suggests.

Center-of-gravity: Domestic demand is assessed as below trend — modest improvement from recent lows but clearly sluggish; investment and consumer spending are not robust; consistent with the 2011–13 "moderate" anchor zone.

Representative quotes:

- Rosengren: "the housing market is showing some tentative signs of improvement" — below-trend recovery language.
- Evans: "the 1990s American consumer doesn't live here anymore" — domestic demand not carrying the recovery.
- Tarullo: "subpar, perhaps even subtrend, economic growth."
- Fisher: "trillions of dollars are sitting on the sidelines. They're not being used for additional lending. They're not being used for job creation."

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending continuing but decelerating; business investment increasingly cautious and defensive rather than expansionary. Housing depressed. Overall reading is below-trend but not contracting.

Representative quotes:
- "Real personal consumption expenditures had continued to expand despite weak growth in real disposable income, but that the pace of expansion appeared to have slowed since earlier this year."
- "Firms had become more cautious in their hiring and investment decisions, with most capital investment being undertaken to improve productivity and reduce costs rather than to expand capacity."
- "Many participants, however, said that business contacts had reported that consumer spending was holding up."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 55 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened modestly relative to recent months due to European stress — spreads widened, equities lower, dollar stronger — but the U.S. financial system is not in crisis; the tightening is moderate and European-driven.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke: "European stresses have lowered our Treasury yields, but they have at the same time lowered stock prices, increased volatility, increased spreads, and strengthened the dollar. If you put all of that together, you get an overall financial impulse that is a negative rather than a positive for economic growth."
- Williams / Dudley (in statement language discussion): "tighter financial conditions" accepted as accurate description of current conditions.
- Stein: "term premiums... near at least recent-history lows and are something like 150 basis points below their norms over the last 15 or so years" — reflects safe-haven distortion, not ease in broad financial conditions.
- Dudley: MEP could "result in tighter financial conditions if it were viewed as ruling out a further large-scale asset purchase program."
- Bullard (minority): "longer-term real interest rates in the U.S. are substantially lower than they were a year ago."

**Minutes — Score: 56 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions tightened somewhat over the intermeeting period due to European stresses and risk-off sentiment, moving from accommodative to somewhat less supportive. Domestic banking fundamentals remain sound, preventing a higher score.

Representative quotes:
- "Many FOMC participants judged that overall financial conditions had become somewhat less supportive of growth in demand for goods and services."
- "Higher risk spreads and lower prices for riskier assets including equities and to broad-based appreciation of the U.S. dollar on foreign exchange markets."
- Offset: "Bank capital levels were quite high... banks had ample liquidity... consumer and business loans were increasing." Also: "credit standards remained tight."
- "A few participants observed that the marked drop in yields on longer-term U.S. Treasury securities could provide some impetus to growth."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 22 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: A large majority supports MEP extension (accommodative), with several wanting more aggressive action; the center of gravity is firmly in "strong dovish / QE era" territory with perhaps 4 of ~18 participants leaning against any new accommodation.

Representative quotes (dovish majority):
- Rosengren: "I think there is a strong case to be made for alternative A... I strongly suspect we will need to do more."
- Evans: "I support any form of additional accommodation. Alternative B does provide additional accommodation, and I could support that, but I would prefer something more like alternative A or even more than that."
- Yellen: "An extension of the MEP is a very modest step and will not be sufficient to promote our objectives... I believe one can make a strong case for action now."
- Dudley: "All of that implies to me... that there is a good case for moving more forcefully now. Nevertheless, I support alternative B."

Representative quotes (hawkish minority):
- Plosser: "I see no compelling reason to institute another round of maturity extension programs."
- Lacker: "I do not believe that the current situation calls for any further monetary policy action."
- Bullard: "I counsel standing pat for today."
- George: "additional policy ease is likely to have little effect on output and employment."

**Minutes — Score: 23 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Firmly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation at the zero bound with active asset purchases, and some participants actively advocating for exploring additional easing tools. Solidly in the QE-era dovish range.

Representative quotes:
- Members agreed to maintain zero rates and continue the maturity extension program (Operation Twist).
- "Several participants commented that it would be desirable to explore the possibility of developing new tools to promote more-accommodative financial conditions and thereby support a stronger economic recovery."
- "All members but one agreed that it would be appropriate to continue through the end of this year the Committee's program to extend the average maturity."
- Minority: One member dissented and "anticipated little if any effect on economic growth." A few expressed concern about upside inflation risks from accommodation.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: This is a meeting with a clear majority position but significant internal division — at minimum 3–4 explicit dissenters from the policy action, sharp interpersonal exchanges, and fundamental disagreements about the Committee's policy framework and communication.

Representative quotes:
- Three to four participants explicitly argue against any MEP extension (Plosser, Lacker, George; Bullard wants B without MEP).
- Fisher's inflammatory "road to perdition" comment and Dudley's sharp response create an interpersonal exchange.
- Kocherlakota raises a fundamental disagreement about the symmetry of the Committee's loss function — "it is necessary to get a discussion going on this."
- Tarullo: explicitly notes he ultimately "didn't vote for the consensus statement in January" and challenges the Committee's effective policy framework.
- Pianalto: "this is a close call for me today."
- Duke: "I support alternative B... but try as hard as I can, I still can't say that I'm proud of it."
- Disagreements extend to economic assessment, forward guidance language, communication structure, and inclusion of paragraph 5.

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent combined with meaningful analytical disagreements on the nature of unemployment and inflation risks, but broad agreement on the policy action itself. This is moderate disagreement.

Representative quotes:
- "All members but one agreed" on the maturity extension program — one formal dissent.
- Substantial intellectual debate on structural vs. cyclical unemployment with distinct camps.
- Inflation risk views ranged from "some" seeing downside risks to "a few" seeing upside risks.
- "The breadth of the structural-vs-cyclical debate and the range of inflation risk views indicate more analytical disagreement than the single dissent might suggest."

Representative quotes:

- Bernanke: "European stresses have... increased volatility, increased spreads, and strengthened the dollar."

**Minutes — Score: 50 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives moderate attention — European crisis spillover risks, reach-for-yield concerns, and Treasury market functioning are all discussed by multiple participants. It is a meaningful topic but does not dominate the meeting.

Representative quotes:

- "A few participants indicated that they were seeing signs that very low interest rates might be inducing some investors to take on imprudent risks in the search for higher nominal returns."
- "Some members noted the risk that continued purchases of longer-term Treasury securities could, at some point, lead to deterioration in the functioning of the Treasury securities market."

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 10 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward guidance signal is unambiguously dovish — "late 2014" liftoff is maintained, with multiple participants pushing for even more accommodative threshold-based or outcome-based guidance; fully consistent with the 3–8 "Evans Rule / QE3 threshold" anchor range.

Representative quotes:
- Existing forward guidance maintains "late 2014" for liftoff — strong easing commitment.

- Evans: "nominal-level income targeting would clearly have a positive effect... My suggestions about economic thresholds related to 7 percent unemployment and 3 percent inflation."
- Raskin: "I do see some limited policy space worth exploring regarding the forward guidance... not simply with the date... like President Evans, President Kocherlakota, and Governor Yellen, I support the consideration of moving the wording from paragraph 3 in alternative A."
- Yellen: "I'm attracted to the revised forward guidance language proposed in alternative A... it's a very constructive suggestion."
- Plosser (minority): his own liftoff expectation is "still well earlier than late 2014."

**Minutes — Score: 10 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is strongly accommodative — zero rates committed through late 2014, ongoing asset purchases, and active discussion of doing even more. Near the most dovish end of forward guidance.

Representative quotes:
- Rate guidance maintained through late 2014 at zero.
- "Several participants commented that it would be desirable to explore the possibility of developing new tools to promote more-accommodative financial conditions."
- Continuing the maturity extension program with discussion of potential additional easing.
- Minority: One member's skepticism about MEP effectiveness and a few members' upside inflation risk concerns suggest some forward-looking hawkishness at the margin.
