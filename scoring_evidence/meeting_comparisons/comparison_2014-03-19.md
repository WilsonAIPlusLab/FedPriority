## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 32 ±6, Moderate confidence | 35 ±5, High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Williams: "The 12-month overall and core PCE inflation rates have stubbornly remained at about 1 percent for almost a year."
- Evans: "We are still running far below our target... only getting to 1¾ percent by the end of 2016."
- Kocherlakota: "PCE inflation has averaged 1.3 percent [since Jan 2012]... Tealbook A doesn't see inflation returning to target until 2018."
- Yellen (summary): "inflation is now stabilized but at a very uncomfortably low level."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Near-unanimous view that PCE inflation is running around 1% — well below the 2% target — with significant concern it may not return. No participant argues current inflation is elevated.

**Transcript minority views:** Bullard forecasts inflation above 2% in 2015 (outlier); Dudley expects drift up from base effects.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective over the intermeeting period."
- "A couple of participants expressed concern that inflation might not return to 2 percent in the next few years."
- "Most participants expected inflation to return to 2 percent over the next few years, supported by stable inflation expectations."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Inflation is running below target; most expect a return to 2% but the timeline is uncertain and a couple worry about persistence. Consistent with the 2013-14 below-target period.

**Minutes minority views:** "A couple of other participants suggested that factors other than economic slack had played a notable role in holding down inflation" (medical services prices). A couple expressed concern about prolonged below-target inflation.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 38 ±6, High confidence | 39 ±5, High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Yellen (summary): "growth has been running only around 2 percent"; economy hasn't required above-2% growth to improve labor market, but outlook projects ~3%.
- Rosengren: "little evidence of a self-sustaining recovery that attains or exceeds 3 percent growth."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Economy is below potential, recovering but gap persists. "Slack remains" language is dominant. The recovery anchor for 2010-14 (35-42) is applicable; by March 2014 the recovery is maturing, placing this near 38.

**Transcript minority views:** Plosser and George note "at or a bit above trend" growth and optimism from business contacts — modest upward pull on the band.

---

**Minutes evidence:**

- "While there was general agreement that slack remains in the labor market."
- "Participants expected economic activity to pick up as the weather-related disruptions... dissipated."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Economy is below potential with slack remaining, but on a recovery path. Weather depressed near-term activity; underlying trend is moderate growth.

**Minutes minority views:** "A few participants highlighted factors other than weather that had likely contributed to the slowdown" (suggesting weakness may be more structural).

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 37 ±7, High confidence | 36 ±7, High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Rosengren: "I continue to estimate that there is significant slack in labor markets. Not only is the unemployment rate at 6.7 percent, well above my estimate of full employment, but other indicators of slack tell a similar story."
- Powell: "my prior is that the unemployment gap more likely understates slack than overstates it."
- Yellen (summary): "the standard unemployment rate measure suggests that there's still a lot of slack in the labor market. Broader measures, like U-6, may suggest yet more slack."
- Kocherlakota: "unemployment rate itself remains high... fraction of people who have been unemployed less than six months who transition into employment still remains quite low."

**Transcript center of gravity:** 6.7% unemployment, meaningful shadow workforce argument from a majority, wages at ~2% vs. 3.5% needed, broad consensus on significant remaining slack. Fits squarely in 31-45 (below full employment, slack remains).

**Transcript minority views:** Lacker and Bullard argue U-3 is a reliable indicator and demographic factors explain LFPR decline, suggesting less cyclical slack.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "While there was general agreement that slack remains in the labor market."
- "Several participants pointed to... considerably more labor market slack than indicated by the unemployment rate alone."
- "Several participants cited low nominal wage growth as pointing to the existence of continued labor market slack."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Clear consensus that labor market slack persists, with ongoing improvement but still well below full employment.

**Minutes minority views:** "A couple of other participants... saw reasons to believe that slack was more limited, viewing the decline in the participation rate as primarily reflecting demographic trends." Wider uncertainty band reflects this disagreement.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 25 ±7, High confidence | 31 ±5, Moderate confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Fisher: "total stock market capitalization as a ratio to nominal GDP rose to 1.25 at the end of last year. That exceeds the previous peak of 1.12 in 2007." Margin debt at record highs, junk spreads at 280 bps — "the lowest since the lows in 2007 just before the financial system began to unravel."
- Stein: CDX high-yield index "around 320 now... its average over the sample period was about 550. So we're about 230 basis points below average." Anecdote of market "meltup," covenant stripping, capitulation by holdout lenders.
- Dudley: "buoyant financial conditions."
- George: "fierce competition among lenders resulting in questionable terms and underwriting standards."

**Transcript center of gravity:** Financial conditions are clearly loose — near 2006/2007 pre-crisis levels by several metrics, though not yet at extreme historical lows. Compressed spreads, record equity valuations, and easy credit availability firmly place this in the loose zone (16-30).

**Transcript minority views:** None arguing for tight conditions.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Participants saw financial conditions as generally consistent with the Committee's policy intentions."
- "A couple of participants pointed to the decline in credit spreads to relatively low levels by historical standards."
- "One participant voiced concern about high levels of margin debt and of equity market valuations."

**Minutes center of gravity:** Financial conditions are accommodative/loose. Credit spreads low, equity valuations elevated, consumer credit growing — all indicators of easy financial conditions.

**Minutes minority views:** None in the tighter direction; concerns are about conditions being too loose.

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 44 ±6, High confidence | 44 ±5, High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**

- Kocherlakota (dissent, from dovish side): alternative B "weakens the credibility of the commitment to return inflation to the 2 percent target from below."
- Plosser (hawkish lean): "I suspect we might be needing to raise the federal funds rate sooner than the Tealbook does"; concerned about rates needing to "rise at a faster pace and perhaps sooner."
- Rosengren: liftoff appropriate toward end of 2015; consistent with significant ongoing accommodation.

**Transcript center of gravity:** The Committee is maintaining a clearly accommodative stance with measured taper. The one formal dissent is from the dovish side. Most participants emphasize "considerable time" before liftoff. This sits in the 41-50 zone — lean dovish to balanced, hold steady with mild accommodation bias.

**Transcript minority views:** Plosser, Lacker (modestly hawkish); Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren (more explicitly dovish within the vote).

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Members decided that it would be appropriate to make a further measured reduction in the pace of its asset purchases at this meeting" (taper from $65B to $55B/month).
- "Most participants also believed... it would be appropriate... to indicate that... economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the target federal funds rate below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run."
- "A few participants proposed adding new language... willingness to keep rates low if projected inflation remained persistently below 2 percent" (more dovish).

**Minutes center of gravity:** Lean dovish to balanced. Tapering continues on schedule (slightly less accommodative), but strong emphasis on continued accommodation and low-for-long rate path. Concern about below-target inflation reinforces dovish lean.

**Minutes minority views:** Some SEP projections shifted up (slightly hawkish).

**Transcript center of gravity:** Uncertainty is elevated above normal. ---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Uncertainty about the trend rate of productivity growth was making it difficult to ascertain the rate of real GDP growth."
- "Participants expressed a range of views regarding the amount of slack and how well the unemployment rate performs as a summary indicator."
- Weather effects obscured underlying economic trends. International developments (China, Ukraine) noted as bears watching.

**Minutes center of gravity:** Somewhat elevated uncertainty, particularly around measuring slack and interpreting weather-distorted data, plus international risks (China, Ukraine). Not unusually high but above baseline.
