### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Participants see core/domestic inflation as genuinely low (below 1% actual, below 2% target), with disinflationary momentum waning but not reversed; headline commodity price pressures acknowledged but core pass-through seen as limited.

Representative quotes:
- "there is essentially no domestically generated inflation. All the inflation we're getting is from abroad, that is, from commodity prices and from import prices." (Bernanke)
- "the downward trend in both core inflation and compensation, returning to a 2 percent core inflation rate in the medium term will certainly require years of much stronger data"; "Despite the increase in energy prices, many key areas have experienced price declines over the past year." (Rosengren)
- "while these developments suggest that disinflationary pressures may be drawing to a close…inflation clearly remains very low." (Pianalto)
- "I see the risks clearly to the upside" on inflation; prices-received index "highest value…since August 2008." (Plosser, minority)

---

**Minutes: 32 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Core inflation subdued and below mandate-consistent levels; commodity prices pose upside risks but have not passed through to core.

Representative quotes:
- "measures of underlying inflation had trended downward"
- "Many participants expected that, with significant slack in resource markets and longer-term inflation expectations stable, measures of core inflation would remain close to current levels in coming quarters"
- "a number of participants cited concerns that inflation was below its mandate-consistent level and was expected to remain so for some time"
- "Some participants expressed concern that...firms might increase them substantially once they found themselves with sufficient pricing power." (minority / upside risk)

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Economy is in recovery (not contracting) but clearly below potential; the output gap persists and is expected to close only gradually over several years.

Representative quotes:
- "we are starting from a very deep hole. The employment-to-population ratio…is at the business cycle low and is as low as it has been in many years." (Bernanke)
- "output well below potential through 2012." (Rosengren)
- "the pace of economic growth is still pretty tepid relative to the depth and duration of the recession that preceded it." (Dudley)
- "considerable slack remains…housing market…exerting downward pressure on economic growth." (Raskin)

---

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Economy firming but substantial slack remains; recovery gradual with output well below potential.

Representative quotes:
- "the pace of the recovery was insufficient to bring about a significant improvement in labor market conditions"
- "Most participants saw the large degree of resource slack in the economy as likely to remain a force restraining inflation"
- "unemployment was expected to remain above...levels consistent with the Committee's objectives for some time"
- Upside scenario noted — "a considerably stronger recovery could take hold" — but presented as a risk, not the baseline.

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Labor market has substantial slack; unemployment far above NAIRU by virtually all participants' estimates; labor market improving but from a deeply depressed level.

Representative quotes:
- "it is likely to be at least four years before we reach full employment"; 4,000 applications for 500 grocery store jobs. (Rosengren)
- "unemployment will decline only very gradually, remaining around 7 percent at the end of 2013"; "the quit rate is exceptionally depressed." (Yellen)
- "Whatever [NAIRU] is today, it's likely far below the actual unemployment rate." (Dudley)
- "there continues to be substantial slack in labor markets." (Evans)

---

**Minutes: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Labor market far below full employment; gradual improvement from deep slack but still very loose.

Representative quotes:
- "Conditions in labor markets continued to improve gradually" with unemployment at 9.4%
- "workers at all skill levels were readily obtainable" (some contacts)
- "participants continued to express disappointment in both the pace and the unevenness of the improvements in labor markets"
- Some contacts reported upgraded skill requirements creating mismatches, but this is a structural nuance, not tightness.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 43 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Consumer spending has picked up meaningfully and equipment/software investment is strong, but housing investment is a deep drag, small business credit is constrained, and the recovery is uneven; net position is below trend but improving.

Representative quotes:
- "consumption spending in the fourth quarter was about 4 percent growth…consumption fundamentals still remain somewhat weak. In particular, labor income has been quite anemic." (Bernanke)
- "data also indicate that business investment in equipment and software remains buoyant." (Lockhart)
- "outside of housing and nonresidential construction — sectors which remain mired in gloom — private spending and production gained some momentum." (Yellen)
- "housing market shows no signs of emerging from the significant overhang." (Raskin)

---

**Minutes: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Consumer spending showed notable pickup but sustainability questioned; business investment moderate; structures weak. Net: approaching but still below trend.

Representative quotes:
- "Spending by households picked up noticeably in the fourth quarter"; "households stepped up sharply their purchases of durable goods, particularly automobiles"
- "business outlays continued to grow at a moderate pace"
- "some participants noted that it was not clear whether the recent pace of consumer spending would be sustained"; "recovery in economic activity was likely to be restrained by a variety of economic factors"
- Optimists saw pent-up demand potentially sustaining spending; pessimists noted volatile categories drove the pickup.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 30 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative for most borrowers; explicit concern from multiple participants about excess liquidity. Credit remains restricted for small firms/households, but aggregate conditions are accommodative.

Representative quotes:
- "there is more than enough liquidity in the system. Businesses and financial intermediaries are flush with funds. Money is burning a hole in the pockets of many." (Fisher)
- "Financial conditions remain supportive." (Bernanke go-round summary)
- Dudley: list of QE2 financial indicator effects — rising equity markets, declining credit spreads, falling dollar — all consistent with monetary ease.
- "early signs of some frothiness" in leveraged loan market; farmland prices accelerating. (Tarullo, caveat)

---

**Minutes: 47 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
Center of gravity: Financial conditions roughly neutral with improving corporate market access but persistent tightness for small firms, housing, and CRE.

Representative quotes:
- "Conditions in financial markets improved somewhat further"; "Credit spreads on the debt of nonfinancial corporations continued to narrow"
- "credit conditions remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms"
- "tight credit conditions in a number of sectors" including housing and commercial real estate
- "Some participants noted that, taken together, these financial developments were consistent with a more accommodative stance of monetary policy."

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Clear majority favoring maintenance of full accommodation; QE2 completed as planned; zero rates with extended-period language maintained; several reluctant B-supporters with hawkish leanings, one formal dissent.

Representative quotes:
- "I support alternative B as written…Inflation remains well below my inflation target and output well below potential through 2012. With the very low inflation rate forecasted to continue for several years, we have ample room to be accommodative." (Rosengren)
- "I continue to hold the view that the U.S. economy requires aggressive monetary policy accommodation in order to be confident that we will escape our current liquidity trap conditions." (Evans)
- "I'm comfortable for now with our current stance…the bar for not completing the announced $600 billion of purchases should be high, and that hurdle has not been breached." (Yellen)
- "the case for continuing the program is eroding a bit…if we were to get very strong employment reports in the next couple of months, I think at the March meeting we're seriously going to want to consider scaling down our purchase program." (Lacker, hawk minority)

---

**Minutes: 22 ±6 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Firmly dovish — maintaining large-scale asset purchases and zero rates. No discussion of early tapering or rate increases.

Representative quotes:
- "members agreed that no changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted"
- Committee "reaffirmed its intention to purchase $600 billion of longer-term Treasury securities"
- "A few members remained unsure of the likely effects of the asset purchase program on the economy, but felt that making changes to the program at this time was not appropriate"
- A few skeptical of QE effectiveness but not pushing to change it.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: One formal dissent + four participants with hawkish reservations (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Kocherlakota) vs. a dovish majority; real disagreement on economic assessment and pace/signaling of exit; multiple contested statement language votes.

Representative quotes:
- Hoenig's formal dissent (referenced throughout): "I want to thank President Hoenig for pointing out the need to contemplate unintended consequences" (Fisher)
- "I would not have supported QE2…we will need to begin removing policy accommodation considerably sooner than anticipated"; straw vote on $80B splits 10 vs. handful. (Plosser)
- "I could argue for alternative C. I could even argue for alternative D." (Fisher)
- Extended language debate: paragraph 1 (B vs. C), $80B per month (10-5 straw vote), "extended period" language retention, numerical inflation target.

---

**Minutes: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
Center of gravity: Broad agreement on policy action with minor differences about the economic outlook and inflation dynamics. No formal dissents but some reservations.

Representative quotes:
- Unanimous vote on ratifying transactions; no formal dissents described
- "the importance of resource slack as a factor influencing inflation was debated"
- "A few members remained unsure of the likely effects of the asset purchase program"
- Debate about slack's role in inflation and QE effectiveness shows some intellectual disagreement, but it did not translate to policy dissent.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 48 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Representative quotes:

Representative quotes:

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 15 ±6 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: The Committee's forward signal is to complete QE2 as planned and maintain extended-period language; clear easing commitment with a very high bar for changing course; only a minority advocates preparing exit communications.

Representative quotes:
- "I think we should continue to remember…we are starting from a very deep hole…a bit of patience is probably a good idea." (Bernanke)
- "the bar for not completing the announced $600 billion of purchases should be high, and that hurdle has not been breached"; retains extended-period language. (Yellen)
- "I currently believe this will be important until actual data for broad-based, underlying, and sustainable inflation are coming in at at least 1½ percent year over year." (Evans)
- "My own prediction…is that we would have to start to think about tightening by the end of 2011." (Kocherlakota, hawkish minority signal)

---

**Minutes: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Strong forward easing bias. Ongoing large-scale asset purchases signal extended accommodation. Exit planning mentioned abstractly but no tightening signal.

Representative quotes:
- Continuing QE2 through end of Q2 2011; "reaffirmed its intention to purchase $600 billion"
- "the Committee should continue its planning for the eventual exit from the current exceptionally accommodative stance of policy" — but no timeline
- Federal funds rate maintained at 0 to 0.25%
- Exit planning language framed as risk mitigation, not a policy signal.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Uncertainty is elevated above normal — particularly around labor market structure, inflation pass-through, and recovery durability — but participants are not in deep-crisis mode; the deflation tail risk has diminished and the recovery is visible.

Representative quotes:
- "all of the careful work, with all of the other uncertainties that are layered on top of it, merely convinces me that we're going to have to look at a wide range of things besides the unemployment rate." (Kocherlakota)
- "we've seen swings in the data before that have proven to be only temporary. I prefer to think about the stronger incoming data as helpfully offsetting some of the downside risks." (Pianalto)
- "I just want to add two other thoughts. I think we are a little bit at risk of getting ahead of ourselves…there was a lot of excitement last winter about improvement in the economic outlook…that turned out to be premature." (Dudley)
- "Estimates are anywhere from 4¼ percentage points to 0.6 percentage point away from our mandate-consistent unemployment rate." (Lacker, wide range)

---

**Minutes: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**
Center of gravity: Uncertainty somewhat elevated but diminishing. Participants more confident than recent meetings but still see significant two-sided risks.

Representative quotes:
- "participants generally expressed greater confidence that the economic recovery would be sustained"
- "downside risks to their forecasts...had diminished"
- "they continued to see significant risks to both sides of the outlook" including Europe, housing, state/local fiscal
- "incoming data...would need to be solid for a while longer to justify a significant upward revision"
