### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimously, participants observe PCE inflation at ~1.0%, well below the 2% target, with several expressing concern about the downward trend since early 2012.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "Inflation falling further below our target and fiscal austerity that is worse than we expected provide ample reasons to strongly prefer alternative B to alternative C...the miss on inflation is getting quite large."
- Bullard: "the headline PCE inflation rate measured from one year earlier is approaching 1 percent, as is the associated core measure. These are particularly low readings."
- Evans: "year-over-year PCE inflation is 1.0 percent. Judged against our 2 percent long-run objective—how would we feel if inflation were 3 percent?"
- Bernanke summary: "inflation remains soft, with headline and core PCE inflation most recently near 1 percent."

Minority views: Plosser and Pianalto see current low readings as partly temporary and note stability in inflation expectations; Pianalto argues she is "not more concerned about disinflation."

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly see inflation below the 2% target with little upward pressure, though some attribute recent softness partly to temporary factors.

Representative quotes:
- "Both headline and core PCE inflation in the first quarter came in below the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent, but these recent lower readings appeared to be due, in part, to temporary factors."
- "A number of participants expressed concern that inflation was below the Committee's target and stressed that future price developments bore careful watching."
- "Most of the recent reports from business contacts revealed little upward pressure on prices or wages."

Minority views: One participant focused on upside inflation risks from accommodative policy; a couple suggested additional easing if inflation fell further.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants uniformly describe an economy below potential, with fiscal drag (sequestration), weak business investment, and moderate growth around 2%, though private-sector resilience (housing, durables) provides some offset.

Representative quotes:
- Dudley: "my expectation...is that the contractionary fiscal impulse will dominate over the near term...Economic growth will disappoint."
- Yellen: "the employment gap currently stands at about 4.2 million, or 1.7 percent of the population. By this dual mandate metric, we are falling very short of meeting our maximum employment mandate."
- Bernanke summary: "the outlook remains for moderate economic growth with slowly improving conditions. Somewhat more resilient private-sector demand, including housing, is being offset, to some degree, by fiscal restraint."
- Lacker: "we've seen several short-lived spurts in economic growth over the last four years, and at this point, it looks to me as if we're fluctuating around a slow growth trend."

Minority views: Plosser and Bullard are somewhat more optimistic (expecting ~3% growth for 2013); Powell strips out "one-timers" to argue underlying growth is stronger.

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is recovering but remains below potential, with significant slack persisting and moderate growth only somewhat exceeding potential output growth.

Representative quotes:
- "The economy was expanding at a moderate pace despite some softness in recent economic data."
- "Persisting slack in resource utilization."
- "A number of participants noted that the balance of risks to growth remained to the downside."

Minority views: Some emphasized underlying momentum and strengthening private demand; others stressed growing fiscal restraint. The range is narrow within the "below potential" band.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: With unemployment at 7.6% and broad measures of labor underutilization elevated, participants universally describe a labor market that is improving but still substantially below full employment, with "substantial improvement" threshold not yet met.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "the overall shortfall of employment from its maximum sustainable level, the employment gap, currently stands at about 4.2 million, or 1.7 percent of the population."
- Rosengren: "unemployment well above full employment...only modest improvement in the unemployment rate that is, to a large degree, reflecting not the strength of labor markets but the weakness in labor force participation."
- Kocherlakota: "unemployment will remain elevated for even longer than [two to three years]."
- Bernanke summary: "labor gap may be higher than the unemployment gap...job growth has been slow and somewhat volatile. Unemployment has come down more quickly than expected...lower unemployment, in part, reflects lower participation rates."

Minority views: Pianalto argues that given declining trend labor force participation, a 168,000/month pace "should perhaps be considered as substantial." Powell and Stein note significant improvement since September.

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving from weak levels but unemployment remains elevated and participation rate declines suggest even more hidden slack than headline unemployment indicates.

Representative quotes:
- "Conditions in the labor market showed some continued improvement, although the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "Several participants cautioned that the drop in the unemployment rate... was also accompanied by another reduction in the labor force participation rate; the decline in labor force participation... could indicate that the reduction in overall labor market slack had been substantially smaller than suggested by the change in the unemployment rate."

Minority views: A few noted businesses unable to find skilled workers, suggesting pockets of tightness, but this is clearly a minority observation.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and housing show resilience (housing notably strong), but business investment is leveling off and enterprise demand is weak; the net position is moderately below trend, with the housing bright spot partially offsetting cautious business spending.

Representative quotes:
- Lockhart: "businesses remain very cautious about expansion-oriented investment and adding to payrolls."
- Tarullo: "capital expenditures now seem to be leveling off as they return to something approaching their historical averages...a lot of large businesses are saying that they're essentially done with investment until they see enough additional demand."
- Fisher: "enterprise demand, as I like to call it—that is, non-consumer-driven demand—remains weak."
- Bernanke summary: "investment is leveling off, and much of it is directed at increasing productivity and reducing costs...Housing is a source of growth."

Minority views: Plosser notes Q1 consumer spending grew at 3.2%; Bernanke sees "private domestic final demand, that is, C plus I, has been growing pretty rapidly over the past six months."

---

**Minutes — Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is moderately supportive and housing is strengthening, but business investment caution and fiscal drag keep overall spending/investment below trend.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer spending was reported to be strong in a number of areas of the country... supported by rising equity and house prices, improved household balance sheets, and easier credit conditions."
- "Growth in business investment spending slowed somewhat... business contacts were reporting continued caution about expanding investment or payrolls."
- "The housing sector improved further" with multiple positive indicators cited.

Minority views: Concerns that consumer spending growth may not be sustainable without a pickup in business investment and hiring.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 28 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, with record-low mortgage rates, near-record C&I lending easing, cov-lite issuance approaching 2007 peaks, and triple-C market access at highs — participants describe an environment of easy credit and compressed spreads, with concern focused on the risk-taking implications rather than stress or tightness.

Representative quotes:
- Stein: "credit conditions are very accommodative. I think the 15-year mortgage just hit an all-time low. We continue to see record lending volumes in leverage lending...For C&I lending, the percentages reporting easing their terms or the spreads are really near record levels."
- Bernanke: "financial conditions are easier. That's shown by many indicators, including financial conditions indexes from the Chicago Fed and other Federal Reserve Banks."
- Evans (reporting contacts): "no one indicated that financial conditions were worse; by all accounts, our accommodative policies are continuing to facilitate business activity."
- Fisher: "quantitative easing is unambiguously inflating asset prices and, according to some, is distorting financial market functionality."

Minority views: Bernanke notes "mortgage supply conditions are still tight" via FHA/GSE tightening of standards; Lacker mentions consumers unable to access credit as freely as during the boom.

---

**Minutes — Score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose and easing further, with QE and global central bank actions contributing. Some concern that conditions may be excessively easy in pockets.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial conditions appeared to have eased further over the intermeeting period: Longer-term interest rates declined significantly, banks loosened their C&I lending terms and standards on balance, and competition to make commercial and auto loans was strong."
- "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant, pointing to the elevated issuance of bonds by lower-credit-quality firms or of bonds with fewer restrictions on collateral and payment terms."

Minority views: A few see conditions as potentially too buoyant, reinforcing the low (loose) score.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The majority supports maintaining the full $85B/month QE3 program with existing thresholds intact; a vocal minority (Duke, Powell, Plosser, George, Bullard) favors signaling a possible June taper, but even they support alternative B today; the overall lean is strongly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation in the QE era.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "We do not have dueling mandates. Each mandate implies the same policy prescription: accommodation."
- Evans: "we need to maintain our full pace of accommodation until the data strongly indicate otherwise. That is, at least maintain the current pace."
- Kocherlakota: "my outlook clearly calls for adding more accommodation...I'm talking about adding accommodation."
- Duke (the most hawkish voice among those supporting B today): "I'm not in favor of making any changes to policy at this meeting, but I would like the statement from this meeting to leave us in the best possible position to start tapering in June."
- Policy outcome: Alternative B (maintain $85B/month purchases unchanged) selected, with unanimous or near-unanimous vote.

Minority views: Fisher, Plosser, George each have significant reservations about the program's costs, and Plosser/George explicitly prefer beginning to taper.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The dominant lean is dovish -- maintaining full QE pace -- but a meaningful minority is moving toward tapering, creating a somewhat wider band than a pure QE-era meeting.

Representative quotes:
- "All but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted in order to foster a stronger economic recovery."
- "Most observed that the outlook for the labor market had shown progress since the program was started in September, but many of these participants indicated that continued progress, more confidence in the outlook, or diminished downside risks would be required before slowing the pace of purchases would become appropriate."
- "A number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting."

Minority views: One member dissented hawkishly; a number willing to taper soon; one wanted more accommodation. The emerging taper discussion makes this less purely dovish than earlier QE meetings.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: No formal dissents but clear bimodal distribution of views between doves (maintain/add) and hawks (begin signaling June taper), with Bernanke explicitly noting the unusual level of disagreement; roughly equivalent to a "two distinct camps" meeting without formal dissents.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke explicitly states: "I will say that I think there was a little more dissonance today than usual...there's a little bit of a bimodal view."
- Clear split: ~5 participants advocate adding/maintaining accommodation fully (Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Yellen, Dudley) vs. ~5 participants favor tapering by summer (Duke, Powell, Plosser, Bullard, George) vs. Fisher (skeptical of whole program).
- No formal dissents recorded on the vote for alternative B.
- Duke's proposed language amendment provokes significant discussion; Stein's communications framework critique generates back-and-forth with Kocherlakota and Bullard.

Minority views: The absence of formal voting dissents prevents this from scoring higher; Bullard's and Williams's nuanced positions occupy the middle ground.

---

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent with a range of views on taper timing, but broad agreement on the current highly accommodative stance. Moderate tactical disagreement without fundamental division.

Representative quotes:
- "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision, expressing concern that the continued high level of monetary accommodation increased the risks of future economic and financial imbalances."
- "A number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting... however, views differed about what evidence would be necessary."
- "One participant preferred to begin decreasing the rate of purchases immediately, while another participant preferred to add more monetary accommodation at the current meeting."

Minority views: The spread from "add more accommodation" to "decrease immediately" spans the full range but most cluster around maintaining the current pace.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a meaningful and recurring topic across multiple participants (Fisher, George, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Stein, Bernanke), discussed in terms of reach-for-yield, cov-lite issuance, and asset price inflation from QE — above the 2013 typical baseline but not a dominant topic rivaling the labor market and inflation discussions.

Representative quotes:
- Fisher: extended discussion of cov-lite issuance (34%, approaching 2007 peak of 38%), triple-C companies' primary market access (46%, near record 55%); "quantitative easing is unambiguously inflating asset prices and distorting financial market functionality."
- George: "evidence of financial imbalances has emerged...covenant-lite loan issuance in 2012 was just below the previous record set in 2007...zero interest rates for an extended period...pose real risk of painful future adjustments."
- Kocherlakota: distributes memo on financial stability and monetary policy intersections; argues explicitly that financial stability tail risks are currently insufficient to justify tightening.
- Plosser: "I'm becoming increasingly concerned that [ZIRP] is fostering buoyancy in asset markets and spurring some investors to take on more risk."
- Bernanke summary: "concerns were expressed about the search for yield and interest rate spikes. Cov-lite issuance and junk issuance are up."

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion from a few participants, focused on reach-for-yield concerns. It is not a dominant topic but more than a passing reference.

Representative quotes:
- "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant, pointing to the elevated issuance of bonds by lower-credit-quality firms or of bonds with fewer restrictions on collateral and payment terms (so-called covenant-lite bonds)."
- "One participant cautioned that the emergence of financial imbalances could prove difficult for regulators to identify and address, and that it would be appropriate to adjust monetary policy to help guard against risks to financial stability."

Minority views: Most participants did not raise financial stability as a concern; it was driven by a few voices.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is strongly easing-committed: QE3 thresholds remain intact, the dominant group sees "a ways down the road" before tapering, and the most prominent alternative discussed (Kocherlakota) would lower the threshold further — firmly in the strong easing commitment range with threshold-based guidance.

Representative quotes:
- Active QE3 at $85B/month maintained with no change; Evans Rule thresholds (6.5% unemployment, 2.5% inflation) remain operative.
- Kocherlakota proposes lowering unemployment threshold to 5.5% — an even more dovish forward signal.
- Bernanke confirms alternative B selected, maintaining "the labor market has improved substantially in a context of price stability" as the purchase end condition.
- Williams: "the time to completely halt our asset purchases is still a ways down the road."

Minority views: Duke, Powell, Plosser, George explicitly want to position for June taper; Duke's amendment would signal adjustable pace.

---

**Minutes — Score: 12 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The formal forward guidance remains strongly dovish via threshold-based commitments, but the nascent taper discussion introduces a slightly less accommodative forward lean than pure threshold guidance alone would imply.

Representative quotes:
- "The Committee also retained its forward guidance about the federal funds rate, including the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates." (Evans Rule: 6.5% unemployment / 2.5% inflation thresholds)
- "Most participants emphasized that it was important for the Committee to be prepared to adjust the pace of its purchases up or down as needed."
- Emerging taper discussion ("a number" willing to slow as early as June) introduces some upward pull on the forward signal.

Minority views: The taper-willing participants push the forward signal slightly higher than the rock-bottom thresholds; one participant wanting more accommodation pulls it lower.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript — Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty about the near-term economic trajectory (spring swoon vs. underlying momentum), the composition of labor market slack (cyclical vs. structural), and the interpretation of March data — above the "risks roughly balanced, standard caveats" baseline but not reaching severe uncertainty.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke: "uncertainty remains high: Is this a new normal?"
- Dudley: "uncertainty about the near-term outlook is quite high."
- Tarullo: "it's pretty murky down there...hard to come to a highly precise sense of how much is cyclical, how much is structural."
- Yellen: "estimation of the trend participation rate is fraught with uncertainty" — key structural question for assessing slack.
- Tarullo: "information over the next several months is likely to be inconclusive" given sequestration masking underlying trends.

Minority views: Plosser, Bullard, and George are somewhat more confident in their forecasts; Pianalto explicitly sees risks as "broadly balanced."

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, driven by mixed data, concern about seasonal slowdown patterns, fiscal drag effects, and difficulty interpreting labor market signals. Not extreme but above routine hedging.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic data releases over the intermeeting period were mixed, raising some concern that the recovery might be slowing after a solid start earlier this year, thereby repeating the pattern observed in recent years."
- "A few participants warned that, in light of ongoing fiscal restraint and a weak global outlook, economic data could remain soft for the next few months."
- Labor force participation trend "admittedly subject to considerable uncertainty."

Minority views: Some put more emphasis on underlying momentum; others stressed headwinds.
