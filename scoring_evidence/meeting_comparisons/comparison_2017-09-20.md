### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 34 ±6 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "core 12-month PCE continues to be low at 1.4 percent. This is a large shortfall in relation to our target of 2 percent. We've now experienced low inflation readings for the past six months." (Kashkari)
- "five straight years of undershooting our target... we can't dismiss the possibility that underlying trend inflation is running below our longer-run objective." (Brainard)
- "inflation at relatively low levels and inflation expectations too low to be consistent with our 2 percent inflation target." (Bullard)

Minority views (from transcript): Rosengren and George view recent softness as more likely temporary; George notes "it has remained within a range consistent with the longer-run goal."

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous view that inflation is meaningfully below the 2% target, with the shortfall now spanning multiple years, placing the score in the disinflation/below-target range.

---

**Minutes: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "PCE price inflation over the 12 months ending in August was estimated to be about 1-1/2 percent, remaining below the Committee's longer-run objective."
- "Many participants continued to believe that the cyclical pressures... were likely to show through to higher inflation over the medium term."
- "Several noted that... convergence to the Committee's symmetric 2 percent inflation objective might take somewhat longer than they anticipated earlier."

Minority views (from minutes): "A few" participants saw upside risks from tight labor markets (would push score higher); "some" discussed secular trends muting inflation further (would push lower).

Center-of-gravity: Inflation clearly running below target at 1.5% with genuine concern about whether the softness is persistent or transitory. The majority still expects a return to 2% but with diminished conviction.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "I expect the economy to overshoot potential further and by a significant degree, with the unemployment rate dipping a touch below 4 percent in the second half of next year." (Williams)
- "economy expanding at a moderate but above-trend pace." (Powell)
- "Tealbook projects real GDP growth to average 3 percent in the second half of 2017... the output gap is estimated to expand to more than 2 percent by end of 2018." (George)

Minority views (from transcript): Bullard argues the economy is likely growing close to trend, not above it, citing the 2% GDP regime since 2009.

Center-of-gravity: Most participants see growth running above potential, with real GDP ~2¼–2½% pace, consistent with a small positive output gap.

---

**Minutes: 53 ±4 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "an economy that was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential"
- "economic activity had been rising moderately so far this year"
- "the unemployment rate... remained below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level"

Minority views (from minutes): "Some" saw room for further labor utilization increases, suggesting slightly less above potential.

Center-of-gravity: Economy modestly above potential. Growth described as "moderate" — not overheating but clearly beyond the sustainable longer-run level.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "my assessment is that from the standpoint of cyclical conditions, we've achieved the maximum employment part of our mandate." (Mester); district contacts report labor tightness "accelerating" with firms paying significant wage increases to retain workers.
- "expect the economy to overshoot potential further... unemployment dipping below 4 percent." (Williams)
- "with labor increasingly tight, the share of firms reporting wage pressures rose above 50 percent in August." (George)

Minority views (from transcript): Kashkari and Powell note residual slack in prime-age employment-population ratio; wage growth subdued in aggregate data despite anecdotal reports.

Center-of-gravity: Broad consensus that the labor market is tight and operating below NAIRU (4.4% vs. NAIRU estimates of 4.7–4.8%), though still some debate about remaining slack.

---

**Minutes: 55 ±6 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "the unemployment rate... remained below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level"
- "Other indicators suggested that labor market conditions had continued to tighten... job openings rate, the quits rate... had returned to pre-recession levels"
- "increases in most aggregate measures of hourly wages and labor compensation remained subdued"

Minority views (from minutes): "Some" saw room for further utilization increases (pushing lower); "a couple" cautioned that wage acceleration may have already begun (pushing higher).

Center-of-gravity: Labor market tight — unemployment below NAIRU, multiple indicators at pre-recession levels. But the puzzle of subdued wages tempers the tightness signal.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "business spending on equipment and intangibles will increase rapidly this quarter and next at an 8 percent annual rate, up from the already solid pace of 6 percent." (Brainard)
- "consumer spending continues at a solid pace and confidence levels remain high. Business fixed investment has picked up this year." (George)
- "strengthening in business investment and a swing in the contribution of net exports from being a moderate drag to being a modestly positive factor." (Powell)

Minority views (from transcript): Williams notes "hard data on consumer spending and demand for credit" is not surging despite asset price gains. Bullard is more cautious on overall growth trajectory.

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending growing at a "moderate to solid" pace, business investment recovering clearly from 2016 lows. Net position is somewhat above trend.

**Minutes: 52 ±4 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

- "equipment investment had picked up during 2017 after having been weak during much of 2016"

Minority views (from minutes): Limited disagreement; auto sales softening noted but offset by investment pickup.

Center-of-gravity: Consumption at a moderate pace (at trend) with improving business investment providing a modest above-trend tilt. Net assessment: slightly above trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are explicitly and repeatedly described as loose/accommodative — lower long-term yields, higher equity/home prices, dollar depreciation, tight risk spreads.

**Minutes: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

Minority views (from minutes): Minimal disagreement on the characterization of conditions as accommodative; debate is about whether this is beneficial or risky.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "I would favor another tightening by the end of this year, presuming incoming data unfold as anticipated." (Rosengren, hawkish lean)
- "I've kept one additional interest rate hike for 2017 in my SEP submission... it's important to keep the possibility of a December rate hike alive." (Dudley)
- "My SEP submission has no additional rate hike this year... the current soft underlying inflation trend just seems too entrenched, and the uncertainties are too large and too one sided." (Evans, dovish)
- "I prefer alternative A... I continue to monitor inflation... looking for signs that the labor force participation story is running to its conclusion." (Kashkari, most dovish)

Center-of-gravity: Majority of participants support continued gradual normalization and are open to/expect December hike. Minority doves (Evans, Kashkari, Bullard on statement language, Brainard cautious). Lean is moderately hawkish but tempered by unanimous hold today and meaningful dovish bloc.

---

**Minutes: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

- "many participants thought that another increase in the target range later this year was likely to be warranted"
- "many participants expressed concern that the low inflation readings this year might reflect... developments that could prove more persistent"
- "A few of these participants thought that no further increases in the federal funds rate were called for in the near term"

Center-of-gravity: Lean hawkish — balance sheet normalization initiated, majority expects another hike soon. But inflation concerns create meaningful caution. Not strongly hawkish because of the inflation-related hesitation.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 68 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "I expect that if the economy continues on its current path, there will be one more increase in the federal funds rate this year." (Fischer)

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is a continuation of the gradual tightening cycle — December hike as base case for most, with 3 hikes expected in 2018 by many. Balance sheet normalization beginning in October adds a tightening dimension.

---

**Minutes: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

- "nearly all supported again indicating... that a gradual approach to increasing the federal funds rate will likely be warranted"
- "A few participants thought that additional increases in the federal funds rate should be deferred"
- Balance sheet normalization announced at this meeting.

Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is mildly hawkish. The dominant signal is continued gradual tightening with another hike likely this year, plus balance sheet normalization underway. Tempered by data-dependence and inflation uncertainty.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 48 ±8 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "there is not yet a clear or satisfactory explanation" for inflation weakness; "I see it as potentially a very close call" on December. (Powell)
- "I must confess, though, that I have not found one argument, or even a combination of potential explanations, to be fully satisfying." (Bostic)
- Yellen discusses multiple competing explanations for low inflation; "we should be prepared to adjust our estimates of the natural rate."

Center-of-gravity: Genuinely elevated uncertainty about inflation dynamics (is it transitory or structural?), near-term data distortions from hurricanes, and r* uncertainty. Multiple competing hypotheses entertained without resolution. However, multiple participants describe risk as "balanced" and there is a clear working hypothesis (transitory).

---

**Minutes: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced"
- "several noted that interpreting the next few inflation reports would likely be complicated by the temporary run-up in energy costs"
- "Several others noted that, in light of the uncertainty around their outlook for inflation, their decision on whether to take such a policy action would depend importantly on whether the economic data in coming months increased their confidence"

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty somewhat elevated, focused primarily on inflation dynamics (transitory vs persistent softness). The broader economic outlook is relatively clear. Hurricane effects add near-term noise but are judged as temporary.
