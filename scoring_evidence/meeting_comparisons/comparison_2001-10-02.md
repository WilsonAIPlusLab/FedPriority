# Side-by-Side Evidence Comparison — FOMC Meeting 2001-10-02

**Context:** First scheduled FOMC meeting after the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks (three weeks post-attack). The economy had been weakening since early 2001; the attacks added an acute shock. The Fed had cut rates from 6.5% to 3.0% since January 2001, including an intermeeting 50 bp cut on September 17. This meeting decided a further 50 bp cut to 2.5%.

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript Score:** Discussion 30 | Staff 38
**Minutes Score:** Discussion 30 | Staff 35

#### Discussion — Transcript
- Moskow: "One positive note is the lack of inflationary pressures in today's economy."
- Parry: "Our best estimate is for inflation of about 1-1/2 percent in the core PCE price index in 2002... in response to the greater expected slack in labor and product markets and a lower path for oil prices."

#### Discussion — Minutes

#### Staff — Minutes

- "Core prices declined in August, notably at the early stages of processing."
- Staff forecast: "current and prospective slack in resource use...would result in some modest deceleration in core PCE and CPI inflation."

**Observation:** All four sources place inflation clearly below target/neutral. Discussion sources score slightly lower (30) than staff sources (35–38), reflecting the discussion's emphasis on zero near-term concern versus the staff's more calibrated "subdued but not deflating" assessment.

## Key Divergences

1. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (Discussion Transcript 58 vs. Minutes 32):** The single largest gap (26 points). The transcript records Ferguson, Minehan, Gramlich, and Greenspan each devoting extended discussion to financial conditions as a policy rationale; the minutes reduce this to contextual notation and emphasize the "sound banking system." This gap is not explained by measurement error — it reflects a genuine difference in what the document type preserves.

2. **DISSENT_INTENSITY (Discussion Transcript 20 vs. Minutes 10):** McTeer's reservation and Guynn's temporariness concern are preserved in the transcript but absent from the minutes. The 10-point gap is attributable entirely to the minutes' "all the members" aggregation convention.

3. **TREASURY_STRESS (Staff Transcript 68 vs. Staff Minutes 60):** The transcript's Kos presentation captures the acute operational dysfunction (government securities dealers physically destroyed, Fed funds market inoperative) more vividly than the minutes' retrospective framing of the disruption as "restored by late September."

4. **EQUITY_VALUATION (Staff Transcript 58 vs. Staff Minutes 68):** Unusually, the minutes score higher here than the transcript. The minutes' language — "fell considerably further," "volatile trading," "sharp declines" — is stronger than the staff transcript's preserved evidence, suggesting the transcript scoring had less equity-specific language to draw on.
