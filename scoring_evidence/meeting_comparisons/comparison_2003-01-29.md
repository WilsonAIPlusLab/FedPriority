## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion — Transcript (32)**

- MR. PARRY: "The excess capacity in the economy brings consumer inflation down at a gradual pace, and inflation in the core PCE price index falls to about 1¼ percent in 2004...our forecast also would leave us with an inflation rate next year that's below 1 percent after taking out the estimated bias."
- MR. KOHN: "Inflation is already low and poised to decline further, perhaps by more than the staff has projected, given the much steeper declines that we've seen in recent years in price indexes other than core PCE."
- MR. BERNANKE: "Given the ongoing slack in utilization of labor and capital and our projection that core PCE inflation may slip to 1 percent or lower, I would hope that in such a circumstance we would be prepared to ease aggressively."
- MS. BIES: "Core consumer price inflation is running well below 2 percent, and people are shopping for bargains."

**Discussion — Minutes (23)**

- "Consumer price inflation would remain subdued; indeed, modest further disinflation might occur over the year ahead."
- "Consumer price inflation probably would edge down over the next several quarters from an already low level."
- "Muted pressures on labor and other resources." PCE forecasts centered at 1.25–1.5%.
- Cross-currents from dollar depreciation and oil noted but described as insufficient to alter the low-inflation direction.

**Staff — Transcript (25)**

- Slifman: core PCE projected to decline further; "continued wide margin of slack in product markets."
- Hourly compensation in "downtrend."
- Deflation risk from zero-lower-bound analysis described as still present.

**Staff — Minutes (30)**

- "Core consumer price inflation, as measured by the consumer price index (CPI) and the chain-weighted personal consumption expenditure (PCE) index, continued to edge lower through the end of the year."
- "The persistence of underutilized resources would tend to foster some moderation in core price inflation."
- "core prices for finished goods declined in November and December" [producer level].

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion — Transcript (34)**

- MR. PARRY: "excess capacity...still would leave the economy with excess capacity through the end of next year."
- MS. MINEHAN: "widening output gap in the context of declining inflation."
- MS. BIES: "That leaves the output gap very wide."
- MR. SANTOMERO: "almost half of our respondents indicated that they were operating at below 70 percent of capacity."

**Discussion — Minutes (35)**

- "Ongoing, albeit diminishing, slack in labor and product markets."
- "Current excess capacity would permit many firms to meet likely demands for some period of time without a significant increase in capital investments."
- GDP forecast 3.25–3.5% with unemployment staying at 5.75–6% — described as growth insufficient to close the gap this year.

**Staff — Transcript (32)**

- Slifman: unemployment projected to peak at 6.2%.
- GDP growth described as sluggish near-term; output gap wide and not closing until late 2003/2004.
- "Significant slack" throughout the economic outlook presentation.

**Staff — Minutes (34)**

- "Economic growth was very slow in the fourth quarter."
- "Capacity utilization in manufacturing fell again in December and was substantially below its long-run average."
- "The persistence of underutilized resources would tend to foster some moderation in core price inflation."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion — Transcript (33)**

- MR. BROADDUS: "Labor markets appear to have softened further in our area. We hear more reports of layoffs. Unemployment rates are rising in all of our District states."
- MR. GUYNN: "Job growth in our region has been stubbornly absent."
- MS. PIANALTO: "70 percent of the respondents said they would not add employees unless output in their firms increased more than 10 percent."
- MR. MOSKOW: temporary help firms — one reporting labor demand "inching forward," the other finding "the recovery actually has stalled."

**Discussion — Minutes (31)**

- "Persisting weakness in numerous regional labor markets."
- "Current reluctance of employers to add to their workforces."
- "Job gains and labor compensation would tend to lag the anticipated strengthening in economic activity."
- Unemployment forecast: 5.75–6%.

**Staff — Transcript (27)**

- Slifman: payrolls "about flat over the next few months."
- Unemployment projected to peak at 6.2%.
- Hiring described as very slow to resume; firms described as having "stretched work forces" but not yet adding headcount.

**Staff — Minutes (32)**

- "The labor market deteriorated further."
- "Private nonfarm payroll employment fell again in December and was at its lowest level since September 1999."
- "The unemployment rate held at 6 percent."
- "the slack in labor markets" used as explicit explanatory phrase for low compensation growth.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion — Transcript (38)**

- MR. HOENIG: "Consumer spending has been sluggish since the last meeting, and in most areas holiday retail sales were flat compared with a year ago despite some heavy discounting...retail activity is just dead."
- MR. KOHN: "business investment continues to disappoint, not yet showing signs of a sustained upturn."
- MR. POOLE: "consumer behavior is tilting toward chicken away from steak...a widespread phenomenon of people buying more goods at lower value price points."
- MS. BIES: "industrial production has fallen in four of the last five months; employment and production hours worked are below August levels."

**Discussion — Minutes (37)**

- "Household spending had been well maintained" but "elevated level of business caution clearly was holding back investment spending, and there were few signs of a pickup in the near term."
- "Generally disappointing holiday sales" with "atypically large and widespread discounting."
- Housing remains strong; inventories at "unusually low" levels.
- "A number of members" cited possible sharp snapback in business spending as an upside scenario.

**Staff — Transcript (38)**

- Economy still in sluggish growth from late 2002; defense spending providing some boost.
- Private sector demand described as weak; Iraq uncertainty weighing on confidence.

**Staff — Minutes (39)**

- Consumer: "Retail sales increased appreciably in November and December" and "Purchases of new motor vehicles were brisk."
- Business: "Business spending on equipment and software appeared to have little or no forward momentum."
- "Both shipments of and orders for nondefense capital goods turned down in the fourth quarter."

### FINANCIAL CONDITIONS / FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

*Note: Discussion track uses FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (higher = tighter); Staff track uses FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (higher = tighter/less accommodative). Both scales: above 50 = tight, below 50 = accommodative.*

**Discussion — Transcript (40)**

- MR. KOHN: "real interest rates for businesses have declined further—substantially for below-investment-grade businesses...risk spreads declined...yield curve remains unusually steeply upward sloped."
- MR. BROADDUS: "financial market conditions, given the economic situation we are witnessing, are relatively strong compared with what they've been in the past."
- MR. MCDONOUGH: "bankers reporting increased loan demand from commercial borrowers as well as tighter lending standards on that segment."
- MR. OLSON: consumer credit quality described as strong; commercial franchise loans strong with the exception of convenience stores.

**Discussion — Minutes (27)**

- "Monetary policy and overall financial conditions had remained accommodative."
- "Wide availability and low cost of capital."
- "Continued favorable effects of low interest rates and widely available financing on consumer purchases."
- Equity market weakness noted by "some members" as a risk, but not characterized as current financial tightening.

**Staff — Transcript (55)**

- Funds rate at 1.25%; ZLB analysis shows policy "already quite accommodative."
- Iraq uncertainty, stock market weakness, and business caution described as still weighing on conditions.
- Fiscal policy described as expansionary (defense spending up), providing partial offset.

**Staff — Minutes (32)**

- "Monetary policy was quite accommodative and well positioned to support a strengthening economic expansion."
- "A substantial decline in yields across the credit spectrum that further narrowed risk spreads."
- Staff forecast references "the considerable monetary ease already in place."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion — Transcript (38)**

- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "my recommendation is essentially that we stay with a 1¼ percent federal funds rate and leave the risks balanced."
- MR. PARRY: "Adding any downside risks for economic activity to this picture would only strengthen the argument that perhaps a further easing of policy might be appropriate before too much more time passes."
- MR. BERNANKE: "I would hope that in such a circumstance we would be prepared to ease aggressively earlier rather than later."
- MR. BROADDUS: "we might need to ease further, and we might need to do so aggressively."

**Discussion — Minutes (43)**

- "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "Accommodative stance of policy...argued for retaining a balanced risks assessment."
- "Committee could envision circumstances when it might find it desirable to adjust its policy stance substantially and promptly in one direction or the other."

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion — Transcript (72)**

- MR. BROADDUS: "it's rare, at least in my memory, that the near-term economic outlook has been so heavily dependent on the prospect and timing of one contingent event" (Iraq war).
- MR. MCTEER: "my sense of uncertainty and lack of conviction about our projections are as great as they've ever been."
- MR. FERGUSON: "we really are confronting a great deal of uncertainty" about geopolitical risks.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the military uncertainty is so overwhelming with respect to the question of potential monetary policy actions that the less we do...the better off we are."

**Discussion — Minutes (72)**

- "Forecasts were subject to substantial uncertainties, dominated at this point by the geopolitical situation."
- "Extremely wide confidence bands."
- "Committee could not rule out a range of plausible economic outcomes, including the possibility of a persisting subpar economic performance or a much stronger than forecast acceleration."
- "Response of the economy was hard to anticipate because of the difficulty of disentangling the effects of current geopolitical tensions from the underlying momentum."

## Points of Full Convergence

- **OUTPUT_GAP**: All four sources score 32–35. Consistent "well below potential, excess capacity" characterization across documents.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: All four sources score 27–33. Consistent "significant slack, unemployment elevated, hiring absent" characterization.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: All four sources score 37–39. Consistent split between resilient consumer and weak business investment.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Both discussion sources score 72. Identical score; near-identical language emphasis on Iraq-driven extraordinary uncertainty.
- **PRODUCTIVITY**: All sources with data score 58–68. Consistent above-trend structural productivity assessment.
