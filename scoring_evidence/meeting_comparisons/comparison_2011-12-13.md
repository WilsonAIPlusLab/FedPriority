### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Participants see current inflation as recently above but now heading to or below 2% target; 12-month readings still elevated near 3% but 6-month measures below 2% and declining; downside risks dominate medium-term outlook; minority flags medium-term upside.

Representative quotes:
- Williams (p.41): "Six-month annualized changes in overall, core, and trimmed mean PCE prices are now all below 2 percent and heading lower. Compensation growth is still soft, and labor cost pressures are basically nonexistent."
- Lacker (p.63): "on a 12-month basis, we are still much closer to 3 percent than to 2 percent."
- Yellen (p.70): "inflation now appears likely to drift substantially below the 2 percent level most of us consider consistent with the Fed's dual mandate."
- Minority: Plosser: "risks of higher inflation in the medium term"; George: "longer-term risks to inflation remain to the upside."

---

**Minutes Score: 33 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Participants see inflation as having moderated and trending further downward, with wide slack expected to keep it subdued. Most see inflation settling at or below mandate-consistent levels.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation had moderated from the rates earlier in the year, and longer-term inflation expectations remained stable."
- "Most participants anticipated that inflation would continue to moderate."
- "Many participants judged that the moderate expansion in economic activity... and the associated gradual reduction in the current wide margins of slack in labor and product markets would be consistent with subdued inflation going forward."
- "Some expressed the concern that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might run below mandate-consistent levels for some time."

Minority views: "A couple of participants noted that the rate of inflation over the past year had not fallen as much as would be expected." "Some participants were concerned that inflation could rise as the recovery continued." "A few participants argued that maintaining a highly accommodative stance... would erode the stability of inflation expectations."

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Near-unanimous view of substantial negative output gap; broad agreement that below-potential growth persists; even Bullard (who argues for smaller gap) does not claim economy is at or above potential.

Representative quotes:
- Evans (p.48–49): "even after accounting for these adjustments, there still appears to be a massive output gap... we see the U.S. output gap and the unemployment rate staying high through at least 2014."
- Tarullo (p.73): "I think the aggregate demand shortfall is still the story, with no particularly good reason to think that there's going to be much progress in creating the additional demand required to move the economy more quickly toward filling the output gap."
- Bernanke summary (p.79): "expected pace of growth may be insufficient to reduce unemployment by very much."
- Minority: Bullard argues conventional output gap estimates are overstated due to housing-bubble-inflated potential.

---

**Minutes Score: 34 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Economy is well below potential with moderate growth insufficient to close the gap quickly. Multiple headwinds restrain recovery.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate rate."
- "Wide margins of slack in labor and product markets."
- "The unemployment rate would decline only gradually."
- Headwinds: "European fiscal/banking issues," "slowdown abroad," "fiscal tightening in the United States," "high levels of uncertainty," "weak housing market," "household deleveraging."

Minority views: Narrow spread — participants broadly agree on the below-potential assessment.

---

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Unemployment at 8.6%, broadly acknowledged as far from full employment; labor market improving slowly but significant slack remains; participation rate declines complicate the picture.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen (p.68–69): "the U.S. economy continues to face an exceptionally prolonged period of unacceptably high unemployment."
- Raskin (p.76): "there are still 24 million underemployed people... At the current pace of improvement, it will be years before the labor market can be described in positive terms."
- Bernanke summary (p.79): "hiring remains slow, participation rates are down, and labor income is stagnant."
- Minority: Bullard notes unemployment has declined faster than expected and argues against conventional large-gap interpretation.

---

**Minutes Score: 32 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Labor market has significant slack. Some improvement in payrolls and claims but the level remains far from full employment.

Representative quotes:
- "The unemployment rate remained elevated despite a noticeable drop in November."
- "Part of the recent decline in the jobless rate was associated with a reduction in labor force participation, the drop in the unemployment rate likely overstated the overall improvement."
- "Unemployment, particularly longer-term unemployment, remained high, and the number of involuntary part-time workers was still elevated."
- "The very modest increases in labor compensation of late suggested that underutilization of labor was still significant."

Minority views: Debate on cyclical vs. structural unemployment widens the band. "Some part of the increase in unemployment since the beginning of the recession had been structural rather than cyclical" — if structural component is large, effective slack is smaller than headline suggests.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Near-term strength in holiday spending and auto sales, but income weakness and wealth losses from housing render it unsustainable; business investment held back by uncertainty; housing construction deeply depressed; net below-trend with some upside surprise in Q4.

Representative quotes:
- Fisher (p.53): "sales paid by credit and debit cards were up 7.4 percent year over year in November. That's the highest year-over-year gain in five years."
- Raskin (p.76–77): "Real disposable personal income declined in both the second and third quarters... Consumer sentiment is still below the levels from last spring... it still remains at levels we usually see in recessions."
- Bernanke summary (p.78–79): "Income growth has been reported as weak so that higher spending implies a drop in saving rates... questions are whether... the higher rate of consumption spending is sustainable."
- Minority: Duke and Fisher see more durable consumer strength.

---

**Minutes Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Net spending picture is below trend — consumer spending is a positive surprise but questioned as sustainable, while business investment is decelerating and housing remains depressed.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer spending in recent months had been stronger than expected."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to be decelerating."
- "Home sales and construction remained at very low levels."
- "Businesses generally reported that they remained cautious regarding capital spending and hiring."
- "A number of those participants suggested that the recent stronger pace of consumer spending might not be sustained."

Minority views: "A few participants thought that the recent strength in motor vehicle sales and other consumer spending could reflect pent-up demand... and so thought that it might persist for a time."

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript Score: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Large corporates and major banks access markets on near-normal terms; specific segments (ABCP, small business, community banks) face tightening; European bank deleveraging creates stress in those channels but U.S. banks stepping in to fill gaps. Net conditions are roughly neutral with mild upward pressure from European channels.

Representative quotes:
- Plosser (p.37): "U.S. financial institutions have been able to access short-term funding markets on mostly normal terms."
- Rosengren (p.41–42): "asset-backed commercial paper maturities have declined and rates have risen... This is another example of a financing structure that might not survive a European crisis."
- Williams (p.41): "tight credit... small business lending remains an area of weakness."
- Minority: Duke is notably more optimistic about overall credit conditions.

---

**Minutes Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Conditions are near neutral domestically but with pockets of tightness (mortgages, small business) and significant stress from European spillover volatility.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial markets remained volatile over the intermeeting period in large part because of developments in Europe."
- "Dollar funding conditions facing European institutions... were still strained."
- "Participants generally saw little evidence of significant new constraints on credit availability for domestic borrowers."
- "Several participants noted that their contacts had ready access to credit at attractive rates."
- "Some participants continued to view credit as tight, particularly in mortgage markets or among small businesses."

Minority views: Split between those seeing credit as freely available and those seeing continued tightness in specific sectors.

---

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript Score: 22 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Broad support for Alternative B (hold), but this is a QE-era meeting at zero lower bound where "hold" means maintaining massive accommodation; 4–5 participants explicitly advocate more easing (Alternative A equivalent); most B supporters signal readiness to ease further in January; QE discussion places center of gravity firmly in strong-dovish range.

Representative quotes:
- Evans (p.96–97): "I continue to not support Alternative B... I support some version of Alternative A... I favor more accommodation. I cannot support Alternative B."
- Yellen (p.103): "I see a compelling case for further policy accommodation... I'm attracted to both of the policy options in Alternative A and believe these steps deserve serious consideration by the Committee in January."
- Plosser (p.93–94): "I see no evidence that trying to push interest rates even lower will have much effect on what ails this economy... I am not confident that inflation expectations will remain anchored over the medium term if we keep trying to increase the degree of accommodation."
- Minority: Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, Kocherlakota all express resistance to further accommodation.

---

**Minutes Score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Solidly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation with focus on downside risks and persistent slack.

Representative quotes:
- "Almost all members agreed to maintain the existing stance of monetary policy at this meeting."
- Continuing Operation Twist, reinvestment, and zero rates.
- No discussion of reducing accommodation; discussion focused on downside risks and insufficient recovery.
- "Some expressed the concern that... inflation might run below mandate-consistent levels for some time."

Minority views: "A few participants argued that maintaining a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy over the medium run would erode the stability of inflation expectations."

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** 1 formal dissent (Evans), 2 additional participants signal they would dissent against date extension (Fisher, Plosser); notable difference in economic assessment between gap-skeptics (Bullard, Kocherlakota) and large-gap camp (Yellen, Evans, Tarullo); Tarullo: "we are quite divided on our substantive views of monetary policy." Moderate-notable disagreement.

Representative quotes:
- Evans (p.96): "I continue to not support Alternative B, as in the November meeting."
- Fisher (p.93): "I would not support and I would vote against—despite my great respect for Eric Rosengren—extending the date past the current language."
- Plosser (p.93): "I dissented in August in part because of the use of the calendar date."
- Limiting factor: Despite disagreements, Alternative B passed with broad support.

---

**Minutes Score: 28 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Broad agreement on policy action with moderate differences in economic assessment. Near-consensus on maintaining stance.

Representative quotes:
- "Almost all members agreed to maintain the existing stance of monetary policy" — implies near-unanimity but not full unanimity.
- Range of views on inflation outlook (below mandate vs. potential rise).
- Debate on cyclical vs. structural unemployment.
- Consumer spending sustainability debated.

### Dimension 8: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability from European channels is discussed by virtually every participant; Rosengren and Dudley devote substantial portions of their statements to specific financial transmission channels; Bernanke's summary dedicates multiple paragraphs. Prominent but not overwhelming — economic assessment and policy debate are equally prominent.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren (p.42–43): Extended analysis of broker-dealer funding risk, money market fund vulnerabilities, ABCP market stress, counterparty risk with French banks and U.S. broker-dealers.
- Dudley (p.63–67): Long treatment of European sovereign debt backstop inadequacy, ECB role, private investor subordination dynamics, broker-dealer funding model risk.
- Fisher (p.57): "I remain deeply concerned that we could have a significant correction in stock markets... we could easily go back to 800 on the S&P."

---

**Minutes Score: 52 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability/conditions are a moderately prominent topic — not dominant but meaningfully discussed as a key risk factor.

Representative quotes:
- "Strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks to economic activity."
- Extended discussion of European sovereign debt crisis, swap lines, banking conditions.
- Discussion of domestic credit conditions, community bank strains, European lenders pulling back.
- "Financial markets would remain focused on the situation in Europe as it evolves."

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Multiple participants explicitly discuss elevated uncertainty; Europe provides a wide scenario range; output gap measurement uncertainty is a specific sub-theme; businesses and households report elevated uncertainty in contacts; this is above normal but below "uncharted territory."

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke summary (p.81): "We clearly have an enormous amount of uncertainty tied to the European situation... with the alternative scenarios... unemployment rate goes to 11.8 percent... or to 7.4 percent."
- Lockhart (p.29–30): District poll found "two uncertainties dominated: economic performance, defined as the sustainability of demand; and economic policy." Contacts say "the uncertainties... are likely to be part of the economic picture for quite a while."
- Williams (p.39): "By almost any metric, uncertainty is elevated. Businesses are uncertain about the economic environment and about the direction of economic policy."

---

**Minutes Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity:** Elevated uncertainty driven by European crisis, fiscal outlook, and disagreement about the structural state of the economy. Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty, not just rhetorical hedging.

Representative quotes:

- "Strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks."
- "Uncertain about the economic outlook... uncertain fiscal outlook."
- "Participants expressed a range of views on the current extent of slack in the labor market."
