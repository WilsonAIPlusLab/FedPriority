# FOMC Meeting 1996-11-13: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 48 +/-7, High confidence)

> "underlying inflation has shown a slight downward trend according to most measures despite the low unemployment rate" — MR. PARRY

> "every broad measure of inflation other than the total CPI has been trending downward since 1993" — MS. YELLEN

> "CPI inflation has been trending up...3 percent inflation is too high" — MR. MELZER

> "most firms continue to report that competitive pressures make raising prices extremely difficult" — MR. MOSKOW

Center of gravity: Current inflation readings are contained and broad measures show stable-to-declining trends, though CPI is near 3% and the Greenbook projects a modest upward creep. The center of gravity sees benign current conditions with acknowledged upside risks from tight labor markets.

**Minutes** (Score: 44 +/-7, High confidence)

> "no broad evidence of rising price inflation"

> "many major measures of inflation had exhibited a slight downtrend since 1993"

> "increases in such costs were still falling short of those that would have been anticipated on the basis of historical experience"

> "several continued to view slightly higher inflation as a likely if not inevitable prospect"

Center of gravity: Current inflation is benign and below historical expectations given labor tightness, but members see asymmetric upside risk. The net assessment is slightly below neutral.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 53 +/-5, High confidence)

> "resource utilization rates are high and slack is limited" — MR. MOSKOW

> "the economy has slowed to trend growth" — MR. MEYER

> "the overall level of activity seems to remain high...labor markets are still quite tight" — MR. BROADDUS

> "the outlook may have shifted toward slightly slower growth, but only at the margin" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: The economy has slowed from above-trend growth to approximately trend, but the level of resource utilization remains high. Multiple participants describe the economy as operating at a high level with limited slack, placing it modestly above potential.

**Minutes** (Score: 50 +/-5, High confidence)

> "appreciable slowing in the expansion to a sustainable pace close to the economy's potential"

> "growth near the economy's potential as they anticipated"

Center of gravity: Economy is operating at or very near potential. Text uses "close to" and "near" potential repeatedly.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 62 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Labor markets remain very tight, and I have no doubt that there are outright labor shortages that are restraining activity" — MR. STERN

> "unemployment in several Eighth District states remains near 20-year lows" — MR. MELZER

> "many reports of labor market tightness...restaurants offering signing bonuses for new employees" — MR. MCTEER

> "labor markets unquestionably are still quite tight" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Nearly every participant describes labor markets as tight, with unemployment at 5.2% (near or below estimated NAIRU). Widespread anecdotal reports of shortages and difficulty finding workers. Wage pressures are moderate but real, though not yet passing through to prices.

**Minutes** (Score: 60 +/-5, High confidence)

> "widespread indications of tight labor markets"

> "comparatively little slack in relation to other producer resources"

> "tight labor markets and to a somewhat faster rise in labor compensation costs this year"

Center of gravity: Labor markets are clearly tight with little slack, but wage pressures are surprisingly muted relative to historical patterns.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 49 +/-7, High confidence)

> "consumer spending stalled, the growth of consumer credit plummeted, and lending standards tightened" in Q3 — MS. YELLEN

> "final demand especially for households was very weak in the third quarter" — MR. BROADDUS

> "Business fixed investment, including nonresidential construction, has been holding up better than most forecasts" — MS. PHILLIPS

> "evidence that growth in consumer spending and business investment may be slowing" — MR. GUYNN

Center of gravity: Consumer spending weakened notably in Q3 (a puzzle to many), while business investment and commercial construction remained solid. Most expect a consumption rebound, but it remains a forecast not a fact. Net position is approximately at trend, with Q3 consumer weakness offset by solid investment.

**Minutes** (Score: 48 +/-6, High confidence)

> "a pickup in the growth of consumer spending to a moderate pace was a likely prospect"

> "Business fixed investment was expected to provide further but diminished impetus"

> residential building "slowing somewhat"

Center of gravity: After a weak Q3, members expect a return to moderate spending growth. Investment still supportive but slowing. Net picture is approximately at trend.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 33 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "the cost of external capital is low as a result of low interest rates and the rise in stock prices. Banks are continuing to provide financing" — MS. PHILLIPS

> "the recent drop in long-term interest rates suggests that inflation expectations have diminished" — MR. BROADDUS

> "lending standards tightened" (on consumer credit side) — MS. YELLEN

> community bankers reporting rising delinquencies and tightening credit quality standards — MR. JORDAN

Center of gravity: Overall financial conditions are loose — long-term rates have declined, stock market surging, corporate financing readily available. However, there is an emerging pocket of tightness in consumer credit (rising delinquencies, tighter lending standards, rising bankruptcies). The dominant signal is accommodative conditions.

**Minutes** (Score: 35 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "generally supportive financial conditions"

> "continued availability of debt and equity financing on favorable terms"

> "still-ample availability of credit to most households"

> "generally tightening credit standards for consumer loans"

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are broadly supportive/accommodative, though some tightening in consumer credit standards noted.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 55 +/-8, High confidence)

> "I would recommend 'B' asymmetric...it would be premature to pull back on the asymmetry" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I would still favor a 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today" — MR. BROADDUS

> "I am going to advocate a modest increase in the funds rate" — MR. MELZER

> "I support 'B' asymmetric, but like President Jordan I do not feel nearly as 'asymmetric' as I did at the time of the last meeting" — MS. RIVLIN

Center of gravity: The overwhelming majority supports holding the funds rate steady with a tightening bias. Two participants (Broaddus, Melzer) favor an immediate 25bp hike. Several others (Minehan, Hoenig) lean toward hiking but accept the hold. A meaningful minority (Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, Kelley) feels less hawkish than before and questions the asymmetric tilt. No one favors easing. This is a lean-hawkish hold.

**Minutes** (Score: 55 +/-5, High confidence)

> "all the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance and the retention of a bias toward restraint"

> "several viewed such a bias as desirable because they continued to believe that the risks remained tilted...toward rising inflation"

> vote was unanimous 12-0 for hold with asymmetric tightening bias

Center of gravity: Unanimous hold with tightening bias. The lean is mildly hawkish — no one favored easing, and the dominant concern was inflation risk.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 37 +/-6, High confidence)

> MR. BROADDUS: favors 25bp hike, explicitly disagrees with hold

> MR. MELZER: advocates 25bp hike, explicitly disagrees with hold

> "do not feel nearly as 'asymmetric' as I did at the time of the last meeting" — MS. RIVLIN

> supports asymmetry "only because we used it in the directives for the previous two meetings" — MR. JORDAN

Center of gravity: No formal dissents in the vote, but two participants explicitly favor a different action (25bp hike). Two more lean toward hiking but acquiesce. Four others express reduced conviction about the tightening bias. Notable disagreement on degree/direction but consensus on the actual vote.

**Minutes** (Score: 20 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Votes for this action: [12 members]. Vote against this action: None."

> "views differed to some extent regarding the most likely course for inflation" but all agreed on action and directive

Center of gravity: Near-consensus on both the decision and the directive. Minor differences in inflation outlook assessment but no operational disagreement.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-7, High confidence)

> Extended discussion of stock market overvaluation — "we are erecting buildings in Boston again...valuations on financial assets...pose a problem for the future" — MR. LINDSEY

> "We are risking a major asset price bubble unless some sense of caution...is injected into these financial markets" — MS. MINEHAN

> "I also share Governor Lindsey's and President Minehan's concern about euphoria in asset markets" — MS. YELLEN

> Lengthy discussion of consumer credit quality deterioration, rising bankruptcies, banking concerns — MR. JORDAN

Center of gravity: Financial stability receives moderate attention. Lindsey's extended stock market discussion is a centerpiece. Several participants (Minehan, Hoenig, Yellen, Melzer) echo bubble/euphoria concerns. Jordan raises banking/consumer credit stress. This is above the typical 1990s baseline but not a dominant topic.

**Minutes** (Score: 12 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> Brief mentions of "possibility of a correction in the stock market"

> "marked increases that had occurred in the value of stock holdings"

> consumer debt strain — all in context of economic outlook, not financial stability per se

Center of gravity: Financial stability is not a focus. References to markets and credit are embedded in consumption/investment assessment, not as standalone stability concerns.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 58 +/-7, High confidence)

> "As long as the labor markets remain as tight as they are, inflation can turn up on a dime" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "these considerations make it likely that we will need to tighten policy in the future" — MR. PARRY

> "we are going to be there [asymmetric] for quite a while because I do not see any signs that we are going to be removing inflation pressures" — MR. LINDSEY

> "I only hope that we are prepared to move if in fact the upside risks...materialize" — MR. GUYNN

Center of gravity: The forward signal is a maintained tightening bias — the asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten. Multiple participants note tightening will likely be needed eventually. However, the urgency has diminished compared to the prior meeting. The signal is mildly hawkish: prepared to tighten if needed but no imminent action expected.

**Minutes** (Score: 57 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable and slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable" (asymmetric toward tightening)

> "a desirable opportunity to pause and observe further developments"

Center of gravity: Mildly hawkish forward lean. The asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten, but the dominant message is patience.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 38 +/-6, High confidence)

> "the wage structure is extraordinarily dynamic and not easy to read...our deficient understanding of the true structure of costs, benefits, and the like should create a degree of humbleness on our part" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> extensive two-sided uncertainty about consumption — "I could build a case" for either direction — MS. YELLEN

> "I don't think we understand this weakness or the related increase in the saving rate very clearly at this point" — MR. BROADDUS

> "there is a good deal of uncertainty about these adjustments in the inflation forecasts" — MR. MOSKOW

Center of gravity: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about: (1) the NAIRU/wage-price relationship, (2) why consumer spending stalled in Q3 and whether it will rebound, (3) productivity measurement. Greenspan's extensive discussion of data limitations adds to the uncertainty tone. However, the overall outlook is not deeply uncertain — most agree on the broad picture of moderate growth.

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "The outlook remained subject, as usual, to considerable uncertainty"

> "the outlook was far from certain"

> "range of potential developments was especially wide" (re fiscal policy)

> "prospective behavior of inventories remained subject to substantial uncertainties"

Center of gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (fiscal, inventories, inflation trajectory), but partially discounted by "as usual" framing.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

*Note: No staff transcript file exists for this meeting date. Part B is based on the staff minutes only.*

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 50 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> "Consumer price inflation had picked up this year because of larger increases in food and energy prices."

> "the advance in consumer prices of items other than food and energy was a little smaller than it had been over the previous twelve months"

> Staff forecast: "Continued pressure on resources...pointed to a likely underlying tendency toward higher inflation over the projection period; however...improved supply conditions in food and energy markets...would damp increases"

Center of gravity: Headline inflation elevated from food/energy but core improving; staff sees offsetting forces going forward, netting to a roughly balanced inflation picture.
