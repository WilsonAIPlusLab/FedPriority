### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 34 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly perceive inflation as stubbornly below target with persistent disinflationary forces (energy, dollar, global); a minority (Bullard, Fischer) view it as close to target net of energy shock but do not move the majority view.

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "PCE inflation has averaged 1.4 percent, while core PCE inflation has averaged 1.5 percent" over six years; "The Phillips curve remains inert, a tiny fraction of its former self."
- Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation is currently at 1.3 percent." Market-based inflation expectations "at or near 10-year lows."
- Tarullo: "both headline and core have been running below that target for nearly the whole period since the financial crisis, and this Committee has pretty consistently overestimated what inflation will be a year in advance."
- Bullard (minority): trimmed mean PCE at 1.7% is "only three-tenths below the Committee's goal."
- Fischer (minority): "largely temporary forces are keeping the inflation rate significantly below its target level."

**Minutes: 34 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Inflation was clearly running below the 2% target at 1.3% core PCE, with further downward pressure from energy and dollar appreciation, though nearly all participants expected a return to 2% over the medium term.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and prices of non-energy imports."
- "The 12-month change in the core PCE price index was 1.3 percent in October."
- "A majority of participants saw the risks to the outlook for inflation as balanced" but "many participants remained concerned about downside risks attending the outlook for inflation."
- Several noted alternative inflation indicators near or above 2%, pulling somewhat higher.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 54 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is broadly running at a modestly above-trend pace, with most participants projecting continued above-potential growth. Not a heated overshoot — the language is consistently "modestly above."

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "I see growth continuing above trend in 2016"; "labor market overshooting our full employment target by a substantial margin."
- Chair Yellen: "real GDP growth will continue to run a bit ahead of potential output growth, thereby generating further improvements in labor market conditions."
- Powell: "real GDP growth continuing modestly above the potential rate."
- Evans (minority): "resource gaps closing in 2017 and then the economy running only a bit above potential in 2018."
- Brainard (minority): emphasized residual slack still present in prime-age participation.

**Minutes: 47 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy was approaching potential, with unemployment near its longer-run level, but with residual slack in broader labor measures keeping it slightly below full capacity.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity was expanding moderately and confirming that underutilization of labor resources had diminished appreciably since early in the year."
- "The unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent in November, was... close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Many members judged that additional progress would be required to reach the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- Some participants stressed remaining underutilization across broader dimensions.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 53 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Majority of participants see the labor market as near full employment, with unemployment at 5% at or near the natural rate. A meaningful group (Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, Yellen) cite remaining slack via participation, wages, and part-time employment. Score is slightly above 50.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "With national unemployment at my estimate of the natural rate of 5 percent, I see the labor market as very close to our maximum employment goal."
- Mester: "I view the current economy at or nearly at full employment from the standpoint of our monetary policy goal."
- Chair Yellen: "the economy is not quite back to full employment. The unemployment rate is still ¼ percentage point above my own estimate of its longer-run level"; "wage growth…remains soft, suggesting not only that the labor market is not all that tight."
- Bullard (minority): labor market "largely healed."

**Minutes: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market was near but not quite at full employment, with the headline unemployment rate at its estimated longer-run level but broader measures still showing some slack and wage growth still subdued.

Representative quotes:
- "The unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent in November... close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Some others indicated that further progress in reducing labor market slack would be required before conditions would be consistent with the Committee's objective of maximum employment."
- "Labor compensation was still increasing at a subdued rate."
- Most expected unemployment to edge below the longer-run level soon.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending solid/moderate, auto sales strong, but business investment weaker (energy capex cuts, cautious capex plans), and inventory concerns noted. Net position at or slightly above trend.

Representative quotes:
- Chair Yellen: "Motor vehicle sales have been robust, retail sales are advancing at a solid pace…Housing continues its gradual recovery, and business fixed investment appears to be expanding steadily outside the drilling and mining sector."
- Brainard: "domestic demand continues to increase at a moderate pace. The recent data on consumer spending have been close to expectations—with auto sales, in particular, remaining strong—and real PCE looks to increase nearly 3 percent this year."
- Lockhart: "consumer spending reports were mixed"; concern about heightened inventory levels potentially weighing on production.
- Kaplan (minority): consumers are "healthier but notably cautious."

**Minutes: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending was solid and the services sector was strong, but manufacturing weakness from the strong dollar and energy price declines, along with soft net exports, kept the overall assessment at roughly trend pace.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer spending continued to rise at a solid rate in recent months; retail sales picked up... and motor vehicle sales remained strong."
- "Business activity was solid outside of sectors adversely affected by low energy prices and weak exports."
- "Manufacturing activity overall continued to be restrained by weakness in industries with significant international exposures."
- Energy sector and export-oriented manufacturing were notably weak.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 43 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions remain broadly accommodative at the aggregate level, despite high-yield stress. Multiple participants specifically noted equity markets "little changed," modest bond yield rise, and only selective credit tightening. Loose-to-neutral range, closer to neutral given the HY stress.

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "credit conditions have tightened somewhat since the previous FOMC meeting" with spreads up; "a healthy repricing of riskier assets and not an unwarranted reduction in credit availability."
- Dudley: "other than in the high-yield market, which is undergoing a lot of stress, financial conditions have not tightened appreciably" even as probability of hike rose from 30% to 90%.
- Kaplan: "High-yield credit spreads have widened" with energy-related ripple effects; Third Avenue fund disruption; "we are just going to have to monitor it."
- Rosengren and Fischer: raised financial stability concerns framed as future risk, not current tightness.

**Minutes: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions tightened modestly, with notable stress emerging in the high-yield and leveraged finance segments, though broader conditions remained relatively orderly with the rate hike well-anticipated.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial conditions tightened modestly over the intermeeting period."
- "Concerns among investors about the high-yield bond market increased notably... after an open-ended mutual fund specializing in junk bonds suspended redemptions and closed."
- "Several participants commented that markets for leveraged finance had been correcting since midyear."
- The high-yield stress was concentrated in energy-related credits; broader financial conditions were not severely strained.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 64 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: An overwhelming consensus in favor of hiking (Alternative B). 14–15 of approximately 16 speaking participants supported the hike. The hawkish lean is clear but tempered by strong emphasis on gradualism and data dependence going forward.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "I unequivocally support alternative B as written. The data convincingly support this decision."
- Fischer: "I favor raising the target federal funds rate to a range of 25 to 50 basis points."
- Tarullo: "I will reluctantly go along with alternative B today" — the sole participant preferring alternative A.
- Brainard and Evans expressed significant reservations but ultimately supported B.

**Minutes: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus to raise rates 25bp for liftoff, but with strong emphasis on gradualism, accommodative remaining stance, and close-call language from some members, placing this in the moderate hawkish range.

Representative quotes:
- "Almost all participants agreed that the improvements that had occurred in the labor market and their confidence in a return of inflation to 2 percent over the medium term now satisfied the Committee's criteria for beginning the policy normalization process."
- "Participants saw several reasons why a gradual removal of policy accommodation would likely be appropriate."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call, particularly given the uncertainty about inflation dynamics."
- A few emphasized financial stability risks could alter the path.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Zero formal voting dissents, but one participant (Tarullo) explicitly preferred a different policy and one (Evans) called it a "tough call," and two (Brainard, Dudley) expressed substantial reservations. The lack of formal dissent despite Tarullo's stated preference for Alternative A is notable — he cited institutional concerns, not a change of view.

Representative quotes:
- Tarullo: "I don't think it's appropriate policy to raise rates today… My policy preference is captured fairly well by alternative A… I will reluctantly go along with alternative B."
- Evans: "I find today to be a tough call. I think the risk-management case for delay until mid-2016 remains strong."
- Brainard: "combination of…core inflation stubbornly below its target…leads me to place somewhat greater weight on the possible regret associated with tightening too early."
- Dudley: expressed substantial reservations about the inflation hypothesis.

**Minutes: 28 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the liftoff decision with no apparent formal dissents, though some members expressed reservations about the close-call nature of the decision and inflation uncertainty.

Representative quotes:
- "Almost all participants agreed that the improvements... now satisfied the Committee's criteria for beginning the policy normalization process."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call."
- Various emphasis differences on inflation risks and remaining slack, but no formal dissents mentioned.
- "Close call" language and differing views on remaining labor slack create modest but not significant disagreement.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 42 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability received moderate discussion from multiple participants — more than a passing mention but not dominating the meeting. The high-yield/energy credit stress was a recurring theme across 5–6 speakers.

Representative quotes:
- Kaplan gave an extended discussion of energy sector credit stress, high-yield widening, and Third Avenue ripple effects.
- Williams discussed commercial real estate booming in the Twelfth District; "potential for imbalances merits close monitoring."
- Fischer: "some signs of accumulating financial stability problems" used as one of three reasons to hike now.
- Rosengren raised macroprudential concerns.

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability received limited but meaningful discussion, focused on the high-yield bond market correction and leveraged finance, with a few flagging it as a potential policy consideration.

Representative quotes:
- "Several participants commented that markets for leveraged finance had been correcting since midyear."
- "Concerns among investors about the high-yield bond market increased notably."
- "A few participants also indicated that significant risks to financial stability, should they emerge, could alter their view of the appropriate policy path."
- Most participants did not dwell on financial stability as a primary concern.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 67 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward guidance is clearly hawkish (hiking cycle has begun) but substantially modulated by "gradual" emphasis, data dependence, and multiple participants' relatively dovish SEP submissions (2–3 hikes in 2016 for many). The signal is "we are tightening but will be patient."

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "I can support alternative B today, in large part because of its important state-dependent characterization of our gradual adjustment path."
- Chair Yellen: "our economic projections imply that the federal funds rate will likely have to rise gradually over the next few years."
- Brainard: "the path is likely to be more gradual and move to a lower level than has been the case in past tightening cycles."
- Williams (minority): had four hikes in 2016 and five in 2017 — the most hawkish forward path discussed.

**Minutes: 63 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal pointed to further gradual tightening, but with heavy emphasis on data dependence, a low neutral rate, and asymmetric risks favoring caution — a mild tightening bias rather than a commitment to aggressive normalization.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants saw several reasons why a gradual removal of policy accommodation would likely be appropriate."
- "The neutral short-term real interest rate was currently close to zero and was expected to rise only slowly."
- "They stressed the importance of communicating clearly that the future policy path could become shallower if the economic expansion weakened."
- A few emphasized that significant financial stability risks could alter the path.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty, predominantly about inflation dynamics and the Phillips curve. Multiple participants explicitly questioned whether historical correlations would reassert themselves. The ZLB proximity made errors more costly. Uncertainty is concentrated on inflation, not comprehensive.

Representative quotes:
- Tarullo gave an extended, multi-page argument on why inflation dynamics were uncertain, including possible secular changes: "it is precisely at times when received wisdom is breaking down that policy missteps look, in retrospect, most likely to occur."
- Brainard: "it would be difficult for me to point to any developments in the intermeeting period that have raised my confidence that core inflation will move to our 2 percent target in the next two to three years."
- Chair Yellen: "taking a cautious approach to tightening remains appropriate in light of the asymmetric risk posed by the effective lower bound"; "heightened uncertainty about the economic effects of higher interest rates."
- Lacker, Mester, and George (minority): expressed fairly balanced/contained uncertainty; Williams explicitly said the decision was clear.

**Minutes: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty was somewhat elevated, concentrated particularly around the inflation outlook and inflation expectations, though most saw balanced risks for economic activity and the labor market.

Representative quotes:
- "The outlook for inflation was subject to considerable uncertainty."
- "Many viewed the persistent weakness in those prices as adding uncertainty or posing important downside risks to the inflation outlook."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call, particularly given the uncertainty about inflation dynamics."
- Some expressed concerns about inflation expectations potentially moving lower; most were more confident about the growth outlook.
