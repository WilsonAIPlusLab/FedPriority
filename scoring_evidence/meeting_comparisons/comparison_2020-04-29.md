|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 18 ±5 [High confidence] | 18 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**
- Clarida: "I also fully agree with the assessment that, on net, the COVID-19 contagion shock is disinflationary, not inflationary… if anything, I think the projected core PCE inflation path in the Tealbook may be too optimistic."

**Discussion Minutes — Center of Gravity:** Participants unanimously view inflation as well below target with multiple disinflationary forces at work; the return to 2% "would likely be further delayed."

**Discussion Minutes — Minority Views:** Some supply constraints noted creating "upward pressure on the prices of some goods," but this was overwhelmed by demand destruction.

**Discussion Minutes — Center of Gravity:** Participants describe a catastrophic contraction — "unprecedented" decline in output, with the economy operating massively below potential.

**Discussion Minutes — Minority Views:** None on the direction; all participants agree on severe contraction. Slight variation on recovery speed informs medium-term scenarios but not current assessment.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

**Note: No staff transcript is available for this meeting. The staff assessment below is drawn exclusively from the staff sections of the published minutes (Staff Review of the Economic Situation, Staff Review of the Financial Situation, Staff Economic Outlook).**

---

### Staff Dimensions — Minutes Only

---

#### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Total PCE price inflation and core PCE price inflation...both increased 1.8 percent over the 12 months ending in February."
- "Inflation was projected to weaken this year, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."

**Center of Gravity:** Inflation already below target at 1.8% and projected to weaken further amid massive economic contraction and energy price declines.

---

#### OUTPUT_GAP: 15 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Real gross domestic product (GDP) declined sharply in the first quarter"
- "a variety of economic indicators were already pointing toward an extraordinary contraction in GDP in the second quarter"
- "Real GDP was forecast to plummet."

**Center of Gravity:** Catastrophic contraction underway — sharp Q1 decline with "extraordinary" Q2 contraction expected. This is among the worst output gaps in modern history.

---

#### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 13 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "U.S. labor market conditions deteriorated substantially in March and April."
- "Initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits skyrocketed in the second half of March through the first half of April."
- "The unemployment rate to soar in the second quarter."

**Center of Gravity:** Labor market in historic collapse — mass layoffs, skyrocketing unemployment claims, with far worse expected. Unemployment already 4.4% in March and expected to soar.

---

#### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 12 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Real PCE declined steeply in the first quarter."
- "Real business fixed investment slumped in the first quarter."
- "Total industrial production fell precipitously in March."
- "Consumer sentiment measures...deteriorated substantially."

**Center of Gravity:** Consumer spending, business investment, and industrial production all in freefall. Virtually every spending category is collapsing.

---

#### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

Not discussed in the staff assessment.

---

#### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 75 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Financing conditions for businesses, households, and state and local governments were strained over the intermeeting period."
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses were strained in March, particularly for lower-rated firms and small businesses."
- "C&I lending conditions were somewhat tight."
- "Banks reported having tightened their C&I lending standards and terms for firms of all sizes."

**Center of Gravity:** Conditions were extremely tight at mid-March and have improved somewhat due to Fed intervention, but remain strained across most markets. Not at crisis peak (85–95) but well above normal.

---

#### TREASURY_STRESS: 68 ±8 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Treasury markets experienced extreme volatility in mid-March, and market liquidity became substantially impaired."
- "Market depth remained exceptionally low and bid-ask spreads for off-the-run securities and long-term on-the-run securities remained elevated."

**Center of Gravity:** Genuine market dysfunction occurred — impaired liquidity, elevated bid-ask spreads, exceptionally low market depth. Conditions improved from the worst but remained abnormal at meeting time.

---

#### CREDIT_SPREADS: 68 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Corporate bond spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened sharply in the beginning of the intermeeting period, and they subsequently retraced most of their increases to end up only somewhat higher on net."
- "Corporate bond spreads at the end of the intermeeting period still stood significantly above their levels in January."

**Center of Gravity:** Spreads spiked dramatically in mid-March, partially retraced, but remain "significantly above" pre-crisis levels. Speculative-grade issuance was "extremely low."

---

#### EQUITY_VALUATION: 63 ±8 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Stock price indexes were exceptionally volatile early in the intermeeting period, and one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index reached a record high."
- "Broad equity price indexes remained, however, markedly below peaks registered earlier this year."

**Center of Gravity:** Equities recovered from worst levels but remain "markedly below" peaks. Record volatility occurred. Staff describes significant stress with partial recovery.

---

#### BANKING_STRESS: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "The banking sector, including the large banks, was resilient coming into this period. Banks were able to meet surging demand for draws on credit lines while also building loan loss reserves."
- "some notable vulnerabilities that had been identified in previous financial stability assessments exacerbated financial strains"
- "possible vulnerabilities in mortgage servicers, insurance companies, and large, highly leveraged financial intermediaries."

**Center of Gravity:** Banks themselves are described as resilient and well-capitalized, but the broader financial system exhibits notable vulnerabilities and stress in non-bank intermediaries. The net picture is near-neutral with a slight tilt toward stress.

---

#### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "The staff's broad dollar index increased, with the dollar appreciating modestly against AFE currencies and notably against EME currencies."
- "The broad dollar index remained significantly higher than at the beginning of the year."

**Center of Gravity:** Dollar strengthened meaningfully, particularly against EME currencies, reflecting safe-haven flows. The broad index is significantly higher than year-start.

---

#### INFLATION_GOODS: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence:**
- "Substantial declines in consumer energy prices, which were reflecting significantly lower crude oil prices."
- Staff projects "sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."

**Center of Gravity:** Goods prices pulled down significantly by energy price collapse.
