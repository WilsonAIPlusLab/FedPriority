### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly see inflation as meaningfully below the 2% target (core PCE ~1.6–1.7%), with gradual upward progress but a persistent shortfall.

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "Over the 12-month period ending in November, core PCE prices increased 1.6 percent. This rate is still notably below 2 percent, but it's up ¼ percentage point from a year earlier."
- Mester: "PCE inflation has continued to rise toward our 2 percent goal, and core PCE inflation has been stable around 1.7 percent."
- Kashkari: "Year-on-year core PCE inflation is still running around 1.6 percent or 1.7 percent… we are still below target, and in my mind it is too soon to declare victory."
- Fischer: "PCE inflation rate is close to reaching its target rate of 2 percent." [minority view, upside]

**Minutes: 44 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is below the 2% target but clearly moving closer; participants see it approaching target gradually without alarm on either side.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation had increased in recent quarters but was still below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective."
- "The 12-month change in headline PCE prices increased further, to 1.6 percent in December... The 12-month change in core PCE prices stayed near 1.7 percent."
- "Most participants continued to expect that inflation would rise to the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term."
- "Some saw a risk that inflationary pressures might develop more rapidly than currently anticipated." [minority upside view]

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The majority see the economy growing at or slightly above trend/potential, with labor markets approaching or at full employment. Firmly above 50 but only modestly.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "Real GDP has increased nearly 2 percent in each of the past two years, which is slightly above my and the Tealbook's estimate of trend growth. Labor markets already reached and perhaps even stretched past maximum employment."
- Dudley: "We are still growing at a slightly above-trend pace, still putting gradual pressure on labor resources."
- Rosengren: "I expect the economy to grow somewhat faster than 2 percent, which is above my estimate of potential at 1¾ percent."
- Bullard: "2 percent growth rate as being statistically indistinguishable from the trend growth rate." [minority view, at potential]

**Minutes: 51 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is essentially at potential, with moderate growth expected to push it slightly above. The staff projection references "operating above its longer-run potential," and participants broadly concur.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace."
- "The unemployment rate remained close to levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Several participants projected a more substantial undershooting" of the longer-run normal unemployment rate.
- "Several others noted remaining slack in labor force participation." [minority view, below potential]

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 54 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The majority of participants see the labor market at or near maximum employment (4.7% unemployment). Most indicators — quits rate, U-6 improving, wage growth picking up — support this. Score is modestly above 50.

Representative quotes:
- Fischer: "Unemployment is at or below most estimates of the natural rate."
- Williams: "Labor markets already reached and perhaps even stretched past maximum employment," describing holiday hiring as "unnerving."
- Kaplan: "relatively low level of labor market slack, and… a reasonable and increasing probability of overshooting our full-employment objective."
- Kashkari: "Not yet convinced we have reached maximum employment"; CBO says employment shortfall of 1.6 million; U-6 still 1 pp above pre-recession levels. [minority view]

**Minutes: 50 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is approximately at full employment — the unemployment rate is near NAIRU, with anecdotal tightness in some sectors balanced by remaining slack measures.

Representative quotes:
- "At 4.7 percent in December, the unemployment rate remained close to levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Several participants' business contacts reported shortages of workers in some occupations."
- "Several other participants thought that some margins of labor underutilization remained, citing the still-high rate of prime-age workers outside the labor force."
- "The still-elevated rates of part-time employment for economic reasons." [remaining slack indicators]

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 45 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are roughly neutral, perhaps slightly accommodative on net: equities have risen substantially, credit spreads have narrowed, but these are offset by a stronger dollar and higher long-term rates.

Representative quotes:
- Dudley: "stocks are up, credit spreads have narrowed a lot, and that's been offset by the dollar appreciation and the increase in Treasury bond yields… movements in financial conditions roughly cancel themselves out."
- Brainard: "valuation pressures have risen to 'notable' for the first time in a year, while financial-sector leverage remains low and risks associated with maturity and liquidity transformation have moved lower."
- Brainard: "the dollar is up about 4 percent since October and over 20 percent since mid-2014. Furthermore, longer-term corporate interest rates are up close to 50 basis points since October."
- Mester: "asset valuation pressures has risen from moderate to notable, and leverage is very high for speculative-grade corporate borrowers." [tightening/risk dimension]

**Minutes: 47 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are roughly neutral — loosening forces (equities, spreads) offset by tightening forces (rates, dollar). Slightly on the accommodative side given equity gains and spread narrowing.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial conditions appeared to have changed little, on net, in recent months: Equity prices had risen and credit spreads had narrowed, but longer-term interest rates had increased and the dollar had appreciated further."
- No participant language in the minutes suggesting stress or unusual tightness/looseness beyond the above summary.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 63 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous alt-B (hold) vote, but the forward lean is clearly hawkish among the majority. Roughly 8 of ~12 substantive participants lean toward March move or sooner; 4 want patience. Current action is hold, but bias is distinctly toward continued tightening in the near term.

Representative quotes:
- Mester: "if we had prepared the public for it, I would be advocating taking another step today… I'd be more comfortable if we were at a higher level of the funds rate."
- Powell: "I see it as appropriate to continue gradually raising the target range for the federal funds rate. In December, I wrote down three increases for 2017, and that still seems about right. If the data are supportive, then I could see the March meeting as an appropriate time for another increase."
- Yellen: "a further increase in the federal funds rate in March may well be appropriate if incoming data suggest that employment and inflation are evolving in line with or more strongly than our current expectations."
- Evans: wants only two hikes and no reinvestment change; Brainard: "useful to gather more information before undertaking a further removal of accommodation." [minority dovish views]

**Minutes: 63 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Clear consensus for continued gradual tightening; many leaning toward hiking fairly soon. Hold at this meeting but strong bias toward near-term action.

Representative quotes:
- "Many participants expressed the view that it might be appropriate to raise the federal funds rate again fairly soon if incoming information on the labor market and inflation was in line with or stronger than their current expectations."
- "Most participants continued to judge that...a gradual pace of rate increases over time was likely to be appropriate."
- "A few participants noted that continuing to remove policy accommodation in a timely manner, potentially at an upcoming meeting, would allow the Committee greater flexibility."
- "A few others continued to see downside risks to inflation or anticipated only a gradual return of inflation to the 2 percent objective." [minority dovish view]

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

Near-consensus acknowledgment of elevated uncertainty places this well above normal. Multiple participants use strong language ("Knightian uncertainty," "very high," "chaotic").

Representative quotes:

**Minutes: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- "Participants again emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the prospects for changes in fiscal and other government policies as well as about the timing and magnitude of the net effects of such changes on economic activity."
