### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- "the trailing 12-month change in the case of core PCE prices is now 1.56 percent, substantially below our objective." (Brainard)
- "we still have not reached our 2 percent target for inflation, and we may not even be making progress toward that goal." (Kashkari)
- "core PCE inflation rate… now at 1.6 percent… suggesting that there's little inflation pressure in the U.S. economy." (Bullard)
- Minority: "I view inflation as being very close to our target." (Fischer); Mester cites Cleveland median CPI at 2.5% and expects sustainable 2% by year-end.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Virtually all participants acknowledge core PCE at 1.6%, unchanged from a year ago, below target with no clear near-term progress; a minority argue alternative measures are near 2% and expect convergence by year-end, but the dominant tone is "below target and concerning."

---

**Minutes — Score: 43 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "Core PCE price inflation... moved down to 1.6 percent over the 12 months ending in March."
- "Most participants viewed the recent softer inflation data as primarily reflecting transitory factors."
- "Several participants emphasized that inflation measured on a 12-month basis had been running very close to the Committee's 2 percent target."
- Minority: "A few expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's objective may have slowed"; "several others continued to see downside risks to the inflation outlook."

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Inflation is slightly below the 2% target; recent readings were soft, but most participants attribute this to transitory factors and expect stabilization around 2%.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 54 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- "We are currently about as close as we've ever been to simultaneously achieving our inflation and full employment mandates, and yet the economy appears on track to outpace potential for the foreseeable future." (Williams)
- "According to the Tealbook, current economic output is above its potential." (George)
- "above-potential GDP growth." (Rosengren)
- Minority: Bullard argues the economy is in a "balanced growth steady state" of ~2% and sees no meaningful overheating; Kashkari notes it is "difficult for me to take too seriously the concerns that the economy may be overheating."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Despite a weak Q1, the consensus view is that the economy is at or modestly above potential for the medium run, with the Q1 miss deemed transitory; the bright-line trigger of "above potential" language from multiple participants requires a score above 50.

---

**Minutes — Score: 52 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- "The unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Growth in economic activity slowed in the first quarter" but the slowing was viewed "as likely to be transitory."
- Participants expected "economic activity would expand at a moderate pace."
- Minority: Narrow disagreement; Q1 weakness was broadly viewed as transitory.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Economy is modestly above potential — unemployment below longer-run normal — though Q1 GDP was temporarily weak.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 57 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- "unemployment rate… 4½ percent, almost ½ percentage point below the staff's estimate of the natural rate." (Fischer)
- "We have now reached or exceeded everyone's longer-run estimate of full employment from the March SEP." (Williams)
- "I continue to hear many of the same old complaints about shortages of skilled workers." (Evans)
- Minority: Kashkari sees ongoing scope for labor force participation gains; Brainard notes part-time-for-economic-reasons and prime-age EPOP remain below pre-crisis levels.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: The labor market is described as tight to very tight, with unemployment clearly below most NAIRU estimates and multiple participants reporting ongoing labor shortages; the consensus is that labor markets have overshot full employment, though wage growth remains muted.

---

**Minutes — Score: 53 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "At 4.5 percent, the unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Contacts in several Districts reported a pickup in wage increases, shortages of workers in selected occupations."
- "Several other participants suggested some margins may remain along which labor market utilization could increase further."
- Minority: Some see remaining slack (labor force participation rising); a few anticipate substantial undershooting of normal unemployment.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Labor market is tight, at or slightly past full employment, with some localized shortages but also possible remaining margins of slack.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 47 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "consumption rose at an annual rate of only 0.3 percent compared with 3 percent for 2016." (Powell)
- "residential construction posted a double-digit increase, drilling for oil and natural gas is rebounding sharply… business spending on equipment and intangibles… rebounded at a strong annualized rate." (Brainard)
- "Business fixed investment picked up in the first quarter." (Mester)
- Minority: Dudley raises structural concerns about motor vehicle sector demand going forward; Bullard worries Q1 data may have more signal than others believe.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Consumer spending was exceptionally weak in Q1 (attributed to transitory factors), but business investment and residential construction were strong, with nearly all participants expecting a consumption rebound; the net is slightly below trend for the current moment but with high confidence of return to ~trend pace.

---

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- "Household spending rose only modestly, but the fundamentals underpinning the continued growth of consumption remained solid."
- "Business fixed investment increased at a solid pace in the first quarter."
- "The slowing in growth during the first quarter was likely to be transitory."
- Minority: "A few emphasized the uncertainty with regard to the reasons for the unexpected weakness in consumer spending."

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Consumer spending was temporarily weak in Q1 while business investment firmed; net assessment is roughly at trend with expectations of rebound.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 34 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- "financial conditions appear to have eased over the past six months despite our actions to remove monetary accommodation on two occasions… low levels of implied volatility and compressed risk spreads." (George)
- "our recent moves to remove accommodation have not tightened financial conditions." (Dudley)
- "financial conditions have eased significantly, on net." (Brainard)
- Minority: Fischer notes negative real rates but frames this through the lens of r* uncertainty rather than explicit looseness concern. Powell mentions compressed risk premiums in equities and corporate debt as a mild concern.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Multiple participants explicitly note that the rate hikes have not translated into tighter financial conditions, with eased conditions described by multiple speakers; the consensus is that conditions are accommodative/somewhat loose.

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**

- "The decline in longer-term interest rates and the modest depreciation of the dollar over the intermeeting period would provide some stimulus to aggregate demand."
- "The Committee's recent policy actions had not resulted in a tightening of financial conditions."
- Minority: Limited discussion; only "some participants" addressed this topic.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Despite rate hikes, financial conditions had not tightened and were somewhat supportive — somewhat loose relative to neutral.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 69 ±8 | Confidence: High**

- "I expect economic conditions, particularly the outlook, to support three more rate increases this year and the start of normalization of the balance sheet also by late this year." (Williams)
- "a path of either three or four increases per year is quite gradual by historical standards." (Rosengren)
- "I anticipate that the Committee will raise the funds rate in June unless the data come in significantly weaker than expected." (Mester)
- Minority: Bullard believes the Committee is "somewhat overcommitted" to a sequence of rate moves; Kashkari says June hike inappropriate without more inflation progress.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: The strong majority view supports continuing gradual tightening, with June hike expected; policy consensus is firmly on a hiking path with balance sheet normalization also approaching; moderated slightly by Bullard's skepticism and Kashkari's conditions on June.

---

**Minutes — Score: 63 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "Most participants judged that if economic information came in about in line with their expectations, it would soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing some policy accommodation."
- "Participants generally reiterated their support for a continued gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate."
- "Several participants, however, pointed to conditions under which the Committee might need to consider a somewhat more rapid removal of monetary accommodation."
- Minority: "A couple of others judged that the Committee could withdraw monetary accommodation even more gradually" (dovish wing); "a couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range... at the current meeting would be warranted" (hawkish wing).

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Moderately hawkish. Consensus for gradual tightening with a near-term hike on the horizon, though no urgency at this meeting.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±8 | Confidence: High**

- Fischer raises fundamental questions about the "long march" strategy in his extended policy remarks.

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: No formal dissents, but the committee shows a clear spectrum from Rosengren/Williams (hawkishly concerned about overshoot) to Bullard/Kashkari (skeptical of further tightening), with substantive disagreements about the inflation outlook and appropriate pace.

---

**Minutes — Score: 28 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- No formal dissents; unanimous hold decision.
- "A couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range for the federal funds rate at the current meeting would be warranted" but accepted the hold.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Broad agreement on holding rates and gradual approach, with minor tactical differences about pace.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Financial stability received a dedicated paragraph with several participants weighing in, but it was not a dominant topic at this meeting.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 74 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- "if economic conditions develop as I expect, a further increase in the target range at our next meeting will probably be appropriate" (Yellen)
- "three more rate increases this year and the start of normalization of the balance sheet also by late this year" (Williams)

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish — June hike widely expected, multiple further hikes in 2017, and balance sheet normalization plan to be released at June meeting; moderated to ~74 by data dependence language and the hold decision itself.

---

**Minutes — Score: 65 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "A continued gradual removal of monetary policy accommodation remained appropriate."

- Minority: A couple favored even more gradual removal; several flagged scenarios for faster removal.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Clear forward signal toward further gradual tightening, with the next hike likely coming soon.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript — Score: 42 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Participants name several genuine uncertainties: whether Q1 weakness is transitory, whether the March inflation miss signals a trend, what fiscal policy will deliver, natural rate of unemployment, r*; this is somewhat elevated above the 2017 baseline but well short of crisis-level uncertainty.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**

- "Prospects for enactment of a more expansionary fiscal program, as well as its size, composition, and timing, remained highly uncertain."

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Moderate uncertainty, mainly around fiscal policy prospects and some structural parameters, but overall outlook little changed and risks balanced.
