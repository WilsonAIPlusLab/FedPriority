# Evidence Comparison: FOMC Meeting 1997-12-16

## Meeting Context
December 16, 1997. Fed funds rate at 5.50% (unchanged since March 1997). U.S. economy in late-cycle expansion with tight labor markets and subdued inflation. Asian financial crisis (Thailand, Korea, Indonesia) is the dominant new uncertainty factor. Decision: hold steady, shift to symmetric directive. One dissent (Broaddus, for immediate 25bp hike).

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 37 ±8 [High] | Minutes: 42 ±7 [Moderate]**

**Transcript (scored: 37)**
- PARRY: "inflation continues subdued" and "we have reduced our forecast since the November meeting"
- HOENIG: "inflation has been quite stable. Core inflation has been stable or declining, pipeline inflation is basically nonexistent, and various measures of short-term and long-term inflation expectations are stable or declining"
- GREENSPAN: "We cannot keep getting such numbers and continue to say that inflation is about to rise. As we keep projecting a higher rate of inflation, it keeps going down"
- BOEHNE: Notes business people asking about *deflation* rather than inflation

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Inflation subdued and declining, well below any level of concern. Tight labor markets and accelerating wages represent a latent upside risk that widens the uncertainty band.

**Minutes (scored: 42)**
- "price inflation had remained subdued, indeed with some key price measures indicating declining inflation, despite the persistence of robust economic growth"
- "multiplying indications of faster wage increases might presage rising price inflation at some point"
- "developments associated with the turmoil in Asia along with the partly related appreciation of the dollar would tend to intensify import competition and damp the prices of goods"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Current inflation clearly subdued and below target, but mounting wage pressures create upside risk. Asian crisis adds disinflationary force. Net assessment is slightly below neutral.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 58 ±7 [High] | Minutes: 58 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript (scored: 58)**
- GREENSPAN: "an economy growing at a pace near 4 percent and operating at a 4.6 percent unemployment rate"
- MEYER: "an economy already operating beyond its point of sustainable capacity, with the prospect of a slow progressive updrift in inflation"
- PARRY: "real GDP growth remains robust in the current quarter" and "the unemployment rate...is now well below the natural rate"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Economy clearly operating above potential with robust growth and very low unemployment, though the Asian shock is expected to bring growth closer to trend.

**Minutes (scored: 58)**

- "the economy possibly producing beyond its potential"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Members view the economy as operating above potential with robust domestic demand, though Asian effects expected to moderate growth going forward.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 70 ±5 [High] | Minutes: 70 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript (scored: 70)**
- STERN: "wage pressures have become pervasive both geographically and by job and skill categories. It is very, very hard to find workers"
- BROADDUS: "record-breaking job vacancy backlogs at a number of temp agencies...seasonal workers...are now making $6.00 an hour or even more"
- MCTEER: "Sizable wage increases are reported for highly skilled technical workers. Salaries have soared as much as $20,000 in the last year for...software engineers"; Intel mailing 14,000 letters to poach engineers with Hawaii trip offers
- JORDAN: "it was impossible to discipline workers because of the hot job market. If an employer tried to tell a worker that there were policies about showing up on time...the worker simply left"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Labor markets extremely tight across virtually all districts and skill levels. Creative and extraordinary measures to attract workers are widespread. Wage pressures are pervasive and accelerating.

**Minutes (scored: 70)**
- "members emphasized the very limited supply of new workers and the extraordinary tightness prevailing in markets throughout the nation"
- "Several reported that the scarcity of available workers was limiting the growth of economic activity in some parts of the country"
- "larger increases were gradually becoming more pervasive as labor markets tightened"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Participants unanimously view labor markets as very tight, with shortages constraining growth and wage pressures building broadly.

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Both consumer spending and business investment described as strong to robust, above trend but with expected moderation from Asia effects and natural deceleration.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 30 ±7 [High] | Minutes: 27 ±6 [Moderate]**

**Transcript (scored: 30)**
- FERGUSON: "credit conditions are not overly tight" and evidence of "some loosening of lending standards"
- MINEHAN: "Commercial real estate lending is seen as a growth opportunity for the first time since the late 1980s" with District loans "growing at better than twice the national rate" and real estate lending "at over three times the national pace"
- BROADDUS: "the capital reflow that is occurring has some of the effects of an easing of monetary policy"
- GREENSPAN: "It is very hard to make the case that we have tight money when the housing market is behaving as well as it is"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Financial conditions are loose. Credit is freely available, lending standards are loosening, capital inflows from Asia are pushing rates down. Housing and commercial RE are booming.

**Minutes (scored: 27)**
- "Current conditions in domestic financial markets clearly remained supportive of spending"
- "the low cost of capital, the ready availability of finance on attractive terms"
- "the cumulative effects of very large increases in household wealth in recent years"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Domestic financial conditions clearly loose and supportive. Credit freely available, equity wealth high, financing terms attractive. One member noted the real federal funds rate was relatively high, but overall conditions are accommodative.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 52 ±6 [High] | Minutes: 53 ±7 [High]**

**Transcript (scored: 52)**
- GREENSPAN: "I would be marginally in favor of alternative B symmetric this time...I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point. I see that as the most probable next move"
- MCDONOUGH: "absent the international events, this might be a rather good opportunity for a 25 basis point move...But given the international considerations, I believe such a move would be very ill-advised"
- BROADDUS (outlier hawkish): "a modest 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today would be a prudent step for us to take"
- KELLEY: "upside risks seem to me to have moderated a bit while...significant downside concerns have now emerged...those risks seem roughly in balance"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Strong consensus for holding rates. Most acknowledge that absent Asia, tightening might have been warranted. Net lean is very slightly hawkish -- hold but next move probably up.

**Minutes (scored: 53)**
- "nearly all the members favored a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "many members commented that...they viewed the risks on balance as pointing to rising price inflation and the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening"
- "most members agreed that the need for such a policy adjustment did not appear to be imminent"
- One dissent (Broaddus) for immediate tightening

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Hold steady with mild tightening bias. Center of gravity just above neutral -- members expect next move to be a hike but see no urgency. Shift to symmetric directive tempers the hawkish lean.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Transcript: 30 ±5 [High] | Minutes: 33 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript (scored: 30)**
- Broaddus is the sole advocate for a rate hike: "I still think, stubbornly no doubt, that a modest 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today would be a prudent step"
- Secondary disagreement on symmetric vs. asymmetric directive: Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Guynn prefer asymmetric but accept symmetric
- No formal dissent expected; broad agreement on holding

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Broad agreement on the hold decision. Moderate differences on directive tilt but all can live with the outcome. One clear policy outlier (Broaddus).

**Minutes (scored: 33)**
- One formal dissent: "Mr. Broaddus dissented because he continued to believe that a modest tightening of policy would be prudent"
- "Views were somewhat more divided with regard to the instruction in the directive relating to the possible adjustment of policy during the intermeeting period"
- "A majority of the members indicated a preference for a shift to a symmetrical directive" vs. "Other members expressed a slight preference for retaining a directive that was tilted toward tightening"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): One formal dissent plus notable disagreement on directive language. Moderate but not severe disagreement. The main policy action (hold) had near-consensus.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Transcript: 62 ±7 [High] | Minutes: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**

**Transcript (scored: 62)**
- MCDONOUGH: Entire remarks devoted to international financial stability -- "the world economy is walking along a rather narrow ridge...to the left, a rather large precipice"; discusses potential "implosion of the financial systems in some of the Asian countries"
- GREENSPAN: "It is conceivable that we could get a significant implosion here. I believe the odds are small but not zero"; discusses equity premium and stock overvaluation
- MINEHAN: Raises alarm on speculative commercial RE construction -- "plans have been submitted for 40 new hotels in Boston proper...11.3 million square feet of speculative office space"; calls for regulatory follow-up on lending standards
- JORDAN: References 1920s parallel of being "too restrictive for goods prices and too expansionary for asset prices"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Financial stability is a prominent topic. The Asian crisis dominates as a financial stability concern. Additionally, domestic concerns about asset prices, lending standards, and speculative construction receive meaningful attention.

**Minutes (scored: 45)**
- Extensive discussion of Asian financial turmoil and its potential U.S. effects
- "increased uncertainty about financial asset values, possibly related in part to further difficulties in Asia, could lead to greater caution in spending"
- "a substantial decline in equity values, should it occur, would have a more pronounced effect by reducing household wealth"
- "a modest firming action might well have outsized effects in financial markets, especially the foreign exchange markets"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Financial conditions and the Asian crisis are significant in the discussion. Members address equity markets, foreign exchange, and potential spillover effects. Discussion is primarily through the lens of macroeconomic impact rather than systemic stability per se.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 55 ±8 [High] | Minutes: 57 ±8 [Moderate]**

**Transcript (scored: 55)**
- GREENSPAN: "I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point. I see that as the most probable next move"
- MEYER: "without changing our view that the next move is still likely to be in a policy tightening direction"
- KELLEY: "the short-term risks seem to me to be more on the downside" -- suggesting near-term caution
- MCDONOUGH: "We may wish to return to asymmetry at some later date"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- next move expected to be up, but timing highly uncertain and data-dependent. Asia introduces significant possibility of easing. Move to symmetric directive signals true two-way flexibility.

**Minutes (scored: 57)**
- "many members commented that...the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening"
- "most members agreed that the need for such a policy adjustment did not appear to be imminent"
- Directive shifted to symmetric: "a slightly higher or a slightly lower federal funds rate might be acceptable"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Forward signal mildly hawkish -- members expect next move to be tightening but are in no hurry and shifted to symmetric directive, acknowledging possibility of easing if Asia deteriorates sharply.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Transcript: 58 ±6 [High] | Minutes: 50 ±7 [High]**

**Transcript (scored: 58)**
- RIVLIN: "The increased uncertainty about world events...has widened our confidence intervals quite a lot, even though we don't know exactly what our confidence intervals are!"
- MEYER: "there is a great deal of uncertainty about the dimension of the Asian turmoil"
- STERN: "more than enough uncertainty to go around. We do not know how severe the Asian problem is going to be. We do not know how far it is going to spread"
- GREENSPAN: discusses widening tails of distributions; "the tails of the various types of distributions...very clearly seem to be getting more extended"

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Uncertainty is elevated and frequently emphasized. Almost every participant highlights heightened uncertainty from Asia. However, domestic economy is familiar ground; it is the international spillover that is uncertain.

**Minutes (scored: 50)**
- "the ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asian economies remained unknown, and more substantial downward pressure on the economies of the United States and its trading partners could not be ruled out"
- "the crosscurrents that were generating the present uncertainties in the outlook for economic activity and inflation made a flexible approach to monetary policy particularly desirable"
- "prevailing near-term uncertainties warranted a cautious wait-and-see policy posture"

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Uncertainty is elevated above normal, driven primarily by the Asian crisis and its unknown spillover effects. Members explicitly cite uncertainty as justification for patience. Moderately elevated -- more than standard caveats but not extreme.
