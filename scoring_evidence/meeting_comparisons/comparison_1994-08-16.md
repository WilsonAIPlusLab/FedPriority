**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "most of them are reporting some input price pressures and, for the first time in a long time, I've heard that now to some extent they are able to pass those price increases through."
- MR. MELZER: "the implicit GDP deflator rose by about 2.8 percent at an annual rate in the first half of 1994 compared to 1.2 percent in the last half of 1993, which is a marked increase indeed."

- "Signs of increasing prices and costs at earlier stages of production appeared to be multiplying, including sizable price increases for a wide range of industrial commodities"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Members see broad inflation as stable but pipeline pressures building meaningfully, with upside risks clearly predominating. Not yet realized in headline measures but perception of building pressure is strong.

**Minority views (minutes):** One member focused on decelerating money growth as evidence policy had already moved substantially to curtail inflation. Competition at retail level limiting pass-through.

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 56 +/-5 [High] | 53 +/-5 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The growth in the last four quarters has been above potential... We are at low unemployment, perhaps at the NAIRU if it really exists."
- MR. PARRY: "most if not all of the unused capacity in labor and product markets has been used up."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Economy operating at or modestly above potential, with strong momentum. Most participants see capacity as fully utilized.

- "Many members observed that they saw the risks as being on the upside of such a projection"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy at or just touching potential, with risk of moving above. The "a bit above" and upside risk language push slightly above 50.

**Minority views (minutes):** "Views varied to some degree with regard to available margins of unemployed resources" — some saw remaining slack, others saw none. Narrow band.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor markets clearly tight with shortages emerging in some areas, though not yet at the severe level. Above balanced but not at late-cycle extreme tightness.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Despite rate hikes since February, financial conditions remain loose. Banks are easing credit, spreads are narrow, markets are accommodative.

**Minority views (minutes):** The rate rises had "some restraining effects" on housing but "not large."

### A6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 71 +/-4 [High] | 72 +/-4 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "another upward notch in rates is clearly called for... I think we clearly demonstrated that the bubble for all practical purposes had been defused, and that we needn't worry about larger increases at this stage."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I'm completely supportive of what you are suggesting" (50bp + discount rate).
- MR. BLINDER: "I'm also supportive of the package... I not only support but support enthusiastically the suggestion of the Chairman."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Near-unanimous support for a 50bp hike with a matching discount rate increase. Decisive hawkish action, moderated by the accompanying pause signal and symmetric directive.

**Minutes evidence:**

- "A smaller step was unlikely to be adequate"
- "A more decisive policy move might reduce the need for further tightening later"
- Unanimous 12-0 vote for 50bp hike

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Strong hawkish consensus for a decisive tightening action. Rejected a smaller move. Unanimous support for 50bp.

### A9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 57 +/-6 [High] | 58 +/-6 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "these actions are expected to be sufficient at least for a time to meet the objective of sustained noninflationary growth."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Forward signal is mildly hawkish — they've tightened decisively and expect a pause, but the door remains open. The symmetric directive and "possibly avert further tightening" language suggest the forward lean is much less hawkish than the current action.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Some genuine uncertainty about capacity and slack, but overall tone is fairly confident about the direction of the economy and the need for policy action. Standard-level uncertainty, somewhat elevated by the capacity estimation difficulty.
