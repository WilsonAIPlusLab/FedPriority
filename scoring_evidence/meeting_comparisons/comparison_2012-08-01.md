### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Participants broadly see inflation as running at or just under the 2 percent target — not a deflation threat but not at or above target either. Near-consensus on this assessment.

Quotes:
- Mr. Fisher: "the trimmed mean has been remarkably steady in the neighborhood just shy of 2 percent... the 12-month rate has flattened out somewhat toward the end of 2011, but it's currently running at 1.9 percent."
- Mr. Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable and does not close for the next two years. And all the while, inflation remains below our 2 percent target."
- Mr. Bullard: "inflation and inflation expectations are nothing like they were in the fall of 2010... as far as I can read the inflation and inflation expectations data, they are really pretty close to our target."

Minority views: Kocherlakota is an outlier arguing inflation will rise above 2.5% by mid-decade under accommodative policy (upside risk view). Pushes uncertainty band modestly wider upward.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Participants broadly viewed inflation as running below target with subdued outlook, driven by resource slack, stable expectations, and falling energy prices.

Quotes:
- "Consumer price inflation had been low in recent months, as declines in the costs of crude oil were passed through to retail energy prices."
- "Inflation was expected to remain subdued, on balance, over coming quarters. In explaining that outlook, participants cited the lack of upward pressure from labor costs and prices of imported commodities as well as the stability of inflation expectations."
- "Most members continued to anticipate that...inflation would run over the medium term at a rate at or below the Committee's objective of 2 percent."

Minority views: "A few expressed concerns that maintaining a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy for an extended period could erode the stability of inflation expectations over time and hence posed upside risks." One member thought economy near potential with upside inflation risk.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Overwhelming majority views the economy as clearly below potential, with a sizeable output gap. The language is "slack remains," "gap not closing," "stuck in the mud." Consistent with 35–40 range.

Quotes:
- Mr. Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable and does not close for the next two years... Simply put, such an outcome is inconsistent with appropriate monetary policy."
- Mr. Powell: "there's a material output gap. I don't know whether it's 4 percent or 2 percent, but it seems highly likely to me that it is material and that we're not making progress toward closing it."
- Mr. Tarullo: "it is much more a matter of the 'stuckness' or the bogged-down quality of the economy... we're at just about the same level of GDP growth."

Minority views: Kocherlakota argues slack is roughly equivalent to early 2004 (a smaller gap). Pushes uncertainty band slightly upward.

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Participants broadly agreed the economy was operating well below potential with substantial slack, though the pace of recovery had decelerated further.

Quotes:
- "A number of participants expressed the view that structural changes in the labor market were not sufficient to explain the high level of unemployment. Those participants saw substantial slack in resource utilization."
- "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to reduce the substantial margin of slack in labor and product markets only slowly over the projection period."
- "Economic activity had decelerated in recent months to a slower pace than they had anticipated."

Minority views: "Several other participants interpreted the moderate pace of the recovery as pointing to a more substantial markdown in the trajectory of potential output" — implying a smaller gap. One member thought the economy "may be operating near its current potential."

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Unemployment at 8.2%. Participants consistently describe significant labor market slack, missing on the employment mandate. Score squarely in the 31–40 range for "below full employment, slack remains."

Quotes:
- Mr. Rosengren: "with a forecast that misses on both elements of the mandate, there should be a strong presumption that we implement alternative A at the next meeting."
- Ms. Yellen: "I anticipate moderate economic growth over coming quarters and no meaningful improvement in the labor market."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "we are missing badly on only one side of our dual mandate" (employment).

Minority views: Kocherlakota argues utilization measures suggest slack equivalent to early 2004 — less slack than consensus.

---

**Minutes — Score: 34 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The labor market was clearly loose with elevated unemployment, slow hiring, and no expectation of reaching full employment for years.

Quotes:
- "The unemployment rate in June remained at an elevated level."
- "Participants generally anticipated that the unemployment rate would decline only slowly toward levels...consistent with the Committee's mandate."
- "Many members expected that at the end of 2014, the unemployment rate would still be well above their estimates of its longer-term normal rate."

Minority views: Some participants argued structural unemployment was elevated due to skill mismatches, which would imply somewhat less cyclical slack.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Weak incoming data described across the board; the economy is "cruddy" but not collapsing. Most expect modest improvement. Score in the "below trend, sluggish" range.

Quotes:
- Ms. Duke: "I term it as 'cruddy.'"
- Mr. Plosser: "the BEA's advance estimate for 2012 quarter-two real GDP came in a little stronger than what was projected in the July Tealbook... the economy does not look particularly worse."
- Mr. Lockhart: "despite the weak incoming data, my outlook has not worsened materially since the last meeting. I continue to expect some strengthening in economic growth over coming quarters."

Minority views: Plosser is more optimistic, maintaining a 3% forecast for 2013–14.

---

**Minutes — Score: 36 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Consumer spending decelerated notably while manufacturing softened and exports weakened; business investment continued to advance but was offset by broad weakness elsewhere. Net assessment is below trend.

Quotes:
- "Consumer spending had slowed considerably since earlier in the year."
- "Many participants reported that...manufacturing activity in their Districts was slow or had declined in recent months."
- "Contacts in several Districts indicated that export demand had weakened as a result of the slowdown in economic activity in Europe; Asia; and some emerging market countries."

Minority views: "A couple cited new autos and tourism as areas of relative strength." Energy investment expanding.

---

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 32 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Safe-haven flows suppressing Treasury yields; corporate borrowing costs at historic lows. Some European risk-spread concern but not dominating domestic conditions.

Quotes:
- Mr. Bullard: "rates are already low. We're benefiting from an extreme flight-to-safety effect globally."
- Mr. Plosser: "save-haven flows have done far more to lower long-term rates than we could ever hope to do with any likely LSAP."
- Mr. Fisher (citing TI issuance): "three-years going at 0.45 percent and the seven-years at 1.65 percent... the cheapest corporate security ever issued in history."

Minority views: Yellen notes that under a European shock, "overall financial conditions would be markedly tighter due to the widening of risk spreads" — flagging a tail risk but not current conditions.

---

**Minutes — Score: 44 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Domestic financial conditions were somewhat loose/accommodative overall, though pockets of tightness persisted for some borrowers and global uncertainty from the euro area created some caution.

Quotes:
- "Participants generally agreed that conditions in domestic credit markets remained more favorable than they were a year ago."
- "Broader financial market conditions seemed reasonably accommodative." Banks seeing increases in mortgage, C&I, and consumer lending.
- "Still-tight borrowing conditions for some households and firms" cited as a headwind. "Credit risk spreads—while still above pre-recession norms..."

Minority views: Euro-area spillover concerns raised by several participants add uncertainty about whether conditions could tighten.

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 20 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The dominant stance is dovish — even those supporting alternative B do so as a tactical delay, not a rejection of easing. The majority anticipates/favors QE3 at the September meeting. This is squarely in the QE-era dovish range (15–25).

Quotes:
- Mr. Williams: "I strongly favor alternative A... The news since then confirms that progress toward our policy goals will likely remain stalled unless we take further, more-significant action."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "my default would be to move in September along the lines of alternative A unless we see some meaningful improvement in the economic outlook."
- Ms. Yellen: "barring such improvements, I think we should definitely take action along the lines of alternative A... I would also strongly endorse the launching of an open-ended LSAP program at the September meeting."

Minority views: Plosser and Lacker are skeptical of further easing. Lacker opposes even the forward guidance language. Fisher expresses the costs of further balance sheet expansion.

---

**Minutes — Score: 24 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The strong majority favored additional accommodation in the near term, with active discussion of QE, forward guidance, and IOR as tools. Policy stance was firmly dovish, characteristic of the QE-era lean.

Quotes:
- "Many members judged that additional monetary accommodation would likely be warranted fairly soon unless incoming information pointed to a substantial and sustainable strengthening in the pace of the economic recovery."
- "A number of them indicated that additional accommodation could help foster a more rapid improvement in labor market conditions in an environment in which price pressures were likely to be subdued."
- Extensive discussion of new LSAP program, forward guidance extension, and IOR reduction as policy tools.

Minority views: "Several members noted the benefits of accumulating further information" (wanting to wait). "One member judged that additional accommodation would likely not be effective." One member thought economy near potential.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: No formal dissents recorded, but the meeting shows significant substantive disagreement — about economic assessment (supply vs. demand shocks), LSAP efficacy, forward guidance design, and urgency of action. Distinct camps with different prescriptions qualify as "notable disagreement" (46–60).

Quotes:
- Distinct camps are visible: Williams/Evans/Rosengren/Tarullo/Kocherlakota explicitly prefer alternative A (act now); Yellen/Duke/Raskin support B but lean strongly toward A in September; Lacker/Plosser are skeptical even of B's forward guidance.
- Mr. Lacker: "I'd oppose the inclusion of forward-guidance language in alternative B at this meeting."
- Mr. Kocherlakota: disagrees with the consensus forecast on normative grounds regarding the balanced approach, while also arguing there's less slack than most.

Minority views: The divergence between Kocherlakota (more accommodation needed for credibility reasons) and Plosser/Lacker (skeptics of further action) represents nearly irreconcilable frameworks, but both sides ultimately accept B, keeping formal dissent at zero.

---

**Minutes — Score: 43 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Moderate disagreement across multiple dimensions — the output gap assessment, the timing and form of additional accommodation, and the efficacy/costs of asset purchases. No formal dissents indicated but distinct camps visible.

Quotes:

- Policy: "Many members judged that additional monetary accommodation would likely be warranted fairly soon" vs. "Several members noted the benefits of accumulating further information" vs. "One member judged that additional accommodation would likely not be effective."
- LSAP: "Many participants expected that such a program could provide additional support" vs. "others questioned the possible efficacy."

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial stability and market functioning are discussed meaningfully by several participants, but primarily as considerations in the LSAP design discussion rather than as stand-alone topics. European financial stress is mentioned as a downside risk but not analyzed in depth.

Quotes:

---

**Minutes — Score: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial stability received moderate attention, primarily through the lens of euro-area spillover risks and, secondarily, through concerns about whether prolonged accommodation could create stability risks. It was a meaningful part of the discussion but not dominant.

Quotes:

- "Several participants indicated that recent trends in euro-area equity indexes and sovereign debt yields had not been encouraging."

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 11 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The forward signal is unambiguously dovish — "at least through late 2014" at the ZLB, plus a strong conditional commitment to QE3 in September. This falls in the 8–15 range for "strong easing bias / signal further accommodation ahead." The meeting explicitly sets up September as the likely launch of QE3.

Quotes:
- The Committee retains "at least through late 2014" forward guidance and adds language signaling willingness to provide "additional accommodation as needed."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "my default would be to move in September along the lines of alternative A unless we see some meaningful improvement in the economic outlook."
- Ms. Yellen: "barring such improvements, I think we should definitely take action along the lines of alternative A" — framing September LSAP as near-certain absent improvement.

---

**Minutes — Score: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The forward signal was strongly dovish — imminent additional easing through QE and/or enhanced forward guidance, with some participants pushing for threshold-based commitments. This is characteristic of pre-QE3 signaling.

Quotes:
- "Many members judged that additional monetary accommodation would likely be warranted fairly soon."
- Discussion of "an extension of the period over which the Committee expected to maintain its target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to ¼ percent."
- "A few participants...suggested that the Committee should consider replacing the calendar date with guidance that was linked more directly to the economic factors" — i.e., threshold-based guidance (Evans Rule precursor).

Minority views: "A few" questioned forward guidance conditionality or wanted to omit it entirely, but these were tactical disagreements, not hawkish signals.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

Center of gravity: Many participants explicitly emphasize elevated uncertainty — about the output gap, LSAP efficacy, European contagion, fiscal cliff, and the appropriate policy path. This is genuine uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging. Score in the 61–75 range for "high: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range."

Quotes:

- Mr. Powell: "I hold that view with substantial uncertainty" (re: efficacy of current channels).

---

**Minutes — Score: 63 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Uncertainty was explicitly described as unusually high by the broad membership, driven by euro-area risks, U.S. fiscal cliff concerns, and global growth slowdown. This is genuine elevated uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging.

Quotes:
- "Members generally attached an unusually high level of uncertainty to their assessments of the economic outlook."
