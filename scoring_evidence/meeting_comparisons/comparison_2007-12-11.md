### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The majority perceive inflation as modestly above 2% or at risk of moving above — primarily from energy, food, and dollar pass-through — but well-contained expectations and a slowing economy cap the upside risk. Headline is clearly elevated; core is borderline.

Quotes:
- Lacker: "Core PCE inflation has edged up over the past two months, and it now appears likely to move above 2 percent for a time... the behavior of energy and food prices over the past several years calls into question the standard presumption... that overall inflation is going to return to the core inflation trend."
- Plosser: "In every three-month window [from March-June], the inflation rate has risen monotonically, now reaching 2.26 percent for the latest three-month period from August to October." CFO survey shows firms planning 2.8% price increases, up from 2%.
- Fisher: Wal-Mart CEO said "I'm totally used to deflation. Deflation is finished." Frito-Lay seeking 5.5% increase.
- Mishkin (minority): "I really do not see the substantial upside risk that a lot of people are talking about... inflation seems to be moving around 2 percent."

**Minutes — Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants perceive inflation as modestly above target with upside risks from commodities and the dollar, but expect gradual moderation. Current readings slightly worse than expected but not alarming.

Quotes:
- "Recent readings on inflation generally were seen as slightly less favorable than in earlier months, partly due to upward revisions to previously published data."
- "Participants remained concerned about upside risks to inflation stemming from elevated prices of energy and non-energy commodities; some also cited the weaker dollar."
- "Most participants continued to anticipate some moderation in core and especially headline inflation over the next few years."
- "Some participants said that higher input costs and rising prices of imports were leading more firms to seek price increases." [minority/upside view]

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus that growth is running and projected to remain below potential; recession probability elevated (Blue Chip at 40%, Mishkin near 50%); all participants use "below potential" language or expect it imminently.

Quotes:
- Yellen: "My modal forecast foresees the economy barely managing to avoid recession, with growth essentially zero this quarter and about 1 percent next quarter. I expect growth to remain below potential throughout next year."
- Geithner: "In our modal forecast we now expect several quarters of growth below potential with real GDP for '08 a bit above 2 percent."
- Bernanke: "Growth in private domestic final demand is projected by the Greenbook to fall to minus 0.2 percent in the fourth quarter and another minus 0.3 percent in the first quarter of next year."
- Plosser (minority): economy "though it will grow below its potential, can weather these forces."

**Minutes — Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is at or slightly below potential, with clear deceleration underway. Not yet in deep recession territory but weakening, with participants expecting sluggish growth.

Quotes:
- "Incoming information pointed to a somewhat weaker outlook for spending than at the time of the October meeting."
- "Growth in late 2007 and during 2008 was likely to be somewhat more sluggish than participants had indicated in their October projections."
- "Some data, including readings on the labor market, suggested that the economy retained forward momentum."
- Range spans from those seeing retained forward momentum to those fearing an unfavorable feedback loop leading to sharper contraction.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 76 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are severely tight in key markets — mortgage markets, leveraged lending, ABCP, interbank term funding. Major financial institutions are sidelining balance sheets. However, credit to investment-grade nonfinancial firms and Main Street businesses remains partially available, preventing a score at the extreme end.

Quotes:
- Geithner: "Financial institutions have seen a sharp increase in their cost of funds, a substantial shortening in maturities at which they borrow, and a significant reduction in their ability to liquidate or borrow against their assets."
- Kohn: "Spreads on corporate bonds have widened sufficiently to actually increase borrowing costs for both investment-grade and junk-bond issuers... secondary markets for nonconforming mortgages remain moribund, with no signs of life."
- Yellen: "The shadow banking sector—the set of markets in which a variety of securitized assets are financed by the issuance of commercial paper—is all but shut for new business."
- Plosser, Lacker, Evans (minority): creditworthy nonfinancial borrowers still have access to credit.

**Minutes — Score: 70 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened significantly, especially in mortgage and interbank markets, with widening credit spreads. However, the broader economy has not yet experienced a full credit freeze — nonfinancial firms and most consumers still have access to credit.

Quotes:
- "Lenders to tighten further the terms on new extensions of credit, not just in the troubled markets for nonconforming mortgages but, in some cases, for other forms of credit as well."
- "Pronounced widening in term spreads for periods extending through year-end" and "diminished liquidity in interbank markets."
- "The cost of credit to most nonfinancial firms remained relatively low" and "consumer credit remained readily available for most households."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 32 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The entire committee favors easing — zero voices for holding or tightening. Disagreement is only between 25bp (majority) and 50bp (vocal minority). The center of gravity is clearly in "moderate dovish" territory.

Quotes:
- Unanimous preference for easing. Policy go-around produces 25bp cut (Alt B) with near-unanimous support.
- Yellen: "I also favor alternative A, a 50 basis point rate cut... this argues for doing so now rather than taking a 'wait and see' approach."
- Kohn: "I think rates are too high right now to support the economy's remaining near full employment and higher than they need to be to contain inflation."
- Lacker (most cautious): "I favor a ¼ point reduction... I would not be surprised if we want to reduce the funds rate another ¼ point at the end of January."

**Minutes — Score: 34 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderately dovish — the Committee is cutting rates but cautiously, balancing downside growth risks against lingering inflation concerns. The 25bp cut (rather than 50bp) and attention to inflation risks temper the dovishness.

Quotes:
- "Members judged that the softening in the outlook for economic growth warranted an easing of the stance of policy at this meeting."
- "Nearly all members judged that a 25 basis point reduction... would be appropriate."
- "Some inflation pressures and risks remained, including pressures from elevated commodity and energy prices and the possibility of upward drift in the public's expectations of inflation."
- Some members noted risk of needing "substantial further easing," while others noted rate cuts "might" need to be reversed.
