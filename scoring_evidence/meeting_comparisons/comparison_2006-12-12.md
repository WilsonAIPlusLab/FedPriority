### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 63 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly agree that core PCE inflation (running ~2.2–2.4%) is above comfort zone and that a gradual decline is expected but not certain, with risks skewed upward — clearly elevated but not in a "highest in decades" panic zone.

Key quotes:
- Moskow: "our most optimistic models...have core PCE inflation flat at 2.4 percent through 2008. I am still concerned that inflation will be too high for too long; and even though the downside risks to growth have increased, I continue to see inflation as the predominant risk."
- Lacker: "The recent news on inflation has been disappointing yet again. It is now quite difficult to discern any moderating trend in core PCE inflation over the past several months."
- Geithner: "underlying inflation still at uncomfortably high levels and considerable uncertainty about whether we'll see enough moderation soon enough to keep expectations stable at reasonable levels."
- Kohn: "the risks around the expected downward trend in inflation remain skewed to the upside."

Minority views: Poole is more sanguine, viewing the Greenbook forecast as "sensible" with balanced risks. Mishkin sees inflation as "likely to decelerate to somewhere around 2.5% core CPI and 2% core PCE."

---

**Minutes: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants unanimously see inflation as uncomfortably above target with upside risks, though they expect gradual moderation, placing this firmly in the "clearly above target" range.

Key quotes:
- "All meeting participants remained concerned about the outlook for inflation."
- "Nearly all participants viewed core inflation as uncomfortably high and stressed the importance of further moderation."
- "The risks around this desired downward path remained to the upside."

Minority views: None meaningful — near-unanimous concern. The modest improvement since spring provides the lower bound of the uncertainty band.

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 45 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous view that the economy is running slightly below potential in the near term due to housing/auto drag, with recovery expected; the output gap is small but negative.

Key quotes:
- Moskow: "economy growing moderately below potential in '07 and increasing at a pace moderately above potential in 2008."
- Hoenig: "we see growth to be below potential but returning gradually over 2007 into 2008."
- Bernanke (summary): "moderate growth perhaps below potential for the next few quarters but returning to potential growth later next year."

Minority views: Warsh sees upside surprises possible on growth. Plosser and Geithner have higher estimates of potential and expect a quicker return.

---

**Minutes: 48 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is seen as near potential but slightly below, with mixed signals across sectors — housing dragging, services strong.

Key quotes:
- "Participants continued to expect the economy to expand at a rate close to or a little below the economy's long-run sustainable pace."
- "Many participants judged that economic activity in the second half of this year was probably a touch softer than had been expected."
- "Indicators of activity in the services sector implied continued brisk growth."

Minority views: The divergence between spending/production data (softer) and labor data (solid) creates some uncertainty about exact positioning.

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 63 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is clearly tight — unemployment at 4.5%, worker shortages reported across multiple districts, wages rising — with only minor softening signals from temp agencies and some trucking.

Key quotes:
- Fisher: "Mr. Chairman, the labor shortage in the Gulf...is significant. One oil company alone estimates that, for petrochemical purposes, they are at least 20,000 workers short...the unions report that they cannot find workers."
- Plosser: "Business contacts continue to cite difficulty in finding qualified workers, especially for skilled and professional positions. Area employers indicate that, over the past few months, wages have been steadily rising at a pace higher than earlier this year."
- Bernanke: "I see a very strong labor market and a very strong services sector."

Minority views: Poole notes UPS and a large trucking firm cut new driver pay and laid off some drivers; Minehan notes temp hiring growth slowing.

---

**Minutes: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is clearly tight with worker shortages reported, though the downward revision to labor compensation data eased some concerns.

Key quotes:
- "Incoming data and reports from businesses suggested that the labor market remained tight."
- "Many business contacts reported difficulties in recruiting suitably qualified workers, especially for certain types of professional and skilled positions."
- "The possibility that the tightness of the labor market could lead to sustained upward pressure on nominal labor costs was viewed as an upside risk."

Minority views: Some participants' concerns were eased by the labor compensation data revision.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 50 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is solid (above trend), but housing investment is sharply negative and business fixed investment has softened, producing a net position near trend — roughly offsetting positives and negatives with consumer outperforming and investment underperforming.

Key quotes:
- Lacker: "consumer spending keeps chugging along at about 3 percent despite weakening auto sales."
- Warsh: "consumer spending for November, you see little to no deterioration in credit quality... They expected to see growth in November on the order of 4 percent; they saw growth of 5.5 and 6 percent."
- Kohn: "downward revision to private final demand... was in business fixed investment" and "The last data point [for housing starts] was a substantial decline."

Minority views: Pianalto is more concerned about consumption spillover from housing via wealth effects and cash-out refinancing.

---

**Minutes: 46 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is steady at trend while business investment is decelerating, pulling the net assessment slightly below trend.

Key quotes:
- Consumer spending "had expanded at a steady pace in recent months, buoyed by continued gains in employment and by a decline in energy prices."
- "Business investment appeared to have decelerated recently, and surveys and orders data pointed to a relatively slow rise in equipment and software spending over the next few quarters."
- "Increases in nonresidential construction spending could be moderating considerably."

Minority views: Business contacts remained optimistic, and longer-term investment outlook was solid, providing upside uncertainty.

---

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 24 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are unambiguously loose — record leveraged loan issuance, tight credit spreads, abundant capital access, low real rates — placing this firmly in the "loose/accommodative" range of the rubric.

Key quotes:
- Plosser (citing manufacturing CEOs): "from their perspective money was almost free... there's plenty of liquidity and interest rates were not limiting them particularly in any way."
- Fisher: "one CEO... does not see much slowing taking place. He said that the availability of capital is like 'dog droppings in Central Park'—no matter where you step, capital is readily available."
- Moskow: "real interest rates are low across the maturity spectrum, and risk spreads remain narrow. Given the liquidity in the system, it's still hard to see current financial conditions as being that restrictive."

Minority views: Bies raises concerns about subprime delinquencies and MBS risk, but acknowledges bank earnings are "really, really strong." Warsh notes some forward corporate spreads are "widening somewhat" but characterizes this as healthy market discipline.

---

**Minutes: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are described as clearly loose/accommodative, supporting spending across sectors.

Key quotes:
- "Growth in consumer spending was expected to be supported by favorable financial conditions."
- "Ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions."
- "Contacts within the construction sector had reported that commercial real estate activity remained robust, encouraged by...accommodative financial conditions."

Minority views: None noted — descriptions uniformly point to accommodative conditions.

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous hold at 5.25% with a broad majority explicitly preserving hawkish forward-leaning language (inflation as "predominant risk," openness to further tightening). Only Poole advocates a balanced risk statement; Lacker would go even more hawkish in language (Alternative C).

Key quotes:
- Kohn: "I'm very comfortable with continuing to suggest not only that we are focused on inflation but also that interest rates are more likely to rise than to fall, given our focus on inflation."
- Hoenig: "If we get evidence that inflation is not moderating, I would be prepared to support additional tightening. On the other hand, it would take a prolonged period of below-trend growth... to convince me that we should ease."
- Yellen: "I still see inflation as the predominant risk, and therefore, like Governor Kohn, I am comfortable with the risk assessment in section 4 as it stands."

Minority views: Poole's strong advocacy for a balanced statement (arguing the current tightening bias is "not credible" given market expectations) is the sole meaningful dovish dissent.

---

**Minutes: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: A hold with a clear tightening bias — inflation is the predominant concern, and the statement retains the possibility of further firming.

Key quotes:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "All members agreed that the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as desired remained the predominant concern."
- "Members agreed that the statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible."

Minority views: One member wanted bidirectional language in the statement; several noted increased downside growth risks.

---

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 30 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: No formal voting dissent; the rate decision is unanimous. The disagreement is primarily about forward guidance language (Poole's balanced view vs. majority's tightening bias) and to a lesser degree about inflation pace (some more alarmed than others). This is "broad agreement with 0-1 dissents and a few reservations."

Key quotes:
- Poole explicitly and at length advocates for a "symmetrical balance-of-risk statement," stating: "I believe that a balanced risk statement...will best serve us in the period between this meeting and our January meeting." This is a sustained, principled disagreement with the majority.
- Lacker prefers Alternative C over Alternative B on statement language, representing a different (more hawkish-leaning) preference from most.
- Pianalto and Bies express reservations about language.

Minority views: The breadth of statement language debate (Alternative A vs. B vs. C, multiple sub-variants) shows some tactical disagreement, but it does not reflect fundamentally different economic views.

---

**Minutes: 25 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the hold decision with minor differences about risk characterization and statement language. One member's dissent on language is notable but limited.

Key quotes:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "One member did not favor language that referenced only the possibility of additional policy firming."
- "Several members judged that the subdued tone of some incoming indicators meant that the downside risks to economic growth in the near term had increased a little."

Minority views: The one member wanting bidirectional language and several noting increased growth risks represent mild divergence.

---

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 47 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives meaningful discussion from multiple participants — particularly Bies on mortgage credit quality risks and Warsh on capital market conditions — making it a notable topic but not the dominant one. Economic and inflation assessment still commands more floor time.

Key quotes:
- Bies: extensive remarks on subprime ARM delinquencies ("rates that have already reset. We've got more to come"), private-label MBS with "very high early default rates," CDO risk, fraud rate on mortgages tripling.
- Warsh: detailed discussion of leveraged buyout markets, LBO floor valuation, record leveraged loan issuance, PIK notes, high-yield issuance at record levels.
- Fisher: "the ready availability of capital... indicates a potential trip wire."

Minority views: Several participants (Poole, Hoenig, Stern) barely touch financial stability beyond brief mentions of credit spreads or equity prices.

---

**Minutes: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Housing correction is discussed as an economic drag, not as a financial stability threat. Financial conditions are mentioned as loose/supportive. Limited attention to financial stability as a distinct concern.

Key quotes:
- Extensive discussion of the housing market correction and potential spillovers to consumer spending.
- "Accommodative financial conditions" mentioned in context of commercial real estate.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market dysfunction, or financial system stress.

Minority views: None.

---

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 65 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The Committee explicitly retains forward guidance language that signals inflation concern remains the predominant risk and that further tightening remains possible — a mild-to-clear tightening forward signal despite holding rates steady.

Key quotes:
- Kohn: "interest rates are more likely to rise than to fall, given our focus on inflation. The market doesn't believe it, but I think that's where we are."
- Bernanke: "our willingness to raise rates if inflation doesn't moderate significantly."
- Final decision preserves section 4 language with inflation as "predominant concern" and implicit openness to future firming.

Minority views: Poole argues this language is "not credible" and would prefer no directional forward signal. Pianalto suggests the language is "becoming stale."

---

**Minutes: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — maintaining the tightening bias in the statement while on hold, with inflation as the predominant risk.

Key quotes:
- "Members agreed that the statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible."
- "One member...believed that...the statement should emphasize that policy could be adjusted in either direction."

Minority views: One member pushed for a more balanced, bidirectional signal.

---

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 44 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is moderately elevated, primarily centered on the depth of the housing correction, the degree of spillover to consumption, and the pace of inflation deceleration. Not extreme — most participants characterize the basic outlook as on track — but the uncertainty bands are acknowledged to have widened since prior meetings.

Key quotes:
- Geithner: "We still face considerable uncertainty about the outlook for growth and the familiar sources of downside risk."
- Moskow: "Our uncertainty about the outlook has increased somewhat."
- Pianalto: "I am concerned that we don't yet have a good handle on where house prices are headed and how the uncertainties surrounding house prices might affect consumer spending."

Minority views: Poole describes the Greenbook as "very sensible" with balanced risks — expressing relatively low uncertainty. Warsh is more confident about consumer strength and growth upside.

---

**Minutes: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants express elevated uncertainty across multiple fronts — housing correction magnitude, inflation trajectory, and mixed economic signals — using "considerable uncertainty" language twice.

Key quotes:
- "Considerable uncertainty regarding the ultimate extent of the housing market correction meant that spillovers to consumption could become more evident."
- "Considerable uncertainty as to the probable pace and extent of the moderation in core inflation."
- "Recent indicators provided mixed signals about the strength of near-term activity."

Minority views: None — uncertainty is broadly shared.
