### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 32 ±7 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Essentially the entire committee agrees PCE inflation has been running ~1.5% for years and will remain below 2% for at least another year and likely longer; the only debate is whether this represents a serious problem or a transitory/acceptable shortfall.

Quotes:
- Rosengren: "current wage and price growth are significantly below levels consistent with our announced 2 percent inflation target... my own forecast does not have us reaching a core PCE inflation rate of 2 percent any time soon."
- Evans: "Since 2008, PCE inflation has averaged just 1½ percent per year... under my SEP forecast we will have missed our inflation target for ten years. I continue to see this outcome as highly unsatisfactory."
- Kocherlakota: "inflation has already been below target for over 30 months... the staff projection is that inflation will be below target in each of the next four years."
- Fisher (more optimistic end): "Dallas Fed trimmed mean PCE inflation has averaged 1.65 percent over the past year... will rise to just shy of 2 percent in 2015."

Minority views: Fisher and Lacker argue the below-target reading is supply-driven (oil) and transitory; they would score inflation pressure somewhat higher (maybe 38–40) if the question were about medium-term trajectory.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±7 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see inflation clearly below target and expected to decline further near term, driven by energy price declines and dollar strength, with no wage pressure emerging and downside risks to inflation persistence.

Quotes:
- "Inflation was continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective, reflecting in part continued reductions in oil prices and falling import prices."
- "Most participants saw no clear evidence of a broad-based acceleration in wages."
- "Participants generally anticipated that inflation was likely to decline further in the near term, reflecting the reduction in oil prices and the effects of the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar on import prices."
- "A number of participants saw a risk that it could run persistently below their 2 percent objective."

Minority views: "A couple of others noted that if the unemployment rate continued to decline quickly, wage and price inflation could rise more than generally anticipated."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 46 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The committee sees growth running above potential (3–3.5%+ vs. potential of ~2–2.5%), with a small output gap remaining (George estimates ~1%) that is rapidly closing; several say the economy is approaching or near potential, but not yet above it in level terms.

Quotes:
- Williams: "I continue to expect real GDP growth to run well above potential output through next year."
- George (Kansas City): "This measure shows the current output gap in the range of about 1 percent. Assuming trend growth of 2 percent, a growth rate of 3 percent over the next year will eliminate the excess capacity in the economy."
- Bullard: "GDP growth has likely been in excess of 4 percent at an annual rate over the second and third quarters of 2014... we're about as close as the Committee has ever been to our goal variables."
- Chair Yellen: "most of you are anticipating above-trend growth... growth looks like it's running near 3½ percent in the second half of the year."

Minority views: Williams explicitly says "well above potential," which would push toward 51–53, but the level gap is still positive (not closed), keeping the center below 50.

---

**Minutes — Score: 44 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is expanding moderately and approaching potential, but underutilization persists — placing the output gap slightly below the midpoint with the gap narrowing.

Quotes:
- "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate pace."
- "Participants judged that the underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish."
- "Several participants, pointing to indicators of consumer and business confidence as well as to the solid record of payroll employment gains in 2014, suggested that the real economy may end up showing more momentum than anticipated."

Minority views: Several saw possible upside momentum; a few thought the energy price boost could be "quite large."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 44 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The committee broadly agrees the labor market is approaching full employment (U-3 at 5.8%, near NAIRU), with exceptional payroll growth and improving JOLTS indicators, but residual slack remains in U-6, part-time employment, and muted wage growth; the market is very close to — but not yet at — full employment.

Quotes:
- Brainard: "Payroll employment is now averaging above 280,000 per month... other margins of slack also showing improvement. The participation rate has been about flat since the spring... The number of employees working part time for economic reasons has moved noticeably lower in the past three months."
- Lacker: "The November employment report brought average monthly payroll employment gains over the past 12 months to 228,000... quits are up 20 percent year-over-year."
- Rosengren: "With U-6 and long-term unemployment still elevated, defining full employment with U-3 historical estimates may be why we are still seeing unusually low wage and price inflation."
- Chair Yellen: "wages gains still look pretty tepid... on a 12-month basis, average hourly earnings have barely edged above 2 percent."

Minority views: Fisher (wants higher score — sees strong momentum) vs. Kocherlakota and Rosengren (see more residual slack given flat Phillips curve and weak wages).

---

**Minutes — Score: 42 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is improving meaningfully across multiple indicators but has not yet reached full employment, with most participants seeing remaining slack and no broad wage acceleration.

Quotes:
- "Participants saw broad-based improvement in labor market conditions over the intermeeting period, including solid gains in payroll employment, a slight reduction in the unemployment rate, and increases in the rates of hiring and quits."
- "The levels of these measures suggested to some participants that there remained more labor market slack than was indicated by the unemployment rate alone."
- "Most participants saw no clear evidence of a broad-based acceleration in wages."

Minority views: "A few others continued to view the unemployment rate as a reliable indicator of overall labor market conditions and saw a narrower degree of labor underutilization remaining." "A few participants suggested that the recent uptick in the employment cost index or average hourly earnings could be a tentative sign of an upturn in wage growth."

Quotes:

- Fisher: "7-Eleven... their average demography is about $48,500 in terms of income level... Their same-store sales are running at 4½ percent November over November" [cites energy savings boosting consumption]; also cites strong capex among manufacturers.
- Plosser: "I project that consumer spending will grow slightly above trend next year. I anticipate that business investment will grow at about trend."
- Brainard: "The data on consumer spending... suggest that second-half GDP is increasing at above a 3 percent pace... But we still see weakness in single-family housing."

Minority views: Lockhart notes Q4 GDP tracking at ~2.5% (step-down from Q2/Q3 above 4%); housing weakness is a drag noted by multiple participants.

**Minutes — Score: 55 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Transcript — Score: 57 ±9 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The large majority supports the transition toward normalization (liftoff likely in 2015, most probably June), a lean hawkish stance — maintaining accommodation for now but clearly signaling the era of near-zero rates is ending; 2 participants dissent toward more aggressive hawkishness and 1 toward more accommodation.

Quotes:

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 62 ±7 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The forward guidance signal is a mild-to-moderate tightening lean — the language explicitly moves toward normalization, identifies 2015 as the liftoff year, and signals "patient" means only about 2 more meetings before the possibility of action; this is clearly pointing the direction of travel toward tightening while retaining data dependence.

Quotes:
- Chair Yellen: "Based on its current outlook, the Committee judges it unlikely to begin the normalization process for at least the next couple of meetings... April and later meetings are completely live possibilities."

- Multiple speakers (Williams, Mester, Plosser, Bullard): consensus that June 2015 is the modal liftoff expectation.
- The transition from "considerable time" to "patient" is itself a hawkish signal — it marks the active preparation phase for normalization.

Minority views: Evans/Kocherlakota would prefer more dovish forward guidance; Fisher/Plosser want more explicitly hawkish signal.

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±7 | Confidence: High**
