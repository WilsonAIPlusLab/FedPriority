### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 57 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The consensus sees core PCE inflation modestly above preferred levels (running ~2.0–2.2%), with genuine upside risk from energy pass-through, but broad confidence that it will moderate — placing inflation clearly above the midpoint but not at an alarming level.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "core PCE inflation over the past twelve months—at 1.9 percent—has come in higher than I would like to see."
- Geithner: "Underlying inflation is still somewhat higher than we would be comfortable with over time. The core indexes are running above levels said to define our preference over time."
- Lacker: "The fourth-quarter core PCE price index came in at 2.2 percent, 0.3 above the Greenbook's estimate... This forecast represents a bulge that is somewhat more extended than I would like to see."
- Fisher (downside): Contacts report "no pricing power," price rollbacks from Procter & Gamble/Kimberly Clark; "There's no pricing on the upside."

Minority views: Fisher's extensive contact-based evidence of no pricing power and even price rollbacks provides the dovish offset. Yellen expects only 1.75% core PCE once energy pass-through is discounted.

---

**Minutes — Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants perceive modest upside inflation risks — core inflation near target but with upward pressures from energy and commodities, partially offset by subdued pass-through and stable expectations.

Representative quotes:
- "Most participants expected core inflation to move up slightly in the near term, reflecting some pass-through of increased energy and other commodity prices."
- "Some members, the possibility of additional policy moves was reinforced by readings on core inflation and inflation expectations that were somewhat higher than was desirable over the long run."
- "The pass-through of higher energy and other commodity prices to prices of core goods and services had remained subdued."

Minority views: Some members see inflation readings as "somewhat higher than desirable"; others note pass-through remains limited.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Virtually all participants view the economy as operating near or very slightly above potential — the weak Q4 GDP is treated as a temporary anomaly, with the baseline expectation being a return to trend-or-slightly-above growth in 2006.

Representative quotes:
- Kohn: "the economy probably is operating in level terms somewhere in the neighborhood of its long-run, sustainable potential and will continue to do so over the next two years with growth broadly in line with the growth of potential."
- Yellen: "The economy is near full employment with real GDP tending toward trend-like growth."
- Hoenig: "growth will be about 3¾ percent in 2006, about ½ percentage point above trend, and will return to trend in 2007."

Minority views: Hoenig's slightly above-trend view and Geithner's "slightly above trend in '06" push modestly higher than exact potential.

Representative quotes:
