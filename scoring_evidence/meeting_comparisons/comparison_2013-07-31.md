## Part A: Participant Discussion

Scale reference: 1 = extreme dovish/low/loose; 100 = extreme hawkish/high/tight; 50 = neutral/historical midpoint.

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]** | **30 ±8 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Bullard: "PCE core inflation measured from one year earlier is about 1.1 percent. I regard 1 percent as an outer bound of where the Committee should be in key measures of inflation when they are below our target of 2 percent."
- Rosengren: "both total and core PCE inflation over the past year are close to 1 percent... we have observed little evidence that we are clearly on the path to our 2 percent target."
- Kocherlakota: "PCE core inflation has averaged less than 1½ percent per year over the past three years. That's the lowest three-year average in half a century."
- Bernanke summary: "inflation remains quite low both in the total and in the core. Special factors have pushed core inflation down."

**Transcript center of gravity**: Virtually all participants agree inflation is well below the 2 percent target, with the dominant concern being that it may persist there or fall further. The discussion culminates in an explicit statement change to acknowledge "inflation persistently below its 2 percent objective could pose risks to economic performance."

**Minutes evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective."
- "A number of others... viewed the low inflation readings as largely reflecting persistently deficient aggregate demand, implying that inflation could remain below 2 percent for a protracted period."
- "A few participants, who felt that the recent low inflation rates were unlikely to persist... suggested that... inflation could be expected to return to 2 percent reasonably quickly."

**Minutes center of gravity**: Inflation is clearly running below the 2% target, with a meaningful contingent of participants concerned it could remain below target for a protracted period due to persistent demand weakness, while only a few see the shortfall as temporary.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **37 ±5 [High confidence]** | **38 ±5 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Lacker: "U.S. economic growth over the next few years is likely to continue at about the pace we've seen since the recovery began... real GDP expanding at about 2 percent."
- Dudley: "I remain less optimistic about the prospects for a pickup in economic growth in the second half... I think we'll be lucky to get annualized real GDP growth as high as 2½ percent in the second half."
- Evans: "there is nothing really tangible in the data at the moment that points to an acceleration in economic activity. This seems to me to be a forecast of hope again."
- Bernanke summary: "confidence about a near-term pickup is not particularly high."

**Transcript center of gravity**: The economy is growing at 2–2.5 percent, below its potential growth rate. Multiple participants explicitly acknowledge the "third year of forecast disappointment" pattern. No one suggests the economy is running at or above potential.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "economic activity expanded at a modest pace during the first half of the year"
- "a number of participants indicated that growth in economic activity during the first half of this year was somewhat below their earlier expectations"
- "Participants generally continued to anticipate that the growth of real GDP would pick up somewhat in the second half of 2013 and strengthen further thereafter"

**Minutes center of gravity**: The economy is operating below potential with a modest pace of expansion, slack remaining in labor and product markets, and fiscal restraint serving as a headwind, though participants expect gradual improvement.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **37 ±5 [High confidence]** | **35 ±5 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Kocherlakota: "prime-age workers [fraction with a job] is still well over 5 percent below its level in early 2007... the unemployment rate, adjusted for [cyclical participation gap], is really still above 8 percent."
- Dudley: "job-finding rate, job-to-job transition rate, hiring rate, quit rate, vacancy-to-unemployment rate — all of these measures are essentially unchanged from where they were in 2012 and are well below the pre-recession levels."

**Minutes evidence**:

**Minutes center of gravity**: Despite solid payroll gains, the labor market remains clearly weak with elevated unemployment, low participation, and high involuntary part-time employment. Multiple indicators point to substantial remaining slack.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **58 ±7 [High confidence]** | **42 ±10 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- Evans: "financial conditions have become more restrictive than they have been for quite a while... Our communications and actions from here need to be extremely careful to not make financial conditions even more restrictive than we inadvertently engineered in June."
- Kocherlakota: "the National Association of Realtors now reports that the recent run-up in mortgage yields has pushed down housing affordability to the historically unusually low levels we last saw in the fall of 2010."
- Dudley: "financial market conditions have tightened considerably since May, more than we want or than we anticipated. The backup in long-term interest rates... has been driven by several factors."
- Bernanke summary: "financial conditions, including higher interest rates, have become more restrictive, which is a concern."

**Transcript center of gravity**: The post-May taper tantrum has led to meaningful tightening in long-term rates and mortgage markets. However, credit availability remains broadly supportive (SLOOS positive, banks well-capitalized, CCC spreads narrow). The tightening is real but not severe.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "some participants felt that, as a result of recent financial market developments, overall financial market conditions had tightened significantly, importantly reflecting larger term premiums"
- "Several others, however, judged that the rise in rates was likely to exert relatively little restraint, or that the increase in equity prices and easing in bank lending standards would largely offset the effects"
- "Some participants also stated that financial developments... might have helped put the financial system on a more sustainable footing, insofar as those developments were associated with an unwinding of unsustainable speculative positions"

**Minutes center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened from very accommodative levels due to the post-June rate rise, but participants are divided on the significance, with some seeing meaningful tightening and others noting offsets from equity gains and easier bank lending. Wide ±10 band reflects the spread.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **47 ±6 [High confidence]** | **32 ±7 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:

**Minutes evidence**:

**Minutes center of gravity**: The committee maintains its strongly accommodative stance with $85B/month in purchases, but the lean is clearly toward beginning to taper in the near term, making this moderately dovish with a normalization trajectory emerging.

**Minutes center of gravity**: The forward signal remains firmly accommodative — threshold-based guidance keeps rates at zero, with discussion of potentially lowering thresholds or adding reinforcement that rate hikes would be distant even past thresholds.
