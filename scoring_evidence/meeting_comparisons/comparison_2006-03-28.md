### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 59 ±7 | Minutes: 55 ±7**

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Ms. Yellen: "Core inflation is pretty steady although, at least by several measures and particularly the core PCE, it is in the top half of the range that I would like to see."
- Ms. Pianalto: "core inflation is currently running at a rate higher than I want to see."
- Mr. Moskow: "the Greenbook forecast has core inflation going up above 2 percent, and I think we don't know exactly how long it would stay at that."
- Mr. Hoenig (minority): "there aren't really inflationary pressures"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The majority of participants perceive core inflation as modestly above their preferred target — in the "top half of the comfort range" — justifying continued tightening but without alarm about imminent acceleration.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "Data over the intermeeting period, including measures of inflation expectations, suggested that underlying inflation was not in the process of moving higher."
- "Some participants held that core inflation and inflation expectations were already toward the upper end of the range that they viewed as consistent with price stability, making them particularly vigilant about upside risks to inflation."
- "With energy prices remaining high, and prices of some other commodities continuing to rise, the risk of at least a temporary impact on core inflation remained a concern."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Participants see inflation as roughly contained near target but with meaningful upside risks from energy prices and resource utilization; "some" view it as already at the upper end of acceptable.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 55 ±6 | Minutes: 53 ±5**

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Ms. Yellen: "the economy essentially at full employment and growth homing in on potential"
- Chairman Bernanke: "I see the economy as still being basically quite strong, and it needs to moderate to become consistent with its long-run potential."
- Mr. Moskow: "the best forecast is that growth will be near potential or higher this year"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly characterize the economy as at or slightly above potential — solid growth — but expect an orderly transition (led by housing cooling) back toward trend, placing the economy in the 51–58 zone.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "Current levels of labor and product market resource utilization were in a zone consistent with little or no remaining economic slack."
- "The economy could well be producing in the neighborhood of its sustainable potential."
- "The economy seemed to be on track to grow near a sustainable pace."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Participants view the economy as operating at or very slightly above potential — essentially in the neighborhood of balance with a slight positive tilt.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 57 ±6 | Minutes: 55 ±6**

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Ms. Yellen: "the economy essentially at full employment"
- Ms. Pianalto: "the Greenbook is showing pressures in the labor market, and that was one of the central reasons for moving the fed funds path upward"
- Mr. Stern: skeptical of NAIRU but still refers to "resource utilization" as a factor
- Chairman Bernanke (implicitly): policy calibration to "resource utilization" throughout

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Most participants see labor markets as tight — near or at full employment — though Pianalto notes she is "not seeing those same [labor market] pressures" in her district, which introduces some uncertainty.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "The labor market was now showing some signs of tightness, consistent with a relatively low jobless rate."
- "There were anecdotal reports of shortages of skilled labor in a few sectors, such as health care, technology, and finance."
- "Participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained. Pressures on unit labor costs appeared contained."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The labor market is modestly tight — showing "some signs of tightness" with scattered shortages, but compensation growth remains moderate and unit labor costs are contained.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Transcript: 55 ±7 | Minutes: 59 ±6**

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Chairman Bernanke: "the economy as still being basically quite strong"
- Ms. Pianalto: "solid growth going forward"
- Ms. Minehan: "orderly transition from a higher rate of growth to a somewhat slower rate of growth"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Consumption and investment are described as solid — at or slightly above trend — with the housing sector already beginning to cool, providing the main mechanism for eventual moderation.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "Consumer spending appeared to be growing at a solid pace... Contacts in the retail sector reported strong demand."
- "Business capital expenditures, especially on equipment and software, appeared to have considerable momentum, supported by strong corporate profits, exceptionally liquid balance sheets, and greater business optimism."
- "The economic expansion appeared to be broad-based."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Spending is above trend — consumer demand is "solid," business investment has "considerable momentum," and the expansion is "broad-based." Housing cooling provides a partial offset but does not dominate.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 22 ±8 | Minutes: 22 ±5**
*(Higher score = tighter conditions)*

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Ms. Bies: "there is a tremendous amount of liquidity in the markets. I look at how much we have raised rates, but the fact that there is so much out there that the banks are trying to lend and that other markets are trying to invest tells me that there is a lot of liquidity."
- Chairman Bernanke: "The mortgage rate is currently about the same as it was when we began tightening in June 2004, and it is still providing support to the housing market."
- Mr. Lacker: "forward short real rates are remarkably low"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Despite 14 consecutive 25bp hikes, participants explicitly note that financial conditions remain very loose — abundant liquidity, low long-term rates, and supportive mortgage market.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "Financial market conditions remained supportive of growth, with long-term rates relatively low, risk spreads in corporate debt markets narrow, and banks seeking lending opportunities."
- "Merger and acquisition activity was strong and infusions of private equity continued at a rapid pace."
- "The effects on spending of the substantial increase in short- and intermediate-term rates since June 2004 had probably not yet been fully felt."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are clearly loose and accommodative despite 21 months of rate hikes. Spreads are narrow, banks are actively lending, and M&A/private equity are booming.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 67 ±7 | Minutes: 65 ±6**
*(Higher score = more hawkish)*

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Virtually unanimous support for 25bp hike (B): "I, too, support the 25 basis point increase" (Minehan, Guynn, Yellen, Moskow, Bies, Warsh, Geithner, Bernanke — all)
- Mr. Lacker: "further moves beyond 5 percent might be necessary"
- Mr. Moskow: "I do not think this is the time for us to give a signal that we are about to stop. The likelihood is that we will probably have to go to 5 percent"
- Mr. Hoenig (dissent tone): "I wouldn't dissent over it if I were a voting member, but I would push very hard for signaling that we are just about at the end"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The Committee is firmly in tightening mode — unanimous 25bp hike — with the center of gravity leaning toward at least one more hike (May) and uncertainty about June, placing this squarely in the "moderate hawkish" 61–70 band.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 4¾ percent at this meeting."
- "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near, and some expressed concerns about the dangers of tightening too much."
- "Keeping rates unchanged would run an unacceptable risk of rising inflation."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unanimous 25bp hike with moderate hawkish lean — still tightening but clearly approaching the end of the cycle. The action is hawkish but the forward posture is increasingly cautious.

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**

- "Risk spreads in corporate debt markets narrow, and banks seeking lending opportunities" — noted but not flagged as a concern.
- "An abrupt rise in long-term interest rates... could weigh on both household and business spending."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are discussed but primarily in terms of their implications for growth and inflation, not as a standalone stability concern. The housing discussion has some financial stability flavor but is framed as an economic variable.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 65 ±8 | Minutes: 64 ±7**
*(Higher score = more hawkish/tightening-oriented forward signal)*

**Transcript evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- Chairman Bernanke: "section 4, which says that policy firming may be needed is in fact a flexible statement... it's entirely possible, depending on the intervening data and the evolution of the economy, that we may choose to signal a halt or even to not move in May"
- Statement adopted (alternative B): retains "some further firming may be needed" language
- Mr. Geithner: "I'd rather take the risk at the margin that we're pushing it up a bit than the risk that we end up pushing it down"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The adopted statement retains a mild tightening bias ("some further firming may be needed") while preserving data-dependence. The forward signal is mildly hawkish — further hikes possible but data-dependent.

**Minutes evidence (verbatim quotes):**
- "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near."
- Retained "some further policy firming may be needed to keep the risks... roughly in balance."
- "Some members expressed concern that retention of the phrase... could be misconstrued as suggesting that the Committee thought that several further tightening steps were likely to be necessary."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is mildly hawkish — retaining a tightening bias — but clearly signaling that the cycle is near its end. The guidance leans hawkish but with diminishing conviction.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Somewhat elevated uncertainty — participants acknowledge genuine difficulty in assessing housing trajectory and remaining slack, but overall the tone is fairly confident about the broad outlook.

**Staff Transcript: 53 ±5 | Staff Minutes: 51 ±3**
