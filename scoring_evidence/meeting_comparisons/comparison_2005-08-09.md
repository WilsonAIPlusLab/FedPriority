## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Disc-T (Score: 58)**
- "The upward revisions to core PCE inflation are a serious concern. Apparently, inflationary pressures were higher than we thought when we introduced our 'measured pace' language." (Moskow)
- "The benchmark revision boosts inflation as measured by the core PCE to 1.9 percent over the last 12 months, which is at the top of my comfort zone." (Yellen)
- "Core PCE inflation is now averaging about 2¼ percent this year and is expected to remain at that pace next year... it represents a continued upward adjustment of the inflation outlook and is at the higher end of what most of us have indicated we are comfortable with." (Hoenig)
- "Incoming information about the most recent several months has suggested an appreciable short-term deceleration... inflation is not on an upward track." (Kohn, counterweight)

**Disc-M (Score: 60)**
- "Higher energy prices and reduced resource slack were seen as pointing to elevated inflation pressures."
- "Underlying core inflation appeared to be running at a pace around the upper end of the range they viewed as consistent with price stability."
- "Most participants viewed the risks to inflation as having ticked up over the intermeeting period."
- "Inflation expectations seemed to remain quite well anchored" and "recent monthly readings indicated that core inflation had been subdued." (partial offset)

**Staff-T (Score: 58)**
- "Core PCE price index has been running in the neighborhood of 2 percent or a little above. It has been higher than we thought because of the oil price." (Wilcox)
- "Output above its potential in 2006 and 2007, and core [PCE] running at 2.2 percent in those two years." (Wilcox)
- "We have a good deal more energy price inflation in this projection, which contributes directly and in full force to the overall PCE inflation rate, and seeps through as well—to the tune of 0.1 percentage point—into core inflation next year." (Wilcox)

**Staff-M (Score: 55)**
- "Core CPI and PCE prices decelerated in recent months, after notable increases earlier in the year."
- "The forecast for core PCE inflation was raised somewhat, owing in part to the recent further rise in energy prices."
- "Crude oil prices continued to rise, reaching record levels in nominal terms."

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Disc-T (Score: 55)**
- "We expect growth to be somewhat above potential over the rest of this year." (Moskow)
- "Growth remains above trend... The monetary policy and financial conditions currently in place are poised to support continued above-trend growth. With growth above trend, the output gap and labor market slack will further diminish." (Hoenig)
- "The risk that aggregate demand may be growing above potential at a time when little slack remains in product and labor markets." (Santomero)
- "The economy is probably a little closer to its long-run sustainable production than we had thought" — framing it as approaching potential, not yet clearly above. (Kohn, counterweight)

**Disc-M (Score: 48)**
- "Current estimates of slack were narrower than those reviewed at the June meeting."
- "Modest wage pressures... suggested that some slack remained in resource utilization."
- "The federal funds rate would remain below the level... necessary to... keep output near potential."

**Staff-T (Score: 57)**
- "Leaving the GDP gap at 0.9 percent rather than the 1.1 percent shown in the June Greenbook." (Wilcox)
- "Had we left the funds rate assumption unchanged at its June trajectory, output would have been above its potential in 2006 and 2007." (Wilcox)

**Staff-M (Score: 50)**
- "The output gap was predicted to be essentially closed by the end of this year."
- "Capacity utilization moved up to its highest level since December 2000."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Disc-T (Score: 53)**
- "The economy appears to be at or near full employment now." (Hoenig)
- "A whisker of slack probably still remains... other measures, such as the employment-population ratio, a survey of job market perceptions, and industrial capacity utilization now signal a bit less slack." (Yellen)
- "My contacts say... they have no problem with labor availability and... there is no wage pressure, except in isolated cases." (Poole)
- "Improving gradually means that we're gradually using up what little remaining slack is there." (Kohn)

**Disc-M (Score: 47)**
- "Members ultimately concurred that the description of labor markets as 'improving gradually' remained appropriate."
- "Modest wage pressures, at least by some measures, suggested that some slack remained in resource utilization."

**Staff-T (Score: 52)**
- "We refined our model of the labor force participation rate... those refinements to the model steepened the downward tilt in the trend, [subtracting] a couple of additional tenths from the level of potential GDP by the end of last year." (Wilcox)
- "[Going] forward we anticipate the actual participation rate... the gap in our projection is closed because we have the [trend participation rate declining]." (Wilcox)

**Staff-M (Score: 51)**
- "The unemployment rate dipped to 5.0 percent in June and held steady at that rate in July."
- "The labor market continued to improve gradually."

- "The surprise was not in investment, which continues to be a bit on the weak side relative to fundamentals, but rather in consumption, which is already quite strong." (Kohn)

**Disc-M (Score: 56)**

- "Housing sales and construction activity generally remained strong."

**Staff-T (Score: 53)**

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*(Discussion sources only)*

**Disc-T (Score: 70)**
- "I continue to think that we should raise rates until we are comfortably in the middle of the neutral range. To me, this means reaching a fed funds rate of about 4½ percent before we pause." (Moskow)
- "I think we're a little behind the curve... I believe we are now at a point where the upside risk to the economy is greater than the downside risk." (Hoenig)
- "The move to a less accommodative posture should continue. And if current trends persist, that process possibly should accelerate." (Minehan)
- All participants: unanimous support for 25bp hike; zero advocating pause or cut.

**Disc-M (Score: 70)**
- "All of the members favored raising the target federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3-1/2 percent."
- "The federal funds rate would remain below the level that members anticipated would prove necessary to contain inflation pressures and keep output near potential, and thus in all likelihood further policy action would be required."
- "Continued removal of policy accommodation at a measured pace."

## Notable Divergences

**OUTPUT_GAP — largest cross-source gap (48 to 57):**
- Disc-M scores 48 (slack remaining, not yet at potential); Disc-T scores 55 and Staff-T scores 57 (above potential). Staff-M scores 50 ("essentially closed"). The minutes' participant synthesis emphasizes residual slack language; the transcript captures individual participants using explicit "above potential/trend" phrasing. Staff-T has the sharpest above-potential reading, citing the explicit GDP gap figure of +0.9%.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS — largest same-type source gap (28 vs 55):**
- Disc-T scores 55; Disc-M scores 28. The transcript records Greenspan's extended housing wealth discussion and Bies's full supervisory briefing on ARM/subprime products. The published minutes compress these into "a few participants voiced concerns" without capturing the depth of the Greenspan and Bies presentations.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS — Disc-M (47) vs Disc-T (53):**
- The minutes emphasize the "improving gradually" language and "some slack remained" framing. The transcript captures individual participants (Hoenig: "at or near full employment"; Poole: no labor availability problems) using tighter characterizations.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE — convergence:**
- Disc-T and Disc-M both score HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN at 70. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE is 67 (Disc-T) vs 72 (Disc-M). Both sources produce closely aligned readings on the policy stance and direction, which is the highest-confidence area of agreement.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — tight cross-source convergence (20–27):**
- All four sources score financial conditions in the 20–27 range. This is the most consistent dimension across all sources and source types, reflecting unambiguous "accommodative/loose" language in every document.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE — moderate convergence (55–60):**
- All sources cluster between 55 and 60. Staff-M is the lowest (55) on the basis of benign recent core readings; Disc-M is the highest (60) reflecting the "elevated" language used in the participant synthesis. The spread is within uncertainty bands for all sources.
