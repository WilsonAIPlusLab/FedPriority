### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 32 ±6 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants unanimously recognize current inflation as substantially below the 2% target (~1.2% core PCE), held down by transitory factors (oil, dollar), with the majority cautiously confident of a return to target over 1–2 years and a meaningful minority (Kocherlakota, Evans) deeply skeptical.

Quotes:
- "Total PCE inflation was only 0.1 percent over the last year. Core PCE prices…rose only 1.2 percent." (Chair Yellen)
- "Core PCE inflation over the past year is currently at 1.2 percent, well below what many of us were forecasting at the beginning of this year." (Rosengren)
- "The Tealbook…is that inflation will remain below 2 percent until 2019…market-based outlooks tell a similar low-inflation story over the medium term." (Kocherlakota)
- "Core inflation through May was 1.2 percent, a figure that was partly held down by the pass-through of lower oil prices." (Fischer)

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is running below target, with energy price and dollar stabilization reducing downward pressure, but core remains well below 2%. Participants expect a gradual rise back toward target.

Quotes:
- "Total PCE inflation continued to run below the Committee's objective."
- "Core inflation was still well below 2 percent."
- "Several participants pointed out that the 3-month change in that index had firmed recently, signaling some improvement in the inflation outlook."

Minority views noted in minutes: A couple concerned low inflation might persist and feed into expectations; several noted firming signals in alternative measures.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 44 ±6 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Most participants view the economy as currently below potential but approaching it — weak first half, but expected to rebound; "slightly below potential" is the consensus, not deep below.

Quotes:
- "real GDP is going to expand at only a pretty paltry 1¼ percent annual rate in the first half of the year, even with a decent rebound in activity in the current quarter." (Chair Yellen)
- "disappointing 1¼ percent annual rate over the first half of the year"; "some questions about the sufficiency of growth in demand." (Brainard)
- "above-trend growth later this year and next" — forecasting a return to above-potential, but not yet there. (Mester)
- "solid underlying momentum in fundamentals, I expect growth to average about 2¾ percent over the next several quarters." (Williams)

**Minutes: 44 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is operating modestly below potential, recovering from a weak Q1, with moderate growth expected to close the remaining gap.

Quotes:
- "Economic activity was expanding moderately after little change in the first quarter of the year."
- "Most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization of labor resources."
- "Participants generally expected real GDP to expand at a rate sufficient to continue to move labor market conditions toward levels judged consistent with the Committee's dual mandate."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 50 ±7 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The center of gravity places the labor market very close to full employment — U-3 at 5.5%, widely described as near the natural rate. There is genuine disagreement about residual slack in broader measures, keeping the score at exactly 50 rather than above.

Quotes:
- "In my view, we are near our goal of maximum employment. The economy will no longer be underutilizing labor resources" (by end of 2015). (Mester)
- "unemployment rate is already very close to the natural rate." (Fischer)
- "unemployment rate is now so close to estimates of the natural rate that I seriously doubt any of the differences are statistically significant." (Lacker)
- "labor force participation rate and the share of employees working part time for economic reasons suggest that slack remains." (Brainard/Tarullo)
- "numerous indicators point to some slack still being out there in the labor market, but likely no more than implied by the U-3 unemployment measure." (Williams)

**Minutes: 43 ±7 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving steadily but most see remaining slack. Wages firming but still subdued. Approaching but not yet at full employment.

Quotes:
- "Most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization of labor resources; some of them anticipated that the utilization gap would close around the end of the year."
- "Several other participants indicated that, in their view, labor market slack had already been largely eliminated."
- "Even with the recent upturn, wage increases remain subdued."

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 44 ±7 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is running below trend expectations given fundamental tailwinds (employment gains, low gasoline prices), and business investment is soft (especially drilling). The net picture is slightly below trend, with cautious optimism about H2 pickup.

Quotes:
- "it remains slower than I would expect on the basis of its strong fundamental drivers. The apparent desire of consumers to save rather than spend the windfall generated by lower oil prices puts at risk the consumer-led recovery." (Rosengren)
- "household spending now looks to have expanded at a solid pace during the first half of the year, growth, nonetheless, has slowed noticeably since the second half of last year." (Yellen)
- "business investment has been somewhat disappointing, with drilling activity continuing to contract sharply and orders and shipments of capital goods staying flat." (Yellen)
- "we're in the midst of the longest sustained improvement in household spending growth we've seen in this recovery" (Lacker — more optimistic)

**Minutes: 48 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending at a moderate pace with positive fundamentals, but business investment soft and net exports weak. Net position is roughly at trend with mixed signals across components.

Quotes:
- "Consumer spending was somewhat better than previously reported, rising at a moderate pace in the first quarter."
- "Many participants anticipated that these factors would support a solid pace of consumer spending going forward."
- "The outlook for business investment remained soft, and net exports were likely to continue to be restrained."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 35 ±7 [Moderate]**

Center-of-gravity: Conditions are broadly described as highly accommodative (ZIRP + large balance sheet). There has been some recent modest tightening from the European bond yield backup and the dollar's strength, but the overwhelming characterization is of accommodative conditions.

Quotes:
- "a zero interest rate policy, combined with a large balance sheet, is in fact providing a large and appropriate amount of accommodation." (Fischer)
- "Even after the first increase, monetary policy will remain very accommodative." (Mester)
- "accompanied by a modest amount of tightening of financial conditions in the United States" (from the German bund reversal). (Tarullo)
- "as long as credit availability remains so tight, housing construction will contribute a lot to real GDP growth anytime soon" — tight in mortgage market specifically. (Yellen)
- "we have a yield curve that is quite a bit steeper than normal" — some tightening from bond market backup. (Dudley)

**Minutes: 45 ±6 [Moderate]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are roughly neutral to slightly loose overall. Treasury yields rose but not dramatically. Housing credit remains tight. Greece risk looms as potential tightening catalyst.

Quotes:
- "Several participants commented on the rise in the 10-year Treasury yield."
- "Volatility in U.S. fixed-income markets was still somewhat below pre-crisis levels."
- "Credit availability was still relatively tight" (housing context).
- "Many participants expressed concern that a failure of Greece and its official creditors to resolve their differences could result in disruptions in financial markets."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 57 ±8 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: The unanimous vote is to hold rates at this meeting. The center-of-gravity lean is toward tightening soon (September for most), with a solid majority expecting liftoff before year-end and one participant (Lacker) preferring to hike immediately. A minority prefers waiting into 2016.

Quotes:
- "My preference would be to raise rates at this meeting." (Lacker — most hawkish)
- "In my view, the economy can support a 25 basis point increase in the federal funds rate." (Mester)
- "I still think September liftoff is the most likely, and I think that's appropriate." (Dudley)
- "I remain convinced that the time for liftoff is fast approaching and that waiting much longer…risks falling behind the curve." (Williams)
- "I currently expect those conditions to be met sometime after our September meeting." (Rosengren — most dovish in this context)
- "I have pushed back my preferred appropriate monetary policy liftoff date to midyear 2016." (Evans)

**Minutes: 48 ±7 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Majority favors continued hold with data-dependent approach, but the Committee is clearly approaching the point of normalization. Lean is slightly dovish to balanced with growing hawkish wing.

Quotes:
- "Most participants judged that the conditions for policy firming had not yet been achieved; a number of them cautioned against a premature decision."
- "Some participants viewed the economic conditions for increasing the target range for the federal funds rate as having been met or were confident that they would be met shortly."
- "One member, however, indicated a readiness to take that step at this meeting."

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate differences, with one near-dissenter ready to hike and a couple seeing labor market already at mandate-consistent levels. No formal dissent but meaningful disagreement about the assessment of conditions.

Quotes:

- "One member, however, indicated a readiness to take that step at this meeting but also expressed a willingness to wait another meeting or two."
- "Most members saw room for additional progress in reducing labor market slack, while a couple of members indicated that they viewed the unemployment rate as very close or essentially identical to its mandate-consistent level."

**Transcript: 33 ±8 [Moderate]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives meaningful but secondary attention. Greece is a recurring reference across many speakers as a risk factor, and some discuss broader market dynamics. This is well above a "passing reference" but clearly below the level of a dominant topic.

Quotes:

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [Moderate]**

Quotes:

- "Some participants reiterated the importance of effective Committee communications in reducing the likelihood of an outsized financial market reaction."
- One mention of "adverse consequences for financial and macroeconomic stability" as risk of delayed normalization.

Quotes:

Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is data-dependent with a building tightening bias. The language clearly points toward approaching normalization but does not commit to a specific timeline. Neutral with mild tightening lean.

Quotes:

- "Many expected that labor market underutilization would be largely eliminated around year-end."

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 55 ±8 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty are identified and discussed at length: the inflation path, the credibility of the inflation target, r* estimation, Greek risk, the GDP/employment disconnect, and structural changes in labor and price dynamics. This is clearly elevated above routine uncertainty.

Quotes:
- "I'm uncertain whether inflation will continue climbing after that point and reach our inflation objective within two to three years." (Yellen)
- "The low point forecasts are telling us that the probability that inflation will return to 2 percent over the medium term is under 50 percent." (Kocherlakota)
- "I've become less sanguine about the message from survey measures of long-term inflation expectations…the Chicago DSGE model currently interprets [data] as a string of mostly negative shocks to the inflation benchmark." (Evans)
- "questions about why productivity growth has been so miserable and the extent to which this reflects headwinds from the crisis, which may be reversed, or some more sustained structural change." (Brainard)
- Multiple speakers on r* uncertainty: Evans, Williams, Lacker, Bullard — extensive discussion of equilibrium real rate estimation uncertainty.

**Minutes: 40 ±5 [High]**

Center-of-gravity: Risks seen as balanced overall but with specific identifiable concerns (Greece, China, productivity, Q1 interpretation) elevating uncertainty somewhat above normal.

Quotes:
- "While participants generally saw the risks to their projections of economic activity and the labor market as balanced, they gave a number of reasons to be cautious in assessing the outlook."
- "Several mentioned their uncertainty about whether Greece and its official creditors would reach an agreement and about the likely pace of economic growth abroad, particularly in China."
- "Other concerns were related to whether the apparent weakness in productivity growth recently would be reversed or continue."
