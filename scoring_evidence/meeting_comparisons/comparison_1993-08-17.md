### A1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 40 +/-7 [High] | 35 +/-5 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. MULLINS: "inflation apparently has settled down in the 3 to 3-1/2 percent range. That is somewhat higher, perhaps 1/2 percent or so higher, than we would have expected earlier this year"
- MR. BROADDUS: "Core CPI has risen at an annual rate of about 2-1/2 percent over the last five months, which is a pretty good hunk of time"
- MR. KELLEY: "more and more it seems that last winter's inflation surge was temporary... inflation now seems to be trendless, albeit at much too high a level"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Inflation settling around 3-3.5%, recent readings encouraging but the level remains above comfort zone. No acceleration, some deceleration.

**Minutes evidence:**

- "some slack in labor and capital resources would persist" keeping inflation down

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation contained and below target, with disinflation as the trend, though persistent expectations provide a floor. The environment is consistent with further disinflation.

### A6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 55 +/-7 [High] | 48 +/-4 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher... we have moved tactically, not strategically"
- MR. MULLINS: "probability we will tighten in the intermeeting period is greater than the probability we will ease... two epsilons versus one epsilon"
- MR. ANGELL: "very uneasy with the fed funds rate where it is... we ought to be fully conscious of the opportunity whenever forces are such that we can get the rate closer to... equilibrium"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unanimous hold at 3% funds rate. Move from asymmetric-toward-tightening to symmetric directive, framed as tactical, not strategic. Universal expectation of eventual tightening. No one advocates easing.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "members agreed that recent developments pointed to the desirability of a steady policy course"
- "it now appeared less likely than at the time of the May and July meetings that the Committee needed to bias its consideration...toward possible tightening"
- "monetary policy probably would have to move in the direction of restraint at some point to resist any incipient tendency for inflationary pressures to intensify"
- One member: "monetary policy had been overly stimulative for some time and...the Committee should move toward restraint at the first favorable opportunity"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Hold posture, having moved to a symmetric/unbiased directive from a previous tightening bias. Current lean essentially neutral with slight dovish shift from prior meetings, though future tightening acknowledged as eventually needed.
