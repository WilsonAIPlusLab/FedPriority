**Context**: 5th consecutive 25bp hike; funds rate raised to 2.25%. Economy in mid-cycle normalization from 2001 recession trough. Core PCE ~1.5%, unemployment ~5.4%, dollar depreciating.

## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence Blocks

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 43 ±6
- "Core PCE inflation is 1½ percent over the most recent twelve-month period, and core CPI inflation is 2 percent. We have seen some increases in core inflation over the last couple of months." (Hoenig)
- "Our outlook for inflation is relatively benign. The fact that higher prices for energy and other inputs did not markedly boost core inflation is certainly consistent with this view." (Moskow)
- "Increases in nominal wages and compensation have remained moderate despite the strength in productivity growth in recent years...Markups of prices over unit labor costs have stayed quite high through the third quarter." (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 45 ±7
- "inflation was anticipated to remain subdued"
- "they saw the risks to stable underlying inflation as still balanced"
- "a number of participants cited developments that could pose upside inflation risks" — dollar depreciation, oil prices, productivity slowdown
- "participants generally expected that inflation would remain low in the foreseeable future"

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 42 ±5
- Stockton: core PCE "roughly unchanged at 1½ percent"
- "Stability in core inflation is brought about by several small offsetting effects" — diminishing slack and productivity slowdown push up; energy price reversal and smaller import price gains push down
- Upside risks from pricing power and productivity slowdown described as "potentially more potent," but compensation steady at 4%, expectations anchored

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 44 ±5
- "Core inflation measures remained subdued, albeit running at a slightly higher pace than last year, owing, in part, to the indirect effects of higher energy prices."
- "Consumer prices jumped in October, as hurricane damage contributed to higher prices for food and energy... Core consumer prices increased much more modestly."
- Staff forecast: "core inflation was seen in the staff forecast as remaining stable" with upward and downward pressures roughly offsetting

**Observation**: All four sources converge tightly — scores 42–45. All describe core inflation as subdued/below target (~1.5% PCE) with acknowledged but non-alarming upside risks. Discussion transcript and minutes align in characterizing risks as "balanced." Staff sources agree on "stable" core forecasts.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 46 ±5
- "We continue to expect that growth will average a bit above potential over the forecast period. Our best guess is that there is still some meaningful resource slack in the economy." (Moskow)
- "a smaller output gap — with the gap perhaps now within the margin of error — and demand growth...slightly above trend." (Geithner)
- "growth remains above trend...we approach potential at the rate of about ¾ percentage point per year." (Hoenig)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 45 ±6
- "economy would continue to expand at a pace that would likely prove sufficient to reduce margins of underutilized resources further"
- "an economy still operating somewhat below its potential"
- "quite possible that the economy could soon be operating close to potential"
- "A few participants also noted that uncertainty about the extent of resource slack in the economy was considerable."

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 47 ±5
- Stockton: economy growing above trend; staff raised funds rate path 50bp to prevent "actual real GDP overshooting potential"
- Asset market strength described as creating "greater upward pressure" on activity
- Employment improving; GDP projected to continue above-trend growth with gap closing

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 44 ±5
- "the economy expanded at a moderate pace over the third quarter and into the current quarter"
- Staff forecast: "economic activity projected to expand at a pace a little above that of its longer-run potential over the coming year"
- Staff forecast references "a narrowing margin of resource slack"

**Observation**: All four sources score 44–47, well-clustered. Consistent picture of above-trend growth gradually closing a gap that all sources agree still exists. Staff transcript is the most optimistic (47) given emphasis on the upward revision to the funds rate path to prevent overshooting.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 44 ±5
- "While there likely is still some slack in labor markets, this is being worked off." (Santomero)
- "labor markets...seem to have been delivering an average monthly level of job growth that will actually reduce, if not eliminate, slack in labor markets over the next year and a half to two years." (Minehan)
- "the labor market is beginning to tighten at the upper skill levels." (Poole); "some skills are now in short supply." (Stern)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 43 ±7
- "labor market conditions were generally seen as gradually improving"
- "employment growth had been fast enough to absorb unutilized labor resources over time"
- "significant tightening in the market for skilled workers in some industries and regions, although demand for less skilled workers still appeared soft"
- "wage and compensation increases had not picked up materially and generally remained moderate"

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 42 ±5
- Stockton: hiring "running around 160,000 per month" and "expect it to pick up to around 225,000 per month by the spring"
- October/November gains "noticeably stronger than had been incorporated"
- Initial claims showing "increase over the past two weeks" flagged as a downside risk
- Improvement described as continuing but pace below prior expectations

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 42 ±5
- "The unemployment rate ticked down to 5.4 percent in November."
- "the labor market continued to improve gradually"
- "Gains in employment were moderate in November after a surge in October"
- Employment gains "fairly widespread" but manufacturing "edged lower"

**Observation**: Tight four-way convergence — scores 42–44. All sources agree on the same underlying condition: gradual improvement, slack still present, tightness emerging at the skilled end but broad labor market still below full employment. Wages moderate confirming slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 56 ±6
- "real consumer spending grew by a healthy 5.1 percent annual rate in the third quarter and advanced strongly in the last two months." (Bies)
- "Consumption has proven surprisingly, at least to me, resilient to higher energy prices, supported by rising housing and equity wealth and continued low intermediate- and long-term interest rates." (Kohn)
- "orders and shipments of nondefense capital goods have been trending upward...housing sales and starts have also remained at elevated levels." (Ferguson)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 60 ±6
- "consumers well positioned to maintain a brisk pace of spending"
- "participants generally were upbeat in their assessment of the prospects for investment"
- "fundamentals underlying the demand for capital goods...appeared quite favorable"
- "recent indications of a softening in high-tech spending" noted as a qualifier

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 58 ±5
- Stockton: real PCE "advanced at an annual rate of more than 5 percent in the third quarter" and 3.5% Q4
- Equipment and software: 17% Q3, 11% Q4
- High-tech spending below expectations; auto sales weakening
- Partial-expensing provision described as approaching a "moment of truth"

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 58 ±5
- "Consumer spending was solid, and investment spending remained strong."
- "Shipments of nondefense capital goods stepped up in October...Shipments of computers jumped...shipments moved up considerably in October from their robust third-quarter pace."
- Staff forecast: consumption to grow "at a pace somewhat above that recorded this year"; business investment to "resume vigorous growth"

**Observation**: All four sources score 56–60 — solid above-trend spending. The discussion minutes score is highest (60) reflecting the "upbeat" and "quite favorable" participant characterizations; the transcript is lowest (56) reflecting individual speakers noting pockets of softness in autos and big-ticket items. Staff and minutes converge at 58.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 23 ±5 [FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS]
- "Financial markets are supportive of continuing growth, with equity markets turning up and longer-term interest rates and spreads low. Indeed, the proliferation of hedge funds and the narrowness of credit spreads suggest that liquidity abounds." (Minehan)
- "overall easing of financial conditions over the intermeeting period." (Kohn)
- "real fed funds rate that continues to be near zero, we could unintentionally be encouraging further imbalances." (Guynn)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 20 ±5 [FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS]
- "accommodative financial conditions should keep business spending on a strong uptrend"
- "intermediate- and long-term interest rates...had remained quite supportive of growth"
- "prolonged period of policy accommodation had generated a significant degree of liquidity"
- "quite narrow credit spreads"

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 43 ±6 [FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — distinct construct]
- Stockton: funds rate at 2% with path raised to 2.75% end of 2005 — "accommodative stance of policy" becoming "less accommodative"
- Stock market 4% higher, house prices 5% higher, oil $3 lower — multiple offsetting forces
- Dollar still below prior projection; fiscal policy shifting to roughly neutral
- This score reflects the composite posture including tightening path, not solely current looseness

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 28 ±5 [FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS]
- Staff forecast: "supported by accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions"
- "supportive financial conditions, ample liquid assets in the corporate sector"
- "Yields on both investment-grade and speculative-grade corporate bonds edged lower"

**Observation**: Discussion track (transcript 23, minutes 20) and staff minutes (28) all firmly describe loose/accommodative conditions. The staff transcript score of 43 is not directly comparable — that file's FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS dimension bundles the ongoing tightening trajectory with current conditions, producing a less extreme reading. Focusing on the three comparably-defined scores (23, 20, 28), convergence is strong.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (Discussion track only)

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 70 ±5
- "I would recommend alternative C — go with an increase of 25 basis points in the funds rate...continue our policy of 'opportunistic de-accommodation.'" (Greenspan)
- All voting and nonvoting participants explicitly support the 25bp hike without reservation
- "We should be thinking about continuing to raise rates on a systematic basis — and by more than assumed in the Greenbook." (Hoenig)
- "I continue to be comfortable with the policy path we're on...we still have a considerable way to go to get back to a more neutral stance." (Guynn)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 66 ±4
- "all of the members favored raising the target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points"
- "the current level of the real funds rate target remained below the level it most likely would need to reach"
- "cost and price pressures were likely to become a clearer intermediate-term risk absent further reduction of accommodation"
- "measured" pace language retained

**Observation**: Transcript (70) vs. minutes (66) — a 4-point gap within each source's stated uncertainty band. The transcript captures individual participant enthusiasm (Hoenig's push for faster pace, Guynn's "considerable way to go"), which elevates the transcript score. The minutes present the same unanimous vote but through the measured lens of the official summary.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY (Discussion track only)

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 20 ±5
- Zero formal dissents recorded; all speakers explicitly "support your recommendation"
- Minor tactical differences: Hoenig wants to consider moving faster; Minehan wants cleaner statement language; Guynn thinks "moderate pace" undersells the economy
- "I would note, given the expectation I think we all have that another 25 basis points may be appropriate at the next meeting, that any change in wording is going to send a mixed signal." (Poole)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 18 ±5
- "all of the members favored raising the target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points"
- "all members agreed" on the statement language
- "a few members believed" forward-looking language should be eliminated vs. "more of the members" wanted to keep it — minor tactical difference only

**Observation**: Near-identical scores (20, 18). Both sources confirm unanimous action with only wording-level tactical differences. No substantive policy disagreement recorded in either source.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (Discussion track only)

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 42 ±8
- "Recent gains in house pricing have been astonishing. Visiting bankers have told us that investment demand is playing an increasing role in this market...surely by this point there is a much fatter tail on the side of a considerable softness or even decline in such prices." (Kohn)
- "The substantial run-up in house prices...may be at least partially attributable to unusually low mortgage rates influenced by our very accommodative policy...those developments and the risks associated with the run-up in house prices probably deserve further study." (Guynn)
- "In condominium development in particular, several bankers have reported that the growth in volume of new condo starts is absolutely unsustainable." (Olson)
- "The proliferation of hedge funds and the narrowness of credit spreads suggest that liquidity abounds, which could have its obvious downside." (Minehan)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 45 ±8
- "a number of participants voiced concerns about domestic and global financial imbalances"
- "signs of potentially excessive risk-taking in financial markets evidenced by quite narrow credit spreads, a pickup in initial public offerings, an upturn in mergers and acquisition activity, and anecdotal reports that speculative demands were becoming apparent in the markets for single-family homes and condominiums"
- Discussion of fiscal deficits, current account deficit, global imbalances

**Observation**: Scores 42 vs. 45 — effectively tied given the uncertainty bands. Both sources confirm meaningful but non-dominant attention to financial stability themes, particularly housing speculation, hedge fund growth, and narrow spreads. The minutes' aggregated language ("a number of participants") may slightly elevate that score by conveying breadth.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (Discussion track only)

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 70 ±5
- "I hope we will not try to signal that we may soon pause in our removal of policy accommodation." (Guynn)
- "It's still too early to signal an imminent pause; that will be a key question for next year." (Moskow)
- "The overall stance of monetary policy should continue to be directed at moving the real fed funds rate higher, with a significant positive slope to the path of the fed funds rate." (Geithner)
- "We should be thinking about continuing to raise rates on a systematic basis...we should not become overly cautious at this time." (Hoenig)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 67 ±5
- "policy accommodation could be removed at a pace that was likely to be measured"
- "the current level of the real funds rate target remained below the level it most likely would need to reach to keep inflation stable and output at its potential"
- Majority favored retaining forward-looking statement language
- "A few members" wanted to drop forward-looking language (interpretable as wanting more flexibility)

**Observation**: Transcript (70) vs. minutes (67) — a 3-point gap. Transcript preserves the explicit anti-pause language from individual participants (Guynn, Moskow, Geithner), which drives the score higher. The minutes' "measured" modifier tempers the signal somewhat.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (Discussion track only)

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 43 ±7
- "We end up in a period where our forecasting is somewhat befogged...I suspect that we will find the resulting forecasts not very useful or accurate when looked at in retrospect." (Greenspan)
- "I do sense increased risks out there...Are things as good as they seem in the forecast before us, or do we stand an increasing chance of a very unpleasant, though possibly unforecastable, surprise?" (Minehan)
- "Extensive discussion of uncertainty about productivity trajectory, with concern that capital deepening slowdown signals something real." (Ferguson)
- "There's a lot of uncertainty about structural productivity...A lot of uncertainty about the appropriate response to dollar movements." (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 37 ±7
- "most participants acknowledged some significant uncertainties in their outlook"
- "the degree of economic slack was quite uncertain"
- Uncertainties about high-tech spending, partial-expensing expiration, productivity growth trajectory
- Overall tone: expansion "firmly established," general confidence in continued growth

**Observation**: Transcript (43) vs. minutes (37) — a 6-point gap. The transcript's higher score reflects the rhetorical weight of individual participant language, particularly Greenspan's "befogged" remark and Minehan's explicit concern about tail risks. The minutes synthesize these into a more measured "most participants acknowledged some significant uncertainties" that reads as moderate confidence. This divergence is consistent with the known compression of individual voice in the minutes format.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 62 ±8
- "Survey estimates of ten-year-ahead expected CPI inflation in the 2½ to 3 percent range...the Bluebook reports expectations measures of between 2½ and 3 percent based on inflation-indexed Treasury spreads. So it seems that a range of measures are telling us that inflation will be noticeably greater than we think it will be." (Lacker)
- "Five-year inflation compensation has increased...five-to-ten-year inflation compensation has moved up steadily in 2003 and 2004 to nearly 2¾ percent." (Hoenig)
- "Inflation compensation over the next five years has moved up noticeably...the five-year TIPS is probably a relatively pure measure of domestic expectations for inflation compensation." (Ferguson)
- "Inflation expectations are sticking at a level somewhat above what we consider comfortable or consistent with our implicit inflation target." (Geithner)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 48 ±5
- "longer-term inflation expectations well-anchored"
- "survey measures of both short- and long-term inflation expectations had been quite stable"
- "increase over the last few months in five-year measures of inflation compensation...might be a warning sign"

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 48 ±5
- Stockton: TIPS five-year compensation "steady in recent months"
- "Five- to ten-year inflation compensation" stable
- "Survey measures — either short-term or long-term" show no breakout
- "Still few signs that this year's price shocks will propagate into sustained higher inflation"

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 48 ±5
- Staff forecast expects core inflation to remain "stable"
- Committee statement (staff-drafted) notes "underlying inflation expected to be relatively low"
- Staff views expectations as well-contained and near target

**Observation**: Significant divergence — discussion transcript scores 62 vs. all other sources at 48. The transcript captures multiple participants explicitly expressing alarm about TIPS breakevens and survey measures running 2.5–3%, above the implicit target. The minutes and both staff sources frame the same data as "well-anchored" with only a "warning sign" qualifier. This is the largest cross-source gap in this meeting. The transcript reveals the underlying concern more clearly than the synthesized sources.

### DOLLAR_CONCERN / DOLLAR_STRENGTH

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 18 ±8 [DOLLAR_CONCERN: higher = worry about strength]
- "A panicky flight from the dollar...would be very harmful." (Bernanke — worried about further weakness)
- "The dollar has to fall enough to ultimately eliminate the U.S. trade deficit." (Gramlich)
- "The value of the dollar has declined. It may be likely to fall more as the country's current account deficit approaches new highs." (Minehan)
- "a substantial fall in the dollar that is much larger than assumed" cited as a nontrivial asymmetric risk. (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 38 ±8 [DOLLAR_CONCERN]
- "recent depreciation of the dollar on foreign exchange markets" noted
- Dollar decline's inflationary pass-through discussed but deemed "limited"
- Concern is about weakness, not strength; score below 50

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 18 ±7 [DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher = stronger dollar]
- Stockton: dollar "has retraced some of its earlier declines" but "was still below our previous projection"
- Current account deficit widening; "betting against the dollar reflects the unsustainability of our deficit" (Kos)
- Dollar weakness ongoing; structural concern flagged

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 25 ±5 [DOLLAR_STRENGTH]
- "The value of the dollar relative to other major currencies declined."
- Staff forecast: "decline in the dollar" referenced as a factor in inflation outlook
- Dollar depreciation described as notable enough to factor into trade dynamics

**Observation**: Discussion transcript (18) and staff transcript (18) are identical; staff minutes (25) is close. The discussion minutes score (38) is notably higher — this likely reflects the minutes' more hedged language about dollar pass-through being "limited," which moderates the weakness concern relative to the raw participant alarm visible in the transcript. All four sources nonetheless place the score below 30 (excluding the discussion minutes outlier), consistently indicating dollar weakness, not strength.

### PRODUCTIVITY

**Discussion Transcript** | Score: 52 ±10
- Ferguson argues extensively that slowdown in capital deepening and high-tech investment may signal structural productivity slowdown; unit labor costs rising
- "We've had only one quarter of slower productivity growth after about half a dozen quarters of growth above the presumed trend...even a couple of quarters of slowdown would tell us very little." (Kohn — skeptical of structural break)
- "More optimistic than the baseline Greenbook projections about productivity because of the comments I've been hearing from my business contacts." (Pianalto)
- "Productivity growth slowing significantly." (Geithner)

**Discussion Minutes** | Score: 60 ±8
- "elevated underlying productivity growth"
- "no clear signs that underlying productivity had slowed appreciably of late"
- "upside risks to the outlook for productivity growth could be significant"
- "productivity growth had slowed appreciably in the most recent quarter" noted as a concern

**Staff Transcript** | Score: 62 ±7
- Stockton: "slowing of structural labor productivity" in forecast
- Yellen: structural could average 3.25% — "about equal to the average rate of productivity growth during the last seven years"
- Businesses "continuing to adapt and reorganize" using new technologies
- ICT technologies "continuing to play out" — risk of faster or slower productivity both plausible

**Staff Minutes** | Score: 55 ±10
- Staff forecast references "a slight step-down in structural productivity growth"
- "markup of prices over labor costs remained somewhat elevated"
- Productivity mentioned in context of inflation stability, not as a standalone topic

**Observation**: Discussion transcript (52) diverges from the other three (55–62). The transcript score reflects the genuine internal debate — Ferguson's bearish view vs. Kohn's and Pianalto's more sanguine assessments produce a centrist uncertain score. The minutes and staff sources, which present a more aggregated or official view, lean toward characterizing productivity as still elevated, yielding higher scores.
