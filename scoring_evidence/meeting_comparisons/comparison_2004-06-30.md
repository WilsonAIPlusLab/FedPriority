### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 60 ±8**

- "We have now seen significant acceleration in underlying inflation to a level somewhat above our range of comfort." (Geithner)
- "The underlying inflation rate, using the market-based core PCE measure, is at an annual rate of 2.1 percent for April and May, and for the latest three months it goes up to 2.4 percent." (Greenspan)
- "I still think that the core price indexes now have embedded in them some inflationary updrift that is due to our earlier accommodative monetary policy." (Hoenig)
- Minority: "I'm still inclined to the view that inflation…is likely to moderate in the second half." (Bernanke)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 57 ±8**

- "Evidence accumulated over the intermeeting period...pointed to some recent acceleration of consumer prices and to some increase in near-term inflation expectations."
- "Large increases in prices of energy and intermediate materials, both of which appeared to be passing through at least in part to core consumer prices."
- "Some of the rise in core inflation in recent months appeared to have resulted from what might well prove to be transitory factors."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 40 ±6**

- "PCE price inflation has stepped up this year." Core PCE accelerated from ~1% to ~1.5%.
- "Inflationary pressures will remain fairly well contained" going forward. Core PCE projected to edge back to 1.25%.
- 70% confidence interval for 2005 core PCE: 0.75% to 2.5%.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 53 ±5**

- "Core consumer price inflation picked up, reflecting in part the pass-through of substantial advances in energy prices and non-energy import prices."
- "Consumer price inflation turned up this year from the very low rates registered in 2003, both for overall measures and for those that exclude food and energy."
- Staff forecast: "Core inflation was projected to fall back later this year from its pace in the first five months and to remain low in 2005, as the transitory effects of higher energy and non-oil import prices waned."

**Divergence note**: The largest gap is between the discussion transcript (60) and the staff transcript (40). Participants perceived inflation as "somewhat above our range of comfort" with upside risk; staff framed the same readings as a temporary pickup from very low 2003 levels with a forecast path back toward 1.25%. Staff minutes (53) are closer to the center.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 44 ±6**

- "The output gap is still sizable. The markup of prices over unit labor costs…is well above its long-run average." (Lacker)
- "4 percent growth—only moderately above the rate of growth of the economy's potential—doesn't seem all that strong" and "continuing though ebbing margins of slack." (Kohn)
- "This forecast is shaped by the assessment of slack remaining in the economy and the belief that growth will not be constrained by resources for some time, perhaps not until year-end 2005." (Minehan)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 45 ±5**

- "Economy that was expanding briskly and was likely to continue to do so for some time."
- "Persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor and other productive resources."
- "The economy continued to move toward full utilization of its resources."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 45 ±5**

- Unemployment "remaining slightly above our estimate of the NAIRU throughout the projection period." (Wilcox)
- GDP growing above trend; gap narrowing steadily. Manufacturing output up 9%.
- Near but not yet at potential.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 44 ±5**

- "Capacity utilization moved higher but stayed below its long-run average."
- Staff forecast describes the economy "moving toward full utilization of its resources."
- Unemployment projections of 5.25–5.5%.

**Divergence note**: All four sources converge tightly at 44–45. Unanimous signal: economy below potential, gap closing, but not yet closed.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 44 ±6**

- "In the household survey, the unemployment rate is unchanged since January, and labor force participation hasn't risen…the workweek…has barely budged off a very low level." (Kohn)
- "The present 5.6 percent rate of employment does not represent full capacity economywide…I remember a few years ago when the unemployment rate went to 4 percent with no real hint of acceleration in inflation." (Gramlich)
- "Unemployment rate, labor force participation rate, and capacity utilization rate have all remained stubbornly low or high — depending on how you think about it." (Ferguson)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 40 ±5**

- "Notable improvement in labor market conditions."
- "Persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor."
- Unemployment forecast: 5.25–5.5% Q4 2004, 5–5.5% Q4 2005.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 42 ±5**

- Monthly payroll increases "will average a little more than 300,000 jobs" through year-end. (Wilcox)
- Unemployment still "slightly above our estimate of the NAIRU."
- Participation rate expected to recover; workweek rebounding.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 40 ±5**

- "The labor market rebounded strongly in recent months. Private nonfarm payrolls grew rapidly in April and May, with hiring widespread across industries."
- "The unemployment rate changed little in recent months, and the labor force participation rate remained low."
- UE projected at 5.25–5.5%.

**Divergence note**: All four sources cluster at 40–44. Agreement that hiring is rapid but unemployment remains above NAIRU and participation is depressed. The transcript score is slightly higher (44) due to participant-level discussion of sector-specific tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 60 ±7**

- "Investment seems likely to have ample reason and room to continue to grow at a rapid rate, with corporate balance sheets strong, profit margins strong, inventories low, and orders running high." (Geithner)
- "Capital expenditures…are strong. Employment growth in the last several months has really come back to life." (Bies)
- FedEx "operating at capacity in its freight business"; UPS says demand is "well above expectations everywhere." (Poole, citing contacts)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 60 ±7**

- "Many of the members emphasized the strength in business capital spending."
- "Business and consumer expenditures were on a strong uptrend."
- "Consumer outlays were rising more moderately in the second quarter and somewhat below expectations."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 63 ±5**

- E&S up about 12%, projected 9% next year. (Stockton)
- "Profit share of GNP now stands at its highest level in more than three decades."
- Consumer spending softer after Greenbook close; partial-expensing expiration creates near-term risk.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 58 ±5**

- "Business fixed investment, boosted by surging outlays on equipment and software, also grew rapidly."
- "Activity in the housing market increased in April and May."
- "Growth in real consumer spending appeared to have slowed somewhat in recent months."

**Divergence note**: All four sources score 58–63. Near-perfect convergence. Business investment the common strong signal; consumer spending modestly softer the common offset.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 28 ±7**

- "Financial conditions remain supportive of above-trend growth, but they have tightened in recent months as markets have come to anticipate our actions." (Kohn)
- Asset quality at banks "best ever." (Olson, banking contacts)
- Greenspan flags carry trades and maturity mismatches as reasons to move gradually — not signs of current stress.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 28 ±6**

- "Continuing stimulus from fiscal and monetary policies, accommodative financial conditions."
- "Financial market conditions had tightened in recent months, but short-term interest rates were quite low, especially when judged against the recent level of inflation."
- "Low capital costs."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 40 ±6**

- "Interest rate that corporations face on new debt" jumped. But average rate on stock of debt more stable.
- Business balance sheets very healthy — debt ratios at lowest in many years, profits at highest share in three decades.
- Funds rate still only 1.25% after hike. Still accommodative overall.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 28 ±5**

- "A favorable financing environment were expected to keep business spending on...a healthy upward trajectory."
- "Short-term interest rates were quite low, especially when judged against the recent level of inflation." Risk spreads unchanged.

**Divergence note**: Staff transcript (40) diverges from the other three (all 28). The staff transcript emphasized that new-debt rates jumped and noted the funds rate had just risen, pulling the score up; the other sources consistently describe the overall environment as clearly accommodative.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

*(Discussion sources only; not a staff dimension.)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 65 ±7**

- "I think the time has come to start moving the fed funds rate up and to get real short-term rates first to a positive position and then to a more neutral level." (Minehan)
- "I would join those who encourage us to have in mind moving back toward neutral at a more deliberate pace and in a very systematic fashion…avoid having to go above neutral later to contain inflation." (Hoenig)
- Most dovish participant: "I'm still comfortable with the main strategy of a gradual increase in rates starting now and at every meeting or so." (Gramlich)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 63 ±5**

- "All of the members indicated that they could support an upward adjustment in the target for the federal funds rate from a level of 1 percent to 1-1/4 percent."
- "Members commented that they could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves."
- "A more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for."
- "A number of members emphasized that they would view the risks as tilted to the upside in the absence of further policy tightening."

**Divergence note**: Scores are close (63 vs. 65). The transcript captures the full intensity of individual hawkish voices (Hoenig, Minehan pushing for deliberate normalization); the minutes' synthesized format rounds to a slightly lower center of gravity.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

*(Discussion sources only.)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 35 ±8**

- Formal vote: unanimous (no dissents recorded).
- "I find it surprisingly hard to see much supporting macro evidence" for being behind the curve. (Gramlich)
- Inflation "is now threatening." (Hoenig) — opposing view within same meeting.
- Language dispute: Minehan and Poole want to remove "measured pace"; Kohn argues to retain it.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 22 ±5**

- "All of the members indicated that they could support" the 25bp hike.
- "A few indicated...their preference would be to remove any characterization of possible future policy actions."
- Inflation debate (transitory vs. persistent camps) present but resolved without dissent.

**Divergence note**: A 13-point gap (35 vs. 22). The transcript exposes the full texture of the Gramlich/Hoenig disagreement and the statement language dispute; the minutes' synthesis compresses these into brief mentions, yielding a lower dissent score. Neither reads a formal dissent.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

*(Discussion sources only.)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 25 ±8**

- "We have to be careful not to move too fast and risk destabilizing the financial system by enforcing maturity mismatches and other idiosyncrasies in the financial system." (Greenspan)
- "Consumer debt and household financial obligations, while a bit off their peak levels, are still much greater than in the 1990s…we have a growing and unsustainable current account deficit." (Guynn)
- "There's a lot we cannot measure and cannot know about how the world will react to the initiation of what will have to be a substantial increase in the fed funds rate." (Geithner)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 18 ±5**

- "Accommodative financial conditions" mentioned as backdrop — no substantive stability discussion.
- Brief mention of housing: "only a few signs that rising mortgage rates were beginning to hold down sales."
- No discussion of asset valuations, systemic risk, or financial imbalances.

**Divergence note**: Transcript (25) vs. minutes (18). Greenspan's specific warning on maturity mismatches and carry trades does not appear in the minutes' participant summary, pulling the minutes score lower.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

*(Discussion sources only.)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 68 ±7**

- Adopted statement retains "measured pace" language but adds a sentence emphasizing the Committee will respond to changes in economic prospects — preserving possibility of 50bp moves. (Greenspan)
- "To remove from our statement any characterization of how we will act in the future." (Minehan — advocating stronger data-dependence)
- "The combination of words that reflects both the central tendency of expectations of a measured pace of policy tightening and the recognition that…the Committee must be alert to the possibility that it could need to take more-decisive action." (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 65 ±7**

- "Members commented that they could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves as likely to be consistent with the attainment of the Committee's objectives."
- "A more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for."
- "Many members also underscored their view that the statement should make clear that the Committee would be prepared to respond to significant changes in economic prospects."

**Divergence note**: Scores close (65 vs. 68). The transcript captures the explicit debate about keeping flexibility for 50bp moves, nudging the score slightly higher.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

*(Discussion sources only.)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 42 ±8**

- "Inflation forecasts as well as output forecasts are, of course, always hazardous and seem particularly so today." (Bernanke)
- "Models that we have on price level changes I think have an r2 of somewhere around 0.5 or 0.6, suggesting that humility in this regard, as with other things, is very important." (Ferguson)
- "There is much we do not understand about the inflation process…and the pickup in core inflation has been a surprise to me." (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 42 ±7**

- "Considerable uncertainty still surrounded the overall extent to which competitive pressures would allow producers to pass through rising costs."
- "Several noted that they now had less confidence in those forecasts than earlier."
- "Just how much slowing of price increases was likely after some relatively elevated readings was difficult to forecast."

**Divergence note**: Exact match (42 vs. 42). Both sources identify inflation dynamics as the locus of uncertainty with growth uncertainty distinctly lower.
