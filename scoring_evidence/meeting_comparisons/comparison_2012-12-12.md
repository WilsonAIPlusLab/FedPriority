### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
*Higher score = more inflation pressure. Below 50 = below-target pressure.*

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "the four-quarter moving average of the change in core PCE has been below 2 percent for four years, and we expect it to remain below our target for another three years." — Rosengren

> "inflation remains relatively tame, likely running at or below the Committee's target over the medium term. Trimmed mean inflation is 1.7 percent over the past 12 months; median inflation was also quite tame." — Bernanke (summary of go-round)

> "both headline and core PCE inflation will remain below 2 percent for the next several years. Uncertainty...putting downward pressure on inflation." — Williams

Center-of-gravity: The overwhelming consensus is that inflation is running below 2% PCE, with trimmed mean measures around 1.6–1.7%, unit labor costs flat or falling, and no wage pressures evident — consistent with multi-year below-target inflation.

Minority views: Plosser warns that the estimated coefficient on the output gap in the Phillips curve "is now quite small" and cautions that relying on resource slack to keep inflation subdued may be wrong; George expects inflation "to begin to rise above 2 percent in 2014 and 2015" under continued accommodation.

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "Consumer price inflation had been running somewhat below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent."

> "Incoming information pointed to stable, low inflation that was running a little below the Committee's longer-run goal."

> "Compensation pressures had remained subdued, and unit labor costs were little changed."

> "Most participants saw the risks to the inflation outlook as broadly balanced."

Center-of-gravity: Inflation perceived as modestly below the 2% target with subdued wage pressures, but not dangerously low — stable and anchored expectations.

Minority views: One participant expressed concern about "considerable uncertainty" in the unemployment-inflation relationship, suggesting inflation might not stay restrained.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
*Higher score = more above potential. Below 50 = below-potential economy.*

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "we are taking significant risks with an economy that is obviously underperforming, and we are missing on both sides of our mandate." — Bernanke

> "the case for insufficient aggregate demand is still a pretty strong one and is likely to be strong regardless of the resolution of the fiscal cliff." — Tarullo

> "second-half GDP growth seems to be around 2 percent as the familiar host of uncertainties and headwinds continue to take their toll." — Evans

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly see the economy operating below potential, with GDP growth around 2% (near or modestly below trend potential), significant labor market slack, and consumption/investment being held back by fiscal uncertainty and headwinds. The economy is recovering but the output gap has not closed.

Minority views: Lacker argues the economy may be near "a balanced growth path right now" given the 2.1 percentage point unemployment decline with modest GDP growth; Plosser is "considerably more optimistic than the Tealbook about labor market conditions."

---

**Minutes — Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "Economic activity and employment continued to expand at a moderate pace."

> "Growth in economic activity continued to be restrained by several persistent headwinds."

> "Without sufficient policy accommodation, economic growth might not be strong enough to generate sustained improvement in labor market conditions."

Center-of-gravity: Economy operating below potential with moderate expansion and persistent headwinds from fiscal uncertainty, deleveraging, and global slowdown.

Minority views: "A number of participants suggested that the business sector was well positioned to expand spending and hiring quickly upon a positive resolution of the fiscal cliff negotiations," suggesting upside potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
*Higher score = tighter labor market. Below 50 = labor market slack.*

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "The JOLTS data for October...showed that the hiring rate remains far below its pre-recession level, and the quit rate remains very depressed. Such readings on turnover suggest no substantial improvement in the labor market outlook thus far." — Yellen

> "U-6, my favorite broadest measure of capacity underutilization in the labor market...is still up at 14.4 percent, very high historically...the number of people employed part-time for economic reasons is still about double the level that prevailed pretty consistently for a decade before the crisis." — Tarullo

> "conditions in the job market remain generally sluggish. The loss of UI benefits may be one reason for lower unemployment, as people are leaving the labor force. Hiring is still weak." — Bernanke (summary)

Center-of-gravity: Despite the unemployment rate declining to 7.7%, participants broadly view the labor market as substantially below full employment, with broad-based measures (U-6 at 14.4%), weak hiring and quit rates, growing involuntary part-time employment, and flat wage growth all signaling significant slack.

Minority views: Lacker argues the 139K/month job gain trend looks like a "balanced growth path"; Plosser projects unemployment reaching 7% by year-end 2013 and is "considerably more optimistic than the Tealbook."

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "The unemployment rate remained elevated."

> "Several pointed to indicators suggesting that rates of hiring remained depressed relative to those observed before the financial crisis."

> "Part of the decline in unemployment in November was attributable to a drop in labor force participation."

Center-of-gravity: Labor market has improved but remains well below full employment with elevated unemployment and depressed hiring rates.

Minority views: "A couple of participants noted that vacancies remained at a high level...suggesting that at least some firms were having a hard time finding suitable workers." This signals modest structural tightness but is a minority observation.

**Transcript — Score: 39 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Business fixed investment is weak and declining amid fiscal uncertainty; consumer spending is softer than expected with real disposable income constrained. Housing is the notable bright spot (Duke, Rosengren highlight it), but overall consumption and investment are below trend, dragged by fiscal uncertainty and household deleveraging.

Minority views: Duke and Dudley are more optimistic about housing momentum and business balance sheet strength as latent drivers of spending recovery; Bullard notes "relatively upbeat households" in the Eighth District.

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

The FOMC is in the process of further expanding accommodation ($85B/month total). Some specific borrowers still face constraints, but the general conditions are loose.

Minority views: Williams notes "Many potential borrowers still face tight credit" and Rosengren discusses the primary-secondary mortgage spread (capacity constraints in mortgage origination). Duke discusses regulatory headwinds to mortgage credit.

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall due to QE-driven accommodation, but residual tightness persists for some borrowers and deleveraging continues. Net: slightly below neutral (somewhat loose to neutral).

Minority views: "A few participants" flagged risk of financial imbalances from prolonged low rates, suggesting conditions may be too easy in some segments.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*Higher score = more hawkish policy stance. Below 50 = dovish.*

**Transcript — Score: 20 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "we've essentially said that we'd do whatever it takes to generate a more rapid improvement in labor market conditions consistent with our price stability goal. To dial back on that commitment now...would, to me, seem to be reneging on our commitment." — Dudley

> "I support alternative B...Additional asset purchases are needed to shorten the time we continue to miss on our inflation and employment targets...Even if we just had one mandate and that was an inflation mandate, I would argue that we should be doing more." — Rosengren

> "we are missing on both sides of our mandate...it is reasonable to follow through on that commitment and to continue the accommodation that we discussed in September." — Bernanke (summary)

Center-of-gravity: The overwhelming center of gravity is strongly dovish — the meeting results in continuation and expansion of QE (from $40B/month MBS to $85B/month combined), adoption of threshold-based forward guidance promising ultra-low rates until 6.5% unemployment, and explicit statements that further accommodation is warranted given below-mandate performance on both inflation and employment. One formal dissent (Lacker) opposing all asset purchases.

Minority views: Stein, Powell, Duke, Pianalto, Bullard, and Plosser would have preferred a smaller or differently structured purchase program. Lacker's formal dissent anchors the minority view firmly.

---

**Minutes — Score: 20 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "All members but one judged that continued provision of monetary accommodation was warranted."

> Committee authorized $40B MBS + $45B Treasury purchases per month. Adopted Evans Rule thresholds.

> "Various members stressed the importance of a continuing assessment of labor market developments."

Center-of-gravity: Strongly dovish — expanding QE and introducing threshold-based forward guidance. Active QE expansion discussion places this in the 15–25 range.

Minority views: One dissent. "A number of participants expressed the concern that additional purchases could complicate the Committee's efforts to eventually withdraw monetary policy accommodation." But this is a caution about risks, not advocacy for tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
*Higher score = more disagreement and dissent.*

**Transcript — Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "It will come as no surprise that I oppose expanding our asset purchases for the same reason I opposed them in September. I view them as unlikely to aid economic growth without unacceptably increasing the risk of inflation." — Lacker

> "I support alternative B, though I do so with fairly serious reservations...I would have preferred an option that kept the MBS purchases going at their current $40 billion clip but began to dial back the Treasury purchases." — Stein

> "I'm not supportive of a program of that size, and I am concerned that we may be sitting on bad news in the form of a disconnection between the expectations of 12 of the 19 participants and those of the market." — Powell

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent (Lacker) plus five or more participants expressing substantial reservations (Stein, Powell, Duke, Pianalto, Bullard wanting $35B instead of $45B, Plosser skeptical of Treasury purchase efficacy). There is also a significant sub-debate about quantitative vs. qualitative thresholds. Disagreements extend to economic assessment and policy design.

---

**Minutes — Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "All members but one" (one formal dissent).

> Range of views on purchase pace/duration: "a few members expressed the view that ongoing asset purchases would likely be warranted until about the end of 2013, while a few others emphasized" [different timeline].

> "A few" preferred qualitative over quantitative guidance.

Center-of-gravity: One dissent with moderate range of views on implementation details, but broad agreement on direction. Typical one-dissent meeting with some tactical disagreement.

Note: Minutes describe the debate over quantitative vs. qualitative thresholds and the range of views on purchase duration as sources of dispersion.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
*Higher score = more weight placed on financial stability concerns.*

> "I don't have a completely strong counterargument. But where I would hope there would be less disagreement is on a proposition that Narayana advanced yesterday, which is that whatever these risks ultimately are, we ought to be pretty up front about them...our policy involves taking on a material amount of fiscal risk." — Stein

> "My 'other' cost category is flashing red: Financial stability, as Governor Stein and President Pianalto both indicated." — Powell

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives moderate-to-prominent discussion across multiple speakers (Yellen, Stein, Powell, Pianalto, Bernanke, Raskin). The dominant theme is the risk of excessive search for yield, leverage buildup, and potential sharp rate spike at exit. It permeates the cost-side analysis of LSAPs throughout both days.

Center-of-gravity: Limited but present discussion — a few participants raised financial stability concerns; it was acknowledged as something to monitor but was not a primary focus.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
*Higher score = more hawkish forward signal. Below 50 = easing commitment.*

**Transcript — Score: 5 ±3 [High confidence]**

> "the Committee views its current low-rate policy as likely to be appropriate at least until the specified thresholds are met" — specifically a 6.5% unemployment rate threshold with an inflation ceiling of 2.5%. — Bernanke (introducing the threshold)

> "Thresholds are better than date guidance because they remove us from the equation on a meeting-by-meeting basis. I don't think we'd want to change our threshold values very often, if at all." — Dudley

> The statement adopts the Evans Rule thresholds, which are described as "consistent with our earlier [mid-2015] date guidance" — signaling rates at near-zero through approximately 2015–2016.

Center-of-gravity: This meeting adopts threshold-based forward guidance — the Evans Rule — promising near-zero rates until a specific unemployment threshold is met, with ongoing QE purchases besides. This is among the strongest forms of easing commitment.

---

**Minutes — Score: 5 ±3 [High confidence]**

> "Most participants favored replacing the calendar-date forward guidance with economic thresholds" of 6.5% unemployment and 2.5% inflation.

> "Thresholds could increase the probability that market reactions to economic developments would move longer-term interest rates in a manner consistent with the Committee's view."

Center-of-gravity: Introduction of the Evans Rule represents threshold-based commitment to near-zero rates — the strongest form of easing commitment in the rubric.

Minority views: "A few" preferred qualitative guidance, and "a few" worried thresholds could be misinterpreted as triggers, but the center strongly favored thresholds.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
*Higher score = more uncertainty expressed.*

**Transcript — Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "I can imagine minus 2 percent, on a Q4-over-Q4 basis, if we fall off the fiscal cliff. But I can imagine plus 4 percent if everything goes swimmingly. It seems to me that the fiscal cliff has been impeding hiring and investment, so there has been some delay in activity." — Dudley

> "We need to acknowledge that there is considerable uncertainty about the relationship between unemployment and inflation, and I think we should be skeptical about relying on resource slack to keep inflation subdued going forward." — Plosser

> "considering a 'Groundhog Day scenario'...None of these costs can be particularly well understood or accurately estimated in the time frame in which we will be continuing our purchases." — Stein

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is elevated and pervasive across two distinct domains: (1) near-term fiscal cliff outcome creates extreme bimodal economic scenarios; (2) structural uncertainty about QE efficacy, the Phillips curve, the participation rate, and the exit path. Multiple participants use explicit "considerable uncertainty," "very difficult to assess," and "cannot be quantified" language.

---

**Minutes — Score: 50 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "Almost all indicated that heightened uncertainty about fiscal policy probably was affecting economic activity adversely."

> "Business contacts in many parts of the country were also said to be highly uncertain about the outlook."

> "The benefits of ongoing purchases were uncertain and that the potential costs could rise."

> "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the relationship between unemployment and inflation."

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty, primarily driven by fiscal cliff negotiations and their potential economic impact, plus uncertainty about QE efficacy. Multiple dimensions of uncertainty discussed by many participants.

Note: Uncertainty is broadly shared — almost all participants flagged fiscal policy uncertainty.
