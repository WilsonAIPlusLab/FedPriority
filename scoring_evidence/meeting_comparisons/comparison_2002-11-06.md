## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 22)**
- "We expect inflation to trend down, with the core PCE index rising by 1½ percent in 2003 and 1¼ percent in 2004...the risk of outright deflation in 2003 and 2004 appears to be quite small." (Parry)
- "We're at or near the lower end of the range that we would set if we did have inflation targets. Our job right now seems to be to make sure that we don't go below that floor." (McTeer)
- "The economy looks as if it will be on a lower track...with greater disinflationary—or even possibly deflationary—pressures than we expected, while inflation is already as low as I would like to see it go." (Kohn)
- "We are dealing with what basically is a latent deflationary type of economy." (Greenspan)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 22)**
- "Inflation was likely to remain subdued and perhaps even to edge a little lower."
- "The prospect of some persisting slack in resource use over coming quarters pointed to further disinflation."
- "Some members referred to the possibility, which they viewed as remote, of a period of deflation."

**Staff-Transcript (Score: 25)**
- Stockton: 25–30% probability of deflation (FRB/US model estimate).
- Core inflation declining; output gap widening — described as "few signs that this period of weakness is spreading or cumulating into something more serious" but deflation risk material.

**Staff-Minutes (Score: 28)**
- "Overall price inflation had fallen over the past year, reflecting both favorable developments in the food and energy sectors and a continuing decline in core inflation."
- "Consumer price inflation continued to trend downward in September. The rise in consumer prices for the year ending in September was considerably smaller than that for the previous twelve-month period."
- Staff forecast: "persistence of underutilized resources was expected to foster a slight moderation in core price inflation."

**Convergence note**: All four sources score 22–28; direction fully consistent. Discussion track and staff minutes are 3–6 points apart. Staff transcript cites the quantitative deflation probability (25–30%) not present in the other sources.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 33)**
- "The output gap is, if anything, growing rather than shrinking...the output gap is likely to grow through about the middle of next year before it finally begins to narrow." (Broaddus)
- "The gap between output and potential output is, if anything, widening rather than diminishing...Capacity utilization has not only been flat for the past year, but its absolute level is at near-record lows." (Bernanke)
- "The output gap has been widened by ¾ point...the gap between actual and potential economic growth is bigger." (Bies)
- "Operating with a higher and possibly rising level of slack." (Kohn)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 30)**
- "Economic growth had slowed to a pace well below that experienced earlier in the year."
- "Slack in labor and product markets at elevated levels."
- "A longer-lasting spell of subpar economic performance than they had anticipated earlier."

**Staff-Transcript (Score: 32)**
- Stockton: GDP 1% Q4, less than 2.5% through mid-2023. "Economy does not really break out of the recent pattern of sluggish growth until late next year." Gap widening. Manufacturing IP declining.

**Staff-Minutes (Score: 33)**
- "Economic growth had slowed from the moderate pace of the third quarter."
- "The level of activity would remain below the economy's potential for a longer time."
- "Capacity utilization in manufacturing edged lower in September and was substantially below its long-run average."

**Convergence note**: Scores cluster tightly at 30–33. All sources use explicit "below potential" / "gap widening" language. High agreement across tracks.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 35)**
- "Labor markets remain slack. A large national retailer said that the number of applications for $8 an hour part-time holiday season jobs was up threefold from last year." (Moskow)
- "Unemployment rate has been stuck at 6.1 percent for the last three months. Employment has declined for five consecutive months." (McTeer)
- "Indicators of labor utilization, including payroll employment, the workweek, and production worker hours, have been basically flat for the year." (Bernanke)
- "Over the past six months, job growth has averaged only 36,000 per month." (Santomero)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 28)**
- "Slack in labor...markets at elevated levels."
- "Pressures on labor and other resources expected to be limited over coming quarters."

**Staff-Transcript (Score: 28)**
- Stockton: "Employment gains appear to have stalled out after a brief upturn this summer."
- "Recent readings on initial claims for unemployment insurance suggest there has been little improvement."

**Staff-Minutes (Score: 32)**
- "Aggregate labor market conditions weakened further in October. Private nonfarm payroll employment declined in September and October."
- "The civilian unemployment rate rose to 5.7 percent in October."
- "Initial claims for unemployment insurance were at a relatively elevated rate."

**Convergence note**: Discussion transcript scores 7 points higher than discussion minutes (35 vs. 28). Staff sources bracket at 28–32. The transcript's richer speaker-level detail (McTeer's 6.1% unemployment, Moskow's anecdote, Bernanke's jobless recovery comparison) produces a slightly higher reading. All sources agree on slack; the gap reflects information density, not directional disagreement.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 35)**
- "Auto sales were high through August but dropped sharply in September and October. Non-auto consumption spending was strong through July but has barely grown since then. Consumer confidence has dropped sharply...evidence of collapse lies all around us." (Gramlich)
- "Weakness in investment persists, and now consumption growth is tapering off." (Kohn)
- "Capital investment is going absolutely nowhere." "Motor vehicle sales slipped...one of the pillars of spending...has been removed." (Greenspan)
- "Real business fixed investment has or is slated to drop in every quarter of 2002 before beginning to grow again next year." (Ferguson)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 33)**
- "Consumer spending appeared to have decelerated since midsummer."
- "An anticipated and hopefully compensating strengthening in business investment had not yet materialized."
- "High and persisting uncertainty...continued to hold down business investment spending."

**Staff-Transcript (Score: 38)**
- Stockton: Consumer spending and business outlays both below expectations. Auto sales "dropped off sharply." "Non-auto spending also has been disappointingly weak." E&S below expectations. Only housing a bright spot.

**Staff-Minutes (Score: 37)**
- "Consumer spending had softened and business investment was still sluggish."
- "Retail sales weakened in September."
- Business fixed investment "edged up" but with "steep downward trend" in telecom/aircraft and nonresidential structures "continued to decline rapidly." Housing "remained very strong."

**Convergence note**: Staff sources (37–38) score 2–5 points above discussion sources (33–35). All sources identify consumer deceleration and investment weakness with housing as offset; the difference is marginal and within uncertainty bands.

### FINANCIAL TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL CONDITIONS

*Polarity note: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (discussion track) — higher = tighter conditions. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (staff track) — per staff v4 rubric, lower = more accommodative. These are not directly comparable; presented separately.*

**Disc-Transcript — FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (Score: 55)**
- "The BBB corporate rate...has actually risen 20 basis points over this period [of 175 bp Fed easing]. Stock prices are off 15 percent...in several key dimensions, financial conditions are tighter and markets less receptive than they were before the current degree of policy accommodation was put in place." (Kohn)
- Goldman Sachs index of overall financial conditions "indicates that monetary conditions actually are tighter now than when we first started easing back in early 2001." (Gramlich, citing external index)
- "Credit spreads suggest that financing is harder to come by for more industries." (Minehan)
- Offsetting: "Solid consumer demand...deposits and wholesale funding to banks are readily available...we see few signs of credit supply problems." (Guynn); "In contrast to 1991, there do not appear to be significant financial headwinds." (Bernanke)

**Disc-Minutes — FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (Score: 60)**
- "Overall financial conditions, including bank lending terms, had become more restrictive this year even though the nominal federal funds rate target had not been changed since late 2001."
- "Substantial decreases in equity wealth."
- "Diminishing stimulus from earlier policy easing actions."

**Staff-Transcript — FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (Score: 55)**
- Stockton: "Real federal funds rate below the estimated range of its equilibrium" — accommodative. But "steep declines in household net worth" still weighing. Business caution, stock market weak, corporate confidence low.

**Staff-Minutes — FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (Score: 38)**
- Staff forecast references "considerable monetary ease" and "low financing costs."
- "Broad equity indexes registered sizable gains" during the intermeeting period.
- Mortgage rates "near historical lows."

**Convergence note**: The largest divergence in this meeting. Discussion track (55–60) reflects participants' emphasis on corporate credit tightening and equity wealth losses. Staff minutes (38) emphasizes monetary accommodation and low financing costs from a rates perspective. Staff transcript (55) is more aligned with discussion sources. The staff minutes score reflects that the intermeeting period saw equity gains and that the staff's own language stressed "considerable monetary ease," while participants focused on the transmission gap between policy rates and corporate borrowing costs.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*(Discussion track only)*

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 18)**
- "My suggestion would be to lower the funds rate 50 basis points." (Greenspan)
- "It's very clear...we need to take out insurance against the downside risks...25 basis points simply is not enough...the action that we take today should be decisive." (McDonough)
- "I think we should ease significantly, primarily to stimulate economic growth." (McTeer)
- "It's time for a little extra push from this Committee." (Kohn)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 22)**
- "All the members favored a proposal to reduce the target...by 50 basis points."
- "A relatively aggressive easing action could help to ensure that the current soft spot...would prove to be temporary."
- "The potential costs of a policy easing action that later proved not to have been needed were quite limited."

**Convergence note**: Both sources are firmly dovish (18–22). Minutes quotes are more aggregated; transcript quotes show the individual unanimity. The 4-point gap is within uncertainty bands.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
*(Discussion track only)*

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 22)**
- Near-unanimous support for 50 bp: Greenspan, McDonough, Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kohn, Jordan, Hoenig, Parry, Stern, Poole, Moskow, Santomero, Guynn, Olson, Bernanke, McTeer.
- "I personally would continue to be patient a little while longer...but if we ease...I have a strong preference for a ½ point reduction." (Broaddus)
- "A little at odds with the logic and the market impact of your recommendation, but I'm willing to go along with it." (Minehan)
- Statement reservations: Poole would retain downside bias; Gramlich has "modest preference" to retain asymmetry.

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 18)**
- "All the members favored" the 50 bp cut.
- "All the members indicated that...they could support a shift...to balanced...although some voiced reservations about the need for such a shift."
- "Some" had "at least a marginal preference for retaining the current tilt toward weakness" but accepted balanced.

**Convergence note**: Both near-consensus; minutes aggregate "all members" language yields slightly lower dissent score (18 vs. 22). No formal dissents in either source.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
*(Discussion track only)*

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 42)**
- Kohn: lengthy discussion of BBB corporate spreads, equity wealth effect, credit transmission mechanism, "automatic stabilizing properties of the financial markets have been short-circuited by heightened perceptions of and aversion to risk."
- Gramlich: Goldman Sachs financial conditions index, default rates, credit spreads, equity valuations.
- Minehan: credit card outstandings in "sharp downward turn," credit spreads wider across industries.
- Hoenig: concerns about consumers taking on added debt and their "fragility for the future."

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 30)**
- Discussion of equity wealth effects, bank lending terms, mortgage rates, and potential market overreaction to the decision.
- Financial conditions mentioned as context for policy rather than as a standalone stability concern.

**Convergence note**: 12-point gap. The transcript captures multiple named participants engaging at length with financial condition transmission; minutes compress this to contextual background language. Both sources treat it as policy-supporting context rather than a dominant stability alarm.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
*(Discussion track only)*

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 40)**
- "Going to a balanced risks statement is appropriate if we do 50 basis points." (Greenspan)
- "If 50 basis points is the right number, it should balance the risks...our hands would not be tied either" for future action. (McDonough)
- "Balanced risk statement...suggests what we've been saying all along, that the fundamentals are still in place for a turnaround." (Bies)
- Dissenting view: "Would be interpreted as saying we're finished reducing rates." (Poole, arguing against balanced statement)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 32)**
- All members supported shifting risk assessment to "balanced for the foreseeable future."
- "Many members" worried retaining weakness tilt would cause markets to expect further easing.
- "Some" preferred keeping the weakness tilt, implying they saw potential need for further easing.

**Convergence note**: Both sources describe a balanced risk statement following the 50 bp cut — a neutral-to-mild-easing forward signal. The 8-point gap (40 vs. 32) reflects the transcript capturing Poole's and Broaddus's preference to retain a downside tilt, which nudges the center of gravity slightly more dovish in the transcript. Both scores are below 50 (easing lean with data dependence).

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
*(Discussion track only)*

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 63)**
- "Uncertainty appears to be the main factor holding back the economy, and the passage of time has done little to resolve it. This persistent overhang of uncertainty concerns me." (Moskow)
- "We are dealing with what basically is a latent deflationary type of economy...We are making policy under uncertainty here. The economy is buffeted by new and not well understood factors." (Greenspan)
- "I'm very uncertain about whether growth in real GDP will pick up next quarter or sometime later next year; it may not happen until 2004 or perhaps beyond that." (Stern)
- "The outlook here is really quite murky." (Ferguson)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 58)**
- "A high degree of uncertainty and related caution" constraining business investment.
- "Geopolitical and other uncertainties" affecting confidence.
- "Dissipation of prevailing uncertainties, including those associated with geopolitical risks."
- "Uncertainties that surrounded prospective market reactions."

**Convergence note**: Both elevated (58–63); transcript carries direct speaker quotes expressing near-universal uncertainty; minutes reflect the aggregated consensus. Both above 50; 5-point gap is within uncertainty bands.

### PRODUCTIVITY

**Disc-Transcript (Score: 72)**
- "Despite the efforts of businesses to trim costs, which have been reflected in astonishing productivity numbers." (Kohn)
- "Productivity looks very good. Given its strength over the past year, we can have more confidence that the underlying trends in productivity remain robust." (Moskow)
- "Productivity is on a strong and favorable trend." (Stern)
- "Assuming a positive underlying trend in productivity...this increased flexibility strongly suggests that the economy is in a very favorable position." (Greenspan)

**Disc-Minutes (Score: 65)**
- "Continuation of a strong uptrend in productivity."
- "The outlook for continued robust growth in structural labor productivity."
- "In the context of rising productivity, profits could prove to be stronger than many now expected."

**Staff-Transcript (Score: 63)**
- Structural productivity still elevated. Business caution constraining hiring. Cyclical productivity implications uncertain.

**Staff-Minutes (Score: 62)**
- "Labor productivity remained on a strong upward trend."
- Staff forecast cites "continuing gains in structural productivity" as providing "significant impetus for spending."

**Convergence note**: Strong agreement across all four sources (62–72). Discussion transcript scores highest (72), reflecting multiple participants using language like "astonishing" and "robust." Staff and minutes sources use "strong upward trend" language, landing 7–10 points lower. All agree on above-trend productivity as a key positive fundamental.

### EQUITY STRESS / EQUITY VALUATION

**Disc-Transcript — EQUITY_STRESS (Score: 62)**
- "Stock prices are off 15 percent" over the intermeeting period (Kohn).
- "This year's stock market declines, forward earnings-price ratios are now back to levels well above the long-term real interest rate." (Gramlich)
- "Dramatic changes in accounting...shift to conservative bookkeeping would induce a significant decline in projected S&P earnings." (Greenspan)
- "Equity markets have seen their lows and it's only a question of how slowly or how rapidly the markets strengthen." (Jordan)

**Disc-Minutes — EQUITY_STRESS (Score: 62)**
- "Substantial decreases in equity wealth."
- Equity declines affecting consumer confidence and spending — members express concern about wealth effects from stock market declines.

**Staff-Minutes — EQUITY_VALUATION (Score: 48 ±10 [Low confidence])**
- "Broad equity indexes registered sizable gains" during intermeeting period.
- "Better-than-expected news on profits for several major corporations buoyed equity prices."

**Convergence note**: Discussion sources (both 62) reflect the cumulative equity market decline and its economic drag. Staff minutes (48) reflects the intermeeting period improvement — equity gains during the specific window before the meeting — rather than the broader 2002 equity bear market context. This is a genuine measurement frame difference, not a substantive disagreement.

## Cross-Source Summary

| Pattern | Observation |
|---|---|
| High agreement (gap ≤ 5) | INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN, DISSENT_INTENSITY, PRODUCTIVITY, EQUITY_STRESS (disc only) |

**Largest cross-track divergence**: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (discussion minutes: 60) vs. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (staff minutes: 38) — a 22-point nominal gap. Partly a polarity/construct difference (FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS scores tighter = higher; FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS scores accommodative = lower), partly a lens difference: participants emphasized the corporate credit tightening and equity wealth destruction, while staff's own forecast language emphasized "considerable monetary ease" and "low financing costs" at the policy rate level.

**Most consistent finding across all four sources**: Output gap clearly negative and widening (30–33), productivity strong (62–72), inflation below target and declining (22–28). These three conclusions are robust across sources.
