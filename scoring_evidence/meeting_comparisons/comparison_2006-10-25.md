# FOMC Meeting 2006-10-25: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL NOTE

The discussion **transcript** for this meeting covers pages 127–191 of the October 24–25, 2006 transcript. It contains exclusively a special committee discussion on whether to adopt an explicit numerical inflation objective / inflation targeting framework. It does **not** contain the standard economic outlook go-around, labor market assessment, financial conditions assessment, or policy rate deliberation. The standard economic discussion was held on Day 1 (pages 1–126), which is not captured in the scored transcript file.

The discussion **minutes** document contains the full synthesized summary of participant views from the complete two-day meeting, including both the economic outlook discussion and the policy decision.

This structural asymmetry means that for most dimensions other than INFLATION_PRESSURE and DISSENT_INTENSITY, the transcript scores near 50 with Low confidence due to absence of relevant discussion, while the minutes provide High-confidence assessments.

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

> "in the discussion around the table yesterday, many of us said that inflation, even in the forecast, is running a bit higher than our comfort zone. We know we've been above it for a couple of years, but with this forecast we'll be above our comfort zones for five years." — Ms. Bies

> "It might be hard, for example, to explain the virtues of a goal that's focused on a 1½ percent midpoint rather than the current inflation of 2½ percent." — Ms. Minehan

> "there seems to be a disconnect between the market's view of long-run inflation, which according to surveys and financial markets seems to remain around 2½ percent, and the statements made by many of us on this Committee who seem to be comfortable with 1 to 2 percent." — Mr. Plosser

> "episodes—in the spring of '05 and the spring of '06—when a surge in actual inflation raised questions in financial markets about our intentions." — Mr. Kohn

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants across the inflation targeting debate acknowledge current inflation (~2.3–2.5% core PCE) is running above their comfort zones, with several noting the forecast keeps it above comfort for years; treated as modestly-above-target and manageable.

---

**Minutes: 65 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "All meeting participants expressed concern about the outlook for inflation."

> "Nearly all participants viewed the current rates of core inflation as uncomfortably high and stressed the importance of further moderation."

> "All participants emphasized that the risks around the desired downward path to inflation remained to the upside."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Universal concern about elevated core inflation; risks firmly to the upside. No participant dissents from the inflation concern. Uncertainty band reflects differing views on pace of moderation only — some noted recent slowing "helped to allay their fears."

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Output gap is not characterized as a current condition in this document. Discussed only abstractly in the context of dual mandate and optimal policy path design. Score defaults to 50 with low confidence.

---

**Minutes: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "The ongoing adjustment in the housing market was likely to depress real activity in the near term."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Economy viewed as near potential but growth trajectory slightly below potential pace, with housing drag offset by resilient private demand. One participant noted possible broader weakness from oil price decline possibly reflecting weak global demand; most saw temporary factors.

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent (Lacker on rate), one clear minority participant opposing the inflation target framework (Minehan), two-to-three expressing strong reservations (Hoenig, Fisher, Warsh). Majority strongly favors direction with significant variation in implementation preferences. Framework dissent is asymmetric: 9+ for, 1 clearly against, 3 with reservations.
