### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 38 ±6 | Minutes score: 38 ±7**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Fischer: "The core PCE price deflator for the 12 months ending in January of this year was 1.7 percent per annum... the median 2016 increase in the core PCE deflator projected in the SEP is 1.6 percent."
- Brainard: "core inflation has remained stuck in the vicinity of 1¼ to 1½ percent for the past three years... we can't entirely rule out the risk that this lengthy underperformance of inflation may be having some depressing effect on inflation expectations."
- Tarullo: "I'm still not at the point at which I can have reasonable confidence that inflation will return to 2 percent within a reasonable period."
- Williams: "The news on inflation, in my view, has generally been good. The January jump in the core PCE to 1.7 percent... I continue to expect to see inflation gradually move back to our 2 percent target by the middle of 2018."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants see inflation as persistently below the 2% target — core PCE at 1.7% on one month's data vs. a trend of ~1.3–1.5% — with cautious optimism about eventual return to target but significant uncertainty about whether the January uptick is durable. Inflation expectations concerns add to the below-target picture.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Inflation picked up in recent months, but it continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective"
- "Participants continued to anticipate that inflation would run below the Committee's 2 percent objective in the near term"
- "Several participants indicated that the persistence of global disinflationary pressures or the possibility that inflation expectations were moving lower continued to pose downside risks to the inflation outlook"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation below target with recent pickup noted, but most expect it to remain below 2% near-term with downside risks from global disinflation.

**Minutes minority views:** "Some participants saw the increase as consistent with a firming trend"; "a few others expressed the view that there were also risks that could lead to inflation running higher than anticipated."

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 53 ±5 | Minutes score: 48 ±5**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Powell: "I see growth continuing at around 2 percent, modestly above potential."
- Williams: "With above-trend GDP growth this year, my projection has the labor market overshooting full employment by a substantial margin."
- Rosengren: "we are quite likely to exceed my estimate of full employment over the forecast horizon."
- Yellen: "I continue to anticipate that growth will be moderate and that labor market conditions will improve further."
- Lacker: "the economy is on a modestly above-potential growth track."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The clear majority characterizes growth as slightly above potential (~2% GDP vs. estimated 1.9–2.0% potential), with the output gap essentially closed or modestly positive. No participant argues the economy is meaningfully below potential.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Economic activity had been expanding moderately despite the global economic and financial developments"
- "Some participants judged that current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment"
- "Some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy near potential, with split views on whether slack remains. Center of gravity is just below potential.

**Minutes minority views:** Some see at/above maximum employment; some see remaining slack. Narrow band reflects views clustering near potential.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 53 ±5 | Minutes score: 49 ±7**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Fischer: "We are close to the maximum sustainable level of employment in the economy. The unemployment rate is close to most FOMC participants' estimates of the longer-run normal unemployment rate."
- Mester: "I believe the economy is basically at our goal of full employment from the perspective of monetary policy."
- George: "With an unemployment rate of 4.9 percent, the economy is near full employment."
- Tarullo: "there is some slack remaining, though, obviously, considerably less than when we were having those debates a couple of years ago."
- Brainard: "The employment-to-population ratio for prime-age workers remains 1¾ percentage points below pre-crisis levels. Involuntary part-time employment is still elevated, and wage growth has shown little sign of acceleration."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The majority places the labor market at or slightly above full employment, with a subset (Brainard, Tarullo, Yellen, Kashkari) arguing some residual slack remains. Wage growth is still modest, preventing the score from moving higher.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Some participants judged that current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment, noting that the unemployment rate was at or below their estimates of its longer-run normal level and citing anecdotal reports of labor shortages or increased wage pressures"
- "Some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment... the still-high rate of involuntary part-time employment and the low level of the employment-to-population ratio for prime-age workers"
- "The surprisingly limited extent to which aggregate data indicated upward pressure on wage growth also suggested some remaining slack"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Near full employment with genuine split on whether slack remains. Limited wage pressure keeps the score just under 50.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript score: 47 ±7 | Minutes score: 44 ±5**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Yellen: "household spending continues to be a relative bright spot in this economy... consumer spending is likely to increase at 2½ percent at an annual rate this quarter."
- Lockhart: "consumer spending remains at a relatively solid 2.7 percent annual growth rate... positive signals on consumer spending, strength of the labor market, and strong reports from the business community."
- Yellen: "spending and production indicators for other sectors of the economy have been weak. Orders and shipments of nondefense capital goods have been essentially flat for some time."
- Evans: "product lines related to capital expenditures continued to be soft."
- Rosengren: "consumption-led economy that is restrained by lackluster business fixed investment and weakness in net exports."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending is solid/at trend; business fixed investment is clearly weak (energy, global uncertainty, dollar effects); net exports are a persistent drag. The net assessment is slightly below trend, with consumer strength offset by capex and export weakness.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Household spending had been increasing at a moderate rate" and "motor vehicle sales remained particularly strong"
- "Business fixed investment and net exports had been soft"; "capital spending plans appeared to remain soft"
- "Declines in net exports to continue to subtract from real GDP growth"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Moderate consumer spending offset by soft business investment and weak net exports, placing the net slightly below trend.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 55 ±7 | Minutes score: 55 ±7**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Brainard: "The extreme financial difficulties faced by some firms in the energy sector have spilled over into financial markets, in which we saw a pronounced widening of corporate bond spreads. Since the middle of last year, triple-B spreads have increased about 75 basis points."
- Mester: "financial condition indexes... eased in February and March, reflecting the increase in stock prices, narrowing of credit spreads, and depreciation of the dollar in recent weeks. On balance, financial conditions are only a bit tighter than anticipated."
- Yellen: "the spread between the yield on triple-B corporate bonds and long-term Treasury yields up about 30 basis points" since December.
- Evans: "the GZ spread widened considerably... effects on GDP from such an exogenous GZ spread widening would be roughly equivalent to a 75 basis point tightening."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions tightened substantially in Jan–Feb, then largely (but not fully) reversed. By meeting date, conditions were described as "only a bit tighter" than December — slightly restrictive vs. neutral but well off the stress peak.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Financial conditions deteriorated, with equity prices falling and credit spreads on riskier corporate bonds widening. Subsequently, investor sentiment rebounded, and domestic and global financial conditions eased on net over the intermeeting period"
- "Also cited wider credit spreads as a factor that was likely to restrain growth in demand"
- "Longer-term interest rates and market participants' expectations for the future path of the federal funds rate remained lower"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Conditions tightened then partially reversed. Net: somewhat tighter than before the turbulence, with credit spreads still wider. Lower rate expectations partially offset.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 45 ±8 | Minutes score: 44 ±7**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Lacker: "Accordingly, I favor alternative C... Raising rates today would be a sign of our confidence in the real economic prospects for our economy." [formal dissent/hike advocate]
- George: "a 25 basis point increase at this meeting is consistent with the economy's fundamentals." [dissenting on policy prescription]
- Brainard: "monetary policy should focus on carefully preserving and protecting the areas of strength in our economy... to guard against the downside risks posed by the current environment." [dovish hold advocate]
- Evans: "A risk-management approach also continues to argue for no change in rates today and a plan for only a couple of increases in 2016."
- Dudley: "If inflation expectations were less damped and the international situation was less unsettled, I would favor tightening at this meeting, actually. But, to me, the risk-management considerations argue for caution right now."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Overwhelming majority favors hold (alternative B); one formal dissent (Lacker for hike); one implicit dissent (George). The majority hold is accompanied by a dovish risk-management lean, with the forward path revised down from December (from implied 4 hikes to ~2).

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Most participants judged it appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at ¼ to ½ percent at this meeting"
- "Many participants expressed the view that a somewhat lower path for the federal funds rate than they had projected in December now seemed most likely to be appropriate"
- "A couple of participants, however, saw an increase in the target range to ½ to ¾ percent as appropriate at this meeting"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Hold with dovish lean — most favor patience, many see lower rate path than December. Still in gradual normalization framework but slower pace.

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript score: 43 ±8 | Minutes score: 33 ±5**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Lacker votes for alternative C (formal dissent — higher rate preferred).
- George: "I support the language in alternative B for paragraphs 1 and 2, but I do not agree with its policy prescription" (functional dissent, though she voted for B).

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** One formal dissent plus substantive forward-path disagreement (hawks vs. doves) and a methodological disagreement about SEP communication (Bullard). This is more than a typical one-dissent meeting but far from a multi-dissent, openly split meeting.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "One member, however, preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate"
- Split views on labor market slack: "some" at max employment vs. "some other" not yet reached
- "Participants expressed a range of views about the likelihood that incoming information would make an adjustment appropriate at the time of their next meeting"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** One formal dissent with moderate differences on labor market assessment and timing of next move. Typical for the era.

### Dimension 8: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript score: 67 ±7 | Minutes score: 48 ±6**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Evans: extensive VAR analysis of GZ spread widening, quantifying tightening impact; discussion of "recurrent risk-off episodes."
- Brainard: detailed discussion of credit spreads, dollar appreciation/tightening feedback, global policy exhaustion, endogeneity of financial conditions to policy path.
- Rosengren: "bank stocks have been particularly volatile. Bank stock prices in Europe were exceptionally hard hit... I have been surprised by the financial sensitivity of these banks during a time when the real economic data have remained relatively benign."
- Dudley: extensive discussion of negative interest rate policies in Japan and Europe, ECB "running out of ammo" narrative, feedback to U.S. financial conditions; bank net interest margin concerns.
- Kaplan: "I believe we will continue to be vulnerable to periodic bouts of financial market tightening."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial markets, conditions, and stability concerns are a co-dominant theme of the meeting alongside the domestic economic assessment, discussed at length by most participants. Clearly above typical 2014–2019 levels of attention.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Many participants expressed a view that the global economic and financial situation still posed appreciable downside risks to the domestic economic outlook"
- "Recent financial market turbulence provided an important reminder that the ability of central banks to offset the effects of adverse economic shocks might be limited"
- Multiple paragraphs discussing financial conditions, global financial developments, and their implications for policy

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions and global financial risks receive moderate-to-substantial attention, woven throughout the discussion and directly influencing policy deliberations.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript score: 60 ±8 | Minutes score: 55 ±8**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Fischer: "We should be careful not to say anything that takes either April or June off the table."
- Yellen: "I do expect that further gradual increases in our target range will be appropriate if the labor market continues to improve, even if the risks pertaining to global conditions do not significantly subside."
- Dudley: "I would be inclined to move again in the near future, presumably at the June meeting."
- Evans: "plan for only a couple of increases in 2016" [most dovish forward signal among the majority].
- Brainard: "one reason why financial conditions have eased recently is precisely because market participants now judge we are likely to go more slowly... if we were to signal a materially steeper path, we would see a broader financial tightening." [implicit signal to stay gradual]

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is clearly tightening-directional — more hikes are coming — but with a "gradual," "data-dependent," and "cautious" character. The SEP median has moved toward 2 hikes in 2016. April is noted as possible but not expected; June is the modal next move.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate"
- "Several members noted that their current projections of the path for the federal funds rate that would likely be appropriate this year and next were lower than they had projected in December"
- "Several expressed the view that a cautious approach to raising rates would be prudent or noted their concern that raising the target range as soon as April would signal a sense of urgency they did not think appropriate"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Forward signal is mild tightening — still on a gradual hiking path but at a lower and more cautious pace than previously projected.

**Minutes minority views:** "Some other participants indicated that an increase in the target range at the Committee's next meeting might well be warranted."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Elevated uncertainty driven by global risks, unresolved factors abroad, and asymmetric policy capacity. Beyond normal hedging but not extreme.

### Staff Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 33 ±5 [Moderate] | Minutes score: 38 ±5 [High]**

**Transcript evidence:** Core PCE ~1.7%. Energy depressing headline. Import prices falling. Gradual return to target projected.

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Inflation below target, persistent disinflationary forces.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "Consumer price inflation was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by decreases in both consumer energy prices and the prices of non-energy imports."
- "Core PCE price inflation...was 1 3/4 percent over the same 12-month period."
- Staff projects inflation "still to be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation is clearly below target with multiple restraining factors, and staff projects it will remain below target for years. Risks weighted to the downside.
