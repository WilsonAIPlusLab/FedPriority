# FOMC Meeting 1995-02-01: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 60 +/-5, High confidence)

> "the economy now has attained levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation, to say nothing of progress toward lower inflation" — MR. PARRY

> "we expect CPI inflation to rise perceptibly to ranges in the neighborhood of 3-1/2 percent by the second half of 1995" — MR. MOSKOW

> "most private forecasters expect little improvement on inflation over the next two years...Blue Chip report predicts 3-1/2 percent inflation" — MR. MELZER

> "I have been wrestling a great deal over the question of whether this is an important enough difference to merit a dissent. I finally decided it is not." [Blinder on preferring to wait, citing favorable Q4 inflation data — minority view]

Center of gravity: Participants see inflation as modestly above an acceptable level (running ~2.7-3%) with significant upward risk given the tight economy. Not yet accelerating at the consumer level, but widely expected to rise to 3-3.5%. Blinder notes a "good inflation surprise in the fourth quarter" as a minority offset.

**Minutes** (Score: 62 +/-6, High confidence)

> "most of the members concluded that some rise in inflation appeared probable over coming quarters, and they were concerned that this upturn would not be reversed and could be extended in the absence of further monetary restraint"

> "the economy had attained levels of labor and capital utilization that implied a strong risk of rising inflation over coming quarters"

> "projections of the rate of inflation, as indexed by the consumer price index, had a central tendency of 3 to 3-1/2 percent"

> "had not led thus far to a broad pickup in inflation as measured by the prices of final goods and services" [counterbalancing note]

Center of gravity: Members broadly perceive building inflation pressure from tight resource utilization, with CPI expected to rise to 3-3.5%. Pressure is anticipated and building rather than fully realized, placing this clearly above neutral but below the "persistent/concerning" range.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 60 +/-5, High confidence)

> "the economy continues to grow faster than its potential" — MR. MOSKOW

> "the U.S. economy continues to grow more rapidly than forecasters had expected and likely more rapidly than can be sustained, with a four-quarter growth rate...of about 4 percent" — MR. MELZER

> "the economy has not shown very much in the way of a slowdown...the data are uniformly very strong" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "tentative signs, not necessarily persuasive but definitely beginning to appear" [of slowdown — minority qualifier] — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Virtually unanimous view that the economy is running above potential, with 4% growth in 1994 vs ~2.5% potential. Some tentative signs of slowing but not yet convincing. Blinder and Yellen see greater possibility of imminent slowdown but acknowledge the economy appears above potential currently.

**Minutes** (Score: 60 +/-5, High confidence)

> "an economy that already seemed to be operating at, and perhaps beyond, sustainable capacity levels and to be continuing to expand at a pace above its long-run potential"

> "capacity utilization continued to move up from already high levels"

> "growth in real GDP had a central tendency of 2 to 3 percent, compared with a growth rate of 4 percent estimated for 1994"

> "several emphasized... anecdotal evidence did not currently point to any significant moderation" [counterpoint to signs of slowing]

Center of gravity: Economy clearly operating above potential with strong momentum, though expected to moderate. The "at, and perhaps beyond" language and above-trend growth rate place this solidly above 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 63 +/-5, High confidence)

> "labor markets remain quite tight. In many metropolitan areas, the reported unemployment rate is below 4 percent, including 3-1/2 percent in metropolitan Detroit." — MR. MOSKOW

> "labor shortages in many parts of the District" — MR. STERN

> "contacts throughout the Eighth District report that labor markets are the tightest they have seen in years" — MR. MELZER

> "average workweek last year was about 2 hours longer" [than normal] — MR. JORDAN [alongside note that manufacturing wages rose only 1.1%]

Center of gravity: Unemployment at 5.4% is well below the 6% NAIRU. Multiple districts report shortages, tight labor markets, below-4% unemployment in metro areas. However, wage pressures remain surprisingly contained (ECI shows little pressure, Jordan notes manufacturing wages up only 1.1%).

**Minutes** (Score: 57 +/-5, High confidence)

> "civilian unemployment rate declined to 5.4 percent"

> "projections of the rate in the fourth quarter of 1995 were centered in a narrow range around 5-1/2 percent"

> "The high levels of resource utilization implied by these projections were viewed by most members as likely to foster somewhat greater pressure on wages and prices"

> "current pressures on labor and other producer resources, which many members saw as portending higher inflation"

Center of gravity: Labor market tight enough to generate inflation concern, with unemployment at 5.4% and expected to remain low. Moderately tight but the minutes characterize this more through the inflation-risk lens than the district-level "shortages" language of the transcript.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 59 +/-7, High confidence)

> "backlogs are continuing to rise...forward commitments continue to stretch out...profit figures remain quite extraordinary" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "surprising and unanticipated slowdown in retail sales in November and December" — MS. YELLEN

> "the consumer is simply overextended. Of the roughly $250 billion increase in personal consumption expenditures...44 percent was put on installment credit" — MR. LINDSEY

Center of gravity: Business investment is uniformly strong with rising backlogs and robust profits. Consumer spending is showing mixed signals -- strong through most of 1994 but softening at year-end. Housing beginning to weaken. Autos showing moderation. Net position is above trend but with some cracks appearing.

**Minutes** (Score: 60 +/-7, High confidence)

> "real business-fixed investment had strengthened further in the fourth quarter" with "considerable momentum"

> "nonresidential construction was now trending higher, with particular strength evident in industrial and commercial construction"

> "sluggish behavior of non-auto retail sales in November and December" but "consumer confidence was at a high level"

> "The expansion continued to display appreciable momentum"

Center of gravity: Business investment is very strong; consumer spending shows mixed signals but overall economy has "appreciable momentum." Net position is above trend, though expected to moderate. The strength in capital goods offsets softness in retail.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "it is very difficult to find any evidence that monetary tightening is constraining debt flows...C&I loans are just extraordinary...we are still getting some easing in credit terms" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "quality spreads within the financial markets also attest to the fact that the forward risk premiums implicit in capital investment are quite modest" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "First District banks showed no relaxation of credit standards...relatively stable loan demand" [a more cautious district note] — MS. MINEHAN

Center of gravity: Despite 300bp of rate hikes, financial conditions remain relatively accommodative. Credit is flowing freely, bank lending is robust, credit terms are actually easing. The bond market is reasonably well-behaved. Conditions are not yet restrictive. Score is below neutral, but transcript gives a somewhat less extreme reading than minutes.

**Minutes** (Score: 27 +/-5, High confidence)

> "they saw little indication that policy tightening actions over the past year were constraining the availability of credit to any observable degree"

> "financial conditions remained broadly supportive of further economic expansion"

> "relatively narrow quality spreads in debt markets attested to a considerable degree of confidence among investors"

> "accommodative lending policies of banking institutions... had encouraged rapid growth in consumer and business loans"

Center of gravity: Despite a year of rate hikes, financial conditions remained clearly accommodative/loose. Credit freely available, spreads narrow, bank lending aggressive. This is well below neutral. The minutes language is stronger and more definitive on accommodation than the transcript.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 67 +/-5, High confidence)

> "raising both the funds rate and the discount rate by 50 basis points makes the most sense" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I think we will be truly symmetric if we raise the federal funds rate to 6 percent" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I do not intend to dissent...the differences that we have largely concern tactics and not strategy" — MS. YELLEN [supporting the hike despite preference to wait]

> "additional tightening probably will be needed in the future" — MR. PARRY [most hawkish minority within the consensus]

Center of gravity: Strong consensus for 50bp hike. Most participants see continuing inflationary risks warranting action. However, the symmetric directive and signals this may be the last hike moderate the hawkishness. Blinder and Yellen would have preferred to wait but chose not to dissent.

**Minutes** (Score: 72 +/-5, High confidence)

> "all the members indicated that they could support some firming in reserve conditions"

> "Most of the members were convinced that current monetary policy should be adjusted promptly to a more clearly restrictive stance"

> "prompt action was needed to counter inflationary pressures and inflationary expectations"

> "a delay in tightening policy would incur an unacceptable risk of allowing further inflationary momentum"

Center of gravity: Strong hawkish consensus for immediate tightening, with only "a few" preferring a short delay (who still voted yes). The 50bp hike with unanimous support reflects strong hawkish conviction. Minutes score is higher than transcript because the synthesized language emphasizes the urgency and consensus of the tightening decision more than the individual qualifications visible in the transcript.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 30 +/-5, High confidence)

> "I have been wrestling a great deal over the question of whether this is an important enough difference to merit a dissent. I finally decided it is not." — MR. BLINDER

> "I do not intend to dissent...the differences that we have largely concern tactics and not strategy" — MS. YELLEN

> Unanimous vote on rates; contested votes on Mexico swap/warehousing arrangements

Center of gravity: No formal dissents on monetary policy. Notable tactical disagreements (Blinder, Yellen prefer to wait; Parry wants asymmetric language) but strategic agreement. Deeper disagreements on Mexico involvement visible in transcript but not on core monetary policy.

**Minutes** (Score: 22 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> "a few preferred to delay such an action" but "indicated that they would join with the other members in voting to tighten"

> "Opinions differed to some degree with regard to both the likely extent of the prospective slowing in economic growth and the outlook for inflation"

> Both monetary policy votes were 12-0 unanimous

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on policy action with minor differences in preferred timing. The "a few preferred delay" camp is a small minority that joined consensus. Minutes show less underlying tension than is visible in the transcript, where individual reservations were expressed more fully.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript** (Score: 60 +/-8, High confidence)

> "The United States is just barely investment grade...I don't think we have much leeway on the down side" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN [on the dollar]

> "we have taken a lot of the bubble out of the market...we have taken the degrees of instability that one can envisage in stock prices down to a much reduced level" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> Extended Mexico crisis discussion dominates large portions of both meeting days (ESF, swap lines, warehousing, systemic risk); securities industry earnings down 50-80% noted by McDonough

Center of gravity: Financial stability concerns are prominent in the transcript, driven primarily by the Mexico crisis and its potential contagion effects. Dollar vulnerability is a key policy consideration. Orange County and financial sector stress in New York also discussed. The transcript score is notably higher because the full extent of the Mexico debate is visible.

**Minutes** (Score: 28 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "Some members expressed concern that a number of banks might have eased their lending standards unduly and thus assumed unwarranted risks in their loan portfolios"

> "The performance of stock market prices and the relatively narrow quality spreads in debt markets attested to a considerable degree of confidence among investors"

> Extensive Mexico financial crisis discussion present but summarized more briefly than in transcript

Center of gravity: Financial stability receives limited but present discussion in the minutes. Bank lending standards concern is a notable mention. Mexico financial crisis is discussed but more as a trade/foreign policy issue than domestic financial stability. The large gap between transcript (60) and minutes (28) reflects that the minutes substantially compressed the Mexico discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 58 +/-7, High confidence)

> "it is by no means evident that if this cracking that we have seen continues...there will be another tightening of policy" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "reasonably likely in my view that this will be the last interest rate increase" — MR. McDONOUGH

> "on the basis of current information, I am envisioning only this 50 basis point increase" — MS. YELLEN

> "additional tightening probably will be needed in the future" — MR. PARRY [dissenting forward view]

Center of gravity: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- the committee just hiked 50bp and chose a symmetric directive. Most participants suggest this may be the last hike, but data dependence is emphasized. A few (Parry) see more tightening ahead.

**Minutes** (Score: 60 +/-6, High confidence)

> "they did not believe that there should be a presumption toward possible further tightening in this period"

> "further monetary restraint might not be needed if... the incoming evidence on the performance of the economy suggested that the expansion was moderating sufficiently"

> "the Committee's most difficult decision over the next several quarters was likely to be that of determining when further tightening was no longer desirable"

Center of gravity: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- they just hiked but explicitly adopted a symmetric (neutral) forward posture, suggesting this could be the last hike if the economy moderates as expected. The forward signal is notably less hawkish than the current action.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 55 +/-6, High confidence)

> "my level of uncertainty about where things are headed is higher than at any previous time over the last six months...the potential forecast error at this point is extremely high" — MS. YELLEN

> "tentative signs, not necessarily persuasive but definitely beginning to appear" [of slowdown] — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> Kelley outlines wide spectrum from "economy slowing now" to "roaring along unimpeded at 4 percent plus"

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven by conflicting signals -- strong data vs. early hints of slowdown, Mexico crisis, unknown NAIRU. Multiple participants explicitly flag high uncertainty about the growth trajectory.

**Minutes** (Score: 48 +/-6, High confidence)

> "their current projections were subject to substantial risks"

> "A key uncertainty in the outlook was whether the slowing in overall economic growth would be sufficient to relieve the current pressures"

> "Fiscal policy was under active debate in the Congress, and the members viewed the outcome of that debate as very uncertain"

> "the precise timing and extent were impossible to predict" [inventory adjustment]

Center of gravity: Multiple sources of uncertainty explicitly acknowledged -- fiscal policy, inventory timing, whether growth will actually slow enough. The word "substantial" applied to risks elevates this. However, members had relatively clear views on the direction of the economy and policy, suggesting the uncertainty is real but bounded. Transcript score is higher, reflecting more vivid individual expressions of uncertainty (Yellen's "highest at any time").

## Notable Gaps and Flags

**Largest gap — FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (transcript 60 vs minutes 28)**: The transcript reveals an extremely lengthy Mexico peso crisis debate spanning both meeting days, including ESF arrangements, swap lines, dollar vulnerability, and systemic contagion risk. The minutes compress this substantially, with Mexico discussed but primarily as a trade/foreign policy consideration rather than a financial stability concern. The transcript score reflects the actual prominence of financial stability language in the meeting room.

**Large gap — FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (transcript 40 vs minutes 27)**: Both sources agree conditions are accommodative, but the minutes use stronger and more categorical language ("little indication," "broadly supportive," "accommodative lending") compared to the more hedged transcript language. Both are below neutral; the minutes place conditions further into accommodative territory.
