### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 20 ±5 [High]**

Center of gravity: The overwhelming consensus is that inflation is meaningfully below target with disinflation risk ongoing. Market-based core PCE ~1.25%, unit labor costs falling, resource slack large.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "there is little evidence of inflationary pressures. A variety of models used by the Boston Fed expect continued disinflation… the very high unemployment rate relative to any reasonable estimate of full employment suggests further disinflation."
- Yellen: "inflation is undesirably low, and, even with large monthly employment gains, the level of resource slack will remain high for an extended period."
- Kohn: "I expect intense competition among workers and businesses to keep costs low and the underlying trend of inflation stable at a low rate, that is, a rate below my objective and/or perhaps even trending lower. Market-based core PCE has continued to go lower to around 1¼ percent."
- Fisher (relative hawk): "the fraction of components in the PCE registering price declines remains elevated and sustained…I remain unalarmed."

Minority views: Plosser and Bullard see medium-term upside inflation risk (2-5 year horizon) from excess reserves, but explicitly place current inflation as non-alarming. Lacker: "case for further disinflation continues to weaken."

---

**Minutes — Score: 23 ±8 [High]**

Center of gravity: The clear consensus was that inflation was subdued and expected to remain so, driven by substantial economic slack and anchored expectations, though views on medium-term risks were divided between downside and upside.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants agreed that underlying inflation currently was subdued and was likely to remain so for some time."
- "Most participants anticipated that substantial slack in labor and product markets, along with well-anchored inflation expectations, would keep inflation subdued in the near term."
- "Some noted the risk that, over the next couple of years, inflation could edge further below the rates they judged most consistent with the Federal Reserve's dual mandate."
- "Some saw inflation risks as tilted to the downside... But others felt that inflation risks were tilted to the upside, particularly in the medium term."

Minority views: "Some" saw upside risks from commodities, import prices, and potential future excess reserve drawdowns, while "some" saw downside risks from slack.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 20 ±5 [High]**

Center of gravity: Economy is deeply below potential. Some growth signs emerging (positive Q3/Q4 GDP) but unemployment at 10% and output still well below potential. Recovery has begun but gap is large.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "economic activity has fallen so far below where it ought to be… the level of resource slack will remain high for an extended period."
- Bernanke: "the employment-to-population ratio has fallen from a peak of 62.7 percent to 58.5…aggregate weekly hours of production workers has fallen more than 8 percent from the peak."
- Rosengren: "even if GDP growth exceeds potential, as I expect it will over the coming quarters, this will likely reduce the unemployment rate only gradually."
- Kohn: "in an environment of high unemployment and excess capital capacity."

Minority views: Some (Plosser, Lacker) argue the "true" output gap may be smaller than conventional measures suggest, but this is a methodological dispute, not a claim the economy is at or above potential.

---

**Minutes — Score: 24 ±5 [High]**

Center of gravity: Participants saw the economy as far below potential with substantial slack that would diminish only gradually, consistent with a deep recession that was beginning to recover.

Representative quotes:
- "Substantial slack in labor and product markets."
- "Resource slack was expected to diminish only slowly."
- "The projected pace of real output growth… was expected to exceed that of potential output by only enough to produce a very gradual reduction in economic slack."
- "Most anticipated that the pickup in output and employment growth would be rather slow relative to past recoveries from deep recessions."

Minority views: Narrow disagreement on this point — "participants agreed" growth was strengthening, but all recognized substantial slack.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 18 ±4 [High]**

Center of gravity: 10% unemployment, massive labor underutilization across multiple dimensions (hours, part-time, discouraged workers). The Greenbook projects unemployment at 9.6% end of 2010 even with assumed recovery. Firmly in the 16-30 range.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "the economy losing only 11,000 jobs…this is still moving in the wrong direction…Initial claims for unemployment insurance remain quite elevated."
- Yellen: "far below maximum sustainable employment…one of them said, 'It's become fashionable not to be hiring.'"
- Bernanke summary: "although job losses have moderated, firms remain quite reluctant to hire, and conditions overall remain very weak."
- Fisher: "More small firms…still plan on reducing employment than plan on adding to their payrolls."

Minority views: None materially dispute labor market weakness.

---

**Minutes — Score: 20 ±5 [High]**

Center of gravity: Labor markets were deeply slack with unemployment elevated, hiring minimal, a record-low employment-to-population ratio, and widespread expectations of only gradual improvement.

Representative quotes:

Minority views: Bullard and Vice Chairman Dudley note some upside surprises (transportation, Christmas activity). Lacker flags retail revenue edging positive for first time since 2007.

Center of gravity: Overwhelming majority supports maintaining max accommodation (alt B with "extended period"). The QE wind-down is framed as technical/mechanical, not tightening. Slightly above 15 because 3-4 hawks explicitly want less accommodative language.

Representative quotes:

Minority views: Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker prefer "some time" over "extended period" and would accept smaller asset purchases. None advocate actual rate hikes.
