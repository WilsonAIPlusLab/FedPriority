## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 32 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "With PCE inflation at 0.7 percent, we are now further from our publicly stated inflation target of 2 percent than the Bank of Japan is from its publicly stated target." (Rosengren)
- "Total PCE inflation moved down to 0.7 percent in October, year over year, and core PCE inflation remains at 1.1 percent... we are well below this level." (Evans)
- "core and headline inflation have fallen to around 1 percent, and the outlook is that inflation will remain muted. The DSGE models all project that inflation will be below 1.8 percent through 2016." (Kocherlakota)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: The overwhelming participant consensus is that inflation is well below the 2% target (0.7% headline, 1.1% core), with a muted outlook and multiple speakers raising deflation risk concerns — placing this firmly in the 26-40 disinflation band, at the lower end given the severity of undershoot.

**Transcript Minority views**: Fisher is comparatively unconcerned, citing stable trimmed-mean PCE at 1.3% for seven months and arguing deflation risk is mitigated. Dudley also less worried than others.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "Inflation continued to run noticeably below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent"
- "many participants expressed concern about the deceleration in consumer prices over the past year"
- "Among the costs of very low or declining inflation that were cited were its effects in raising real interest rates and debt burdens"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Inflation clearly below target with active concern about the low level and its economic costs; not deflation alarm but meaningful worry about persistent undershooting.

**Minutes Minority views**: "Some participants suggested that inflation, while low, was unlikely to slow further, pointing to core, trimmed mean, or sticky-price inflation measures" — this moderates the concern somewhat.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 39 ±5 [High confidence] | Minutes: 39 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "output growth has not yet moved noticeably above the 2 percent range" and projects "real GDP projected to expand just 2¼ percent." (Yellen)
- "we have not, in my view at least, definitively broken out of the 2 percent annualized growth trajectory we've been on for several years." (Dudley)
- "somewhat more upbeat picture" but most forecasts "did not make substantial changes." (Bernanke summary)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing at or slightly below potential (~2-2.5%), with most participants forecasting a pickup to ~3% in 2014 but not yet there. Slack remains in most assessments.

**Transcript Minority views**: Williams and Bullard are somewhat more optimistic, arguing the drag factors are waning. Lacker is more pessimistic, projecting only ~2% growth.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "economy was expanding at a moderate pace"
- "considerable slack remaining in the labor market" and unemployment "remained elevated"
- "almost all participants continued to project that the rate of growth of economic activity would strengthen in coming years"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Economy below potential with persistent slack, but recovery solidifying and outlook improving. Moderate expansion, not yet closing the gap.

**Minutes Minority views**: "A few participants raised the possibility that recent declines in inflation might suggest that the economic recovery was not as strong as some thought."

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 39 ±6 [High confidence] | Minutes: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "the unemployment rate, which, at 7 percent, remains above the peak unemployment rate of the previous recession." (Rosengren)
- "there is still a long way to go" on maximum employment. (Yellen)
- "I would characterize the marked decline in the unemployment rate since we started our latest asset purchase program as representing substantial progress toward our maximum employment goal" — but still at 7%, well above NAIRU. (Williams)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 7.0% with NAIRU estimated around 5.2-5.5% represents meaningful slack in the labor market. Substantial improvement since September 2012 is acknowledged, but full employment remains distant.

**Transcript Minority views**: Kocherlakota argues the employment-to-population ratio for prime-age workers is essentially flat for two years, suggesting the labor market is even weaker than the unemployment rate implies. Tarullo is skeptical of conclusions about structural vs. cyclical participation decline.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "increases in nonfarm payroll employment of more than 200,000 per month in October and November and the decline in the unemployment rate to 7 percent as encouraging signs"
- "several others continued to see important elements of cyclical weakness in the low labor force participation rate and cited other indicators of considerable slack in the labor market, including the still-high levels of long-duration unemployment and of workers employed part time for economic reasons"
- "most measures of wages had not accelerated"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving meaningfully but still well below full employment with considerable slack across multiple dimensions.

**Minutes Minority views**: "Some participants cited research that found that demographic and other structural factors... accounted for much of the recent decline in participation" (implying less slack than headline suggests).

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Transcript: 44 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 46 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "The recent increases in retail sales, motor vehicle demand, and sentiment point to a healthy pickup in consumer spending in the current quarter." (Evans)
- "Business fixed investment still seems inexplicably weak relative to the fundamentals. We have a rising stock market, high cash flow, relatively clean balance sheets, and the level of investment is not high enough to really grow capacity at any meaningful pace." (Dudley)
- "it's worth noting that business investment and new capital goods orders have been relatively weak of late, and housing has been relatively weak as well." (Lacker)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is picking up modestly from depressed levels, but business capital spending is conspicuously weak and housing has slowed due to higher mortgage rates. The net position is below-to-at trend — approaching the lower end of "at trend."

**Transcript Minority views**: Williams and Stein are more upbeat on consumer spending momentum; Lacker remains skeptical about whether the spending pickup is durable.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "Consumer spending appeared to be strengthening, with solid gains in retail sales in recent months and a rebound in motor vehicle sales"
- "Business investment appeared to be advancing at a moderate rate"
- "Activity in the housing sector slowed in recent months"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending strengthening and business investment moderate, offset partially by housing slowdown. Net position approximately at trend with mild upward momentum.

**Minutes Minority views**: "A couple of participants reported that their contacts continued to focus on investments intended to reduce costs and were still cautious regarding investment to expand capacity."

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence] | Minutes: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "Sustained low interest rates, abundant credit, improved household and strong corporate finances, and pent-up demand for durable goods are providing the basis for a slight acceleration." (Fisher)
- "we are seeing loan tenor, structure, and pricing pressures in our high-growth area...we very clearly see banks rushing in to provide credit." (Fisher)
- Stein: concern about "reaching-for-yield pressures may continue to build" — implying conditions are currently very loose.

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose, with low interest rates, abundant credit, competitive bank lending, and rising asset prices. The financial stability concern is precisely that conditions are too easy.

**Transcript Minority views**: Tarullo warns that wealth-effect-dependent consumption is vulnerable to any tightening — acknowledging the fragility of current loose conditions.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "the projected improvement in economic activity was expected to be supported by... a further easing of credit conditions"
- "current market expectations were reasonably well aligned with the Committee's recent policy communications"
- Vulnerability concerns about "rise in forward price-to-earnings ratios," "increased level of equity repurchases," "rise in margin credit," "increase in issuance of leveraged loans" — all indicating loose conditions

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain accommodative/loose; credit easing continues. Concerns about misinterpretation of taper are about maintaining this looseness, not about existing tightness.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 57 ±8 [High confidence] | Minutes: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- Majority decision: Alternative B (taper $10B in asset purchases beginning January), supported by ~14 of 17 participants.
- "I think the time has come to begin to reduce the pace of asset purchases at this meeting." (Dudley)
- "My recommendation will be that the Committee not taper at this meeting." (Kocherlakota, dissent) / "My strong preference is to do nothing at this meeting." (Rosengren)
- "I can support the current alternative B" as a "third-best policy." (Evans, reluctant)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: The majority favors beginning normalization via a modest $10B taper, marking the first concrete step toward unwinding accommodation. However, the forward guidance is explicitly dovish (rates low "well past" the 6.5% threshold), and several key participants are reluctant or opposed. The action is a lean-hawkish step with strong dovish guardrails.

**Transcript Minority views**: 2 clear dissenters (Kocherlakota, Rosengren), 1 reluctant third-choice supporter (Evans), and multiple participants wanting either a larger taper (Plosser, Lockhart, George, Fisher, Lacker) or more certainty in the path.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "most participants saw a reduction in the pace of purchases as appropriate at this meeting" (modest $10B taper)
- "many judged that the Committee should proceed cautiously in taking its first action to reduce the pace of asset purchases"
- "In the view of one member, a reduction in the pace of purchases was premature"
- "almost all members agreed to add language indicating... it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Modest hawkish step (beginning taper) from a deeply accommodative baseline, counterbalanced by strengthened dovish rate guidance and cautious tone. Still firmly in accommodative territory.

**Minutes Minority views**: "Some" wanted to continue purchases unchanged; "some other participants preferred a larger reduction" — spread across both directions.

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Transcript: 45 ±8 [High confidence] | Minutes: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- Kocherlakota: explicitly recommends against tapering; will likely dissent formally.
- "I cannot support the current alternative B." (Rosengren)
- Evans: supports B as "third-best policy" only, with significant reservations about financial conditions tightening.
- "my instincts would lean more with Narayana and Eric" but supports B. (Tarullo)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Meaningful disagreement across multiple dimensions — timing of taper, size of taper, forward guidance design, interpretation of labor slack, and importance of low inflation. Two formal dissents likely; several reluctant supporters. The disagreement extends to fundamental economic questions (Phillips curve, optimal control, how to read the unemployment rate), not just tactics.

**Transcript Minority views**: The disagreement is multi-directional — some want more aggressive tapering (Plosser, Lockhart), others oppose any tapering (Kocherlakota, Rosengren).

#### Minutes Evidence
- One member viewed "a reduction in the pace of purchases was premature"
- "Some also expressed concern about the potential for an unintended tightening"
- Multiple camps: some wanted no taper, most favored modest taper, "some other participants preferred a larger reduction," "a few proposed... a more deterministic path"
- Forward guidance also debated: "a few members suggested lowering the unemployment threshold to 6 percent" vs. "most members wanted to make no change"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: One dissent with notable tactical disagreement across multiple camps on pace and forward guidance, but broad agreement on direction.

### Dimension 8: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Transcript: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence] | Minutes: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- Stein: extensive presentation on financial stability thresholds, reaching-for-yield, and the "worry index" from 4 to potentially 7-8 if rates stay at zero through 2017-18.
- "the most convincing cost [of QE] was financial stability." (Bernanke)
- "I am open...to the inclusion of a financial stability knockout clause." (Kocherlakota)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a meaningful but not dominant topic. Stein gives the most substantive presentation; Bernanke explicitly names it as the most compelling cost of continued QE; several others make passing references. It informs but does not dominate the policy discussion.

**Transcript Minority views**: Most participants are not alarmed about current conditions; Stein and George are the most emphatic. The concern is prospective (building risks) rather than current stress.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "Participants also reviewed indicators of financial vulnerabilities that could pose risks to financial stability and the broader economy"
- "several participants commented on the rise in forward price-to-earnings ratios for some small-cap stocks, the increased level of equity repurchases, or the rise in margin credit"
- "A couple of participants offered views on the role of financial stability in monetary policy decisionmaking more broadly"

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Dedicated paragraph with several participants engaging on specific financial vulnerability indicators. More than passing mention but not a dominant theme.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 18 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 15 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- "I think it's appropriate to wait to raise the funds rate target well past the time that unemployment declines below 6½ percent. Under my modal outlook...I consider it appropriate to defer liftoff until late 2015." (Yellen)
- Evans: calls for strengthening the statement to emphasize low inflation monitoring; prefers lowering the unemployment threshold to 6%.
- Alternative B paragraph 5: funds rate to remain low "well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6½ percent" — "especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal."

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly dovish — an explicit commitment to holding rates near zero well past the already-low 6.5% unemployment threshold, with inflation referenced as an additional reason to delay liftoff. Multiple participants (Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota) want even more dovish guidance.

**Transcript Minority views**: Plosser, George, and Lacker prefer less forward guidance extension or a different formulation. Plosser prefers articulating the reaction function rather than extending qualitative time-based guidance.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "almost all members agreed to add language indicating... it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's longer-run objective"
- "several participants saw the need to clearly convey that inflation remains an important consideration in adjusting the target funds rate"
- Threshold-based forward guidance still in place (6.5% unemployment)

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Forward guidance is strongly dovish — rates to remain low well past the already-dovish threshold, reinforced with inflation conditionality. This is a strengthening of the low-for-long commitment.

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Transcript: 40 ±8 [High confidence] | Minutes: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- Lacker: attaches a "Lockhart asterisk" reflecting "weak conviction" to his forecast.
- "I am still far from certain, and in thinking about not only our policy decision today but also what and how we communicate about our likely decisions going forward, I find it important to contemplate several alternatives." (Yellen)
- "we are going to need to be a little more tentative and pragmatic in decisionmaking over the course of the next several years." (Tarullo)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is above normal — particularly around the inflation puzzle (why is inflation so low, what will bring it back), the labor force participation debate (structural vs. cyclical), and the timing/market reaction to tapering. However, the baseline modal forecast is reasonably clear, and most participants have not dramatically revised their projections.

**Transcript Minority views**: Williams and Fisher are more confident in their respective assessments; Kocherlakota and Rosengren express deeper uncertainty about whether the baseline scenario will materialize.

#### Minutes Evidence
- "most expressed greater confidence in the outlook and saw the risks associated with their forecasts... as more nearly balanced than earlier in the year"
- "the prospect that the Congress would shortly reach an accord on the budget seemed to be reducing uncertainty"
- "the projected pickup in economic growth was not assured"; concerns about persistence of low inflation

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: Declining uncertainty with more balanced risks, though residual concerns about inflation persistence and recovery durability. Standard-to-slightly-elevated uncertainty.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence] | Minutes: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

#### Transcript Evidence
- Core PCE "running at 1.3 percent pace" — "low end of range." "All measures have tended to trend lower since early last year." "Core import prices declining." "Expected to edge up to pace of 1¾ percent in 2016." Below target, persistent undershoot. (Reeve)

**Transcript Center-of-gravity**: Below neutral — significant, persistent undershoot.

#### Minutes Evidence

**Minutes Center-of-gravity**: With headline PCE below 1% and core barely above 1%, staff clearly sees inflation running well below the 2% target with no near-term normalization expected.
