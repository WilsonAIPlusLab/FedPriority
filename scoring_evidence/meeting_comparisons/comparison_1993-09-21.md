# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison — 1993-09-21

**Meeting context**: September 1993. Federal funds rate at 3%. Unemployment ~6.7%. Economy in moderate post-recession recovery ("jobless recovery"). Long-term rates declining. Deficit-reduction legislation recently passed.

## CROSS-TRACK NOTES (Factual Observations Only)

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Discussion track (both sources) scores 35; Staff transcript scores 40; Staff minutes scores 35. The staff Q&A captures Prell's specific data points (3¼% core CPI, sticky expectations) yielding a slightly higher score than the discussion-level assessment.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff minutes (38) sits 5 points below staff transcript (43) and 4-6 points below discussion sources (42-44). The minutes' "limited reductions in slack" framing produces a more subdued gap assessment than the transcript's "very close to potential" language from Prell.

3. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The largest gap in the discussion track (15 vs. 25). The transcript scorer emphasizes the unanimous vote and near-convergence of views; the minutes scorer weights the explicit hawk-dove documentation more heavily.

4. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Discussion transcript (28) vs. discussion minutes (15) — a 13-point gap. The full transcript captures the length and substantive detail of McDonough's equity market concerns, which the minutes compresses to one brief paragraph with "a few members."

5. **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff transcript (55) vs. staff minutes (42) — a 13-point gap. Prell's Q&A language explicitly states expectations "still have not adjusted downward" and remain "in the 3 percent area," while the minutes characterize the staff projection as expecting "some tempering." The transcript score reflects where expectations currently stand; the minutes score reflects the projected direction.

6. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Scored only in staff minutes (28, "depreciated substantially"). Not assessable from staff Q&A transcript due to absence of direct Truman commentary on the overall dollar level.

7. **All sources agree** on: financial conditions being clearly accommodative (scores 22-30 range), labor market having meaningful slack (35-38), inflation being below any crisis threshold (35-40), and forward guidance being neutral (50 in both discussion sources).
