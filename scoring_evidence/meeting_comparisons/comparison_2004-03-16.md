## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 38**

- Parry: "The most recent data on core inflation continue to indicate that we are close to price stability. Our expectation for core PCE price inflation in 2004 remains at 1 percent."
- Kohn: "After declining for three years, underlying inflation may be flattening out, as the Greenbook states, but the evidence seems inconclusive to me. The three-month rate of core PCE has come up, but from an extraordinarily low level, and core CPI continues to decelerate."
- Pianalto: "That suggests to me that this long period of disinflation we've been living through is bottoming out."
- Guynn: "The list of goods and services with outsized price increases is beginning to grow... it has been quite a long time since I've heard so much chatter about price increases."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 35**

- "members generally anticipated that core inflation would remain near current low levels, with output growth only gradually eliminating slack in labor and goods markets and strong growth in productivity limiting increases in unit labor costs"
- "many Committee members commented that persisting slack in labor and output markets would keep inflation low"
- "Some members thought that core inflation had stabilized and was unlikely to move lower. Increases in the prices of energy, other commodities, and non-oil imports... suggested that the downtrend in inflation had ended"
- Statement retained "a slight tilt toward the possibility of some further disinflation"

**Staff Transcript — Score: 27**

- Stockton: baseline "incorporates lower inflation expectations." Core PCE ~1%.
- "straws in the wind" — dollar decline, commodity acceleration, suggest upside risk.
- Higher NAIRU alternative simulation shows core PCE rising to 2% by 2005.
- Staff notes "risk balance" could shift on inflation.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 25**

- "Core consumer price inflation stayed very low in early 2004. Over the twelve months ending in January, the increase in core consumer prices was around 1 percent--about 3/4 percentage point below the increase over the preceding twelve months."
- "Core inflation was projected to remain low over the forecast period."
- Staff also notes energy price surges and mixed labor cost signals (hourly earnings decelerating, ECI accelerating slightly).

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 38**

- Kohn: "One effect of this combination is to leave the economy with a somewhat larger estimated output gap last quarter and this quarter and one that is closing more slowly than we'd like."
- Ferguson: "The staff has taken this on board by marking down their forecast. The output gap is probably up, and the employment gap is up."
- Kohn: "The low level of inflation and the high level of the output gap mean that the welfare costs of economic growth running a little stronger than expected are considerably lower than the welfare costs of it running a little weaker."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 42**

- "overall economic activity still seemed to be increasing at a solid pace"
- "Committee members generally expected that... monetary policy... would continue to foster a pace of output growth that exceeded that of its potential"
- "Although economic slack likely would be declining, it was expected to be a little higher than previously had been anticipated"
- "persisting slack in labor and output markets"

**Staff Transcript — Score: 40**

- Stockton: GDP growth revised down for H1 but still above trend.
- "Projected output gap has increased appreciably."
- "Wider gap presumably would put downward pressure on core inflation."
- Economy still below potential but closing.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 40**

- "Capacity utilization continued to move higher in January and February, although it remained below its longer-run average."
- "The pace of economic expansion was forecasted to be sufficient to reduce resource slack over this year and next, although the employment data... indicated that this process would begin from a higher rate of unused resources than had been previously expected."
- FOMC statement referenced "resource use slack."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 36**

- Santomero: "The labor market remains the weak spot not only in manufacturing but for the District economy as a whole."
- Pianalto: "I'm still hearing a great deal of reluctance to add people to the payrolls. Business managers have become very adept at expanding output without adding to the workforce."
- Ferguson: "years after the formal end of the recession, we still seem to be dealing with what appears to be yet another jobless recovery."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 31**

- "employment growth had been disappointing. While job losses had moderated, hiring had yet to strengthen, holding down net increases in employment"
- "soft labor markets underscored the desirability of a monetary policy strategy characterized by continued patience"
- "persistent weakness in employment"
- "Lingering business caution likely accounted for a good deal of the lag in job creation"

**Staff Transcript — Score: 30**

- "Soft February jobs report."
- "Employment gap has been widening, given that job growth... has fallen well short of most estimates of the growth of the working-age population."
- Employment situation "stabilizing at a very low level" with little evidence of "really moving up."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 32**

- "The January and February employment reports depicted a labor market that was slow to pick up momentum. The modest gains posted in private nonfarm payrolls over the period were smaller than had been expected by most forecasters."
- Staff forecast references "a higher rate of unused resources than had been previously expected."
- "Despite the weakness in employment, the unemployment rate in January and February came in below its fourth-quarter level."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 52**

- Kohn: "Most notably, business spending on capital equipment remains quite strong, with no signs of tapering off."
- Geithner: "We expect investment to continue to strengthen at a quite rapid rate, supported by confidence in demand growth, competitive pressures to upgrade, ample profit margins, and very favorable financial conditions."
- Poole (FedEx contact): "They see a very clear, progressive strengthening of demand... for the first time in this recovery period the year-over-year change in U.S. outbound traffic is now positive."
- Ferguson: notes "all of the factors that have been supporting it are going to be weaker and not stronger going forward" — downside risk to consumer spending.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 57**

- "investment spending had continued to expand at a robust pace. Members anticipated vigorous growth in investment outlays going forward"
- "Consumer spending outside the auto sector had remained strong"
- "firms were investing primarily to replace old equipment or to reduce costs, but remained hesitant to expand capacity"
- "sales of motor vehicles had fallen back noticeably"

**Staff Transcript — Score: 55**

- E&S spending — "orders and shipments were very robust."
- Housing strong. Consumer spending supported by income growth.
- GDP growth revised down for H1; manufacturing IP softer.
- Mixed signals: strong business spending offset by disappointing employment and softer manufacturing.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 60**

- "spending for equipment and software was apparently advancing vigorously."
- "Retail sales rose briskly, on average, in January and February."
- "Strong advances in real disposable income were expected to keep consumer spending on a solid upward trajectory. Business spending on equipment and software was seen as increasing briskly."
- Nonresidential structures described as having "languished."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 37**

- Greenspan's recommendation: "leaving the inflation risks sentence unchanged" — retain slight downside bias, no rate change.
- Kohn: "I'd be quite hesitant about allowing such concerns to have an effect on policy" — strong patience advocate.
- Poole: "I would, however, move to balance on the inflation statement" — representative of the hawkish-leaning minority.
- Greenspan: "We are in a position where we can sit with patience for a considerable period."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 43**

- "all the members favored the retention of the current target rate of 1 percent"
- "the current accommodative stance of monetary policy remained appropriate"
- "continued patience" characterizes the strategy
- "some members, while supporting an unchanged policy at this meeting, nonetheless emphasized that the maintenance of a very accommodative monetary policy over an extended period... called for careful attention to the possible emergence of inflationary pressures"

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 42**

- Greenspan: "This is a very close decision actually — not so much in the numbers but in the fact that all of us, as best I can judge, could go either way if we had to."
- Kohn jokes about "lining up against my colleagues to my right" — explicit acknowledgment of distinct camps.
- Bernanke: "I will align myself with the Kohn axis."
- No formal dissents in the vote despite the ~50-50 internal split on inflation risk language.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 32**

- "all the members favored the retention of the current target rate of 1 percent"
- "Many members held that a case could be made for moving to a balanced risk assessment with regard to the outlook for inflation, with a number of them expressing a marginal preference for such a change"
- "While many viewed it as a close call, all members indicated that they could support a proposal to retain the existing wording"
- Divergent views on inflation outlook: stabilized vs. could go lower.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 45**

- Bernanke's entire go-around devoted to proposing a Financial Stability Report, discussing housing bubbles, risk spread compression, GSE risks, Basel II.
- Kohn: lengthy discussion of "unusual distortions in asset prices… house prices fall into this category, as do risk spreads… and perhaps even the level of long-term rates themselves."
- Greenspan: "the persistence of low market interest rates is fostering increased efforts to reach for higher yields… probably one of the most important issues confronting us."
- Guynn: "growing concern about potential overbuilding and worrisome speculation in the real estate markets, especially in Florida."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 32**

- "some members were concerned that keeping monetary policy stimulative for so long might be encouraging increased leverage and excessive risk-taking. Such developments could heighten the potential for the emergence of financial and economic instability when policy tightening proved necessary in the future"
- "speculative forces might be boosting housing demand in some parts of the country... the possibility that house prices might be moving into the high end of the range that could be consistent with fundamentals"
- Raised only by "some members," not a dominant topic.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 38**

- Guynn: "the error bands around expected employment gains over coming quarters are quite wide."
- Kohn: "Uncertainty in the current situation seems greatest about the rate of growth of potential GDP… as well as the level of the output gap."
- Greenspan: "the interesting question relates to employment and whether its recent weakness reflects statistical noise or whether there's something going on that we did not anticipate."
- Gramlich: notes first Greenbook downgrade after upgrades, calls it "way too early to tell whether this recent downgrade is a mere blip or the start of something more meaningful."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 40**

- "The reasons for the reduced optimism were not entirely clear"
- "The extent and duration of the resulting restraint on hiring were difficult to assess"
- Debate on inflation direction: some think stabilized, others think could go lower.
- Debate on productivity: could slow (raising costs) or remain rapid (putting downward pressure on prices).

**Staff Minutes — Score: 62**

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 40**

**Staff Transcript — Score: 47**

**Staff Minutes — Score: 40**

**Staff Minutes — Score: 52 (DOLLAR_STRENGTH — higher = stronger)**
