### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 42 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- Powell: "Despite lots of reports of bottlenecks and supply constraints, inflation has remained near our 2 percent objective. In fact, it has lately come in just a bit more muted than expected."
- Clarida: "over the past six months, through October, core PCE inflation has been running at an annual rate of 1.5 percent."
- Bullard: "inflation by our preferred core PCE measure is 1.8 percent year-over-year—still below our target and not meaningfully different than it was in late 2016."
- Kashkari: "12-month core inflation is now at 1.8 percent. Since June, all of the monthly numbers have come in below 2 percent."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Participants overwhelmingly describe inflation as slightly below the 2% target with recent softening, and nearly all note downside risks from declining TIPS breakevens and oil prices; the handful of upside signals (wage pass-through, materials costs) are treated as offsets rather than dominant forces.

---

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- "Both overall and core PCE price inflation remained near 2 percent on a 12-month basis, but that core inflation had edged lower in recent months."
- "Participants generally continued to view recent price developments as consistent with their expectation that inflation would remain near the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective on a sustained basis."
- "A few participants pointed to anecdotal and survey evidence indicating rising input costs... reports from business contacts and surveys in some other Districts suggested some moderation in inflationary pressure."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Inflation essentially at target with a slight downward drift in core measures; participants viewed this as consistent with 2% on a sustained basis — neither alarming nor particularly subdued.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

- Rosengren: "unemployment is already at a level well below the range of most forecasters' estimates of what is sustainable in the long run."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Unemployment at 3.7% (near 50-year low), near-universal reports of hiring difficulties, and wage growth clearly trending upward place the labor market firmly in the "very tight" zone; the score of 68 reflects strong tightness without reaching the historical extremes of 2022–23.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 58 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- Brainard: "financial conditions have tightened considerably in recent months. Various indexes suggest that financial conditions are now a neutral factor for real GDP growth in coming periods, compared with a strong tailwind midyear. Corporate bond spreads... now close to the middle of the distribution."
- Kaplan: "credit spreads on corporate debt are widening... we haven't seen any high-yield debt issuance... we may be at the beginning of a credit crunch."
- Clarida: "we've had a big shift in financial conditions indexes... borrowing costs are up, with a rise in credit spreads."
- Williams: "sharp tightening in financial conditions—in terms of asset prices, widening risk spreads, and the general shift towards a more risk-off sentiment."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Participants broadly agree financial conditions shifted from loose/accommodative (early 2018) to roughly neutral-to-somewhat-tight by year-end, with equity declines, spread widening, and a shutting of the high-yield issuance window; this is clearly tighter than midyear but not yet crisis-level.

---

**Minutes — Score: 60 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- "A couple of participants noted that the tightening in financial conditions so far did not appear to be restraining real activity, although a more persistent tightening would undoubtedly weigh on business and household spending."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Conditions clearly tightened from the previous accommodative/neutral baseline — equity declines, wider spreads, flatter curve. Somewhat tight but not at crisis-level stress.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 63 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- Majority (11+): support 25bp hike at this meeting (Clarida, Rosengren, Mester, Evans, Barkin, Brainard, Daly, Bowman, George, Quarles, Williams support alt-B).
- Explicit hold preferences (3): Bullard: "first-best for today, I think, is alternative A"; Harker: "I support alternative A as written"; Kashkari: "I favor alternative A."
- Mester (for hike): "there's also a case for moving the funds rate up today... I support increasing the target range of the federal funds rate 25 basis points."
- Powell: "With growth expected to decline to closer to trend next year, we can afford to be patient about further policy firming, especially in an environment in which inflation is still muted."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): The meeting delivers a 25bp hike (hawkish action) but the forward lean is sharply moderated — the median SEP drops to 2 hikes for 2019, virtually all participants express patience and data-dependence going forward, and 3 explicitly preferred no hike; this places the overall lean in "moderate hawkish" territory rather than "strong hawkish."

---

**Minutes — Score: 60 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- "Most participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting."
- "A few participants, however, favored no change in the target range at this meeting."
- "Many participants expressed the view that, especially in an environment of muted inflation pressures, the Committee could afford to be patient about further policy firming."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Majority supported 25bp hike (hawkish current action), but the forward lean shifted notably dovish with "patience" language and downgraded rate path projections. Net lean: moderately hawkish for the current meeting, but meaningfully less hawkish than previous meetings in the cycle.

Center-of-gravity (minutes): The forward signal is mildly hawkish in baseline (further gradual increases expected) but significantly tempered by patience language and uncertainty about timing/extent.
