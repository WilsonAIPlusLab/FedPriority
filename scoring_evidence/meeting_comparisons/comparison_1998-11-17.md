# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1998-11-17

**Context**: November 1998. Post-LTCM/Russia default era. Third consecutive rate cut (25bp to 4.75%) with move to symmetric directive. Financial markets recovering from September–October crisis but not fully normalized. Domestic economy resilient but decelerating.

---

## Part A: Discussion (Participant Views)

### Sources
- **Transcript**: Individually identified speakers (~18 participants). Scored from verbatim go-around and policy discussion.
- **Minutes**: Synthesized summary of participant views. Scored from sections with participant language cues ("members observed," "members noted," etc.).

---

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **35 ±7 [High confidence]** | **35 ±7 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the inflation rate is still falling... the real funds rate is rising" and "unit labor costs have been rising at a rate of less than 1-1/2 percent... total unit costs... increasing at a shade above 1 percent"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "We have had steady unemployment at below 5 percent for seven quarters now, and the signs of acceleration of inflation still are not much more visible now than they were then"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Price pressures remain benign despite tight labor markets"
- MR. PARRY: "inflation in the core CPI to average 2-1/4 percent both this year and next"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "price inflation remained subdued"
- "several broad measures of prices suggested that inflation might be on a declining trend"
- "A number of members expected that, on balance, inflation might be less favorable next year, though any deterioration in underlying trends should be relatively limited; others anticipated little change in and possibly some further ebbing of price inflation"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly view inflation as well contained and possibly still declining despite very tight labor markets. Productivity gains and international disinflationary forces keeping prices in check.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Inflation subdued and possibly declining, well below concerning levels, though tight labor markets create some latent risk.

| | Transcript | Minutes |

**Transcript evidence**:

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **67 ±5 [High confidence]** | **67 ±5 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "We are suffering from a severe shortage of people... there just are not enough people" and construction workers "complaining that they're not getting their usual time off for deer hunting"
- MR. HOENIG: "District labor markets are still very tight. More than half of our Beigebook and business contacts are talking about raising wages more than what they consider normal"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the number who still emphasize the tightness of labor markets and the difficulty they are having finding qualified people at all levels"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "very little evidence that those markets are tightening further at this time, but I also would not want to argue that we are seeing any significant labor market easing"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "labor markets remained exceptionally tight, though there was little evidence that they had tightened further in recent weeks"
- "persistently tight labor markets in much of the nation"
- unemployment "near 4-1/2 percent"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Nearly every participant reports very tight labor markets with difficulty finding workers, unfilled positions, and wage pressures. Unemployment below 5% for seven quarters.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Labor markets very tight by historical standards. No easing of tightness yet despite economic concerns.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **57 ±8 [High confidence]** | **54 ±8 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "Consumer spending in October in particular was considerably stronger than anticipated. Automobile sales and outlays for other durable goods were especially robust"
- MS. RIVLIN: "Consumers are spending cheerfully, buying houses and cars and maintaining a fairly robust outlook"
- MR. FERGUSON: "Business fixed investment fell in the last quarter at about a 1 percent annual rate... even investment in producers' durable equipment barely budged"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending notably above trend and resilient; business fixed investment has turned negative. Net position modestly above trend.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending remains strong but business investment decelerating notably. Net position modestly above trend but weakening.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **60 ±8 [High confidence]** | **62 ±8 [Moderate confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "yield spreads and risk aversion, which had been quite substantial through October and have eased quite measurably in the last several weeks, have stopped easing. In fact, there actually has been some minor upturn"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "risk spreads are still high, and the real federal funds rate is still high also. I think the period of the credit crunch is not entirely behind us"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "weakness in fixed income markets, although somewhat reduced, is still a potentially serious risk to the real economy"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Financial markets have improved from their earlier unstable condition, but they are not yet back to normal"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "many risk spreads were still at unusually high levels"
- "financing generally had become less available and more expensive for higher-risk business borrowers"
- banks "tightened their credit terms and standards for most new loans and lines of credit"
- "some members" noted "rapid growth of liquidity and bank credit suggested that financial conditions were not excessively tight"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions somewhat tight — improved substantially from September/October crisis peak but still notably tighter than pre-August 1998 norms.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Conditions meaningfully tighter than normal — spreads elevated, credit harder to obtain — but improving from the acute stress.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **37 ±8 [High confidence]** | **33 ±8 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I see very little risk in moving down another 25 basis points today, provided we indicate that we are on hold... move down and go symmetric"
- MR. MEYER: "another quarter point move would complete what I think would be an appropriate adjustment to the discrete change in the outlook"
- MR. BROADDUS (opposing): "I just respectfully do not believe that either of those two arguments is very compelling today... I would have a strong preference for alternative B"
- MS. MINEHAN (reluctant): "I am not going to dissent because I think it's a close call... I will support your proposal to ease, but I am nervous about it"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "nearly all the members indicated that they could accept a proposal to reduce the federal funds rate by a further 25 basis points"
- "Most members saw little risk that a modest easing would ignite inflationary pressures"
- "the risks of inaction were greater in terms of the potential financial consequences"
- "Some members" preferred to await further developments; Mr. Jordan dissented

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Majority supports 25bp cut as insurance against downside risks, framed as completing the easing cycle. About 6 participants prefer holding. Lean is moderately dovish with neutral forward posture.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Moderate dovish — majority favors easing as insurance against financial and economic downside risks, completing the policy adjustment.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **44 ±7 [High confidence]** | **35 ±7 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would have a strong preference for alternative B" (hold)
- MR. HOENIG: "my preference would be to wait... However, like others, I am not going to dissent"
- MR. POOLE: "I will support your recommendation, but with considerable reluctance. I am concerned that we are pouring gasoline rather than water onto this economy"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting clearly in favor of waiting... I will support your proposal to ease, but I am nervous about it"

**Minutes evidence**:
- One formal dissent (Mr. Jordan)
- "This policy decision was viewed as a close call by several members"
- "Some members indicated that...they had a preference for awaiting further developments"
- "All but one of these members could endorse the decision to ease"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: No formal dissents recorded in the transcript, but approximately 6 participants preferred holding and at least 3 more were reluctant supporters. Committee closely divided.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with notable reservations from several members, but ultimate near-consensus on the action.

*Note: Transcript score (44) is higher than minutes score (35) because the transcript captures the full extent of internal reluctance among non-dissenters, while the minutes records only one formal dissent.*

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **65 ±7 [High confidence]** | **62 ±7 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. HOENIG: "I have concerns that a bubble economy syndrome may be building"
- MR. KELLEY: "I worry more today about the potential for very serious mischief that could arise in the stock market if it should boom to new highs. That could put us in severe jeopardy of a crash"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "the members gave considerable attention to recent financial developments and their implications for the economic outlook"
- "continuing fragility of financial markets...posed a considerable downside risk to the expansion"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions a prominent topic — discussed at length by most participants. Topics include credit market dysfunction, risk spreads, stock market valuation/bubble concerns, Brazil contagion risk, year-end funding pressures.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions prominent topic, rivaling the economic assessment in emphasis. Multiple paragraphs devoted to financial market developments.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **50 ±7 [High confidence]** | **48 ±7 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "move down and go symmetric... putting policy on indefinite hold... stay on hold, and watch events as they materialize over the weeks and possibly even months ahead"
- MR. MEYER: "The move to a symmetric directive signals the pause that I believe is in order after the move"
- MR. POOLE: "I would hope that... you could indicate to the market that it looks as if our easing is finished for the time being"
- MR. FERGUSON: "symmetry is an important internal signal that we are prepared to go either way if the facts so indicate"

**Minutes evidence**:
- "the Committee favored a change to symmetry from the asymmetry toward ease in its recent directives"
- "further easing was not likely to be needed over the months ahead unless ongoing developments pointed to a more substantial decline in the growth of economic activity"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Forward signal explicitly neutral. Symmetric directive signals easing cycle is complete. Multiple participants frame this as the final cut. Committee data-dependent going forward, prepared to move in either direction.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Neutral forward posture — easing cycle viewed as complete, with data dependence going forward. Slight residual easing bias from the articulated conditions for further cuts.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by international developments, financial market fragility, and difficulty forecasting the magnitude of the expected slowdown.

## Part B: Staff Assessments

### Sources
- **Staff Transcript**: Scored from verbatim staff presentations (Prell, Fisher, Johnson).

### S1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Staff Transcript | Staff Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **40 ±6 [Moderate confidence]** | **40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]** |

**Staff Transcript evidence**:
- Prell: favorable news on wages and prices; inflation expectations easing; oil prices low
- Center-of-gravity: Very low inflation, below neutral

**Staff Minutes evidence**:

- Center-of-gravity: Current inflation subdued with energy prices dragging headline down and core modestly firming; overall below neutral
