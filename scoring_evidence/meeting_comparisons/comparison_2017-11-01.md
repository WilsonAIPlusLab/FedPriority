### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: All participants acknowledge inflation is well below the 2% target — core PCE at 1.3% YoY — with most describing it as "persistent," "puzzling," and showing no clear trajectory back to target in the near term.

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "core PCE prices were up only 1.3 percent over the past 12 months, down ½ percentage point from the previous year. Even controlling for idiosyncratic factors in a variety of ways, the same puzzling decline is evident."
- Kashkari: "Headline PCE inflation has averaged 1.3 percent over this period [since 2012]. I'm trying to imagine how the Committee would react if we had 2.7 percent inflation, on average, for six years."
- Evans: "With each new low reading, it gets harder and harder for me to feel comfortable with the idea that the step-down in inflation earlier in the year was simply transitory."
- Bullard: "I consider the U.S. economy overall to still be in the low-inflation, low-growth regime."

**Minutes score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see inflation as clearly below the 2% target, with debate about whether the weakness is transitory or persistent, and notable concern about possible downward drift in expectations.

Representative quotes:
- "On a 12-month basis, PCE price inflation in September, at 1.6 percent, remained below the Committee's longer-run objective; core PCE price inflation...was only 1.3 percent."
- "Many participants judged that much of the recent softness in core inflation reflected temporary or idiosyncratic factors."
- "Many participants observed that there was some likelihood that inflation might remain below 2 percent for longer than they currently expected."
- "Several participants expressed concern that the persistently weak inflation data could lead to a decline in longer-term inflation expectations."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous agreement that GDP growth is running above potential/trend (~3% in Q2 and Q3). Most participants project above-trend growth to continue. However, the economy is not described as overheating dramatically — "moderately above" or "somewhat above" trend is the dominant characterization.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "This pace of growth is substantially above the economy's potential."
- Williams (policy round): "The economy continues to grow above potential. We're in the midst of a substantial overshooting of our full employment goal."
- Powell: "As we look through the effects of the recent hurricanes, the incoming data show an economy that is expanding at a moderate but above-trend pace."
- Mester: "I expect above-trend growth over the remainder of this year and over the next couple of years."

**Minutes score: 54 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy modestly above potential — "at or above full employment" with above-trend growth, but not running hot. The "at or above" qualifier suggests the center sees it as right around or slightly above.

Representative quotes:
- "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment."
- "Participants saw the incoming information on spending and the labor market as consistent with continued above-trend economic growth."
- "GDP was expected to grow at a pace exceeding that of potential output."
- Unemployment at 4.2%, "falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 63 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The large majority of participants view the labor market as clearly tight — unemployment well below most natural rate estimates, initial claims at multi-decade lows, widespread reports of worker shortages and wage pressures in specific sectors. However, Kashkari argues persistent slack remains, and Bostic's contacts provide nuance.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "unemployment rate...declined to 4.2 percent and is now well below the SEP's 4.6 percent median estimate of the natural rate. With real GDP expected to continue growing faster than potential GDP, the unemployment rate is likely to fall further below my current estimate of full employment."
- George: "With the unemployment rate at 4.2 percent, its lowest rate since February 2001, I see little evidence of slack remaining in the labor market."
- Mester: "the unemployment rate, at 4.2 percent, is at its lowest level since 2001 and well below estimates of its longer-run level."
- Rosengren: "Initial claims for unemployment insurance are now at lows last seen in the 1970s."

**Minutes score: 56 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is tight — at or slightly beyond full employment — but the tightness is moderate, as evidenced by only modest wage gains and mixed reports of labor shortages.

Representative quotes:
- "The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2 percent in September, falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment and anticipated that the labor market would tighten somewhat further."
- "Reports from some Districts pointed to difficulty attracting and retaining labor, but anecdotal information from other Districts suggested that workers with the requisite skills remained reasonably available."
- "Overall, wage increases were generally seen as modest."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 68 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Approximately 10 of 14 speaking participants lean toward continued gradual tightening and support a December hike (conditioned on data). Roughly 4 participants (Evans, Kashkari, Bullard, Brainard) advocate for greater caution or delay. Maps to moderate hawkish — majority favors continued tightening, though not "aggressive" or "strong hawkish."

Representative quotes:
- Mester: "I anticipate that, so long as conditions remain largely as they are today...I'll support an increase in the funds rate in December."
- Williams: "I have a high bar for surprises that could change my mind about the appropriateness of a December rate increase."
- Dudley: "tightening in monetary policy in December is already broadly expected...Shifting the stance of monetary policy slightly in the direction of less accommodation seems appropriate."
- Evans (dovish dissent): "I continue to believe that achieving our symmetric 2 percent objective...would be strongly aided by delaying further rate increases until we have more evidence that inflation is indeed moving back toward target."
- Kashkari: "I don't think we should raise the federal funds rate until core PCE inflation actually hits 2 percent on a 12-month basis."

**Minutes score: 64 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate hawkish lean — majority ready for another hike soon (December) but with notable caution from a meaningful minority focused on inflation shortfall.

Representative quotes:
- "All participants thought that it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term if incoming information left the medium-term outlook broadly unchanged."
- "Several participants indicated that their decision...would depend importantly on whether the upcoming economic data boosted their confidence that inflation was headed toward the Committee's objective."
- "A few other participants thought that additional policy firming should be deferred until incoming information confirmed that inflation was clearly on a path toward the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective."

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: All participants supported Alt-B (no change today), creating zero formal dissent on the vote. However, 4 participants have distinctly different views on the forward path — ranging from "no December hike" (Bullard, Kashkari) to "wait and see" (Evans, Brainard). The disagreement extends to the analytical framework (Phillips curve relevance, role of inflation expectations, regime analysis).

Representative quotes:

- Bullard: "As I sit here today, my preference is for no December rate move."

**Minutes score: 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate differences in views, especially about inflation dynamics and the appropriate pace of further tightening. No formal dissent, but meaningful disagreement about the forward path and the interpretation of soft inflation data.

Representative quotes:

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript score: 72 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The dominant forward signal is: (1) no change today, (2) likely December hike, (3) continued gradual rate increases through 2018-2019 (approximately 6 more hikes in the SEP through end-2019), (4) balance sheet normalization ongoing. This is a clear tightening signal with multiple further steps anticipated.

Representative quotes:

- Powell: "I continue to see a gradual removal of accommodation through rate increases and a shrinking balance sheet as the appropriate path...an increase in December is likely to be appropriate."

**Minutes score: 69 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Clear forward signal toward further gradual tightening, with a near-term hike strongly expected. The "gradual increases" language and near-term hike signal place this solidly in the mild-to-moderate tightening bias range.

Representative quotes:
- "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term."

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript score: 48 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is elevated above normal but not at crisis/extreme levels. Four distinct sources of uncertainty are active: (1) hurricane data distortions, (2) the inflation puzzle, (3) natural rate of unemployment estimates, (4) fiscal policy composition and timing. Multiple participants express "some" to "considerable" uncertainty, but none describe the situation as "uncharted territory."

Representative quotes:
- Powell: "With wide confidence intervals surrounding estimates of the natural rate, we should remain open to the possibility that it is lower than we may think."
- Mester: "I acknowledge and find it dissatisfying that we can't forecast inflation with much precision."
- Bostic: "It's hard to know the degree to which this collection of forces can contain wage pressures and for how long."
- Yellen: "I must confess that as each month goes by without a pickup in the monthly data, my confidence in this story slips a bit further."
- Brainard: "the scope, scale, and composition of any such legislation remain uncertain."

**Minutes score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, concentrated around inflation dynamics (why inflation is soft despite tight labor markets). Growth and employment outlook are relatively clear, but inflation puzzlement adds uncertainty. Risks characterized as "roughly balanced" keeps this from scoring higher.

Representative quotes:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared to be roughly balanced."
- Hurricane disruptions "made it more difficult than usual to interpret some of this information."
- "Several participants pointed to a diminished responsiveness of inflation to resource utilization" — suggesting standard models may not apply.
- "A couple of participants discussed the possibility that potential alternative frameworks for the conduct of monetary policy could be helpful" — questioning the framework itself.
