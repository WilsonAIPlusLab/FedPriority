## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion Transcript (60 ±7)**

- GREENSPAN: "That the core rate of inflation is rising is in my view unambiguous, no matter what price series we deal with."
- BROADDUS: "core measures of inflation have risen 1/2 percentage point... the ECI was up 4.6 percent over the last four quarters. I believe that's the biggest four-quarter increase in ten years."
- MOSKOW: "higher inflation is already more than a risk; as the Greenbook notes, both core CPI and core PCE inflation are running 1/2 point above their lows for the cycle."
- HOENIG (counterpoint): "the underlying inflation rate remains relatively modest in my opinion."

**Discussion Minutes (62 ±7)**

- "members continued to be concerned about the outlook for inflation"
- "core consumer price measures also displayed a gradual uptrend"
- "the members generally agreed that the risks were in the direction of a heightening in inflation pressures"
- "a few members commented that measures of core inflation already were near or slightly above levels that they viewed as acceptable for the longer run"

**Staff Transcript (56 ±7)**

- MR. STOCKTON: "My judgment would be that there has been a pickup in underlying core inflation. But the core PCE has probably understated that pickup... I'd say that a rate somewhere in between those two measurements probably captures the right sense of what has been going on." [Core CPI ~2.5%, core PCE ~1.7%; true rate ~2–2.25%]
- MR. STOCKTON: "By our assessment, we need to hold the real federal funds rate at about its current level in order to cap inflation at roughly 2-1/4 percent for core PCE."

**Staff Minutes (57 ±5)**

- "Core inflation appeared to be increasing, but very gradually and in part reflecting the indirect effects of higher energy costs."
- "Core price inflation was projected to rise a little over the forecast horizon, in part as a result of higher import prices but largely as a consequence of further increases in nominal labor compensation gains that would not be fully offset by growth in productivity."

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript (54 ±6)**

- MEYER: "below-trend growth when output is above potential, as I believe is the case, is desirable. That's exactly what we need to mitigate the risks of rising core inflation."
- GREENSPAN's statement: "the economy could expand for a time at a pace below the productivity-enhanced rate of growth of its potential to produce."
- SANTOMERO: "Growth definitely has slowed in the third quarter--to a reported 2.7 percent pace, which is below estimates of potential... this slowdown in economic growth is from a high level."

**Discussion Minutes (52 ±6)**

- "Aggregate demand currently appeared to be growing at a pace a little below the rate of increase in the economy's output potential."
- "growth in overall demand now seemed to have declined to a more sustainable pace and probably would continue to expand for a time at a rate below that of the economy's output potential."
- Context of tight labor markets and resource pressures suggests economy still at or slightly above potential even as growth decelerates.

**Staff Transcript (57 ±6)**

- MR. REINHART (partial text): "the economy is operating beyond its [potential]" — explicit above-potential signal.
- MR. STOCKTON: "We still estimate that real GDP rose about 2-1/2 percent at an annual rate in the third quarter and will rise about 3-1/2 percent in the current quarter."

**Staff Minutes (53 ±5)**

- "economic growth had slowed appreciably from the rapid pace in the first half of the year."
- Staff forecast: "the economic expansion...would be sustained over the forecast horizon at a rate a little below the staff's current estimate of the economy's potential output."
- Unemployment at 3.9% ("current cyclical low") indicates economy still operating above potential in levels.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion Transcript (70 ±5)**

- MOSKOW: "Circuit City... announced the layoff of 260 workers. By the end of the day... their human resources people had found new jobs at other nearby firms for all 260 workers." Unemployment in Iowa at 2.3%.
- MINEHAN: "Ski areas in Vermont are importing seasonal workers from unlikely places like Jamaica, Australia, and New Zealand."
- BROADDUS: "Some retailers have actually had to cut back on the hours they are open because they can't find people to staff their stores."
- MEYER: "unemployment rate today is at its cyclical low and significantly below the staff's estimate of the short-run NAIRU."

**Discussion Minutes (70 ±5)**

- "labor markets were likely to remain unusually tight for some time"
- "the anticipated persistence of very tight conditions in labor markets that would enable wages to catch up to earlier gains in labor productivity"
- "measures of labor compensation appeared to be accelerating"

**Staff Transcript (68 ±6)**

- MR. MOSKOW: "labor markets are very tight now by historical standards and productivity is increasing at very high rates by historical standards."
- MR. MOSKOW: "their forecast for job openings, which I'll mention later, has set another record. There are a lot of openings that haven't been filled."
- MR. STOCKTON: Forecasting "private payroll employment gains of about 130,000" for November; unemployment rate ~3.9–4.0%.

**Staff Minutes (68 ±4)**

- "The civilian unemployment rate held at its current cyclical low of 3.9 percent in October."
- "unemployment remained very low."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion Transcript (48 ±7)**

- GREENSPAN: "high-tech area... growth is decelerating from 50 percent to 20 percent... we are indeed seeing some softening in consumer expenditures."
- KELLEY: "consumer spending in the weaker third quarter was considerably stronger than in the more robust second quarter... Consumer confidence remains very high."
- FERGUSON: "investment spending is on a bit more of a knife-edge now than it has been for some time... consumption is also on a trajectory for slower growth."
- SANTOMERO: "Business fixed investment... grew at half the second-quarter pace."

**Discussion Minutes (46 ±7)**

- "substantial moderation in the growth of business fixed investment in recent months"
- "recent deceleration was especially pronounced in expenditures for high-tech equipment and software, though such spending was still growing at a robust pace"
- Consumer spending: "limited information available on more recent developments suggested some softening."
- "appreciable further growth in investment spending seemed to be in prospect for coming quarters, though undoubtedly at a slower pace"

**Staff Transcript (46 ±8)**

- MR. STOCKTON: "retail sales rose a relatively modest 0.3 percent last month after jumping 0.9 percent in September... auto sales fell about a million units at an annual rate in October, to a 16.7 million unit pace." PCE forecast: "projected increase in real PCE of 3-1/2 percent in Q4."
- MR. STOCKTON: "growth of real GDP appears to have slowed considerably in the second half of the year." "Investment spending for high-tech equipment, on net, has also slowed some of late... there has been a drop-off in spending for communications equipment."

**Staff Minutes (45 ±5)**

- "The slowdown was most apparent in housing construction and business investment in equipment and software, while consumer spending remained on a relatively solid upward trend."
- "Business investment in durable equipment and software decelerated sharply in the third quarter."

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Discussion Transcript (63 ±5)**

- GREENSPAN: "quite remarkable tightening not only of the junk bond markets but of all the investment-grade markets, and very particularly the banking markets. We are getting some serious restraint."
- GUYNN: "bank examiners... see a measurable slowdown in loan demand across most types of business loans and a further tightening in credit standards... the loan pipeline is almost dry beyond 60 days."
- HOENIG: "financial conditions have tightened... higher interest rate spreads... tighter lending terms and standards, lower equity prices."
- GREENSPAN (counterpoint): "this is not 1998, when for a few days it was impossible to get a bid on an investment-grade bond."

**Discussion Minutes (64 ±6)**

- "emergence in recent months of less accommodative financial conditions for many businesses, including some further tightening since the meeting in early October"
- "spreads in securities markets had widened, most sharply on obligations of borrowers rated below investment grade"
- "tightening terms and standards at banks for business borrowers"
- "considerable weakening recently in overall growth of credit to nonfinancial businesses"

**Staff Transcript (63 ±7)**

- MR. STOCKTON: "financial conditions have tightened noticeably over the intermeeting period, and we expect this to persist and perhaps to intensify a bit over the projection interval. Interest rates have risen especially sharply for below-investment-grade borrowers."
- MR. STOCKTON: "banks have further tightened terms and standards on loans to business customers. Taken together, these developments have led us to mark down our forecast for the growth of real GDP by a bit more than 1/4 percentage point in each of the next two years."

**Staff Minutes (62 ±5)**

- "substantial increases in interest rates on lower-rated investment-grade and high-yield bonds, and the early November survey of senior loan officers indicated that banks had tightened further their standards and terms for business loans."
- Staff forecast: "some firming of conditions in credit markets" would hold back demand.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*(Discussion sources only)*

**Discussion Transcript (55 ±8)**

- GREENSPAN: Proposes holding rates unchanged with inflation-tilted risk statement but adds new language acknowledging economic weakness.
- MEYER: "I would prefer that we be very cautious about removing an assessment that the risks are weighted mainly toward higher inflation."
- POOLE (minority): "I would prefer a balanced risks statement... I would not like to leave the market with the message that we don't see any reasonable prospect of a policy easing."
- MCTEER (minority): "I would strongly prefer a statement of balanced risks."

**Discussion Minutes (56 ±5)**

- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" at 6.5%.
- "persisting risks of heightened inflation pressures remained a policy concern"
- "they were unanimous in concluding that such a change [to balanced risk] would be premature at this time"
- "a stronger case could be made for a shift to a balanced risk statement than at the previous meeting" and "several commented that developments might be moving in a direction that would make a shift advisable in the relatively near future"

## Cross-Source Notes

**DISSENT_INTENSITY gap (transcript 40 vs. minutes 18):** The transcript captures explicit individual-level preferences — Poole, Hoenig, and McTeer each state a preference for balanced risks by name; Gramlich names four colleagues sympathetic to that view. The minutes record only that "all members" voted to hold and "a few indicated that the decision was a close call," compressing the degree of internal disagreement visible in the verbatim record.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH gap (staff transcript 70 vs. staff minutes 54):** The staff transcript (Fisher's desk report) details that the euro has lost 20 percent against the dollar over the year with multiple other currency depreciations, producing a high score. The minutes summarize only that "the trade-weighted value of the dollar increased slightly further on balance over the intermeeting interval," reflecting only the marginal intermeeting change rather than the cumulative appreciation context.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: discussion sources (60–62) vs. staff sources (56–57):** Discussion participants cite specific percent-point pickup figures and characterize the direction as "unambiguous" or "more than a risk." Staff characterizes the same data as "very gradually" increasing toward approximately 2–2.25%. The staff framing is more measured in tone despite the same underlying data.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: transcript (55) vs. minutes (60):** The minutes record only the official statement retained and the observation that several members anticipated a near-term shift; they omit the multiple individual statements anticipating easing as the next move (McTeer, Guynn), which pull the transcript score toward the dovish side of the hawkish posture.
