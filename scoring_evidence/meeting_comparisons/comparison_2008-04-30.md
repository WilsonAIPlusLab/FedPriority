### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 68 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Minutes score: 64 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- "Inflation is high, unacceptably high in my view, and has been that way for a sustained period... year-over-year CPI inflation was 4 percent in March, and year-over-year PCE inflation was 3.4 percent—well above their 2007 levels." (Plosser)
- "Something persistent and pernicious, Mr. Chairman, has been occurring on the inflation front... prices paid and prices received are near 20-year highs for our firms." (Fisher)
- "Uncomfortably high total inflation that is feeding through to some limited extent into core inflation and, by some measures, into inflation expectations, especially near-term expectations." (Kohn)
- "Inflation outlook has worsened considerably... we are entering a dangerous period in which inflation expectations are beginning to move higher." (Hoenig)

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Every single participant acknowledges inflation running clearly above target (headline ~4%, core PCE above 2%) with meaningful upside risks from commodities, dollar depreciation, and possible expectations drift; the center-of-gravity is clearly elevated but not yet characterized as extreme or accelerating into a wage-price spiral.

**Transcript minority views**: Mishkin, Rosengren, and Geithner offer more sanguine readings — noting that core has not accelerated, labor compensation is contained, and long-run expectations remain "reasonably well anchored around 2 percent," which prevents a score above 75.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "crude oil prices to record levels, together with rapid increases in food and import prices in recent months, was likely to put upward pressure on inflation over the next few quarters"
- "total PCE inflation was projected to moderate from its current elevated level"
- "Several participants reported that business contacts had expressed growing concerns about the increase in their input costs and that there were signs that an increasing number of firms were seeking to pass on these higher costs"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants view headline inflation as clearly elevated due to energy/food prices, with meaningful upside risk from cost pass-through and potential inflation expectations drift, though core has improved somewhat and moderation is expected.

**Minutes minority views**: "Other participants noted...the extent of the pass-through...appeared relatively limited" and wage gains "remained moderate" — some see less pressure.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 30 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Minutes score: 34 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- "I am reasonably confident that we are in a recession. We don't see these dynamic patterns of employment, sentiment, and so on without a recession being eventually called by the NBER." (Bernanke)
- "Real GDP growth coming in around 1.5 percent for 2008... output gap opening this year." (Plosser)
- "A noticeable output gap opening this year... possible that some portion of 2008 might eventually be labeled a recession." (Evans)
- "Unemployment rate remains well above the NAIRU, even at the end of 2010." (Rosengren)

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The consensus view is that the economy is in or entering a mild-to-moderate recession with a clearly negative output gap opening and rising unemployment expected to remain elevated through 2010, placing this well below potential but not at catastrophic GFC-trough levels.

**Transcript minority views**: Lacker is less convinced of severe spillovers from financial markets to the real economy; Evans was "pleasantly surprised" no major downside surprises occurred since March, nudging toward the higher end of the band.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "many participants judging that real GDP was likely to contract slightly in the first half of 2008"
- "slack in resource utilization was likely to increase"
- "GDP growth was expected to begin to recover in the second half of this year"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly below potential with a negative output gap that is widening, though recovery anticipated in H2 2008. Not a deep recession yet but meaningful contraction.

**Minutes minority views**: "Views varied about the likely pace and vigor of the recovery through 2009" — some more pessimistic, some more optimistic about the recovery path.

**Transcript minority views**: Lockhart, Bullard, and Evans note that the "discovery process concerning asset-backed securities... has effectively come to a close" and markets have "improved substantially" — which adds uncertainty about the exact level but does not change the directional signal.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Minutes score: 32 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- Majority favors cutting 25bp (Alt B): "I think we should be lowering interest rates 25 basis points." (Kohn); "I definitely support a 25 basis point cut." (Yellen); "I'm going to recommend alternative B—25 basis points but with a stronger indication of a pause." (Bernanke)
- Significant minority favors holding (Alt C): "What I would like to do... is to make the case for why we should stand pat today." (Plosser); "I am in favor of a pause." (Fisher); "I believe that we should leave the fed funds rate alone today." (Lacker); "I do continue to hold the view that easing policy today is a mistake." (Hoenig); "I suggest that we put that on hold." (Bullard); "I, too, favor maintaining the federal funds rate at 2¼ percent today." (Evans)
- Final vote includes at least 2 formal dissents (Fisher and Plosser).

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: The majority votes to ease 25bp but pairs this with a strong pause signal removing easing-bias language; this is a moderately dovish lean — leaning toward providing some accommodation — but substantially restrained by the inflation concerns driving 6 participants toward holding.

**Transcript minority views**: The unusually large minority (6 out of ~16-17 participants) explicitly favoring no cut pushes the score toward the higher (less dovish) end of the moderate-dovish band.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points at this meeting"
- "the outlook for growth and employment remained weak and slack in resource utilization was likely to increase"
- "Some participants noted that the level of the federal funds target...was relatively low by historical standards"

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Majority favors continued easing (25bp cut), but this is a deceleration from prior larger cuts, and significant inflation concerns create tension. Moderate dovish lean.

**Minutes minority views**: Some see rates as already low relative to inflation, suggesting less room/appetite for further easing. Document is truncated so full dissent picture is incomplete.
