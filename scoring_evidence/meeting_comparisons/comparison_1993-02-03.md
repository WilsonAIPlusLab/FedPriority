# FOMC Meeting 1993-02-03: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 35 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "I think the way to deal with Bob's point on inflation may simply be to say something along the lines of: 'Just recognize the progress that has been made in bringing inflation down.'" — MR. MELZER

> "the adjustment is really consistent with our prior inflation goals and doesn't represent any attempt on our part to increase the pace of disinflation" — MR. PARRY

> "you will see on that bottom chart for all commodities ex-food and ex-oil a price move... I do want to call the Committee's attention to what I think is a possibility of our getting behind the curve" — MR. ANGELL (minority)

Center of gravity: Most participants view inflation as well-behaved and declining, with disinflation progress acknowledged. A small minority (Angell, with sympathy from Melzer and Broaddus) sees early commodity price signals of potential future inflation pressure.

**Minutes** (Score: 30 +/-7, High confidence)

> "Most of the members anticipated that the trend toward lower price and wage inflation would be sustained over the year ahead, and one member observed that the disinflationary momentum in the economy might well be underestimated."

> "Inflation remained quite subdued—only a bit above some estimates of price stability—and likely to moderate further in coming quarters in the view of most members."

> "Some commodity price indexes had edged higher recently...Lumber prices in particular had risen considerably."

Center of gravity: Most members see inflation as subdued and declining further, with only modest commodity price stirrings at the margin. The dominant view is continued disinflation.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "we are beginning to see some evidence that overall investment is beginning to move. We're seeing it obviously in capital goods; we're seeing it in residential construction" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "the commercial real estate problem is having a larger macroeconomic effect than I think we fully realized... this [sector] seems to me to be the one remaining major factor holding the recovery back" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "even if the economy performs well... if real GDP is 3 percent, we're still going to hear the criticism... that we are holding back the pace of this recovery" — MR. MULLINS

Center of gravity: The economy is recovering (two quarters of 3.5% GDP growth) but still operating below potential, with significant headwinds from commercial real estate and weak job creation.

**Minutes** (Score: 38 +/-6, High confidence)

> "Continued, albeit reduced, margins of unutilized or underutilized productive resources."

> "With some slack in productive resources persisting, price and cost pressures would remain subdued."

> "The members were encouraged by the mounting evidence of appreciable momentum in the economic expansion."

Center of gravity: Economy is recovering with appreciable momentum but still clearly below potential. Slack is diminishing but persists.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 32 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "the lack of job growth that politically is desired" — MR. ANGELL

> "the employment issue might be taking hold" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (tentative, forward-looking)

> "the kind of job growth that politically is desired" and ongoing criticism about insufficient recovery pace — MR. MULLINS

Center of gravity: The labor market is clearly slack with the "jobless recovery" a recurring theme. Unemployment remains elevated and job creation is insufficient, though Greenspan tentatively suggests employment may be starting to improve.

**Minutes** (Score: 30 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Despite tepid job growth..." Unemployment projected at "upper half of a 6-1/2 to 7 percent range" for Q4 1993.

> "Announcements by prominent firms of cutbacks in their workforces had continued into the new year."

> "The resulting layoffs had damped overall job growth."

Center of gravity: Labor market is significantly slack. Unemployment well above full employment. Restructuring layoffs continue. Job growth modest and net positive but tepid.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 43 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "overall investment is beginning to move. We're seeing it obviously in capital goods; we're seeing it in residential construction. And I suspect inventories may be preparing to move" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "the turnover in existing homes and the realized capital gains... coupled with the refinancing, are making a major contribution. But that can't go on indefinitely" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "we really cannot continue to get moderate real growth unless it's investment-led" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Investment is beginning to pick up (capital goods, residential construction) and consumption is sustained by refinancing/home turnover, but the overall level remains somewhat below trend with commercial real estate dead and sustainability questioned.

**Minutes** (Score: 52 +/-7, High confidence)

> "The economy was benefitting from considerable growth in consumer spending, from rising business expenditures for producer equipment, and from increasing outlays for housing."

> "Retail sales had strengthened markedly during the closing months of 1992, and several members commented that such sales had continued to display surprising vigor."

> "Commercial construction activity, however, was likely to remain quite sluggish." Exports face "greater downside risk."

> "Some members nonetheless expressed a degree of concern about the sustainability of the gains in consumer spending unless there were faster growth in employment."

Center of gravity: Consumer spending and equipment investment are strong and above recent trend, housing is accelerating. But commercial construction and exports are weak. Net is modestly above trend.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 45 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "the uncertain values in these markets are having a major impact on commercial banks' lending patterns... a major factor stunting the growth in lending, despite the obvious very heavy liquidity of the commercial banks" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "There has to be a reason why lending is as dull as it is" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "there's more bank aggressiveness, not less, going on. Restructuring has occurred more, not less, than a year ago" — MR. LINDSEY

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are mixed — rates are very low (3% fed funds), refinancing is booming, and overall liquidity is ample, but bank credit channels remain impaired by commercial real estate exposure and uncertain collateral values. The net is slightly loose of neutral but with significant credit friction.

**Minutes** (Score: 52 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "There were few indications thus far of any easing in terms or standards on business loans."

> "Improved profitability and new capital-market issuance had strengthened the capital positions of banking institutions, and in general they were now in a much better position to augment their lending activities."

> "Banks were likely to remain generally unaggressive in bidding for deposits."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are near neutral but still leaning slightly tight — bank lending standards have not eased despite improved bank capital. The steep yield curve and declining interest rates are accommodative, but bank behavior remains cautious.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 51 +/-5, High confidence)

> Unanimous vote for "B" symmetric (hold fed funds rate at 3%, symmetric directive)

> "I'm inclined to believe that we ought to be thinking about tightening at this stage. But I just don't have the stomach for doing it... I would prefer the alternative of asymmetric toward tightness" — MR. ANGELL

> "I favor 'B' asymmetric toward tightening, but I could support symmetry" — MR. MELZER

Center of gravity: Near-unanimous support for holding steady with a symmetric directive. The meeting tilts essentially neutral with a very faint hawkish lean from Angell and Melzer, who prefer asymmetry toward tightening but do not dissent.

**Minutes** (Score: 51 +/-5, High confidence)

> "All of the members endorsed a proposal to maintain unchanged conditions in reserve markets."

> "The members generally agreed that recent economic developments tended to reinforce the view that monetary policy was on an appropriate course."

> "Some commented that a further easing move at this juncture might well have adverse effects on inflation sentiment."

> "In the view of one member, these developments might argue for a tilt in the directive toward possible restraint."

Center of gravity: Firmly hold steady. No one argues for easing. Some express concern that easing would be counterproductive. One member leans toward a tightening tilt. The lean is very marginally hawkish relative to the hold — no easing bias present.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 22 +/-5, High confidence)

> "I think there's a fair consensus on 'B' symmetric" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "You have unanimous consent!" — MR. ANGELL

> "I'm not going to vote against... it seems to me that what we're doing is backing you up as the Chairman" — MR. ANGELL (on M2 ranges)

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on the policy decision with zero dissents. Some variety of views on M2 range technicalities but all converged. Angell and Melzer expressed mild hawkish preferences but explicitly chose not to press them.

**Minutes** (Score: 12 +/-5, High confidence)

> "By unanimous vote" on both the monetary aggregate ranges and the policy directive.

> "All of the members indicated that they favored a directive that called for maintaining the existing degree of pressure on reserve positions."

> "a number of members indicated that they preferred somewhat different ranges" (on M2 range specifics only)

Center of gravity: Near-consensus. Unanimous votes on all decisions. Disagreements limited to technical details of M2 ranges, not policy direction.

> "the uncertain values in these markets are having a major impact on commercial banks' lending patterns... the fact that a lending officer... cannot presume that commercial real estate mortgages can be unloaded expeditiously at a price that is reasonably forecastable has been a major factor stunting the growth in lending" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "it is a very large part of the collateral for the financial intermediary system and a horrendous part of the nonperforming loans" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Greenspan devoted meaningful attention to commercial real estate as a drag on financial intermediaries and bank lending, but this was primarily one speaker's focus framed as an economic headwind rather than a systemic stability concern. Other participants did not substantially engage with this theme.

**Minutes** (Score: 10 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> Balance sheet repair and banking conditions are discussed as economic context, not as financial stability threats.

> No discussion of systemic risk, bubbles, market stress, or financial stability as a distinct policy concern.

Center of gravity: Financial stability is essentially not a topic of concern. Banking and credit conditions are mentioned instrumentally in the context of economic outlook.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 50 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "I find it hard to think of any reason why we would want to [change the stance of policy] over the next 6-week period" — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> Symmetric directive adopted unanimously

> "I do want to call the Committee's attention to what I think is a possibility of our getting behind the curve by maintaining the fed funds rate at 3 percent for too long" — MR. ANGELL (minority forward-looking hawkish signal)

Center of gravity: The forward signal is neutral — symmetric directive, no indication of imminent action in either direction. The consensus is to hold and observe. Angell and Melzer provide a faint hawkish forward lean that keeps the score from dropping below 50.

**Minutes** (Score: 50 +/-5, High confidence)

> "They also noted their preference for, or acceptance of, a directive that did not include a presumption about the likely direction of any adjustment to policy over the intermeeting period."

> "Slightly greater reserve restraint or slightly lesser reserve restraint would be acceptable during the intermeeting period."

Center of gravity: Perfectly symmetric, neutral forward signal. No directional lean embedded in the forward guidance.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 42 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "there's just too much uncertainty here" — MR. ANGELL

> "The uncertainties are very, very significant" — MR. KEEHN

> "We're dealing with large uncertainties, vitally important unknowables" — MR. KELLEY

> "if our staff forecast is right, 3 percent is probably not a problem; if the Blue Chip forecast is right, it's probably a big problem" — MR. MELZER

Center of gravity: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about the M2/velocity relationship and its implications, but also about the economic outlook more broadly. The staff vs. Blue Chip forecast divergence on inflation adds to uncertainty. However, this is not deep Knightian uncertainty — most participants feel reasonably confident about the broad economic picture.

**Minutes** (Score: 55 +/-7, High confidence)

> "The outlook remained subject to a good deal of uncertainty, and some commented that substantial deviations—in either direction—from their current forecasts could not be ruled out."

> "It was noted in this connection that the specifics of the President's fiscal policy proposals were still unknown."

> Specific uncertainty sources cited: fiscal policy specifics unknown, restructuring/layoff trajectory, bank lending behavior, M2 velocity relationship ("considerable uncertainty").

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple specific sources of genuine uncertainty cited. "A good deal of uncertainty" and "considerable uncertainty" language used. Not just standard hedging — participants identify concrete unknowns.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 33 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> MR. PRELL (responding to Parry): "We feel somewhat vindicated by events, which showed in fact that we could have some decent growth with the disinflation trend continuing... with significant slack, the tendency is for inflation to slow."

> Greenbook projects inflation below 3 percent; most private forecasters expect higher inflation than the staff, which considers its own lower forecast credible. In 1993, only 3 of 50 Blue Chip forecasters see inflation as low as or lower than the Greenbook forecast.

Center of gravity: Staff views inflation as contained and declining — below any 2% target analog, with disinflation momentum continuing into 1993-94, driven by slack in labor and product markets.

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 32 +/-5, High confidence)

> "on balance they suggested that inflation was trending gradually lower" (staff data review)

> "The rise in this index [CPI core] in 1992 was the smallest for any year since the early 1970s, when wage and price controls were in effect"

> "The persisting slack in resource utilization over the forecast horizon was expected to be associated with some additional progress in reducing inflation" (staff forecast)

> "the rise in total compensation over the year as a whole was considerably smaller than in 1991"

Center of gravity: Staff presents a clear picture of ongoing disinflation, with core CPI at its lowest annual increase since the early 1970s and labor costs decelerating, while the staff forecast projects further moderation.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 37 +/-6, Moderate-High confidence)

> MR. PRELL (productivity Q&A): "In early phases of recovery we get very large productivity increases as some stretching of the available work force occurs. This time we had a lot of labor shedding and we haven't had even the normal employment gains." Implies economy is in early recovery, below potential.

> Prell's Q4 1992 GDP estimate of "3.6 percent" described as the Greenbook baseline — consistent with modest recovery pace, not an above-potential boom.

Center of gravity: Economy is in early expansion phase of recovery from the 1990-91 recession; output is below potential with significant slack remaining, though the gap is gradually closing.

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 38 +/-5, High confidence)

> "economic activity would expand over the year ahead at a pace that would be sufficient to reduce gradually margins of unemployed labor and capital" (staff forecast)

> "The persisting slack in resource utilization over the forecast horizon"

> UE at 7.3%, "almost 1/2 percentage point below its mid-year peak"

> "economic activity rose appreciably further in the fourth quarter" (staff data review)

Center of gravity: The economy is clearly operating below potential with significant slack in labor and capital, though the gap is narrowing as activity expands. The staff explicitly references persisting margins of unused resources.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 24 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> MR. PRELL (responding to Boehne): "At this stage, given the shedding of labor that has occurred, we'd expect to see a larger proportion showing up in actual employment [hours versus numbers of workers]." Firms reluctant to add workers; "structural adjustments by larger firms."

> MR. PRELL (Broaddus follow-up): "We would expect the unemployment rate, as a first approximation, probably to be 1/2 percentage point lower at the end of a year" in a 4% growth scenario. With actual UE ~7.1-7.3% in early 1993, even strong growth only modestly reduces unemployment.

Center of gravity: Labor market has significant slack with unemployment substantially above NAIRU; employment recovery is lagging GDP recovery due to productivity gains and continued labor shedding at major firms.

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 32 +/-4, High confidence)

> "The civilian unemployment rate remained at 7.3 percent, almost 1/2 percentage point below its mid-year peak but slightly above its level at the beginning of the year"

> "Total nonfarm payroll employment registered a small increase in December for the fourth consecutive month"

> "employment appeared to be on a modest upward trajectory, despite a continuing flow of announcements of layoffs by large corporations"

Center of gravity: The labor market is clearly loose with 7.3% unemployment and only modest payroll growth, well above estimates of full employment and with ongoing corporate layoff announcements.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 41 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> MR. SLIFMAN (responding to Stern on housing equity): "Some of [the capital gains] are being used to support consumption... from the refinancing data that a fair amount of cash is being taken out there, too." Housing equity liquification providing incremental support to consumer spending.

> MR. PRELL (inventory discussion): "Retailers reported stronger-than-expected sales coming out of the Christmas season, which suggests their inventories probably are in good shape." Consumer spending holding up.

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is recovering modestly, supported by housing equity effects and better retail performance, but remains below historical trend; business investment not described as strong; net position is slightly below-trend but improving.

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 55 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Consumer spending was up substantially in the fourth quarter"

> "Real outlays for business fixed investment apparently registered a notable gain in the fourth quarter, particularly for producers' durable equipment"

> "Housing starts surged in December, with single family starts reaching their highest level in nearly three years"

> Staff forecast: "further solid gains in business fixed investment and in homebuying"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending, equipment investment, and housing are all showing strong momentum — "substantially," "notable gain," and "surged" — though commercial construction remains weak. Net spending picture is moderately above trend.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 30 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> MR. MCDONOUGH (fiscal package discussion): Prior to the Bentsen $30B announcement, markets had been pricing in a "$15 to $20 billion" package; implication is that conditions are currently easy/accommodative and the main risk is a rate backup from a larger-than-expected fiscal stimulus.

> Context: Fed funds rate at 3%; discussion throughout is of low rates and potential upward pressure, not of tight credit or restrictive conditions. Slifman's cash-out refinancing data implies mortgage availability is open.

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are loose/accommodative — rates are low, credit is available (cash-out refinancing occurring), and the primary market concern is the possibility of fiscal-stimulus-driven tightening, not current restrictiveness.

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 35 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "Most other short-term interest rates declined somewhat over the intermeeting period"

> "Intermediate- and long-term rates, including those on fixed-rate mortgages, also moved somewhat lower"

> Staff forecast: "Recent declines in long-term interest rates and more optimistic attitudes...were expected to support further solid gains"

Center of gravity: Declining interest rates across the yield curve, including mortgage rates, are creating supportive financial conditions for the expansion, though credit availability is still constrained.
