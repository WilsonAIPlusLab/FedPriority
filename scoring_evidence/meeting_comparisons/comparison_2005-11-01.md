# FOMC Meeting 2005-11-01: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 62 ±8 [High confidence])

> "It's still clear from our discussions with business operators that price pressures are building... I feel that the heightened inflationary pressures that we talked about last time are lurking... one can sense, even though we don't see it in the data, inflation pressures creeping up like little cat's feet." — Fisher

> "our index for expected wage increases in manufacturing has skyrocketed to over 56 for October... consistent with the anticipation of a fairly broad-based upswing in inflation." TIPS "bouncing around in the neighborhood of 2.6." — Lacker

> "core CPI increased only 1.3 percent at an annual rate, while the core PCE price index was up 1.5 percent... I see no significant pressure for higher inflation coming from increased inflation expectations." — Yellen (counterbalance)

> "reports on inflation are almost uniformly on the upside rather than the downside... I believe that the inflation risks are asymmetric. It's much easier for me to imagine a ½ point forecast error with inflation coming in on the high side." — Poole

Center of gravity: The Committee senses building inflation pressures from energy cost pass-through and rising pricing power, with realized core data still benign (~1.5% PCE) but substantial anecdotal and leading evidence of upside risk, placing sentiment clearly above neutral at 62.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence])

> "Although oil and gasoline prices had fallen in recent weeks and core inflation had remained benign, some businesses had reported increased ability to pass through cost increases"

> "upside risks to the outlook for underlying inflation remained a key concern"

> "participants remained concerned about heightened inflation pressures"

Center of gravity: Core inflation is currently subdued, but participants express clear concern about upside risks from energy cost pass-through, placing perceived inflation pressure modestly above the neutral midpoint.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 57 ±5 [High confidence])

> "the resilience that the U.S. economy has so far experienced following the hurricanes suggests that real GDP will grow at above trend, at least over the near-term forecast period." — Hoenig

> "our forecast is for growth to continue at a pace just above our estimates of potential in '06—somewhere between 3 and 3.5 percent." — Geithner

> "demand and output, as he noted, appear to be continuing to grow at a pace that over time is likely to gradually put added pressure on resources." — Kohn

Center of gravity: Participants broadly see the economy running modestly above potential, with growth momentum intact despite hurricane disruptions, consistent with a score just above 50 at 57.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence])

> "The economy seemed to be growing at a fairly strong pace, despite the temporary disruptions associated with the hurricanes"

> "underlying economic slack was likely quite limited"

Center of gravity: Economy growing solidly with very limited slack, placing it modestly above potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence])

> "Labor markets are also starting to generate cost pressures... Many firms... now feel they have pushed productivity growth as far as it can go and... feel they need to hire more permanent employees... paying higher wages and also spending more on recruiting and retention bonuses." — Moskow

> "The economy now appears to be close to full employment, with a good deal of momentum." — Yellen

> trucking and shipping firms "having a difficult time finding qualified drivers." — Pianalto

Center of gravity: The labor market is clearly tight — near full employment, with staffing agencies reporting genuine shortages and firms budgeting higher 2006 wage increases — but not yet at extreme tightness, placing the score at 60.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 50 ±7 [Moderate confidence])

> "Wage gains had remained modest relative to continued strong productivity growth, suggesting that labor costs were not putting much upward pressure on prices"

> "underlying economic slack was likely quite limited" (general economy, not labor-specific)

Center of gravity: Labor market is near balance — slack limited overall but wages remain modest, suggesting neither tight nor loose.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 54 ±7 [High confidence])

> "Wal-Mart October same-store sales [up] about 4 percent, excluding gasoline. So sales were much stronger than they would have anticipated." Package delivery anticipating "very strong holiday season." — Poole

> "The underlying pace of demand growth seems reasonably strong—a bit stronger than we thought at our last meeting." Also: "strong stability in the growth of household consumption." — Geithner

> "continued growth in nominal consumer spending ex-autos since Katrina is evidence that the effects of energy price increases have not yet dampened household demand." — Lacker

> "downside risks to consumer spending still remain elevated" due to energy costs and potential substitution effects; auto sales dropped. — Guynn (counterbalance)

Center of gravity: Consumer spending has been resilient and somewhat stronger than feared post-hurricane, but participants flag visible near-term downside risk from higher heating costs and weakening auto sector, yielding a slightly above-trend score of 54.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 54 ±6 [High confidence])

> "spending seemed to have held up fairly well, aside from a drop in purchases of autos"

> "Growth in business investment spending seemed to remain moderate overall, but anecdotal reports suggested that a number of firms had boosted their plans for capital spending"

> "The housing market had remained robust"

> "Some participants noted...the erosion of consumer confidence, still-elevated gasoline prices, and the prospect of higher heating bills might augur weakness ahead." (minority view)

Center of gravity: Consumer spending holding up, business investment moderate but strengthening, housing robust — collectively slightly above trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 28 ±8 [High confidence])

> "The real funds rate is still low, there is substantial monetary accommodation in the system." — Guynn

> "Financial conditions remain supportive; fiscal policy is stimulative." — Hoenig

> "Financial conditions have tightened. Interest rates have moved higher; in response the exchange rate has firmed and stock prices have dropped a little... risk spreads have widened just a little." — Kohn (partial counterpoint)

Center of gravity: Despite ongoing tightening, multiple participants explicitly describe financial conditions as still supportive/accommodative and the real funds rate as still low, placing conditions in the loose-to-neutral range at 28.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 25 ±8 [Moderate confidence])

> "The rise in longer-term real interest rates and some widening of private credit spreads in recent months were seen as perhaps having a little restraining effect on the investment outlook"

> No discussion of credit stress, lending freezes, or tight conditions

Center of gravity: Financial conditions remain relatively loose in 2005, with only minor tightening noted from rising real rates and modestly wider spreads.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 72 ±5 [High confidence])

> Unanimous: Every single participant explicitly supports the 25bp hike. No dissenting voices.

> "I favor further tightening to contain inflationary impulses and expectations." — Fisher

> "should more definite signs of pass-through emerge in coming months, we should be prepared to communicate forcefully and convincingly our intention not to accommodate a deterioration in core inflation." — Lacker

> "I'm comfortable with tightening again at this meeting and signaling that we do not think we are finished removing accommodation." — Kohn

Center of gravity: The Committee is strongly and uniformly hawkish — unanimous 25bp hike with widespread consensus for at least one or two further increases, placing the score at 72.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence])

> "all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 4 percent"

> "all members believed it important to continue removing monetary policy accommodation in order to check upside risks to inflation"

> "Some members cautioned that risks of going too far with the tightening process could also eventually emerge"

Center of gravity: Unanimous support for continued tightening to contain inflation risks, placing the lean solidly hawkish, though with emerging caution about endpoint.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

> "I agree with your recommendations." — Kohn

> "I support your recommendations, Mr. Chairman." — Lacker

> The only "disagreement" is tone: Fisher notes he is "quite hawkish even relative to President Lacker"; but this doesn't produce a dissent.

Center of gravity: Broad agreement with minor tonal differences; unanimous vote and near-universal verbal concurrence puts dissent at a low level of 20.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 15 ±5 [High confidence])

> "all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points"

> "all members agreed to indicate...that a continued measured pace of policy firming remained likely"

> "some members cautioned" represents soft difference in outlook, not disagreement on action. (minority view)

Center of gravity: Near-unanimity on both action and forward guidance. Mild caution from "some" about going too far, but no dissents or strong reservations.

Center of gravity: Financial stability is addressed as context — primarily through housing moderation and consumer credit trends — but it is not a focal concern for most participants, yielding a score of 32.

Center of gravity: Brief mentions of housing valuation and credit spreads, but financial stability is not a focal topic — more contextual than deliberative.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 72 ±6 [High confidence])

> "the outlook for growth and inflation suggests that further monetary policy firming is likely to be necessary" — Geithner (proposed as future statement language)

> "I'm comfortable with tightening again at this meeting and signaling that we do not think we are finished removing accommodation." — Kohn

Center of gravity: Forward guidance is clearly hawkish — nearly all participants signal that further tightening is likely ahead — but with explicit acknowledgment that the cycle is approaching its end, making this a "further increases ahead, likely 1-2 more" signal at 72.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 72 ±6 [High confidence])

> "policy setting would need to be increasingly sensitive to incoming economic data"

> "Some members cautioned that risks of going too far with the tightening process could also eventually emerge"

Center of gravity: Clear forward signal of continued rate hikes, but with emerging data-dependence caveat — hawkish forward lean with modestly softening edges.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 48 ±8 [High confidence])

> "we're really delving into areas where our past experience is not all that helpful... What we have to do, I think, is come to grips with a very large element of uncertainty regarding how this process is emerging and how it's going to come out." — Greenspan

> "considerable uncertainty still clouds the outlook." — Yellen

> "there's a wider band of uncertainty around the economic outlook than there was pre-hurricane." — Santomero

> "Given the range of uncertainty, however, the important point for us at this meeting is that growth is likely to slow to closer to the rate of growth of potential." — Kohn

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is moderately elevated — driven by energy pass-through timing, neutral rate estimation, and Greenspan's structural uncertainty about global labor supply — but not extreme; most participants share the broad Greenbook outlook, placing uncertainty at 48.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence])

> "the near-term outlook continued to be subject to considerable uncertainty given the difficulties in assessing the net effects of the downturn in consumer confidence and the rise in energy prices through the summer, on the one hand, and the rebuilding from hurricane damage, on the other"

> "there was a risk that the large cumulative rise in energy and petroleum product prices through the summer would be transmitted to core consumer prices"

Center of gravity: Explicit "considerable uncertainty" language about near-term outlook, driven by hurricane effects and energy price pass-through, though longer-run outlook is more settled.

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence])

> "core PCE [personal consumption expenditures] inflation will be edging up early next year to a pace just shy of 2½ percent." — Stockton

> "embedded in this forecast is some implicit [increasing] inflation momentum on the order of about ½ percentage point... the pickup in actual core inflation that we've had, which has been a deterioration of about a percentage point [from 2003 to 2006]." — Stockton

> "indications of a noticeable deterioration in inflation expectations are largely limited to the very recent step-up in the Michigan Survey... last week's news on labor costs was a touch to the low side... provided few hints of any sizable acceleration." — Stockton

Center of gravity: Staff sees core PCE inflation running above 2% and edging toward 2½%, with some backward feed-through from headline energy prices into expectations, but with contained labor costs and an expected moderation as energy effects fade.

---

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence])

> "Despite the large increase in consumer energy prices since midyear, core price inflation was restrained through September."

> "Favorable incoming data led the staff to reduce its forecast for near-term core inflation a bit."

> Staff projects "core inflation to pick up modestly over coming quarters owing to the lagged effects of higher energy prices."

Center of gravity: Core inflation contained but energy-related upside risks create slight above-neutral pressure reading.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 57 ±6 [High confidence])

> "real GDP will be growing at a 3¾ percent rate in the first half of next year — faster than potential." — Stockton

> "Real GDP is estimated to have increased 3.8 percent at an annual rate last quarter... the growth of real GDP is expected to be close to the pace of potential during the next two years — a bit above it in 2006 and a bit below it in 2007." — Stockton

Center of gravity: The economy is currently operating modestly above potential growth pace, with the gap expected to narrow as policy tightening takes hold. Near-term output is above trend but converging.

---

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 53 ±5 [Moderate confidence])

> "The economy had a good deal of forward momentum in the third quarter."

> "Manufacturing capacity utilization... was still noticeably above its year-earlier level."

> Staff projects "moderate economic growth for the second half of 2005" with pickup in 2006.

Center of gravity: Economy growing with solid momentum; capacity utilization above year-ago levels; staff sees moderate-to-solid growth trajectory.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 53 ±7 [Moderate confidence])

> Employment gains "outside the areas affected by Katrina were on pace with those earlier in the year." Upside risk scenario mentions "margins of slack in both product and labor markets" remain, but this is framed as an upside risk concern, not the baseline. — Stockton

> Labor market is not described as severely tight — the focus is on whether expansion can continue to push output above potential. — Stockton

Center of gravity: Labor market is near but slightly above NAIRU levels (~5% unemployment in late 2005), with employment maintaining its pre-hurricane pace once stripped of storm effects. Staff does not describe significant slack nor acute tightness.

---

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 50 ±5 [Moderate confidence])

> "The unemployment rate rose 0.2 percentage points to 5.1 percent."

> "Employment in the areas unaffected by the hurricane increased at a rate in line with the average pace over the previous twelve months."

Center of gravity: UE at 5.1% near estimated NAIRU for the era; hurricane distortions make underlying conditions hard to assess precisely but labor market near balance.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 53 ±7 [Moderate confidence])

> "Motor vehicle sales have dropped off sharply since July, and consumer spending excluding motor vehicles slowed noticeably over the same period and is estimated to have actually declined in September." Also notes "steep drop in consumer sentiment." — Stockton

> "continued solid gains in capital spending that underpin our [baseline] forecast." Third-quarter GDP 3.8%, and "the economy [was] at least as strong, if not a bit stronger, than we had anticipated." — Stockton

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is softening in Q4 (driven partly by hurricane/gasoline disruptions and partly by genuine caution), but business investment remains solid and overall momentum supports a near-trend GDP outlook.

---

**Staff Minutes** (Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence])

> "Real consumer spending increased at a moderate rate in the third quarter as a whole, although it declined in August and September."

> "Residential construction continued at a robust pace."

> Equipment/software "sluggish in the summer, but a broad-based pickup in orders... suggested some firming."

Center of gravity: Housing very strong, business investment firming, consumer spending softening from hurricane/energy effects. Net slightly above trend.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Staff Transcript** (Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence])

> "if there is some suggestion that financial conditions are tightening [from rising real yields], the evidence elsewhere was mixed." — KOS

Center of gravity: Credit freely flowing, lending standards easing, debt growing briskly — classic accommodative financial conditions despite ongoing rate hikes.
