## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence Comparison

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Disc-Transcript: 55**
- "The gradual increase in core inflation over the past year is a concern to me... our more accommodative policy may very well lead consumers, laborers, and businesses to ratchet up their inflation expectations." (Hoenig)
- "The modest rise in core inflation and the upward adjustment to the staff's inflation forecast support this assessment. These developments confirm, in my view, that inflation risks were indeed building before the economy slowed so sharply." (Meyer)
- "the pricing power of business firms is very limited at this point... the capacity to raise prices in an environment such as this is extraordinarily restricted." (Greenspan)

**Disc-Minutes: 53**
- "A number of members commented that the persisting updrift in some key measures of core inflation had become increasingly worrisome."
- "Most members did not foresee a significant rise in inflation as a likely prospect" citing competitive conditions and expected easing of labor pressures.
- "Not all measures of core inflation had accelerated; in particular, core PCE price inflation had been quite stable."

**Staff-Transcript: 48**
- Staff (Stockton) focus is on weakness; energy prices declining help contain inflation; slack developing.
- No explicit inflation-elevated language from staff in transcript; focus on output contraction as constraint on price pressures.

**Staff-Minutes: 45**
- "Price inflation had picked up a little but, abstracting from energy, had remained relatively subdued."
- Core CPI "picked up slightly" on year-over-year basis but core PCE "recorded a small decline on a year-over-year basis."
- Staff forecast: "The period of subpar expansion was expected to foster an easing of pressures on resources and some moderation in core price inflation."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Disc-Transcript: 35**
- "an economy growing--and likely to continue to grow far below its potential" (Ferguson)
- "Overall industrial production is down, capacity utilization rates are at 10-year lows, and inventories outside of transportation and high-tech are building up." (Gramlich)
- "Nearly half the respondents expect an outright recession... the risks continue to be skewed toward economic weakness." (Moskow)

**Disc-Minutes: 36**
- "The slowdown in the expansion to a now quite sluggish pace was likely to be more prolonged than they had anticipated earlier."
- "It was not entirely clear that the slowing in the growth of the economy had bottomed out."
- "Continuing subpar economic growth" and "economy perhaps still in the midst of a process of weakening growth in aggregate demand."

**Staff-Transcript: 42**
- "the economy, at present, is very weak — not in recession, but not far from it either." (Stockton)
- Below potential now; manufacturing declining; investment falling.
- Staff forecast baseline: high-tech recession; capital spending falling sharply.

**Staff-Minutes: 33**
- "The economic expansion remained very sluggish."
- "The rate of utilization of manufacturing capacity fell even further below its long-run average."
- Staff forecast: expansion would "gradually regain strength" and "move back toward a rate near the staff's current estimate of the growth of the economy's potential output."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Disc-Transcript: 42**
- "Slower employment growth has boosted unemployment rates... However, as of yet, slackening labor markets have not resulted in lower wage inflation... total compensation in the West grew by 5 percent." (Parry)
- "the Manpower survey of hiring intentions... the lowest since 1993 and the two-quarter drop is the largest ever outside a recessionary period." (Moskow)
- "openings for over 1,000 registered nurses... many staff support positions continue to go unfilled... unemployment rate dropping to 3.8 percent and 3.5 percent" in Ohio (Jordan)

**Disc-Minutes: 48**
- "Widespread evidence of some lessening of pressures in most labor markets across the nation had not yet resulted in lower wage inflation."
- "Labor markets were still tight by historical standards."
- "Further increases in unemployment in the period just ahead."

**Staff-Transcript: 50**
- "labor income weakening." Employment softening. UE still moderate.
- Staff characterization places labor near neutral — weakening but not yet slack.

**Staff-Minutes: 42**
- "labor demand had weakened considerably and unemployment had risen."
- "Private nonfarm payroll employment fell sharply in April."
- Unemployment rate at 4.5% in April; initial claims at "highest level since 1993."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Disc-Transcript: 33**
- "[Investment] remains largely sidelined... he simply was sitting on several large and very strategic investment spending proposals... he just wasn't going to turn them loose right now." (Guynn)
- "almost all manufacturers... were reviewing, freezing, or scaling back capital spending plans." (Moskow)
- "April retail sales were robust... Consumer sentiment showed an improvement" but also "record household debt burdens, over-investment by business firms." (Kelley)

**Disc-Minutes: 34**
- "Consumer spending had held up reasonably well in recent months despite a variety of adverse developments."
- "A softening in business demand for capital equipment had accounted for much of the slowdown"; "the latest available data on new orders pointed to further, and possibly larger, declines in business spending."
- "Ongoing efforts to reduce excess inventories were continuing to curb output" — inventory liquidation a major drag.

**Staff-Transcript: 32**
- "only a meager gain in consumer spending." (Stockton)
- "Real E&S spending to decline again this quarter." Capital goods orders "fell virtually across the board."
- "Consumer sentiment still down."

**Staff-Minutes: 35**
- "Household spending... had held up relatively well, but business investment was quite weak and appeared to be decreasing further."
- Retail sales "rose briskly in April."
- High-tech equipment spending "decreased on a quarterly basis for the first time since the 1990 recession"; new orders data "suggested that some further slippage in future spending for equipment was likely."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Disc-Transcript: 40** (FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS — higher = tighter)
- "The decline in short-term rates, the decline in risk spreads, and double-digit rates of growth in M2 are having beneficial effects." (Hoenig)
- "bankers do say that lending standards are being tightened modestly, to some extent in response to pressure from regulators." (Poole)
- "equity indices... have in fact experienced growth of 6 to 9 percent... businesses are issuing bonds to fill their coffers." (Ferguson)

**Disc-Minutes: 50** (FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS — Low confidence, ±10)
- "Negative wealth effects of stock market declines" mentioned as headwind.
- "Very attractive mortgage interest rates" supporting housing.
- "policy might still be considered to be only marginally accommodative."

**Staff-Transcript: 55** (FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — higher = tighter)
- "strength of the dollar and the weakness in stock prices have likely acted to offset some of the stimulus" from rate cuts. (Stockton)
- Funds rate in "territory that implies a considerable stimulus to economic activity over time" — but offset by dollar/stocks.

**Staff-Minutes: 55** (FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — higher = tighter)
- Staff forecast identifies headwinds from "the decline in household net worth associated with the earlier downturn in equity prices, the continuation of relatively stringent terms and conditions on some types of loans by financial institutions, and the appreciation of the dollar."
- "risk premiums on lower-grade private debt obligations" narrowed; equity prices rose in intermeeting period.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Disc-Transcript: 32**
- "I would hope that a 4 percent funds rate is as low as we will have to go." (Greenspan)
- "I think we should pull back on the throttle today... I'm of the view that a 25 basis point cut... is what we should do now." (Hoenig)
- "the economy, both domestically and internationally, is currently in a very tenuous state, with still significant downside risks... I think we can safely lean harder against these recessionary winds." (Gramlich)

**Disc-Minutes: 30**
- "All but one of the members indicated that they could support a proposal calling for further easing of reserve conditions consistent with a 50 basis point reduction."
- "All the members agreed that further easing was desirable in light of what they viewed as the continuing weakness in the economy."
- "Some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near."

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Disc-Transcript: 48**
- "I'd be happier with no change or a 1/4 point reduction today." (Jordan)
- "I'm of the view that a 25 basis point cut... is what we should do now." (Hoenig)
- "I actually would prefer to come out of today's meeting with some clear signal that we have slowed the pace of easing... a 25 basis point easing would be a cleaner, preferable action." (Guynn)
- Multiple others (Broaddus, Minehan, Santomero, Stern, Meyer) expressed sympathy for 25bp but ultimately supported 50bp.

**Disc-Minutes: 32**
- "One member expressed a strong preference for a 25 basis point reduction and two others indicated that they could have accepted that more limited easing move."
- "Despite their somewhat differing preferences, all the members agreed that further easing was desirable."
- Members who preferred 25bp emphasized different risk weighting but shared the same directional assessment.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Disc-Transcript: 35**
- "even if higher inflation is not a concern, financial market excesses should be. Extended periods with interest rates that are arguably too low for too long... can produce a predilection for one-way market bets." (Minehan)
- "stock market prices are still probably higher than what our models say would be consistent with their fundamental determinants. The trouble... is that it never stops where the models say it should." (Greenspan)
- Extensive discussion of market volatility and uncertainty in interpreting market signals. (McDonough)

**Disc-Minutes: 18**
- Stock market declines discussed as wealth effect on consumers, not as financial stability concern per se.
- Bond yields discussed in context of inflation expectations.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market dysfunction, or financial stability frameworks.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Disc-Transcript: 35**
- "I would hope that a 4 percent funds rate is as low as we will have to go... we should begin in speeches or interviews to address the possibility that 250 basis points of ease... may be enough." (Greenspan)
- "There will be a very large signal received from this statement, which is that we've removed any notion of an intermeeting move." (McDonough)
- "we should start indicating that 250 basis points of ease may be enough." (Moskow)

**Disc-Minutes: 32**
- "The Committee continued to regard the risks to the economic outlook as being tilted toward weakness even after today's easing action."
- "Members anticipated that a neutral balance of risks statement could be appropriate before long."
- "It was not clear how much more the federal funds rate might have to be reduced after today... some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Disc-Transcript: 65**
- "the main message the market is conveying is that it is extremely uncertain. So am I... our models don't work in assessing the effects of a loss of consumer confidence." (McDonough)
- "the risks are really different from any I've ever seen... the risk distribution is not symmetric. Indeed, it has a fairly worrisome tail at the wrong end." (Greenspan)
- "the economic situation this quarter continues to be highly uncertain." (Moskow)
- "the region's increasing concentration of information technology companies... does not bode well... this is the region's largest area of vulnerability and uncertainty." (Minehan)

**Disc-Minutes: 57**
- "The members were uncertain as to the degree and timing of the strengthening in final demand."
- "How long inventory cutbacks would continue to exert a significant drag on the economic expansion remained a key uncertainty."
- "Economy perhaps still in the midst of a process of weakening growth in aggregate demand of unknown persistence and dimension."
- "Given prevailing uncertainties, it would be premature for the Committee to shift its balance of risks statement."

### 12. Staff-Only Dimensions

#### CREDIT_SPREADS (Staff-Minutes: 48)
- "a narrowing of risk premiums on lower-grade private debt obligations."
- "The debt of domestic nonfinancial sectors had grown at a moderate pace."
- Spreads recently narrowed from elevated levels; near neutral.

#### BANKING_STRESS (Staff-Minutes: 55)
- Staff forecast references "the continuation of relatively stringent terms and conditions on some types of loans by financial institutions."
- Modest tightening in bank credit supply; no systemic concern raised.

#### DOLLAR_STRENGTH (Staff-Transcript: 62 / Staff-Minutes: 55)
- Transcript: "strength of the dollar... have likely acted to offset some of the stimulus" from rate cuts. (Stockton)
- Minutes: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar... changed little on balance over the intermeeting interval." Staff forecast: "the appreciation of the dollar" cited as a factor holding back domestic demand.

#### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (Disc-Transcript: 58 / Disc-Minutes: 52)
- Disc-Transcript: Hoenig and Santomero explicitly concerned about expectations ratcheting up with accommodative policy.
- Disc-Minutes: "Some of the recent increases in bond yields could represent a rise in long-term inflation expectations." But "survey data did not confirm any increase in long-term inflation expectations."

## Notable Divergences

| Dimension | Gap | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / CONDITIONS | Disc-Transcript 40 vs. Staff-Minutes 55 | Participants (transcript) emphasize loosening from rate cuts and narrowing spreads; staff emphasize residual tightness from lending standards, dollar, and prior equity wealth losses. |
| DISSENT_INTENSITY | Disc-Transcript 48 vs. Disc-Minutes 32 | Transcript captures extensive individual expressions of 25bp preference and intra-committee debate; minutes compress this to "one member" and "two others," substantially reducing apparent dissent. |
| FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS | Disc-Transcript 35 vs. Disc-Minutes 18 | Transcript contains Minehan's explicit financial-excesses warning and Greenspan's stock valuation discussion; minutes omit these as separate financial stability concerns. |
| UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED | Disc-Transcript 65 vs. Disc-Minutes 57 | Transcript captures specific rhetorical intensity ("models don't work," "risks different from any I've ever seen"); minutes convey uncertainty through more measured quantifier language. |
| OUTPUT_GAP | Staff-Transcript 42 vs. Staff-Minutes 33 | Transcript Stockton framing ("very weak, not far from recession") maps to a higher absolute level than the minutes' "very sluggish" characterization with manufacturing capacity further below long-run average. |
| LABOR_TIGHTNESS | Disc-Minutes 48 vs. Disc-Transcript 42 / Staff-Minutes 42 | Minutes emphasize "still tight by historical standards"; transcript and staff-minutes both weight the sharp deterioration in payrolls and hiring intentions more heavily. |
