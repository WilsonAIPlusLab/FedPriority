# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1997-03-25

## Meeting Context
March 1997. Economy in its sixth year of expansion. Unemployment ~5.3%, real GDP growth running above trend at 3.5%+, core CPI ~2.5%. Fed funds rate at 5.25% for over a year. This meeting produces a unanimous 25bp hike to 5.50% — the first tightening since February 1994. Debate centers on whether to do 25bp or 50bp and whether to adopt a symmetric or asymmetric directive.

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 50 ±8 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 55 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: "inflation is flat to down according to many statistical series"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still do not have concrete evidence to suggest that the inflation rate has stopped going down"
- MR. PARRY: "I see a significant risk of an increase in underlying inflation in the years ahead... our forecast shows core CPI inflation of around 2-3/4 percent in 1997 and 3 percent in 1998"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "we have raised the rate of core inflation expected to be reached in 1998 to 3.3 percent"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Current inflation readings are at or slightly below target, but nearly every participant sees significant upside risk due to tight labor markets and above-trend growth. Score of 50 reflects the current benign reading; wide uncertainty band captures strong forward-looking concern.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "inflation had remained relatively subdued, and there were no signs in price data that it was picking up"
- "the risks of a rise in inflation down the road had increased appreciably"
- "inflationary developments in the economy had become a matter of more urgent concern for monetary policy"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Current inflation is subdued, but members see appreciably increased forward risks due to tight resources and above-potential growth. Score modestly above midpoint on net, reflecting the "more urgent concern" framing.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 57 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "the economy is now operating at a very high level and it strikes me as being quite vulnerable to upside shocks"
- MR. PARRY: "excess demand pressures most likely are building in the economy"
- MR. KELLEY: "Growth for the past four quarters including this one is going to be somewhere in excess of 3-1/2 percent. That is certainly well beyond anybody's estimate of potential in an economy whose resources already seem to be fully utilized."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are not at this stage moving into what I would describe as an overheated boom. We are short of that."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Economy operating modestly above potential with growth well above trend. Not yet overheating but clearly beyond sustainable capacity, particularly in labor markets.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "the surprising strength in economic activity over the closing months of 1996 was persisting in 1997"
- "an economy that already was operating at high levels of resource utilization"
- "prospects for a substantial period of economic expansion at a rate that exceeded the estimated growth of potential"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Members broadly agreed the economy was operating above potential with growth exceeding potential growth.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight throughout most of the District"
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor shortages are constraining both employment and output growth"
- MS. MINEHAN: "the definition of 'selected' [job categories that are tight] seems to be broadening a bit"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the tightness in labor markets is increasing... we are running out of space to expand, at least in terms of labor market resources"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Labor markets clearly tight across virtually all districts, with shortages broadening from skilled to unskilled categories. Wage pressure present but contained by temporary factors (job insecurity, rising participation, strong dollar).

**Minutes evidence:**
- "labor markets already were tight" and "tightness in labor markets appeared to be intensifying"
- "rise in labor force participation to a high percentage of the working age population had helped to keep the unemployment rate from falling, but the unexpected increase in participation was itself suggesting tight conditions"
- "data on worker compensation were somewhat mixed, but they suggested some acceleration on balance"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor markets described as tight and intensifying, with multiple data points and widespread anecdotal confirmation. Scores identical across both sources.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript score: 64 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 63 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MS. RIVLIN: "Consumer spending is strong; inventories are lean; business investment, housing, and nonresidential construction, you name it, look good. It just does not look like an economy that is running out of steam."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the levels of consumer spending, consumer confidence, and residential construction are so high... business fixed investment seems unusually robust"
- MR. KELLEY: "sky-high consumer confidence with jobs plentiful, and supportive wealth effects"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "personal consumption expenditures are... significantly affected by the wealth effect"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Consumption and investment running well above trend, driven by wealth effects, high consumer confidence, and strong business investment.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "members emphasized the strength of consumer spending in recent months" with "an ebullient consumer sector"
- "Business fixed investment, which had remained on a steep uptrend for an extended period"
- "a number of members expressed the view...that the risks to such a forecast were in the direction of more robust consumer spending"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Both consumer spending and business investment described as strong, with upside risks to consumer spending. Scores nearly identical.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 24 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "financial conditions remain very comfortable whether we look at interest rates, growth in the broad measures of money, equity prices... or credit availability in general"
- MR. JORDAN: "lending standards relating to the construction of commercial real estate have disappeared in the last couple of months"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Competition for bank lending is stiff, with the predictable squeezing of margins and slippage of underwriting standards"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions clearly loose — equity prices elevated, credit freely available, lending standards slipping, risk premiums compressed. Broad agreement that conditions are easy.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "financial conditions that, broadly considered, were not substantially different from those now prevailing" (i.e., conditions have not tightened despite strong economy)
- "readily available financing" for commercial construction; "speculative building activity" emerging
- Stock market wealth effects boosting consumer spending; mortgage rates risen somewhat but housing still affordable

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions described as accommodative — financing available, wealth effects positive, no tightening despite strong economy. The 11-point gap between sources reflects the transcript's more pointed characterizations (Jordan's "disappeared" standards) versus the minutes' more measured language.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I do not think that there is any particular urgency to move in a very aggressive way. I do think we have to move... 25 basis points is enough for now"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the case for a larger move is stronger than indicated by others"
- MR. PARRY: "estimated Taylor Rule... suggests a gradual rise in the funds rate totaling about 100 basis points"
- MS. RIVLIN: "A small move to restrain growth and the future acceleration of inflation that seems increasingly likely could prolong this remarkable expansion"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unanimous support for 25bp tightening. Several participants make the case for 50bp or asymmetric directive. No one argues for holding. Debate is about how much to tighten, not whether to tighten.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "all the members indicated that they supported or could accept a directive that called for a slight increase in the degree of pressure on reserve positions"
- "they saw a clear need for a preemptive policy action that would head off any pickup of inflation"
- "A few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unanimous support for a rate hike framed as preemptive. The center supported 25bp; a few wanted more. Scores are identical across both sources.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript score: 28 ±5 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- Unanimous vote for 25bp with symmetric directive
- MR. BROADDUS: "I can accept your recommendation... the case for a larger move is stronger" (mild reservation)
- MR. PARRY: "I also would strongly prefer to have language in the directive that is asymmetric toward further tightening" (strongest procedural disagreement)
- Several others note preference for asymmetry but accept symmetry without dissent

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Broad agreement on direction and magnitude. Differences are about pace and forward signaling (symmetric vs. asymmetric directive), not about fundamental assessment or whether to act. No formal dissents.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Votes against this action: None" — unanimous
- "A few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed"
- Debate over symmetric vs. asymmetric directive: "a majority...favored a symmetric directive" while "other members believed...additional tightening would be needed sooner rather than later"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** No formal dissent, but internal debate about size of move and directive tilt. The transcript scores slightly higher (28 vs. 22) reflecting the more vivid individual expressions of preference visible in the full record.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
**Minutes score: 18 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "lending agreements with no takeouts being arranged, no permanent financing being locked up, no tenants being signed" (commercial real estate lending concerns)
- MR. GUYNN: "a few of my contacts in real estate and banking are expressing some concern about developers who seem to be forgetting the lessons of the 1980s"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: discusses declining risk premiums, wealth effects, and notes "What we are trying to avoid is bubbles that break"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial stability receives scattered mentions — commercial real estate excesses, wealth effects, lending standards — but these are subsidiary to the main macroeconomic discussion.

**Minutes evidence:**
- Stock market wealth effects discussed as transmission mechanism to consumer spending: "run-up in household net worth stemming from the earlier buoyant performance of the stock market"
- "speculative building activity" noted in a few areas
- No discussion of systemic risk, asset bubbles as policy concern, or financial imbalances per se

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions mentioned as context for the economic outlook, not as a standalone stability concern. The 12-point gap reflects the transcript capturing Jordan's and Greenspan's more pointed remarks that were compressed or omitted in the minutes summary.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript score: 67 ±6 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think the odds are better than 50/50 that the move we are considering will not be our last tightening move. We are very likely to have to move again"
- MR. BROADDUS: "this move very likely will not be our last and that we will have to tighten further... tightening may happen sooner rather than later, perhaps before the May meeting"
- MR. PARRY: Taylor Rule suggests 100bp total tightening through year-end
- MR. MEYER: "containing a threat to higher inflation is likely to require some further increase in short-term rates beyond the small change contemplated today"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Forward signal clearly tilted toward further tightening. The Committee chose a symmetric directive, but the discussion strongly suggests this is the beginning of a tightening cycle.

**Minutes evidence:**
- Symmetric directive: "did not include a presumption about adjustments to policy during the intermeeting period"
- "additional policy tightening might be needed at some point, it did not appear very likely that developments during the intermeeting period would require a further policy move"
- "Other members believed...additional tightening would be needed sooner rather than later"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The symmetric directive signals no preset path, but the context of a rate hike and discussion of possible further tightening creates a mild hawkish lean. The 9-point gap reflects that the minutes emphasize the symmetric directive outcome while the transcript captures Greenspan's explicit "better than 50/50" language.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript score: 52 ±7 [High confidence]**
**Minutes score: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the best thing to concentrate on at the moment... is what we do not know... we are not, and certainly I am not, altogether sure why inflation and the cost-push causes of inflation have been so benevolent"
- MR. PARRY: "I emphasize the word 'risk' because we have not actually seen signs of rising inflation in the data. This fact obviously raises uncertainty about the future."
- MR. STERN: "We are uncertain about the outlook; we are uncertain about the effectiveness of policy; and because of those uncertainties, I think we ought to be cautious."
- MR. BOEHNE: "there is a greater divergence between the person-in-the-street view and this macro framework than I have seen in a long time"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants express elevated uncertainty, particularly about why inflation has remained quiescent despite tight labor markets. Productivity puzzle, NAIRU uncertainty, and unpredictable temporary factors contribute to genuine intellectual puzzlement.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "substantial uncertainty surrounded the timing and extent of such slowing in the quarters ahead"
- "it was not possible to forecast with confidence when the period of favorable price behavior would end"
- "the outlook for economic activity and inflation remained subject to substantial uncertainties"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The word "substantial" is used twice regarding uncertainty, particularly about when expansion would slow and when inflation would rise. The 10-point gap suggests the transcript conveys more vivid and diverse uncertainty expressions — including McDonough's and Stern's explicit admissions — compared to the more formulaic minutes language.
