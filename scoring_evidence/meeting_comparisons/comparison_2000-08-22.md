## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores: DT 55 | DM 58 | ST 59 | SM 57**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- "The acceleration of price inflation is essentially still a forecast...the rate of increase of core PCE inflation for the last three months is...only slightly higher than 1/2 percent at an annual rate." (Greenspan)
- "Core inflation this year and next is still expected to be higher than it was last year...the balance of risks remains tilted toward higher inflation." (Parry)
- "Core CPI inflation today is exactly the same as it was at the end of 1995 on a methodologically consistent basis." (Meyer)

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "Overall measures of price inflation had picked up to fairly high levels by the standards of recent years, largely as a result of higher energy costs."
- "Core consumer price indices remained relatively damped and had risen only a little over the last year, especially when measured by the PCE chain-price index."
- "The members saw a mild upward trend in key measures of inflation as a distinct possibility, albeit one that was subject to considerable uncertainty."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "Labor markets remain extremely tight. Virtually all of the data on hourly compensation have come in to the high side of our June forecast — decidedly so in the case of the ECI, which outstripped our second-quarter forecast by nearly a percentage point at an annual rate." (Stockton)
- "We now anticipate that core PCE price inflation will move above 2 percent by the end of next year...still nearly 1/2 percentage point higher than its current pace." (Stockton)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Recent data suggested that price inflation had picked up slightly."
- "Core CPI prices increased somewhat more than in the previous twelve-month period."
- "The change in compensation rates was substantially larger than the change over the previous four-quarter period." But "unit labor costs had changed little and measures of core price inflation had increased only mildly."

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores: DT 55 | DM 58 | ST 61 | SM 57**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- "The gap between aggregate supply and demand is beginning to close" and "the Greenbook forecast implies that that gap will narrow considerably more." (Greenspan)
- "Growth substantially below potential over the forecast horizon, we expect the unemployment rate to begin rising." (McDonough)
- "I still judge the economy to be strong, as evidenced by the strong GDP report and the continued strength in business investment." (Guynn)

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "Growth of aggregate demand and that of aggregate supply were coming into closer balance."
- "Labor markets would remain exceptionally tight."
- "Business outlays for capital equipment and software had continued to rise at exceptional rates."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "We still think the 6 percent pace of the past four quarters is not sustainable. But because of our supply-side revisions, that pace is not as far above potential growth as before." (Stockton)
- "In this projection, we anticipate the unemployment rate to rise to about 4-1/4 percent by the end of next year...this remains too low to prevent a further gradual deterioration in inflation." (Stockton)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Economic activity was expanding at a more moderate pace than earlier in the year" — from an "elevated pace."
- Staff forecast: expansion "would be sustained at a rate a little below that of the staff's upwardly revised estimate of the economy's potential output."
- Capacity utilization "around its long-term average."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores: DT 70 | DM 70 | ST 69 | SM 73**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- "Fairfax County, Virginia...the reported unemployment rate is 0.9 percent." (Broaddus)
- "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight, with several New England states recording unemployment rates below 3 percent." (Minehan)
- "The job market has gone from super hot to what I would describe as sort of warm." (Ferguson)
- "Texas unemployment rate at 4.1 percent is now at its lowest level since 1974." (McTeer)

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "Members anticipated that labor markets would remain exceptionally tight."
- "Unusually taut labor markets could begin at some point to show through to increases in labor compensation in excess of productivity gains."
- "A period of unusually low unemployment rates."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "Labor markets remain extremely tight" and "there have been more than a few instances in which hiring has been held back by a lack of qualified workers." (Stockton)
- The ECI outstripped the June forecast "by nearly a percentage point at an annual rate." (Stockton)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Labor markets remained extremely tight by historical standards."
- "The civilian unemployment rate remained at 4.0 percent in July."
- "Some measures of labor compensation had accelerated."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores: DT 52 | DM 57 | ST 58 | SM 55**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- "Demand growth moderated from the first quarter to the second...retailing, motor vehicles--especially heavy trucks--machine tools, steel, aluminum, and paper" all slowing. (Moskow)
- "In complete contrast to developments in the consumer and housing sectors is what is going on in the capital goods markets...overall capital investment continues to boom." (Greenspan)
- Semiconductor business reporting "90 percent increase in revenues and a 40 percent increase in orders." (Minehan)

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "Growth in consumer spending had moderated substantially after a period of exceptional gains."
- "Business outlays for capital equipment and software had continued to rise at exceptional rates, even after several years of rapid growth."
- "The housing sector provided the clearest indication of a response of aggregate demand to firming interest rates."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "Outlays for capital equipment have continued to surge ahead. One of the most notable aspects of the recent annual revision to the national accounts was that the investment boom of recent years is now estimated to have been even stronger than previously thought." (Stockton)
- "Real PCE slowed sharply in the second quarter, and, although we are forecasting a larger gain in the current quarter, the projected increase in spending remains well below that seen late last year and early this year." (Stockton)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Growth in consumer spending had slowed from the outsized gains seen earlier."
- "Business spending on equipment and software had continued to surge."
- "Total private housing starts fell further in June and July."

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Scores: DT 53 | DM 58 | ST 60 | SM 58**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- "Financial conditions have in fact eased a little since our last meeting. Real interest rates fell...real interest rates for investment-grade borrowers appear to be up only about 1 percentage point since May 1999." (Ferguson)
- Bankers "have really tightened conditions by demanding more equity." (Jordan/McTeer, paraphrased)
- "Amazingly consistent report from our larger banks of a falloff in loan demand across the region." (Guynn)

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "Real interest rates for private borrowers were still at relatively elevated levels."
- "Banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans."
- "The recent rally in domestic financial markets could be viewed as having partially eroded the degree of monetary restraint."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "Tighter monetary policy appears to be leaving its clearest mark on housing activity. Starts of single-family homes have trended down since the beginning of the year." (Stockton)
- "Mortgage rates are up considerably from their lows and that ARM rates also are significantly higher than they have been." (Stockton, Q&A)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Other interest rates generally moved lower over the period."
- Mortgage rates "well above their average for last year."
- "Banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans."

### PRODUCTIVITY
**Scores: DT 82 (optional) | DM 75 | ST 74 | SM 70**

**DT (Discussion Transcript):**
- Multiple participants note extraordinary productivity growth; Greenspan references "phenomenal" productivity.
- Meyer: "Core CPI inflation today is exactly the same as it was at the end of 1995 on a methodologically consistent basis" — attributable to productivity offsetting cost pressures.

**DM (Discussion Minutes):**
- "An apparent continued acceleration in underlying productivity was boosting the economy's potential output growth."
- "The widespread application of technological advances and the associated surge in outlays for capital equipment had been fostering an acceleration in labor productivity that seemed to be ongoing."

**ST (Staff Transcript):**
- "The annual revisions portray a pattern of greater acceleration of actual labor productivity, with the annual increase through the second quarter of this year in excess of 5 percent." (Stockton)
- Staff raised structural productivity growth to "3-1/2 percent this year and 3-3/4 percent in 2001, up about 1/2 percentage point from our last forecast." (Stockton)

**SM (Staff Minutes):**
- "Productivity also continuing to accelerate."
- "Unit labor costs had changed little" despite compensation acceleration — implying strong productivity.
- Staff forecast: "upwardly revised estimate of the economy's potential output."

## Cross-Source Observations

**Points of agreement across all four sources:**
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: All four sources independently converge on 69-73, with explicit "extremely tight" or "exceedingly tight" language in every source. This is the most consistently scored dimension.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE / HAWKISH_LEAN: Both discussion sources score identically (58/62 on lean/guidance). Unanimous hold with inflation risk bias is unambiguous.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Both discussion sources score 12. All-member unanimity on both the decision and risk assessment language.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: All four sources independently arrive at 52, reflecting anchored expectations with minor upside risk from energy.
- PRODUCTIVITY: All four sources place this in the 70-82 range, with explicit "acceleration" language in every source.

**Points of divergence:**
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: DT scores this lowest (52) reflecting heavier weight on consumer/housing weakness; the other three sources weight the investment boom more heavily (55-58). The transcripts show Greenspan himself explicitly contrasts the two sectors.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: DT scores 53 (Ferguson's emphasis on market-based conditions easing), while DM, ST, and SM converge at 58-60 (emphasizing elevated real rates and bank lending tightening). DT captures intra-meeting heterogeneity not visible in the synthesized sources.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: ST (36) and SM (53) diverge by 17 points — the largest cross-source gap. ST (Fisher's testimony) has direct corporate yield curve data showing spreads compressing; SM lacks direct spread data and infers from bank lending standards alone (low confidence flagged).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: ST (67) vs SM (57) — a 10-point gap. ST reflects Fisher's detailed euro/dollar analysis and the alternative scenario run by Johnson; SM captures the summary appreciation language only.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: DT (20) vs DM (30). The minutes consolidate scattered mentions into more prominent discussion; the transcript shows these were brief and peripheral.
