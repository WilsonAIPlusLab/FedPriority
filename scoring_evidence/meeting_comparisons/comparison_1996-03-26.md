# FOMC Meeting 1996-03-26: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 52 +/-7, High confidence)

> "What I find most striking about the current situation is the consistency of many inflation indicators. They all seem to be pointing toward core CPI inflation remaining around 3 percent." — MR. PARRY

> "We are more than five years into an economic expansion. We have seen little if any acceleration in inflation." — MR. BOEHNE

> "some recent data are worrisome, including the employment cost index for total compensation...the long-bond yield...also has increased more than 50 basis points since the last meeting." — MR. MELZER

Center of gravity: Most participants see inflation as stable around 3% CPI — slightly above comfort — with no acceleration but modest upside risks from tight labor markets and the ECI jump. Melzer sees inflation expectations at an unacceptable 3% and wants active disinflation; Guynn is optimistic inflation could moderate to 2.5%.

**Minutes** (Score: 47 +/-8, High confidence)

> "Members' assessments tended to center on expectations of little change in average consumer price inflation over the projection horizon."

> "Their expectations ranged from essentially unchanged to slightly higher inflation in comparison with 1995."

> "Those who emphasized the risks of higher-than-projected inflation tended to cite the potential for increasing wage and price pressures in an economy that already was operating at or close to its estimated capacity."

> "Other members saw only a limited risk of higher inflation, and a few indicated that they did not rule out some reduction in consumer price inflation."

Center of gravity: Members see inflation as essentially stable and contained, with asymmetric upside risk. The minutes framing is somewhat more sanguine than the transcript, placing the score just below the midpoint rather than above it.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 51 +/-5, High confidence)

> "both the unemployment and the capacity utilization rates are near common estimates of their natural rates" — MR. PARRY

> "the economy has been operating for a while now somewhere in the neighborhood of nonaccelerating inflation capacity" — MR. BROADDUS

> "the output gap was about nil prior to 1995 and...some slack has built up in the aggregate economy" — MR. MOSKOW

Center of gravity: Participants describe the economy as operating at or very near potential. Parry thinks unemployment and capacity utilization are slightly on the low side of natural rates (slight above-potential lean); Moskow thinks modest slack may have built up. Range is narrow.

**Minutes** (Score: 50 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Members continued to view trend growth at a pace near the economy's potential as the most probable outcome."

> "An economy that already was operating at or close to its estimated capacity."

> "Sufficient capacity in the economy to allow room for moderate growth."

Center of gravity: Economy operating right at potential. The minutes language is a close match to the transcript, with both sources converging on near-potential language.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 57 +/-6, High confidence)

> "The anecdotal comments around the table universally have referred to tight labor markets...In Indianapolis...'you can't hire people at any wage.'" — MR. LINDSEY

> "Labor markets remain very tight. There are a few more, but still scattered, indications of increasing wage pressures." — MR. STERN

> "labor markets appear to continue to get tighter. More of our directors are reporting tight labor markets for entry-level and some skilled jobs." — MR. HOENIG

Center of gravity: Virtually every district reports tight labor markets. Wage pressures remain surprisingly subdued, attributed to "job insecurity" effects. Tightness is real but not yet translating into wage acceleration.

**Minutes** (Score: 56 +/-6, High confidence)

> "Relatively low unemployment nationwide and widespread anecdotal reports of labor shortages."

> "Increases in labor costs had been unusually subdued in light of the relatively low unemployment."

> "Only scattered evidence of accelerating increases in worker compensation associated with labor shortages."

Center of gravity: Labor market moderately tight — low unemployment, widespread shortages reported — but wage pressures have not materialized. Both sources converge closely on this assessment.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 55 +/-6, High confidence)

> "most of the other recent monthly reports on industrial production, car sales, and other retail sales are stronger than we had anticipated" — MR. BROADDUS

> "The consumer, while somewhat stretched in terms of debt, still appears to have ample purchasing power for sustainable spending growth. And the strengthening in residential construction reflects positive consumer sentiment." — MR. BOEHNE

> business fixed investment "pretty unlikely...continue at the same pace as in 1994 and 1995" but "the cost of capital is still fairly low" and "profits and cash flows should be reasonably strong." — MS. PHILLIPS

Center of gravity: Consumer spending resilient, housing strong across most districts. Business fixed investment moderating from high levels but still solid. February rebound was broad-based. Overall moderately above trend.

**Minutes** (Score: 55 +/-6, High confidence)

> "Appreciable strengthening in retail sales that tended to support forecasts of sustained growth in consumer spending."

> "The prospects for business capital spending remained a supportive element...but growth in such spending was expected to slow considerably from its rapid pace over the past few years."

> "Recent data were indicative of considerable underlying strength in housing markets."

Center of gravity: Consumer spending strengthening, investment moderating from high levels, housing strong. Overall modestly above trend. Transcript and minutes scores are identical at 55.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 48 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "the really spectacular rise in both intermediate- and long-term interest rates since the beginning of the year including...the extraordinary 25 basis point jump in the 30-year bond rate" — MR. BROADDUS

> "loans are readily available at our banks to both commercial and other borrowers" — MR. HOENIG

> "plenty of credit availability. The banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion." — MS. PHILLIPS

Center of gravity: Long-term rates have risen sharply (50+ bps since the last meeting), representing some tightening of conditions. However, credit remains freely available and bank lending is strong. Net conditions are roughly neutral.

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "Financial conditions had tightened somewhat as a consequence of the recent rise in intermediate- and long-term interest rates."

> "Relatively low long-term interest rates and the related substantial appreciation in the value of stock and bond market holdings had been important factors helping to sustain spending."

> "The ready availability and fairly low cost of business finance in equity and debt markets."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat accommodative overall — credit available, equity wealth supportive — but have tightened at the margin due to the long-rate backup. The minutes score (40) indicates a more accommodative reading than the transcript (48), reflecting different emphasis on the balance between ample credit and rising long rates.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 55 +/-5, High confidence)

> "further easing would be inappropriate, given the increasing strength of the economy...If economic activity continues to pick up, it might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate." — MR. PARRY

> "we have to contemplate the possibility that we may need to reverse the move we made last month at some point in the not too distant future" — MR. BROADDUS

> "I would subscribe...to doing nothing today...an obvious symmetry." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Unanimous support for holding at 5.25% with symmetric directive. The lean is mildly hawkish — several participants signal the next move is more likely a tightening, and no one advocates further easing. Melzer advocates a more restrictive stance.

**Minutes** (Score: 52 +/-4, High confidence)

> "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure in reserve markets."

> "The economy seemed to have adequate forward momentum and did not appear to require any further stimulus."

> "Information on the economy and prices did not seem to indicate developing inflation pressures that needed to be contained by tightening policy at this juncture."

> "A number of members noted that inflation was not expected to moderate further...and that it could move higher and the Committee would need to be particularly vigilant."

Center of gravity: Firmly on hold with symmetric directive. Slight hawkish lean from the inflation vigilance language and explicit statement that no further stimulus is needed. The minutes score (52) is slightly more neutral than the transcript (55), reflecting the minutes' emphasis on the symmetric directive over the individual hawkish voices.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 18 +/-5, High confidence)

> All participants explicitly agree with the Chairman's recommendation to hold with symmetric directive.

> MR. MELZER expresses concern that "the current policy stance is inappropriate given the 3 percent inflation expectations" but agrees with holding for now.

> No formal dissents.

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on holding with symmetric bias. Minor differences in emphasis about inflation risks and the wisdom of the prior easing, but no real disagreement on the current action. Melzer's critique represents the strongest dissenting voice.

**Minutes** (Score: 18 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Votes against this action: None."

> "Members did not differ greatly in their assessments of the most probable course of inflation."

> "Members expressed somewhat differing views about possible deviations of inflation from their expectations."

Center of gravity: Near-consensus with zero dissents. Minor emphasis differences on inflation risk direction, but these did not affect the policy conclusion. Both sources converge exactly at 18.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript** (Score: 25 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "The market is probably high by any objective measure that we can find...If we get a very significant contraction in stock prices, I think it will have a quite measurable wealth effect." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I am particularly concerned at this stage about the possibility of a significant stock market correction. The current level of stock prices is not impossible, but it is increasingly difficult to justify in terms of fundamentals." — MS. YELLEN

> "the level of prices in capital markets...a booming capital market has made equity capital very cheap" — MR. LINDSEY

Center of gravity: Greenspan, Yellen, and Lindsey devote meaningful discussion to stock market valuations and potential correction risks. Most other participants do not focus on financial stability. Moderate attention from a few key voices.

**Minutes** (Score: 15 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> "Stock market prices had risen to comparatively high levels in relation to earnings and interest rates and might be vulnerable to further weakness in the debt markets."

> Brief mention of consumer indebtedness as a risk factor.

Center of gravity: Financial stability receives only passing mention in the minutes. The stock valuation comment is notable but brief and does not constitute sustained discussion. The minutes score (15) is lower than the transcript (25), consistent with the minutes compressing the more extended individual commentary from Greenspan, Yellen, and Lindsey.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 55 +/-6, High confidence)

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Symmetric directive, "doing nothing today"

> "I would hope that we might have a conference call" if March data show continued strength, to consider reversing course — MR. BROADDUS

> "I can well imagine that our next move could be either up or down" — MS. PHILLIPS

Center of gravity: Forward signal is neutral on paper (symmetric directive) but the discussion lean is mildly hawkish, with multiple participants flagging that the next move is more likely a tightening if data continue strong. No one suggests further easing.

**Minutes** (Score: 52 +/-5, High confidence)

> "The members favored an unbiased instruction in the directive that did not prejudge possible intermeeting adjustments to policy in either direction."

> "Slightly greater reserve restraint or slightly lesser reserve restraint would be acceptable."

> "The Committee would need to be particularly vigilant in guarding against" inflation moving higher.

Center of gravity: Officially symmetric forward guidance with a very slight hawkish tilt from the vigilance language. The minutes score (52) is modestly more neutral than the transcript (55), reflecting the minutes' emphasis on the explicit symmetric directive language over the individual hawkish signals.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 38 +/-7, Moderate confidence)

> "unusual circumstances of late 1995 and early 1996 certainly have muddied the waters considerably and left us with more uncertainty than would usually be the case" — MS. PHILLIPS

> "we probably are going to be surprised by more rather than less volatility in the economy. I do not know in which direction the economy will be going." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "the unusual circumstances...have muddied the waters considerably" — MR. GUYNN

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated due to weather disruptions, government shutdowns, and data revisions making the recent past hard to read. But most participants feel the underlying outlook is reasonably clear — moderate growth near potential. Not extreme uncertainty.

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-6, High confidence)

> "Substantial uncertainties attended the economic outlook."

> "A number of members observed that an economic performance that differed considerably in either direction from their current forecasts might well materialize over the projection period."

> "Considerable volatility could be expected in the short-run performance of the economy."

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated — members explicitly use "substantial uncertainties" and flag two-sided risks. Both sources score close together (38 vs. 40), with the minutes language slightly more emphatic ("substantial uncertainties").
