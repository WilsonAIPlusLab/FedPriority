### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "The inflation outlook remains subdued. I see little evidence that inflation is, indeed, trending upward over the medium term... the staff now projects that inflation will remain below target until early in the next decade." — Kocherlakota

> "Several of you emphasized that inflation has been running well below our 2 percent objective and noted that there hasn't been any significant progress in closing the shortfall. On a 12-month basis, both total and core PCE inflation are running at about 1½ percent." — Yellen (summary)

> "The low current inflation rate and the disinflationary pressures we are currently experiencing indicate we may not make much progress toward our inflation goal in the near term." — Rosengren

**Center of gravity (Transcript):** Essentially all participants acknowledge inflation at ~1.5% PCE, well below target, with near-term further decline expected from energy prices and dollar appreciation. Majority expects gradual return to 2%; the most dovish voices (Kocherlakota, Evans) express concern the trend is deteriorating. Score of 36 reflects the 2013–19 below-target anchor, adjusted down for the specifically acute disinflationary pressures at this meeting. Minority views: Lacker, Plosser, Williams, Mester argue inflation will return to target as slack diminishes; Fisher notes trimmed mean stable at 1.65%.

---

**Minutes — Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Inflation was continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective."

> "Most participants anticipated that inflation was likely to edge lower in the near term, reflecting the decline in oil and other commodity prices and lower import prices."

> "A few expressed concern that inflation might persist below the Committee's objective for quite some time."

**Center of gravity (Minutes):** Inflation clearly below the 2% target and expected to decline further near-term before returning to target over the medium term. Consistent with the 2013–19 "below target" anchor range. No participants raised upside inflation concerns; band slightly wider on downside.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 44 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "I believe that... appreciable slack still remains in the labor market, so we do have some way to go before reaching our goal of maximum employment." — Yellen (summary)

> "I remain upbeat about the prospects for growth... real GDP growth looks to be coming in around a solid 3 percent for the second half of this year and going into next year." — Williams

> "Most of us appear to anticipate economic growth running near 3 percent in the second half of the year." — Yellen (summary)

**Center of gravity (Transcript):** Economy recovering at a solid pace with GDP near 3%, above trend, and labor market improving. Yet "appreciable slack still remains" — the output gap is negative but closing. Consistent with the 35–42 recovery range, on the upper end at 44 given the strong growth pace. Brainard emphasizes downside risks and continued slack; Tarullo is less sanguine about international spillovers.
