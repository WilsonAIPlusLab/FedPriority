## Part A: Discussion — Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### A1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 52 ±8 [High] | Minutes: 47 ±8 [High] | Gap: 5**

**Transcript (center-of-gravity):** Participants see actual inflation as still low but perceive the tide turning — risks have shifted clearly to the upside with multiple early signs of building pressure.

| Transcript Quotes | Minutes Quotes |
|---|---|
| Greenspan: "the three-month average for the core PCE is a rise at an annual rate of 1.1 percent. This is not evidence that somehow we are far behind the curve." | "current indicators continued on the whole to point to quiescent wage and price behavior" |
| Meyer: "Inflation, after falling throughout the last few years, is stabilizing in the near term... and is poised to move higher going forward." | "the firming of oil and other commodity prices... could be read as suggesting at least that the trend toward lower inflation was coming to an end and perhaps also as harbingers of a less favorable inflation performance going forward" |
| Broaddus: April CPI "offers the first direct evidence in some time that inflation pressures may be building." | "members were concerned that if recent developments continued... inflation was more likely to rise over time" |
| [McTeer: more sanguine — "less and less discussion of upward pressure on wages" in his district.] | "Several members commented in particular that substantial weight should not be attached to the one-month jump in the just-released CPI data" |

**Minutes (center-of-gravity):** Inflation is currently well-contained, but members see a clear shift in risk toward rising inflation. The balance tips just below neutral — current conditions are benign but trajectory concern pulls the score close to 50.

## Notable Gaps and Observations

1. **DISSENT_INTENSITY (gap: 26)** — The largest discussion-track gap. The transcript records approximately 5 participants explicitly preferring an immediate 25bp rate hike over the majority's preference for a tilt only. The minutes record the unanimous final outcome. This structural gap is expected: minutes suppress the discussion-phase disagreement by design.

2. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (gap: 14)** — The minutes aggregate and emphasize uncertainty language ("substantial," "considerable," "wide range") across participants. The transcript records both highly confident voices (Broaddus, Jordan) and more uncertain ones (Parry, Poole), with the confident voices appearing to dominate the tone.

3. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH (gap: 18 in staff)** — Fisher's transcript presentation emphasizes global dollar strength with NAFTA exceptions. The minutes staff data section leads with the trade-weighted depreciation figure, reversing the directional read. Both are factually accurate descriptions of the same underlying data; the difference is framing and metric choice.

4. **EQUITY_VALUATION (gap: 18 in staff)** — Prell in the transcript explicitly frames high equity prices as a problem sustaining unsustainable demand, communicating a clear overvaluation concern. The minutes record equity gains factually without conveying this concern to the same degree.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (discussion gap: 5)** — The transcript slightly exceeds the minutes. In the transcript, individual participants express more urgent inflation concern (Broaddus calls the April CPI "the first direct evidence in some time"). The minutes characterize the situation as "quiescent" with a one-month spike, which several members cautioned against over-reading.

6. **Strong convergence** across OUTPUT_GAP (0), LABOR_TIGHTNESS (2-3), CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (1-4), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS/TIGHTNESS (2), and TREASURY_STRESS (0) — both within the discussion track and between staff sources. The macroeconomic conditions assessment is essentially identical across all four sources.
