### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Headline inflation is meaningfully elevated by commodity shocks and broadly acknowledged, but the majority of participants still regard core/underlying inflation as low with limited pass-through expected — placing the center modestly above 50 but not dramatically so.

Minority views: Fisher, Plosser, and Lacker argue pass-through risks are underappreciated, pushing the upper bound to ~66.

Representative quotes:
- Bullard: "Since we embarked on QE2, many measures of inflation have bottomed out and turned higher...according to the recent TIPS market, this Committee is set to miss its implicit inflation target of 2 percent or so on the high side at any horizon."
- Plosser: "prices-paid and prices-received indexes in our manufacturing survey hit their highest levels in three years...I see inflation risks as clearly to the upside."
- Pianalto: "The recent surge in oil prices has heightened concerns about inflation...I have shifted the balance of risks on inflation to the upside."
- Evans/Williams/Yellen (counterweight): core PCE ~0.8–1%, limited pass-through expected, "transient" effects, underlying inflation "subdued."

**Minutes score: 47 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see underlying inflation as subdued but bottoming, with headline boosted by commodities. The net assessment is slightly below the 50 midpoint, with upside risk from commodity pass-through.

Minority views: "A few" worried about inflation expectations becoming unanchored if balance sheet size raises doubts; some debated whether slack meaningfully restrains inflation, citing the 2003–04 episode.

Representative quotes:
- "Measures of underlying inflation remained subdued" and "core inflation and other measures of underlying inflation remained subdued, though they appeared to have bottomed out."
- "A number of business contacts indicated that they were passing on at least a portion of these higher costs to their customers."
- "Several of them indicated, in light of recent developments, that the risks to their forecasts of inflation had shifted somewhat to the upside."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is below potential but recovering — a gap clearly persists, with broad agreement that slack remains substantial despite improvement since the 2009 trough. Consistent with the 35–42 range for 2010–14 recovery period.

Minority views: Plosser argues growth is "above trend" going forward but acknowledges current gap; this informs the upper bound (~43).

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "I continue to believe that there still is substantial slack in labor and most product markets."
- Williams: "given the significant amount of slack in the economy today, I expect core inflation to remain low."
- Yellen: "there is an awfully long road to travel to attain our maximum employment objective."
- Bernanke summary: "Indicators continue to point to a strengthening though moderate recovery in 2011. Private-sector demand may be leading to self-sustaining growth."

**Minutes score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy recovering but clearly below potential, with substantial slack acknowledged broadly.

Minority views: Narrow range of views — all agree slack is substantial. Uncertainty about degree of slack noted.

Representative quotes:
- "The economic recovery was on a firmer footing" and "the recovery was gaining traction."
- "Significant slack in resource utilization" and "there was still substantial slack in the labor market."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 8.9%, well above NAIRU; broad agreement that labor market is still loose with meaningful slack. Multiple participants debate whether unemployment decline reflects real improvement or labor force exits. Consistent with anchor 35–40 for 2011–14.

Minority views: Kocherlakota argues slack may be smaller than thought; Fisher/Texas boasts regional tightness — both inform the upper bound (~41).

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "I don't think we're looking yet at a vibrant labor market with robust hiring. Layoffs are down, but hiring has yet to pick up in a meaningful way."
- Yellen: "I am skeptical that conditions have improved and slack has diminished by quite as much as Tealbook assumes...job gains since employment stopped falling have been modest."
- Kocherlakota: "The question for us that's important in terms of slack is: Should we expect those people to return to the labor force?" (U-5 uncertainty).
- Bernanke: unemployment fell but "in part this improvement reflects fewer layoffs and exits from the labor force rather than new hiring."

**Minutes score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Substantial labor market slack despite declining unemployment rate. The unemployment decline overstates improvement.

Minority views: Scattered skilled-worker shortages noted in some regions, but these are isolated and do not shift the center of gravity.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants generally judged that there was still substantial slack in the labor market."
- "Several participants noted that the drop in unemployment was attributable more to people withdrawing from the labor force and to fewer layoffs than to increased hiring."
- "Scattered reports indicated that firms in some regions were having difficulty hiring some types of highly skilled workers."

**Transcript score: 43 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Manufacturing and capital equipment strong, auto sales strong, but consumer spending cautious and housing deeply depressed. Net position is modestly below trend — below the 46–54 "at trend" range.

Minority views: Plosser and Evans are more optimistic about overall trajectory (~50), while Raskin/Yellen flag housing and consumer concerns (~36).

Representative quotes:
- Lacker: "household spending continues to show, I think, decent momentum despite horrendous weather and a sharp climb in energy prices. Business investment in equipment and software continues to show strength."
- Williams: "consumers appear very cautious about buying certain big-ticket items, such as furniture and appliances, in part because rising gasoline prices are draining their purchasing power."
- Yellen: "signals pertaining to consumer spending have been harder to read, but on balance, they too point to at least moderate growth in line with disposable income."
- Raskin: "The rising cost of food and fuel, especially if these factors continue to rise, together with the effect of suboptimal job creation, could hinder or even derail growth in consumer demand."

**Minutes score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate expansion with business investment firming, consumer spending growing but below expectations, and housing depressed. Below trend but improving.

Minority views: Business contacts more optimistic than macro data; consumer side weighed down by gasoline prices and housing weakness.

Representative quotes:
- "Spending by households...rose further during the early part of 2011, with auto sales showing particular strength."
- "Some participants noted that growth in consumer spending so far this year had not been as vigorous as they had anticipated."
- "Manufacturing firms were particularly upbeat. Some contacts reported they were increasing capital budgets."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Overall financial conditions are loose to neutral. Accommodation is still substantial, credit is flowing in most segments, and Fisher's financial stability concerns are about excessive looseness/risk-taking, not tightness. Residential real estate is the main pocket of tightness. Score of 38 reflects somewhat loose to neutral conditions.

Minority views: Pockets of tightness in small business lending and residential RE push toward 45.

Representative quotes:
- Duke: "continued gradual improvement in credit quality and weak loan demand as universal themes...the business credit market appears to me to be ready to fund demand by businesses whenever it materializes."
- Bernanke summary: "credit conditions have improved in some areas, including autos, student loans, and C&I."
- Fisher: "signs of the intoxicating ambrosia of cheap money and readily available money...surge in covenant-lite loans" (signaling loose, not tight conditions).
- Duke: "Everything related to residential real estate...is mired deep in the mud." (Pockets of tightness)

**Minutes score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Mixed conditions — loosening for large firms and consumer credit, still tight for small business and CRE. Net slightly below neutral (slightly loose side given the easing trend).

Minority views: A few concerned about excessive easing in some sectors, widening the band.

Representative quotes:
- "Credit conditions remained uneven. ...Large firms...continued to find credit...available on relatively attractive terms; however, credit conditions reportedly remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms."
- "Terms and conditions for auto loans had returned to historical norms."
- "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions in some sectors was becoming or might become excessive."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 28 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish majority favoring Alternative B — completing QE2 at full scale, maintaining extended period language, no tapering. Hawkish minority (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher) expresses reservations but accepts B for this meeting. This is firmly in the QE-era dovish range (15–25 anchor), nudged slightly higher (~28) by the meaningful hawkish minority and exit planning discussions.

Minority views: Plosser ("we will need to tighten policy considerably sooner"), Lacker ("attracted to alternative C"), Fisher (wants to end QE2 and begin exit) push toward 40–45.

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "I expect to support completing our $600 billion of purchases until we're finished in June...I have a preference for no tapering."
- Williams: "We're still falling well short on both parts of our dual mandate...the current very accommodative stance of monetary policy remains entirely appropriate."
- Yellen: "I favor completing our announced program of Treasury purchases and maintaining the 'extended period' language."
- Dudley: "There is no question in my mind at this point that we should complete our $600 billion Treasury purchase program."

**Minutes score: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Firmly in the accommodative/dovish camp — continuing QE2, maintaining near-zero rates. But the center is beginning to shift as exit planning is discussed, placing this at the upper end of the QE-era dovish range.

Minority views: "A few" on each side — some wanting tightening this year, some wanting accommodation beyond 2011.

Representative quotes:
- "Committee members agreed that no changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted at this meeting."
