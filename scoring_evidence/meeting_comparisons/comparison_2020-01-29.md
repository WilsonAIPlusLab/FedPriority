### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 37 ±5 \| High confidence | 40 ±5 \| High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "for more than 10 years into the expansion, it remains below our target, with core PCE inflation still running at 1.6 percent on a 12-month basis." (Kashkari, policy go-round)
- "At present, and as has been the case for most of the past eight years, PCE inflation is running somewhat below our objective." (Clarida, outlook go-round)
- "Inflation remains below 2 percent, and both survey- and market-based measures suggest inflation expectations are running somewhat below our target." (Brainard, policy go-round)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Nearly all participants agree inflation is persistently below the 2% target (core PCE ~1.6%), with expectations also somewhat below target, placing the meeting firmly in the 31-40 "below target" range.

**Transcript minority views:** Bostic and Quarles argue that broader measures (Dallas trimmed mean, Cleveland trimmed CPI) show inflation near or at 2%, suggesting true underlying inflation may be closer to target.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "recent readings on overall and core PCE price inflation, measured on a 12-month basis, had continued to run below 2 percent"
- "Participants generally expected inflation to move closer to 2 percent in the coming months" but "a few participants expressed less confidence in this outlook"
- "a couple of participants...noted that some alternative inflation indicators, including trimmed mean measures, suggested that there had been a modest step-up in underlying inflation during 2019"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation running below target but expected to gradually converge; not alarming but persistently below objective. Consistent with the 2018–2019 "below target" anchor range (38–42).

**Minutes minority views:** A few see less confidence inflation will return to 2%; a couple see trimmed mean already near target.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 53 ±5 \| High confidence | 52 ±5 \| High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "real GDP growth at 2.3 percent is above most estimates of trend… one-tenth above my estimate of the trend rate." (Clarida)
- "real GDP is still growing above its potential rate." (Rosengren)
- "Moderate growth overall… The labor market continues to be strong." (Powell, summary)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Most participants describe growth as at or slightly above trend, unemployment well below NAIRU, suggesting the economy is modestly above potential — consistent with a score just above 50.

**Transcript minority views:** Kashkari explicitly disputes the positive output gap: "I don't see any evidence that the economy is operating above potential," based on tepid wage growth.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "economic activity had risen at a moderate rate"
- Staff forecast: output expanding "at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth"
- "business fixed investment and exports had remained weak" offset by strong labor market and solid consumer spending

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy near potential, perhaps modestly above. Growth is moderate, labor market is strong, but business investment is weak. Net assessment: approximately at potential with a slight lean above.

**Minutes minority views:** A few think employment is still below maximum, implying output gap could be near zero or slightly negative.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 66 ±5 \| High confidence | 60 ±6 \| High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "Labor market remains strong… the unemployment rate stood at 3½ percent in December… District contacts view the labor market as tight. Many contacts continue to report that it's difficult to find the employees they need." (Mester)
- "The unemployment rate is at a 50-year low." (Clarida)
- "The labor market is still remarkably strong and the pace of job creation has remained healthy." (Bowman)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Virtually all participants characterize the labor market as tight or very tight, with unemployment at a 50-year low (3.5%), placing the score in the 65–70 range.

**Transcript minority views:** Daly argues structural declines in u* mean the labor market is not as tight as it appears; Kashkari believes maximum employment has not been reached.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "unemployment rate at a 50-year low and continued solid job gains"
- "contacts reported difficulties in finding qualified workers or that observed wage growth might currently understate the degree of tightness"
- "aggregate measures of nominal wages continued to rise at a moderate pace broadly in line with productivity growth" — moderating the tightness signal
- "many participants pointed to the strong performance of labor force participation" and "possibility that there was some room for labor force participation to rise further"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor market clearly tight — historically low unemployment, reports of worker shortages — but wage growth is moderate and participation is strong, tempering the degree of tightness. Scores well above 50 but not in the "severe shortages" range.

**Minutes minority views:** Several note absence of strong wage pressures; some suggest labor market may not be as tight as unemployment rate implies. A couple worry tight labor markets could lead to imbalances.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 48 ±6 \| High confidence | 47 ±6 \| High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**

- "The mix continues to be of strong consumer spending and weak investment." (Mester)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending is solid-to-moderate; business investment is weak; housing is rebounding. Net effect is near-trend overall spending and investment, producing a score just below 50.

**Transcript minority views:** Barkin is somewhat more optimistic about consumer fundamentals and a potential business investment rebound; Kashkari notes domestic demand "looks pretty weak."

- "business investment and exports remained weak and that manufacturing output had declined over the past year"
- "many participants were encouraged by the significant pickup since last summer in residential investment"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending is moderate/solid, residential investment is improving, but business fixed investment and exports are weak. The net position is close to trend — consumer strength roughly offsets business weakness. Slightly below trend due to the weak investment drag.

**Minutes minority views:** Some cautiously optimistic that trade developments will boost business confidence; several skeptical the trade deal will have much effect.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 43 ±5 \| High confidence | 48 ±4 \| High confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- All 16 participants support Alternative B (hold rates unchanged at 1.5–1.75%).
- "I do believe that our current target range for the funds rate is delivering a somewhat accommodative policy." (Clarida)
- "I prefer alternative A in this meeting but will support alternative B" — signaling a preference for even more dovish commitment language. (Kashkari)
- No participant advocates for rate hike; Bullard notes market may be pricing in further easing.

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unanimous hold with an explicit dovish lean — policy described as "accommodative," willingness to tolerate overshoot, high bar for rate hike, Kashkari preferring even more dovish guidance. Score near 43 (lean dovish to balanced).

**Transcript minority views:** Rosengren and George note upside risks from financial imbalances and question whether enough "insurance" has been provided — a mild relative-hawk lean within a generally dovish consensus.

---

**Minutes evidence:**
- "participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 1½ to 1¾ percent"
- "current stance of policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time, provided that incoming information about the economy remained broadly consistent with this economic outlook"
- "Several participants suggested that inflation modestly exceeding 2 percent for a period would be consistent with the achievement of the Committee's longer-run inflation objective"
- "a few participants observed that the actual level of employment might still be below maximum employment"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Firm hold with no interest in tightening and a mild dovish lean via inflation-overshoot tolerance and sense that there may be room to run. Not actively dovish — no one is calling for cuts — but the lean is slightly below balanced.

**Minutes minority views:** "A couple of other participants expressed concern that tight labor markets have in the past been associated with economic and financial imbalances" — a hawkish minority voice.

**Transcript evidence:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is essentially neutral — hold for now, data-dependent. The slight dovish lean comes from the inflation-overshoot tolerance language, but there is no explicit easing signal. Neutral with a mild dovish tinge.

**Minutes minority views:** No explicit tightening signals from any participants in the forward-looking discussion.
