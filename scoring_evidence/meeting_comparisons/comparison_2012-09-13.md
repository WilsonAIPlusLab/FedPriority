### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The vast majority perceive inflation as running below the 2% target but not in deflation territory, with inflation expectations anchored; a small minority sees modest upside pressure.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "Inflation is quite low, and unemployment is quite high."
- Fisher: "everybody agrees at the table that inflation is not an immediate or presently foreseeable threat."
- Pianalto: "deflation is not a serious risk" (implying neither is inflation).
- Kocherlakota (minority): sees "modestly more inflationary pressures than what are in this forecast, maybe one-tenth or two-tenths or three-tenths more."
- George (minority): raises concern that another LSAP "could push longer-term expectations above a level consistent with our objectives."

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is subdued and expected to stay at or below the 2% target. The broad consensus is that slack is keeping prices in check.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer price inflation remained subdued despite recent increases in the prices of some key commodities."
- "Participants generally anticipated that inflation over the medium run would likely run at or below the 2 percent rate."
- "Most participants saw the risks to the outlook for inflation as roughly balanced."
- Minority: "A few participants felt that maintaining a highly accommodative stance...could unmoor longer-term inflation expectations and...posed upside risks to inflation."

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy is operating clearly below potential with persistent slack — consistent with 2010–14 recovery anchor of 35–42. Placed at lower end given sluggish 2% growth path and August payroll disappointment.

Representative quotes:
- Lacker: "We're in a period of sluggish economic growth, clearly... GDP growth has averaged around 2 percent since the recovery began."
- Plosser: "The recovery is frustratingly slow. The unemployment rate is uncomfortably and tragically high."
- Bernanke: "very serious problems with unemployment are not improving."
- Lacker (methodological note): challenges whether the "relevant gap is quite large," but this is not a claim the economy is near potential.

**Minutes — Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is growing moderately but clearly below potential with persistent headwinds. Significant downside risks noted.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace in recent months."
- "The pace of the economic recovery would likely remain moderate over coming quarters."
- "They saw slack in resource utilization as remaining wide, indicating an important role for additional policy accommodation."
- Minority: "A few participants...mentioned the possibility that economic growth could be more rapid than currently anticipated." A few argued structural factors reduce the effective output gap.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is uniformly characterized as weak, with 8%+ unemployment, disappointing payrolls, and stalled progress — mapping to the 35–40 anchor range for 2011–14; placed at the lower end given the specific August disappointment.

Representative quotes:
- Pianalto: "With the unemployment rate at 8.1 percent and the August employment report as disappointing as it was."
- Rosengren: "most of our forecasts for the past nine months have overestimated how quickly we would see improvements in either element of our mandate."
- Williams: "progress toward our policy goals would remain stalled."
- No participant argues the labor market is tight or approaching full employment.

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is clearly slack with high unemployment, disappointing job growth, and declining participation. Well below full employment.

Representative quotes:
- "The available indicators pointed to continued weakness in overall labor market conditions."
- "Growth in employment had been disappointing, with the average monthly increases in payrolls so far this year below last year's pace."
- "The labor force participation rate and employment-to-population ratios were at or near post-recession lows."
- Minority: "A few participants reiterated their view that the persistently high level of unemployment reflected the effect of structural factors."

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and business investment are broadly sluggish/below trend; housing is a modest positive but does not offset overall weakness.

Representative quotes:
- Lacker: "the tenor of our anecdotal reports has been middling of late."
- Bullard: "We are looking at economic data that are middling."
- Fisher (on business fixed investment): "9 out of 10 [CEOs] was, you could cut the cost of borrowing by 1 full percentage point and we'll use it to buy back stock" rather than job-creating capex.
- Williams (positive note): housing is "just beginning to recover."

**Minutes — Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Below-trend spending and investment. Consumer spending slightly better than expected but investment slowing and business caution pervasive. Housing improving from depressed levels.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer spending had continued to advance" but "households were still in the process of deleveraging, confidence was low."
- "Growth in business fixed investment appeared to have slowed."
- Firms "remain reluctant to hire or expand capacity."
- Retail sales "somewhat stronger than expected" (modest upside note).

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Domestic financial conditions are generally accommodative and supportive; spreads are compressed, equities are supportive, and credit is available — consistent with the loose-to-neutral range.

Representative quotes:
- Bullard: "financial stress, which is relatively low, at least for now."
- Fisher (on financial markets dashboard): "things obviously are going our way" on high-yield spreads and equity prices.
- Pianalto: "Financial markets are not in disarray."
- Dudley (minority caveat): the European situation is "tenuous" and "still a long way to go."
- George: raises concern about imbalances building in a low-rate environment.

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Broadly accommodative financial conditions with low rates and rising equity prices, but pockets of tightness in credit for some borrowers. Somewhat loose overall.

Representative quotes:
- "Indicators of financial stress in the United States were not especially high and overall conditions in U.S. financial markets remained favorable."
- "Longer-term interest rates were low and supportive of economic growth, while equity prices had risen."
- "Sentiment in financial markets improved notably during the intermeeting period."
- "still-tight credit conditions for some households and businesses" (caveat).
- One participant noted low rates could "ultimately lead to financial imbalances."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 18 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: A strong majority (roughly 12–13 out of 17–19 participants) supports launching QE3 (open-ended MBS purchases at $40B/month). The presence of an open-ended QE debate and launch firmly places this in the 15–20 range per rubric anchors.

Representative quotes (dovish majority):
- Williams: "The case for further action is clear. In the absence of significant additional monetary stimulus, progress toward our policy goals would remain stalled. Such an outcome is simply inconsistent with the notion of appropriate monetary policy."
- Kocherlakota: "additional accommodation is appropriate... act forcefully and to enhance the benefits of our large-scale asset purchases as much as possible."
- Rosengren: "Alternative B embraces the sense of doing what it takes to move the economy at an acceptable pace toward full employment."

Representative quotes (hawkish minority):
- Lacker: "I cannot support a new asset purchase program... Further stimulus at this time is unlikely to improve economic growth much without causing an unwanted increase in inflation."
- Plosser: "not in favor of undertaking a new round of asset purchases today... I am not in favor of B."
- George: "the risks associated with further action carry less precise measurement, but pose significant cost if realized."
- Fisher: "I disagree with this course. I think it is a mistake."

**Minutes — Score: 20 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish lean. The majority favors launching a new large-scale asset purchase program. This is classic QE-era dovishness (15–25 range per rubric).

Representative quotes (dovish majority):
- "Many participants anticipated that such a program [LSAP] would provide support to the economic recovery."
- "Most participants thought these risks could be managed."
- The meeting is actively debating implementing QE3 — additional large-scale asset purchases.

Representative quotes (hawkish minority):
- "A few expressed skepticism that additional policy accommodation could help spur an economy."
- "Several participants reiterated their concern that additional purchases might complicate the Committee's efforts to withdraw monetary policy accommodation."

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent (Lacker), at least 2–3 others strongly opposed (Plosser, George, Fisher), with several reluctant supporters (Pianalto, Duke, Lockhart). Disagreement extends to the fundamental assessment of efficacy, appropriate response to weak data, and communication design (5 vs. 5'). Maps to 46–60 range — notable disagreement with distinct camps.

Representative quotes:
- Lacker formally dissents from the action.
- Plosser: "Obviously, I am not in favor of B" — extensive policy-level objection.
- George: "the data flow since the last meeting was a bit softer than I had expected, but not sufficiently so to warrant a further expansion of our balance sheet."
- Fisher: "I disagree with this course. I think it is a mistake."
- Williams, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Dudley, Yellen: firm supporters. Stein, Powell, Pianalto, Duke, Lockhart: reluctant/cautious supporters.

**Minutes — Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Notable disagreement across multiple dimensions (efficacy of QE, nature of unemployment, inflation risks). The majority prevails but there is a meaningful minority with substantive objections.

Representative quotes:
- Extended debate on LSAP costs/benefits with multiple viewpoints.
- On labor: structural vs. cyclical unemployment debate ("a few" vs. "several").
- "A few expressed skepticism that additional policy accommodation could help spur an economy."
- "Several participants reiterated their concern that additional purchases might complicate the Committee's efforts to withdraw monetary policy accommodation."
- "Some participants thought past purchases were useful...and were less confident of the efficacy of additional purchases under present circumstances."
- "Most participants thought these risks could be managed" (majority view).

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript — Score: 45 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Several participants address financial stability meaningfully, particularly around Europe and the ECB; monitoring of potential domestic imbalances is also discussed. The topic receives moderate attention — not a dominant focus but more than passing mentions.

Representative quotes:
- Vice Chairman Dudley: extensive remarks on the ECB OMT and European sovereign situation.
- George: "I continue to find a lot of value in this" (financial stability monitoring) and raises concern about imbalances in low-rate environment — "the point at which to know when to take signal from some of those things is very difficult."
- Rosengren asks about money market fund vulnerabilities in Europe.
- Other participants treat financial stability as secondary to the immediate LSAP/forward guidance decision.

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are discussed as context for the economic outlook and policy debate, but financial stability per se is not a prominent focus. A couple of individual participants flag stability concerns.

Representative quotes:
- "Sentiment in financial markets improved notably during the intermeeting period."
- Discussion of ECB actions and euro-area risks.
- "One participant noted that...low interest rates could ultimately lead to financial imbalances."
- One participant raised "excessive risk-taking" concerns from additional asset purchases.
- Financial imbalance/risk-taking concerns raised by individual participants, not the center of gravity.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The adopted policy combines open-ended QE with extended low-rate guidance — a very strong easing commitment. The final statement retains paragraph 5 (calendar-based mid-2015) rather than 5' (threshold-based), which is slightly less aggressive than the Evans Rule but still firmly in the strong easing commitment range.

Representative quotes:
- The Committee adopts open-ended MBS purchases until the labor market "substantially improves," plus extended funds rate guidance through mid-2015.
- Williams: open-ended purchases "serve as a useful automatic stabilizer."
- Kocherlakota (advocating for 5'): the guidance "provides protection against inflation" and is explicitly designed as maximum accommodation.
- Chairman Bernanke (summarizing): "we want to see some progress — not sharp progress, but some progress — in the unemployment rate."
- Skeptics (Bullard, Lacker, Plosser, George) prefer less commitment.

**Minutes — Score: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Extremely dovish forward signal. Open-ended QE plus extended calendar-based (and soon threshold-based) forward guidance on rates. This is among the most dovish forward guidance configurations.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants' forecasts for economic activity...were also associated with an outlook for the unemployment rate to remain close to recent levels through 2012 and then to decline gradually."
- "Additional purchases could reinforce the Committee's forward guidance regarding the federal funds rate."
- The meeting launched QE3 with open-ended purchases and extended the rate guidance to "at least through mid-2015."
- Skeptics exist but the forward signal from the majority and the Committee action is strongly accommodative.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript — Score: 58 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty — the fiscal cliff, Europe, LSAP exit mechanics, and model uncertainty all feature prominently. The multiple simultaneous risks and "uncharted territory" of exit from QE3 push the score toward the higher end of the elevated uncertainty range.

Representative quotes:
- Williams: "all the uncertainties of the economy, the fiscal cliff, and Europe."
- Fisher: "going beyond the first part of 2013 is largely pure guesswork."
- Dudley (on yield curve confidence intervals): "we are probably even more uncertain about the shape of the yield curve because you have never gone from purchasing all of these assets to selling all of these assets. So we don't really know how the market is going to react."
- Plosser: "The consequences may prove very disruptive to the U.S. economy."
- Bernanke and staff present point estimates but explicitly acknowledge wide uncertainty bands.

**Minutes — Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited prominently: European crisis, U.S. fiscal cliff, efficacy of QE, structural vs. cyclical labor market debate. "Many" participants flag uncertainty explicitly.

Representative quotes:
- "Many participants also noted that a high level of uncertainty regarding the European fiscal and banking crisis and the outlook for U.S. fiscal and regulatory policies was weighing on confidence."
- "Business contacts in many parts of the country were reported to be highly uncertain about the outlook."
- "A number of participants highlighted the uncertainty about the overall effects of additional purchases on financial markets and the real economy."
- "Participants still saw significant downside risks."
- Minority: "Others questioned the role of uncertainty about policy as a factor constraining aggregate demand."
