### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 30 ±8 | Minutes score: 28 ±7**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "staff revised down its core PCE inflation estimate in the fourth quarter to 1.1 percent from 1.6 percent at the time of the previous meeting... staff currently anticipates that core PCE inflation will continue to run close to 1 percent during the winter"
— Yellen (summary)

> "Except for a couple of months in early 2012, PCE core inflation has been below 2 percent for more than six years. Headline inflation has been below 2 percent for more than two and a half years... disinflationary trends seem strong"
— Kocherlakota

> "inflation developments since December have not been supportive of the assessment that inflation is headed back to 2 percent within an acceptable period of time... inclination today would be to nudge that path down just a touch further"
— Evans

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Participants uniformly see inflation as meaningfully below target (core ~1.1%, headline near zero), with market-based inflation compensation declining and some concerns about expectations drift. Minority view (Fischer, Mester) acknowledges readings below target but sees oil as temporary.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "Inflation had declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices"

> "Several participants remarked that inflation measures that excluded energy items had also moved down in recent months"

> "Several participants saw the continuing weakness of core inflation measures as a concern"

> "Market-based measures of inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead had registered a further decline"

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** Participants clearly see inflation well below target, driven by energy but with core also softening. Most expect a gradual return to 2% but there is genuine concern about the decline in inflation compensation and weak core readings. Minority upside view: "A few participants voiced concern that nominal wage growth might rise rapidly and inflation might exceed 2 percent for a time." Minority downside view: "A few participants suggested that the weakness of nominal wage growth indicated that core and headline inflation could take longer to return to 2 percent."

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 42 ±7 | Minutes score: 48 ±5**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "I expect our full employment goal will be reached by the end of this year... real GDP growth... nearly 3 percent"
— Williams

> "most of you seem reasonably confident that real GDP will continue expanding at a solid pace... real GDP likely expanded at a rapid pace, maybe about 3¾ percent or so, in the second half of last year"
— Yellen (summary)

> "I think we still have some ways to go before we are back to maximum employment... unemployment gap in the staff estimate understates the true degree of slack in the labor market" (cyclical shortfall in LFPR, involuntary part-time)
— Yellen

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Above-trend GDP growth (forecasts ~3%) and rapidly improving labor market signal an economy closing the gap, but broad consensus that slack remains. Score below 50 per bright-line rule. Bullard and Mester describe growth as clearly above trend with less residual slack concern; Kocherlakota, Evans, Tarullo see more slack.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "Economic activity had been expanding at a solid pace"

> "Real GDP continued to grow faster than potential GDP late in the year and during January"

> "The underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish"

> "Risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced"

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** The economy is growing above its potential growth rate, which means the remaining (small) output gap is closing. The level is near but still slightly below potential, as underutilization is "continuing to diminish" (not yet eliminated). Narrow range of views on this dimension; broad agreement on solid growth trajectory.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 47 ±6 | Minutes score: 43 ±8**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "continued exceptional job growth and steady declines in unemployment and other measures of slack, I expect our full employment goal will be reached by the end of this year, if not sooner"
— Williams

> "if we normalize the underutilization of hours in relation to past cycles, this would imply an unemployment rate of about 6.1 percent currently, so that's a gap of 0.5 percentage point"
— Dudley

> "there likely remains an appreciable margin of slack in the labor market despite these recent gains... elevated involuntary part-time employment... labor force participation rate remains low"
— Yellen (summary)

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** U-3 at 5.6%, approaching estimates of NAIRU (~5–5.5%), with broadly improving indicators. But broad-based residual slack widely acknowledged. Lacker, Bullard, Mester see tightening as more advanced; Kocherlakota/Evans/Tarullo see more slack.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "Labor market conditions improved further, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate"

> "Some participants believed that considerable labor market slack remained, especially when indicators other than the unemployment rate were taken into account, including the unusually large fraction of the labor force working part time for economic reasons"

> "A few others saw only a limited degree of remaining labor underutilization or anticipated that underutilization would be eliminated relatively soon"

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** Improving but still below full employment. The center sees diminishing but still meaningful slack. Range from "considerable slack" (some) to "limited underutilization" (a few), producing a wider uncertainty band.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript score: 52 ±7 | Minutes score: 51 ±5**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "Tealbook has revised its projections of consumer spending growth markedly higher... expects consumption growth at about 4 percent this year... now convinced that this is likely to persist"
— Lacker

> "consumption increasing by around 4 percent, supported by the gain in real disposable income from lower prices at the pump; business fixed investment continuing to advance despite a temporary drag from falling drilling and mining investment"
— Brainard

> "incoming data as consistent with an economy that continues to grow at an above-trend pace, marked by rising household and business confidence"
— Dudley

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending at 4% is notably above trend, supported by oil dividend and improving labor incomes. Business investment mixed (cap-ex plans rising broadly; energy sector cutting sharply). Net exports negative (dollar). On net, spending/investment at or slightly above trend.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "Household spending was rising moderately"

> "Recent declines in oil prices, which had boosted household purchasing power, were among the factors likely to underpin consumer spending"

> "Industry contacts pointed to generally solid business conditions"

> "Recovery in the housing sector remained slow" and energy sector investment could damp activity

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending moderate with positive tailwinds from oil; business conditions solid; but housing slow and energy investment a drag. Net assessment is roughly at trend, perhaps slightly above. Limited disagreement on this dimension.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 35 ±8 | Minutes score: 32 ±6**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "January Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey shows that credit standards are easing and credit demand is rising"
— Mester

> "a balance sheet of more than $4 trillion that is providing substantial stimulus"
— Williams

> "the dollar's appreciation delivers an extra degree of financial restrictiveness that acts as if monetary policy has already tightened a bit"

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Near-zero federal funds rate plus $4T balance sheet = highly accommodative conditions. Credit availability good, spreads not mentioned as elevated, equity prices relatively high, long-term rates very low. Strong dollar provides a partial offset. Overall conditions in the "somewhat loose to neutral" range.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "A couple of participants noted that the reduction in longer-term real interest rates tended to make U.S. financial conditions more accommodative"

> "Easing credit standards" cited as supporting household spending

> "Low interest rates" noted as a positive factor

> Substantial decline in nominal longer-term interest rates noted

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are described as accommodative — low rates, easing credit standards, declining long-term yields. Dollar appreciation provides some offset but the net picture is loose conditions. Some noted dollar strength as a restraining factor, but this was more about trade effects than overall financial tightness.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 47 ±8 | Minutes score: 45 ±8**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> Alternative B (hold, keep "patient") supported by all (including Kocherlakota who reversed prior dissents)

> "The time is simply not right for liftoff... our topic, I think, should be turning more to how we can use tools to ease financial market conditions and bolster the credibility of that inflation target"
— Kocherlakota

> "I believe we may be underestimating the costs of moving too slowly... if the economy evolves as I anticipate, I believe it will be appropriate for us to lift off relatively soon"
— Mester

> "liftoff could come as soon as the June meeting... I intend to be particularly alert for any incoming evidence that serves to cast doubt on our projection"
— Yellen

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Committee unanimously holds (Alternative B) maintaining "patient" language. Policy debate is about when to signal liftoff (March vs. later). A slight majority lean toward June or September liftoff, but the dovish minority (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Brainard) want more patience. Lacker, Mester, George, Bullard would score higher (want to remove "patient" sooner); Kocherlakota, Evans would score lower (want more accommodation).

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "Many participants indicated that their assessment of the balance of risks...had inclined them toward keeping the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound for a longer time"

> "Some observed that...it might be appropriate to begin policy firming in the near term"

> "One participant recommended that...the Committee consider ways to use its tools to provide more, not less, accommodation"

> Active discussion of when to begin normalization — liftoff is on the table

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** The majority leans toward patience/holding longer at the lower bound, but a significant minority sees near-term firming as appropriate. The overall lean is slightly dovish — hold with eventual normalization in mind. Range from one participant wanting more accommodation to some wanting near-term firming.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript score: 37 ±8 | Minutes score: 52 ±7**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> Statement maintains "patient" language — "As I see it, liftoff could come as soon as the June meeting... I intend to be particularly alert for any incoming evidence that serves to cast doubt"
— Yellen

> "at a minimum I recommend that we commit to keeping the federal funds rate in its current range until the one-to-two-year-ahead outlook for inflation has returned to 2 percent"
— Kocherlakota

> "the risks at liftoff remain asymmetric... I would rather wait longer and, if needed, tighten faster"
— Rosengren

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** "Patient" language retained signals at least two more meetings before liftoff. Modal forward signal: watch data, liftoff possible in June or September, but leaning toward later given inflation concerns. Kocherlakota advocates even more accommodation. Lacker, Mester, George favor removing "patient" in March; they would set forward guidance at ~55–60.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> Discussion about approaching normalization: "the federal funds rate may have already been kept at its lower bound for a sufficient length of time"

> "Many participants regarded dropping the 'patient' language...as risking a shift in market expectations"

> "A number of participants suggested that they would need to see further improvement in labor market conditions...before beginning policy normalization"

> "The Committee should communicate clearly that policy decisions will be data data-dependent"

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is roughly neutral with a very mild tilt toward eventual tightening. Normalization is on the horizon but the Committee is not yet ready to signal it strongly. Data dependence is emphasized. Range from those ready to signal near-term firming to those wanting more accommodation.

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript score: 58 ±8 | Minutes score: 42 ±6**

**Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

> "I find it difficult to reconcile the data on growth in employment with the data on wages and prices... a puzzling and confusing disconnect... I don't think we can entirely rule out the possibility that an unusual and persistent state of the world is developing"
— Lockhart

> "I don't like those odds" (2-out-of-3 possible interpretations of low Treasury rates imply inadequate accommodation)
— Evans

> "I find [low long-term rates] hard to square with our December SEP assessments... These scenarios hardly exhaust the set of possible explanations for the current constellation of financial market prices"
— Yellen

**Transcript — Center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants express genuine uncertainty across: (1) inflation expectations drift vs. temporary, (2) true natural rate of unemployment, (3) dollar impact on inflation, (4) global growth spillovers, (5) efficacy of Phillips curve models. Elevated — multiple participants saying they have difficulty making sense of the data.

**Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

> "The risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced"

> "Wide agreement that it would be difficult to specify in advance an exhaustive list of economic indicators"

> "Many participants continued to judge that a deterioration in the foreign economic situation could pose downside risks"

> "Uncertainties about the inflation outlook" noted; debate about inflation compensation interpretation

**Minutes — Center-of-gravity:** Somewhat elevated uncertainty, particularly around inflation dynamics and international developments. Risks seen as "nearly balanced" but with notable concerns about foreign developments, inflation compensation, and the uncharted nature of the liftoff decision. Broad agreement it was somewhat elevated.
