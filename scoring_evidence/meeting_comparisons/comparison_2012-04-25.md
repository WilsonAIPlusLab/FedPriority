### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Participants see inflation at or slightly above the 2% target — primarily driven by oil/gasoline with anchored expectations. The modal view is that inflation is transient and will return to or below 2%. A minority (Plosser, Kocherlakota) see upside risk.

Representative quotes:
- Fisher: "our current work indicates that the 12-month rate has held pretty steady at 1.9 percent for four months. I regard that as a useful indicator of where inflation is trending... I continue to adhere to my view that we're going to trend toward 2 percent."
- Plosser: "Inflation developments are less encouraging. Inflation remains above our target and has been more stubborn than many anticipated. The Tealbook continues to expect inflation to move back toward our target and even below it, but has, once again, had to increase its forecast based on incoming data."
- Pianalto: "The median CPI and the trimmed mean CPI have both risen 2.3 percent over the last three months. Core PCE inflation is likely to rise at a similar rate in the first quarter, quite a bit faster than I had expected back in January."
- Bernanke (summary): "inflation has been a little higher than expected; however it is very close to 2 percent, and it does seem to be well anchored overall... it is very much gasoline that is driving a lot of this."

**Minutes — Score: 47 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Most participants see the current above-target inflation as temporary and energy-driven, with inflation expected to return to or below 2%. The center of gravity is near-neutral with a slight pickup acknowledged.

Representative quotes:
- "Mainly reflecting the increase in the prices of crude oil and gasoline earlier this year, inflation had picked up somewhat."
- "Most participants anticipated that after the temporary effect of the rise in oil and gasoline prices had run its course, inflation would be at or below the 2 percent rate."
- "Some participants saw a risk that inflation pressures could increase...they pointed to the fact that inflation was currently above target."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Broad consensus that the economy is below potential with significant slack remaining, albeit recovering. The debate is about the SIZE of the gap, not its sign. Even the most hawkish participants acknowledge the gap, though they argue it is smaller.

Representative quotes:
- Evans: "Resource slack remains unacceptably high... the latest Tealbook assessment today has the output gap at 4¾ percent."
- Rosengren: "expect growth that makes only plodding improvements in a dismal employment situation... Tealbook... unemployment rate at the end of 2013 at 7.7 percent, only ½ percentage point lower than the current unemployment rate."
- Bernanke (summary): "moderate economic growth with slow improvement in unemployment." "I would still stick with the moderate economic growth–slow improvement in the labor market scenario."
- Williams: "even with the better data, I still expect only a moderate pace of real GDP growth of about 2½ percent this year and 2¾ percent next year."

**Minutes — Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Participants clearly view the economy as operating below potential with moderate recovery. "Low rates of resource utilization" is unambiguous.

Representative quotes:
- "The economy continued to expand moderately."
- "Low rates of resource utilization" (endorsed in forward guidance language).
- "A number of factors continued to be seen as likely limiting the economic expansion to a moderate pace in the near term."

Minority view: "A few participants noted that current measures of labor market slack would be overstated if structural factors accounted for a large portion of the current high levels of unemployment."

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Clear consensus that labor markets are well below full employment, with 8.2% unemployment and most forecasts showing only gradual improvement. Multiple participants discuss Okun's law puzzle, participation rate decline, structural vs. cyclical factors.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "we are far from full employment. My outlook is for only gradual progress in meeting that objective."
- Williams: "I expect the unemployment rate will remain above 8 percent this year and slowly decline to about 7 percent at the end of 2014."
- Plosser: "The unemployment rate, though still very elevated at 8.2 percent, is at its lowest level in three years."
- Rosengren: "unemployment rate at the end of 2013 at 7.7 percent, only ½ percentage point lower than the current unemployment rate... plodding improvements in a dismal employment situation."

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Labor market is improving but still carries significant slack. Unemployment is elevated, wage pressures limited. Spotty tightness for highly qualified workers only.

Representative quotes:
- "The unemployment rate had declined further, although it remained elevated."
- "Contacts in some parts of the country said that highly qualified workers were in short supply; overall, however, wage pressures had been limited so far."
- "A few participants...said that in their view, slack remained high and weak aggregate demand was the major reason that unemployment was still elevated."

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 44 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Consumer spending improving modestly, with bright spots in autos and selective categories, but underlying income growth weak. Business investment improving but focused on productivity rather than capacity expansion. Net picture is below-to-at trend.

Representative quotes:
- Fisher: "retail sales ex. autos, ex. gas was up 6.6 percent through March. All 11 sectors that are tracked by that survey have come in positive for the second month in a row." "The logistics companies are reporting the best first quarter in six years."
- Plosser: "Household spending continues to advance, coming in stronger than the March Tealbook had forecast; March retail sales expanded at a solid rate."
- Bernanke (summary): "for consumer spending, while improving, there doesn't seem to be the fuel needed to accelerate. Disposable income growth was 0.8 percent in all of 2011 and is estimated to be at a 0.3 percent rate in the first quarter."
- Williams: "expect only a moderate pace of real GDP growth of about 2½ percent this year."

**Minutes — Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Below-trend but expanding. Consumer spending moderate, business investment cautious and productivity-focused rather than capacity-expanding, housing depressed.

Representative quotes:
- "Consumer spending continued to expand moderately, notwithstanding high gasoline prices."
- "Many firms remained somewhat cautious in their hiring and investment decisions, with most capital investment being undertaken to improve productivity...rather than to expand capacity."
- "The level of activity in the [housing] sector remained depressed."

Minority view: "A few were more optimistic about the potential for a more rapid housing recovery."

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 32 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: US financial conditions are broadly accommodative — low rates, improving credit availability, improving bank conditions. The financial stability discussion highlights risks from *prolonged* low rates (reach for yield, pension/insurance vulnerabilities, farmland prices), not from tightness. European stress is real but not yet transmitted materially into US financial conditions at this meeting.

Representative quotes:
- Bullard: "measures of U.S. financial stress, such as the St. Louis Financial Stress Index, remain low." ECB's LTRO "diminished the probability of financial meltdown in Europe."
- Williams: "Credit conditions continue to improve."
- Bernanke (go-round summary): "In U.S. markets, credit conditions are improving, as bank credit quality and loan demand both improve. There has been a pickup in consumer credit demand, and spreads on C&I loans have come in."
- Bernanke (summary): "Italian and Spanish spreads to bunds are both up about 90 basis points since the March meeting." [European financial stress referenced as a risk throughout.]

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Domestic financial conditions are somewhat loose and improving. European spillover risks provide a tightening offset. Net position: somewhat loose.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial conditions in U.S. markets continued to improve; bank credit quality and loan demand both increased."
- "Investment-grade corporate bond yields were flat to down slightly and remained at very low levels."
- "Investors' concerns about the sovereign debt and banking situation in the euro area intensified during the intermeeting period."

Minority view: "A few participants indicated...very low interest rates might be inducing some investors to take on imprudent risks in the search for higher nominal returns."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 28 ±9 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The broad majority supports maintaining existing maximum accommodation (alternative B, no new easing, "late 2014" guidance intact). Several participants (Evans, Rosengren, Yellen, Raskin) would welcome more accommodation if needed. A clear minority of 4-6 participants advocate earlier exit.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "I support alternative B... we are far from full employment... I believe it is also important for us to remain prepared to provide additional monetary accommodation as warranted."
- Evans: "I favor a strongly accommodative monetary policy stance that reduces resource slack more quickly than our SEP and Tealbook projections."
- Bernanke: "There's broad support for alternative B... I do think that maintaining the 'late 2014' language is still appropriate."
- Lacker (explicit dissent): "mid-2013 is when I expect the liftoff. I'm clearly not the median. I'll dissent if the statement isn't close to that."
- Plosser: "I do think it is very likely that economic conditions will warrant a change in policy considerably sooner than 2014. Indeed, based on my forecast, I believe it will be appropriate to begin reducing the level of accommodation as soon as either late this year or early 2013."

**Minutes — Score: 22 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Firmly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation with near-zero rates, Operation Twist, and strong forward guidance. No new easing launched but no discussion of unwinding.

Representative quotes:
- "The Committee members reached the collective judgment that it would be appropriate to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance of monetary policy."
- "Almost all of the members again agreed to indicate that the Committee expects to maintain a highly accommodative stance...at least through late 2014."
- "Some Committee members indicated that their policy judgment reflected in part their perception of downside risks to growth."

Minority view: "One member thought that there were upside risks to inflation, especially if the current degree of highly accommodative monetary policy were maintained much beyond this year."

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: One formal dissent (Lacker) with explicit statement of intent to vote against. Strong reservations from Plosser, Kocherlakota, and Bullard on policy direction. A near-8-8 straw poll on statement language reveals deep division even within the alternative B camp. The economic assessment also showed notable differences on potential output size and whether the gap was as large as Tealbook estimated.

Representative quotes:

- Straw poll on "supported by highly accommodative monetary policy" language: approximately 8 votes to drop, 8-9 to keep — nearly split the room.
- Plosser: "I do think it is very likely that economic conditions will warrant a change in policy considerably sooner than 2014. Indeed, based on my forecast, I believe it will be appropriate to begin reducing the level of accommodation as soon as either late this year or early 2013."
- Kocherlakota: "Clearly, this is not going to be a winning recommendation at this meeting, but I do favor alternative C."

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on policy action with minor differences. Intellectual disagreement on economic assessment (slack measurement, inflation outlook) but near-consensus on policy.

Representative quotes:
- "Almost all of the members again agreed" on forward guidance (implying one or possibly two did not fully agree).
- Range of views on structural vs. cyclical unemployment and inflation risks.
- "One member thought that there were upside risks to inflation."

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: The forward signal from this meeting is clearly dovish — "rates exceptionally low through late 2014" is maintained as a date-based forward commitment. The optimal control advocates (Yellen, Evans) argue for rates lower even longer (mid-2015 or later). The meeting's forward signal is a strong easing commitment.

Representative quotes:
- Bernanke: "There's broad support for alternative B... maintaining the 'late 2014' language is still appropriate."
- Yellen: "Given that my current estimate of the appropriate time for liftoff is mid-2015, I remain quite comfortable with our current policy guidance."
- Rosengren: "The Tealbook forecast has the unemployment rate above 7 percent and PCE inflation below 2 percent in 2014. I agree with this forecast, which would imply to me that we should not be tightening until 2015."
- Evans: "Displaying a greater commitment to the optimal control monetary policy makes a lot of sense to me — it would generate better outcomes. The comments about how we should keep the funds rate lower longer — that would be excellent."

**Minutes — Score: 6 ±4 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Very strong dovish forward guidance — date-based commitment through late 2014. Some members even argued for longer. Near the most dovish end of the forward guidance spectrum.

Representative quotes:
- "Currently anticipates that economic conditions...are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014."
- "The need to compensate for a substantial period during which the policy rate was constrained by the zero bound was also cited by a few members as a possible reason to maintain a very low level of the federal funds rate for a longer period."
- "Almost all of the members agreed that the change in the outlook over the intermeeting period was insufficient to warrant an adjustment to the Committee's forward guidance."

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript — Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty on multiple fronts — the Okun's law puzzle (structural vs. cyclical unemployment), European downside risks, fiscal cliff, participation rate behavior, potential output level. The structural uncertainty about "what the economy actually looks like" (with strong disagreement on output gap size) is a unique feature. Most participants explicitly mention "considerable uncertainty."

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "Considerable uncertainty attaches to both my unemployment and inflation forecasts."
- Bernanke: "What we are looking at is a great deal of uncertainty about how this recovery is going to proceed."
- Pianalto: "70 percent confidence interval that spans early 2013 through the end of 2015" for policy liftoff.
- Rosengren: "I see a strange dichotomy between my modal and mean forecasts... My mean is much lower. The modal forecast assumes that downside risks do not materialize."
- Williams: "there are countless risks to my forecast for economic activity besides the uncertainty regarding the participation rate and unemployment. Most are to the downside."

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty from multiple sources: fiscal cliff risk, European situation, structural vs. cyclical debate, weather effects on data. Genuine analytical uncertainty about the state of the economy, not just rhetorical hedging.

Representative quotes:
- "Continued uncertainty about the strength and durability of the economic recovery, as well as about government policies."
- "Several participants indicated that uncertainty about the trajectory of future fiscal policy could lead businesses to defer hiring and investment."
- "Others thought it was premature to infer a stronger underlying trend from the recent positive indicators."
