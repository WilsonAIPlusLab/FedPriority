# FOMC Meeting 1993-05-18: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence])

> "The core CPI rate, as I calculate it, now has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate over the first months of the year and that really bothers me...inflation is no longer coming down." — MR. BROADDUS

> "I think we have 4 percent inflation...the inflation bear has been sighted." — MR. MULLINS

> "although the recent numbers are a little unnerving, I do think it's far too soon to conclude that we are experiencing a basic shift in the outlook for prices." — MR. KEEHN

> "in spite of the inflation numbers that have been reported, I don't hear anybody talking about price increases at all." — MR. FORRESTAL

Center of gravity: Participants are collectively disturbed by the recent inflation uptick (core CPI spiking to 4.5% annualized), which has derailed the expected disinflation path, but are divided on whether it represents a genuine trend or temporary aberrations. The center of gravity sees inflation as modestly above target and no longer improving.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence])

> "Members expressed particular concern about the rise in various measures of inflation over the past several months."

> "Higher inflation expectations, if sustained, would be detrimental to economic performance, and the risks of an uptrend in inflation clearly had increased."

> "It was premature in the view of many members to conclude that the inflationary trend had tilted upward."

Center of gravity: Members see inflation as somewhat elevated above expectations, with worsening inflation expectations. However, many attribute the rise to temporary factors and note continued slack as a restraining force. The concern is real but not yet indicating entrenched higher inflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence])

> "Manufacturing in our area remains sluggish and it is consistently operating below capacity." — MR. HOENIG

> "the fundamentals of the slack in the economy" [and describes] "sluggish growth." — MR. KELLEY

> "my sense continues to be that we're on a path of moderate growth...between 2-1/2 and 3-1/2 percent." — MR. MULLINS

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN references "slack markets" repeatedly.

Center of gravity: The economy is growing moderately but clearly remains below potential, with substantial slack still evident in manufacturing capacity and broader economic activity. First quarter GDP was notably weak.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence])

> "The economic expansion has slowed in recent months." Unemployment at 7.0%.

> "Still considerable slack in labor and product markets."

> "Members continued to report uneven conditions ranging from apparently moderate gains in some parts of the country to mixed or marginally declining activity in others."

Center of gravity: The economy is clearly operating below potential with considerable slack, but expansion continues with "sustainable momentum." Not a deep recession, but a sluggish recovery with persistent slack.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence])

> "Major companies--and I must say everyone I have talked to in the manufacturing sector--say they will continue to reduce their employment and will produce more products with fewer people." — MR. KEEHN

> "The economy is growing enough to produce some jobs but hardly enough, really, to accommodate even the population growth." — MS. PHILLIPS

> "no sentiment to increase employment." — MR. JORDAN

> "unemployment has come down from the peak last summer of 7.7 percent to 7 percent." — MR. MULLINS

Center of gravity: The labor market has significant slack with 7% unemployment. While job growth is occurring, ongoing corporate restructuring, downsizing, and reluctance to hire permanent employees keep the labor market well below full employment.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence])

> "Continuing efforts to constrain costs and to hold down or reduce employment levels, notably of permanent workers."

> "Business firms generally appeared disposed to continue to meet increases in demand through overtime work and temporary workers."

> Unemployment at 7.0%; "still considerable slack in labor and product markets."

Center of gravity: The labor market is clearly slack. Firms are reluctant to hire, preferring temporary workers. Unemployment is well above full employment. Compensation inflation has "stalled" its downtrend, suggesting very limited labor market pressure.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence])

> MR. KEEHN: Auto industry confidence "somewhat stronger than it has been," production schedules up 10% year-over-year; steel orders at 110% of capacity. But truck prices "really have not been increased."

> "Retail sales are going up negligibly...sluggish loan demand, or flat loan demand really." — MR. BOEHNE

> "businesses are focusing now more than ever on cost containment." — MR. HOENIG

> "Consumers and businesses continue to be more interested in debt reduction than in re-leveraging." — MR. LAWARE

Center of gravity: Consumer spending and business investment are growing modestly but unevenly. Auto and housing sectors show strength, but retail sales are sluggish and business investment is constrained by fiscal policy uncertainty and ongoing balance sheet deleveraging.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 39 +/-6 [Moderate confidence])

> "Anecdotal reports from around the country suggested generally lackluster retail sales over the first four months of the year."

> "Spending for business equipment had continued to help maintain the expansion" but "numerous firms reported that they were holding off on making major new investment commitments."

> "Nonresidential construction remained uneven and on the whole relatively subdued."

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is below trend (lackluster), business equipment spending is a bright spot but being curtailed by uncertainty, and construction is subdued. Overall somewhat below trend but not contracting.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence])

> "a decided change in the attitude about lending by banks...banks have become more aggressive. They are now actively seeking loans." — MR. KEEHN

> "Banks are reporting higher loan demand...the credit situation seems to be improving." — MR. FORRESTAL

> "very, very strong inflows into bond funds, junk bond funds" -- suggesting ample liquidity. — MR. SYRON

> "the only component of the current environment that is stimulative or conducive to growth is the capital market environment." — MR. MULLINS

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. The fed funds rate is at a historically low 3%, banks are becoming more aggressive lenders, and capital markets are accommodative. The credit crunch that characterized the early recovery has eased significantly.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence])

> "Relatively low interest rates, receptive financial markets, and the more aggressive lending policies of some depository institutions."

> Mr. Angell: "low real interest rates, a very steep yield curve."

> "Monetary policy would still be stimulative after a modest tightening move."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are accommodative/loose. Low real interest rates, receptive markets, and improving lending conditions. This is clearly below the midpoint on the tightness scale.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 56 +/-10 [High confidence])

> "I think Governor Mullins is right that a 3 percent funds rate is too low...My inclination for today would be to go to a tilt toward tightness." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I want to raise the rate today. I'd be satisfied with 25 basis points." — MR. ANGELL

> "a tightening action could well abort the expansion...I would not be in favor of any move at this time." — MR. FORRESTAL

> Vote: 10-2 for Alternative B asymmetric toward tightening. Angell dissented wanting immediate tightening; Boehne dissented wanting symmetric.

Center of gravity: The Committee leans hawkish, agreeing the 3% funds rate is too low and will need to rise, but the majority prefers to wait for more data before acting. The asymmetric directive signals a clear tightening bias without immediate action.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence])

> "All but two of the members indicated that they preferred or could accept a directive that called for maintaining the existing degree of pressure on reserve positions and that included a bias toward possible firming."

> "A majority of the members" supported "positioning policy for a move toward restraint should signs of intensifying inflation continue to multiply."

> Mr. Angell dissented wanting immediate tightening; Mr. Boehne dissented preferring no tightening bias.

Center of gravity: The Committee holds steady but adopts a tightening bias. This is a lean-hawkish posture — not yet acting but positioning to act. The current action is unchanged, so the score stays near 50 but tilted hawkish.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 48 +/-8 [High confidence])

> Two formal dissents from opposite directions: Angell (wanted tightening) and Boehne (wanted symmetric/no bias).

> "I would again say that the best thing to do...is to [vote for] an increase at this meeting." — MR. LINDSEY

> "I would not be in favor of any move at this time...I hope if we have to take action that we do it very, very deliberatively." — MR. FORRESTAL

> Extended debate over symmetric vs. asymmetric directive required multiple rounds of discussion.

Center of gravity: Notable disagreement with distinct camps. One group wanted immediate tightening, another wanted to hold with tightening bias, and a third wanted symmetric hold. Two formal dissents from opposite directions is an unusual configuration reflecting genuine philosophical differences.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 44 +/-6 [High confidence])

> Two formal dissents: Angell (wanted tightening) and Boehne (opposed tightening bias).

> "The members differed to some extent with regard to the most likely outcome" for inflation.

> "Some members preferred to retain a directive that did not incorporate a presumption about the likely direction" — minority initially opposed.

Center of gravity: Two dissents pulling in opposite directions is notable. Additionally, the inflation outlook generated genuine disagreement among members, with "a number" stressing sustained risk and "others" emphasizing slack. Moderate-to-notable disagreement.

Center of gravity: Financial stability is essentially not discussed as a topic. The few references to financial markets are about inflation signaling (gold, long bond) rather than systemic risk.

**Minutes** (Score: 10 +/-5 [Moderate confidence])

> No discussion of systemic risk, asset valuations, bank health, or financial stability per se.

Center of gravity: Financial stability is essentially not a topic. Market references serve inflation monitoring, not financial stability analysis. Consistent with typical 1993-era low emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence])

> "there is a remote possibility that we may not want to move at some point in the future, it has to be pretty remote" -- strongly implying tightening is coming. — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I rather doubt that anyone parachuting into this economic environment would choose a 3 percent federal funds rate." — MR. MULLINS

> "we seem to be drifting away from [price stability]. I don't think it's fatal if the drift goes on a little longer." — MR. STERN

> The asymmetric directive itself is the forward signal.

Center of gravity: The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. Nearly all participants agree the 3% funds rate is unsustainably low, with debate only about timing. The asymmetric directive, combined with Greenspan's explicit statement that a move is very likely, sends a mild-to-moderate tightening signal.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence])

> "In the view of a majority of the members, wage and price developments over recent months were sufficiently worrisome to warrant positioning policy for a move toward restraint."

> "Slightly greater reserve restraint would be acceptable or slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable" — asymmetric language leaning toward tightening.

> "If a policy tightening action were not needed, an asymmetric directive would nonetheless underscore the Committee's concern about recent inflation readings."

Center of gravity: The forward signal is a mild tightening lean, contingent on incoming inflation data. The asymmetric directive signals the next move is more likely to be a tightening. However, it is conditional ("should signs continue to multiply") and the Committee agreed on intermeeting consultation before acting, indicating caution.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence])

> "we now have before us about the widest spectrum of fundamentally conflicting possibilities that I can remember." — MR. KELLEY

> "great certainty at this particular stage about what any of these variables is doing is probably ill advised. We are in an unstable environment." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "an enormous amount of uncertainty; and to some extent...it's hard to have very strong convictions about the intermediate-term [outlook for] either output or prices." — MR. SYRON

> Nearly every president cited pervasive business uncertainty driven by fiscal policy/health care reform.

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is high and explicitly acknowledged. Participants are genuinely puzzled by the combination of slack growth and rising inflation. Political/fiscal uncertainty is a dominant theme from district reports. Multiple participants express low confidence in any particular outlook scenario.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence])

> "Several emphasized that the outlook was subject to substantial uncertainty stemming to an important extent from the unsettled course of legislation aimed at reducing the federal deficit."

> "A considerable degree of uncertainty surrounded the outlook for inflation."

> "Prevailing economic uncertainties, notably those generated by the current legislative debate about federal taxes and spending."

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is elevated. Multiple references to "substantial uncertainty," "considerable uncertainty," and "uncertainties" driven by both inflation developments and fiscal legislation. Members explicitly acknowledge not understanding recent inflation dynamics ("it remained difficult to find a satisfactory explanation").

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 65 +/-8 [Moderate confidence])

> MR. PRELL: "we were carrying through higher levels of inflation throughout and moderating somewhat further the tradeoff; in a sense, we were getting less bang for the unemployment [buck]" — acknowledged Greenbook revised inflation upward with real growth revised down.

> MR. PRELL on owner-equivalent rent: "a not insignificant part of the April CPI is a big increase in owner equivalent rent, which popped up...the rate of return estimate—that has been rising fairly significantly of late."

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (analytical): "core CPI rate, as I calculate it, now has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate over the first months of the year."

Center of gravity: Staff confronts an unexpected acceleration in inflation — core CPI ~4.5% annualized in early 1993, with broad-based pressures from steel (protectionism), medical services, owner-equivalent rent, and automotive prices — clearly above any price stability objective and explicitly described as a puzzle given continued slack.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence])

> "increases in wages and prices had been appreciably larger thus far in 1993 than in the second half of last year"

> "Excluding the food and energy components, producer prices advanced over the first four months of 1993 at a faster pace than in 1992"

> "The deceleration of labor costs also appeared to have stalled in 1993"

Center of gravity: Staff reports a modest acceleration in both prices and wages from the subdued rates of 1992, though the staff projection expects this to be temporary given persistent slack.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence])

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (analytical): "with the excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market...to argue that there is something of significance working [to push inflation]...questions that we have here."

> MR. PRELL: "the bigger driver in this was simply a recognition that the trends had not been developing as we had anticipated and we were carrying through higher levels of inflation throughout." Q1 real GDP weaker than expected; Greenbook growth revised to ~2.2%.

Center of gravity: The economy in early 1993 is recovering from the 1990-91 recession but remains clearly below potential, with excess capacity, elevated unemployment (~7%), and Q1 real growth disappointing to the downside. Slack is the baseline characterization even as the inflation puzzle is debated.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence])

> "the pace of the economic expansion had slowed in recent months" with "considerable slack in the economy"

> Staff projection: "economic activity would grow at a moderate pace and that such growth would foster a gradual reduction in margins of unemployed labor and capital"

Center of gravity: Staff clearly identifies considerable slack persisting in the economy, with expansion slowing and large margins of unused capacity remaining.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 31 +/-7 [Moderate confidence])

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "with the excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market...there is something there, especially the size of the defense adjustment that's going on."

> MR. PRELL (on NAIRU discussion): "feel that in effect the NAIRU has been raised...the gap is small—[but] the tradeoff could be affected if the expectations formation process is different than the backward-looking version that's inherent in a simple Phillips curve model."

Center of gravity: Labor market is clearly below full employment in May 1993. Unemployment was approximately 7% nationally. Prell explicitly discusses "labor market slack issue" and the difficulty of explaining inflation despite that slack.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 30 +/-4 [High confidence])

> "The civilian unemployment rate remained at 7.0 percent"

> "Total nonfarm payroll employment rose only slightly on balance over March and April"

> "reductions in payrolls were widespread" in manufacturing

Center of gravity: At 7.0% unemployment with only slight job gains and widespread manufacturing payroll losses, the labor market is clearly loose with significant slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence])

> MR. PRELL: "coming into the first quarter we initially expected growth in the high 2 percent area for the quarter [but] real output growth [in the first quarter] was much weaker than expected." Greenbook revised to ~2.2% GDP growth.

> CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (regional context): "businesses are thinking about or have put [plans] on hold because of expectations about taxes...businesses are focusing now more than ever on cost containment and trying to get their profits up."

Center of gravity: Real demand was disappointing in Q1 1993, with businesses deferring investment due to fiscal uncertainty and consumers facing headwinds. The Greenbook revised growth down significantly. Consumption/investment is below trend — the stagflation puzzle is precisely that inflation accelerated despite weak real demand.

---

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence])

> "consumer spending had been quite sluggish, reflecting limited gains in employment and real labor income"

> "Business outlays for durable equipment had remained strong" but "nonresidential construction...continued weakness"

> "retail sales had been lackluster after the strong increases of the latter part of 1992"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is clearly sluggish while business equipment investment is a bright spot; net spending and investment are modestly below trend.

Center of gravity: Staff characterizes the current policy stance (3% funds rate) as accommodative/stimulative, providing sufficient support to offset fiscal drag. Financial conditions are loose/accommodative.

**Minutes** (Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence])

Center of gravity: With the federal funds rate at 3% for an extended period and the staff describing an "increasingly favorable financial environment," conditions are clearly accommodative.
