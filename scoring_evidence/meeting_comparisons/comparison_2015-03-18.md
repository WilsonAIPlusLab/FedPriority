### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **32 ±6 [High confidence]** | **30 ±5 [High confidence]** |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

1. Rosengren: "the incoming price data indicate that even the lowest forecast in the range for December will likely be too high. The Tealbook has lowered its forecast for 2015 Q4-to-Q4 core PCE inflation from 1½ percent to 1.3 percent, and I have lowered mine to 1.2 percent."
2. Brainard: "The 12-month change in core PCE prices in January was 1.3 percent…the three-month change in the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas trimmed mean rate was only 0.7 percent."
3. Evans: "I see inflation only slowly crawling back up to 2 percent sometime in 2018 or later."
4. Kocherlakota: "I don't see inflation returning to 2 percent until late 2018" and "low inflation and the low-inflation outlook has reduced the credibility of the inflation target."
5. Lacker (hawkish outlier): "prices have been held down lately by two clearly temporary factors: low oil prices and the strengthening dollar…I am confident that both headline and core inflation will move to 2 percent once these two temporary influences are behind us."

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Near-universal agreement that current inflation is well below the 2% target (core PCE ~1.3%), with the majority either uncertain about or pessimistic on the return path; only a minority (Lacker, Fischer, Mester) express high confidence in timely return to target.

---

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

1. "Inflation had declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices, and was expected to stay near its recent low level in the near term."
2. "Many participants judged that the inflation data received over the intermeeting period had been about in line with their expectations that inflation would move temporarily further below the Committee's goal."
3. One participant cited "a substantial increase in the probability that inflation would remain well below the Committee's target over the next decade."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Participants broadly agree inflation is well below the 2% target, driven by energy and dollar, but expect it to rise gradually. Current readings are low with near-term low persistence.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Economy is below potential but closing the gap. Growth moderated somewhat in Q1 (weather, ports), but underlying trajectory is toward potential.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **46 ±5 [High confidence]** | **40 ±6 [High confidence]** |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

1. Williams: "The unemployment rate has fallen more than 1 percentage point over the past year, and I expect it to decline to under 5 percent by the end of this year, falling below my estimate of the natural rate of 5.2 percent."
2. Mester: "By a broad set of indicators, labor market conditions are strong and continue to improve. Monthly job gains have averaged more than 280,000 over the past three months…We are nearing our goal of maximum employment."
3. Kocherlakota: "In the Ninth District…other than in a few specific occupations and locales, compensation pressures remain, at best, moderate…businesses continue to be able to find the workers that they want without paying them a lot."
4. Yellen: "in spite of the decline in the unemployment rate, all but two of you continue to think that some slack remains in the labor market."

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** The labor market is nearing but has not yet reached full employment; unemployment at 5.5% is approaching NAIRU estimates of ~5.0–5.2%, but wages remain subdued (~2% growth), suggesting meaningful residual slack — particularly in broader measures like U-6 and involuntary part-time employment.

---

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes:**

1. "Labor market conditions continued to improve, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate."
2. "Many judged that some degree of labor market slack remained, as evidenced by the low rate of labor force participation, still-elevated involuntary part-time employment, or subdued growth in wages."
3. "Wage pressures generally reported to be modest."
4. One participant "saw some evidence of rising wage growth and suggested that compositional changes in the labor force could be masking underlying wage pressures."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Improving labor market but not yet at full employment. Slack remains in participation, part-time, and wages.

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

2. Brainard: "consumer spending appears to have increased robustly at the end of last year. **Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes:**

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Financial conditions remain generally accommodative (zero lower bound, ongoing reinvestment) with dollar appreciation as a modest tightening channel. Very limited direct discussion.

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** The committee takes a definitive lean-hawkish step by removing "patient" and signaling liftoff is possible at any post-April meeting, though the forward guidance is carefully hedged and the Chair explicitly says June is not particularly likely — the stance is thus "approaching liftoff" rather than "committed to liftoff."

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes:**
