### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 36 ±6 | High Confidence | 39 ±5 | High Confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Daly (MS. DALY): "I project core PCE inflation will rise just to 1.6 percent this year and 1.8 percent in 2020, reaching 2 percent only in 2021."
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Core inflation of 1.6 percent is not okay."
- Bullard (MR. BULLARD): "Pressure on inflation remains muted, with the Tealbook now projecting core PCE inflation of just 1.6 percent for 2019 and 1.9 percent for 2020. In my view, this inflation outlook is unsatisfactory."
- Chair Powell: "We now forecast an increase of only 1.6 percent for 2019...there is a rising risk that inflation will fail to reach, let alone move beyond, 2 percent even during this long expansion."

**Transcript center of gravity**: Inflation is running persistently ~40 bps below the 2% target, with a multi-year track record of undershoots. The construct "below target" is well below 50. Not deflationary (no price declines), but clearly in the disinflation/below-target range. Minority views: Mester notes median PCE at 2.7% and cyclical core at 2.9%, and the Dallas trimmed mean near 2%. These suggest some alternative measures point higher, warranting a slightly wider uncertainty band.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy were running below 2 percent."
- "Inflation pressures remained muted."
- "Participants generally expected that inflation would return to the 2 percent objective as the economic expansion continued" but "various participants were concerned that indicators were suggesting that the level of longer-term inflation expectations was too low."

**Minutes center of gravity**: Participants see inflation persistently running below the 2% target with muted pressures; expectations of return to target exist but confidence is not high. Minority views: "A few participants commented on factors that may temporarily exert upward pressure" (mild upside), while others "expressed less confidence that inflation would return promptly to the 2 percent objective" (downside).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 67 ±6 | High Confidence | 57 ±5 | High Confidence |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "Last week's employment report continued to confirm a strong economy, with higher payroll employment than expected, steady wage growth, and a 50-year record-low unemployment rate...continued difficulty hiring workers in a very tight labor market."
- George (MS. GEORGE): "We continue to hear from our District contacts that tight labor market conditions and difficulty finding qualified workers are constraining their hiring plans. More contacts report difficulty hiring or retaining workers compared with the previous year, with upward of 70 percent of our contacts indicating they increased wages."
- Chair Powell: "With the unemployment rate at 3½ percent, prime-age labor force participation on the rise, and payrolls running at 200,000 a month, the labor market is strong."

**Transcript center of gravity**: Very low unemployment, widespread employer difficulty hiring, wage gains at low end. Consistent with the 53–65 range (tight) but leaning toward the higher end given the 50-year unemployment low and widespread difficulty finding workers. Minority views: Williams questions whether "tight" is the right term without clear upward wage/inflation pressure. Kashkari and Williams note possible hidden labor supply.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Conditions in the labor market remained strong, with the unemployment rate at a 50-year low, job gains remaining solid, and some measures of labor force participation increasing further."
- "Various participants remarked that there were some indications that further strengthening in overall labor market conditions was possible without creating undesirable pressures on resources."
- "Measures of wage growth had generally remained moderate."

**Minutes center of gravity**: Labor market is tight by historical standards but not generating excessive wage pressure; participants believe there is still some room for further tightening. Minority views: "A few participants commented that increases in the labor force would likely moderate as slack in the labor market diminished."
