## Part A: Discussion — Side-by-Side Evidence

### A1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 58 ±7 [High]** | **Minutes: 56 ±7 [High]**

**Transcript (participant statements):**
- MR. PARRY: "fourth-quarter surge in GDP-based inflation measures...a serious concern...our staff forecast shows an upward trend in core CPI from just under 2 percent last year to 2-1/4 percent this year and 2-1/2 percent next year."
- MR. GUYNN: "I believe and would even argue that we are beginning to see the whites of inflation's eyes."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "This is not an inflationary price environment or an inflationary cost environment...unit labor costs have barely moved."
- MR. KELLEY: "a number of price series are beginning to move...it is still only a trickle and far from a flood."

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Participants perceive inflation as slightly above target and building, with core measures expected to drift from ~2% toward 2.5%, but current cost pressures remain contained by strong productivity growth and firms' inability to pass through costs.

**Minutes (synthesized member views):**
- "core inflation remained subdued" (current state)
- "the members continued to see the risks as primarily tilted toward rising inflationary pressures"
- "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk of deterioration in the underlying inflation picture over time"
- "several cited recent statistical and anecdotal evidence of larger increases in labor compensation"
- "some nonlabor input prices already were rising faster"

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Members see core inflation currently contained but a mounting set of upside risks from tight labor, energy passthrough, and strong demand — placing the score modestly above neutral.
