## Part A: Participant Discussion

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|

**Transcript quotes:**
- Bullard: "further disinflation and possible deflation is a clear near-term risk. Deflationary expectations could become entrenched during 2009... a deflationary trap is, to my thinking, a steady state: You get stuck there, and you cannot get out."

**Minutes quotes:**

- "A few pointing to the risk of deflation. Even without a continuation of outright price declines, falling expectations of inflation would raise the real rate of interest."
- "Rising unemployment and falling capacity utilization were holding down wages and prices and inflation expectations appeared subdued."

**Minority views (Transcript):** Plosser notes "the risk of a sustained deflation, at least in the near term, is somewhat abated" — marginally less alarmed, widening the band slightly upward.

**Minority views (Minutes):** A few see outright deflation risk (would push to 10-12), while the majority sees simply subdued/below desirable (15-20).

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 5 ±3 | High confidence | 5 ±3 | High confidence |
| **Center of gravity** | Emergency-level dovish. All participants support maximum accommodation; debate is only about how large and what composition. **Transcript quotes:**
