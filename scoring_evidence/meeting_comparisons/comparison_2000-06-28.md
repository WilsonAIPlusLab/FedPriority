## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 57**

- MR. PARRY: "core PCE price index to increase 2.1 and 2.2 percent this year and next, well above last year's 1.5 percent rate... the balance of risks for inflation is pretty clearly on the up side."
- MR. MEYER: "the data on prices are clear and that inflation is rising. It is rising whether one looks at overall inflation or at the core inflation measures."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the signs of acceleration in inflation do not look so strong either... 'scant' is the operative word."
- MR. BROADDUS: "emerging trend of higher core inflation and deteriorating prospects for inflation."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 60**

- "Steep increases in energy prices had boosted overall rates of inflation somewhat, and in addition the higher energy prices likely had contributed indirectly to the rise in core measures of inflation."
- "A number of members...were concerned that these prices might well continue to accelerate gradually, even assuming that economic expansion would be sustained at a pace close to the economy's potential."
- "Other members were more optimistic that core inflation might be contained near current levels."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 62**

- MR. STOCKTON: "we estimate that the level of actual GDP is about 3 percent above the level of potential...a 3 percent imbalance between aggregate demand and aggregate supply at the end of last year is likely to be associated with a 0.6 percentage point step-up in inflation this year."
- MR. STOCKTON: "demand has been rising more quickly than supply and with...over the forecast period, we expect [unit labor costs] to pick up...The result is an acceleration [in costs] in the nonfinancial corporate sector over the forecast period."
- MR. STOCKTON: "The increase in total PCE prices steps up this year and drops back to a 2 percent [rate in 2001]."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 62**

- "Core consumer prices continued to evidence some acceleration, to an important extent reflecting some indirect effects of the sharp increase in oil prices over the past year."
- Staff forecast: "Core price inflation was projected to rise noticeably over the forecast horizon, partly as a result of higher import prices and some firming of gains in nominal labor compensation in persistently tight labor markets that would not be fully offset by productivity growth."

**Observations**: All four sources land in the 57–62 band. The staff quantifies a 3% output gap → 0.6pp core acceleration. Discussion transcript carries a wider range because individual member views span from Gramlich ("scant") to Meyer ("clearly rising"). The minutes synthesize this to a moderate above-target reading.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 60**

- MR. MEYER: "I am at least as convinced as the staff that output is beyond potential."
- MS. MINEHAN: "growth rate for the first half of the year would be above nearly all estimates of potential GDP."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the evidence that the expansion is slowing to a certain extent is pretty much unambiguous" [but continued strong investment and productivity noted].

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 58**

- "Producer resources...were already operating at very high levels."
- "Growth in aggregate demand was moderating to a pace closer to that of potential supply were still partial and tentative."
- Real GDP forecasts centered on 4–4.5% for 2000, decelerating to 3.25–3.75% for 2001.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 67**

- MR. STOCKTON: "currently we estimate that the level of actual GDP is about 3 percent above the level of potential."
- MR. STOCKTON: "Domestic spending, which has been rising at more than a 5-1/2 percent pace over the past two years, is expected to increase just 3-1/2 percent in 2001."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 58**

- "The economic expansion was moderating somewhat from a very rapid pace in the first quarter."
- Staff forecast: "the economic expansion would moderate gradually from its currently elevated pace to a rate around or perhaps a little below the growth of the economy's estimated potential."

**Observations**: The staff transcript scores notably higher (67) because Stockton explicitly states a 3% positive output gap with a number. The other three sources describe "elevated" conditions without such precision, landing at 58–60. The staff minutes paraphrase the same forecast in softer language ("currently elevated pace"), which the scorer weighted less than the numerical 3% gap in the full transcript.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 70**

- MS. MINEHAN: "remains difficult to find workers of almost any type... 10 to 20 percent increases for various classes of workers... 'All the new college graduates were hired even before they graduated.'"
- MR. POOLE: UPS entry-level workers receiving massive signing bonuses and attendance bonuses on top of base wage, with severe difficulty hitting hiring targets.
- MR. MOSKOW: "our labor markets are still very tight."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 70**

- "Unsustainably tight labor markets."
- "Labor markets remained unusually tight."
- Unemployment forecasts centered on ~4% for Q4 2000 and 4–4.25% for Q4 2001.

**Staff Transcript — Score: 70**

- MR. STOCKTON: "Pressures in labor markets are relatively intense over the next year and a half."
- MR. STOCKTON (Q&A): Staff NAIRU estimate ~5.25%; current unemployment rate "a tiny bit below even the low NAIRU."
- District evidence: "labor markets remain very tight. Unemployment rates in the District are now slightly below the national average."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 70**

- "The civilian unemployment rate averaged 4.0 percent over April and May."
- Staff forecast: "persistently tight labor markets that would not be fully offset by productivity growth."

**Observations**: Perfect agreement across all four sources at 70. This is the most stable dimension in this meeting. Whether viewed through individual anecdotes (Minehan, Poole), minutes synthesis ("unsustainably tight"), or staff quantification (UE well below NAIRU ~5.25%), the reading is uniformly very tight labor markets.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 57**

- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "chain store figures have been flat since the second or third week of May... motor vehicles clearly is easing"; but also "orders coming into the capital goods companies...have remained very strong, and backlogs continue to build."
- MR. GUYNN: "some noticeable falloff in consumer spending for big ticket, high-end products" alongside "business investment spending...is likely to remain strong."
- MR. POOLE: FedEx and UPS "expecting volume to continue very strong and, if anything, stronger than it has been."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 53**

- "Growth in consumer spending had slowed appreciably in recent months from the unusually robust pace."
- "Growth in business fixed investment had moderated in recent months but was expected to remain quite robust."
- "The housing market also provided clear evidence of weakening demand."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 62**

- MR. STOCKTON: "Domestic spending, which has been rising at more than a 5-1/2 percent pace over the past two years..."
- MR. STOCKTON: "In contrast to the reasonably clear signs of slowing in residential investment, the recent indicators of capital spending by businesses have remained very strong...new orders for nondefense capital goods moved up in recent months."
- MR. STOCKTON: Housing starts expected to slide from 1.67 million to ~1.5 million; consumer spending in "steady deceleration...this year and next."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 55**

- "Growth of consumer spending apparently slowed considerably in the second quarter after outsized gains in several previous quarters."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to be on track for another rapid increase in the second quarter."
- "Total private housing starts fell in May to their lowest level since the middle of last year."

**Observations**: Range of 53–62. Staff transcript is highest because it anchors to the 5.5% pace over two years as the baseline. Discussion minutes is lowest, reflecting the minutes' emphasis on the consumer spending deceleration and housing weakness. All four sources agree on the structure: consumer softening and housing weakness partially offset by strong business investment.

### 5. FINANCIAL TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 57**

- MR. GUYNN: "more reports of tightening credit standards and more cautious and less accommodative lending... the 50 basis points of tightening that we did at our May meeting got people's attention."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the increase in fixed rate mortgages has been a crucial element in housing" and notes the flat yield curve eliminating the ARM option.
- MR. FERGUSON: "financial market conditions between this and our previous meeting have become, if anything, a little more accommodative. Net corporate borrowings...seem to have picked up."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 60**

- "Cumulative effects of the firming in financial conditions this year had been accompanied by an increasing number of anecdotal reports of more cautious business sentiment."
- "Less favorable financial conditions in debt and equity markets."
- "Real interest rates were already relatively elevated."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 57**

- MR. STOCKTON: "We have assumed further tightening of financial conditions to begin to relieve some of the inflationary pressures...The additional tightening...would bring the funds rate a bit above the upper end of the range over the past decade."
- MR. STOCKTON: "stock prices have risen more than 12 percent over the past year" [offset to tightening] vs. model expected a 13% drop that did not materialize.
- MR. STOCKTON: "there is little reason to have expected much restraining effect yet from the change in financial conditions over the past year. But moving forward...the restraining influence of financial conditions should begin to show through more noticeably."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 58**

- "Open market operations during the intermeeting period were directed toward implementing the desired increased pressure on reserve positions."
- "Higher mortgage rates apparently were exerting a restraining effect on residential housing activity."
- "Yields on longer-term Treasury securities and investment-grade corporate bonds declined appreciably."

**Observations**: Tight cluster: 57–60. Discussion transcript notes the partial offset from Ferguson's observation of recent easing in market conditions. Staff explicitly models the equity resilience as dampening the tightening impulse. Discussion minutes synthesizes to a clean "tightened materially" reading. All four sources agree conditions are tighter than neutral but not severely restrictive.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (Discussion sources only)

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 60**

- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the probability that we are through tightening in this cyclical expansion has to be less than 50-50... we should be very careful about suggesting in any way that we might be at the edge."
- MR. MEYER: "our work is not yet done... I believe that the data are likely to show the wisdom of moving further perhaps as early as by the next meeting."
- MR. BROADDUS: "a solid case could be made for a further move today."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "It is at least possible that the Fed has already done enough tightening."

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 60**

- "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "Many Committee members noted that...additional firming of policy could well be needed at some point in the future."
- "A number also expressed the opinion that less tightening probably would be required than they had thought at the time of the May meeting."

**Observations**: Exact match at 60. Both sources convey a unanimous hold with tightening bias, with Broaddus as the hawkish outlier and Gramlich/Jordan/McTeer as the less hawkish wing. The minutes' additional detail — "less tightening...required than they had thought at the time of the May meeting" — signals modest dovish drift relative to May.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY (Discussion sources only)

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 32**

- Unanimous vote to hold with asymmetric tightening bias.
- MR. BROADDUS: Strongly argued for tightening today but accepted the Chairman's proposal.
- MR. MEYER: "I did have to struggle more than most with this decision."
- Substantial philosophical disagreement about NAIRU but convergence on policy action.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 18**

- "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "All the members agreed that the [balance of risks] should continue to express...risks remained weighted toward rising inflation."
- Nuanced differences on inflation outlook described as within-consensus.

**Observations**: Gap of 14 points. The transcript captures the internal struggle more directly — Broaddus's explicit preference to hike today, Meyer's "struggle," and the visible NAIRU debate give a fuller picture of near-dissent tension. The minutes smooths these over with "all the members agreed" language, yielding a much lower dissent reading.

### 8. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (Discussion sources only)

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 63**

**Observations**: Small gap (2 points), near-identical reading. Both confirm a mild-to-moderate tightening bias in the forward signal. The transcript's Kelley quote ("perhaps quite soon and perhaps by a considerable amount") is the most hawkish individual statement; the minutes' third bullet is the most dovish qualifier.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 48**

- "Indications of slowing economic expansion were still tentative."
- "The uncertainties surrounding the outlook for the economy, notably the extent and duration of the recent moderation in spending."

**Observations**: Small gap (4 points). Both reflect elevated uncertainty about the durability of the slowdown. The transcript's richer individual expressions (Greenspan's two-scenario framing, Kelley's colorful litany) push the score slightly higher than the minutes' more formal "substantial uncertainty" summary.

### 10. PRODUCTIVITY (All four sources)

**Discussion Transcript — Score: 72**

- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "no evidence of which I am aware that suggests any significant deterioration in productivity growth...multifactor productivity has been running at a fairly strong pace and, if anything, has been rising."
- MR. MOSKOW: Directors reported "definite acceleration in productivity in their industries over the past three years."
- MS. MINEHAN and MR. PARRY reference accelerating structural productivity.

**Discussion Minutes — Score: 68**

- "Rising productivity growth had contained labor cost pressures."
- "Despite the moderation in the expansion of activity, there were no early signs of any slowing in the growth of productivity."
- "Rising productivity was likely to continue to restrain unit labor costs."

**Staff Transcript — Score: 65**

- MR. STOCKTON: "upward productivity growth on 'equilibrium' [interest rate]" raised the equilibrium rate.
- MR. STOCKTON: Near-term, "productivity likely to slow" cyclically as demand outpaces supply; but structural level remains elevated.
- District evidence: Directors report "definite acceleration in productivity in their industries over the past three years as a result of investment in information processing equipment."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 62**

- Staff forecast: labor compensation "that would not be fully offset by productivity growth" — implying productivity growth is strong but insufficient to fully absorb wage pressures.
- Implicit treatment of productivity as above-trend, consistent with late-1990s productivity boom.

**Observations**: Descending gradient from discussion transcript (72) to discussion minutes (68) to staff transcript (65) to staff minutes (62). The discussion participants, especially Greenspan and Moskow, make the most emphatic positive claims about productivity. The staff framing is more cautious — the staff transcript notes the near-term cyclical deceleration in productivity (which drives the cost acceleration story), and the minutes further mutes the signal.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH (Staff sources only)

**Staff Transcript — Score: 62**

- MS. JOHNSON: "the average foreign exchange value of the dollar in terms of the currencies of the other major industrial countries...has risen on balance so far this year, although it has most recently given back some of that appreciation."
- MS. JOHNSON: "Through May, the dollar continued its upward trend in terms of the euro." Against yen: "the dollar today is little changed from where it started the year."
- Staff projects real dollar depreciation over the forecast period, but current reading is appreciation.

**Staff Minutes — Score: 42**

- "The trade-weighted value of the dollar depreciated somewhat over the intermeeting period against an index of major currencies."
- "By contrast, the dollar strengthened a little against the currencies of a group of other important trading partners."

**Observations**: Notable divergence of 20 points. The staff transcript (Johnson's briefing) describes the year-to-date picture: dollar has risen against the euro and is flat against yen — net appreciation. The staff minutes describes the intermeeting period only: modest depreciation against major currencies over the weeks between meetings. These are different time windows. The transcript gives the cumulative YTD reading; the minutes gives the short-term intermeeting change. This explains the apparent discrepancy rather than indicating a genuine disagreement.

### 12. EQUITY_VALUATION (Staff sources only)

**Staff Transcript — Score: 28**

- District go-around: "the days of irrational exuberance are over and now we just have exuberance."
- MR. STOCKTON: "the resilience [of equities]...damps considerably the restraining effects that would be expected to accompany a substantial run-up in the funds rate."

**Staff Minutes — Score: 48**

- "Sharp further declines in equity prices...Subsequently, debt and equity markets rebounded...most broad stock price indexes ended the period little changed."
- Staff forecast: "anticipated waning of positive wealth effects associated with earlier large gains in equity prices."

**Observations**: Gap of 20 points. Staff transcript explicitly benchmarks current equity levels against model predictions (above by the 13% miss) and uses the "exuberance" characterization, generating a lower (more-elevated-valuation) score. Staff minutes focuses on the intermeeting price action (sharp drop then rebound to flat) with no explicit valuation benchmark, yielding a near-neutral score. The transcript's richer analytical commentary on valuation levels drives the divergence.
