### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 46 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: A few participants (Plosser, Kocherlakota) note core inflation running at or slightly above 2% currently, but the clear majority expects inflation to subside below target over the medium term amid slack and falling commodity prices, leaving the center of gravity just slightly below the 50 midpoint.

Representative quotes:
- Plosser: "Core PCE inflation ran a bit more than 2 percent in the second quarter... we've now had three months of upward revisions in prices for core goods and services... we are also seeing rising inflation."
- Evans: "Recent developments also suggest that inflation risks over the medium term continue to recede. I continue to believe that inflation over the medium term will underrun our mandate-consistent inflation objective."
- Bernanke (summary): "oil down almost 30 percent since its peak in April... inflation expectations apparently reasonably stable. It doesn't seem that high inflation is a near-term concern."

Minority views: Plosser and Kocherlakota each argue that inflation has been surprising to the upside relative to forecast and deserves more weight.

---

**Minutes: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see inflation as recently somewhat elevated but moderating, with transitory factors waning. Net assessment is slightly above the balanced midpoint given the recent rise in core inflation.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation had moderated in recent months after having been somewhat elevated earlier this year."
- "With apparently significant slack in labor and product markets, slow wage growth, and little evidence of pricing power among firms, inflation was likely to decline somewhat over time."
- "A number of participants noted that core inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall."

Minority views: "A couple of others...suggested that the juxtaposition of higher core inflation and somewhat lower unemployment could imply that the level of potential output was lower than had been thought" — implying more persistent inflation pressure.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous view that the economy is well below potential, with a substantial minority raising stall-speed/recession concerns; even the more optimistic participants acknowledge the outlook has materially worsened.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "the data we have received since June revealed broad-based weakness... the latest data indicate that the U.S. economy has been running alarmingly close to stall speed over recent months"; Markov model shows >60% probability of stall-speed or recession.
- Tarullo: "the debate today is whether even a weak and halting recovery can continue or whether we're already at or below stall speed and quite possibly slipping into another recession."
- Bernanke (summary): "Many saw the risk to the recovery as now to the downside, and some worry that the economy might be near stall speed."

Minority views: Plosser and Bullard argue the GDP revisions may reflect lower potential growth rather than a wider output gap.

---

**Minutes: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is clearly operating well below potential, with significant slack and a deteriorating trajectory.

Representative quotes:
- "Economic growth so far this year was considerably slower than they had expected."
- "Apparently significant slack in labor and product markets."
- "Many participants pointed to the recent downward revision to estimates of economic activity over the past three years."

Minority views: Some noted potential output itself may be lower, which would narrow the gap somewhat.

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 9%, employment-to-population at 30-year lows, 4.6 unemployed per job opening — every participant acknowledges the labor market is deeply slack with no meaningful near-term improvement expected.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "the employment-to-population ratio the lowest since 1983."
- Raskin: "the share of the working-age population that is now employed stands at 58.1 percent, the lowest level since 1983... there are 4.6 unemployed workers for every job opening."
- Evans: "9 percent unemployment... a 9 percent unemployment policy loss is equivalent to an inflation crisis on the order of 5 percent."

Minority views: Lacker presents research suggesting long-term unemployment reflects structural/heterogeneity factors rather than cyclical demand.

---

**Minutes: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Significant labor market slack with deterioration. Well below full employment.

Representative quotes:
- "Overall labor market conditions had deteriorated in recent months."
- "Exceptionally high level of long-term unemployment."
- "The employment report for July...was still seen as indicating relatively weak conditions."

Minority views: All see weakness; disagreements are about causes (skills mismatch vs. cyclical).

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending was contracting in monthly terms — three consecutive PCE declines — with business investment cautious. Multiple participants describe demand as broadly weak across income groups and business sizes.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "real personal consumption expenditures have declined in each of the past three monthly readings... a pattern of three consecutive monthly declines in real PCE has occurred only on a handful of occasions over the postwar period — [all associated with recessions]."
- Fisher: "mall traffic... dropped to zero from a pace in the first quarter of 3 percent. Off-mall traffic has dropped to 2.6 percent over the past three weeks, largely owing to sales of food."
- Pianalto: "the key source of restraint on the pace of GDP growth is consumption. Household spending is likely to remain soft while income growth is limited and households continue to repair their balance sheets."

Minority views: Auto production recovery and some pockets of corporate strength (large firms) support a modest floor.

---

**Minutes: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending unexpectedly weak, business investment slowing, housing subdued. Below trend across the board.

Representative quotes:
- "Most meeting participants indicated that the weakness in consumer spending in recent months was unexpected."
- "Business outlays on equipment and software continued to advance, although at a slower pace."
- "Housing construction was quite subdued."

Minority views: Some expected auto sales to pick up as supply chain disruptions eased.

---

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 58 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Conditions are moderately tighter on net — equity market stress, bank funding pressures, and mortgage market tightness — partially offset by sharply lower Treasury yields in a flight-to-safety environment. The committee's own deletion of the "more restrictive" language from the statement reflects genuine ambiguity about the net direction.

Representative quotes:
- Dudley: "stocks down, credit spreads wider, flight into safe-haven currencies... bank stocks falling very, very sharply and CDS spreads widening... indicators of dollar funding problems, such as the three-month currency swap rate, indicate an increase in dollar funding problems for European banks."
- Duke: "standards for first mortgages and home equity loans are extremely tight. Outstandings are steadily declining, and delinquency rates remain stubbornly high."
- Lacker/Lockhart/Fisher: explicitly contested the "financial conditions have become more restrictive" statement, noting Treasury rates and mortgage rates had actually fallen.

Minority views: The three hawks correctly note that broad interest-rate-based measures of financial conditions loosened, creating significant uncertainty.

---

**Minutes: 58 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions somewhat tight — funding markets tightened, credit still constrained, volatility elevated. Banks strengthening and credit quality improving provide partial offset.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial markets were volatile over the intermeeting period."
- "Conditions in funding markets had tightened."
- "Several participants also pointed to financial constraints, particularly depressed home prices and still-tight credit conditions."

Minority views: "The condition of U.S. banks had strengthened in recent quarters and...credit quality of both businesses and households had continued to improve."

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 18 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: A strong majority (~11 of 16 participants) supported adopting dovish forward guidance specifying low rates through mid-2013, with several wanting additional tools (Operation Twist, IOER cut, balance sheet floor). Three formal dissents objected even to this minimal step.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "I consider the case for policy action compelling... our arsenal is not empty, and we should avoid any inclination to save our ammunition... I strongly support your proposal to substitute the language related to 'extended period' proposed in paragraph 3 of alternative A for that in alternative B."
- Bernanke: "This is the minimum we should do if we want to avoid conveying essentially insouciance in the face of what is clearly a deteriorating situation."
- Plosser (dissenter): "I recommend taking no action today... I'm very dubious that even more accommodative monetary policy is likely to be a potent tool."

Minority views: Fisher, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Lacker all opposed the 2013 guidance.

---

**Minutes: 25 ±12 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Clearly dovish — majority favored additional accommodation via strengthened forward guidance. QE-era meeting focused on tools for further easing.

Representative quotes:
- "Most members agreed that the economic outlook had deteriorated by enough to warrant a Committee response."
- "Some members expressed the view that additional accommodation was warranted."
- "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move."
- "Three members dissented because they preferred to retain the forward guidance language."

Minority views: Three dissenters opposed the dovish shift; "some participants judged that none of the tools available...would likely do much to promote a faster economic recovery."

---

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 65 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Three formal dissents emerged alongside a protracted policy debate with sharp exchanges over the wisdom of calendar-date forward guidance, the effectiveness of monetary policy, and structural vs. cyclical interpretations of unemployment — reflecting significant ideological division within the Committee.

Representative quotes:
- Three confirmed dissents: Fisher ("I cannot support that"), Kocherlakota ("I would not be able to support the alternative, even as rewritten"), and Plosser ("I'm inclined to, unless I hear something different").
- Bullard: "I do not think we would be wise to tie policy action to the calendar... going with the 2013 language will put us in a box that we may not want to be in going forward."
- Bernanke: "It would be very unpleasant to have three dissents, but I guess if that's where we end up, that's where we end up."

Minority views: Whether Bullard and Lacker formally dissented remains unclear from the transcript text.

---

**Minutes: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: High disagreement — three formal dissents (rare), plus a faction wanting even stronger action. Clear camps with different policy prescriptions.

Representative quotes:
- "Three members dissented because they preferred to retain the forward guidance language employed in the June statement."
- "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move."
- "Some participants judged that none of the tools available to the Committee would likely do much."

Minority views: The three dissenters represent a significant hawkish minority. The "more substantial move" faction represents a dovish minority.

---

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 65 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability received prominent, extended treatment from several participants (Dudley, Duke, Rosengren in particular) and was referenced by most others in passing, reflecting concerns about both domestic bank stress and European contagion; however, the economic weakness discussion occupied roughly equal weight.

Representative quotes:
- Dudley: extended discussion of bank stock declines, CDS spreads widening, European bank dollar-funding pressure, broker-dealer risk, potential credit rating cascade.
- Duke: full detailed discussion of banking system health — credit metrics, loan demand, profitability challenges, BNY Mellon negative deposit rates.
- Rosengren: "Bank of America shares yesterday closed at under $7 a share... Their credit default swap rate was over 200... there remains a possibility that the next financial shock could still be generated by a large, troubled domestic bank."

Minority views: Some participants (Tarullo, Lacker) devoted little discussion specifically to financial stability conditions.

---

**Minutes: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate discussion of financial conditions — market volatility, European risks, and funding market tightening discussed by several participants, but not the dominant theme.

Representative quotes:
- "Financial markets were volatile over the intermeeting period, as investors responded to news on the European fiscal situation and the negotiations regarding the debt ceiling."
- "Broad declines in stock prices and interest rates."
- "Potential financial and economic spillovers if the situation in Europe were to deteriorate."

Minority views: N/A.

---

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 8 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The adopted forward guidance explicitly commits (conditionally) to near-zero rates through at least mid-2013 — a strong dovish forward signal with a specific calendar date, placing this firmly in the "strong easing bias with near-date-based commitment" zone.

Representative quotes:
- Statement adopted: "Based on its current projections, the Committee anticipates that economic conditions — including low rates of resource utilization and a subdued outlook for inflation over the medium run — are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- Bernanke: "I think that doing that would be encouraging... it would cut off, or at least reduce, the probability of a near-term tightening."
- Evans: "I certainly support that [the 2013 language]... I suspect that we would have ended up there anyway. So we should reap the benefit of reducing the uncertainty."

Minority views: Bullard and Plosser argue this guidance will have no effect since it merely matches market expectations.

---

**Minutes: 5 ±3 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Explicit date-based forward guidance committing to near-zero rates for nearly two more years — the strongest form of easing guidance short of threshold-based rules.

Representative quotes:
- "The Committee agreed...to state that economic conditions are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- "Reinforcing the Committee's forward guidance about the likely path of monetary policy was seen as a possible way to reduce interest rates."

Minority views: "A few participants emphasized that guidance focusing solely on the state of the economy would be preferable to guidance that named specific spans of time."

---

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 70 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Multiple participants independently emphasize that uncertainty is unusually high even by typical FOMC standards — driven by the combination of stall-speed concerns, European contagion risk, fundamental disagreement about structural vs. cyclical forces, and the difficulty of measuring the output gap after major NIPA revisions.

Representative quotes:
- Lockhart: "there's simply a lot that we don't know at the moment... We don't know if the growth-restraining effects of the first half will fall away... We don't know whether the consensus base-case forecast will materialize... We really don't know how the European debt situation is going to play out."
- Bernanke (summary): "Uncertainty about the forecast may be, really and truly in this case, higher than usual."
- Lacker: "we find ourselves in uncharted waters, and that's a phrase we've been using for four years now. But we've again veered farther away from the charts we have."

Minority views: Plosser and Lacker express unusual clarity about one thing: they believe monetary policy cannot help with structural problems.

---

**Minutes: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty — participants explicitly noted it had risen "appreciably," with many seeing increased downside risks. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (fiscal, European, household conditions).

Representative quotes:
- "The degree of uncertainty surrounding the outlook for economic growth as having risen appreciably."
- "Many participants also saw an increase in the downside risks to economic growth."
- "The underlying strength of the economic recovery remained uncertain."

Minority views: Narrow — broad agreement on elevated uncertainty.
