### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "Core PCE inflation for the past year is 1.4 percent, significantly below the previous year and well below where most of us expect the inflation rate to settle in the longer run." — Rosengren

> "The basic empirical Phillips curve model predicts substantial further disinflation." — Yellen

> "Core services inflation has come down a lot; core goods inflation seems likely to follow, given the large amount of excess capacity available…we're already below a rate of core inflation consistent with our longer-term forecast." — Dudley

**Minority (transcript):** "I see only upside risks to inflation relative to the latest Greenbook forecast." — Plosser; Lacker emphasizes market expectations of higher rates are "closer to the mark" on inflation.

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** The majority of participants perceive ongoing disinflation with core PCE at 1.4%, clearly below the implicit 2% target, with most expecting further decline; a minority (Plosser, Lacker, Bullard partially) flags upside risks via inflation expectations and balance-sheet channels.

---

**Minutes: 24 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years, leading to subdued and potentially declining wage and price inflation."

> "A few continued to see some risk of substantial further disinflation, but that risk had eased somewhat further over the intermeeting period."

> "Many participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlook over the next few quarters as being roughly balanced."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Inflation well below target with substantial slack expected to keep prices subdued, but active deflation fears have faded somewhat.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "Business caution, together with low utilization rates and substantial excess capacity, suggest that many firms may be slow to add workers and make new capital investments." — Bernanke synthesis

> "Our own measure at the Dallas Fed of global capacity utilization shows record lows." — Fisher

> "This tepid growth is from the bottom of a very deep recession and leaves the unemployment rate remaining above 9 percent during this phase of the recovery." — Evans

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** All participants agree the economy is operating far below potential — the recovery has just begun after a deep recession; slack is described as "substantial" by nearly every participant; the output gap is measured at between -3% (Plosser's DSGE) and -6% or -7% (Greenbook), both well below potential.

---

**Minutes: 24 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years."

> "The economy was projected to expand over the remainder of 2009 and during 2010, but at a pace that was unlikely to reduce the unemployment rate appreciably."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Economy far below potential with substantial slack expected to persist for years. Recovery has begun but is "quite restrained."

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 18 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "The employment situation continues to be dismal…To say that we are nowhere near achieving the maximum employment component of our dual mandate would be a major understatement." — Tarullo

> "Payrolls are still falling, and the unemployment rate is still rising." — Dudley

> "Qualified job applicants are plentiful, that voluntary quits have plummeted, and that workers are thankful to have jobs and are readily accepting lower wage increases and even wage cuts." — Yellen

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Near-unanimous view of a severely weak labor market at or near 10% unemployment, with layoffs still ongoing, no indication of firms anticipating hiring, and wage growth collapsing; labor market is close to a cyclical bottom but still far from maximum employment.

---

**Minutes: 20 ±4 [High confidence]**

> "Labor market conditions remained weak."

> "Job losses remained sizable and the unemployment rate was high."

> "The unusually large fraction of those who were working part time for economic reasons, the unusually low level of the average workweek...all pointed to a period of modest job gains, and thus only a slow decline in the unemployment rate."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Very weak labor market with high unemployment, high underemployment, and only a slow improvement expected. Firms will be slow to hire.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 33 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "General merchandise retailers reported continuing restrained consumer demand. Transport firms reported very little in the way of increased activity." — Lockhart

> "Retail sales remain anemic…I can't find a single sector where a leading CEO is budgeting to do anything other than to contain costs." — Fisher

> "Retail sales and consumer spending have picked up a bit, in part reflecting special factors, such as the 'cash-for-clunkers' program…especially weak labor markets, tight credit, and the slowing of the benefits of the stimulus package seem likely to restrain consumption growth." — Bernanke synthesis

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Consumer spending is below trend but has stabilized and shown modest improvement driven largely by government programs; nonresidential investment is contracting sharply; residential investment is showing nascent improvement from very depressed levels; business investment intentions remain frozen; net position is below trend but recovering from near-collapse earlier in 2009.

---

**Minutes: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Consumer spending had picked up more than expected over the intermeeting period, but participants saw that increase as partly reflecting special factors like the cash-for-clunkers program."

> "Participants generally expected no more than moderate growth in consumer spending over the near term. Households still faced considerable headwinds."

> "Firms appeared to be reducing inventories and fixed investment at a slower pace than earlier in the year." Business contacts "remained cautious about the recovery."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Spending and investment still below trend. Consumer spending picking up but driven by temporary factors; business investment still contracting, though more slowly. Significant headwinds persist.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "bank credit to households and small businesses remains very tight, and, if anything, tightened further over the third quarter…the spreads on business loan rates over base rates in the Survey of Terms of Business Lending, and the problems perceived by small businesses in the NFIB survey." — Kohn

> "Banks report total unused commitments are down $1.5 trillion from the peak." — Duke

> "Bank credit remains tight. Bank loans to smaller businesses, households, and commercial real estate, in particular, have fallen dramatically." — Bernanke synthesis

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Financial conditions are a tale of two markets — securities markets (equities, credit spreads) have improved substantially since the crisis, but banking credit is persistently tight and tightening further; the net position is somewhat tight, driven primarily by the banking sector; not a crisis-level tightness but clearly above neutral.

---

**Minutes: 64 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "Credit from banks remained difficult to obtain and costly for many borrowers."

> "Credit remained quite tight for many businesses and households dependent on banks, and many regional and small banks were vulnerable."

> "Financial market conditions had continued to improve over the past several months." "Markets for corporate debt continued to improve, private credit spreads narrowed further."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Financial conditions still tight overall, especially for bank-dependent borrowers, but improving significantly from crisis peaks. Capital markets recovery contrasts with persistent bank credit tightness.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 18 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "I support alternative B…we should purchase the entire $1¼ trillion in mortgage-backed securities…I have a strong preference not to end the program prematurely." — Rosengren

> "I am pretty sure that, if it were possible, we would at this point have taken the federal funds rate into negative territory." — Yellen

> Straw vote outcome: 10 participants voted to DROP "up to" (commit firmly to full $1.25T MBS), 5 voted to retain it; Hoenig was the only advocate of Alt C (scaling back purchases).

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Overwhelming majority committed to completing $1.25 trillion MBS program, maintaining zero rates for "extended period," and explicitly rejecting any near-term policy tightening; even the more hawkish voices (Plosser, Lacker) explicitly stated they had no near-term inflation fears and were focused on communication/forward guidance rather than immediate action.

---

**Minutes: 20 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

> "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Firmly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation with no near-term tightening considered. Exit strategy discussion is about communication/credibility, not imminent action.

---

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "It seems that this is the most disparate set of commentary I've heard on policy alternatives." — Evans

> "Participants differed on the fundamental determinants of inflation and consequently on their forecasts." — Bernanke

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** Substantial and genuine disagreement extends to the economic assessment (inflation determinants) and tactical policy choices (MBS program completion), but NOT to the fundamental stance — all participants accepted zero rates as appropriate, none called for immediate hikes; the debate is about degree of accommodation and future flexibility rather than directional disagreement.

---

**Minutes: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

On financial conditions: divergent views between those seeing a positive feedback loop and those emphasizing remaining strains. On housing: "many" marked up forecasts while "some viewed the improvement as quite tentative."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Broad agreement on the overall picture with moderate differences on risk emphasis and nuances. No formal dissents noted.

**Minutes: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Financial stability/conditions are a moderately prominent topic. Multiple participants engage substantively with financial market conditions, but economic conditions (output, labor, spending) receive comparable or greater emphasis.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "I think it will be at least an extended period—whatever that is—before we have to do that [raise rates]. So one implication is that I don't think we need to tighten in any way, shape, or form in response to the better outlook." — Kohn

> Yellen proposed replacing vague language with "The Committee anticipates that low resource utilization and subdued inflation are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels…" — explicitly signaling rates held down by macro conditions, not a calendar.

> "comparing where the market is in terms of the timing of raising the federal funds rate and where the Greenbook is, there's a pretty big gap. So…we're probably going to go slower than the market expects." — Dudley

**Center-of-gravity (transcript):** The Committee's forward signal is unambiguously dovish — "extended period" language retained for zero rates, full $1.25 trillion MBS program committed, tapering only to execute existing purchases (not to reduce total accommodation), and majority explicitly arguing that market expectations of rate hikes are too early.

---

**Minutes: 17 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

> "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace to prevent any persistent increase in inflation."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Forward signal is dovish — continued accommodation expected for an extended period. The exit strategy discussion is about credibility and communication, not signaling near-term tightening.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 62 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "People who hang around central banking and financial markets really don't know what to think about the unconventional policies…informed opinion is all over the map…some people think there might be a lot of deflation, and some people think there might be a lot of inflation." — Bullard

---

> "Households' saving behavior remained an important source of uncertainty in the outlook."

**Center-of-gravity (minutes):** Elevated uncertainty. Participants are more confident a recovery is under way than at earlier meetings, but "considerable uncertainty" remains about its strength and sustainability.
