### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Scores: Disc. Transcript 57 | Disc. Minutes 58 | Staff Transcript 57 | Staff Minutes 57**

**Discussion Transcript:**
- MR. LACKER: "2 percent is the upper limit of my own comfort zone for core PCE inflation. Moreover, market participants are aware that the core PCE index is the Fed's preferred inflation measure. And if we allow it to drift much above 2 percent, we run the risk of unhinging longer-run inflation expectations."
- MS. PIANALTO: "I know that I have consistently underestimated inflation. And now we're facing oil prices of close to $60 per barrel and there is a risk, I believe, that unit labor costs could trend up."
- MR. FISHER (minority): "soundings in our District and nationally indicate no pricing power. And I detect a slight, but noticeable, shift among the people we speak with toward concerns about slower growth and away from concerns about inflation."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "risks are clearly balanced with respect to the outlook for both output and inflation."

**Discussion Minutes:**
- "Core consumer price inflation had been higher than most participants had expected at the start of the year, reflecting, at least in part, the pass-through effects of higher energy, commodity, and import prices."
- "Some participants expressed concern that, with policy still accommodative, the underlying pace of inflation might be in the process of stepping up, perhaps to a level that was at the upper end of the range that they viewed as compatible with the Committee's price stability objective."
- "Other meeting participants emphasized that recent core inflation data had been relatively restrained, and anecdotal reports suggested that pricing power at many firms remained quite limited."

**Staff Transcript:**
- MR. WILCOX: "we project core PCE prices to rise a bit more than 2 percent this year and a tad less than 2 percent in 2006, up from the 1½ percent increase recorded last year."
- MR. WILCOX: "the current Greenbook calls for core PCE inflation to come in this year at 2.1 percent" with forecast for 2006 dropping to ~1¾%.
- Staff views the impetus from energy and import prices as transitory; compensation-led spiral not the base case.

**Staff Minutes:**
- "measures of core consumer price inflation over the past year were somewhat above those for the comparable period a year ago"
- "The forecast for consumer price inflation was revised up, with inflation seen as somewhat higher this year than in 2004, reflecting in part higher import prices and the direct and indirect effects of higher energy prices."

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Scores: Disc. Transcript 54 | Disc. Minutes 52 | Staff Transcript 48 | Staff Minutes 53**

**Discussion Transcript:**
- MR. HOENIG: "We agree with the consensus of GDP growth running about 3½ percent, slightly over our potential rate."
- VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER: "growth slightly above trend from a starting point where the remaining amount of resource slack, if any, is substantially diminished."
- MS. YELLEN: "Real GDP appears poised to grow just slightly above its potential rate, gradually eliminating remaining slack."
- MR. KOHN: "prospects for growth... close to the growth rate of potential, perhaps a bit above" and "output gap about disappearing."

**Discussion Minutes:**
- "Many meeting participants noted that incoming data had been reassuring about the strength of the expansion."
- "Margins of slack resources narrowing and inflation somewhat higher."
- GDP forecasts in the 3 to 3-3/4 percent range, with members expecting output "near potential."

**Staff Transcript:**
- MR. WILCOX: "the balance of evidence as suggesting that a little slack remains in the labor market" and "total hours worked remain a little below the estimated trend."
- MR. WILCOX: "the output gap closes only a little [throughout the projection period]" and unemployment is "currently just a tenth of a percentage point above our estimate of the NAIRU."
- Real GDP projected at ~3½% — slightly above potential — but output gap remains marginally negative throughout.

**Staff Minutes:**
- "In the staff forecast prepared for this meeting, the economy was seen as likely to expand this year and next at a rate just above its potential."
- "the economy was expanding at a moderate pace in the second quarter."

*Note: The staff transcript and minutes diverge slightly from the discussion sources. Staff transcript scores 48 (a little slack remains) while the discussion transcript scores 54 (participants emphasize "above trend" language). The staff minutes score 53 based on the "just above potential" rate language. This is the largest cross-source gap in this meeting.*

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Scores: Disc. Transcript 53 | Disc. Minutes 50 | Staff Transcript 50 | Staff Minutes 51**

**Discussion Transcript:**
- MR. SANTOMERO: "Business contacts continue to report that it is difficult to find employees to fill openings. One of our contacts says the labor shortage in southern New Jersey has reached crisis proportions."
- MS. YELLEN: "the unemployment rate is relatively low, near most estimates of NAIRU. But several other measures... do suggest that slack remains."
- MR. POOLE: "labor supply of hourly workers is plentiful... a new Wal-Mart store opening in San Diego, where there were 10,000 applicants for 400 jobs."

**Discussion Minutes:**
- "Some limited remaining slack in labor markets likely damping growth in compensation."
- "Anecdotal reports of labor market conditions continued to point to shortages of labor only for certain, mostly skilled, occupations."
- Unemployment at 5.1%, forecasts of 5–5.25% for Q4 2005 (near estimated NAIRU).

**Staff Transcript:**
- MR. WILCOX: "the unemployment rate is currently just a tenth of a percentage point above our estimate of the NAIRU."
- MR. WILCOX: "the margin [of slack] is narrow, and narrower today than it was six months or a year ago. Moreover, such slack as may still exist will, on our projection, essentially be taken up over the course of the next year and a half."

**Staff Minutes:**
- "The unemployment rate dipped to 5.1 percent in May."
- "the labor force participation rate moved up a bit, suggesting that the labor market had strengthened enough to attract some individuals back into the workforce."
- "Labor demand continued to expand."

Discussion sources score above 50 because participants predominantly use "at or above potential" phrasing. Difference likely reflects participants and staff using the same data but characterizing the output gap direction differently.

The broader range here reflects different emphases: discussion minutes capture the formal statement language ("accommodative"); staff transcript notes some tightening underway from the FF rate path; discussion transcript focuses on the loose mortgage and risk-appetite environment.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68/55. The transcript-vs-minutes gap here reflects the structural difference between a full go-around (with multiple speakers devoting extended remarks) and a synthesized summary. Not a substantive disagreement about facts.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65/71. Minutes capture the formal statement's explicit tightening signal; transcript reflects the internal discussion where participants characterize the statement as "close to neutral" and approaching a pause.

**Notable divergence (gap > 15 points):**
- INFLATION_HOUSING: 75/62/60/67. The discussion transcript score (75) is driven by specific speaker quotes with precise figures (Florida prices +25-30%, national +8-10%). The other sources produce lower scores because they present the same facts in summarized form without the vivid regional examples.
