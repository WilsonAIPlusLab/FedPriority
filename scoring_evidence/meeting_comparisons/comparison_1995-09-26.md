## Part A: Discussion — All 10 Dimensions

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **48 ±6 [High]** | **52 ±7 [Moderate]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "CPI inflation at 3 percent or slightly less in 1995, 1996, and 1997--somewhat above last year's 2.6 percent rate."
- MS. MINEHAN: "many business executives... continue to report an inability to raise prices even in the face of rising demand. Competition remains too keen."
- MR. HOENIG: "I question whether the overall core inflation rate will drop much in the next year."
- MR. BLINDER: "core CPI inflation reads 3.1, 2.8, and 2.7 percent."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Participants view inflation as roughly contained near 3% CPI -- present but not accelerating, with little prospect of meaningful further decline.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "the slower increases in key measures of consumer and producer prices since earlier in the year were a welcome development"
- "many expressed concern, however, that significant further progress toward achieving stable prices might not be made over the next year or two"
- "the labor market remained tight and capacity utilization was still above its historical average"
- "a few members commented that current forecasts were subject to a range of error that included a risk of some intensification of inflationary pressures"

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Inflation has moderated from earlier in the year but remains at a level that most members do not expect to decline further; the labor market and capacity utilization keep latent pressure alive.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **49 ±5 [High]** | **50 ±5 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "The unemployment and capacity utilization rates indicate that it is unlikely that there currently is any excess capacity pushing down on inflation."
- MR. BROADDUS: "we are operating at close to full capacity in many industries and sectors."
- MR. BLINDER: Growth rates of "2.1, 2.2, and 2.0 percent... all below the growth rate of potential."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: The economy is roughly at potential following the Q2 inventory correction, but growth is running at or slightly below trend, meaning the output gap is near zero with a risk of opening slightly negative.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "Growth at a pace averaging close to, or perhaps slightly below, the economy's potential was viewed as the most likely prospect for the year ahead"
- "the economy...appeared on balance to have considerable and desirable expansionary momentum"
- "many members saw the risks of a shortfall from expectations as slightly greater than those of significantly faster growth"

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Members explicitly describe the economy as growing near potential -- the quintessential at-potential characterization. The slight downside risk bias keeps the score right at or marginally below 50.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **54 ±7 [High]** | **56 ±5 [Moderate]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "District labor markets remain tight and wage pressures continue to intensify at the low end of the wage scale... average unemployment rates in the five District states were 4.6 percent."
- MR. JORDAN: Director reported "he could not hire anybody. His firm runs ads and nobody applies."
- MR. STERN: "Labor markets are tight. The rate of unemployment in Minnesota is at or near an all-time low."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Labor markets are moderately tight at the national level (5.6% unemployment, near NAIRU) with notably tighter conditions in several districts. Wage pressure is emerging in specific segments but is not yet broad-based.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "the labor market remained tight"
- "the rise in worker compensation had been unusually restrained in recent years in relation to the strong demand for workers"
- "continued strength in the demand for labor might be expected to induce more rapid increases in labor compensation over time"
- "still widespread concerns about job security associated with ongoing business restructuring and downsizing activities"

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: The labor market is described as tight with strong demand for workers, but wage pressures are unusually restrained due to worker job-security fears and restructuring.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Consumer spending moderate, business investment strong, housing picking up, and the inventory drag fading. Net assessment is slightly above trend -- solid but not booming.

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Very limited direct participant discussion of financial conditions. Low confidence due to minimal direct evidence.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **44 ±8 [High]** | **46 ±6 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would suggest that it be done with the Committee's concurrence... I put on the table 'B' symmetrical." Also: "I think it is correct to argue that we are probably at a real funds rate level that is higher than the long-term equilibrium."
- MS. YELLEN: "We have what Charles Schultz dubbed a 'termites in the basement' problem... the real funds rate would need to decline."
- MR. MELZER: "I view policy as being somewhat restrictive, and I think that is quite appropriate... I would be very careful not to ease before we saw some evidence" of declining inflation expectations.
- MR. BOEHNE: "Almost surely we will have to adjust policy at some point in coming months."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Unanimous support for holding rates steady, but the majority lean toward eventual easing. A significant minority (Melzer, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Kelley) favors the current restrictive stance as appropriate. The net lean is mildly dovish -- hold now with an easing bias.

**Minutes evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure on reserve positions"
- "several members expressed the opinion that policy might have to be eased eventually in light of the downside risks"
- "Other members...placed more emphasis on current forecasts of little or no progress in reducing inflation...thought it would be premature to ease"
- "the direction of the next policy move was not clear in the view of some members"

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Unanimous hold with no directive bias. The committee is split between those leaning toward eventual easing and those wanting to hold for inflation reasons. Center of gravity is balanced-to-mildly-dovish.

**Minutes evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Unanimous vote with symmetric directive, but underlying disagreement about future direction. Broad agreement on current action with notable differences about future direction.
