### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants perceive headline inflation materially above target (PCE near 3%, core near 2.25%), above prior expectations, but the majority view current pressures as temporary/transitory with a modal path toward moderation — creating tension between the elevated current reading and the expected future path.

Quotes:
- Lacker: "Core inflation is 2¼ percent over the last three months and the three months before that, and survey measures of inflation are ticking up... I do not think we now want or need more monetary stimulus."
- Bernanke (chairman's summary): "Inflation has not yet come down by as much as expected. Core inflation has picked up, and there is some uncertainty about whether that increase is temporary or not. Most saw inflation as likely to move toward mandate-consistent levels in the medium term, with some seeing approximately balanced risks, but others now seeing risks to the upside."
- Plosser: "While we are seeing weak activity, 12-month inflation rates continue to accelerate. It's a quite different situation than we faced in the fall of 2010."

Minority views: Lacker, Kocherlakota, Plosser, Fisher see inflation as a more serious ongoing concern (would push toward 62–65); Yellen, Williams, Raskin, Tarullo view current readings as transitory and inflation risks as subordinate to growth/employment risks (would push toward 54–56).

---

**Minutes: 43 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Inflation moderating but not as fast as expected; most see it settling at or below mandate-consistent levels, but the upward surprise in core tempers the assessment. Slightly below the neutral midpoint.

Quotes:
- "Inflation appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year as prices of energy and some commodities declined from their peaks, but inflation had not yet come down as much as participants had expected earlier this year."
- "Most participants anticipated that, with stable inflation expectations, significant slack in labor and product markets, slow wage growth, and little evidence of pricing power among firms, inflation was likely to decline moderately over time."
- "Some noted that core as well as headline inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall."

Minority views: "Some" saw upside inflation risks from accommodative policy. "A few" suggested less slack than thought, given juxtaposition of higher core inflation and lower unemployment.

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly agree the economy is operating significantly below potential, with growth near stall speed, unemployment at 9.1%, and meaningful recession risk — a well-below-potential assessment consistent with the 31–40 range.

Quotes:
- Rosengren: "The economy has been growing below potential, and I fear that even without a European crisis, the threat of a crisis and the underlying challenges in housing, state and local governments, and the labor market have sapped the confidence of consumers and businesses."
- Yellen: "real PCE has advanced at an annual rate of only 0.4 percent, a truly glacial pace... I have become increasingly concerned that the economy is near stall speed and could easily slip into recession."
- Williams: "I lowered my GDP growth forecast for both 2011 and 2012 by 2 full percentage points" from prior forecasts; recession probability cited at 35 percent when European data included.

Minority views: Lockhart presents devil's advocate case for why conditions may not be as bad as feared; Kocherlakota argues supply-side damage may reduce the true gap; Lacker sees the economy growing slowly but not contracting. None disputes that output is below potential.

---

**Minutes: 34 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy well below potential with persistent slack, slow growth, and significant downside risks. Consistent with 2011 recovery-era slack.

Quotes:
- "Economic growth remained slow but did not suggest a contraction in activity."
- "Significant slack in labor and product markets" and "low rates of resource utilization."
- "Many participants saw significant downside risks to economic growth."

Minority views: Limited disagreement on the direction — all see slack. Some variation in magnitude (reasons for weakness "unclear").

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 9.1 percent with extensive slack; even those arguing for some structural component (Lacker, Kocherlakota) do not claim anything near full employment; consistent with the 31–45 "below full employment" range, closer to 35–38 given the depth of slack.

Quotes:
- Raskin: "payrolls were flat in August, the workweek fell, and aggregate hours worked in the private sector have declined over the past few months... the share of those unemployed for 27 weeks and longer now at 43 percent, well above the 15 percent historical average."
- Williams: "the unemployment rate... is 9.1 percent" and his staff estimate effective natural rate "between 6 and 7 percent" — implying large cyclical gap; "even the long-term unemployed are currently finding jobs at a rate greater than 10 percent per month."
- Bernanke (summary): "The labor market has deteriorated further, with workweeks and aggregate hours worked declining. Participants debated the extent to which persistent unemployment is structural or is becoming structural."

Minority views: Kocherlakota and Lacker argue the efficient level of employment may be lower than headline NAIRU measures suggest, potentially reducing the true slack. George (Tenth District) notes labor shortages for high-skilled positions.

---

**Minutes: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Clearly slack labor market with elevated unemployment, weak hiring, and deteriorating indicators. Some debate about cyclical vs. structural causes but unanimous view that conditions are weak.

Quotes:
- "Overall labor market conditions had shown no improvement or had deteriorated in recent months and the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "The employment report for August showed weak job gains. Moreover, both the average workweek and aggregate hours worked declined."
- "Many participants judged that weak demand was of most importance" for explaining labor slack, while "a few argued that structural and geographic mismatches were key."

Minority views: "A few" emphasized structural mismatches (which could imply tighter effective labor market than headline unemployment suggests), but this was the minority.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is sluggish to anemic; business investment is cautious with companies in contingency-planning mode; housing is severely depressed; export strength and some manufacturing pockets provide partial offset but do not alter the below-trend aggregate assessment.

Quotes:
- Yellen: "real PCE has advanced at an annual rate of only 0.4 percent, a truly glacial pace. Spending on core nondurables... has actually declined since March. Moreover, real spending on motor vehicles in August remains nearly 12 percent lower than in March."
- Raskin: "discretionary consumer spending... is still down 1.2 percent more than three years after the business cycle peak. Going back decades, such spending has never remained weak for that long."
- Evans (business contacts): "One of my region's best-performing large manufacturers... has instructed all of its units to mock up plans for a 30 percent reduction in capital expenditure spending if it finds it needs to pull the trigger."

Minority views: Some district contacts (Fisher's rail/energy, Bullard's agriculture/IT) show pockets of strength, and auto sector recovery noted. Export demand healthy.

---

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Below-trend spending. Consumer spending modest at best with terrible confidence. Business investment still expanding but threatened. Housing deeply depressed. Exports a bright spot but insufficient to offset weakness elsewhere.

Quotes:
- "Participants noted modest growth in consumer spending on average in recent months." "Consumer confidence had dropped to historically low levels."
- "Business sentiment had worsened, seemingly in response to weaker economic prospects." "Business investment in equipment and software had continued to expand in recent months, but some contacts expressed concern that firms would cut capital spending."
- "The housing sector remained depressed, with construction at very low levels."

Minority views: Minimal divergence — broad agreement on weakness.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 62 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened materially — spreads widened, CDS elevated, money market stress from European exposure, equity market declines. Not yet at 2008 crisis levels for U.S. specifically, but trending in that direction due to European contagion. Conditions in the 55–70 "somewhat tight to tight" range.

Quotes:
- Yellen: "spreads on high-yield bonds have reached recession levels, CMBS spreads have similarly widened, CDS spreads on several large banking organizations have risen to levels close to those prevailing at the time of the Lehman default."
- Bernanke: "if you look at financial conditions indexes, they show considerable tightening... Financial conditions have deteriorated." Also from his summary: "Financial conditions have continued to be strained — even reminiscent of 2008 in some dimensions."
- Williams: "Stresses in the European financial sector continue to mount, and by several measures... those strains are now comparable to what they were in September 2008."

Minority views: Duke notes U.S. bank credit quality improving, loan demand weak but manageable. The "tight" characterization is more acute for European institutions and in cross-border funding markets than for domestic U.S. credit availability per se.

---

**Minutes: 63 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened notably, driven by European crisis spillovers, wider risk spreads, and tight credit standards. U.S. banking system somewhat insulated by stronger capital positions, tempering the score.

Quotes:
- "Several participants argued that broader financial conditions had become less accommodative over the intermeeting period: Risk spreads had widened appreciably."
- "Conditions in bank funding markets had tightened, particularly for European banks." "Some large U.S. banks had seen further pressure on their stock prices and CDS spreads."
- "Still-tight credit standards meant that many households were unable to qualify for loans." "Spreads between mortgage rates and yields on Treasury securities were unusually wide."

Minority views: "Some participants noted that the reduction in leverage and increase in financial firms' liquidity cushions" had attenuated effects, and banks "remained willing to lend" — this moderates the tightness somewhat.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 22 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Majority (approx. 10–11 of 17) favor additional accommodation via MEP; 3 formal dissents favor no change; 1–2 want even more accommodation (Evans, Rosengren). The center of gravity is firmly in "strong dovish: majority favors maintaining/providing maximum accommodation" territory, consistent with the 15–28 range.

Quotes:
- Outcome: alternative B adopted (Operation Twist / MEP) — providing additional accommodation from zero lower bound.
- Yellen: "I support alternative B. The economic outlook has deteriorated significantly... I strongly support taking actions at this meeting to foster a stronger recovery."
- Evans: "My preference... would be to continue providing some form of aggressive forward guidance" with unemployment/inflation triggers; favors alternative A plus MEP.
- Dissents for alternative C (no action): Kocherlakota, Plosser, Fisher — but these 3 represent the hawkish minority.

Minority views: Kocherlakota (argues current policy already consistent with Taylor rule, credibility concerns), Plosser (argues action unlikely to help and risks inflation credibility), Fisher (argues limited efficacy, costs outweigh benefits).

---

**Minutes: 22 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Majority favors easing via Operation Twist, which is an active accommodation measure during the QE era. Firmly dovish, though not as aggressive as outright QE expansion.

Quotes:
- "Most members agreed that the revisions to the economic outlook warranted some additional monetary policy accommodation to support a stronger recovery."
- "Those viewing greater policy accommodation as appropriate at this meeting generally supported a maturity extension program" — Operation Twist ($400B).
- "Two members said that current conditions and the outlook could justify stronger policy action." "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate at this time."

Minority views: Three members opposed any additional accommodation (hawkish minority); two wanted even more action (dovish minority). This wide spread justifies ±10.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 65 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Three formal dissents with distinct reasoning (some against the action, some wanting more), combined with widespread heterogeneity among supporters about the form of action. This is firmly in the 61–75 "significant division" range. The disagreement extends beyond tactics to fundamental framework questions (flexible vs. hard inflation targeting, role of unemployment in reaction function, credibility concerns).

Quotes:
- Three formal voting dissents: Kocherlakota and Plosser (both prefer C based on inflation/credibility concerns), Fisher (prefers C based on efficacy concerns and preserving ammunition).
- Bernanke: "I was going to say on both sides, but there are probably about five or six different sides here."
- Evans advocates for alternative A (more aggressive communication + MEP); Rosengren prefers combination of A and B; Lockhart presents devil's advocate for C; Bullard argues for different MEP structure (3′); George finds action "somewhat premature."

---

**Minutes: 68 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Three dissents against accommodation plus two members wanting stronger action creates a three-camp dynamic. This is well above typical disagreement. Three formal dissents is historically very unusual.

Quotes:
- "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate at this time." (3 dissents)
- "Two members said that current conditions and the outlook could justify stronger policy action." (dovish wing)
- "One member who opposed the maturity extension program also opposed the change in reinvestment policy."

---

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability — specifically the European sovereign debt crisis and its potential U.S. contagion — is a prominent, extensively discussed topic addressed at length by most participants. Meets the 65–75 "prominent topic; most participants discuss at length" criterion.

Quotes:
- Rosengren devotes most of his economic go-round to financial stability: money market fund risk, European bank dollar funding mismatch, "SIV problem experienced in 2007," Bank of America and Morgan Stanley CDS above 300. "My biggest concern is that we will have at least a serious threat of a financial crisis this fall."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: extended analysis of EFSF, ECB collateral capacity, Italian/Spanish sovereign debt backstop needs, euro-system contradictions.
- Bernanke (his own economic remarks): "The most important development over the summer is that financial instability looks to be rearing its ugly head once again... some of the same adverse feedback loop between the economy and financial conditions looks to be in operation."

---

**Minutes: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a prominent topic with several paragraphs devoted to it, but the meeting is primarily about growth/employment concerns and the policy response. Financial conditions are discussed as a channel of risk rather than the sole focus.

Quotes:
- Extensive discussion of European sovereign debt/banking problems, potential "serious spillovers to the U.S. economy."
- Multiple paragraphs on financial market volatility, risk spreads, bank funding markets, CDS spreads, bank stock prices.
- Mortgage market dysfunction discussed (wide spreads, tight credit).

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The Committee maintains an explicit commitment to near-zero rates through mid-2013, discusses extending further or converting to threshold-based guidance, and explicitly frames the MEP as additional easing. The forward signal is clearly dovish/easing, consistent with the 8–18 "strong easing bias/low for long" range.

Quotes:
- Existing "mid-2013" zero-rate commitment maintained and elaborated.
- Multiple participants argue for extending or strengthening this guidance further (Evans: "most effective to continue providing some form of aggressive forward guidance"; Kocherlakota proposes duration-based guidance of "12 quarters").
- Bernanke: "I think I need to mention that the mid-2013 language needs further elaboration... I think this is something that should remain very firmly on the table."

Minority views: Kocherlakota, Plosser, Fisher want no additional accommodation (push toward 30–40 on forward guidance), but they are the minority.

---

**Minutes: 8 ±4 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Date-based forward guidance committing to near-zero rates through mid-2013 is among the strongest dovish forward signals, just short of explicit threshold-based guidance. Additional accommodation program reinforces the easing signal.

Quotes:
- "The Committee also decided to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent and to reaffirm its anticipation that economic conditions...are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- "A couple of members noted that they would prefer to change the Committee's forward guidance to provide greater clarity about the economic conditions that would be likely to warrant maintaining exceptionally low levels" — suggesting even more explicit dovish guidance.
- Operation Twist itself is a forward commitment to purchases through June 2012.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 72 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Nearly every participant emphasizes deep uncertainty — about Europe, about the inflation path, about the output gap/structural unemployment split, about transmission mechanisms. Multiple participants explicitly describe the situation as unprecedented or unusually difficult. Firmly in the 61–75 "high: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range" zone.

Quotes:
- Williams: "Uncertainty in U.S. financial markets has soared to one of the highest levels in 50 years, exceeded only by the recent financial crisis and the 1987 stock market crash. We face an enormous uncertainty shock that could easily tip us into a recession."
- Bernanke: "These are very, very difficult decisions, and I appreciate everyone's serious consideration." He also notes: "The economy does seem to still be growing, and a few better numbers could improve sentiment. But... humility in our forecasting is always in order."
- Pianalto: "highly uncertain economic environment"; Lockhart: "atmosphere of impending crisis"; multiple participants cite inability to forecast recession vs. slow growth, or the inflationary vs. disinflationary outcome.

---

**Minutes: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty with specific emphasis on downside risks, unclear reasons for recovery weakness, and European spillover potential. Multiple participants address uncertainty as a theme, not just as a hedge.

Quotes:
- "Participants continued to see the outlook for growth and inflation as more uncertain than usual."
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounding the outlook for a gradual pickup in economic growth."
- "The reasons for the weakness were unclear, contributing to greater uncertainty about the economic outlook."
- "Many participants saw significant downside risks to economic growth."

### S1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript (Staff): 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The staff projects PCE inflation below 2 percent over the next two years, declining from an elevated 2011 figure driven by commodity prices, with ongoing slack keeping core inflation subdued.

Quotes:
- MR. SLIFMAN: "with long-run inflation expectations well anchored, and considerable slack remaining in labor and product markets, we expect overall PCE prices to rise 1¼ percent in each of the next two years after increasing about 2½ percent in 2011."
- MR. SLIFMAN: "commodity prices have come off their recent peaks, and import price inflation is slowing."

---

**Minutes (Staff): 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation moderating from earlier elevated readings, with subdued outlook given persistent slack and stable expectations. Below target but not deflationary.

Quotes:
- "Consumer price inflation appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year, and measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable."
- Staff outlook: "inflation would be subdued in 2012 and 2013" with "considerable slack in labor and product markets."
