# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1995-07-06

## Meeting Context
Mid-1995. The Fed funds rate stood at 6% following 300bp of tightening from early 1994 through February 1995. By July 1995, growth had stalled — a possible negative GDP quarter in Q2 — prompting the Committee's first rate cut of the cycle. CPI inflation was running approximately 3–3.5%. Unemployment stood at 5.7%. This is the "soft landing" episode.

### A1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see CPI inflation at 3–3.5%, modestly above implicit target, but broadly believe the cyclical peak is at hand and the secular downward trend continues.

Representative quotes:
- MR. BROADDUS: "3.5 percent for 1995... we are on the high side there"
- MR. KELLEY: "inflation may be peaking cyclically somewhere right in here... we are not at price level stability"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "encouraging evidence that the cyclical peak in inflation may be close at hand"
- MR. BOEHNE: "uptick in cyclical inflationary pressures. However, with wage pressures subdued and price competition intense... we still appear to be on track for achieving price stability over time"

Minority views: Melzer more hawkish ("trend CPI inflation of 3 percent just isn't good enough"); Jordan sees very little inflation pressure in his district.

---

**Minutes: 46 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Members see inflation as modestly present but clearly diminishing, with resource pressures easing. The "recent uptick" is acknowledged but expected to reverse.

Representative quotes:
- "the risk of continuing increases in inflation had fallen considerably; indeed, in the view of many members inflation should moderate over the projection period"
- "most of the members believed that the underlying trend of inflation was now tilted toward gradual deceleration"
- "some still saw the potential for little or no progress in unwinding the recent uptick in inflation"
- CPI forecasts: 3.125–3.375% for 1995, 2.875–3.25% for 1996

Minority views: "Some still saw the potential for little or no progress in unwinding the recent uptick in inflation."

### A2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The economy has shifted from above potential (late 1994) to at or slightly below potential, with a possible negative GDP quarter. Most see a pause rather than recession.

Representative quotes:
- MR. PARRY: "real GDP declined at a rate between 1 and 2 percent in the second quarter... flirting with recession"
- MR. HOENIG: "economy is going through a pause and an adjustment phase... couple of quarters of growth below trend"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we clearly have managed to contain a highly unstable and inflationary business cycle expansion... I don't think we have solved the problem of the business cycle"
- MR. BLINDER: "we are in a cloud... retail sales were up just 0.4 percent from November to May"

Minority views: Blinder and Yellen see more downside risk; Melzer and Jordan see the economy's fundamental strength persisting.

---

**Minutes: 43 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is currently below potential due to the growth pause and inventory correction, but expected to return to potential by late 1995 or early 1996. Downside risks predominate.

Representative quotes:
- "the apparent pause in the expansion was likely to prove temporary, and their forecasts generally pointed to an upturn in overall economic activity to a pace in the neighborhood of the economy's potential"
- "much of the economic information... had suggested a greater softening in the economy than they had anticipated"
- "risks to the outlook were tilted to the downside"
- Real GDP growth central tendency: 1.5–2% for 1995 (below potential)

Minority views: Some noted "most recent data and some of the anecdotal reports... had a better tone," slightly offsetting the downside lean.

Minority views: Jordan's district is extremely tight (sub-3% in many counties); some districts (New York, New England) are weaker.

Representative quotes:

## Cross-Source Notes

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS large gap (Staff Transcript 55 vs. Staff Minutes 32)**: The transcript captures only Prell's brief reference to the 6% funds rate level as the reason easing is being conceptually considered, and did not include the full data review summarized in the minutes. The minutes' staff data review contains extensive language about declining rates, rising equity and bond prices, and "ready availability of financing." The minutes score (32) reflects the fuller picture; the transcript score carries Low confidence and should be weighted accordingly.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH gap (Staff Transcript 42 vs. Staff Minutes 25)**: The transcript focused on international economies via Johnson's briefing, which described general currency pressures directionally but without the specific "declined considerably" and "fell sharply" quantitative language present in the minutes' staff data review.

**Discussion DISSENT_INTENSITY gap (Transcript 38 vs. Minutes 27)**: The transcript records verbatim camp formation — explicit statements from Blinder, Minehan, Hoenig, and Melzer about their preferred versus accepted actions. The minutes smooth this into general characterizations of broad agreement, obscuring the three-camp structure visible in the transcript.

**Discussion UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED gap (Transcript 52 vs. Minutes 42)**: The transcript preserves individually vivid expressions of uncertainty (Phillips's "fork in the road," Blinder's recession probability models "30 to 60 percent," Greenspan's "6 weeks or 2 months" framing). The minutes convey uncertainty through quantifier language ("risks tilted to the downside," "substantial uncertainty") but at a lower register.

**Discussion INFLATION_PRESSURE gap (Transcript 55 vs. Minutes 46)**: The transcript's higher score reflects the direct prevalence of individual quotes citing specific inflation figures (3.5%, "inching up," "not at price level stability"). The minutes' more aggregated language emphasizes the moderating direction more than the level.
