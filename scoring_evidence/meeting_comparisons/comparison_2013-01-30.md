### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "both headline and core PCE inflation will remain around 1½ percent, below our 2 percent target, for the next few years."
- MR. EVANS: "core PCE inflation has been well below our long-run inflation objective of 2 percent, and I infer that we've been fighting substantial disinflationary forces for some time."
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "inflation pressures seem likely to remain muted. I expect inflation to be around 1.6 percent in 2013."

Center-of-gravity: The vast majority of participants see inflation running persistently below the 2% target with disinflationary forces present; minority hawkish concerns (Fisher, Lacker, George on medium-term risk) prevent a lower score.

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Nearly all participants anticipated that inflation over the medium-term would run at or below the Committee's 2 percent objective."
- "There was little evidence of wage or cost pressures outside of isolated sectors, and measures of inflation expectations remained stable."
- "A few participants expressed concerns that the current highly accommodative stance of monetary policy posed upside risks to inflation in the medium or longer term."

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus view that inflation is running at or below the 2% target with little pressure, consistent with subdued conditions and substantial resource slack.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "there is still a long way to go to reach potential."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "our economy continues to suffer from insufficient aggregate demand."
- MR. TARULLO: "this economy still has 4 million fewer jobs than it did in January 2008...the basic picture of job growth in the United States continues to be steady but it is a painfully slow process."

Center-of-gravity: Economy is below potential with a meaningful output gap persisting; housing and autos show pockets of strength but GDP growth is only ~2–2.5%, well below what would close the gap rapidly.

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

- "Many of these participants worried that, should the economy continue to operate below potential for too long, reduced investment and underutilization of labor could further undermine the growth of potential output over time."
- "Growth in economic activity had paused in recent months, in large part because of weather-related disruptions and other transitory factors."

Center-of-gravity: Economy operating below potential on a moderate recovery path, with persistent slack and concern about hysteresis effects from prolonged underutilization.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:

- "Several participants expressed concern that continuation of only slow job growth and persistently high long-duration unemployment could lead to permanent damage to the labor market."

Center-of-gravity: Labor market has significant slack with unemployment well above the longer-run normal level and broad underutilization.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 20 ±5 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- MR. EVANS: "my outlook is premised on maintaining our open-ended asset purchases until we're highly confident that we will have evidence of a substantial improvement in the labor markets."
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "I will suggest that the Committee should begin to move...toward lowering the unemployment threshold to 5½ percent" [i.e., even more accommodation].
- MR. POWELL: "I support alternative B at this meeting. However, I also believe that it will soon be time to move to something like alternative C, probably well before the end of this year."

Center-of-gravity: The strong majority explicitly supports continuing open-ended QE; a vocal minority (Powell, Stein, George, Pianalto, Fisher) raises cost concerns and hints at eventual tapering but votes for alternative B today; the policy stance is firmly in strong-dovish territory.

**Minutes — Score: 22 ±7 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Most participants commented that the Committee's asset purchases had been effective in easing financial conditions and helping stimulate economic activity."
- "Many participants also expressed some concerns about potential costs and risks arising from further asset purchases."
- "A number of participants stated that an ongoing evaluation of the efficacy, costs, and risks of asset purchases might well lead the Committee to taper or end its purchases before it judged that a substantial improvement in the outlook for the labor market had occurred."
- "All but one member judged that maintaining the highly [accommodative stance was appropriate]."

Center-of-gravity: Firmly dovish — QE3 ongoing, near-zero rates, threshold-based forward guidance — but with a growing internal debate about the costs and potential tapering of asset purchases, which lifts the score from the most dovish range.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 7 ±4 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- MR. EVANS: "my outlook is premised on maintaining our open-ended asset purchases until we're highly confident...substantial improvement in the labor markets." [Signals extended QE]
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "I will suggest that the Committee should begin to move...toward lowering the unemployment threshold to 5½ percent." [Wants even more dovish guidance]
- MS. YELLEN: "today, with the unemployment rate far above 6½ percent, inflation expectations well anchored, and my forecast for inflation one to two years ahead running well below 2½ percent, I consider the status quo appropriate."

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is the threshold-based QE3/Evans Rule framework: zero rates "until 6.5% unemployment," open-ended asset purchases, and the center of gravity would keep rates low well beyond 2015; some speakers want the threshold moved even lower.

**Minutes — Score: 8 ±4 [High confidence]**

Quotes:
- "Participants also discussed the economic thresholds in the Committee's forward guidance on the path of the federal funds rate. On the whole, participants judged that financial markets had adapted to the shift from date-based communication to guidance based on economic thresholds without difficulty."
- "One participant also indicated a preference for lowering the threshold for the unemployment rate as a means of providing additional accommodation."
- "A few participants noted examples of past instances in which policymakers had prematurely removed accommodation, with adverse effects on economic growth, employment, and price stability."
- "A number of participants discussed the possibility of providing monetary accommodation by holding securities for a longer period."

Center-of-gravity: Forward guidance is anchored by the Evans Rule thresholds (6.5% unemployment, 2.5% inflation), which commit to near-zero rates well into the future. Discussions of lowering thresholds and holding securities longer reinforce the strongly dovish forward signal.
