### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Inflation seems to be firming a bit, with core at 1.6 percent over the past 12 months through April...I see underlying inflation as being fairly close to 2 percent." — Powell

> "I cannot express a similar confidence about reaching our inflation target...I could see the attractor falling even further below 2 percent." — Evans

> "the persistently low level of inflation, together with signs of a deterioration in inflation expectations...downside risks remain." — Brainard

**Center-of-gravity**: The large majority view inflation as below the 2% target (core PCE ~1.6%) with gradual convergence expected, but a meaningful minority (Evans, Brainard, Tarullo, Kashkari) see genuine downside risks to achieving target, anchoring the score toward the lower end of the below-target band.

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."

> "Core PCE price inflation registered an increase of 1.6 percent for the 12 months ending in April."

> "Most participants expected to see continued progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective" but "other participants were less confident that inflation would return to its target level over the medium term."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running meaningfully below target with some firming in core measures but persistent downside risks from global factors and declining inflation compensation measures.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 51 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Output and unemployment gaps have essentially closed already. And with substantial monetary stimulus still in play, I expect these aggregate indicators to overshoot their marks this year." — Williams

> "growth would turn out to be somewhat below the 2.3 percent average pace...but rapid enough to put very gradual upward pressure on resource utilization...the economy is near full employment, some slack still exists." — Brainard

> "We are now at my estimate of full employment." — Rosengren

**Center-of-gravity**: The large majority of speakers see the output gap as essentially closed or slightly positive, with Williams, Rosengren, George, Mester, Harker, and Lacker explicitly saying the economy is at or beyond potential; a minority (Brainard, Evans, Tarullo) see modest remaining slack, placing the center just above 50.

---

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Growth in economic activity appeared to have picked up" but "business fixed investment had been soft."

> "Some of them judged that labor market conditions were now at or close to the Committee's objectives."

> Others saw "the recent soft readings on payroll jobs...suggested a possible downshift in the pace of improvement."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential but with downside uncertainty from the weak May employment report and soft business investment.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 51 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "At our March meeting, every participant said that the longer-run unemployment rate was greater than or equal to the May number of 4.7 percent. So the notion that labor market resources are fully utilized shouldn't be too controversial." — Lacker

> notes involuntary part-time employment jumped 468,000 in one month; "I lean slightly toward taking some signal from the May numbers." — Tarullo

> "The economy is at maximum employment from the standpoint of what monetary policy can do." — Mester

**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 4.7% — at or below most participants' NAIRU estimates — places the labor market at or just beyond full employment per the majority, but the weak May payroll data and Tarullo/Dudley/Brainard's slack indicators hold the score close to neutral.

**Minority views (transcript)**: Dudley: "I don't view the pickup in wage compensation as very large...somewhat less than what I would evaluate as consistent with reaching full employment." Brainard: "prime-age men employment share still more than 2 percentage points below pre-crisis norms."

---

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "The employment report for May showed considerably weaker growth in payrolls than had been expected."

> "Some noted that other indicators did not corroborate a material weakening of labor market conditions."

> "Some of them judged that labor market conditions were now at or close to the Committee's objectives."

**Center-of-gravity**: Near full employment by most measures, but the May report introduced significant uncertainty about the underlying pace of improvement. Multiple interpretations of the data coexist.

**Minority views (minutes)**: "A few participants suggested that the weak employment growth may instead reflect supply constraints associated with a general tightening of labor market conditions" (would imply tighter); others pointed to broad weakness indicators.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 46 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "Consumption increased robustly in April, led by strong retail and auto sales...conditions are quite supportive of strong consumption...Investment has been weak, however, with business fixed investment falling in Q4 and Q1 and expected to edge down further." — Powell

> "I have the chronic weakness that we've seen in investment spending...transitioning from a period in which we had very strong real income gains supporting consumer spending to a situation in which real income gains are going to be distinctly slower." — Dudley

> "the picture for aggregate demand, at least for the consumer, is quite positive...wage growth has been steadily increasing." — George

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is above trend (strong retail, auto sales, income growth) but business fixed investment has contracted for two consecutive quarters and is flat in Q2, with multiple participants flagging cautious business sentiment and weak corporate capital plans; the net picture is slightly below trend when investment weakness is weighted.

---

**Minutes — Score: 47 ±6 [High confidence]**

> "Growth of consumer spending appeared to have picked up...Retail sales posted strong gains in April and May."

> "the softness in business fixed investment since late last year persisted early in the second quarter."

> "Some participants mentioned that the sluggishness in business investment could portend a broader economic slowdown."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer rebound offset by persistently soft business investment, netting to near-trend overall spending.

> "the easing in financial conditions is very welcome." — Brainard

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants consistently describe financial conditions as having eased since December, with lower long-term rates, higher equity prices, and a weaker dollar all described as providing accommodation; conditions are clearly in the accommodative/somewhat loose zone.

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions described as accommodative overall, though with downside risks from upcoming global events.

**Minority views (minutes)**: "A couple of participants observed that financial strains caused by previous declines in energy prices had continued for firms or financial institutions in their Districts."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 55 ±10 [High confidence]**

> "Were it not for the upcoming Brexit vote...I would be advocating increasing the rate at this meeting...if the data continue to support the current outlook, I believe it will be appropriate to raise the funds rate very soon." [Prefers alt-C] — Mester

> "prudent risk management would argue for waiting until the totality of the data provides greater confidence...appropriate path...could be even more shallow and gradual." [Supports alt-B with no changes] — Brainard

> "I expect I will be arguing that it's time to reengage on our announced policy of a gradual removal of accommodation" [if data improve by July]. — Williams

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee unanimously supports holding today (alt-B), but a hawkish minority (Mester, George, Lacker) prefers alt-C's explicit forward tightening language, a broad centrist majority leans toward July as potentially live, and a dovish minority (Brainard, Evans, Tarullo, Kashkari) sees little urgency to tighten; the center of gravity is a mild hold-with-tightening-bias.

---

**Minutes — Score: 50 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "Members agreed to leave the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged."

> "Some of them emphasized that...taking another step in removing monetary accommodation should not be delayed too long."

> "However, some other participants were uncertain whether economic conditions would soon warrant an increase."

> "A few pointed out that...maintaining an accommodative stance of policy could extend the strengthening of the labor market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Balanced — unanimous hold with genuine two-sidedness. Hawks want to resume hiking soon; doves want more evidence and see asymmetric risks from premature tightening.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

> "I prefer alternative C...alternative B has a more negative tone that places too much weight on the disappointing May employment report." — Mester

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal vote dissents, but a wide spectrum of views ranging from alt-A sympathizers (Evans, Brainard-leaning) through the alt-B consensus to explicit alt-C preferences (Mester, George, Lacker), plus Bullard's idiosyncratic regime-based flat path, produces notable internal disagreement about both economic assessment and forward guidance.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript — Score: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "On that score, I'd emphasize that we're close to our full employment objective, that the underlying pace of inflation is not far from 2 percent...Depending on the data, it could be appropriate to take another cautious policy step in July." — Fischer

> "if the Brexit vote prove uneventful...and the next labor market report shows improvement, I would like to keep an open mind on appropriate policy for July." — Rosengren

> "our expectation still is, over time, that some further increases will be appropriate if we do see the labor market on a solid path." — Chair Yellen (closing)

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee's forward signal is a mild tightening bias — hold today, data-dependent path, with a broad majority expecting to raise again in coming months if data cooperate; the alt-B statement does not contain explicit forward-tightening language, but the press conference messaging and most speakers' forward lean is in the mild-tightening zone.

---

**Minutes — Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "Most still expected that the appropriate target range for the federal funds rate...would rise gradually in coming years."

> "Some noted that their forecasts were now consistent with a shallower path than they had expected at the time of the March meeting."

> "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — gradual hikes expected ahead, but the path has been downgraded and is highly data-dependent.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript — Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

> "The data we've received since April have been mixed, which leaves considerable uncertainty about what to infer about the pace of progress toward our goals." — Brainard

> "The future is uncertain. There's not a whole lot we can do about that. So, one way or the other, modern monetary policy will continue to have to be data dependent." — Fischer

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty is the defining characteristic of this meeting, driven by two simultaneous novel risks — the anomalous May employment report and the imminent Brexit vote — pushing uncertainty above the normal "risks roughly balanced" level but not into the extreme range of crisis-era meetings.

---

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty from multiple simultaneous sources — weak employment data, Brexit, neutral rate uncertainty, and global risks — leading participants to delay action pending further information.
