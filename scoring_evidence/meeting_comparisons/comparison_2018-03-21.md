# FOMC Meeting 2018-03-21: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

## Part A: Participant Discussion

Scores are on a 0–100 scale. Sources: Discussion Transcript (DT) and Discussion Minutes (DM).

---

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 44 ±7 [High confidence] | 47 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "core PCE is at 1.5 percent, and we should not declare victory prematurely." — Kashkari

> "inflation expectations currently appear to be stubbornly below our 2 percent target." — Evans

> "reflecting the recent firming in the inflation readings...my modal projection is that inflation will gradually rise over the forecast horizon, reaching our 2 percent goal on a sustained basis sometime in the first half of next year." — Mester

> "I believe for now it's effectively at our target. Monthly inflation has picked up over the past half-year." — Barkin

**Center of gravity (DT)**: Participants see inflation as slightly below the 2 percent target on a 12-month basis but firming, with the 6-month measure having reached 2%. Most expect gradual convergence to target over 2018–2019.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "Both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy continued to run below 2 percent."

> "Many participants stated that recent readings from indicators on inflation and inflation expectations increased their confidence that inflation would rise to the Committee's 2 percent objective in coming months."

> "A few participants cautioned that...the levels of these indicators remained too low to be consistent with the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Inflation still running below 2% target but participants broadly expect it to move up to target soon. The reading is slightly below neutral.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 58 ±6 [High confidence] | 57 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "All signs point to an economy operating above full employment...real GDP grew much more rapidly than potential GDP last year, and I expect a repeat of this pattern this year." — Williams

> "my forecast is for real GDP to exceed potential by almost 1 percentage point this year." — Rosengren

> "close to full employment and growing above its trend rate." — Brainard

> "the economy is very likely in the vicinity of maximum sustainable employment, and that momentum in the job market...will further tighten labor market conditions." — Powell

**Center of gravity (DT)**: Clear consensus that the economy is operating above potential, with fiscal stimulus expected to push it further above trend in 2018–2019.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "Participants generally saw the news on spending and the labor market over the past few quarters as being consistent with continued above-trend growth."

> "The economy was operating at a high level of resource utilization."

> "A number of participants offered their views on the potential benefits and costs associated with an economy operating well above potential for a prolonged period."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Participants clearly view the economy as above potential, with above-trend growth and high resource utilization. Fiscal stimulus expected to boost further.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 62 ±8 [High confidence] | 60 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "Labor markets are tight and getting tighter as you can see in the recent jobs report and comments given by our contacts." — Barkin

> "employment rates, both in aggregate and in the case of various demographic groups, have reached lows that would have been dismissed as unattainable just a few years ago." — Williams

> "Labor shortages are the biggest constraint we see to energy production growth in 2018, and labor shortages are also, we think, the biggest constraint to growth across industries." — Kaplan

> "Monthly job gains averaged 240,000 over the past three months...more than twice the pace required to keep the unemployment rate stable." — Powell

**Center of gravity (DT)**: The labor market is clearly in tight territory — widespread reports of labor shortages, unemployment at 4.1% and expected to fall to 3.5–3.7% over the horizon. Wage growth puzzlingly modest despite tightness.

**Minority views (DT)**: Kashkari argues the gauge is broken and there may be more hidden slack; believes 4.1% headline may overstate tightness because prime-age LFP is still recovering.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "Most participants described labor market conditions as strong, noting that payroll gains had remained well above the pace regarded as consistent with absorbing new labor force entrants."

> "In some Districts, reports from business contacts or evidence from surveys pointed to continuing shortages of workers in segments of the labor market."

> "Most still described the pace of wage gains as moderate; a few participants cited this fact as suggesting that there was room for the labor market to strengthen somewhat further."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Labor market tight — low unemployment, strong payroll gains, reported worker shortages — but wage growth still moderate, suggesting not yet severely tight.

**Minority views (DM)**: A few argue unemployment can sustainably go lower, suggesting effective tightness may be less than headline numbers imply.

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 53 ±7 [Moderate confidence] | 55 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "Consumption, in particular, appears to have slowed significantly from a very strong 4¼ percent annualized growth in the fourth quarter to an estimated 1½ percent in the current quarter." — Powell

> "payrolls have strengthened from an already expansionary pace...consumer sentiment and business surveys remain strong." — Brainard

**Center of gravity (DT)**: Q1 spending data weak, but participants nearly unanimously attribute this to temporary/seasonal factors; underlying fundamentals (sentiment, tax cuts, global growth) are strong. Net assessment is at or slightly above trend.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "Participants noted incoming data suggesting some slowing in the rate of growth of household spending and business fixed investment after strong fourth-quarter readings. However, they expected that the first-quarter softness would be transitory."

> "Many participants reported considerable optimism among the business contacts in their Districts, consistent with a firming in business expenditures."

> "Strong economic fundamentals" including "high levels of consumer and business sentiment, supportive financial conditions."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Above trend overall — strong fundamentals and business optimism, but some temporary Q1 softness. Net assessment is solidly positive.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 70 ±7 [High confidence] | 71 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "my SEP submission calls for three additional rate increases this year and another four next year." — Williams

> "four 25 basis point hikes are likely to be appropriate this year." — Dudley

> "I maintain four rate increases as appropriate in 2018." — George

> Rosengren advocates for possible faster pace if economy performs as Tealbook predicts.

**Center of gravity (DT)**: Near-universal support for the 25bp hike at this meeting, with most participants signaling 3–4 more hikes in 2018 and a path that likely goes above the long-run neutral rate by 2019–2020.

**Minority views (DT)**: Evans preferred to hold today but accepted consensus; Kashkari preferred alt-A (hold) but accepted alt-B for continuity; Bullard supports alt-B but is more concerned about over-tightening past 2%.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "Almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting."

> "All participants saw some further firming of the stance of monetary policy as likely to be warranted."

> "A number of participants indicated that the appropriate path for the federal funds rate over the next few years would likely be slightly steeper than they had previously expected."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Near-unanimous support for current hike, universal support for further firming, and some seeing a steeper path than before. Solidly hawkish in the gradual-hiking mold.

**Minority views (DM)**: "A couple of participants pointed to possible benefits of postponing an increase" — very small minority.

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Financial stability mentioned by a few participants in the context of prolonged above-potential growth and accommodative conditions, but not a major focus of the discussion.

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Forward signal is clearly in a tightening direction — further gradual hikes ahead, with some seeing a steeper path and eventual above-neutral rates.

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

| | Discussion Transcript | Discussion Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 45 ±8 [High confidence] | 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence] |

**Discussion Transcript — Representative Quotes**

> "considerable uncertainty about just how strong that impetus is at the present...In view of the state of our knowledge about various unobservables, such as the natural rate of unemployment and the neutral rate of interest, we need to consider the two-sided risks." — Powell

> "we don't have a lot of experience with a procyclical fiscal experiment of this nature, so it's hard to know with any precision how the economy is likely to respond." — Brainard

> Bullard: full session devoted to r* uncertainty.

**Discussion Minutes — Representative Quotes**

> "A strong majority of participants viewed the prospect of retaliatory trade actions by other countries, as well as other issues and uncertainties associated with trade policies, as downside risks for the U.S. economy."

> "Participants generally regarded the magnitude and timing of the economic effects of the fiscal policy changes as uncertain."

**Center of gravity (DM)**: Baseline risks roughly balanced, but trade policy and fiscal uncertainty flagged as notable sources of uncertainty. Somewhat elevated but not deeply uncertain.
