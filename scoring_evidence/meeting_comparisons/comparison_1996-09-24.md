## Part A: Discussion — Side-by-Side Evidence

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 55 ±8 [High] | Minutes: 50 ±7 [High]**

| Transcript (speakers) | Minutes (synthesized) |
|---|---|
| YELLEN: "I would characterize the economy as operating in an inflationary danger zone." | "the rate of price inflation had not picked up and the prospects were good that inflation would remain contained for some time" |
| KELLEY: "There continues to be... no visible sign of rising price inflation yet." | "the risks continued to be tilted to some extent in the direction of rising price inflation over the forecast horizon" |
| PARRY: "With unemployment clearly below the natural rate, wages are accelerating and price pressures are building." | "wages were rising at a somewhat faster pace" but "rising labor costs were not currently being passed through to prices" |
| GREENSPAN: "it is very difficult to get around the fact that hourly wages and salaries are accelerating" but "The performance on the price side, I think, is very impressive." | [Minority views] "Some" see inflation as more likely to rise; "many others" are "not persuaded of the inevitability." |

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Actual inflation benign and declining, but nearly all participants see upside risk from tight labor markets and accelerating wages. Tension between current good data and forward-looking concern places this modestly above neutral.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants see current inflation as contained with steady/declining trend but acknowledge upside risks from tight labor markets. Balance of current benign readings vs. forward-looking risk concern lands at the midpoint.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 55 ±6 [High] | Minutes: 55 ±5 [High]**

| Transcript (speakers) | Minutes (synthesized) |
|---|---|
| PARRY: "The economy has been operating at a level somewhat above its long-term potential for some time now." | "the high level of production" and "capacity utilization already at high levels" |
| GUYNN: "GDP growth is averaging just under 3 percent on a year-over-year basis." | "further growth at a pace near that of the economy's potential as a likely prospect" (i.e., currently slightly above) |
| MEYER: "growth has slowed much more sharply in the third quarter... moving down to a pace close to trend." | "slowing in the economic expansion from a rapid and unsustainable pace in the second quarter" |
| GREENSPAN: growth moderating, inventory effects boosting Q3 but final sales weakening. | [Narrow range of views; general agreement on the assessment.] |

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Economy has been above potential but is moderating toward trend. Most see it as still somewhat above potential.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Economy operating modestly above potential but slowing toward it. Production and capacity utilization at high levels.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 63 ±5 [High] | Minutes: 62 ±5 [High]**

| Transcript (speakers) | Minutes (synthesized) |
|---|---|
| BROADDUS: "persistent and increasingly widespread reports of very tight labor market conditions" | "labor markets appeared to have tightened further in recent months" |
| YELLEN: "it is now 'impossible' to find qualified entry-level workers, whereas six months ago the word 'difficult' would have been used more frequently." | "increasingly tight labor markets that under similar conditions historically had been associated with considerable upward pressure on nominal labor compensation" |
| GREENSPAN: "the labor markets are getting increasingly tight" | "prolonged periods of taut labor markets" (Stern's view, but reflects broader concern) |
| Multiple District reports of worker shortages, signing bonuses, difficulty finding workers at all skill levels. | [Broad consensus; no minority dissent on direction.] |

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Virtually unanimous that labor markets are tight and tightening further. Unemployment at 5.1%, below most NAIRU estimates. Tight but not "severe shortages" — more "low unemployment, hard to find workers, moderate wage pressure."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Clear consensus that labor markets are tight and tightening further. Unemployment at 5.1% and wages accelerating. Not yet at "severe shortage" level but firmly tight.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| Transcript (speakers) | Minutes (synthesized) |
|---|---|
| PARRY: "I believe it would be prudent to raise interest rates 25 basis points at this meeting." | "nearly all the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance and the retention of a bias toward restraint" |
| GREENSPAN: "I continue to believe that the probability of our having to move, though declining, is still above 50 percent" but recommends hold with asymmetric directive. | "A few members indicated that they could vote for some slight tightening in policy" |
| YELLEN: "would also have been quite willing to support an upward adjustment of 25 basis points today." | "retaining an asymmetric directive that was biased toward restraint would be consistent with their assessments of the inflation risks" |
| MEYER: "I could make the case... a cautious step toward restraint would be prudent" but supports hold. | [One formal dissent for tightening: Mr. Stern.] |

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Clear tightening bias. At least 5 speakers explicitly favor a rate hike; several more are very close. Decision is to hold with asymmetric tightening directive.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Hold with tightening bias. Nearly all support the hold, but the bias is clearly toward restraint. A few would have preferred tightening, and one dissented for tightening.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| Transcript (speakers) | Minutes (synthesized) |
|---|---|
| GREENSPAN: asymmetric directive toward tightening; "the probability of our having to move... is still above 50 percent." | "retaining an asymmetric directive that was biased toward restraint" |
| GUYNN: "a modest increase... is probably desirable now or sometime soon." | "information suggesting that the odds on higher inflation had risen should be met with a prompt policy firming" |

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Clear tightening bias in the forward signal. The asymmetric directive explicitly leans toward restraint, and the committee signals readiness for "prompt" firming if inflation odds rise.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Multiple members highlight genuine uncertainty about the inflation-resource utilization nexus and the sustainability of benign price behavior. More than routine hedging — a core theme.
