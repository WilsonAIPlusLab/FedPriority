## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores**: Disc-T 30, Disc-M 28, Staff-T 30, Staff-M 30

**Discussion transcript**:
- "The vast majority of our contacts continue to say that they have little pricing power." (Moskow)
- "we continue to expect inflation to remain well contained, with the core PCE price index increasing about 1½ percent next year." (Parry)
- "The risks of inflation, if anything, have receded during the intermeeting period... slack in labor markets and capacity utilization... it is hard to see that inflation pressures are likely to emerge during the forecast period." (Ferguson)
- "already at 1½ percent inflation... inflation could fall further before it stabilizes." (Kohn)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "The outlook for inflation remained very favorable in the context of continuing slack in labor markets and robust growth in structural productivity."
- "members saw little reason for concern about the prospect of an increase in inflation in the foreseeable future"
- "the risks of any significant run-up in inflation appeared to have receded"
- Minor upward pressures noted: "healthcare insurance costs...steel prices, some materials costs, and...some upward pressures on import prices"

**Staff transcript**:
- Inflation subdued; core PCE near 1%. No inflation concerns despite corporate accounting scandals. Potential GDP revised down, partly offsetting gap effects.

**Staff minutes**:
- "Overall price inflation had fallen sharply over the past year, largely reflecting developments in the food and energy sectors, and core inflation had eased a little."
- "The persistence of underutilized resources was expected to foster some moderation in core price inflation."

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores**: Disc-T 35, Disc-M 34, Staff-T 34, Staff-M 33

**Discussion transcript**:
- "the output gap... stays stubbornly high at 1.7 in 2002 and 1.3 in 2003... the risks are clearly that we are moving further and further away from achieving sustainable growth." (Ferguson)
- "it could be some time before the rate of expansion is fast enough to begin to reduce margins of underutilized resources. Indeed, such margins could widen before they begin to narrow." (Kohn)
- "below-potential growth for much of the period." (Minehan)
- "significant slack in resource utilization certainly remains, especially in manufacturing." (Parry)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "much of the incoming information on economic activity had been disappointing, and many indicated that they had marked down their growth forecasts"
- "margins of underutilized labor and other producer resources"
- "more time than anticipated earlier was likely to elapse before the expansion reached a pace that would begin to reduce margins of underutilized labor"
- Members "continued to place favorable odds on an underlying outlook of strengthening expansion"

**Staff transcript**:
- GDP growth revised down approximately 0.3pp for 2002 and 0.4pp for 2003. Manufacturing output soft. Recovery disappointing. Economy still below potential.

**Staff minutes**:
- "economic activity expanded only slightly in the second quarter."
- "the level of activity would remain below the economy's potential for a somewhat longer time."
- "Capacity utilization in manufacturing... remained substantially below its long-run average."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores**: Disc-T 34, Disc-M 33, Staff-T 30, Staff-M 32

**Discussion transcript**:
- "Labor markets remain slack, with firms having no difficulty filling almost any position at this point." (Hoenig)
- "Labor turnover is down, and labor is readily available. I hear that repeatedly." (Poole)
- "a jobless recovery similar to the one following the 1990-91 recession." (McTeer)
- "6 percent unemployment... softness in labor markets hold down labor costs." (Kohn)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "continuing slack in labor markets"
- "margins of underutilized labor and other producer resources"
- "the anticipated pickup in employment" (forward-looking, implying current weakness)
- "more cautious business spending and hiring, at least temporarily"

**Staff transcript**:
- Workweek dropped sharply — "very large drop." Hours worked declining. Employment flat to declining. Initial claims elevated. Manufacturing employment weak.

**Staff minutes**:
- "the demand for labor services had increased only slightly and the unemployment rate had risen."
- "The civilian unemployment rate edged up in June, to 5.9 percent, and was unchanged in July."
- "aggregate hours worked by production or nonsupervisory workers declined steeply."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores**: Disc-T 38, Disc-M 37, Staff-T 42, Staff-M 38

**Discussion transcript**:
- "we still are not hearing any reports of plans to increase [capital spending] outlays... only a very few indicated that they had moved up the timing of some investments." (Moskow)
- "businesses still very much hunkered down, putting off both investment spending and additions to staff." (Guynn)
- "whatever little capital spending is going on now is aimed almost entirely at increasing productivity rather than enlarging capacity." (Broaddus)
- "Both housing and auto sales remain near record highs... persistent growth of real disposable income... foundation for continued... increases in consumer spending." (Bies)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "household spending was continuing to play a key role in sustaining the expansion. Retail sales...had been well maintained"
- "The weakness in business fixed investment was still a depressant on overall economic activity"
- "business sentiment remained extraordinarily cautious"
- "The prospects for an upturn in nonresidential construction appeared to many to be more bleak"

**Staff transcript**:
- Consumer spending resilient but business sector still weak. Stock market crash weighing on confidence. GDP growth revised down. Corporate scandal effects damaging business confidence.

**Staff minutes**:
- "final sales changed little: business capital spending weakened somewhat further while growth in consumer spending, residential housing expenditures, and government outlays slowed."
- "Retail sales were relatively brisk in June and July despite plunging equity prices."

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Scores**: Disc-T 63, Disc-M 58, Staff-T 60, Staff-M 63

**Discussion transcript**:
- "the corporate bond market has virtually dried up in the last month or so. Banks also are tightening terms... demand more information, and enforce covenants more closely." (Kohn)
- "net equity issuance was negative... bond issuance and bank loans... conditions are far less accommodative than they were even a short time ago." (Ferguson)
- "the syndicated loan market is very, very quiet... availability of the syndicated loan market is going to be quite questionable." (McDonough)
- "credit standards have become tighter... asked to show improved ratios before the banks will renew those lines." (Hoenig)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "heightened degree of risk aversion that had led to widened credit spreads in financial markets and the curtailment of credit availability to potential borrowers"
- "skittishness in debt and equity markets"
- Offsets noted: "home mortgage financing remained widely available at low interest rates," "overall condition of the banking system remained sound with bank credit widely available"

**Staff transcript**:
- "sharp decline and heightened volatility in equity prices." "General reduction in the willingness on the part of investors to bear risk." Corporate governance crisis. Risk spreads widening. Brazil crisis. Despite accommodative rates, financial conditions tightened significantly.

**Staff minutes**:
- "equity prices plunged before recovering somewhat later in the intermeeting period; on net, the major broad equity indexes were down substantially."
- "steep increases in corporate debt yields, particularly for lower-quality issues."
- "Borrowing by domestic nonfinancial businesses had been weak recently, likely reflecting deteriorating conditions in credit markets."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Scores**: Disc-T 38, Disc-M 38 (discussion sources only)

**Discussion transcript**:
- "I would recommend that we stay where we are with respect to rates today. But I do agree with those who have argued... that it is essential that we at least recognize what is going on. Clearly, the risks are not balanced." (Greenspan)
- "I think I would have been... one among perhaps not many at this table who could have voted for an easing in policy." (Minehan)
- "if output gaps appear to be increasing... even without a recession I would be in favor of easing policy." (Kohn)
- "I would not favor such a move today... real funds rate is already at or near the zero level." (Broaddus, minority)

**Discussion minutes**:
- "all the members were in favor of an unchanged policy stance consistent with retaining a target rate of 1-3/4 percent"
- "All the members indicated that they could accept, and most said they preferred, a proposal to shift the Committee's assessment of the risks to the economy...tilted toward weakness"
- "risks of a more extended period of subpar growth had increased while those of inflation had declined"
- "A few expressed a preference to retain the current balanced risks statement"

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Scores**: Disc-T 35, Disc-M 22 (discussion sources only)

**Discussion transcript**:
- Rate decision (hold) supported unanimously — no formal dissents.
- Balance-of-risks debate divides the room: approximately 5-6 participants (Broaddus, Poole, Hoenig, Parry, Guynn, Moskow) prefer not shifting to weakness but will acquiesce.
- "I would not favor moving the tilt either at this stage." (Broaddus)
- "I'd like to associate myself with the position that Al Broaddus spelled out." (Poole)

**Discussion minutes**:
- Unanimous on holding rates.
- "All the members indicated that they could accept" risk shift; "most said they preferred" it.
- "A few expressed a preference to retain the current balanced risks statement" — described as minor tactical disagreement only.
- "All the members agreed...on the desirability of communicating...their view that the expansion recently had been less robust than expected"

*Note: The transcript records an explicit named minority of ~5-6 participants and their reservations at length; the minutes compress this to "a few" with a brief characterization, producing a lower dissent score in the minutes source.*

**Discussion minutes**:

*Note: The transcript devotes substantially more discussion space to financial stability across multiple named speakers; the minutes compress this into a single detailed paragraph, producing a lower emphasis score.*

**Discussion minutes**:

## Cross-Source Observations

**High convergence** (all four sources within tight range):
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28–30 across all four sources. Complete agreement on subdued, declining inflation below target.
- OUTPUT_GAP: 33–35. All sources describe economy clearly below potential; gap at risk of widening.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30–34. All sources confirm significant slack; 5.9% unemployment, declining hours, weak hiring.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 37–42. All sources show consumer holding up, business investment the drag.
- PRODUCTIVITY: 65–72. All sources highlight strong productivity as a positive structural feature.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (discussion): 32–34. Both discussion sources agree on dovish easing-leaning bias signal.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (discussion): Both score 38 exactly.

**Moderate divergence** (one source notably different):
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58–63. Discussion minutes at 58 vs. other three sources in 60–63 range. The minutes give more weight to the offset from sound mortgage/banking conditions.
- CREDIT_STRESS / CREDIT_SPREADS: 58–68. Discussion transcript at 68 (highest); discussion minutes at 58 (lowest). Staff sources in between (60, 65).
- EQUITY_STRESS / EQUITY_VALUATION: 58–72. Discussion transcript at 72 (most prominent); staff transcript at 58 (most restrained). Difference reflects minutes/transcript summarization and staff vs. participant framing.

**Larger divergence** (discussion sources only, structural by design):
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Disc-T 35 vs. Disc-M 22. The transcript preserves individual dissenting voices at length; the minutes compress the minority to "a few" expressing a mild preference.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Disc-T 73 vs. Disc-M 50. The transcript records multiple extended individual treatments of financial markets (McDonough, Greenspan, Kohn, Ferguson, Minehan); the minutes produce a single comprehensive paragraph covering the same themes.
