### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Scores**: Disc-T 61 | Disc-M 57 | Staff-T 52 | Staff-M 53

**Disc-T evidence**:
- MR. HOENIG: "Core inflation, by whatever measure you pick... drifted higher last year. Not only did core inflation drift higher, it was unexpectedly higher than earlier projections... I am concerned that, with less slack in the economy this year, we will end up with yet higher inflation."
- MR. GUYNN: "Both goods and services inflation are now showing signs of picking up... the surge in PCE and PPI data in some recent months."
- MS. BIES: "Over the last few months, we have seen core inflation move modestly higher... unit labor costs began rising in the second quarter of 2004... and grew at 2.3 percent in the last quarter."
- MR. POOLE: "I view the inflation forecast more as a median... the distribution is skewed to the right—substantially higher probability that we could have a ½-point upside surprise."

**Disc-M evidence**:
- "pressures on inflation have picked up in recent months and pricing power is more evident"
- "many participants indicated that their uncertainty about the intensity of inflation pressures had risen... the distribution of possible inflation outcomes was now tilted a little to the upside"
- "many participants stated that they expected total inflation to diminish and any rise in core consumer inflation to be limited"

**Staff-T evidence**:
- MR. STOCKTON: "we have raised the projected increase in core PCE prices to 1¾ percent in 2005 and 1½ percent in 2006—about ¼ percentage point higher than our previous projection in both years"
- MR. STOCKTON: "price developments over the intermeeting period have been troubling... higher energy and materials prices are adding to business costs, and higher prices for imports are lessening competitive pressures on the pricing decisions of domestic producers"
- MR. STOCKTON: "the most disquieting development on the inflation front has not been the run-up in energy and commodity prices, but has been the apparent rise in inflation expectations... as best as we can judge by readings from the inflation swaps market"

**Staff-M evidence**:
- "Core consumer price inflation over the same period was fairly subdued."
- Staff forecast: "Inflation in core consumer prices was seen as being boosted a bit by the effects of higher import and energy costs in the near term but still largely contained."
- "Consumer prices edged higher in January after having been flat in December."

**Observation**: All four sources register inflation pressure modestly above neutral. Disc-T scores highest (61) because individual speakers (Hoenig, Guynn, Bies, Santomero) express stronger concern in direct speech than the synthesized minutes convey. Staff sources score lower (52–53), anchored to the Greenbook's 1¾% core PCE projection still below 2%. The gap between Disc-T and Staff-T (9 points) reflects the difference between participant rhetoric and staff's more formal quantitative framing.
