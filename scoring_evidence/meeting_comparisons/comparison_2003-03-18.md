### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores: DT=30, DM=22, ST=25, SM=30**

| Source | Representative Quotes |
|---|---|
| **DT** | "wage and price pressures obviously remain pretty well subdued" (Hoenig); "core PCE price index is now forecast to go up by just 1¼ percent in 2004" (Parry); "with a sizable output gap and low and declining inflation...could be deflationary rather than inflationary" (Kohn); "we may experience some sort of disinflation...this Committee should be prepared to deal with the risk of deflation" (Broaddus) |
| **DM** | "the likelihood of continued low or even declining inflation was high, especially given expectations of persisting slack in resource utilization"; "members saw further disinflation in core prices as a distinct possibility over the next several quarters"; "well-entrenched expectations of low inflation" |
| **ST** | Inflation subdued; war-related energy elevated headline but core declining; output gap wide and widening; deflation risk from prior meetings still relevant; core PCE ~1% |
| **SM** | "Most measures of core consumer prices and labor costs decelerated in the twelve-month period ended in January"; "core price inflation for finished goods eased"; "the persistence of underutilized resources was expected to maintain some downward pressure on core price inflation over the forecast period" |

**Observation:** All four sources place inflation clearly below target with a disinflationary direction. DM scores lowest (22) — the minutes language ("distinct possibility" of further disinflation) is somewhat more alarming in tone than the transcript's individual voices. DT and SM converge at 30; ST at 25. Deflation is a tail risk across sources.

The SM score (28) directly contradicts this, using "considerable monetary ease" language from the same source document. This discrepancy warrants attention.

## Notes

- The staff transcript (ST) FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS score (60) is the only cross-source outlier above 50 for a dimension where other sources score below 50. This reflects a construct ambiguity: the ST scorer interpreted financial conditions as net-tightening due to equity/confidence effects overriding rate accommodation, while the SM scorer (same source document, different scorer) found the "considerable monetary ease" language controlling.
- The UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED dimension shows the largest absolute transcript-vs-minutes gap (82 vs 72) for discussion sources. The Knightian uncertainty framing — explicitly philosophical in the transcript — is compressed in the minutes to "unusually high degree of uncertainty."
- All sources agree on the direction of every core economic dimension (below-50 for inflation, output gap, labor, consumption/investment). There are no polarity reversals.
- Meeting-level flag: Iraq War eve meeting. Committee omitted balance-of-risks statement, citing inability to assign probability distributions (Knightian uncertainty). This is historically unusual and documented in the transcript.
