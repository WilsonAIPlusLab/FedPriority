# FOMC Meeting Comparison: 2000-05-16

**Meeting context**: Late-stage dot-com boom. The Fed escalated from 25bp to 50bp increments, raising the federal funds rate to 6.5%. Economy running well above trend with historically tight labor markets and rising inflation concerns. This was Mike Prell's final FOMC meeting as chief economist.

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

- MR. BROADDUS: "it seems to me that there is at least some evidence that we are experiencing some increase in actual inflation... the 12-month change in the core CPI was 2.2 percent... approaching a rate that is 1/2 point higher than it was just a few months ago"
- MR. PARRY: "the news on inflation itself has been worrisome. With inflation coming in on the high side in the first quarter, we now have had two straight quarters with inflation rates that were above the previously established level"
- MR. GUYNN: "the inflation outlook has clearly deteriorated"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (counterweight): "there is no evidence at this stage that we are experiencing a real underlying deterioration in inflation of the kind that we typically have seen in the past"

*Transcript center-of-gravity*: Participants broadly see inflation as modestly above target and trending upward, with rising compensation costs as a key driver; Greenspan and Ferguson temper with more nuanced views.

**Minutes: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

- "risks of acceleration in core prices were now appreciably higher given current trends in aggregate demand, pressures on resources"
- "early signs of a possible upturn in underlying price inflation"
- "rising wages and benefits and increasing costs of nonlabor inputs could no longer be fully offset by improvements in productivity"
- Offsetting: "strong competition across much of the economy" constraining price increases

*Minutes center-of-gravity*: Members see inflation pressure as meaningfully above neutral — not yet realized broad inflation, but risks clearly elevated with early signs of pass-through from costs to prices.

## Cross-Track Observations

**Strongest agreements** (Discussion Transcript vs. Minutes): OUTPUT_GAP (65 vs. 65), LABOR_TIGHTNESS (73 vs. 73), FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (73 vs. 70). The economic diagnosis and policy direction are captured similarly in both formats.

**Largest discussion gaps**: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (35 vs. 12) and DISSENT_INTENSITY (30 vs. 12). Individual speakers' concerns about stock market volatility and individual dissenting/reluctant votes are substantially smoothed in the minutes synthesis.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS divergence**: Transcript scores 58 (tighter than neutral) while minutes scores 42 (slightly accommodative). Ferguson's and Greenspan's explicit descriptions of corporate bond rate increases, reduced debt issuance, and tightening bank standards appear in the transcript but are not reflected in the synthesized minutes, which emphasize the equity wealth-effect transmission channel.

**Staff consistency**: Staff transcript and minutes are closely aligned on core economic conditions (INFLATION_PRESSURE, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, DOLLAR_STRENGTH). The main staff divergence is EQUITY_VALUATION (18 vs. 30): Prell's "speculative bubble of extraordinary proportions" assessment in the transcript is not captured in the staff review section of the minutes.

**Discussion vs. Staff alignment**: Both tracks agree on OUTPUT_GAP (~65), LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~70-73), and INFLATION_PRESSURE (~60-63). The discussion track produces a higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN reading than would be derivable from staff alone, reflecting that policy advocacy came from participants rather than staff.
