### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript Score: 60 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Participants see headline and core inflation as having risen notably from 2010 lows to modestly above the 2% target range, with a real division between those who view this as persistent (hawkish minority) and those expecting transitory factors to reverse (moderate majority). The current reading is clearly elevated relative to prior period but expected by the majority to return below 2%.

Quotes:
- MR. LACKER: "Over the past three months, core PCE inflation has been running at an annual rate of about 2.3 percent...This looks like a fairly persistent step-up."
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "Over the past six months, annualized core PCE inflation was 1½ percent; this is a sharp acceleration...Such a rapid rate of increase in six-month PCE core inflation has occurred but twice in the past 15 years."
- MS. YELLEN: "core PCE inflation in the three months ending in April...were 1.9 percent and 2.5 percent, respectively...a large fraction of this increase was attributable to surging commodity and food prices and Japanese supply disruptions."
- Minority (hawkish): Plosser projects 2¼% in 2012 and sees a risk of "missing emerging signs that medium-term inflation is accelerating."
- Minority (dovish): Evans/Williams expect core and total PCE at ~1½% in 2012–13.

**Minutes Score: 58 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Inflation is currently elevated above target due to commodity prices and supply disruptions, with widespread price increases, but most participants expect it to subside; the upside risk camp widens uncertainty.

Quotes:
- "Higher prices for energy and other commodities had boosted inflation this year."
- "A number of participants pointed out that the recent faster pace of price increases was widespread across many categories of spending and was evident in inflation measures such as trimmed means or medians."
- "Most participants expected that much of the rise in headline inflation this year would prove transitory."
- "A number of participants judged the risks to the outlook for inflation as tilted to the upside."
- Minority: "A few participants saw a continuation of the current stance of monetary policy as posing some upside risk to inflation expectations." Others pointed to stable inflation expectations and slack limiting pressures.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: All participants agree the economy is running below potential with a persistent output gap; the dispute is only about whether the gap is structural/permanent (Lacker's "neoclassical" view) or cyclical (majority view). Score reflects consistent "slack remains, below potential" language across the full table.

Quotes:
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Since the beginning of the recovery, we have only grown slightly above potential, and our forecasts have been too optimistic...What accounts for the inability of this recovery to make significant headway in reducing the significant slack in the economy?"
- MR. LACKER: "Real GDP growth has averaged just 2¾ percent over the last two years, and the level of employment is almost 5 percent below its cyclical peak."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "output growth has been below what we think to be potential output growth...Overall, downside risks to growth have increased as the slow pace of the recovery brings the economy near stall speed."
- Minority: Kocherlakota suggests potential output may be lower than staff estimates, which would imply a smaller gap — but even on his view, current activity is at/below potential, not above.

**Minutes Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: The economy is operating well below potential with persistent slack, though recovering at a moderate pace; some participants question whether potential output itself has fallen, which would narrow the gap.

Quotes:
- "The economic recovery was continuing at a moderate pace, though somewhat more slowly than they had anticipated."
- "Persistent slack in labor and product markets would likely limit upward pressures on prices."
- "Several participants observed that...loss of skills caused by high levels of long-term unemployment...may have temporarily reduced the economy's level of potential output."
- Minority: "Others...saw the recent configuration of slower growth and higher inflation as suggesting that there might be less slack in labor and product markets than had been thought."

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Near-universal agreement that labor markets remain substantially below full employment with unemployment at 9.1%, wage growth subdued, and hiring plans cautious. This is firmly in the 31–40 "below full employment, slack remains" range.

Quotes:
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "respondents in our District...indicate...that labor markets are relatively slack...workers are widely available with muted wage pressures...unemployment in our District is well under 7 percent."
- MS. PIANALTO: "With unemployment still very high, growth in labor costs is likely to remain low."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "expecting the unemployment rate to remain above 8 percent at the end of 2012."
- MR. FISHER: Noted businesses are investing in productivity in lieu of hiring, suggesting labor market improvement is being structurally impeded.

**Minutes Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: The labor market is clearly loose with elevated unemployment, disappointing job growth, subdued wages, and businesses reluctant to hire, placing conditions well below full employment.

Quotes:
- "The most recent data on employment had been disappointing, and new claims for unemployment insurance remained elevated."
- "The recent deterioration in labor market conditions was a particular concern."
- "Wage gains were generally reported to be subdued."
- "Several participants reported feedback from business contacts who were delaying hiring."
- Narrow exception: "Wages for a few skilled job categories in which workers were in short supply were said to be increasing relatively more rapidly."

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Consumer spending is below trend, dampened by energy prices, housing wealth effects, and labor market caution. Business investment in equipment/software provides partial offset. Net position is below trend but not collapsing — more "sluggish" than "contracting."

Quotes:
- MS. YELLEN: "Higher food and gas prices have taken an obvious toll on cash-strapped consumers...the response of consumer spending to the increase in higher food and energy prices may have been abnormally large in recent months."
- MR. LOCKHART: "few of our contacts cited major changes to their plans for the remainder of the year...higher gasoline prices that reduced shopping trips or ate into consumer discretionary spending."
- MR. EVANS: "revisions to income, weak jobs and claims numbers in May, and the softness in second-quarter consumer spending throw cold water on my previously more optimistic forecast."
- Partial offset: Bullard and Lockhart note some sectors (energy, agriculture, high-tech, tourism) remain strong; Williams notes Silicon Valley boom.

**Minutes Score: 37 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Spending and investment are below trend — capex had been robust but is softening, housing remains depressed, and consumer spending is sluggish due to weak income growth and commodity price headwinds.

Quotes:
- "Business investment in equipment and software generally remained robust, but growth in new orders...appeared to have slowed."
- "The housing sector, including residential construction and home sales, remained depressed."
- "Sluggish growth of income and consumption."
- "Business contacts in some regions reported that they were reducing capital budgets."
- Partial offset: "Business contacts in some sectors—most notably energy and high tech—reported that activity and business sentiment had strengthened further."

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript Score: 44 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Financial conditions tightened modestly from accommodative levels during the intermeeting period (equity weakness, spread widening), but commercial banking liquidity remains ample and C&I lending competition is fierce. Net position is slightly tighter than neutral but not at a restrictive level.

Quotes:
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "Financial conditions weakened during the intermeeting period, and volatility increased. Equities, bond yields, and bond spreads all reacted to the disappointing economic news."
- MR. WILLIAMS: "lower Treasury yields, the faltering stock market, and the rise in high-yield bond spreads since our last meeting."
- MS. DUKE: "Financing conditions in the dealer space tightened noticeably in recent weeks...all banks characterize the competition, in C&I lending especially, as irrational."
- Note: Duke also observed substantial populations of borrowers who cannot access lower rates, suggesting impaired transmission rather than tight overall conditions per se.

**Minutes Score: 55 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Financial conditions are somewhat tight, with widening spreads, equity selloff, and tight credit for small firms, though large firms retain easy access; a mixed but moderately restrictive picture.

Quotes:
- "Equity markets had seen a broad selloff, and risk spreads for many corporate borrowers had widened noticeably."
- "Credit conditions remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms."
- "Large businesses that have access to capital markets continued to enjoy ready access to credit...on relatively attractive terms."
- Partial offset: "A few participants expressed concern that credit conditions in some sectors...might have eased too much" (agriculture).

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript Score: 36 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: Overwhelming consensus (~11 of ~15+ visible participants) supports Alternative B — maintaining accommodation at 0–0.25% with "extended period" language and completing QE2 without extension. A small minority (Plosser explicitly, Kocherlakota conditionally) lean toward near-term tightening signals. A few members (Rosengren, Williams, Evans) advocate contingency planning for more accommodation. The center sits firmly in dovish-hold territory.

Quotes:
- MR. LOCKHART: "I support alternative B...I think maintaining the current extent of accommodation will help mitigate some of the downside risks to the outlook."
- MR. WILLIAMS: "I favor alternative B...the current very accommodative stance of monetary policy remains entirely appropriate. Indeed, I would like to echo President Rosengren's comments: I worry that 2011 could become a replay of 2010."
- MR. PLOSSER: "Given inflation developments and the upside risk I see to inflation forecasts, I think we may very well need to take some action to withdraw some accommodation sooner than what's priced into the funds futures market...I believe we may need to take action before the end of the year."
- MR. FISHER: "I'm getting increasingly close to alternative C, but I'm going to suggest at this meeting that we should play it up the middle."

**Minutes Score: 42 ±10 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: The committee is in a hold posture with a slight dovish lean, having just completed QE2; the majority favors patience, with conditional voices on both sides but more weight given to the possibility of additional easing than to early tightening.

Quotes:
- "Members agreed that it was appropriate to maintain the Committee's current policy stance and accumulate further information."
- "A few members noted that...the Committee might have to consider providing additional monetary policy stimulus."
- "A few members viewed the increase in inflation risks as suggesting...removing policy accommodation sooner than currently anticipated."
- "Some participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow...it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript Score: 25 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: The forward guidance firmly commits to extended near-zero rates ("extended period" language retained, Kocherlakota's proposal for timing signals rejected). The tone is "low for long" with several participants explicitly flagging readiness for more accommodation. This corresponds to the 16–25 "mild easing bias, low for long emphasis" range.

Quotes:
- MS. YELLEN: "When we include 'extended period' language in the statement, we are saying the FOMC sees very low probability that it will need to tighten in three to six months...The funds rate target could well...stay exceptionally low for a very long time."
- VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: "It is really important that people don't think that spending so much time on this contingency plan means that exit necessarily lies close at hand."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Should the economy start growing less than potential with the unemployment rate rising and the inflation rate declining, we would need to consider additional asset purchases."
- Minority: Plosser and Kocherlakota's conditional signals of eventual tightening push the band upward.

**Minutes Score: 30 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity summary: The forward signal is mildly dovish — the committee is maintaining maximum accommodation with the extended period language, has just completed QE2, and the conditional next-step discussion leans toward possible further easing rather than tightening.

Quotes:
- "Appropriate to maintain the Committee's current policy stance and accumulate further information regarding the outlook for growth and inflation before deciding on the next policy step."
- "Some participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow...it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."
- QE2 just completed; "extended period" language maintained.
- Minority: "A few members" see conditions potentially warranting earlier removal of accommodation — but "several" and "some" lean toward more accommodation if needed.
