### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **48 ±7 [High confidence]** | **50 ±7 [Moderate confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Lacker: "Core PCE inflation averaged under 1.4 percent for the past five months, and overall PCE inflation was under 2 percent for both June and July."
- Pianalto: "The incoming inflation report continues to point to a gradual disinflation...I am still expecting core PCE inflation to be just under 2 percent."
- Kohn: "Upside inflation risks have not disappeared. Unit labor costs have been rising...Resource utilization remains high by historical standards."
- Plosser: "headline inflation remains quite elevated" [minority view noted in text]

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly see inflation pressure as having moderated to at or slightly below target (core PCE declining below 2%), with persistent but diminished upside risks. The dominant tone is encouragement and relief, not concern — a distinct shift from prior meetings.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "incoming data on core inflation continued to be favorable, and they generally were a little more confident that the decline in inflation earlier this year would be sustained"
- "Participants nonetheless remained concerned about possible upside risks to inflation. Higher benefit costs, rising unit labor costs more generally, reduced markups, and levels of resource utilization... that remained relatively high were all cited as factors"
- "Inflation expectations seemed to be contained"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation recently favorable and expectations contained, but participants see meaningful upside risks from costs and resource utilization. Balanced assessment.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **40 ±7 [High confidence]** | **44 ±6 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Yellen: "I've downgraded my forecast for growth in the fourth quarter...The downside risks to this forecast are substantial and worrisome."
- Plosser: "Return to trend growth...will be delayed by a couple of quarters...won't be under way until certainly late in 2008."
- Kohn: "Some downshift in GDP is highly likely over the next few quarters...the output gap is noticeably wider at the end of '08."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The consensus is that the economy is operating below potential and moving further below. The transition from near-potential to clearly below-potential growth is occurring in real time. No participant argues the economy is at or above trend.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "economic growth likely to run below its potential for a while"
- "recent data and anecdotal information pointed to a possible nascent slowdown in the pace of expansion"
- "risks to growth skewed to the downside"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy near but dipping below potential, with forward expectation clearly below potential. Not yet in contraction. Some noted "resilience of the economy" leaving open possibility that effects prove "limited."

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **55 ±8 [High confidence]** | **48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Plosser: "Some firms continue to support what has already been said — difficulty in finding qualified workers...Our three-state unemployment rate remains relatively low."
- Stern: "Employment has been sustained, and if anything, the reports of the scarcity of skilled labor have probably increased."
- Yellen: "The August employment report showed a marked deceleration in payroll employment growth over the past three months."
- Bernanke [summary]: "Labor markets do remain tight...but there have been some signs of slowing." [noted in Part 1 analysis]

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The labor market is still tight by historical standards — low unemployment, difficulty finding skilled workers — but is visibly softening. Nonfarm payrolls fell 4,000 in August, and revisions show prior months were weaker. Labor is above NAIRU but clearly transitioning downward.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "labor markets across the country generally remained fairly tight, with several participants citing continued reports of shortages of labor from their contacts in some sectors"
- "trend growth in jobs had fallen off even prior to the recent financial market strains, and participants judged that some further slowing of employment growth was likely"
- "initial claims for unemployment insurance did not indicate a substantial and widespread weakening in labor demand"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Currently still fairly tight but with clear softening trajectory. Near NAIRU but loosening at the margin. Several citing shortages vs. evidence of layoffs in financial services and construction create a moderate spread.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Moderate but below-trend, with significant downside risks from credit tightening weighing on both consumer and lower-rated business spending. Business investment seen as relatively resilient (internally financed) vs. consumer spending facing more headwinds.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **72 ±8 [High confidence]** | **67 ±8 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Rosengren: "Overnight Eurodollar trades...frequently been trading much higher than overnight fed funds...One-month and three-month LIBOR rates have remained very elevated...asset-backed commercial paper market has remained under stress."
- Yellen: "Availability of lending of some types, including subprime and alt-A mortgages, has diminished substantially or disappeared entirely. Moreover, banks and other financial institutions are imposing tighter terms and conditions across a broad range of corporate and household lending programs."
- Kohn: "Greatly reduced funding in term markets spreading the constriction of credit potentially well beyond the mortgage and leveraged-loan markets...an already weak housing market has been most directly affected."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions have tightened significantly across multiple dimensions — subprime/alt-A funding gone, jumbo mortgage spreads elevated, ABCP market stressed, LIBOR-fed funds spread elevated, tighter lending standards broadly. Creditworthy prime borrowers can still access credit, preventing a 85+ score, but the stress is widespread and severe for non-prime and structured-finance sectors.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "banks might need to take a large volume of assets onto their balance sheets... might well lead banks to tighten the availability of credit to households and firms"
- "subprime mortgages remained essentially unavailable, little activity was evident in the markets for other nonprime mortgages, and prime jumbo mortgage borrowers faced higher rates and tighter lending standards"
- "risk spreads probably would be wider and credit terms tighter than they had been a few months ago"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Significant tightening in credit conditions across multiple channels, though not yet a full credit freeze. Conforming mortgages and investment-grade corporate credit still functioning. Conditions "seemed to have improved somewhat in the most recent days."

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **20 ±5 [High confidence]** | **27 ±5 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Yellen: "I strongly support the Bluebook alternative B. I think a 50 basis point cut right away is appropriate."
- Rosengren: "I prefer a more aggressive approach. A 50 basis point reduction will offset some of the tightening that has already occurred."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Near-consensus for a 50bp immediate cut. Approximately 14-15 of 17 participants explicitly support or accept 50bp. Only Fisher definitively advocates 25bp; Lacker has a "mild" preference for 25 but explicitly says he "could support" 50.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "all members favored an easing of the stance of monetary policy"
- "all members agreed that a rate cut of 50 basis points at this meeting was the most prudent course of action"
- "Without such policy action, members saw a risk that tightening credit conditions and an intensifying housing correction would lead to significant broader weakness in output and employment"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unanimously dovish — a 50bp cut to preemptively counter financial tightening and downside growth risks. None — unanimity on action.

### Dimension 8: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

| **Score** | **90 ±5 [High confidence]** | **75 ±6 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**

**Minutes quotes:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial stability/conditions are the dominant topic of the meeting, rivaling or exceeding the traditional economic assessment in emphasis. All participants engage with this topic prominently.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **35 ±8 [High confidence]** | **32 ±8 [Moderate confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Bernanke: "We should be prepared to take this back, and I state that for the record." (No commitment to further cuts.)
- Mishkin: "We do not want to say to the markets that we have to keep on going when we're hoping that we won't have to keep on going."
- Hoenig: "I would like to be certain that we don't convey that other moves will follow in the future unless the economic and inflation outlooks require it."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The Committee adopts alternative B — uncertainty language, not an explicit downward bias. Multiple participants explicitly argue against signaling further cuts. The forward signal is "we acted, we're watching, we may need to do more but we're not pre-committing." Easing lean with strong data dependence.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "the Committee decided to refrain from providing an explicit assessment of the balance of risks"
- "Future actions would depend on how economic prospects were affected by evolving market developments and by other factors"
- Context: 50bp cut with "growth likely to run below its potential for a while" implies easing lean

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Deliberately ambiguous forward signal with an implicit easing lean. Data-dependent but in a context that suggests further easing is more likely than reversal. The deliberate ambiguity itself reflects some uncertainty about the forward path among participants.

### Dimension 10: UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **72 ±7 [High confidence]** | **75 ±6 [High confidence]** |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Kohn: "I feel that I can honestly say that the uncertainties around the output forecast were indeed larger than usual this time...we don't have many degrees of freedom to test hypotheses about the sorts of relationships that we're talking about here."
- Pianalto: "I don't pretend to have a very confident forecast of where the bottom of the residential real estate market lies...our forecast models are not well equipped to accommodate the role that shifting expectations play in shaping the economic outlook."
- Lacker: "Beyond housing, any projections of real effects from events in financial markets seem like guesswork at this point."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Essentially every participant explicitly flags unusually elevated uncertainty. The common theme is that models cannot adequately capture the financial shock's transmission to the real economy. Multiple participants note they have "very little hard data" and that outcomes are "hard to know."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "the outlook for economic activity as characterized by particularly high uncertainty, with the risks to growth skewed to the downside"
- "the incoming data in many cases were of limited value in assessing the likely evolution of economic activity and prices"
- Committee refrained from balance-of-risks statement because it "could give the mistaken impression that the Committee was more certain about the economic outlook than was in fact the case"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Very high uncertainty explicitly acknowledged. The refusal to provide a balance-of-risks assessment is an unusually strong signal of uncertainty. Broadly shared view with no dissent.
