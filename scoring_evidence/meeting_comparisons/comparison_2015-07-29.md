### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "Inflation remains noticeably and persistently below our 2 percent target...the recent data suggest an underlying trend of price inflation of around 1¼ to 1½ percent."
- Evans: "on the pricing front, there were no reports of any pickup in inflation. To the contrary, anyone with exposure to commodity markets was talking about downward price pressures."
- Kocherlakota: "core PCE inflation has been below our adopted target of 2 percent for nearly seven years."
- Powell: "staff estimates that total PCE inflation was only 0.2 percent for the 12 months ending in June, and that core inflation was only 1¼ percent."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Every participant acknowledges inflation is clearly below the 2 percent PCE target (core approximately 1.25–1.7 percent), with temporary factors (oil, dollar) holding it lower; most expect eventual return to target, but the current state is clearly deflationary relative to the mandate.

Minority views (transcript): Lacker and Mester see recent firming signs (median CPI near 2.25 percent, core CPI at 2.4 percent annualized recently) as somewhat more encouraging; Bullard notes private-sector forecasts project headline PCE near target by year-end 2016.

---

**Minutes score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and further decreases in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Some participants cited downside risks to inflation, pointing to the absence of any noticeable response of inflation to the reduction in resource slack over the past several years."
- "Most participants still expected that the downward pressure on inflation from the previous declines in energy prices and the effects of past dollar appreciation would prove to be temporary."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Inflation running below target with some downside risks, but most participants expected a gradual return to 2% — placing this in the "below target; slack keeping prices in check" range.

Minority views (minutes): Some cited alternative measures (trimmed mean, median CPI, core CPI) running higher, slightly tempering the below-target signal.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript score: 46 ±6 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- Brainard: "we have not seen a bounceback sufficient to compensate for the slowdown in the first quarter...still-present economic slack."
- Yellen: "I still believe that we still have a ways to go beyond what is likely to be accomplished in the next couple of months" (before reaching full employment); "starting the normalization process before all slack in the economy has been eliminated."
- Evans: "resource gaps essentially closing by late 2016" (not yet closed).
- Williams: "With growth above trend, I expect continued progress in closing output and unemployment gaps." (Forward-looking; current gaps not yet closed.)

Center-of-gravity (transcript): The economy is approaching but not yet at potential; resource gaps are closing but not closed. Most participants see the output gap as slightly negative and narrowing.

Minority views (transcript): Bullard argues the unemployment path is more optimistic (implying output closer to or above potential by some measures); Mester says "at or nearly at...maximum employment," implying the economy may be near or at potential.

---

**Minutes score: 44 ±5 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- "Economic activity had been expanding moderately in recent months."
- "Most members saw room for some additional progress in reducing labor market slack, although several viewed current labor market conditions as at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment."
- "A few participants observed that although GDP growth appeared to have picked up in recent months relative to the first-quarter pace, the level of GDP remained lower than had been projected earlier in the year."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Economy slightly below potential but closing the gap; "underutilization" still cited but "diminishing."

Minority views (minutes): Several viewed labor conditions as already at or near maximum employment, pulling the range upward.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- Mester: "the evidence suggests that the economy is at or nearly at the Fed's mandated monetary policy goal of maximum employment."
- Williams: "we are finally closing in on full employment...we're very near to achieving our employment objective."
- Fischer: "the unemployment rate is near most estimates of the longer-run rate of unemployment projected by participants in the SEP."
- Brainard: "Looking at a wide range of labor market indicators suggests to me that some slack still remains, even with overall unemployment at 5.3 percent."
- Tarullo: "I still believe there is a nontrivial amount of additional slack that could be taken up."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Unemployment at 5.3 percent is near but arguably slightly above NAIRU (~5.0–5.1 percent per most estimates); most participants see the labor market as near full employment, with some noting residual slack from participation and involuntary part-time; tightening continues at strong pace (200K+ jobs/month).

Minority views (transcript): Kocherlakota and Tarullo argue the natural rate may be lower than 5 percent, implying more slack remains; Lacker and George believe labor conditions fully or more than justify liftoff now.

---

**Minutes score: 46 ±6 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- "Underutilization of labor resources had continued to diminish" and "several participants contended that...some noticeable margins of slack remained."
- "Anecdotal reports of firms having concerns about retaining workers and facing difficulties in filling even medium- and lower-skilled jobs."
- "Several viewed current labor market conditions as at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Labor market approaching full employment with remaining pockets of slack; near NAIRU but not clearly past it.

Minority views (minutes): Split between those seeing remaining slack (high part-time, discouraged workers) and those seeing near-full employment with hiring difficulties.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript score: 50 ±7 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- Fischer: "Personal consumption expenditures are expected to continue to grow at an annual rate of a little more than 3 percent...residential investment...more than 5 percent."
- Brainard: "Consumption remains moderate, buoyed by strong fundamentals, but apparently not strong enough to overcome the continued drag on investment and production from earlier oil price declines."
- Dudley: "Household spending should be well supported over the near term by solid job gains."
- Evans: "businesses that manufacture heavy equipment continue to experience sluggish domestic demand."
- Harker: "consumer will underpin an economy that should perform at or a bit above trend."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): Consumer spending is solid and growing at moderate pace (~3 percent); residential investment picking up; business/capital goods investment subdued, especially energy-related; net exports a drag. Net picture is at-trend or very slightly above for consumption, below-trend for business investment — overall balanced near 50.

Minority views (transcript): Lockhart and Mester are more optimistic; Brainard more cautious (investment drag, tepid).

---

**Minutes score: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- "Participants generally expected consumer spending to rise moderately over the near term" with "continued gains in employment and income, high household net worth, and low gasoline prices" as supports.
- "Business fixed investment remained soft even as the drag from the sharp contraction in drilling rigs over the first half of this year appeared to be fading."
- "Net exports to continue to subtract from GDP growth over the second half of the year."

Center-of-gravity (minutes): Consumer spending moderate, business investment soft, net exports a drag — net position roughly at trend, slightly below.

Minority views (minutes): A couple concerned about consumer spending disappointment despite favorable conditions.

Minority views (transcript): Kocherlakota and Tarullo note some valuation pressures in certain asset classes; Tarullo references leverage concerns outside prudentially regulated institutions. These are noted but not alarm-level.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript score: 59 ±8 [High confidence]**

Representative quotes:
- Fischer: "The criteria for beginning normalization have very nearly been met, and we need to be willing to move."
- Lacker: "the case for raising rates has been getting even stronger."
- Williams: "I anticipate that it will be appropriate to raise the target funds rate at our September meeting."
- Kocherlakota: "I recommend alternative A." [explicitly dovish]
- Brainard: "I would favor liftoff in September only if both inflation and labor market conditions make it compelling, and I'd like to maintain optionality on December."
- Evans: "I'm still skeptical that the data will support reasonable confidence in this inflation outlook by the end of the year."
- Yellen: "liftoff at our next meeting, in September, is a distinct possibility."

Center-of-gravity (transcript): The majority favor imminent tightening (September liftoff) with current hold and pre-commitment signals; this is a "lean hawkish" meeting where the policy rate is 0 but tightening is clearly anticipated and favored by most. Score of 59 reflects "hold steady with tightening bias; approaching time to begin."

Minority views (transcript): Kocherlakota (strongly dovish, prefers alternative A and defer liftoff), Evans and Brainard (dovish lean, uncertain about inflation confidence), Tarullo (moderate dove on inflation criteria).
