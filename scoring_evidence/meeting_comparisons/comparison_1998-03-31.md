# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1998-03-31

## Meeting Context
March 1998. Late 1990s expansion. U.S. economy growing above trend, labor markets extremely tight, inflation low due to temporary favorable supply factors (falling oil, dollar appreciation, Asian import competition). Asian financial crisis ongoing but U.S. largely insulated. Fed funds rate at 5.50%, held steady for nearly two years. Decision: hold rates, shift to asymmetric tightening bias. One formal dissent (Jordan, favoring immediate tightening).

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Transcript: 45 ±8 [High confidence] | Minutes: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the overall inflation rate has shown no evidence of accelerating at this stage... we are beginning to see pressures emerge in the cost structure"
- PARRY: "core price inflation probably should come in at a rate of around 2 percent" but "it is uncertain... whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure"
- MOSKOW: "Every council member expects inflation to accelerate noticeably over the next 12 months"
- MINEHAN: "Wage and benefit increases have crept up by some measures into the 4 percent range"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "price inflation remained quite low — in fact, it was still declining by some measures — and there was little evidence of any potential acceleration in current price data or in anecdotal reports"
- "the risk that inflation might move higher over the longer run seemed to have increased"
- "any upsurge in inflation seemed unlikely in the nearer term"
- Jordan dissented on grounds that "recent price statistics understated the trend rates of inflation"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Inflation currently low (~2% core) due to temporary favorable factors; compensation running ~4%; most participants see building pressures ahead as temporary factors fade.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Members see current inflation as low and benign; acknowledge rising risks over the longer run; the score reflects low current readings pulled modestly toward midpoint by forward concerns.

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Transcript: 60 ±5 [High confidence] | Minutes: 62 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- POOLE: "Economic activity is bumping along... above and beyond capacity"
- MEYER: "it is not imprudent to operate for a while beyond the point of long-run sustainable capacity"
- GREENSPAN: "we have an economic performance that is essentially unsustainable"
- MOSKOW: "GDP growth consistently above its trend rate"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Domestic demand was exceeding expectations and was likely to continue to increase rapidly"
- Members concerned that foreign trade restraint "might not exert enough restraint on aggregate demand to slow the expansion to a sustainable pace in line with the growth of the economy's potential"
- "economic activity continued to grow rapidly during the early months of 1998"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Near-universal agreement the economy is operating above potential; GDP growth well above trend; not extreme overheating since no product/materials shortages, but clearly above potential.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy clearly operating above potential with strong domestic demand; foreign trade and inventory adjustments provide partial offset.

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 68 ±5 [High confidence] | Minutes: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- JORDAN: "550,000 employees... over 50,000 [job openings]. That total is 10 percent of current employment"
- BOEHNE: "The most frequently heard comment is that qualified workers are scarce and they are becoming more expensive"
- BROADDUS: employer "now defines a 'hot job prospect' as somebody who shows up for an interview and passes a drug test"
- PARRY: "there is little question that labor markets will remain very tight this year. There is already significant upward pressure on wages"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Anecdotal reports from across the nation continued to suggest exceptionally tight labor markets and growing indications of somewhat faster increases in labor compensation"
- "Despite tightening labor markets" / "adding to pressures on labor resources"
- Unemployment at 4.6%, equaling expansion low

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Every single participant describes labor markets as "very tight"; compensation growth ~4%; extraordinary anecdotes of difficulty hiring at all skill levels.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Members describe labor markets as exceptionally tight with acceleration in compensation; consistent with "very tight" range; no disagreement on tightness, only on policy response.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Transcript: 66 ±5 [High confidence] | Minutes: 66 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the extraordinary domestic demand that we are observing is almost surely a spillover from the unbelievable uptrend in the ratio of wealth to disposable income"
- MEYER: "continued exceptional momentum in private domestic demand and payroll employment"
- BROADDUS: consumer spending "going great guns"
- MOSKOW: "Consumer fundamentals are strong and firms seem poised to remain big spenders for high-tech capital goods"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Consumer expenditures, especially for durable goods, had risen sharply thus far this year"
- "little reason to anticipate substantial slowing in the growth of consumer or business expenditures"
- "Attractive financing conditions and favorable business confidence also were expected to support substantial further growth in business investment, especially in 'high-tech' equipment"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Domestic demand exceptionally strong across consumer spending, housing, and business investment; some drag from net exports and potential inventory correction but domestic fundamentals overwhelm negatives.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Both consumer spending and business investment well above trend, fueled by wealth effects, confidence, and favorable financing; housing at a "high level"; inventory moderation and trade deficit noted as partial offsets.

---

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Transcript: 18 ±5 [High confidence] | Minutes: 20 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- MINEHAN: "monetary policy is not tight; it is not even neutral. It is accommodative to an increasingly speculative environment"
- MEYER: "equity prices are soaring, credit availability is ample, underwriting standards may have eased somewhat, loan pricing is aggressive, and money growth is rapid"
- GREENSPAN: "yield spreads have fallen to unbelievable numbers, the slack attitude of lending officers has become a potential concern"
- FERGUSON: "Risk spreads for private debt instruments are quite narrow... credit to private nonfinancial borrowers... is running well above the pace of the last couple of years"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "accommodative conditions in key segments of financial markets"
- "ample liquidity and accommodative financial conditions"
- "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads in credit markets as additional evidence that financial conditions were not restraining final demands very much"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are extremely accommodative — loose credit, narrow spreads, soaring equities, ample liquidity, aggressive lending; multiple participants describe conditions as fueling speculation; universal agreement conditions are very loose.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative — low cost of capital, elevated equity wealth, narrow credit spreads, strong money growth; no disagreement on the assessment.

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Transcript: 62 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 56 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "I think we ought to be moving back to asymmetry... I do not think it is appropriate to move at this stage" but "we are running out of time"
- BROADDUS: "it will not surprise you or anyone else that I would still prefer to take action today"
- MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting prepared to vote for an increase of 25 basis points"
- JORDAN: "I would strongly prefer taking action today"
- GRAMLICH: "if somebody had proposed that we raise the fed funds rate by 25 basis points, I would have supported that, too"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "all but one of the members indicated... they preferred or could accept a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance... with a shift from the current symmetrical directive to an asymmetrical directive tilted toward restraint"
- "Most saw little urgency to tighten policy at this meeting"
- "several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future"
- Jordan dissented, favoring immediate tightening; "some" noted tightening "was not inevitable" and could prove "premature"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Committee voted to hold but adopted tightening bias; at least 4 members would have preferred an immediate hike; no one advocated easing; lean is moderately hawkish — hold now but prepare to tighten soon.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Hold with a hawkish tilt; majority supports patience now but acknowledges tightening is increasingly likely; one formal dissent (Jordan) for immediate action.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Transcript: 38 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- BROADDUS: "I would still prefer to take action today"
- MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting prepared to vote for an increase of 25 basis points... and I also came prepared to dissent"
- GRAMLICH: "if somebody had proposed that we raise the fed funds rate by 25 basis points, I would have supported that, too"
- RIVLIN: "it is not inconceivable that we will be back in six weeks when something... has weakened the expansion and we may not be quite as sure"

**Minutes evidence:**
- One formal dissent (Jordan) for immediate tightening
- "some" thought tightening was "not inevitable" while "several" thought it could come "in the near future"
- "all but one member supported" the directive

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** No formal dissents recorded for most members, but 3-4 members stated preference for immediate action; disagreement is about timing (now vs. soon), not direction; all agree on asymmetric tightening bias.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** One formal dissent with broad agreement among remaining members, though with varying degrees of urgency; Jordan's dissent is well-articulated but isolated.

---

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Transcript: 55 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of stock market valuation, equity premium, earnings expectations, wealth effect cycle, and vulnerability of the financial system
- JORDAN: "I also continue to be concerned that we may never see the effects of monetary excesses in output prices, but rather that we will see them in asset prices"
- MINEHAN: "I think we face a real risk of feeding the speculative excess that will ultimately come to haunt us"
- RIVLIN: "our worries about speculation, loose credit"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The recent further increases in equity prices from already high levels played an important role in the assessments of several members. The stock market's rise was viewed as somewhat puzzling"
- "several members again reported indications of speculative nonresidential construction"
- "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads in credit markets"
- Asian financial turmoil discussed at length

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial stability and speculative excess receive substantial attention from many participants; the stock market, real estate speculation, and loose credit discussed at length; significant but not dominant topic.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial stability issues receive moderate attention — equity market valuations, speculative construction, Asian turmoil, and narrow risk spreads discussed meaningfully by multiple members; a significant secondary theme.

---

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Transcript: 68 ±7 [High confidence] | Minutes: 64 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the clock is starting to tick... unless the evidence of an Asian slowdown is fairly pronounced and/or the stock market goes in the other direction, we will be singing a different tune the next time we meet"
- BOEHNE: "we indeed will have to face up to making a policy adjustment and probably more than once"
- KELLEY: "a move to tighten is becoming increasingly advisable"
- Discussion about laying groundwork for tightening via policy speeches

**Minutes evidence:**
- Shift to asymmetric directive "tilted toward restraint"
- "the Committee's judgment that the information... had altered the inflation risks enough to make some tightening a likely prospect in the not too distant future"
- "several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future"
- "Some" members noted tightening "was not inevitable," tempering the signal slightly

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Forward signal clearly toward tightening; committee discussed how to prepare markets for a rate hike; asymmetric directive is an explicit tightening signal; conditionality (depends on Asia, stock market) keeps score from being higher.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Clear forward signal toward tightening, though data-dependent; asymmetric directive is an explicit bias signal; score in "mild tightening bias" range.

---

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Transcript: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence] | Minutes: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Transcript evidence:**
- PARRY: "it is uncertain in my view whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure"
- RIVLIN: "it is not inconceivable that we will be back in six weeks when something... has weakened the expansion and we may not be quite as sure"
- GREENSPAN: Discussion of unknown timing of Asian effects and stock market correction
- STERN: "the kicker here is productivity. I don't want to get caught up in how we measure productivity"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "the factors that had produced the favorable inflation results of recent years were not all well understood, and consequently expectations of greater price pressures had to be tentative"
- "outcomes for economic growth and exchange rates were still very much in doubt" (re: Asia)
- "the ultimate extent of retarding effects from the financial turmoil in Asia was still uncertain"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Moderate uncertainty; key unknowns are (1) magnitude/timing of Asian spillover, (2) whether the stock market will correct, (3) sustainability of productivity gains, (4) persistence of favorable supply shocks; but strong confidence in the near-term domestic picture.

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily around the inflation outlook (why is inflation so low despite tight labor markets?) and Asian crisis spillovers; not extreme — members reasonably confident in domestic picture but uncertain about external risks.

### S1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Staff Transcript: 44 ±5 [High confidence] | Staff Minutes: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Staff Transcript evidence:**
- CPI "very favorable"; oil prices down; compensation forecast raised "just a touch more"
- Center-of-gravity: Below neutral — benign

**Staff Minutes evidence:**
- "price inflation abated further, primarily as a consequence of large declines in energy prices"
- "producer price inflation was negligible"
- "Tight labor markets appeared to be putting some upward pressure on labor compensation, but the pickup was limited"
- Staff forecast: "inflation was expected to increase somewhat in response to persisting tightness in labor markets and a diminishing drag from non-oil import prices"

**Staff Transcript center-of-gravity:** Below neutral; benign; energy and imports holding down headline while labor costs nudge slightly higher.

**Staff Minutes center-of-gravity:** Current inflation low and still declining, held down by energy; staff flags labor cost pressures building — net below neutral but not deeply so.

### S2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Staff Transcript: 56 ±5 [High confidence] | Staff Minutes: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Staff Transcript evidence:**
- GDP revised upward; economy above potential
- Center-of-gravity: Above potential

**Staff Minutes evidence:**
- "the economy continued to expand rapidly during the early months of 1998"
- "pressures on production resources would abate to a degree as output growth slowed" (implying current pressures exist)
- Staff forecasting needed slowdown to return to sustainable pace

**Staff Transcript center-of-gravity:** Economy expanding above potential; upward-revised GDP growth.

**Staff Minutes center-of-gravity:** Economy expanding rapidly, above sustainable pace; staff forecasting needed deceleration — clearly above potential.

### S3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Staff Transcript evidence:**
- Labor market "extremely taut"; anecdotal acceleration of compensation; recruiting and training costs up
- Center-of-gravity: Very tight

**Staff Minutes evidence:**
- "civilian unemployment rate edged down to 4.6 percent in February, equaling its low for the current economic expansion"
- "persisting pressures on employment costs associated with tight labor markets"

**Staff Transcript center-of-gravity:** Extremely tight labor market with accelerating compensation anecdotally.

**Staff Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unemployment at cycle lows, labor explicitly described as "tight," payroll growth "sharp."

**Staff Transcript: 58 ±5 [High confidence] | Staff Minutes: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Staff Transcript evidence:**

**Staff Minutes evidence:**
- "strong growth in private domestic spending for consumption, housing, and business equipment"

- "sales of existing homes reaching a record high"

**Staff Transcript center-of-gravity:** Market-driven spending exceptionally strong; wealth effect driving consumption.

**Staff Minutes center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending, housing, and business equipment all showing strong or record performance — well above trend.

**Staff Minutes center-of-gravity:** Rapid monetary growth, strong credit expansion, rising equity markets — all point to loose/accommodative financial conditions.
