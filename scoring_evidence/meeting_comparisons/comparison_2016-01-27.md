### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 32 ±7 [High] | 33 ±6 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Fischer: "the staff's forecast of PCE inflation for 2016 that is only 0.7 percent, with even the forecast of the core PCE inflation at 1.3 percent"
- Rosengren: "the Tealbook expects that total PCE inflation for Q1 of 2016 will be negative, and that core PCE inflation will be 1.2 percent in Q1"
- Yellen: "core inflation is now projected to remain near 1¼ percent...through end of this year, while headline inflation is expected to remain below 1 percent"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** All participants agree inflation is substantially below the 2% target, with headline near zero and core around 1.25–1.3%. The persistent string of oil price declines and dollar appreciation continue to suppress both headline and core. A handful of participants (Fischer, Mester) retain confidence that inflation will return to 2% over the medium term, but even they acknowledge the path is delayed further.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "a number of participants indicated that, in light of recent developments, they viewed the outlook for inflation as somewhat more uncertain or saw the risks as being to the downside."
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had declined to historically low levels."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Participants see inflation clearly below target with additional downward forces from energy and the dollar; most expect a gradual return to 2% but downside risks are increasing.

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 63 ±7 [High] | 63 ±6 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Brainard: "financial conditions have tightened considerably since we last met...roughly equivalent to two additional hikes in the federal funds rate"
- Yellen: "net GDP effects of the rise in the dollar, the fall in stock prices, and the movements in longer-term interest rates are roughly equivalent to an increase in the federal funds rate of at least 50 basis points"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions tightened substantially over the intermeeting period — equity market down 7–10%, credit spreads widened considerably (especially in energy sector), dollar appreciated further. **Minutes evidence:**
- "Almost all participants cited a number of recent events as indicative of tighter financial conditions in the United States; these events included declines in equity prices, a widening in credit spreads, a further rise in the exchange value of the dollar, and an increase in financial market volatility."
- "Some participants also pointed to significantly tighter financing conditions for speculative-grade firms and small businesses, and to reports of tighter standards at banks for C&I and CRE loans."
- "The effects of these financial developments, if they were to persist, may be roughly equivalent to those from further firming in monetary policy."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions have tightened notably across multiple dimensions; participants view this as a significant development with policy-equivalent implications.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 42 ±6 [High] | 48 ±6 [High] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Lockhart: "I support the substance of alternative B...to position the Committee as agnostic about a March policy action"; comfortable with April if data don't support March
- Tarullo: "quite surprised if it was appropriate in March to increase rates"; prefers alt-A language signaling downside risks

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Universal support for holding rates unchanged at this meeting. Most participants acknowledge the hiking path has been implicitly shallowed. Tarullo, Brainard, Kashkari, and Dudley are clearly skeptical of a March hike. Fischer, Mester, and George are more willing to maintain a gradual hiking path if data confirm it. The overall lean is modestly dovish — willing to pause longer and needing convincing evidence before resuming hikes.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Several participants noted that monetary policy was less well positioned to respond effectively to shocks that reduce inflation or real activity than to upside shocks, and that waiting for additional information...before taking the next step to reduce policy accommodation would be prudent."
- "While participants continued to expect that gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy would be appropriate, they emphasized that the timing and pace of adjustments will depend on future economic and financial market developments."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Participants are in a cautious hold posture — still on a gradual normalization path but emphasizing patience and data dependence, with asymmetric risk concerns tilting slightly dovish.
