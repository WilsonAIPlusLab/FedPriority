### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Scores**: DT 60 | DM 57 | ST 54 | SM 55

**Discussion Transcript (DT)**:
- "core PCE inflation continues to move up. The latest report shows it at a 2.9 percent annual rate over the last three months, considerably above the 1.7 percent rate over the twelve months to March." (Lacker)
- "core CPI has risen by 1.3 percentage points to 3.3 percent in the last three months. Core PCE is up a full point to 2.6 percent." (Bies)
- "core inflation is now higher than at any time since the second half of 2001… In my opinion it is above comfortable levels." (Hoenig)
- "Readings on overall and especially core inflation have been uncomfortably and unexpectedly high." (Yellen)

**Discussion Minutes (DM)**:
- "Core measures of price inflation had moved up over recent quarters and particularly so over the last few months."
- "Participants voiced concerns about recent price trends; they expected inflation to remain contained but also perceived that the risks to that inflation outlook now might be skewed somewhat to the upside."
- "Measures of core inflation were thought likely to remain in check over the remainder of this year and next."

**Staff Transcript (ST)**:
- "core consumer prices are up only 1¾ percent over the past 12 months—a pace that remains low even by the standards of the past decade. And we are projecting core PCE inflation to remain at or below 2 percent over the projection period." (Stockton)
- "we have revised up our forecast for headline PCE inflation in the first half of the year by about ¼ percentage point to 2¾ percent at an annual rate... the consequence of a series of cost shocks could look to many people an awful lot like an accelerating price level." (Stockton)

**Staff Minutes (SM)**:
- "Sharp increases in energy prices pushed up headline inflation, and core measures were also somewhat elevated."
- "Core consumer prices also rose a bit more rapidly recently, but the increase over the twelve months ending in March was little different than over the year-earlier period."

**Observation**: DT scores highest (60) because individual participants speak in the most alarmed terms about short-run core readings (2.6–2.9% annualized). DM (57) softens the picture by noting expectations of remaining contained. ST and SM (54–55) score the same conceptual range from the staff perspective, which anchors to 12-month core PCE near 1.75% while flagging upside risk from energy pass-through. The gap between DT and staff sources (6 points) reflects a genuine difference: the staff 12-month core was "low even by the standards of the past decade," while participants focused on the recent 3-month spike.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Scores**: DT 45 | DM 46 | ST 44 | SM 46

**Discussion Transcript (DT)**:
- "the Greenbook forecast has a more protracted narrowing of the output gap, with a significantly wider gap still near 1 percent to the end of 2006." (Lacker)
- "Capacity utilization in manufacturing is still below its long-run average… appreciable slack remains in labor markets." (Kohn)
- "Our forecast in Boston and that in the Greenbook suggest that growth will continue at a pace that will use up at least some excess capacity by the end of the forecast period." (Minehan)

**Discussion Minutes (DM)**:
- "Economic fundamentals... were expected to support economic growth at a pace sufficient to gradually eliminate remaining slack in resource utilization."
- "Most members regarded the recent slower growth of economic activity as likely to be transitory."

**Staff Transcript (ST)**:
- "the GDP gap at the end of 2006 is about 1 percent in this forecast rather than being nearly eliminated as it was in our previous forecast." (Stockton)
- "we also revised down our projection for second-quarter growth of real GDP to 3½ percent, about ½ percentage point below our previous projection. Some of that weakness is projected to carry forward." (Stockton)

**Staff Minutes (SM)**:
- "Economic growth was projected to run a bit above the staff's estimate of the economy's potential" (referring to the growth rate, not the gap level).
- "Remaining slack in resource markets" and capacity utilization "remained a bit below its thirty-year average."

**Observation**: All four sources converge tightly (44–46). The staff transcript is marginally lowest (44) due to explicit downward revision of the output gap forecast to ~1% below potential by end-2006. Staff minutes, discussion transcript, and discussion minutes cluster at 45–46. This is one of the most consistent dimensions across all four sources.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Scores**: DT 46 | DM 43 | ST 48 | SM 48

**Discussion Transcript (DT)**:
- "at least some, and possibly considerable, slack remains in labor markets… no difficulties whatever in hiring at any skill level, with only a few exceptions." (Yellen)
- "In almost no case… does anyone report any pressures in the labor market, either on wages or the availability of labor." (Poole)
- "The damped growth of compensation even in the face of higher headline inflation indicates to me that appreciable slack remains in labor markets." (Kohn)
- "labor markets appear to be gradually tightening, especially in areas where mining and energy activity are strong." (Stern)

**Discussion Minutes (DM)**:
- "Available indicators of wages and benefits had registered only modest growth, suggesting to many that some slack in labor markets persisted."
- "The behavior of compensation suggesting a lack of pressure in labor markets."

**Staff Transcript (ST)**:
- "initial claims for unemployment insurance have averaged about 325,000 over the past month—a figure at the low end of the range that has prevailed as the labor market has gradually but steadily improved. And insured unemployment has also continued to drift lower." (Stockton)
- "Private payrolls increased by just 100,000 in March." (Stockton)

**Staff Minutes (SM)**:
- "The unemployment rate moved down to 5.2 percent in March."
- "Suggesting a gradual erosion of slack in labor markets were surveys indicating that some employers were finding some jobs requiring special skills harder to fill."

**Observation**: Staff sources (48 each) score slightly higher than discussion sources (43–46), reflecting a difference in framing: staff emphasized improving indicators (claims at low end, unemployment drifting lower, erosion of slack), while participants — especially Yellen, Poole, and Kohn — placed more weight on the anecdotal absence of hiring pressure and subdued compensation. DM (43) is the lowest, pulling from the most dovish participant characterizations.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Scores**: DT 47 | DM 46 | ST 42 | SM 52

**Discussion Transcript (DT)**:
- "both consumption and investment falling short of expectations." (Kohn)
- "Contractions were also seen in retail sales, with and without autos, and in home building." (Minehan)

- "private domestic final sales were really quite good in the quarter, growing… at somewhere in the high 3 percent range." (Bies)

**Discussion Minutes (DM)**:
- "Capital expenditures advanced briskly over the first quarter, but at a pace significantly below that registered over the latter half of last year."
- "The pace of consumption growth had fallen off appreciably toward the end of the first quarter."
- "Capital expenditures were seen as likely to remain quite robust, spurred by strong economic fundamentals."

**Staff Transcript (ST)**:
- "A very weak retail sales report for March held down the growth of overall consumer spending in the first quarter. With orders and shipments for capital equipment having dropped sharply in March, growth of equipment and software spending is looking a bit weaker in the first half of this year than was incorporated in the previous forecast." (Stockton)
- "home sales remained strong, and the reports we have received from the automakers suggest that motor vehicle sales were solid in April." (Stockton)

**Staff Minutes (SM)**:
- "Consumer spending advanced solidly in the first quarter despite some slowing in automobile sales. However, much of that strength was registered early in the quarter, and spending in March was subdued."
- "Growth of business spending on equipment and software moderated substantially in the first quarter from the very high rates of last year, but appeared to retain considerable momentum."

**Observation**: This dimension shows the widest within-category spread (42–52). SM scores highest (52) because it characterizes Q1 consumer spending as having "advanced solidly" with E&S retaining "considerable momentum" — a more balanced reading. ST scores lowest (42) because it focuses on the weak March data point and the downward revision to near-term capital spending. DT (47) and DM (46) sit in the middle, reflecting the mixed participant views. The SM vs. ST gap (10 points) from the same underlying period is notable and likely reflects differences in how staff-attributable language is parsed from minutes vs. what Stockton actually said in the transcript.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
*(Discussion sources only)*

**Scores**: DT 38 | DM 22

**Discussion Transcript (DT)**:
- "I know I'm in the minority here… I'm starting to feel like Alice in Wonderland with this statement." (Minehan)
- Poole explicitly circulated an "alternative D" language and argued for explicit two-sided flexibility.
- "In my view, even with appropriate monetary policy action, the risk to the attainment of sustainable growth is unbalanced to the downside, while the risk to the attainment of price stability is unbalanced to the upside." (Yellen)
- No formal dissents on the vote (unanimous for 25bp and alternative B).

**Discussion Minutes (DM)**:
- "All members favored raising the target" — unanimous on action.
- "All members agreed to retain the forward-looking language" — unanimous on communication.
- "some members commented that this greater uncertainty called for eliminating or paring back forward-looking language."

**Observation**: The 16-point gap between DT (38) and DM (22) is the largest disagreement in this meeting. The transcript captures the substantive internal friction — Minehan's "Alice in Wonderland" remark, Poole's alternative D proposal, and the visible factional divide over "measured pace" language — which the minutes' synthesis of unanimous outcomes largely obscures. This is a structural property of minutes vs. transcripts: formal unanimity suppresses evidence of deliberative disagreement.
