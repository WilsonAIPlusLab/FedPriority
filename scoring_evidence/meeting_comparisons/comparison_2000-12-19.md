### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
*Higher = more inflation pressure. Scores: Disc-T 52, Disc-M 46, Staff-T 53, Staff-M 54.*

**Discussion — Transcript (52)**
- Hoenig: "Despite higher energy prices and rising import prices, core inflation remains modest."
- Gramlich: "The core PCE bounces around and we make a lot of the fact that it has risen recently, but it's still below its levels of 1996 and 1997."
- Greenspan: "the outlook is for a very significantly subdued rate of inflation... pricing power in the corporate sector has been falling quite appreciably in the last six to eight weeks."
- Kelley: "By most measures inflation is off its lows and is slowly rising."

**Discussion — Minutes (46)**
- "Against the backdrop of slowing economic growth, core inflation had remained quiescent."
- "All the members agreed that the risks of higher inflation had diminished materially."
- "Subdued inflation was a reasonable prospect."
- [Residual upside risk noted:] "Labor markets in general were still very tight and likely would remain taut relative to historical experience."

**Staff — Transcript (53)**
- Stockton: "we are essentially projecting no further acceleration in price inflation, with core PCE and core CPI inflation leveling off at a 2 and 2-1/2 percent pace, respectively, over the forecast period."
- Stockton: "those pressures are now about fully offset by the indirect effects of declining oil prices on business costs and nominal compensation."

**Staff — Minutes (54)**
- "Evidence on core price inflation was mixed; by one measure, it appeared to be increasing very gradually, in part reflecting the indirect effects of higher energy costs, but by another it had remained at a relatively subdued level."
- "Core price inflation was projected to rise only slightly over the forecast horizon."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
*Higher = more above potential. Scores: Disc-T 49, Disc-M 53, Staff-T 40, Staff-M 45.*

**Discussion — Transcript (49)**
- Greenspan: "rate of economic expansion has very clearly and unambiguously moved down dramatically from its pace of earlier this year... if it stabilized at its current level it would be sustainable."
- Moskow: "The national economy has clearly entered a period of below-trend growth."
- Meyer: "the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity before this loss of momentum."
- Broaddus: "6 percent growth rate was not sustainable... we are getting the slowing we needed."

**Discussion — Minutes (53)**
- "The deceleration in the economy had occurred from an unsustainably high growth rate in the first half of the year."
- "High level of resource utilization" still referenced as a factor.
- "Moderate economic expansion" still the baseline, but risks shifting to downside.

**Staff — Transcript (40)**
- Stockton: "we see a confluence of factors combining to produce a period of very subdued growth and rising unemployment over the next several quarters."
- Stockton: "we have knocked about 1 to 1-1/2 percentage points off of our projection of real GDP growth this quarter and next" — GDP forecast now around 2-1/2 percent.

**Staff — Minutes (45)**
- "the economic expansion had slowed considerably, to a rate somewhat below the staff's current estimate of the growth of the economy's potential output."
- "economic activity, which had expanded at an appreciably lower pace since midyear, might have slowed further in recent months."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
*Higher = tighter labor market. Scores: Disc-T 60, Disc-M 67, Staff-T 63, Staff-M 62.*

**Discussion — Transcript (60)**
- Moskow: "Labor markets are still tight, but an increasing number of layoffs and plant shutdowns have been reported."
- Broaddus: "labor markets are loosening a bit; they are a little less tight. Trucking companies in particular have reported fewer difficulties finding and retaining drivers."
- Jordan: "Labor markets are reported to have eased considerably with the major exception of the health-care sector."
- Santomero: "labor markets in the District as still tight... unemployment rate in our region has remained at 4 percent or less all year."

**Discussion — Minutes (67)**
- "Labor markets in general were still very tight and likely would remain taut relative to historical experience."
- "Many of the factory workers losing their jobs were readily finding employment elsewhere in what generally continued to be characterized as very tight labor markets across the country."
- "Some members noted... a modest reduction in labor market strains in some areas and industries."

**Staff — Transcript (63)**
- Stockton: "labor market pressures abate more quickly in this forecast" — acknowledging the current tight state from which easing is projected.
- Stockton: "projecting a more substantial slowdown in activity next year than in the last Greenbook, labor market pressures abate more quickly."
- [Era context: unemployment ~3.9–4.0% in December 2000.]

**Staff — Minutes (62)**
- "the civilian unemployment rate edged up to 4 percent in November, its average thus far this year."
- "Growth in private nonfarm payroll employment moderated a little further on balance in October and November."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
*Higher = stronger spending/investment. Scores: Disc-T 35, Disc-M 37, Staff-T 38, Staff-M 40.*

**Discussion — Transcript (35)**
- Jordan: "Retailers... report sharply lower year-over-year sales"; steel industry "described as dire"; trucking industry "in a depression not a recession."
- Moskow: "contacts have used words like 'sharp,' 'dramatic,' and 'tremendous' to describe the slowdown."
- Stern: "consumer spending has to be described as no better than mediocre"; "profit margins are being squeezed."
- Kelley: "seriously weakening news about autos, consumer confidence, corporate earnings, retail sales, high-tech expenditures."

**Discussion — Minutes (37)**
- "Slowing in the growth of consumer spending... consumer sentiment seemed to have deteriorated appreciably... retail sales were widely indicated to have softened."
- "Growth in business expenditures for equipment and software had moderated substantially in recent months from very high rates of increase."
- "Cutbacks in inventory investment or runoffs of existing inventories accounted for a significant part of the recent moderation."

**Staff — Transcript (38)**
- Stockton: "we have lowered our forecast for the growth of real PCE noticeably in the near term to about 3 percent. That's still not shabby, but it's a far cry from the 5 percent plus pace of the past few years."
- Stockton: "we have marked down further our projection for high-tech equipment spending... with the coming year being one of comparative weakness followed by some recovery in 2002."

**Staff — Minutes (40)**
- "Consumer spending and business purchases of equipment and software had decelerated markedly after having registered extraordinary gains in the first half of the year."
- "Consumer spending appeared to be decelerating noticeably further in the fourth quarter."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
*Higher = tighter financial conditions. Scores: Disc-T 65, Disc-M 62, Staff-T 65, Staff-M 63.*

**Discussion — Transcript (65)**
- Hoenig: "tighter financial conditions pose an additional and important downside risk. These include higher interest rate spreads, lower equity prices, tighter lending terms and standards, increased risk aversion toward more speculative ventures, and a rise in junk bond default rates."
- Ferguson: "Moody's... expectation is that between 385 and 400 companies will default... more than triple the number of defaults we've had since December of 1999."
- Guynn: "bankers are telling us that loan growth has slowed considerably across all business lines... borrowers are reportedly complaining about the shrinking syndicated loan markets."

**Discussion — Minutes (62)**
- "Tightening financial conditions in the form of declining equity prices and stricter credit terms for many business borrowers."
- "Sharp deterioration in the equity prices of high-tech companies."
- "Negative wealth effects of further declines in stock market prices" affecting consumers.

**Staff — Transcript (65)**
- Stockton: "the tightening of financial conditions this fall and the apparent slowdown in the growth of output and final sales seems very likely to take a toll on capital spending."
- Fisher: "the pattern in 2000 [in A2/P2 commercial paper spreads] is outside the range of recent experience, reflecting something more than normal risk aversion associated with the window-dressing of year-end balance sheets."

**Staff — Minutes (63)**
- "deteriorating conditions in some segments of financial markets."
- Staff forecast: "relatively high interest rates and the somewhat stringent credit terms and conditions on some types of loans by financial institutions."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*Higher = more hawkish current stance. Scores: Disc-T 42, Disc-M 40. (Discussion track only.)*

**Discussion — Transcript (42)**
- Greenspan: "no change in the funds rate, but with a bias toward the down side and the recognition that, if the erosion continues, we very likely will have to move before the next meeting."
- Gramlich: "I think monetary policy has become too tight... I see very few costs in cutting rates soon and very many benefits."
- Poole: "this is an appropriate time to ease... we need to reposition ourselves to get back in the middle of the playing field."
- Broaddus: "I think we still have time to proceed in a measured manner... there is a risk in easing policy more than expected at a time when labor markets... are still quite tight and inflation is drifting upward."

**Discussion — Minutes (40)**
- "All the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance, consistent with a federal funds rate averaging about 6-1/2 percent."
- "Most members believed the risks were now weighted toward conditions that could generate economic weakness."
- "Those few members who expressed a preference for easing at this meeting" accepted delay.
- "The Committee should be prepared to respond promptly to indications of further weakness."

## Cross-Source Observations

**High convergence (within ~5 points across all four sources):**
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35–40. All four sources agree on clear below-trend spending, with participants using particularly sharp language ("dire," "depression," "dramatic").
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62–65. All four sources consistently describe tightened conditions via spreads, equity declines, and stricter credit terms.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (discussion track): Both Disc-T and Disc-M score 32 — the easing signal is explicit and identical in both documents.

**Moderate divergence (6–13 points):**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Disc-M (46) is 6–8 points below the other three sources (52–54). The minutes emphasize that inflation risks "had diminished materially" and risks were "quiescent," whereas the transcript captures individual voices noting upward drift and ongoing concern.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Disc-M (67) is 5–7 points above the other three (60–63). The minutes use "very tight" language with emphasis on workers displaced from manufacturing readily finding jobs elsewhere; the transcript captures more district-level reports of easing at the margin.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Staff-T (40) is 9–13 points below the discussion sources (49, 53). Staff framing uses "below potential" language explicitly; discussion participants describe "below-trend growth" but with unemployment still at 4%, placing the level closer to potential.

**Larger divergence:**
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Disc-T (45) vs. Disc-M (25). The transcript preserves explicit statements of preference for alternatives (Gramlich, Poole, Broaddus, Jordan's exchanges) that are compressed in the minutes to "those few members... could accept a delay" and "in one view."
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Disc-T (58) vs. Disc-M (45). Greenspan's extended discussion of nonlinear dynamics and forecasting limitations at turning points is preserved in the transcript and absent from the minutes summary.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Disc-T (55) vs. Disc-M (40). Hoenig's and Ferguson's extended financial-conditions discussions are visible in the transcript but condensed in the minutes to transmission-channel framing.
