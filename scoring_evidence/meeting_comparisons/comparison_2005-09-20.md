## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
*Disc-T: 63 | Disc-M: 62 | Staff-T: 60 | Staff-M: 57*

**Disc-T (transcript quotes):**
- Moskow: "the Greenbook forecast for core PCE inflation next year has been raised to 2¼ percent…the outlook has deteriorated…we can't expect continued outsized gains in productivity to hold down unit labor costs."
- Lacker: "Core PCE inflation is near 2½ percent throughout 2006 and does not fall below 2 percent until 2007. Inflation expectations move upward as well…This would put inflation appreciably beyond a range I view as consistent with price stability."
- Santomero: "prices paid index showed its strongest increase since 1973 and is at its highest level since January."
- Minority (Yellen): core inflation "well contained"; recent 1.5% pace encouraging.

**Disc-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Participants' concerns about inflation prospects generally had increased over the intermeeting period."
- "Several participants voiced concern that the effects of the hurricane were likely to add to already considerable pressures on prices."
- "Core inflation in recent months had been quite damped, and market-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had risen only modestly of late."

**Staff-T (transcript quotes):**
- Stockton: "The economy has been subjected to a further energy price shock at a time when households and businesses already were having to cope with the effects of nearly two years of steadily climbing prices."
- Stockton: Core PCE forecast "goes up to 2¼ percent next year [2006] and then comes back" toward 2%; Michigan year-ahead at 4.6%, "highest level since 1990."
- Stockton identifies three upside risk channels: breakout in inflation expectations, workers restoring real wages, businesses repairing profit margins from energy costs.

**Staff-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Core consumer price inflation remained benign in July and August. However, the surge in energy prices considerably boosted overall consumer price inflation over those months."
- "The staff revised upward its forecast of overall inflation for 2005 and of core inflation for 2006, reflecting the effects of higher energy prices."

### OUTPUT_GAP
*Disc-T: 55 | Disc-M: 53 | Staff-T: 55 | Staff-M: 53*

**Disc-T (transcript quotes):**
- Santomero: "incoming data on the national economy pre-Katrina indicated that the expansion was continuing on a solid footing, with GDP growth slightly higher than potential."
- Santomero (citing Yellen): "We are at a whisker from potential output."
- Ferguson: "the economy—supported by a particularly strong housing market and accommodative monetary policy—appeared to be growing above potential."

**Disc-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Output and employment appeared to have been growing at a good pace before Hurricane Katrina's landfall."
- "The margin of unutilized resources in the overall economy was probably thin."
- Hurricane effects seen as "essentially temporary" with "probably little effect on the economy's intermediate-term growth prospects."

**Staff-T (transcript quotes):**
- Stockton: "A massive dose of fiscal stimulus is about to be layered on top of an economy that was already nearing its productive limits."
- Stockton: Pre-Katrina economy "growing at a brisk pace" and on track for "growth in the vicinity of 4 percent in the second half."
- Stockton (Q&A): "We wouldn't necessarily tell you to take the output gap estimates in the second half seriously in terms of the notion that a lot more slack has opened up in the economy."

**Staff-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Expansion of economic activity had been solid."
- "Overall capacity utilization through August remained close to its highest level since December 2000."
- Unemployment at 4.9%, "its lowest level since August 2001."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
*Disc-T: 62 | Disc-M: 57 | Staff-T: 53 | Staff-M: 53*

**Disc-T (transcript quotes):**
- Santomero: "Labor markets continued to improve, with little or no slack remaining."
- Minehan: "increasing problems in finding skilled labor, even people to fill teller positions."
- Guynn: "shortages in several markets in a number of skilled and unskilled jobs…raised wages by $2.50 an hour in several categories."
- Stern: "employment gains are being constrained by a lack of available labor."

**Disc-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Anecdotal information continued to point to shortages of certain types of labor, such as truck drivers."
- "Some business contacts reported difficulties in hiring more generally, a development that had prompted some firms to boost wages."
- "Measures of labor costs were giving conflicting signals."

**Staff-T (transcript quotes):**
- Stockton: "Gains in private payrolls had averaged about 175,000 in recent months, and with the readings on initial claims remaining in the low 300,000s, a continuation of those increases seemed likely."
- Stockton: "While the labor market has tightened up, we don't hear much from our business contacts to suggest that the competitive environment has changed in a way that would have altered their ability or inclination to grant substantially larger pay increases."

**Staff-M (minutes quotes):**
- "The unemployment rate dipped to 4.9 percent in August, its lowest level since August 2001."
- "Payroll employment grew at a good pace in August, and the average increase over the most recent three months was largely on par with the advances made since the fourth quarter of last year. Employment gains were widespread across industries."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
*Disc-T: 50 | Disc-M: 53 | Staff-T: 59 | Staff-M: 55*

**Disc-T (transcript quotes):**
- Pianalto: "already witnessing some retrenchment in consumer spending."
- Poole: Wal-Mart "cutting back purchases for the holiday season."
- Bies: "business spending on equipment and software has only modest forward momentum."
- Geithner: "reasonable growth in household income…favorable financial conditions."

**Disc-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Household spending had been especially strong" and "fairly robust before the hurricane."
- "Business fixed investment had been somewhat weaker than expected."
- "Consumption would probably expand at a moderate pace--perhaps a little below the pace of income growth."

**Staff-T (transcript quotes):**
- Stockton: "The household sector seemed especially buoyant. Sales of motor vehicles were receiving a considerable boost from the employee discount programs. And last week's retail sales report for August suggested consumer spending excluding motor vehicles had been well maintained. Meanwhile, housing activity remained strong, with both starts and sales holding near historic highs."
- Stockton: Business sector mixed — "Growth of factory output has slowed noticeably" and equipment spending outside tech "about flat since the turn of the year."

**Staff-M (minutes quotes):**
- "Real personal consumption expenditures, boosted by motor vehicles purchases in response to employee-discount programs, were robust during the summer."
- "Activity in the housing sector remained brisk."
- "Available data on orders and shipments suggested some softness in the third quarter" for equipment/software; "real spending on nonresidential construction remained lackluster."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*Disc-T: 74 | Disc-M: 72 | Staff: not scored*

**Disc-T (transcript quotes):**
- Geithner: "we need to continue to tighten monetary policy at this meeting and beyond."

- Greenspan: "I would opt for a 25 basis point increase in the funds rate."
- Pause minority — Poole: "I retain my preference for no change but I note that the market has priced in almost a 100 percent probability of a 25 basis point increase today." Olson: supports "alternative A" (pause/deferral), acknowledges "that's very much a minority view."

- "A pause in policy tightening at this meeting had the potential to mislead the public."

**Staff-M (minutes quotes):**
- "One survey of households in early September indicated that near-term inflation expectations jumped and that longer-term inflation expectations edged higher."
- Market measures: "inflation compensation slightly higher."

## Anomaly Notes

- Hurricane Katrina (August 29, 2005) dominates all four documents. All tracks distinguish sharply between temporary near-term disruption and fundamental medium-term path.
- Hurricane Rita was actively forming during the meeting; mentioned in the transcript discussion.
- Staff-T records a localized Treasury futures settlement squeeze (fails $90–$110 billion in 10-year contract) that does not appear in Staff-M; Kos characterizes the cash market as unaffected.
- Disc-M uses pre-2009 unsegmented minutes format; staff and participant content are interleaved.
- Poole expressed a preference for no change but did not file a formal dissent vote. Olson explicitly supported alternative A (pause) and acknowledged it as a minority view.
