### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 39 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly characterize inflation as persistently below the 2% target, with recent annual revisions confirming lower underlying trends. The prevailing view is "stubbornly below target" but not at risk of deflation spiral. The Dallas trimmed mean at ~2% and a few recent good monthly readings provide the floor for the score.

Representative quotes:
> "core PCE inflation is around 1½ percent. It's well below our goal. And the recent annual revision to the data shows that underlying trend inflation is somewhat lower than we thought." — Vice Chair Williams

> "breakeven inflation rates remain some 30 basis points below the levels that prevailed before the financial crisis... at least in the Michigan survey, they're little changed at a historically very low level." — Clarida

> "underlying trend inflation is mired somewhat below 2 percent." — Governor Brainard

Minority views: Barkin and Kaplan (citing the Dallas trimmed mean at 2% and recent above-target monthly PCE readings) see inflation as closer to target, pushing the uncertainty band slightly higher. Mester and Quarles are less alarmed than the center.

---

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Participants see inflation running below the 2% target, with core PCE at 1.6%. While some took comfort from the recent firming and trimmed mean near 2%, the overall assessment is that inflation pressures are muted and below objective.

Representative quotes:
> "Both overall and core PCE price inflation rates continued to run below the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective; the latest reading on the 12-month change in the core PCE price index was 1.6 percent."

> "Inflation pressures continued to be muted, notwithstanding the firming in the overall and core PCE price indexes in the three months ending in June relative to earlier in the year."

> "Many other participants...saw the recent inflation data as consistent with the view that the lower readings earlier this year were largely transitory, and noted that the trimmed mean measure of PCE price inflation...was running around 2 percent."

Minority views: Many participants saw recent data as consistent with a transitory dip resolving, which would place inflation closer to target; a few noted trimmed mean at 2%.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Growth is near potential; the economy is not in recession or clearly below potential, but the weak investment sector tempers any assessment of clearly above-potential performance. Slight majority see output near or modestly above trend.

Representative quotes:
> "according to the staff, real GDP growth will edge down to roughly potential." — Powell

> "real GDP rising a bit under 2½ percent in 2019... a moderately stronger forecast than I had in June." — Evans

> "I'm going to try to make a case for the claim that we are currently well on track to achieving our dual mandate... if it were a submission round, I would be penciling in modest markups to my forecasts." — Bostic

Minority views: Several participants note weak business investment and global headwinds, pulling toward at or slightly below trend for some components, but consumption strength keeps overall near-to-above-trend.

---

**Minutes — Score: 50 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Participants describe an economy growing at a moderate pace, expected to converge to potential in the second half of 2019. No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language from participants; the economy is essentially at potential.

Representative quotes:
> "Economic activity had risen at a moderate rate."

> "The economy continued to expand at a moderate pace, and participants generally expected GDP growth to slow a bit to around its estimated potential rate in the second half of the year."

> "Incoming data over the intermeeting period had been largely positive and...the economy had been resilient."

Minority views: Some concern about deceleration from trade/global weakness could push slightly below potential; the staff forecast suggests slightly above. Narrow band.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 68 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous that labor market conditions are historically tight — unemployment at 50-year lows, participation improving, employers reporting difficulty finding workers. This is clearly in the 65–75 range.

Representative quotes:
> "labor market continues to be the best in generations, with low unemployment, better-than-expected labor force participation, and wage growth that's not far from its pre-crisis trend." — Quarles

> "entry hiring is very tight, especially for production employees like tellers or retail clerks or food service... I'm hearing firms turn down or defer work rather than escalating wages and increasing hiring." — Barkin

> "Unemployment rates remain at historically low levels, and labor force participation rates have stabilized for prime-age individuals." — George

Minority views: None on the level of tightness — all participants agree the labor market is very tight. The debate is about what this tightness means for wages and inflation (flat Phillips curve), not whether conditions are tight.

---

**Minutes — Score: 57 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is clearly tight — low unemployment, firms have difficulty finding workers, wage pressures emerging for lower-wage workers — but it is not overheating. Wage growth is moderate and consistent with low productivity growth, not generating inflationary pressure.

Representative quotes:
> "Participants judged that conditions remained strong, with the unemployment rate near historical lows and continued solid job gains."

> "Reports from business contacts pointed to continued strong labor demand, with many firms reporting difficulty finding workers to meet current demand."

> "Several participants remarked that there seemed to be little sign of overheating in labor markets, citing the combination of muted inflation pressures and moderate wage growth."

Minority views: Several noted no overheating, placing tightness in a more benign light. Several noted "notable wage pressures for lower-wage workers," suggesting somewhat greater tightness in segments.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript — Score: 52 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is robust — a clear strength. Business investment is weak, residential investment contracted, exports dragged. The net is a consumer-supported, investment-drag story that puts the overall spending/investment composite modestly above trend, but not strongly so.

Representative quotes:
> "PCE surged at an annual rate of 4.3 percent. Business fixed investment, however, was down 0.6 percent." — Powell

> "Robust consumer spending is balanced by weakness in housing, business and fixed investment, and exports." — Williams

> "Consumer spending growth rebounded strongly in the second quarter following the first quarter lull, and real disposable income is reported to have increased 3.2 percent over the past four quarters." — Bowman

Minority views: Bostic is more optimistic; George and Barkin note deceleration. Overall, the "moderate" label is apt.

---

**Minutes — Score: 48 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is robust and the main engine of the expansion, but business fixed investment is soft, manufacturing is declining, and residential investment continues to fall. The net assessment is approximately at trend, with the strong consumer offset by weak investment.

Representative quotes:
> "Participants commented on the robust pace of consumer spending...household spending would likely continue to be supported by strong labor market conditions, rising incomes, and upbeat consumer sentiment."

> "Growth of business fixed investment had been soft." "Trade uncertainty...continuing to weigh on business confidence and firms' capital expenditure plans."

> "Manufacturing production had declined so far this year." "A few participants noted that the continued softness in residential investment was a concern."

Minority views: A few noted some improvement in business sentiment and new capital goods orders, while others highlighted ongoing agricultural and energy sector challenges.

**Minutes — Score: 33 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Borrowing rates are low, equities near highs, credit freely available, banks easing lending standards. The only note of tightness is the inverted yield curve, mentioned by a few participants.

Representative quotes:

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript — Score: 33 ±8 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: A majority voted for a 25bp cut — placing the center of gravity in the 26–40 "moderate dovish" range. The cut is characterized as a "mid-cycle insurance adjustment," not the beginning of a sustained easing cycle. Powell's explicit "midcycle adjustment to a somewhat more accommodative stance, not beginning an extended loosening cycle" defines the majority view.

Representative quotes:
> "I support alternative A at this meeting... 50 basis point cut... commit to hold the federal funds rate... until we see core inflation return to our target." — Kashkari (more dovish than consensus)

> "I continue to see our current policy settings as appropriate today, along the lines of alternative C." — George (more hawkish than consensus)

> "I support no change in the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting." — Mester (hawkish minority)

> "I see the Committee as adjusting policy midcycle to a somewhat more accommodative stance, not beginning an extended loosening cycle, and I intend to make that clear." — Powell (majority framing)

Minority views: Kashkari's 50bp preference pulls toward ~18–22; the 4 Alt-C dissenters (George, Mester, Rosengren, Bostic) pull toward ~45–50.

---

**Minutes — Score: 37 ±8 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: The majority favors a 25bp cut as an insurance/adjustment move, not the start of aggressive easing. The framing as "mid-cycle adjustment" rather than the beginning of an easing cycle moderates the dovish signal. Still clearly on the easing side.

Representative quotes:
> "All but two members agreed to lower the target range for the federal funds rate to 2 to 2-1/4 percent at this meeting."

> "A couple of participants indicated that they would have preferred a 50 basis point cut...to better address the stubbornly low inflation rates."

> "Several participants favored maintaining the same target range at this meeting, judging that the real economy continued to be in a good place."

> "Most participants viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing...as part of a recalibration of the stance of policy, or mid-cycle adjustment."

Minority views: A couple wanted 50bp (more dovish); several/two wanted no change (hawkish).

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript — Score: 65 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: This is among the higher-dissent meetings in this era. At least 4 participants formally support an alternative other than the majority's Alt-B, plus one partial dissent and several "reluctant" supporters. The disagreement spans both directions (left and right) and extends to the fundamental rationale for the action.

Representative quotes:
> Multiple formal dissents: Kashkari (Alt-A, 50bp), George (Alt-C, hold), Rosengren (Alt-C, hold), Mester (Alt-C, hold), Bostic (Alt-C, hold).

> "I cannot support alternative B as written" — Harker (partial dissent on balance sheet component)

> "today's move makes me uncomfortable... moving again in September feels quick." — Barkin

Minority views / anomaly: The dissent is unusually structured — one dovish dissent (Kashkari, wants more) and four hawkish dissents (want less or nothing). This bimodal distribution is rare and reflects genuine strategic disagreement about the right policy response to persistent below-target inflation.

---

**Minutes — Score: 42 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Center-of-gravity: There is a clear three-way split: most favor 25bp, several favor no change (two dissenting formally), and a couple favor 50bp. This represents moderate disagreement with two formal dissents and meaningful minority camps on both sides.

Representative quotes:
> "All but two members agreed to lower the target range." (Two formal dissents for no change.)

> "A couple of participants indicated that they would have preferred a 50 basis point cut."

> "Several participants favored maintaining the same target range at this meeting."

Minority views: The disagreement spans both the direction (no change) and magnitude (50bp), creating a wider-than-usual spread.

Representative quotes:

**Minutes — Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

Representative quotes:

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is elevated relative to a typical expansion but not extreme. The uncertainty around trade policy is widely acknowledged; the uncertainty around key structural variables (r*, u*, slope of Phillips curve) is emphasized strongly in the framework review session and bleeds into the economic discussion.

Representative quotes:

Representative quotes:
