**Note on transcript structure**: The 2018-08-01 meeting had an unusual two-part structure. Day 1 morning was a dedicated framework/ELB (effective lower bound) policy tools discussion about hypothetical future scenarios — not a standard economic go-round. Scoring is based on the economic go-round (Day 1 afternoon), financial stability go-round, and policy go-round (Day 2).

### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 52 ±5 [High] | 52 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript quotes**:
- Brainard: "core PCE inflation over the past year is very close to our target, in its May reading...Dallas Fed's trimmed mean rate was still somewhat below the target at 1.8 percent"
- Kaplan: "last reading of the Dallas trimmed mean—for June—was 1.8 percent...we now feel more strongly that by year-end, the trimmed mean number will hit 2 percent"
- Williams: "the 12-month percent change in the core PCE price index is near our objective of 2 percent. It should continue to remain there for the rest of the year"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly view inflation as having reached or very nearly reached the 2% target (headline PCE), with underlying measures like the Dallas trimmed mean still slightly below; the view is "at target" rather than clearly above it.

**Transcript minority views**: George sees "upside risk to inflation" citing ISM prices paid at 7-year highs. Harker notes "some of the highest readings on prices paid and prices received in series history" in manufacturing.

---

**Minutes quotes**:
- "Both overall inflation and core inflation...had remained near 2 percent on a 12-month basis."
- "Many participants anticipated that, over the medium term, high levels of resource utilization and stable inflation expectations would keep inflation near 2 percent."
- "Reports from several Districts suggested that firms had greater scope than in the recent past to raise prices in response to strong demand or increases in input costs, including those associated with tariff increases."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as essentially at the 2% target with modestly balanced upside risks from tariffs and pricing power, offset by some noting weak pass-through from resource pressures and trimmed-mean still slightly below 2%.

**Minutes minority views**: "A few participants observed that the trimmed mean measure of inflation...was still below 2 percent." "Some participants observed that inflation in recent years had shown only a weak connection to measures of resource pressures."

**Transcript quotes**:

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants clearly view the economy as operating above potential and continuing to do so, with strong GDP growth exceeding potential output growth. The "further above" language indicates a meaningful positive gap.

**Minutes minority views**: "Several participants stressed the possibility that real GDP growth in the second quarter may have been boosted by transitory factors."

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: The labor market is clearly tight with low unemployment, high job openings and quits, and reports of labor shortages from business contacts, though wages have only modestly picked up.

**Minutes minority views**: "A few participants suggested there could still be some labor market slack, citing recent increases in labor force participation rates." "Measures of aggregate nominal wage growth had so far picked up only modestly."

**Minutes minority views**: "Persistent weakness and the risk of a further slowdown in residential investment were also noted." "Agricultural sector had been adversely affected."

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 72 ±6 [High] | 68 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript quotes**:

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous support for the hold today (Alt B), with strong consensus for September and likely December hikes; the center is clearly hawkish tightening bias.

**Transcript minority views**: Bullard argues no need for preemptive action given flat Phillips curve and neutral rate possibly already reached; Kashkari cautious about moving to contractionary stance; Kaplan wants to pause and assess once neutral is reached.

---

**Minutes quotes**:

- "Participants generally expected that further gradual increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be consistent with a sustained expansion."
- "Participants agreed that it would be appropriate for the Committee to leave the target range...unchanged at this meeting."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Participants hold at this meeting but have a clear tightening bias with "many" signaling another hike "soon." The consensus is for continued gradual removal of accommodation. This is moderate hawkish — active hiking cycle with data-dependent forward lean.

**Minutes minority views**: "Some participants suggested that, in the event of a major escalation in trade disputes...presented a challenge in determining the appropriate monetary policy response." Trade tensions introduce some caution.

**Transcript quotes**:

**Transcript minority views**: Disagreement about "accommodative" language timing (September vs. December vs. January) was notable but resolved into consensus that it will happen "later this year."

---

**Minutes quotes**:

- "Participants agreed that it would be appropriate...to leave the target range...unchanged at this meeting."

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on both the economic assessment and the policy action. Differences are nuanced (pace/timing, specific indicator interpretations) rather than fundamental, with no formal dissents evident.

**Minutes minority views**: Differences on labor slack, yield curve significance, and inflation-resource utilization link, but these are minor emphasis differences rather than policy disagreements.
