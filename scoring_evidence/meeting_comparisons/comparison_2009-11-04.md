# FOMC Meeting 2009-11-04: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 20 ±6 [High] | 23 ±8 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** The overwhelming majority view near-term inflation as subdued and below target, with core PCE at ~1.3% and falling; deflation risk acknowledged but described as fading. A minority (Plosser, Bullard) warn of medium-term upside risks.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Participants see inflation as meaningfully below target, held down by substantial economic slack and decelerating wages. The dominant view is subdued inflation, though a minority flags commodity/import price upside risks.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Dudley: "The core PCE deflator has risen only 1.3 percent over the past year, down more than a percentage point from a year ago...unit labor costs are falling quite rapidly."
- Yellen: "I expect substantial slack in labor and goods markets to reduce core PCE price inflation from about 1½ percent this year to 1 percent in 2010 and 2011."
- Bullard: "I continue to believe that there is a near-term deflation risk and a medium-term risk of higher-than-desired inflation...the near-term deflation risk is fading."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "most participants anticipated that substantial slack in both labor and product markets would likely keep inflation subdued"
- "the considerable decelerations in wages and unit labor costs this year were cited as factors putting downward pressure on inflation"
- "some participants noted that the recent rise in the prices of oil and other commodities, as well as increases in import prices stemming from the decline in the foreign exchange value of the dollar, could boost inflation pressures"

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 23 ±5 [High] | 22 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** All participants agree the economy is far below potential. Q3 GDP was +3.5% but is the first positive print after four consecutive declines. The output gap is large; recovery is beginning but slow.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** The economy is far below potential. While recovery has begun (Q3 GDP positive), participants see substantial slack that will only close very gradually. Industrial utilization is low and businesses are cautious.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Bernanke: "Despite the weaker output path...unemployment expected to remain well above 8% through 2011...employment-to-population ratio is at a long-term low."
- Rosengren: "In light of the substantial excess capacity in the economy and given that credit is likely to remain constrained, I expect a gradual disinflation over the forecast period."
- Kohn: "Outcomes are still quite unsatisfactory through 2012...in 2012 we still have resource utilization a couple of percentage points below where we think it should be."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "substantial slack in both labor and product markets"
- "a modest pace of expansion would imply only slow improvement in the labor market next year, with unemployment remaining high"
- "low industrial utilization rates"

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 20 ±4 [High] | 18 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** Near-universal agreement that the labor market is extremely weak. Unemployment approaching 10%, payrolls still declining, hiring frozen.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** The labor market is deeply slack. Unemployment is near 10%, involuntary part-time work is unusually elevated, and businesses plan to delay hiring by boosting hours and productivity first.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Bernanke: "The labor market remains very weak...joblessness, employment-to-population ratios...are extraordinarily high...unemployment has a very large component of permanent separations and long-term unemployment."
- Yellen: "unemployment rising to 10 percent this year and remaining above 8 percent through 2011...weak labor market and stagnating wage income."
- Dudley: "The unemployment rate is very high; core inflation is low. This implies that our focus should be on getting the unemployment rate down...likely to be our focus for an extended period."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "weakness in labor market conditions remained an important concern to meeting participants, with unemployment expected to remain elevated for some time"
- "the unusually large fraction of those who were working part time for economic reasons and the unusually low level of the average workweek pointed to only a gradual decline in the unemployment rate"
- "business contacts reported that they would be cautious in their hiring and would continue to aggressively seek cost savings"

### Dimension 4: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 35 ±7 [High] | 32 ±7 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** Spending and investment are below trend. Government programs (cash-for-clunkers, homebuyer credit) provided temporary lift; underlying private demand is weak. Business investment is effectively frozen. Housing stabilizing from low base. Not in freefall but clearly "below trend; sluggish."

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Spending and investment are below trend. Consumer spending is picking up but remains constrained by weak labor markets, tight credit, and reduced wealth. Business investment has stabilized but is subdued. CRE is a notable drag.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Fisher: "Little—actually no—enthusiasm for expanding payrolls and cap-ex...domestic cap-ex is dead." AT&T cutting cap-ex 15%, head count by 12,000.
- Rosengren: "Growth in consumption and housing...both grew in the third quarter, they were supported by a variety of government programs, which are now in the process of winding down."
- Lacker: "Consumer spending seems to be on a moderate upward trend, as does the housing market. Business investment in equipment and software...seems to have stabilized."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "consumer spending appeared to be rising even apart from the effects of fiscal incentives to purchase autos" but "participants saw that increase as partly reflecting special factors"
- "cautious business sentiment, together with low industrial utilization rates, was likely to keep new capital spending subdued"
- "CRE activity continued to fall markedly in most Districts"

### Dimension 5: FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 60 ±8 [High] | 60 ±8 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** Conditions are bifurcated — large firms with capital market access find conditions "closer to normal" (spreads narrowed), but the banking channel is severely restricted for small/medium businesses. Net effect: somewhat tight overall, tighter than neutral especially through bank credit channel.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Financial conditions present a dichotomy. Capital markets have improved substantially, but bank lending remains tight and contracting. For the broad economy (especially small firms and consumers), conditions remain meaningfully tighter than normal.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Kohn: "Bank credit remains quite tight. On the quantity side, bank lending continued to decline very rapidly...banks continue to cut back on all kinds of credit lines."
- Evans: "Small and medium-sized companies that rely on bank credit continue to complain about onerous terms and an inadequate supply of borrowing options."
- Bernanke: "Nominal C&I bank loans outstanding are down about 12 percent, or $200 billion, over the past three quarters...terms and conditions...far greater than would be expected historically."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "financial market conditions... were substantially better than earlier in the year" with "equity prices considerably higher, private credit spreads substantially lower"
- "bank credit remained tight... banks continued to tighten lending standards for C&I loans and consumer loans... bank loans continued to contract sharply in all categories"
- "smaller firms, which tend to be more dependent on commercial banks for financing, reportedly faced substantial constraints in their access to credit"

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 20 ±5 [High] | 20 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** Near-consensus for maintaining maximum accommodation. The vote supports Alternative B (hold at zero, retain "extended period," complete QE program). Only Plosser advocates for earlier exit signals and alt C language elements. Strong dovish lean.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Strongly dovish. The Committee maintains maximum accommodation (zero rates + LSAPs) and sees no need for change given elevated unemployment and below-target inflation. The exit-strategy communication discussion is about credibility, not imminent tightening.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Evans: "Any tightening of the fed funds rate...before June 2010 seems unlikely to me...I agree with alternative B."
- Yellen: "I favor alternative B. With the funds rate effectively constrained at zero and our large-scale asset-purchase programs nearing completion, communication relating to the future path of policy is the main tool now available."
- Rosengren: "I am strongly in favor of retaining the 'extended period' language...I can easily conceive of conditions under which we would need to provide additional stimulus to the economy."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "members agreed that no substantive changes to the Committee's federal funds target range or large-scale asset purchase programs were warranted"
- "most members projected that over the next couple of years, the unemployment rate would remain quite elevated and the level of inflation would remain below rates consistent over the longer run with the Federal Reserve's objectives"
- "all participants agreed that it was important for... the Federal Reserve to continue to clearly communicate its ability and intent to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace"

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 38 ±7 [High] | 28 ±7 [Moderate] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** No formal dissents recorded in the vote; near-consensus on Alternative B. However, there is genuine and substantive intellectual disagreement on inflation dynamics (Phillips curve vs. expectations models), output gap measurement, and exit timing. Multiple "reluctant" supporters of the language changes. Score consistent with "moderate differences; notable differences about pace/risk; reservations but no formal dissents."

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Broad agreement on policy action with no formal dissents. However, there are notable differences in inflation risk assessment (downside vs. upside) and in the degree of optimism about the recovery's sustainability. These are assessment differences, not policy differences.

**Transcript quotes:**
- Bernanke summary: "Participants continue to disagree on the appropriate weights to place on resource slack, inflation expectations, and other factors in forecasting inflation."
- Plosser: "I have a general preference for the language in alternative C" — advocates strongly for earlier exit signals throughout policy discussion.
- Tarullo: "I guess my own preference would have been to hold this language until perhaps the December meeting" — expresses concern about the conditionality language.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "members agreed that no substantive changes... were warranted" (consensus on action)
- On inflation risks: "Some saw the risks as tilted to the downside in the near term... But others felt that risks were tilted to the upside over a longer horizon"
- "some participants expressed concerns about the ability of the economy to generate a self-sustaining recovery without government support"

## Part B: Staff Assessment

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript (Staff) | Minutes (Staff) |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 22 ±5 [High] | 24 ±5 [High] |

**Transcript — Center of gravity:** Staff projects PCE inflation well below the 2% target at roughly 1–1.5%, with persistent disinflation driven by large labor and product market slack; deflation risk not acute but inflation meaningfully below target.

**Minutes — Center of gravity:** Inflation currently subdued and staff forecasting further disinflation — solidly below target with slack-driven downward pressure.

**Transcript quotes (MR. STOCKTON):**
- "we project real PCE [inflation] at an annual rate of 1.5 percent in the third quarter... 1.1 percent pace in 2010 and a 1 percent [pace]... that we expect to prevail in product and [labor] markets... disinflation is relatively muted owing to [slack]"
- "the indirect effects of higher [energy prices] results in an increase from [1] percent this year... Beyond that, our [core projection is] about the same as September, as the indirect effects of higher [energy] prices... inflation [remains subdued given the] slack in this forecast."

**Minutes quotes:**
- "consumer price inflation remained subdued in recent months"
- "the staff continued to project that core inflation would slow somewhat further over the next two years" due to "significant underutilization of resources expected to persist for several years"
