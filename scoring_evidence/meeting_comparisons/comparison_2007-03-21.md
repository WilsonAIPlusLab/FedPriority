### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 65 ±7 [High confidence] | 64 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "The twelve-month change in the core CPI was 2.7 percent, and the three-month change has reaccelerated. I find the upward trend in core inflation over the past year, from 2 to 2¾ percent, troubling." (Plosser)
- "On a six-month basis through January, both our trimmed mean at the Dallas Federal Reserve Bank and your PCE excluding food and energy are stuck at 2.1 percent. On a twelve-month basis, the trimmed mean was stuck at 2.5 percent... sixty-eight percent of the components rose 3 percent or more." (Fisher)
- "Some were skeptical that any meaningful progress against inflation is being made... resource utilization pressures, particularly tight labor markets, pose a longer-term inflation threat." (Bernanke summary)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as clearly above the 2% target and not yet on a convincing downward path, with core measures running 2.3-2.7%, generating widespread concern and outweighing the minority view that moderation is underway.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The prevailing level of inflation remained uncomfortably high, and the latest information cast some doubt on whether core inflation was on the expected downward path."
- "All meeting participants expressed concern about the risks to this outlook. The latest readings on core inflation were higher than expected."
- "Participants agreed that risks around the expected and desired path of a gradual decline in core inflation remained mainly to the upside; some noted that upside risks to inflation appeared to have increased slightly."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Participants unanimously see inflation as uncomfortably elevated with upside risks increasing, though most still expect gradual moderation — placing this in the "clearly above target" range.

### Dimension 2: OUTPUT_GAP

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 45 ±5 [High confidence] | 53 ±6 [Moderate confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "Growth modestly below potential, along with the unwinding of some special factors like rent increases, should allow further declines in inflation." (Kohn)
- "I still think the current episode of below-trend growth is fundamentally a transitory phenomenon." (Lacker)
- "Most participants today agree that growth looks as though it's going to be slower... Many people have marked down growth expectations for the remainder of the year." (Bernanke summary)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The economy is running at or slightly below potential; the housing correction is the primary drag with non-housing components still growing. Not deeply below potential — Kohn even suggests output "might even be beyond potential" in level terms, but trend growth has clearly downshifted.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The economy was likely to expand at a moderate pace in coming quarters... close to or modestly below the economy's trend growth rate."
- "Rates of resource utilization that were near the high end of historical experience suggested a possibility that inflation pressures could build."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Growth near trend with resource utilization at the high end of historical experience places the economy modestly above potential in level terms, even as growth is near/below trend.

### Dimension 3: LABOR_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 63 ±6 [High confidence] | 59 ±5 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "Labor markets in the region remain tight... employers continue to complain of difficulty finding both skilled and unskilled labor... acceleration in wages appears to be more widespread." (Plosser)
- "solid job gains led by further tightening in our labor markets... shortages of skilled labor... shortages of some lower-skilled workers." (Hoenig)
- "at 4½ percent, the unemployment rate is low by historical standards, and this suggests to me that the risks from resource utilization remain toward higher inflation." (Kohn)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unemployment at 4.5%, below NAIRU, widespread reports of skill shortages, wage acceleration in professional and managerial occupations — clearly tight, though not the extreme of 1999-2000 or 2022-23.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Anecdotal and statistical evidence suggested that labor markets remained relatively tight."
- "Business contacts continued to report shortages of skilled workers in technical and professional fields, with significant wage pressures in some occupations, as well as a scarcity of less skilled and unskilled workers in some areas."
- "Aggregate measures of labor compensation were showing only moderate increases."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Labor markets clearly tight with reported shortages across skill levels, though aggregate wage growth still moderate — consistent with a tight but not extremely tight market.

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 57 ±6 [High confidence] | 59 ±6 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- "I continue to favor statement language that reflects an explicit bias toward firming... I remain firm until we actually see the inflation numbers consistently improve." (Hoenig)
- "I remain somewhat more concerned about inflation risk, and it is wise for our message on that to be consistent over time." (Yellen)
- "I think we all see the risk that inflation won't moderate as still being substantial and probably greater than the risk of a shortfall in output... I counsel patience on the rate." (Bernanke)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Unanimous vote to hold at 5.25%. Inflation is explicitly labeled the "predominant" concern. No participant advocates cutting. However, the removal of the "additional firming" language represents a meaningful dovish shift in the forward lean. The current meeting is best described as lean hawkish / inflation-focused hold.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "The Committee's predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate as expected."
- "The Committee agreed that further policy firming might prove necessary... but... the statement should no longer cite only the possibility of further firming."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Unanimous hold with inflation as the predominant concern and a retained firming bias, but notable softening of forward language to acknowledge two-sided risks — lean hawkish but less so than prior meetings.

### Dimension 9: FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 54 ±7 [High confidence] | 57 ±7 [High confidence] |

**Transcript evidence:**
- The meeting explicitly removes "additional firming" language and replaces it with "future policy adjustments will depend on..." — a deliberate move away from tightening signal.
- "the probability that we will tighten further has significantly diminished... we need to give ourselves the flexibility now to move to neutral sooner than might have seemed likely." (Geithner)
- "our challenge is to recognize that the real economy has shifted somewhat... maintaining our primary emphasis on inflation risks... not inducing a further decline in expected federal funds rates." (Bernanke)

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Forward guidance shifts from explicit tightening bias to data-dependent/balanced posture while retaining an asymmetric inflation emphasis. This is essentially neutral forward guidance with a slight hawkish lean from the inflation "predominant concern" language.

**Minutes evidence:**
- "The Committee agreed that further policy firming might prove necessary to foster lower inflation."
- "The statement should no longer cite only the possibility of further firming. Instead, the statement should indicate that future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth."

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** A notable shift from one-directional tightening signal to a more balanced, data-dependent stance — but still retaining a mild tightening bias given the inflation emphasis.
