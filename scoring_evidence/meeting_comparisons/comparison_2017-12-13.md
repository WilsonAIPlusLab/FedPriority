### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 38 ±6 | Moderate Confidence**
**Minutes: 39 ±6 | High Confidence**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Yellen: "the 12-month measure of core inflation remains disquietingly low—well below our 2 percent objective"
- Kashkari: "The core 12-month PCE inflation rate remains low at 1.45 percent on a 12-month basis. This is a large shortfall relative to our target of 2 percent."
- Rosengren: "the annualized core PCE inflation rate over the past three months has been 1.9 percent, a rate substantially above the average over the past year"
- Brainard: "over the 12 months through October, core PCE price inflation was only 1.4 percent...As we look over the past five years, core inflation has systematically fallen short of our 2 percent objective"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as below target (~1.4% core PCE year-over-year), with transitory factors given credit for much of the 2017 shortfall and tentative recent signs of firming, but no participant describes current inflation as at or above target.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "PCE price inflation over the 12 months ending in October, at 1.6 percent, continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent; core PCE price inflation... was only 1.4 percent"
- "Many indicated that they expected cyclical pressures associated with a tightening labor market to show through to higher inflation over the medium term"
- "some participants observed that there was a possibility that inflation might stay below the objective for longer than they currently expected"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Inflation is running well below 2%, with "many" expecting a gradual return but "some" worried it could stay low longer. The balance tips modestly below-target with forward hopes of normalization.

**Minutes minority views:** "Several" concerned about declining inflation expectations (pushes lower); "a few" noted transitory factors explain the softness (pushes higher in forward view).

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 55 ±5 | High Confidence**
**Minutes: 53 ±5 | High Confidence**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Williams: "real GDP growth will likely come in close to 2.5 percent this year...this pace is above the economy's potential growth rate"
- Yellen: "real GDP appears to have continued to expand somewhat faster than potential output in the second half of the year"
- Rosengren: "real GDP growth for the past three quarters of this year likely to exceed my estimate of potential growth of 1¾ percent by more than 1 percentage point"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Near-unanimous agreement that GDP is growing above potential, but the excess is moderate (~0.5–1 percentage point above trend), placing the economy modestly above potential rather than severely overheated.

**Transcript minority views:** Bullard notes slowing to ~2 percent is "most likely scenario" and emphasizes low long-run potential growth, suggesting the overshoot may be modest or temporary.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "incoming information on spending and the labor market as consistent with continued above-trend growth and a further strengthening in labor market conditions"
- "economic activity had been rising at a solid rate"
- Participants discussed the risk that "output expanded well beyond its maximum sustainable level"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The economy is growing above trend with a slight positive output gap. Participants see the economy as modestly above potential, consistent with late 2017 conditions.

**Minutes minority views:** Narrow — broad agreement on the characterization. Uncertainty is more about the trajectory (tax reform effects) than the current level.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 63 ±7 | High Confidence**
**Minutes: 57 ±5 | High Confidence**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Powell: "the labor market, by most indicators, suggests we are now at full employment. Indeed, the unemployment rate is now below all of our individual assessments of the natural rate."
- Yellen: "at 4.1 percent, now stands almost ½ percentage point below my assessment of its longer-run sustainable level"
- Mester: "unemployment rate will move below 4 percent next year, well below most estimates of its longer-run level"
- Kashkari: "we've likely underestimated labor market slack...continued strong job growth coupled with a lack of wage growth is hard to reconcile with the idea that we're at maximum employment."

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** The overwhelming majority of participants view the labor market as at or beyond full employment, with unemployment (4.1%) below individual NAIRU estimates, though wage growth has not fully responded (moderate surprise), consistent with tight but not unprecedented tightness.

**Transcript minority views:** Kashkari and Evans argue there is more residual slack than the headline suggests.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "unemployment rate declining further and payroll gains well above a pace consistent with maintaining a stable unemployment rate over time"
- "in several Districts, reports from business contacts or evidence from surveys pointed to some difficulty in finding qualified workers; in some cases, labor shortages were making it hard to fill customer demand"
- "pace of wage increases had generally been modest and in line with inflation and productivity growth"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** The labor market is notably tight — low and falling unemployment, worker shortages in several Districts — but wage pressures remain limited, preventing a higher score.

**Minutes minority views:** "A few" judged tightness would translate to wage acceleration; "another observed that the absence of broad-based upward wage pressures suggested scope for further improvement."

**Minutes minority views:** "Some business contacts... cautious about expanding capital spending" and may use tax savings for buybacks instead.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 28 ±6 | High Confidence**
**Minutes: 32 ±5 | High Confidence**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Brainard: "financial conditions remain very accommodative by historical standards—a prominent theme in my discussions with market participants. Equity prices are up nearly 3 percent since our meeting and 18 percent so far this year. Corporate bond spreads remain below longer-run averages, especially in the case of high-yield bonds, for which spreads are near historical lows."
- Dudley: "financial market conditions today are even easier than when we began to remove monetary policy accommodation in 2015"
- Rosengren: "the modest reductions in the balance sheet and the increases to date in the federal funds rate have not led to a tightening of overall financial conditions"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants converge on the assessment that financial conditions remain loose/accommodative despite three years of rate hikes, with equity prices elevated, credit spreads compressed, and the dollar weaker — this is loose, not tight, financial conditions.

**Transcript minority views:** None — this assessment is near-unanimous.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "some participants observed that financial conditions remained accommodative, citing a range of indicators including low interest rates, narrow credit spreads, high equity values, a lower dollar, and some evidence of easier terms for lending to risky borrowers"
- "a couple of participants expressed concern that the persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could, over time, pose risks to financial stability"
- "term premiums on longer-term nominal Treasury securities remained low"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose despite ongoing rate hikes. Multiple indicators cited pointing to easy conditions. Score well below neutral.

**Minutes minority views:** No participants described conditions as tight.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 72 ±7 | High Confidence**
**Minutes: 68 ±8 | High Confidence**

**Transcript quotes:**
- Williams: "I support alternative B...I envision three rate increases in the coming year and an additional three in 2019"
- Rosengren: "I support alternative B...five ¼ point change increases over the course of 2018"

**Transcript center-of-gravity:** Approximately 11 of 13 substantive policy voters support the 25bp hike (alternative B), with the median path implying 2–3 further hikes in 2018; 2 participants formally oppose (Evans, Kashkari), and a few others (Harker, Bullard) express reservations about the forward path but accept today's action.

**Transcript minority views:** Evans and Kashkari favor no action; Bullard accepts the hike but argues the projected 2018 path is too aggressive; Harker and Brainard urge caution/patience on future moves.

**Minutes quotes:**
- "almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting"
- "Most participants reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach to raising the target range"
- "Two members preferred to leave the target range at 1 to 1-1/4 percent"
- "A few other participants mentioned that they saw as appropriate a pace of additional policy tightening... somewhat faster than that implied by the December SEP median forecast"

**Minutes center-of-gravity:** Strong consensus for a 25bp hike with continued gradual tightening. The center is clearly hawkish (active tightening) but moderate in pace.

**Minutes minority views:** Two dovish dissents (no hike) and a few wanting faster tightening create a wider band of ±8 around the hawkish-but-gradual center.
