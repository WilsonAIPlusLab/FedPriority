### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 44 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Inflation is below target (headline approaching 2% on energy; core stuck at ~1.7%) with broadly stable expectations — slightly below target but trending in the right direction.

Quotes:
- MS. MESTER: "PCE inflation rose to 1.9 percent, reflecting higher energy prices, and core PCE inflation remains stable at about 1.7 percent."
- MR. KASHKARI: "Core PCE has barely moved. I find it interesting that so many of us are declaring victory that we are back to our inflation target largely because energy prices have gone up."
- CHAIR YELLEN: "Core inflation has been running close to 1¾ percent since the summer and remains somewhat below our longer-run 2 percent objective."

Minority views: Fischer says "we are very close to attaining our targets" for both mandates, which would push slightly higher. Kashkari's skepticism about declaring victory keeps the score from rising above 45.

---

**Minutes: 45 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Inflation is approaching but has not yet reached the 2% target; headline at 1.9%, core still somewhat below. The center of gravity sees inflation slightly below target with expectation of convergence.

Quotes:
- "Inflation had increased in recent quarters and moved close to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective; excluding energy and food prices, inflation was little changed and had continued to run somewhat below 2 percent."
- "Some participants cautioned that progress toward the Committee's inflation objective should not be overstated; they noted that inflation had been persistently below 2 percent during the current economic expansion."
- "Nearly all members judged that the Committee had not yet achieved its objective for headline inflation on a sustained basis."

Minority views: A few saw inflation data as stronger than expected and anticipated reaching 2% this year (upward pull). Some cautioned against overstating progress (downward pull). These roughly offset, keeping uncertainty moderate.

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Economy at or modestly above potential. Most participants describe growth as slightly above trend; Yellen's more cautious characterization notes no acceleration. Bullard (GDP barely at trend, steady state) is an outlier.

Quotes:
- MR. DUDLEY: "I'm now a bit more confident that the economic expansion will continue at a slightly above-trend pace over the next few years."
- MS. MESTER: "Real GDP growth continues at or above its trend pace."
- CHAIR YELLEN: "Real GDP appears to have increased 2 percent over the past four quarters, about the same moderate pace…spending and production indicators do not suggest that overall activity is accelerating."

Minority views: Bullard argues for essentially zero output gap — GDP at 2%, unemployment stable, regime-like; this is a material dissent that widens the uncertainty band.

---

**Minutes: 52 ±4 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Economy at or very slightly above potential, with moderate growth expected to continue. Unemployment modestly below its long-run rate suggests a small positive output gap.

Quotes:
- "Nearly all participants judged that the U.S. economy was operating at or near maximum employment."
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace."
- "Most participants still expected that... the unemployment rate would remain only modestly below their estimates of the longer-run normal rate of unemployment."

Minority views: "Some other participants, however, anticipated a more substantial undershoot" of unemployment, which would imply a slightly larger above-potential reading.

---

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 53 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Majority view is at or just past full employment; wages subdued enough to prevent a clearly "tight" reading.

Quotes:
- MS. MESTER: "We have met the full employment part of our dual mandate. Job gains have averaged more than 200,000 per month over the past three months."
- CHAIR YELLEN: "The labor market is more or less back to normal but not overheating…measures of wage growth remain subdued."
- MR. KASHKARI: "The job market may still have some slack left in it…I keep hearing us declare that we're at the natural rate of unemployment, and that this labor force participation story is just temporary…yet it keeps continuing."

Minority views: Kashkari and Powell both note possible additional labor slack; Powell explicitly lowered his estimate of the natural rate.

---

**Minutes: 53 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Near full employment with emerging signs of tightness (worker shortages, difficulty recruiting) but moderated by evidence of remaining slack (modest wage growth, involuntary part-time).

Quotes:
- "Business contacts in many Districts reported difficulty recruiting qualified workers and indicated that they had to either offer higher wages or hire workers with lower qualifications than desired."
- "Several other participants cited evidence that some slack remained in the labor market, such as still-modest aggregate wage growth and the unevenness of wage gains across industries, an elevated share of employees working part time for economic reasons."
- "Tight labor markets were said to increasingly be a factor in businesses' planning."

Minority views: Some see remaining slack, others see tightness building. This creates a moderate uncertainty band.

---

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 49 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Consumer spending soft in Q1 (seen as temporary); business investment improving from depressed base; residential solid. Essentially at-trend with soft patch.

Quotes:
- MR. POWELL: "The increase in the last two years can be fully accounted for by increased personal consumption, which has been supported by above-trend increases in employment, moderate wage gains, and rising wealth."
- CHAIR YELLEN: "Real PCE spending outside of motor vehicles fell in January, and orders and shipments for capital goods have picked up only modestly from the unusual weakness last year."
- MS. BRAINARD: "After changing little the past two years, there are currently signs of renewed growth in business investment."

Minority views: Evans contacts note optimism not yet translating to actual orders (Caterpillar, ArcelorMittal), reinforcing the "at-trend but not above" reading.

---

**Minutes: 51 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Consumer spending moderate and supported by fundamentals; business sector firming but investment constrained by policy uncertainty and excess capacity. Net position is roughly at trend.

Quotes:
- "Consumer spending was likely to contribute significantly to economic growth this year" though January slowdown "likely temporary."
- "Manufacturing activity in their Districts had strengthened further, and reports from the service sector were positive. Business optimism remained elevated."
- "Slower economic growth, ample existing capacity, and modest returns in the energy sector were noted as factors that were continuing to restrain overall capital spending."

Minority views: Some businesses waiting on policy clarity before expanding; a few reporting increased capex. Views moderately dispersed.

---

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative; multiple participants explicitly note easing conditions despite tightening cycle.

Quotes:
- MR. DUDLEY: "The rise in equity prices and the narrowing of credit spreads has outweighed the rise in long-term Treasury yields…one investment bank recently pegged the easing in their well-known financial conditions index over the past few months as equivalent to a reduction of 40 to 70 basis points in short-term rates."
- MR. POWELL: "Equity markets are booming, which is adding accommodation as the Committee is seeking a gradual withdrawal of it. Overall, financial conditions are actually looser than they were a year ago."
- MS. BRAINARD: "Since the beginning of the year, equity prices are up close to 5 percent. The dollar has declined slightly, and spreads on high-yield corporate bonds have moved down further."

Minority views: None — every participant discussing financial conditions describes them as loose or easing.

---

**Minutes: 36 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial conditions clearly accommodative and easing further due to equity/risk asset gains, despite modest rise in longer-term rates. Well below neutral tightness.

Quotes:
- "Financial conditions remained accommodative despite the rise in longer-term interest rates in recent months and continued to support the expansion of economic activity."
- "Several of them citing [the rise in equity prices] as contributing to an easing of financial conditions."
- "Prices of other risk assets, such as emerging market stocks, high-yield corporate bonds, and commercial real estate, had also risen significantly."

Minority views: Mortgage credit still restricted for some borrowers provides a minor tightening offset.

---

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 70 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Near-consensus 25bp hike (17 for, 1 against); majority explicitly supports continued gradual hiking path; some (Rosengren, Harker, Mester) argue for even more aggressive normalization.

Quotes:
- MS. MESTER: "Given my outlook…I view an upward path to the federal funds rate as appropriate and favor a 25 basis point increase…we need to focus on the risks of moving rates up too slowly."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Even after this increase, short-term real rates are quite negative and are not consistent with an economy likely to be approaching both elements of the dual mandate."
- MR. KASHKARI: Voted Alternative A (no hike) — the sole formal dissent.

Minority views: Kashkari formally dissents; Bullard accepts B with reservations and worries about overly hawkish trajectory; Evans projects only 2 hikes; Brainard emphasizes gradualism. These moderate the score from a higher reading.

---

**Minutes: 67 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Near-consensus for a 25bp hike with a gradual further tightening path. Moderate hawkish lean characteristic of the 2017 hiking cycle.

Quotes:
- "Almost all participants saw the incoming data as consistent with an increase of 25 basis points in the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Participants generally continued to judge that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate."
- "Several other participants judged that... such circumstances could warrant a faster pace of scaling back accommodation than implied by the medians."
- "In the view of one member, it was premature to raise the target range."

Minority views: One dovish dissenter (downward); several wanting faster pace (upward). These create the uncertainty band.

---

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus several meaningful reservations about pace and statement language; the debate about statement language ran across the full policy round.

Quotes:
- MR. KASHKARI: Formal vote for Alternative A; "I support alternative A today."
- MR. BULLARD: "All of these concerns have me leaning toward alternative A for today…I could be persuaded to support alternative B for today." (Accepted B with notable reservations)
- Extended statement language debate — substantial disagreement among Lacker (who recommended deleting the "carefully monitor" sentence), Evans/Brainard/Kashkari (who strongly opposed this), Mester (preferred changes to "gradual" language), George (concerned about core inflation framing).

Minority views: The formal dissent is counterbalanced by the large majority (16–1 likely); score reflects the breadth of expressed reservations rather than just the single vote.

---

**Minutes: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: One formal dissent with some range of views on pace and inflation prospects, but broad agreement on the action and general direction. Typical one-dissent meeting.

Quotes:
- "All but one member agreed to raise the target range." (One formal dissent)
- "Participants held different views regarding prospects for the attainment of the Committee's inflation goal."
- Several wanted faster pace vs. a few wanting to remain accommodative longer, but broad agreement on the 25bp hike and gradual path.

Minority views: The dovish dissenter and the hawks wanting faster pace create modest disagreement.

---

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Several participants discuss financial stability meaningfully — Rosengren most extensively, Brainard, Powell, Fischer, Kashkari all mention it. It is a meaningful side topic, not the dominant theme.

Quotes:
- MR. ROSENGREN: "A number of asset prices are also showing signs of froth…Emerging market stock prices have increased rapidly…high-yield corporate bond spreads have come down significantly…stock P/E ratios have risen well above their five-year average, and capitalization rates for commercial real estate are quite low by historical standards."
- MR. FISCHER: "The maintenance of financial stability appears to be especially difficult…calls to take more risks, to reduce capital requirements and other burdensome regulations, become more strident."
- MS. BRAINARD: "Valuations in some asset markets, such as equities, now appear somewhat stretched and could move sharply lower if growth and earnings do not pick up as much as appears to be priced in."

---

**Minutes: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center of gravity: Financial conditions and asset valuations received a dedicated paragraph with meaningful discussion by many participants, but it was not a dominant topic and did not drive policy conclusions.

Quotes:
- "Many participants discussed the implications of the rise in equity prices over the past few months."
- "Some participants viewed equity prices as quite high relative to standard valuation measures."
- "Prices of other risk assets... had also risen significantly."

Minority views: Limited divergence on this topic.

---

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 72 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Clear signal of continued gradual hiking; balance sheet normalization expected this year; "only" removed from guidance language signals the Committee sees itself on a tightening path, not just a one-off.

Quotes:
- CHAIR YELLEN: "I intend to emphasize…gradual increases in the federal funds rate would be warranted to achieve and sustain our objectives, and that our basic strategy remains unchanged." — Removed "only" from "only gradual" statement language.
- MR. WILLIAMS: "I continue to pencil in four funds rate increases for this year and even more for 2018."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "I would prefer to move to an approach that makes an interest rate increase at every press conference meeting the policy expectation unless the economy proves significantly weaker or stronger."

Minority views: Evans (two hikes base case), Brainard (gradualism emphasis, asymmetric risks), Kaplan (slightly flatter path) moderate the signal somewhat.

---

**Minutes: 65 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Forward signal is for continued gradual tightening, consistent with the hiking cycle. Not aggressive but clearly in a tightening direction.

Quotes:
- "Participants generally continued to judge that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate to promote the Committee's objectives."
- "A few noted that it could take some time for inflation to rise to 2 percent on a sustained basis, and thus monetary policy would likely need to remain accommodative for a while longer."
- "Several other participants judged that... such circumstances could warrant a faster pace of scaling back accommodation."

Minority views: Some lean dovish on forward guidance (patience on inflation); others lean hawkish (faster pace). Creates moderate band.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Fiscal policy uncertainty is a near-universal caveat; European political risks and China are mentioned; but the baseline outlook is described by most as "little changed" and risks as "roughly balanced." Genuine but bounded uncertainty.

Quotes:
- MS. MESTER: "There's still considerable uncertainty about U.S. fiscal and other government policies. As some of that uncertainty is resolved, we may need to change our outlook."
- MR. EVANS: "Much of this optimism is driven by hopes for the future…everyone is still focused on developments in Washington. This underscores the importance of greater clarity on fiscal, trade, and regulatory policies. Uncertainty remains high."
- MR. HARKER: "I have yet to factor in any prospective change in fiscal policy. To me, there is still too much uncertainty about the scope and timing to attempt that undertaking."

---

**Minutes: 48 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven specifically by fiscal policy unknowns, but the base economic outlook is stable and data are "broadly in line with expectations." Genuine uncertainty (not rhetorical hedging) about policy changes.

Quotes:
- "Participants continued to underscore the considerable uncertainty about the timing and nature of potential changes to fiscal policies as well as the size of the effects."
- "About half of the participants did not incorporate explicit assumptions about fiscal policy in their projections."
- "Members continued to judge that there was significant uncertainty about the effects of possible changes in fiscal and other government policies."

Minority views: Limited spread on this dimension; most acknowledge the fiscal uncertainty.

### S1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Staff Minutes: 43 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Headline near target but core persistently below at 1.75%, with staff expecting gradual convergence but not reaching 2% by 2019.

Quotes:
- "The 12-month change in consumer prices...was close to the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent; excluding food and energy prices, inflation was little changed and continued to run somewhat below 2 percent."
- "Core PCE price inflation...was 1 3/4 percent."
- Staff projects inflation "slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."

### S2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Staff Minutes: 53 ±4 [High confidence]**

Center of gravity: Economy modestly above potential with staff projecting it to remain so; the "modestly" qualifier keeps the score close to 50.

Quotes:

- "The economy was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential."

### S3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Staff Minutes: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**
