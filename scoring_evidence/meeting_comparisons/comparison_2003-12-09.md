## Dimension-by-Dimension Evidence

---

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
*Higher score = more upward pressure. All four sources: 25–30 (well below midpoint).*

**Discussion Transcript (30 ±7)**
- "we are at a very low inflation rate, 1 percent or thereabouts, and the output gap, however one measures it, is quite sizable." (Greenspan)
- "there is just no way to get around the fact that this kind of unit labor cost performance constitutes a moderate disinflation risk in the outlook, at least for now… we are now at the lower bound of what I'll call—for lack of a better term—our inflation tolerance range." (Broaddus)
- "the odds of inflation rising significantly any time soon from its current very low level seem small." (Bernanke)
- "core inflation is already near the bottom of my preferred range and is more likely to drop than increase." (Gramlich)

**Discussion Minutes (25 ±8)**
- "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack in labor and goods markets and further gains in productivity would keep inflation low, with a number of members seeing a small further decline in inflation as a distinct possibility."
- "Most members currently considered the upside risks to inflation to be a bit less pressing than those on the downside for the next few quarters."
- "The risk of a pernicious deflation...was now regarded by most as virtually nil."

**Staff Transcript (25 ±5)**
- Stockton: "core prices will increase in 2004 and 2005 at about the same pace as this year." [Core PCE ~1%]
- "Economic slack is expected to diminish only gradually."
- "Rapid increases in structural labor productivity hold down costs."
- Commodity prices rising but characterized as "minor consequence" for aggregate inflation.

**Staff Minutes (27 ±5)**
- "Core consumer price inflation remained quiescent."
- "Core consumer prices rose only slightly and noticeably less than in the previous twelve-month period."
- Staff forecast projects "some slight downward pressure on core consumer price inflation... given ongoing slack in resource utilization."

**Observation**: All four sources agree on below-target, subdued inflation. DT scores marginally higher (30) reflecting some participants describing risk as "balanced"; DM/ST/SM cluster at 25–27 using language like "quiescent," "virtually nil" deflation risk, and forecast of further disinflation.

### OUTPUT_GAP
*Higher score = more above potential. All four sources: 35–40 (below potential, gap closing).*

**Discussion Transcript (38 ±6)**
- "the output gap, however one measures it, is quite sizable." (Greenspan)
- "significant slack remains in both product and labor markets." (Santomero)
- "output gaps remain sizable… A zero real funds rate may not be way out of line for a 2 percent output gap." (Kohn)
- "the weight of the evidence continues to support those who believe that considerable slack remains in the economy." (Bernanke)

**Discussion Minutes (35 ±5)**
- "Wide margins of unused labor and other resources."
- "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack."
- "They generally expected growth to run at a rate that would trim slack in labor and goods markets over the forecast period."

**Staff Transcript (38 ±5)**
- Stockton: GDP growing well above trend; projected decline in unemployment rate to 5.25% by end of next year signals gap still present.
- Manufacturing capacity utilization still well below normal.
- Gap closing but still below potential.

**Staff Minutes (40 ±5)**
- "Capacity utilization edged up in October but remained well below its longer-term average."
- Staff forecast: "the economic expansion was likely to be sufficiently robust to reduce economic slack substantially in coming quarters."
- "Ongoing slack in resource utilization."

**Observation**: Tight clustering across all four sources (35–40). All confirm below-potential conditions with slack persisting. SM scores slightly higher (40) because minutes staff content emphasizes the trajectory of recovery alongside current slack; DM scores slightly lower (35) due to heavier emphasis on "wide margins."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
*Higher score = tighter labor market. Range: 30–36 across sources.*

**Discussion Transcript (36 ±6)**
- "the labor market appears to be improving modestly." (Greenspan statement language)
- "the deterioration of the labor market from 2000 until now is comparable to what occurred during the deep 1981-82 recession" on the employment-to-population ratio; "current household employment…remains some 2.9 million jobs below normal." (Bernanke)
- "significant slack remains in both product and labor markets." (Santomero)
- "Labor markets continue to improve… the increases were below their expectations given the strong GDP growth we've seen." (Moskow)

**Discussion Minutes (30 ±5)**
- "The current unemployment rate likely understated the slack in labor markets, since the labor market participation rate had fallen significantly of late."
- "Employment had revived" and members expected "further gains in employment."
- Manufacturing employment "displayed signs of stabilizing."

**Staff Transcript (30 ±5)**
- Stockton: "Private payrolls rose 50,000 in November and have been increasing about 90,000 per month, on average, over the past three months."
- "The workweek increased again last month."
- "Unemployment rate declined another tenth, to 5.9 percent."

**Staff Minutes (34 ±5)**
- "The unemployment rate fell to 5.9 percent in November, down from a recent peak of 6.4 percent at midyear."
- "The labor market had finally shown signs of some improvement in recent months."
- Manufacturing employment "continued to fall."

**Observation**: DT scores highest (36); DM and ST score lowest (30). The divergence reflects what each source emphasizes: DT captures individual speakers' direct assessments that show some regional improvement alongside the national weakness; DM places more weight on the participation-rate-adjusted slack argument; ST and SM focus on the aggregate data (90k payrolls/month, 5.9% unemployment). All sources agree the labor market is loose.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
*Higher score = stronger spending and investment. All sources: 55–60 (above trend).*

**Discussion Transcript (55 ±7)**
- "the incoming data since the last meeting clearly indicate that household consumption and residential investment have remained robust. Importantly…business fixed investment is also starting to become firmer." (Ferguson)
- UPS contact: "'We're getting the doors blown off with volume'… the huge automated facility in Louisville has been operating at capacity on a number of days." (Poole)
- "the continuing surge in capital spending and the pickup in hiring suggest a rebound in business confidence… housing demand has remained strong." (Kohn)
- "growth is more balanced in terms of coming from more sources. It is no longer relying totally on consumer spending; business capital investment has resumed." (Bies)

**Discussion Minutes (58 ±7)**
- "Spending by households had remained strong even as the effects of tax cuts and mortgage refinancing began to wane."
- "Investment outlays had increased rapidly" and "the increase in outlays for equipment and software in the third quarter was even larger than had been thought."
- "Inventory investment could be expected to contribute to growth in aggregate demand in coming quarters."

**Staff Transcript (55 ±6)**
- Stockton: E&S spending "seems likely to post an increase at an annual rate" — strong pace.
- "Consumer outlays soared this summer" with tax cuts; housing "stands out" as strong.
- Manufacturing production rising at 5%+ annualized pace.

**Staff Minutes (60 ±5)**
- "Spending for equipment and software and residential construction continued to surge."
- Equipment and software spending "grew in the third quarter at the fastest pace since 1998."
- "Single-family housing starts reached a record high."

**Observation**: All four sources clearly above 50. SM scores highest (60) using "surge" and "record high" language for equipment and housing. DM closely follows (58). DT and ST score 55, with DT noting some moderating signals (consumer price-sensitivity, Wal-Mart softer). The spread (55–60) is narrow and directionally unanimous.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS / FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
*Higher score = tighter conditions. All sources: 20–32 (loose/accommodative).*

**Discussion Transcript (24 ±6)**
- "financial conditions are becoming more accommodative even as the faster economic expansion takes hold" — driven by "persistent erosion of risk premiums… rising equity prices… falling dollar and stable Treasury bond rates." (Kohn)
- "accommodative monetary policy, stimulative financial conditions." (Hoenig)
- "combination of historically low interest rates, the home refinancing boom, and this summer's tax cut." (Ferguson)
- "the current federal funds rate is well below any estimate of the equilibrium rate." (Greenspan)

**Discussion Minutes (20 ±5)**
- "Accommodative conditions in financial markets" (mentioned twice).
- "Highly stimulative macroeconomic policies."
- Business investment gains owed partly to "accommodative financial markets."

**Staff Transcript (32 ±6)**
- Stockton: "Interest rates are low, and risk spreads have come in — creating favorable conditions for businesses and households. The stock market has been up substantially."
- Dollar falling further; fed funds at 1%.

**Staff Minutes (28 ±5)**
- Staff forecast: "Accommodative financial conditions… would continue to provide significant impetus to business and consumer spending."
- Speculative-grade yields declined with narrowing risk spreads; equities up moderately.

**Observation**: All sources agree conditions are loose/accommodative. DM scores lowest (20) — the minutes language uses "accommodative" unambiguously with no qualifications. ST scores highest among the four (32) — Stockton's characterization is affirmative ("favorable conditions") but slightly less emphatic than the discussion participants' characterizations.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
*Discussion sources only. Higher score = more hawkish. Both: 27–30 (clearly dovish).*

**Discussion Transcript (27 ±6)**
- "my recommendation…not move the funds rate… policy accommodation can be maintained for a considerable period." (Greenspan)
- "as a Committee, we should continue to remain patient and not choke off growth unnecessarily." (Bernanke)
- "the shift in the balance of risks around achieving our objectives for output and prices does not call for a change in policy any time soon in my view." (Kohn)
- Near-unanimous (12+ speakers) support for retaining "considerable period" language.

**Discussion Minutes (30 ±8)**
- "All the members favored an unchanged policy that continued to incorporate a target of 1 percent for the federal funds rate."
- "The currently accommodative policy stance remained appropriate in a period characterized by very low inflation, wide margins of unused labor and other resources."
- "Members saw substantial benefit in maintaining an unchanged policy stance and considerable risk in taking preemptive action."
- "At some point, a move in the direction of a less accommodative and more neutral policy posture might well be necessary."

**Observation**: Both sources score in the 27–30 range, firmly dovish. DT scores slightly lower (27) because individual transcript voices are captured directly endorsing accommodation; DM scores 30 because the minutes language also includes an acknowledgment that "at some point" tightening will be needed — a slightly less purely dovish signal.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
*Discussion sources only. Higher score = more internal disagreement.*

**Discussion Transcript (28 ±7)**
- Vote appears unanimous on the rate decision (no formal dissents noted).
- Poole: "when I walked in here my view was to try to change the statement along the following lines…I'd also delete the 'considerable period' sentence altogether." Ultimately supports recommendation.
- Hoenig: "my strong preference" to drop "considerable period." Supports rate decision.
- Broaddus: "my own preference would have been to leave all of the statement unchanged." Also supports.

**Discussion Minutes (30 ±7)**
- "All the members favored an unchanged policy" — unanimous rate decision.
- "Views differed with regard to the reference in recent statements to maintaining an accommodative monetary policy 'for a considerable period.'"
- "A number of members argued that its deletion would serve to enhance the Committee's flexibility. A majority, however, preferred to retain the phrase."

**Observation**: Both sources score 28–30. Zero formal dissents on the rate action; disagreement is confined to "considerable period" language, where DT captures bidirectional dissatisfaction (some want to drop it, Broaddus wants no change), while DM summarizes a cleaner majority-vs-minority split.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
*Discussion sources only. Higher score = more prominent stability discussion.*

**Discussion Transcript (35 ±8)**
- Geithner (extended): "the scale of dollar reserve accumulation… potential cliffs for the dollar… the extent of the decline in credit spreads and the magnitude of opportunities for the development of leveraged positions combined with the large scale of the mortgage-backed market mean that we have to be attentive to the risks that will come from a sustained period of low rates."
- Guynn: "approximately 40 percent of the increase in GDP since the trough is due to housing and durable goods purchases… measures of consumer debt show that consumers clearly have increased their debt burdens… delinquency rates on subprime mortgages far exceed those of other mortgages… credit card lenders, their chargeoff ratios are now approaching 7 percent."
- Olson: discusses banking industry profitability and asset quality improvement.

**Discussion Minutes (18 ±5)**
- "Accommodative conditions in financial markets" mentioned as context for the expansion.
- "Some members pointed to signs of increased financial stress" among households.
- "Some members expressed concern about the possible adverse effects of such deficits on financial markets."

**Observation**: Largest divergence in the comparison — DT scores 35 vs. DM 18. The transcript captures extended, specific financial stability commentary from Geithner (dollar vulnerabilities, leveraged positions, MBS market risks) and Guynn (consumer leverage, subprime delinquencies, credit card chargeoffs). The minutes compress this to brief, generic phrases ("some members pointed to signs"). This is a case where the minutes' aggregation substantially reduces the apparent prominence of stability discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
*Discussion sources only. Higher score = more hawkish forward signal. Both: 18–20 (firmly dovish).*

**Discussion Transcript (20 ±5)**
- Greenspan's proposed statement: "the Committee believes that policy accommodation can be maintained for a considerable period."
- Near-unanimous support for retaining "considerable period" language.
- Greenspan maps out possible sequence: retain "considerable period" now → shift to "patience" language in January → possibly begin raising in March–May if necessary.
- "I think it would be a mistake to drop that language entirely. Making it conditional is the right way to proceed." (Bernanke)

**Discussion Minutes (18 ±7)**
- "A majority...preferred to retain the phrase ['considerable period'], at least for now."
- "All the members saw merit in associating it more clearly with economic conditions, specifically the persistence of quite low inflation and slack in resource use."
- "Most members endorsed a proposal to delete as no longer necessary the previous summary statement relating to the risks to growth and inflation taken together."

**Observation**: Near-identical scoring (18–20). Both sources show the forward signal is strongly accommodative with "considerable period" retained. DT scores marginally higher (20) because the transcript includes Greenspan's explicit statement of the normalization sequence ahead, which adds a faint tightening signal not present in the minutes language.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
*Discussion sources only. Higher score = more uncertainty.*

**Discussion Transcript (40 ±8)**
- "It's important to note that we face a fair degree of uncertainty over many of the key elements of this recovery." (Geithner)
- "I have to admit to a great deal of uncertainty about any particular forecast." (Minehan)
- "The major unknown is productivity." (Kohn)
- "the potential variance of that measurement has increased considerably." (Greenspan, on output gap measurement)

**Discussion Minutes (38 ±7)**
- "Still considerable uncertainty about the speed with which those margins would be worked down."
- "Large current account deficits and their potential correction added to uncertainty about the longer-term prospects."
- "Assessments by individual members of the likely pace of the expansion going forward differed somewhat."

**Observation**: Essentially identical (38–40). Both sources reflect somewhat elevated uncertainty centered on the pace of slack absorption, productivity trajectory, and external sector. The transcript adds specific measurement uncertainties (output gap, Phillips curve extrapolation) not captured in the minutes.

### PRODUCTIVITY
*All sources score above 65 (clearly above trend).*

**Discussion Transcript (75 ±7)**
- "robust underlying growth in productivity." (Greenspan statement)
- "unit labor costs—which have been falling rapidly as productivity has surged." (Bernanke)
- "I think the pace of productivity growth may well continue to run above historically high levels." (Bies)
- "The major unknown is productivity, where I suspect there is a greater possibility of an upward than a downward surprise." (Kohn)

**Discussion Minutes (65 ±8)**
- "Further gains in productivity would keep inflation low."
- "Further surprising strength in productivity, which would reduce labor costs."
- "Rapid productivity growth also could limit employment gains."

**Staff Transcript (70 ±6)**
- Stockton: "Rapid increases in structural labor productivity hold down costs."
- "The ability of firms to continue to extract greater efficiencies." Structural productivity estimated at 2.5%+.

**Staff Minutes (65 ±8)**
- Staff forecast: "substantial gains in structural productivity" as a key driver of expansion.

**Observation**: DT scores highest (75) — the transcript captures multiple named speakers explicitly endorsing above-trend productivity as a durable phenomenon; DM and SM score 65, reflecting the minutes/staff content's more summary treatment. All four agree productivity is a prominent theme running well above historical trend.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
*Higher score = expectations more elevated/unanchored.*

**Discussion Transcript (38 ±8)**
- "rise in some measures of inflation expectations suggest that the risk of further declines in key price series has diminished." (Geithner)
- "inflation compensation has ticked up recently, though the reasons…may be subject to some questions." (Ferguson)
- "very little evidence that the markets are pricing in a buildup in long-term inflation expectations." (Greenspan)

**Discussion Minutes (42 ±8)**
- "Members also expressed concern about the potential for an increase in inflation expectations given highly stimulative macroeconomic policies."
- "Some noted that the rise in recent months in inflation compensation, as measured by the difference between the yields on nominal and indexed Treasury securities, could potentially point in this direction."

**Staff Transcript (45 ±5)**
- Stockton: "inflation expectations are anticipated to hold steady or drift lower."
- TIPS breakevens "moved up since the summer" but "range in which it has fluctuated over the past two years."
- Michigan survey expectations "haven't broken out to the upside."

**Staff Minutes (N/A)**
- Inflation compensation discussion is in the members' section of the minutes; staff-attributable content does not address expectations measures directly.

**Observation**: DT scores lowest (38) — discussion participants are relatively sanguine about expectations remaining anchored. DM scores 42, placing slightly more emphasis on the concern about expectations rising given "highly stimulative macroeconomic policies." ST scores 45 but on a near-neutral basis — Stockton presents expectations as stable without significant concern. SM has no staff-attributable content on this dimension.

### DOLLAR_CONCERN / DOLLAR_STRENGTH
*Higher score = stronger dollar / less concern about weakness. All sources below 50 = dollar weakening.*

**Discussion Transcript (20 ±8)**
- "the dollar obviously has been falling recently." (Parry)
- Broaddus asks whether dollar weakness reflects foreign concerns that U.S. monetary policy is too accommodative.
- "potential cliffs for the dollar." (Geithner)
- "concern about the dollar from foreign investors reflects the seeming lack of concern for fiscal responsibility." (Olson)

**Discussion Minutes (35 ±10)**
- "The weaker dollar" mentioned as contributing to upward price pressure and supporting exports.
- "Large current account deficits and their potential correction added to uncertainty."

**Staff Transcript (20 ±7)**
- Stockton: "the exchange value of the dollar has fallen further and is expected to edge down further."
- Kos: current account deficit discussion, dollar depreciation dynamics.

**Staff Minutes (35 ±5)**
- "The exchange value of the dollar, as measured by the major currencies index, declined somewhat, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- Dollar depreciated due to trade friction and current account deficit concerns.

**Observation**: DT and ST both score 20, reflecting stronger and more explicit concern about dollar weakness trajectory. DM and SM both score 35, where the minutes language characterizes dollar decline as more modest ("declined somewhat") and more matter-of-fact. The divergence partly reflects the intermeeting move (SM captures the specific intermeeting decline, which was moderate) versus the cumulative trend framing in the transcript (DT captures the full-year context).

## Cross-Source Summary

**High convergence (spread ≤ 8 points)**: INFLATION_PRESSURE (25–30), OUTPUT_GAP (35–40), CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (55–60), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (20–32), FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18–20), UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (38–40), DISSENT_INTENSITY (28–30), HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (27–30).

**Moderate divergence (spread 9–17 points)**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30–36 — regional detail in DT vs. aggregate data emphasis in SM/ST), PRODUCTIVITY (65–75 — DT captures named speaker endorsements; minutes compress to summary), INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (38–45 — DT more sanguine; ST near neutral; DM raises slight concern).

**Notable divergence**: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (18 DM vs. 35 DT). The minutes compress extended, specific financial stability commentary (Geithner on dollar/leverage/MBS risks; Guynn on subprime and consumer debt) into two brief phrases. This is the largest minutes-vs-transcript gap in the comparison.

**Dollar (DT/ST 20 vs. DM/SM 35)**: Transcript sources score the dollar dimension more bearishly than minutes sources, reflecting the difference between the cumulative-decline framing in live discussion and the intermeeting-period framing in the written record.
