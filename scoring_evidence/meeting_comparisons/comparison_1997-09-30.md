# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1997-09-30

## Meeting Context
September 1997. Fed funds rate at 5.5% (unchanged since March 1997 hike). Late-1990s expansion. Unemployment at 4.8–4.9%, near the expansion low. Inflation remarkably subdued despite extremely tight labor markets — the "new paradigm" productivity debate active. Asian financial crisis deepening (Southeast Asian currency depreciations underway) but limited US impact expected. FOMC votes unanimously to hold rates steady with an asymmetric tightening directive. Greenspan puts the probability of a November move at "50/50."

## Cross-Source Summary

### High-agreement dimensions (within 10 points across all four sources)
- **OUTPUT_GAP**: Transcript 60, Minutes 62, Staff-T 60, Staff-M 60. Near-perfect agreement. All sources describe an economy running clearly above potential with strong momentum and resource pressures building.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Transcript 67, Minutes 68, Staff-T 65, Staff-M 62. Tight clustering. All sources use explicit tight/taut language; unemployment near expansion lows; shortages widespread across districts and industries.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS/TIGHTNESS**: Transcript 22, Minutes 25, Staff-T 30, Staff-M 30. All sources agree conditions are accommodative/loose. Staff language ("financial exuberance," "readily available") and participant language ("abundant liquidity," "everybody is chasing deals") align.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Transcript 58, Minutes 58. Identical scores. Hold today with strong tightening bias — the most explicitly stated forward lean in the record for this era.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Transcript 45, Minutes 45. Identical scores. "New paradigm" debate generates genuine and explicit uncertainty about whether traditional inflation-unemployment models apply.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: All sources 42–45 range. Expectations viewed as anchored and stable, slightly below the midpoint given the benign current inflation readings.

### Notable divergences

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Transcript 40 vs. Minutes 45 vs. Staff-T 42 vs. Staff-M 38. Modest 7-point spread. Discussion transcript and staff transcript cluster (40–42); discussion minutes is slightly higher (45, reflecting forward-looking concern about mounting wage risks articulated by members); staff minutes is lowest (38, anchored to explicit data: PPI "down slightly," CPI "considerably less"). The divergence reflects the difference between current-reading emphasis and forward-risk emphasis.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (Staff)**: Staff-T 60 vs. Staff-M 68. The 8-point gap is the largest within-staff divergence. The minutes record uses stronger language — "robust," "steep uptrend," "exceptional" — than the transcript summary captures. The minutes staff sections report hard data (retail sales, business fixed investment) with superlatives; the transcript's Prell framing emphasizes the wealth-effect mechanism without the same data-density language.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Transcript 30 vs. Minutes 18. The transcript captures Broaddus on the "20 percent stock market decline" risk and Jordan's bankers on "skimpy pricing" and "chasing deals" — content that did not carry over into the synthesized minutes. The minutes treat financial conditions only as context for spending assessment.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Transcript 25 vs. Minutes 18. The transcript preserves individual speaker positions (Minehan would have moved today; Jordan thinks "we are probably late"; Rivlin "less convinced about November") that show a wider distribution of views than the unanimous vote and synthesized minutes language convey.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (Staff)**: Transcript 15 vs. Minutes 45. Largest divergence in Part B. The transcript captures Prell's explicit narrative that stock prices reflect "new era fever" and that staff "predicts" a decline. The minutes record only that prices "increased appreciably" to new highs, with no valuation concern expressed. This is a structural feature of the transcript-vs-minutes comparison: editorial staff assessments appear in transcripts but are filtered out of the published minutes.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (Staff)**: Transcript 65 vs. Minutes 40. The transcript reflects the cumulative dollar strength incorporated in the inflation forecast; the minutes report only the most recent intermeeting change (a modest net trade-weighted decline). The two measurements answer different questions: level strength vs. recent-period change.
