### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 40 ±8 [Low confidence]**

> "inflation is projected to remain subdued over the medium term, longer-term inflation expectations continue to be well anchored" — Plosser/Bullard citing Alternative C with approval; this is the stated condition for maintaining ZLB.

> Yellen references "our modal inflation outlook" near 2%, and argues that the 2% goal "is not a ceiling."

> Kocherlakota: "You've always got to predict that inflation will come back to target because you're doing it under optimal policy" — acknowledging inflation is not already at target in current trajectories.

**Center of gravity (transcript)**: Participants treat inflation as near or slightly below the 2% target, justifying continued accommodation. No participants express concern about overheating or inflation running too hot. Plosser notes concern that prolonged accommodation may eventually generate inflation pressures; Fisher observes retail price increases and energy cost pressures — but these are prospective risks, not current alarm.

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

> "Inflation had been subdued in recent months, although prices of crude oil and gasoline had increased of late."

> "Most meeting participants saw little evidence of cost pressures."

> "Most participants anticipated that after the temporary effect of the rise in oil and gasoline prices had run its course, inflation would be at or below the 2 percent rate."

> "A few participants were concerned that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might be below the mandate-consistent rate for some time."

**Center of gravity (minutes)**: Participants perceive inflation as subdued and below target, with a temporary oil-price bump that does not alter the medium-term view. The majority expects inflation at or below 2%. Minority: "other participants...were worried that inflation pressures could increase as the expansion continued" and cited risks from oil prices and accommodative policy.

> Pianalto: unemployment threshold discussions at 7–7.5% as liftoff triggers, while actual unemployment is ~8.3% — confirms gap of multiple percentage points still remaining.
