# FOMC Meeting 1993-03-23: Transcript vs Minutes Evidence Comparison

---

## Part A: Participant Discussion

### INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript** (Score: 53 +/-8, High confidence)

> "The recent price numbers strike me more as an aberration than as signalling a new round of inflationary pressures...subdued rather than increasing inflationary pressures." — MR. BOEHNE

> "in three out of the past five months core CPI has been 1/2 percent and over the past six months core CPI has been 4 percent...it's hard to convince oneself that we're safely gliding on the Greenbook path toward continued gradual disinflation." — MR. MULLINS

> "Everything we can see is exceptionally well behaved in the crucial area of labor costs...wage rates are clearly under control." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: The majority views inflation fundamentals as well-behaved (labor costs restrained, slack remaining) but acknowledges that measured CPI has been running above comfort levels for several months, creating a modest upward pressure signal.

**Minutes** (Score: 37 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "the members generally gave more weight to the behavior of unit labor costs, which indicated that much of the economy's underlying cost structure did not reflect any signs of a pickup in inflationary pressures"

> "most of the members concluded that the evidence at this point did not confirm a resurgence in inflationary pressures, and some commented that further modest disinflation remained a reasonable expectation"

> "the faster increases in consumer prices in recent months and a sharp upturn in the prices of certain producer materials tended to raise concerns, or at least a degree of unease"

Center of gravity: The majority of members see inflation as contained and supported by favorable unit labor costs and slack, though recent price upticks have introduced some unease. The center of gravity is below-target inflation with upside risk flags.

### OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-5, High confidence)

> "the outlook for inflation in our view is reasonably favorable with real GDP remaining below potential." — MR. PARRY

> "There is ample slack in the economy both on the labor side and in terms of capacity utilization." — MR. MULLINS

> "we have at this stage a still severely impaired financial system." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: The economy is in a moderate recovery (~3% growth) but remains below potential. Slack persists in labor markets and capacity utilization. Multiple structural headwinds (defense cuts, restructuring, fiscal uncertainty) restrain the pace.

**Minutes** (Score: 38 +/-6, High confidence)

> "sustained economic expansion, though at a pace appreciably below that now indicated for the fourth quarter of 1992"

> "considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources"

> "a number of factors were likely to limit its strength, including ongoing balance sheet and business restructuring activities, the outlook for a more restrictive federal budget, and continuing weakness in key foreign markets"

Center of gravity: The economy is recovering but remains meaningfully below potential, with considerable slack in both labor and capital utilization and multiple headwinds limiting growth momentum.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 35 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Labor supplies are still plentiful. Factory activity is rising but firms are reporting little change in current or future employment levels." — MR. FORRESTAL

> "there are a couple of areas of spot labor shortages...it doesn't seem to be very pervasive at the moment." — MR. STERN

> "there is a great reluctance to add workers, especially low skilled." — MR. JORDAN

Center of gravity: Labor markets have significant remaining slack with ~7% unemployment. Firms are actively resisting hiring, preferring to restructure and automate. Some early, isolated signs of tightening (spot shortages, longer workweeks) but these are not pervasive.

**Minutes** (Score: 32 +/-5, High confidence)

> "considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources"

> "absence of any firming in wage pressures" and "increases in wages have remained subdued"

> "continuing reliance of numerous firms on overtime work to meet growing demand rather than incurring the considerable costs of adding new workers"

Center of gravity: The labor market has significant slack with 7.0% unemployment. Firms are reluctant to hire, relying on overtime instead. Wage pressures are absent. Some worker recalls and new hires are emerging, but the market is clearly below full employment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript** (Score: 48 +/-5, High confidence)

> "We are seeing consumer purchasing continue; it's not surging, but it's at least continuing at a reputable pace." — MS. PHILLIPS

> Auto industry "second-quarter domestic production schedules...set at about 12 percent over the second quarter of last year." Heavy trucks up 17%, medium trucks up 13%. — MR. KEEHN

> "I hear very little talk these days that the expansion will grind to a halt...But there still is a lot of caution about just how fast the expansion will proceed." — MR. BOEHNE

Center of gravity: Spending and investment are at a moderate pace, roughly at trend. Some sectors show strength (autos, housing, capital goods) but offset by restructuring, defense cuts, and consumer caution. The overall picture is moderate, sustained growth rather than a strong surge.

**Minutes** (Score: 45 +/-7, High confidence)

> "the consumer sector was viewed as likely to play a key role in sustaining the expansion this year" but with expected moderation from Q4 pace

> "Business spending on producers' durable equipment also was believed likely to continue to provide appreciable stimulus"

> "business spending for nonresidential structures probably would continue to be held back by weakness in office construction stemming from widespread overcapacity"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is moderating from a strong Q4 but remains a pillar of the expansion. Business equipment investment is solid. However, nonresidential construction is weak due to overcapacity, and housing starts were "somewhat disappointing." Net assessment is slightly below trend but moderately positive.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript** (Score: 38 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "We are not getting an aggregate level of finance that is anywhere near being consistent with very strong nominal GDP growth...we are getting a significant profit margin opening up which is financing a good deal of the activity. [Businesses] are financing essentially from cash flow, not from credit." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "We have the lowest interest rates in several decades and the stock averages are within whispering distance of their all-time highs." — MR. MULLINS

> Mutual fund managers see "very, very strong inflows" but also "nervousness...because of the inevitable reach for yields." — MR. SYRON

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall — interest rates are at multi-decade lows, the bond market has rallied strongly, and the stock market is near record highs. However, credit growth is exceptionally weak and M2 is declining, suggesting the transmission to the real economy is impaired.

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-8, Moderate confidence)

> "greatly reduced interest rates" cited as a positive factor; "a number of members commented that other indicators suggested that current monetary policy was in fact quite accommodative"

> "continuation of balance sheet restructuring activities by financial institutions and the associated caution on the part of these institutions with regard to extending loans still appeared to be exerting a significantly inhibiting effect on the overall growth in spending"

> "extensions of credit and growth in overall nonfinancial sector debt were not consistent with an economy that was generating significant inflationary pressures"

Center of gravity: Interest rates are very low, making conditions generally loose/accommodative. However, bank balance sheet restructuring and caution in lending partially offset this, creating a somewhat unusual environment of low rates but impaired credit transmission. Net conditions are somewhat loose but not fully accommodative due to credit channel constraints.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript** (Score: 52 +/-7, High confidence)

> "at a 3 percent funds rate and effectively zero or slightly negative real rates, there is no way that we can stay at this level for any extended period...In my judgment this is not the time to be immediately concerned about this problem." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "I choose alternative C...now is the time to say that monetary policy is working and we need to [begin to adjust] it before the time comes to do something drastic." — MR. ANGELL

> "it is premature to make any move at this time. I think it would be a serious mistake for the Committee to take action." — MR. FORRESTAL

Center of gravity: The overwhelming majority supports holding the fed funds rate at 3% with symmetric language. However, the discussion is dominated by inflation concerns and the recognition that tightening will come — the question is when, not if. Two members (Angell, Lindsey) explicitly prefer tightening now. The lean is mildly hawkish hold.

**Minutes** (Score: 47 +/-8, High confidence)

> "most of the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure on reserve positions"

> "the current degree of reserve pressure continued to represent a policy stance that was appropriately balanced in light of the opposing risks of a faltering economic expansion and a resurgence of inflation"

> "two members supported an immediate move to tighten reserve conditions" citing "concerns about the outlook for inflation"

Center of gravity: The majority (10-2) favors holding the accommodative stance, viewing it as balanced between growth and inflation risks. The lean is very slightly dovish — they are consciously maintaining low rates to support a fragile expansion. The two hawkish dissenters pull the center slightly toward neutral.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript** (Score: 40 +/-5, High confidence)

> "I choose alternative C...I am only the fool who sees it correctly!" — MR. ANGELL

> "I have managed to convince myself that at this point long-term economic growth without inflation can best be had with a small rise, maybe 1/8 point." — MR. LINDSEY

> "at this stage I would support the Chairman's judgment to ride this out a bit longer" (despite extensive hawkish analysis). — MR. MULLINS

Center of gravity: There is genuine disagreement, with 2 explicit dissenters for tightening and several more expressing hawkish reservations. The debate is substantive and extended, but ultimately the vast majority converges on holding steady. The disagreement is about assessment of inflation risks more than fundamental framework differences.

**Minutes** (Score: 40 +/-5, High confidence)

> "Votes against this action: Messrs. Angell and Lindsey" — two formal dissents

> Dissenters offered detailed reasoning: "monetary policy currently was overly accommodative as suggested by various indicators such as recent data on consumer and producer prices, the upswing in commodity prices, the low level of real short-term interest rates"

> Mr. Angell provided an extended, detailed rationale including historical precedent arguments

Center of gravity: Two formal dissents with substantive, well-articulated disagreement about inflation risks and policy direction. The majority was fairly cohesive but "several members acknowledged" some of the dissenters' concerns about low real rates. This is moderate disagreement — more than typical one-dissent meetings.

Center of gravity: Financial stability is mentioned in passing — bond market vulnerability, stock market being "high," and reach for yield — but it is not a sustained or prominent topic. The meeting's focus is overwhelmingly on inflation dynamics and real economic conditions.

**Minutes** (Score: 10 +/-5, Moderate confidence)

> Brief references to "financial markets" and bond yields but no substantive discussion of financial stability risks

> Bank restructuring discussed as a credit channel issue, not a stability concern

> One mention: "a shift in the direction of policy...would be likely to have an especially pronounced impact on financial markets" — but this is about policy signaling, not stability

Center of gravity: Financial stability is essentially not a topic of discussion at this meeting. References to financial conditions are about monetary policy transmission, not stability concerns. Consistent with the low-attention 1993 era anchor.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript** (Score: 57 +/-7, High confidence)

> "we are going to have to move...in a somewhat aggressive manner. It's just that I cannot get myself to believe that this is the particular point." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

> "the next several months are going to be a really critical period for monetary policy...if we get another month or two of crummy inflation data, I think we need to think about a change." — MR. BROADDUS

> "even with symmetry, we certainly have the capability of taking action in the period between now and the next meeting." — MR. PARRY

Center of gravity: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward eventual tightening. There is near-universal agreement that the 3% funds rate is unsustainably low and the next move will be up. The timing is data-dependent, especially on upcoming CPI reports. The directive is symmetric but the discussion strongly signals a tightening bias.

**Minutes** (Score: 52 +/-6, Moderate confidence)

> "a preference for retaining the symmetry of the existing directive" — no presumption about direction

> "Some observed that a policy change during the intermeeting period, if any, might well be in the direction of a tightening move"

> "a tilt in the directive toward restraint would give a misleading indication of the Committee's current intentions"

Center of gravity: The forward signal is essentially neutral (symmetric directive) with the faintest hawkish lean — some members acknowledged the next move, if any, would likely be tightening. But this was explicitly not strong enough to warrant an asymmetric directive. Score is just barely above neutral.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript** (Score: 50 +/-7, High confidence)

> "forecasting in this environment is like trying to solve an equation with 23 unknowns." — MR. LAWARE

> "I still have this nagging sense of unease that things are either going to be better or worse than that rather benign forecast, and I can't decide which way it's going to be." — VICE CHAIRMAN CORRIGAN

> "this business cycle is different from anything any of us has seen. It has different dynamics; it has different characteristics." — CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is elevated. Participants cite multiple major unknowns: the Clinton fiscal/health care program effects, trade policy (NAFTA, GATT), international weakness (Japan, Europe, Russia), the meaning of weak M2, and whether recent CPI firming is aberrant or fundamental. The business cycle itself is described as unusual.

**Minutes** (Score: 42 +/-7, High confidence)

> "some members cautioned that...the current expansion differed in important respects from earlier cyclical recoveries and in light of the attendant uncertainties a considerably different result--in either direction--could not be ruled out"

> "considerable uncertainty that continued to surround the outlook for broad money relative to spending"

> "many business contacts were uncertain about the outlook for demand in their own industries or the potential strength of the overall expansion"

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Members note the unusual nature of this recovery, two-sided risks, difficulty predicting prices, and considerable uncertainty around money-spending relationships. This goes beyond routine hedging but is not at the "particularly uncertain" level. The word "considerable" appears multiple times.
