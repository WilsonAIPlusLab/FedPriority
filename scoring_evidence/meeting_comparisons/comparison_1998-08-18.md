### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **38 ±8 [High]** | **42 ±8 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Parry: "The news on inflation certainly continues to be outstanding. The GDP price index came in under 1 percent for the second straight quarter."
- Meyer: "on balance they are showing few signs of building inflation pressure"
- Boehne: "U.S. inflation remains subdued, and in my estimation the threat of a breakout on the upside has lessened"
- Moskow: "competition constrains their ability to raise prices" but notes "labor cost increases of 4 to 6 percent"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "Consumer prices and inflation more generally had remained remarkably subdued in the context of very tight labor markets and upward pressure on labor compensation."
- "Rising price inflation remained an important threat. Significant additional tightening in labor markets would, of course, exacerbate that risk."
- "Members remained persuaded that a significant rise in price inflation was not likely to occur in the nearer term."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Current inflation clearly below target; benign price readings driven by favorable supply shocks (Asia, oil, strong dollar); rising wage pressures create forward risk.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Inflation currently subdued thanks to special factors (dollar, commodities, productivity); members widely acknowledge forward risks from tight labor markets; net perception slightly below midpoint with upside risk.

**Minority views (transcript)**: Minehan projects CPI inflation rising to 3% by Q4 1999; Guynn sees upside inflation risk; Jordan cites median CPI at 2.8-3.0%.

**Minority views (minutes)**: "Many" continued to emphasize inflation risks could not be ignored; one member (Jordan) saw inflation acceleration risk from monetary growth.

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **57 ±7 [High]** | **55 ±7 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence:**

- Meyer: "The economy grew at a 3-1/2 percent rate in the first half despite a subtraction of 2-1/2 percentage points stemming from the decline in net exports"

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Economy has been running above potential with very strong domestic demand; external drag pulling growth toward or below trend; accumulated above-potential position still evident in tight labor markets.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential but expected to slow toward potential due to Asian drag; bright-line applies — "producing beyond" language yields score above 50.

**Minority views (transcript)**: Gramlich and Rivlin emphasize slowing below trend; McTeer sees downside risks as greater.

**Minority views (minutes)**: Some anticipate growth slowing to or below potential, but current position remains above.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **40 ±10 [Moderate]** | **38 ±8 [Moderate]** |

**Transcript evidence:**

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Conditions still accommodative overall but tightening at the margin due to equity declines and widening risk spreads; moving from loose toward neutral.

**Minority views**: McDonough (transcript) emphasizes reduced liquidity and market uncertainty; Jordan (minutes) cited "ample credit provision encourages speculative lending" (implying too loose).

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **48 ±7 [High]** | **51 ±7 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence:**
- Greenspan: "alternative B, meaning 'no change' in the rate structure, is the appropriate position"
- McDonough: "I very strongly prefer that we move to a symmetric directive... I think our next rate move is equally likely to be up or down"
- Gramlich: "the time is coming, perhaps very soon, when the right thing to do may be to lower the funds rate a bit"
- Minehan: "I continue to believe that our next move should be to raise the federal funds rate" but acknowledges "a lot of worrisome downside risks"
- Meyer: "I still believe... the next move for the funds rate will be up rather than down" but supports symmetric posture

**Minutes evidence:**
- "All but one of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining a steady policy stance."
- "A majority of the members agreed that the risks to the economic outlook were now more balanced and called for a shift from asymmetry to symmetry."
- "Other members continued to believe that the risks were still tilted to some degree toward rising inflation...still preferred an asymmetrical directive but could accept symmetry."
- One dissent (Jordan) for tightening.

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for holding rates; shift from asymmetric (tightening bias) to symmetric directive represents mild dovish shift; most see risks as more balanced with downside from international sector offsetting domestic upside inflation risks.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Hold steady with shift from tightening bias to neutral; majority has moved to balanced risk assessment; a minority retains mild hawkish lean; net essentially balanced with tiny hawkish residue.

**Minority views (transcript)**: McTeer wants easing now (dovish outlier); Jordan argues tightening needed (hawkish outlier).

**Minority views (minutes)**: Jordan dissented for tightening; "other members" preferred asymmetric (hawkish) directive.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **38 ±6 [High]** | **30 ±5 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence:**
- No formal dissents — unanimous vote for alternative B with symmetric directive
- But wide range: McTeer calls for easing; Jordan implies tightening needed; Minehan/Meyer/Guynn/Kelley/Moskow preferred asymmetric tilt
- Kelley: "I would prefer to stay with asymmetry because the basic structure of the risks... is still intact"
- Jordan: "the more costly mistake to correct will be the one that occurs if it turns out that excess demand growth does not in fact slow down"

**Minutes evidence:**
- One formal dissent (Jordan) for tightening.
- "A majority" favored symmetry; "other members" preferred asymmetry but "could accept symmetry."
- Final vote: 10-1.

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates but notable differences on directive tilt and risk assessment; about 5-6 participants preferred to retain asymmetric tightening tilt; wide spread of views but no formal dissents.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: One dissent with some internal disagreement about risk balance; policy decision broadly agreed upon; typical one-dissent meeting with additional preference differences.

**Note on divergence**: The transcript scores higher (38 vs 30) because the transcript reveals the full extent of internal disagreement (McTeer calling for easing, multiple members preferring asymmetry) without registering a formal dissent, while the minutes records one formal dissent but less internal texture.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | **62 ±7 [High]** | **55 ±7 [High]** |

**Transcript evidence:**
- McDonough: "I do not recall a time when there has been more uncertainty in financial markets"
- Stern: "the risks look to me to be very large... risks that I see on both sides are significant"
- Greenspan: extensive discussion of data problems — "our data have deserted us" — employment surveys diverging, inflation measures diverging, productivity data uncertain
- Ferguson: "a delicate balance... fraught with uncertainties, none of which is of our own making"

**Minutes evidence:**
- "External sector as a major source of uncertainty."
- "Duration and extent of disruptions in Asian and other economies could not be anticipated with any degree of confidence."

**Transcript center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated; multiple participants emphasize large risks on both sides, unprecedented financial market uncertainty, and fundamental difficulty reading the data; Greenspan's extended discussion of statistical discrepancies adds analytical uncertainty unusual for FOMC deliberations.

**Minutes center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, primarily driven by the evolving Asian crisis and its unknown domestic spillovers; multiple members emphasize this.

**Note on divergence**: Transcript scores higher (62 vs 55) because it captures Greenspan's unusual "our data have deserted us" framing and McDonough's "I do not recall a time when there has been more uncertainty" — vivid expressions that the minutes flatten into more measured language.
