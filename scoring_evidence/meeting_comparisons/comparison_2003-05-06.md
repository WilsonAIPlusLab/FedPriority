### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Scores: Disc-T 18 | Disc-M 20 | Staff-T 22 | Staff-M 22**

**Discussion Transcript (18):**
- "Inflation as measured by the chain version of core CPI has fallen from 1.8 percent last year to 1.2 percent this year... Excluding nonmarket prices, core PCE inflation fell from 1.3 percent in the year ending March 2002 to 0.9 percent in the year ending March 2003, and it has averaged 0.2 percent over the past six months." (Bernanke)
- "We expect core PCE inflation to fall to just over 1 percent both this year and next. After adjusting for a reasonable estimate of measurement bias, the two forecasts imply true inflation of about ½ of 1 percent... suggests an uncomfortably high 20 percent probability that true inflation could fall below zero next year." (Parry)
- "Inflation has fallen to dangerously low levels, and both arrows are pointed down... The Greenbook probability of deflation has risen to 35 percent." (Gramlich)

**Discussion Minutes (20):**
- "Members anticipated that inflation would remain at a low level for an extended period and indeed that the probability of further disinflation was higher than that of a pickup in inflation, given the current high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets."
- "Even assuming a pickup in the expansion of economic activity... excess capacity in labor and product markets would remain elevated and might well foster further disinflation over coming quarters."
- "Members also agreed that there was only a remote possibility that the process of disinflation would cumulate to the point of a decline for an extended period in the general price level."

**Staff Transcript (22):**
- Deflation risk still high (~35% probability from prior analysis). GDP gap widening further. Payrolls declining. IP falling. Core inflation at lowest point. Productivity surge keeping costs down.
- Direction: Below neutral — very subdued, deflation risk at peak.

**Staff Minutes (22):**
- "Core consumer price inflation moved down further in the first quarter from its already low level."
- "Given the ongoing slack in resource utilization, downward restraint on core price inflation was expected to persist over the forecast period."

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Scores: Disc-T 35 | Disc-M 30 | Staff-T 30 | Staff-M 32**

**Discussion Transcript (35):**
- "Hours worked are still contracting, and the output gap appears to be widening further." (Broaddus)
- "The output gap envisioned in the baseline persists for several quarters; it is not closed during the forecast period." (Ferguson)
- "We will have had nearly four years of below-potential growth and an output gap even into 2005. Unemployment rises through most of the forecast period." (Minehan)

**Discussion Minutes (30):**
- "Current high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets, which seemed likely to diminish only gradually."
- "Anecdotal reports by business contacts tended to emphasize widespread excess capacity as a reason for holding down business capital spending."
- "Near-term sluggishness in economic growth would give way to more vigorous expansion as the year progressed."

**Staff Transcript (30):**
- GDP revised down. "GDP gap higher, on average, in 2004 than in our previous projection." (Stockton)
- Economy not breaking out of sluggish pattern. Manufacturing contracting. Only defense spending keeping GDP positive.

**Staff Minutes (32):**
- "Economic activity continued to grow at a subpar pace in recent months."
- "Capacity utilization in manufacturing reaching a twenty-year low."
- Staff forecast: "economic expansion would be sluggish in the near term" with "persisting weakness in product and labor markets."

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Scores: Disc-T 30 | Disc-M 28 | Staff-T 24 | Staff-M 30**

**Discussion Transcript (30):**

- "The preliminary calculations for Manpower's third-quarter hiring plan survey, and the index fell from 11 to 6, which is the lowest level since 1991." (Moskow)
- "Regional labor markets also remain weak... employment in the region has continued to contract this year." (Santomero)

**Discussion Minutes (28):**

- "The latest reports on economic activity generally were disappointing, notably those relating to employment."

**Staff Minutes (30):**
- "The unemployment rate rose to 6 percent in April, with increases spread widely across most demographic groups."
- "Private nonfarm payroll employment continued to fall in April."
- "Initial claims for unemployment insurance remained at an elevated level, suggesting further labor market weakness in May."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Scores: Disc-T 37 | Disc-M 34 | Staff-T 35 | Staff-M 36**

**Discussion Transcript (37):**
- "There is no sign at all of a postwar bounce in the information that we have... His [Wal-Mart contact's] said there is no evidence of any postwar bounce in consumer spending." (Poole)
- "I continue to question business people closely about their capital spending plans, and I've yet to find any concrete evidence pointing to a firming of spending plans later this year. There is just too much excess capacity in too many firms." (Pianalto)
- "Labor markets remain quite slack... there's a trend toward less spending." (Minehan)

**Discussion Minutes (34):**
- "Business fixed investment remained a key factor... persisting weakness in such spending in association with gloomy sentiment and a high degree of risk aversion among business decisionmakers."
- "An appreciable decline in sales of motor vehicles and slower growth in other consumer spending in the first quarter."
- "The members did not see any firm indications of significant acceleration in consumer spending."

**Staff Transcript (35):**
- "Consumption and investment taken together rose just 1½ percent at an annual rate" Q1. (Stockton)
- "Housing not been as buoyant as we had expected." Business spending "below expectations."
- IP declining. Defense only strong component.

**Staff Minutes (36):**
- "Consumer spending advanced slightly in the first quarter and housing activity remained at a high level, but business investment slowed."
- "Real outlays on equipment and software declined in the first quarter."
- Staff forecast: "businesses and consumers were likely to hold down their spending."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Scores: Disc-T 22 | Disc-M 38**
*(Discussion track only)*

**Discussion Transcript (22):**
- "As insurance against downside surprises to both economic activity and inflation in the future, it would seem prudent to ease policy further." (Parry)
- "There appears to be a prima facie case for easing policy. I tend to agree with that conclusion, and barring an exceptionally strong near-term turnaround in the economy, I hope that the Committee will adopt a posture of leaning toward ease." (Bernanke)
- "We probably would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless we see a fairly substantial pickup in economic activity." (Greenspan)

**Discussion Minutes (38):**
- "All the members indicated that they could support a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "The members acknowledged that a case could be made for easing policy immediately... Nonetheless, they concluded that, on balance, an easing action was not desirable at this time."
- "Some members cautioned that persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends should not provide a basis for prolonged inaction... an easing move might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting."

*The 16-point gap between Disc-T (22) and Disc-M (38) reflects a structural difference: the transcript captures each member's explicit easing preference with strong individual language; the minutes record only the unanimous hold outcome plus soft hedging language, omitting the intensity of individual dovish statements.*

## Cross-Source Notes

**High convergence (all four sources within ±5–8 points):**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: 18–22. All four sources converge tightly on very low, declining inflation with deflation risk. The transcript records individual quantitative estimates (core PCE at 0.9%, 6-month rate at 0.2%) that give slightly lower scores; the staff documents note the same facts in more neutral language, producing scores 4 points higher.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 34–37. Tight convergence across all four sources. Consumer spending weak to modest; business investment declining; housing stable.
- PRODUCTIVITY: 63–65. Unanimous — all four sources describe structural productivity as elevated and supportive.
- OUTPUT_GAP: 30–35. Convergent direction; Disc-T is 5 points higher than the others, reflecting individual member statements about "widening" and "into 2005" language vs. the more aggregate staff and minutes descriptions.

**Moderate divergence (transcript vs. minutes):**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Disc-T 22 vs. Disc-M 38. The transcript records numerous individual statements of preference for immediate or imminent easing; the minutes records only the unanimous hold and uses softer language about "a case could be made." This is a structural transcript-vs.-minutes gap: the minutes documents the institutional outcome, not the deliberative preferences.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Disc-T 40 vs. Disc-M 22. The transcript preserves Parry's explicit dissent from the consensus hold, Broaddus's and Moskow's alternative statement preferences, and 4–5 distinct positions on the "taken together" sentence. The minutes collapses this to "two members" with mild emphasis differences.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Disc-T 60 vs. Disc-M 50. Transcript includes vivid individual statements about the unusual forecasting difficulty; minutes phrases this more calmly as "persisting uncertainty."
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Disc-T 38 vs. Disc-M 25. Transcript includes McDonough's extended dollar/euro discussion and Greenspan's financial markets commentary; minutes treats these as brief background.

**Notable single-source divergence:**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (Staff-T): 45 vs. 28 for the other three sources. Staff-T framed the improving post-war financial conditions as "below neutral but improving significantly," landing near 45. The other three sources emphasize the accommodative stance of policy and continued looseness, landing at 28. The divergence may reflect Staff-T weighting the transition from stressed to recovering rather than the absolute level of accommodation.
