### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

> MR. WILLIAMS: "with the 12-month core inflation rate at 1.9 percent and headline at 2 percent, we are now, in fact, closing in on our 2 percent inflation target. And I expect core inflation to soon reach and then to modestly exceed our target."

> MR. BULLARD: "headline PCE inflation measured from one year earlier is now 2 percent, and core PCE inflation measured from one year earlier is 1.9 percent. In central banking, this is about the best that can be achieved." [But also:] "the Dallas Fed trimmed mean was 1.8 percent a year ago and is 1.8 percent today."

> VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: "core PCE inflation in March 2017, without all of these [transitory] things, was 1.52 percent; core PCE inflation in March 2018 was 1.51 percent."

> MR. EVANS: "I'm still concerned, though, that inflation expectations remain somewhat below levels consistent with our symmetric 2 percent target."

**Center-of-gravity (Transcript)**: Headline and core PCE have just reached ~2%, but participants are divided about whether this reflects genuine underlying pressure (many point to transitory factors, low expectations) versus a durable achievement. The center sits just fractionally above the midpoint — at target but not clearly above it on a sustained basis.

---

**Minutes: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

> "The 12-month changes in overall and core PCE prices moved up in March, to 2 percent and 1.9 percent, respectively."

> "Most participants viewed the recent firming in inflation as providing some reassurance that inflation was on a trajectory to achieve the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective on a sustained basis."

> "Some participants noted that inflation was likely to modestly overshoot 2 percent for a time." vs. "several participants suggested that the underlying trend in inflation had changed little."

**Center-of-gravity (Minutes)**: Inflation has essentially reached the 2% target after years of undershooting; the center of gravity sees it at target with modest upside risk but no alarm.

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

The market is clearly tight, but Kashkari/Bullard prevent a score in the 70s. Score anchored at 64 — clearly tight, moderate worker shortages, but not at extreme historical tightness.
