### INFLATION_PRESSURE

## Notable Cross-Source Observations

1. **DISSENT_INTENSITY gap (Transcript 45 vs. Minutes 30)**: The transcript records named individual expressions of reluctance (Hoenig, Kelley, Stern) and a direct public rebuttal between McDonough and Poole. The minutes record one formal dissent and general "some members" language. The transcript captures the interpersonal intensity; the minutes flatten it to near-consensus.

2. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED gap (Transcript 62 vs. Minutes 50)**: The transcript contains multiple explicit first-person statements about the limits of knowledge (Greenspan: "we don't in fact know"; Poole: "decidedly atypical"). The minutes convey elevated uncertainty but through more neutral collective language.

3. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS gap (Transcript 38 vs. Minutes 25)**: The transcript records extended discussion of stock valuations, P/E ratios, earnings expectations, securities-firm profits, and Latin American instability. The minutes compress these into one paragraph on financial conditions as economic context.

4. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS divergence (Disc-Transcript 39 vs. Staff-Minutes 46)**: Discussion participants emphasize rising layoffs and spreading weakness (pushing score down); staff minutes present the same period as UE at 4.4% — still near NAIRU — with job losses as a deteriorating trend (score near neutral). The staff is anchored to the unemployment rate level; participants weight the rate of change.

5. **OUTPUT_GAP divergence (Staff-Minutes 32 vs. Staff-Transcript 40)**: The staff minutes provide direct language ("lowest capacity utilization since 1983," "grow little if at all") supporting a lower score; the staff transcript score is inferred at Low confidence because the meeting was dominated by the productivity briefing with no standard economic assessment.

6. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT convergence**: All four sources independently score this at 33–34, making it the most consistent dimension across the meeting.

7. **Staff-Transcript anomaly flag**: The June 2001 staff transcript was dominated by a special growth-accounting/productivity briefing (Sichel/Struckmeyer). Standard economic and financial dimensions lack direct staff assessment; most staff-transcript scores are inferred at Low confidence and should be weighted accordingly in any cross-method analysis.
