### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-universal view that inflation is meaningfully below the 2% target at 1.2% PCE, with forecasts projecting continued below-target readings; no participant sees current inflation as adequate or elevated.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "The 12-month change in the overall PCE chain index stands at 1.2 percent through January, 0.8 percentage point below our 2 percent target."
- Williams: "Inflation is well below our target. We're missing on both sides of our mandate."
- Kocherlakota: "when the outlook for inflation is as weak as it is, and where we have a target, we've said, of 2 percent inflation — it's very hard for us to be backing away from that in such a significant way."
- Bullard: "Inflation is currently low according to our preferred measures...We definitely need to defend our inflation target from the low side."

Minority views: George mentions risk of "higher long-term inflation expectations" from prolonged easing — a tail risk not a current reading. Bullard projects over-2% inflation in 2014 but acknowledges he needs incoming data to confirm.

---

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Nearly all participants see inflation below target with little near-term pressure; the center of gravity is clearly below the 2% objective with some downside risk.

Representative quotes:
- "Inflation had been running below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time, and nearly all of the participants anticipated that it would run at or below 2 percent over the medium term."
- "Participants saw little near-term inflationary pressure."
- "some participants saw downside risks to inflation, especially if economic activity did not pick up as projected."

Minority views: "a few participants noted that the risk remained that inflationary pressures could rise as the expansion continued, especially if monetary policy remained highly accommodative for too long."

---

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly characterize the economy as below potential — growing but frustratingly slowly, with fiscal headwinds from sequester and tax increases keeping the gap open.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "My forecast, like that of the Tealbook, envisions growth barely exceeding potential and little further improvement in the labor market over this coming year as increased fiscal drag offsets the improved momentum."
- Rosengren: "The economy is continuing to grow, despite much more fiscal austerity than many people anticipated... the progress toward normalcy is frustratingly slow."
- Lockhart: Q4 GDP ~0.6%, "still below the trend of the recovery."

Minority views: George and Pianalto are somewhat more optimistic about the private sector, but both still characterize growth as insufficient to meet mandate objectives.

---

**Minutes: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Economy is growing moderately but below potential, with fiscal headwinds and significant remaining slack. Recovery in progress but output gap still negative.

Representative quotes:
- "the economy had returned to moderate growth following a pause late last year"
- "fiscal policy was exerting significant near-term restraint on the economy"
- "slack in resource utilization remaining"

Minority views: "a few noted that the downside risks may have diminished" — modest optimism at the margin but no one suggests the gap is closing.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market is clearly below full employment (7.7% unemployment, estimated 2.5pp above NAIRU) but showing genuine improvement; the debate is whether "substantial improvement" has been reached, not whether the market is tight.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "The unemployment rate is 7.7 percent, 2½ percent above my estimate of full employment."
- Powell: "The unemployment rate has come down about ½ percentage point more than expectation. Payroll employment is stronger, averaging 200,000 for the past five months."
- Yellen: "I certainly do not see this criterion [substantial improvement] as yet being met, but there has been some improvement in the labor market."
- Pianalto: "I would judge the outlook for labor market conditions to have improved substantially once I see six to nine months of average job gains of about 200,000 per month. Since we have already had five months at that pace, I am almost there."

Minority views: More hawkish participants (Plosser, George, Lacker) argue improvement is sufficient to begin taper, but even they do not claim the labor market is at or above full employment.

---

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving from weak levels but unemployment still elevated with cautious hiring; well below full employment.

Representative quotes:
- "the unemployment rate remained elevated"
- "participants generally saw the unemployment rate as still elevated and were not yet confident that the recent progress toward the Committee's employment objective would be sustained"
- "firms reportedly remained cautious about hiring"

Minority views: Some noted signs of improvement, and one noted payroll gains "might be more meaningful than would first appear," but the consensus is that the market remains slack.

### D4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Transcript: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Spending and investment are below trend but showing resilient positive momentum given the severity of fiscal headwinds; the tone is "sluggish but better than feared."

Representative quotes:
- Lockhart: GDP Q4 ~0.6%, "still below the trend of the recovery."
- Rosengren: "The economy is continuing to grow, despite much more fiscal austerity than many people anticipated." "If you had told me last fall that we were going to have a sequester and the tax increase that we had, I would have expected a much weaker first half than what we've actually seen."
- George: "private-sector demand and housing markets appear poised to support growth going forward."
- Duke: "job creation held up remarkably well as we approached the fiscal cliff."

Minority views: Participants do not disagree substantially on the consumption/investment assessment; the main disagreement is about how to respond to it.

---

**Minutes: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Spending is improving and "somewhat encouraging" with moderate acceleration, but remains below trend with fiscal headwinds and cautious consumers.

Representative quotes:
- "most participants noted that the data on spending were somewhat encouraging, particularly with regard to spending on automobiles, other consumer durables, and housing"
- "Firms reported increased planning for capital expenditures, supported by low interest rates and substantial cash holdings"
- "a couple of other participants thought that overall consumer spending was likely still held back"

Minority views: Some see payroll tax and student debt as restraints; others emphasize pent-up demand support.

### D5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are accommodative/loose — the LSAP program has produced improvements in financial conditions, bank health has recovered substantially, and concerns about "reach for yield" confirm conditions are easy rather than tight.

Representative quotes:
- Dudley: "We are getting significant positive benefit in terms of financial conditions, and the improvement in financial conditions does seem to be lending support to the economy."
- Fisher: "The 2012 banking data show it was a good year for banks. We're back above a 1 percent ROA. Noncurrent loans are continuing to fall nationwide. The proportion of banks that are 1- and 2-rated is now 70 percent."
- Rosengren: "yields on Spanish and Italian debt have been pretty well behaved, given the sequence of shocks that we've had."
- George: "incentives to reach for yield continue" — implying yields are compressed and credit is easy.

Minority views: Plosser's concern about "buoyancy in asset prices" and George's "reach for yield" concern both presuppose loose conditions, reinforcing the low score rather than challenging it.

---

**Minutes: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose, with reach-for-yield behavior indicating easy credit and low rates supporting activity.

Representative quotes:
- "Accommodative monetary policy was likely providing important support to these developments"
- "investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk -- either credit risk or interest rate risk -- in an effort to boost returns"
- "easing of credit constraints allowed more borrowers to take advantage of lower interest rates"

Minority views: Some participants note "episodic strains in European markets" but domestic conditions described as loose.

### D6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Transcript: 28 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The unanimous vote for maintaining full QE at $85B/month places this firmly in the moderate-dovish range; while a growing minority is preparing for tapering at future meetings, the current meeting's action and the majority view are solidly for continued maximum accommodation.

Representative quotes (dovish majority):
- Williams: "In my view, we'll need to continue our Treasury and MBS purchases at least well into the second half of the year."
- Kocherlakota: "I am all about trying to keep the level of accommodation the same. I just think it is really hard when you look at our outlooks to argue we should be tightening in a significant way in June."
- Evans: "The economic and inflation situations today call for continued strong accommodation."
- Yellen: "I think we should continue our asset purchases at an undiminished pace."

Minority views (hawkish tail): Plosser: "Were I not afraid of surprising the public, I would advocate beginning to taper purchases at this meeting." Lacker: "I'd favor moving toward tapering as soon as possible." George: "I believe we should start laying the groundwork for bringing asset purchases to an end."

---

**Minutes: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Firmly accommodative with QE continuing at full pace, but the beginning of taper discussions introduces a modest hawkish lean relative to peak QE dovishness.

Representative quotes:
- "all but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted"
- Committee agreed to continue purchases of $40B MBS + $45B Treasuries per month
- "Many participants... expressed the view that continued solid improvement in the outlook for the labor market could prompt the Committee to slow the pace of purchases beginning at some point over the next several meetings"

Minority views: One formal dissent (hawkish); "a few" want to end purchases soon; "a few" want to continue at least through year-end.

### D7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Transcript: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Zero formal dissents but multiple distinct camps with fundamentally different views on how accommodative policy should be; the underlying disagreement is deep but contained within a unanimous vote.

Representative quotes:
- No formal voting dissents are recorded in this transcript.
- Kocherlakota characterizes "a basic tension...between two forces — credibility and balance sheet size restrictions."
- Distinct camps: (1) dovish/credibility camp (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Yellen) vs. (2) early-taper camp (Plosser, Lacker, George) vs. (3) conditional-June-taper camp (Stein, Pianalto, Duke, Powell, Fisher).
- Tarullo: "some of those who feel like expectations got out ahead of where they and the Committee are."

Minority views: The very existence of Plosser/Lacker/George wanting immediate taper vs. Kocherlakota/Evans/Yellen wanting to hold firm is the most notable tension.

---

**Minutes: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent plus a wide spectrum of views on taper timing. Agreement on the general accommodative stance but notable disagreement on the trajectory of asset purchases.

Representative quotes:
- "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision"
- "A range of views was expressed regarding the economic and labor market conditions that would call for an adjustment in the pace of purchases"
- Views range from "bring the program to a close relatively soon" to "purchases might well continue at the current pace at least through the end of the year"

Minority views: The dissenter and those wanting early taper closure form one camp; those wanting purchases through year-end form another.

### D8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Transcript: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a prominently discussed topic touched by 6+ participants meaningfully, but it serves as a backdrop to the tapering/exit debate rather than being the dominant stand-alone concern.

Representative quotes:
- Plosser: Extended remarks on asset price buoyancy, wealth effects, and risk of "a sharp correction in the markets, which could dash confidence and set back the recovery."
- George: Extended discussion of "incentives to reach for yield...community banks, insurance companies, pension funds, and individual savers...actively looking for riskier alternatives."
- Lacker: Extensive discussion of MBS REITs, moral hazard, non-Treasury asset holdings.
- Stein: Discusses "market fragility issues" and MBS space.
- Dudley: Notes agency MBS REIT moral hazard concern.
- Rosengren: "Financial stability does not appear to be at risk from our actions to date."

Minority views: Rosengren explicitly argues financial stability is not currently at risk — this limits the "alarm" dimension but not the attention score.

---

**Minutes: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability received prominent attention, with a dedicated review of QE costs and risks. More attention than typical 2013 meetings due to the formal efficacy/costs review.

Representative quotes:
- "Participants discussed their assessments of risks to financial stability, particularly in light of the Committee's highly accommodative stance of monetary policy."
- "Many participants noted that in the current low-interest rate environment, investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk"
- Extensive multi-paragraph discussion of costs/risks of asset purchases to financial stability, market functioning, and exit risks

Minority views: N/A — most participants engaged with the topic, varying in degree of concern.

### D9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Transcript: 10 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The formal forward guidance retains the Evans Rule threshold-based commitment and open-ended QE — a very strong easing signal; the meeting's only forward nudge is gentle optionality about adjusting the pace, which is not a hawkish signal but a modest clarification that purchases are not unconditional.

Representative quotes:
- The Evans Rule (6.5% unemployment threshold) remains intact with no changes.
- The open-ended QE program at $85B/month is continued with no tapering.
- Kocherlakota: "our commitment is to accommodation and not to a particular tool."
- Yellen: "reaffirm our commitment to continue asset purchases until we have seen a substantial improvement in the outlook for the labor market in the context of price stability."

Minority views: Several participants argue internally for tapering at June — but this is not embedded in the statement or official guidance at this meeting.

---

**Minutes: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Threshold-based forward guidance (Evans Rule) fully in place, signaling extended accommodation. Conditional taper discussion introduces a mild hint of future normalization, but the dominant signal remains "low for long" with QE continuing.

Representative quotes:
- "The Committee also retained its forward guidance about the federal funds rate, including the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates."
- "continued solid improvement in the outlook for the labor market could prompt the Committee to slow the pace of purchases beginning at some point over the next several meetings"
- "were the outlook to deteriorate, the pace of purchases could be increased"

Minority views: Some want taper soon; others want purchases through year-end.

### D10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Transcript: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is elevated above baseline given fiscal drag sequencing, European risks (Cyprus, Italy), and genuine ambiguity about when "substantial improvement" thresholds will be met, but participants are not expressing deep or unprecedented uncertainty — this is standard recovery-path uncertainty.

Representative quotes:
- Tarullo: "I think in a way...a little bit of uncertainty in those who read the statement and hear the Chairman's press conference is actually sort of a good thing."
- Kocherlakota: "there is no way to solve this. There is no way to cut this Gordian knot."
- Stein: Works through decision tree scenarios (200K jobs vs. 140–160K vs. below) to frame uncertainty about outcomes.
- Bullard: Praises open-ended program as automatic stabilizer precisely because the macro path is uncertain.

Minority views: No participant expresses extreme or Knightian uncertainty; the uncertainty is primarily about the pace of healing, not about the direction.

---

**Minutes: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Moderate, somewhat elevated uncertainty — standard for this period. Uncertainty about QE effects and sustainability of improvement, but some contacts report declining uncertainty. Balanced overall.

Representative quotes:
- "participants recognized that estimates of the economic effects were necessarily imprecise and covered a wide range"
- "were not yet confident that the recent progress toward the Committee's employment objective would be sustained"
- "a few participants indicated that their contacts saw the level of uncertainty about the economic outlook as having declined recently"

Minority views: One participant sees housing outlook as "quite uncertain and potentially subject to rapid changes."
