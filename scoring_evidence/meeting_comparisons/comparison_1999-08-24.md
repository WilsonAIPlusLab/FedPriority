## Notable Gaps and Observations

**Discussion track**: Scores are closely aligned across transcript and minutes for all core dimensions (largest gap: FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS at 16 points, where the transcript captures extensive discussion of Y2K liquidity risks, international fragility, and equity correction scenarios that the minutes compress to transmission-mechanism language only).

**Staff track**: Several large gaps warrant attention:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (15 points): Prell's transcript language is explicitly alarmed about wage surprises and forecast revisions; minutes describes the same data with "subdued" core language.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (22 points): Fisher's detailed financial markets briefing in the transcript documents widening to above-October 1998 levels; the minutes staff-attributable text lacks this spread analysis.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH (22 points): Fisher's transcript emphasis on the dollar being "quite strong" in historic terms contrasts with the minutes focus on the intermeeting depreciation move.
- EQUITY_VALUATION (17 points): Transcript reads near neutral; minutes scoring draws on wealth-effect language to infer elevated valuation concern.

These gaps reflect the compression from transcript to minutes rather than contradictory readings — the underlying data and economic facts are consistent across sources.
