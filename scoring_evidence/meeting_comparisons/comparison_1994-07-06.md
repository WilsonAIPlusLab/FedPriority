## Cross-Source Summary Notes

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE gap — Discussion vs. Staff**: Discussion sources (56-57) score this dimension noticeably higher than staff sources (47-50). Participants focus on acceleration since September 1993 and upside risks; staff focus on current core readings which show deceleration over the trailing 12 months. The two framings (risk-based vs. data-based) produce the gap without contradiction.

2. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS/CONDITIONS gap**: Discussion sources (57) and staff sources (44-45) diverge on this dimension due to different reference points. Discussion participants compare conditions to the very accommodative 1993 baseline ("30-year bond up 200bp from October lows"); staff language anchors to current accommodation level ("less accommodative degree of reserve pressure").

3. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT — Staff transcript outlier**: Staff transcript scores 64, reflecting Keehn's district anecdotes (steel "very strong," appliances "record levels," trucks booked to mid-1995). The other three sources range 56-57. The staff transcript's district specificity drives the higher reading.

4. **DISSENT_INTENSITY transcript vs. minutes gap (42 vs. 22)**: The largest gap in the discussion dimensions. The transcript preserves Melzer and Broaddus's explicit 50bp advocacy, Greenspan's characterization of only a "modest majority" for asymmetry, and the range from Stern's symmetric preference to Broaddus's immediate hike demand. The minutes synthesize this to "views differed to some degree" with "members generally concluded."

5. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: All three sources that score this dimension (discussion transcript, staff transcript, staff minutes) place it well below 50, confirming notable dollar weakness as a consistent cross-source finding. The discussion minutes score of 60 is anomalous and likely reflects a different construct interpretation (concern about weakness rather than strength).

6. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE coherence**: Both discussion sources produce closely aligned hawkish forward guidance scores (70 and 67), supported by Greenspan's explicit August 50bp comment and McDonough's intermeeting move discussion. No staff equivalent dimension scored.
