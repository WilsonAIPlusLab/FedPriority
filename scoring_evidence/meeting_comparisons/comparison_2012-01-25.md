### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript — Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The overwhelming consensus is that PCE inflation has decelerated and is expected to remain below the 2% target for the entire forecast horizon; the main debate is whether it undershoots modestly or significantly, not whether it is at or above target.

Representative quotes:
- Rosengren: "PCE inflation rate is 1.3 percent" in Tealbook end-2013 forecast; "there is surprising agreement that over the next two years, we will fall short of both elements of our mandate."
- Williams: "I foresee PCE inflation around 1½ percent this year and in 2013—and below our shared goal of 2 percent. Inflationary pressures have clearly diminished over the past six months."
- Evans: "With our inflation projections not even close to any reasonable upper tolerance range relative to our 2 percent objective, this argues for balance."
- Bernanke (synthesis): "Most do see PCE inflation as remaining at or below the 2 percent objective for the next couple of years...inflation risks seem relatively low at this stage."

Minority views (transcript): Plosser sees inflation "above our goal" currently; Lacker projects inflation "fluctuating around 2 percent."

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus that inflation is subdued and at or below 2%, with some worried about further downward drift. Clearly below target but not in deflationary territory.

Representative quotes:
- "almost all participants expected inflation to remain subdued in coming quarters—that is, to run at or below the 2 percent level"
- "inflation had been subdued in recent months, there was little evidence of wage or cost pressures"
- "some remained concerned that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might continue to drift down and run below mandate-consistent levels for some time"

Minority views (minutes): "a couple of participants were concerned that inflation could rise as the recovery continued."

### D2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Transcript — Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Essentially all participants agree the economy is substantially below potential. The optimists see it recovering toward trend but still short; the pessimists see a prolonged gap. No participant claims above-potential output.

Representative quotes:
- Yellen: "more than 20 percentage points of cumulative unemployment gaps and nearly 50 percentage points of cumulative output gaps over the decade following the onset of the recession in December 2007."
- Evans: "exhibit 1, our forecast for the unemployment rate exhibits a long period of substantial deviations above what everybody's long-run sustainable unemployment rate is."
- Williams: "the economy remains in a frustrating, stutter-step recovery, alternating between modest and moderate growth; this kind of pace won't do much to reduce slack in the economy."
- Bernanke (synthesis): "modest, near-trend economic growth over the next year, which of course implies very slow progress in the labor market."

Minority views (transcript): Plosser expects "economy to grow close to 3 percent—slightly above trend," though this refers to the growth rate, not the current gap being closed.

---

**Minutes — Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Clear consensus that significant slack persists in both product and labor markets. Economy expanding but far too slowly to close the gap. "Wide margins of slack" is strong language.

Representative quotes:
- "the modest expansion in economic activity that they were projecting would be consistent with a gradual reduction in the current wide margins of slack in labor and product markets"
- "the economy had been expanding moderately"
- Participants listed multiple headwinds restraining the pace of expansion.

Minority views (minutes): None significantly challenge the below-potential assessment.

### D3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Transcript — Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: The labor market is far from full employment with unemployment at ~8.5% and extensive evidence of hidden slack. Some improvement noted over the past year but still firmly in "significant slack remains" territory.

Representative quotes:
- Pianalto: "there are about four job seekers for every job opening" — vs. "fewer than two" before the recession.
- Tarullo: "We're still 6 million jobs short of the number of employed persons in the economy before the recession...Even if we were to assume...250,000 a month...it would take well over two years."
- Williams: "All indicators point to a lot of slack remaining in labor markets...much of the recent decline in the unemployment rate comes from people leaving the labor force."
- Rosengren: "unemployment rate is 8.2 percent" end-2013, vs. full employment — a "substantial shortfall."

Minority views (transcript): Plosser more optimistic — sees unemployment heading to ~7–8% by end-2013; Kocherlakota describes Minnesota at 6% with labor markets "almost normalized" but national picture still weak.

---

**Minutes — Score: 30 ±8 [High confidence]**

Center-of-gravity: Labor market has significant slack with elevated unemployment and underemployment despite some gradual improvement.

Representative quotes:

Minority views (minutes): Debate over structural vs. cyclical component — "Very high long-duration unemployment might indicate a mismatch" — creates uncertainty about the degree of slack but not the direction.
