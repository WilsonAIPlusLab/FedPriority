# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison — 1994-03-22

## Meeting Context
March 22, 1994. Second meeting of the tightening cycle (first hike: February 4, 1994, +25bp). Economy accelerating after the 1990-91 recession recovery. Core CPI near multi-year lows but pipeline pressures building. Bond markets experiencing significant turbulence. Debate at this meeting: 25bp vs. 50bp increase; symmetric vs. asymmetric directive. Final action: +25bp (two dissents for larger move, from Broaddus and Jordan).

### Dimensions with Notable Gaps (gap ≥ 10 points)
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Transcript 58, Minutes 40 — gap of 18. The transcript records extensive, named-speaker discussion by Greenspan (1987 parallels, mutual fund risks, "tensile strength" of the financial system), McDonough (near-crisis phone coordination with money center banks and European central banks, JP Morgan/Bankers Trust rumors), Lindsey (margin debt at 1987 levels), and Phillips (derivatives complexity). The minutes compresses this to general references to bond/equity volatility and market sensitivity without preserving the near-crisis narrative or the framing of financial fragility as the binding constraint on tightening pace.

### Discussion vs. Staff Divergences
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Discussion (both sources: 53) vs. Staff transcript (46) and Staff minutes (42). Discussion participants interpret labor conditions as approaching cyclical-peak tightness; staff reporters emphasize UE at 6.5% and mixed signals (layoffs continuing alongside new hiring willingness).
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Discussion (59-60) vs. Staff transcript (65) and Staff minutes (57). The staff transcript's district reporters (Keehn) cite exceptionally high specific readings (retail +17%, steel flat out) that push the staff transcript score above the discussion average.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Discussion transcript (58) is the only source where this receives extended treatment as the binding constraint on policy; all other sources score it lower (40 in discussion minutes; not a separate dimension in staff sources).
