# FOMC Meeting Evidence Comparison: 1997-08-19

## Meeting Context
August 1997. Fed funds rate at 5.5%. Late-1990s expansion. Unemployment at 4.8% nationally, with some districts reporting 2-3%. Inflation subdued despite tight labor markets — the "New Economy" productivity puzzle. Asian financial crisis beginning (Thailand, July 1997) but not yet a major US concern. FOMC holds rates steady; unanimous hold with asymmetric tightening bias.

## Cross-Source Summary

### High-agreement dimensions (within 10 points across all sources)
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Transcript 65, Minutes 65, Staff-T 63, Staff-M 62. Exceptional agreement. All sources use explicit tight/taut language; unemployment at 4.8% matching expansion low; labor shortages ubiquitous.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Transcript 60, Minutes 62, Staff-T 58, Staff-M 60. All sources agree on above-trend spending. Language ranges from "high-spirited consumers" (staff) to "double-digit rates of increase" (discussion) to "substantially further" business investment (minutes).
- **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS/CONDITIONS**: Transcript 27, Minutes 28, Staff-T 38, Staff-M 32. All sources agree conditions are accommodative/loose. Credit readily available, market rates declining, equity prices rising.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff-T 62, Staff-M 67. Consistent across staff sources.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Transcript 60, Minutes 62. Consistent: asymmetric tightening bias, next move expected to be firming.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Transcript 12, Minutes 10. Near-total consensus on both.

### Notable divergences

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Transcript 38 vs. Minutes 44 vs. Staff-T 40 vs. Staff-M 32. An 12-point spread. Discussion transcript and staff transcript cluster (38-40); discussion minutes is higher (44, reflecting more forward-looking concern about emerging wage pressures); staff minutes is lowest (32, reflecting explicit PPI deflation language and data characterization). The divergence is partly a present-tense vs. forward-looking framing difference.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Transcript 58 vs. Minutes 52. Discussion transcript's 6-point gap reflects the transcript's more prominent "2 percentage points above potential" language (McDonough) versus the minutes' more cautious "near potential" framing. Staff scores are intermediate (54-56).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Transcript 30 vs. Minutes 12. The largest divergence in Part A. The transcript captures Greenspan-Minehan exchange on asset price bubbles and Jordan's concerns about farmland/real estate/credit standards — content that did not carry over into the synthesized minutes, which treat financial conditions only as context for spending.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Transcript 45 vs. Minutes 32. The transcript captures the extended paradigm debate (Kelley's "straws in the wind," Rivlin's "stuck between old and new paradigms," Jordan's "not until November") as genuinely elevated uncertainty. The minutes present a more confident, synthesized version of the outlook.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (Staff)**: Transcript 22 vs. Minutes 45. Large divergence. The transcript captures Prell's explicit prediction of a stock market decline and the saving-rate/stock-wealth story. The minutes record only that equity prices "increased on balance" with no valuation concern expressed by staff.
