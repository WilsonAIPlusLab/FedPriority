# Evidence Comparison: FOMC Meeting 1996-08-20

**Meeting context**: August 1996, mid-expansion. Fed funds rate at 5.25% (held). Economy growing above trend in first half, labor markets tight, but inflation readings benign. Key tension: tight labor markets and rising ECI vs. contained price measures. Committee voted to hold with asymmetric tightening bias; one formal dissent (Stern).

---

## Part A: Discussion (Participant Views)

### Dimension 1: INFLATION_PRESSURE

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 53 ±8 [High] | 52 ±7 [Moderate] |
| **Center-of-gravity** | Current inflation benign, broad measures still declining; upside risks from tight labor markets and rising ECI | Members see current inflation as contained but perceive clear upside risks from labor costs and resource pressures |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Broaddus: "the pressure on resources is still likely to push the trend rate of inflation up a bit, perhaps to above 3 percent over the projection period"
- Meyer: "broad measures of price inflation are still declining... The inflation rate in each case is about 2 percent over the past year"
- Phillips: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued for this stage of an expansion... the risks remain on the up side"
- Boehne: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued... upward wage pressures will not likely bubble up into upward price pressures as much as one might think"

**Minutes quotes:**
- "broad measures of price inflation, adjusted to exclude their volatile food and energy components, did not exhibit any uptrend despite robust growth in economic activity this year and high levels of resource use"
- "the inflation risks in the outlook clearly seemed to be to the upside, with the potential for more inflation stemming from rising labor compensation costs"
- "Upward wage adjustments were becoming more evident and increases in overall compensation had edged up"

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|

**Transcript quotes:**

**Minutes quotes:**

- "growth in such spending could be expected to slow in the context of increasingly satisfied pent-up demands"
- "Real consumer spending weakened somewhat on balance over June and July following several months of robust growth"

### Dimension 6: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 56 ±8 [High] | 56 ±5 [High] |
| **Center-of-gravity** | Hold with tightening bias; ~6 of 19 favored immediate tightening; most holders acknowledged tightening likely needed soon | Hold with clear tightening bias; majority chose patience but signaled readiness to tighten; one formal dissenter |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Greenspan: "if the economy is in the process of growing at an unsustainable pace... raising the federal funds rate only 25 basis points is not going to help much... I believe that we can prudently hold off for a while... I trust we will continue our asymmetric bias"
- Broaddus: "I still favor some tightening of monetary policy... a 50 basis point move today would essentially drop both shoes"
- Yellen: "I continue to consider the inflationary risks biased to the up side... I support your recommendation [to hold]"
- Rivlin: "I still believe it is possible that we will not have to move... The general view seems to be that it is a question of when rather than whether"

**Minutes quotes:**
- "all but one of the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "they also agreed on the desirability of a directive that remained biased toward possible tightening"
- "any tightening should be implemented promptly if developments were perceived as pointing to rising inflation"
- Mr. Stern dissented favoring "modestly more restrictive" policy

### Dimension 7: DISSENT_INTENSITY

| | Transcript | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| **Score** | 47 ±7 [High] | 30 ±5 [High] |
| **Center-of-gravity** | Notable disagreement: ~6 participants favored immediate tightening, many holders expressed reluctance | One formal dissent (Stern), majority cohesive on rationale; standard one-dissent meeting |

**Transcript quotes:**
- Six participants explicitly favored tightening: Broaddus (50bp), Melzer (50bp), Parry (25bp), Hoenig (25bp), Stern (25bp), Minehan (25bp)
- McDonough held but said: "When we move... the move should be 50 basis points"
- Moskow held but expressed concern: "waiting makes the September and November meetings very crucial decision points"
- Phillips held but noted: "my suspicion is that at some point we are going to need to tighten"

**Minutes quotes:**
- "all but one of the members supported" the hold decision
- "Mr. Stern dissented because he believed that policy should become modestly more restrictive"
- The majority showed broad agreement on rationale (wait and watch for inflation signals)

*Note: The transcript reveals a much broader coalition favoring tightening (at least 6 participants), while the minutes records only 1 formal dissent (Stern). The transcript score (47) reflects the breadth of hawkish sentiment visible in the unedited record; the minutes score (30) reflects the compressed summary showing only the formal dissent.*

**Transcript quotes:**
