### D1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Transcript score: 22 ±6 | Minutes score: 22 ±8**

| Transcript (verbatim quotes) | Minutes (verbatim quotes) |
|---|---|
| Yellen: "I foresee a slow recovery over the next several years with stubbornly high unemployment and very low price inflation." | "Most participants anticipated that substantial slack in resource utilization would lead to subdued and potentially declining wage and price inflation over the next few years; a few saw a risk of substantial disinflation." |
| Yellen: "The intense downward wage pressure reported by my contacts could result in disinflation over the next few years that is more severe than in the Greenbook forecast." | "Several were skeptical that temporarily low levels of resource utilization would reduce inflation appreciably, given the loose empirical relationship of economic slack to inflation." |
| Dudley: "Despite the big buildup of slack in the economy, the core measures of inflation have been somewhat sticky. I don't think this will last. Thus, I think the risks on inflation are still likely to be on the downside over the next year or two." | [Concerns noted about] "balance sheet expansion and deficits potentially leading to higher inflation expectations if policies not adjusted." |
| Kohn: "The underlying pressure here is for lower inflation with the weakness in compensation and labor costs... There won't be any pricing power for a number of years under the most likely scenario." | |
| Evans: "resource gaps will be an important factor restraining inflation over the immediate projection period and beyond." | |

**Transcript center of gravity:** Overwhelming consensus that inflation is below target with meaningful disinflation risk over the next 1–2 years; core PCE running ~1.5% and expected to dip further before recovering. A minority (Plosser, Lacker, Bullard) warn against over-relying on output gap forecasts and flag longer-term inflation risk from monetary expansion, but do not claim current elevated inflation.

**Minutes center of gravity:** The majority view is that inflation is subdued and likely to decline further due to massive slack, placing this firmly in the "below target; slack keeping prices in check" range. Several skeptics doubt slack will reduce inflation, and concerns about future inflation from balance sheet expansion widen the band upward.
