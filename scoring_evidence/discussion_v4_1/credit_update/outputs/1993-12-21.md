# Blind check, FOMC 1993-12-21: CREDIT_SPREADS and EQUITY_VALUATION

I read the input to its last line (the vote read-out). The document holds a policy go-around, a vote on an unchanged policy with a symmetric directive, and a closing item on the October 15 tapes. It is not special-topic.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses spread, yield-premium or risk-premium wording about credit instruments. The only yield talk concerns Treasury and long-term rates (Broaddus: "the 1/2 point backup in long-term interest rates we've seen recently is a warning"; Phillips: "the recent backup that we've seen in long-term rates is showing the deterioration in inflation expectations"). That is a policy and expectations point, not a credit spread.

Lending and availability language is present, so under clarification 3 the dimension is not N/A. It rests on availability, not on spreads. The evidence is thin and points mildly to easy to normal credit availability:
- Phillips: "the financial sector is certainly well positioned to support growth"; consumers are "borrowing to do it."
- Jordan: banks "say they are getting business loan demand."
- Hoenig: "loan growth is fairly strong" in Denver, Omaha and Lincoln.
- Greenspan: "It doesn't look yet as though we are getting the surge in credit demands in the system"; net nonfinancial debt is "finally beginning to move" but "still moving at a very slow pace relative to history."
- Angell, on the other side, says "a very significant real asset deflation... has altered the behavior of the commercial banking industry in regard to making loans." This describes a past restraint, not a current one.
- Lindsey: "You have to be crazy to go into C&I loans" (the context is firms with plenty of cash, so it concerns demand).

Most of this is loan demand, not supply or terms. Nobody says credit is tight or rationed, and the financial sector is described as supportive. I read availability as normal to slightly easy. About five participants touch lending, but none speaks to spreads or terms. Rule applied: lending and availability without spread wording gives a Low-confidence score with UNC of at least +-10, and the score rests on availability. Score 43, UNC +-12, Low.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Mullins gives an explicit view of valuation against fundamentals: "Last summer when the stock market was at record levels and we had a weak first-half GDP performance there was concern that the market was getting ahead of economic fundamentals. Now, six months later, the market has gone essentially sideways for half a year. The economic outlook has caught up with the market... I no longer hear the scare stories about market fragility, which probably means it's now that we should worry. But there is a sense in which the economic outlook has filled in underneath the market." The view is that an earlier valuation concern has faded and the market is now roughly supported by fundamentals. The "we should worry" remark is a wry aside, and the concern is not endorsed.

Lindsey adds a mild speculation note: asset re-diversification "out of dividend paying stocks into OTC stocks; 85 percent of OTC stocks, I'm told, have outperformed the DOW this year. They are nondividend paying, basically speculative," plus the new-issue example of Boston Chicken. This hints at some speculative froth in a segment, not a view that the market overall is overvalued. It is partly a wealth and portfolio-allocation channel point.

Two participants speak. One gives a valuation view, roughly fair after earlier concern. The other makes a small speculative aside. Rule applied: a stated valuation view is scored on the table (46-54, roughly fair, no particular concern), nudged slightly toward concern by Lindsey's speculation remark and Mullins' lingering caution. Score 45, UNC +-8, Moderate.
