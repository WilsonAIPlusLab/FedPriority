# Blind check, 1993-09-21 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

Read to the last line (policy vote: 12 Yes roll call, "B" symmetric, no dissents). Staff text is not in the file.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses spread, premium or quality-spread wording. The only yield remarks are Treasury/long-bond levels (Mullins: "the long bond yield has come down 50 basis points in the last month"; Broaddus: "further decline in long-term rates"), which are not credit spreads. Under clarification 3 the score therefore rests on lending availability and terms only, so CONF is Low and UNC is at least +-10. The score rests on availability, not on any spread wording.

Availability evidence is mixed but leans easy:
- Easier side. Mr. Syron: "There is an improvement in bank willingness to lend ... there is more lending now even in small commercial real estate ventures." Mr. Melzer: "an increase in loan activity across the board in the second quarter ... Consumer, real estate, and business categories were all growing." Ms. Phillips: "The broader monetary aggregates and even credit are showing some signs of strength. The banking system is stronger; the financial markets are stronger." Mr. Mullins: "substantial easing in the capital market environment" and "the financial system has eased." Mr. Hoenig: banks' earnings "extremely well", with a couple of Oklahoma banks "actually talking about making loans." Chairman Greenspan: balance-sheet stringency in commercial property "is beginning to ease."
- Tighter or less favorable side. Mr. Forrestal: small business people "complain, rather bitterly ... about the lack of credit availability", though "the farmers ... say the banks are quite aggressive." Mr. Boehne: "weak commercial loan demand" (a demand statement, not a supply constraint). Mr. Lindsey: tightened bank regulation and down-payment standards hurting minority communities (a niche supply remark).

About seven participants say credit is easing or available, with two or three carrying tighter-side remarks that are partial and local. Net reading: availability somewhat easier than normal, but no stated spread level. Score 44, below 50 on the availability reading, +-10, Low.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Participants state a valuation or concern view, so the score is placed on the table (clarification 2).
- Vice Chairman McDonough: "growing concern in the financial markets about the level of the DOW and of the equity markets in general ... as to the sustainability of these levels of equity prices", "very serious cause for concern on our part", with mutual-fund redemption risk if there were "a sharp correction."
- Ms. Phillips: "when I look at the stock market that I do see it as more problematic than the bond market, although I'm not one who has visions of tulips ... But it is at a higher level."
- Mr. Syron: "very strong flows [and] increasingly nervous fund managers" (supports the concern reading).
- Mr. Angell goes the other way in part: with downsizing efficiencies "the profit numbers ought to sustain the kind of equity prices that we see", but he finds the lack of foreign inflow "confusing."

Three participants voice concern about elevated or unsustainable levels. Phillips explicitly rules out bubble talk and McDonough speaks of a possible correction. That is "modestly above fair value / somewhat elevated", not "stretched" or "frothy". Score 34, +-8, Moderate (several participants, but concern is stated in measured terms and Angell partly offsets).
