## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses the words "credit spreads", "corporate spreads" or "risk spreads". The evidence is indirect, from risk-premium wording and financial-conditions language.

- Chairman Greenspan: "we have been through an exceptionally long period of disinflation, which has brought actual inflation expectations down and term premiums down. Everything is at its bottom. He also says there will be "much, much less certainty than has been exhibited in financial markets over the last year or so."

Mr. Moskow says "so much liquidity in the financial system".

Mr. Fisher: a bank CEO says "loan demand is stronger than horseradish" (demand, not supply). Ms. Minehan's bank contact says "the banking business had been a bit tough" because of the yield curve. Neither of these bears on spreads.

Greenspan's "nowhere to go except up" is a forward comment and does not change the current level. About five participants support the below-50 reading (Greenspan, Minehan, Geithner, Kohn, Bies, with Moskow and Stern as weaker support). Only Minehan and Greenspan speak to risk or premium pricing, and neither speaks to credit spreads as such. Because the evidence is indirect and rests largely on "favorable financial conditions" and liquidity wording, the score is Low confidence with uncertainty of +-10.
