## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses spread, premium or credit-yield wording. Nobody states a level for spreads. The only material is lending/availability language, so I apply the clarification rule that availability or lending language without spread wording can support a score with CONF Low and UNC at least +-10. This score rests on availability, not on spreads.

Evidence:
- Governor Phillips: "I think the financial markets since our last meeting have ... generally have improved--the dollar, the stock market, the bond markets. This means a continued availability of relatively low cost capital, which bodes well for the business investment outlook." This is the clearest statement. It points to ample, cheap capital, which is on the below-50 (favorable) side.
- President Forrestal: "bankers are now becoming much more cautious about financing these activities [speculative office and industrial projects], which I guess is good news. Bank lending remains mixed with the strength being in the business area." This is mixed. It is a mild tightening of CRE financing that he calls good news, not a stress signal.
- Presidents Boehne, Hoenig, Minehan and Vice Chairman McDonough talk about loan demand or loan growth (flat, slowed, 6 percent growth, weak consumer loan demand). That is demand and quantity, not terms or spreads, so I give it no weight. President Melzer's "total bank loan growth has been rapid" is also quantity.
- Governor Yellen's "longer-term yields apparently embody an assumption of declining short-term rates" and President Broaddus's "periodic inflation scares in the bond markets" concern Treasury and inflation expectations, not credit spreads.

Reading: availability and cost of capital are described as favorable, with one minor note of caution in CRE bank financing. About two participants (Phillips mainly, Forrestal in part) support this. The score is slightly below 50 on availability alone, with wide uncertainty.

Score: 43, UNC +-10, CONF Low.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Only one participant mentions equities, and only as a price move.
- Governor Phillips: financial markets "generally have improved--the dollar, the stock market, the bond markets." There is no valuation view (no "rich", "stretched", "undervalued"), and the remark is tied to the lower cost of capital for investment, a spending channel.
- Governor Lindsey's remark that "If we observe bubbles occurring in the economy ... sectoral monitoring" is a general point about the Mack bill's price-stability definition. He draws it from the Massachusetts real estate experience. It is not about current equity valuations.
- President Moskow's wealth-effect question concerns lower taxes and government spending, not equities.

Clarification rule applied: a price move with no valuation view scores 45-55 on the side of the move with CONF Low. Stocks rose or "improved", which puts it just below 50. It is not N/A because equities were named as having moved. Only one participant supports it, and the wording is brief and general.

Score: 47, UNC +-8, CONF Low.
