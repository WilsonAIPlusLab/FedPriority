# Blind check, 2002-03-19: CREDIT_SPREADS and EQUITY_VALUATION

The whole file was read to the last line (vote read-out, all ten voters Yes). The meeting has a policy go-around and a vote, so it is not special-topic. Staff and Desk text is not in the file.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant states a credit spread level or a spread direction. The evidence is availability and risk-premium wording, so the clarification 3 availability rule applies (Low confidence, UNC at least +-10). The score rests on availability and on a loosely related risk-premium remark.

- Vice Chairman McDonough, on the Second District: "Bankers report some let-up in consumer loan demand. They are enforcing tighter credit standards on their commercial borrowers, and delinquency rates are holding about the same." This is a lending-standards statement with no spread wording. It points to somewhat less credit availability for businesses, so it sits slightly above 50.
- Mr. Jordan, policy round: "We cut rates rapidly because the risk premium rose rapidly in terms of investment decisionmaking. And if the risk premium is only gradually revised downward as businesspeople gain confidence..." This is a risk-premium direction remark about investment decisions, not a credit-spread level. It implies a premium that was raised and is only slowly coming down, so a mildly elevated premium. It supports the same side of 50 but is not a market credit-spread statement.
- Mr. Olson mentions that the commercial mortgage-backed market "seems to have identified and isolated the areas of risk", which is neutral to slightly reassuring.
- Mr. Jordan also says the "apparent inflation premium in bond yields" stayed steady after the attacks. That is an inflation premium, not credit, and is not used.

Two participants give indirect support, and the rest of the discussion does not touch credit. Rule applied: availability or risk-premium language without spread wording supports a score with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10, and the evidence rests on availability. Score 55, UNC +-12, Low.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

- Ms. Minehan, on risks to the forecast: "On the downside, I think the very heights of the equity markets and PE ratios suggest some level of fragility and risk in that area." This is a stated valuation concern view: high equity levels and P/E ratios judged a source of fragility. "Some level of fragility" is a moderate concern, not "bubble" language. On the table this is the "somewhat elevated" / modestly above fair value band (31-45), toward the middle of it because the wording is hedged and the concern is placed among several downside risks.
- Other mentions are price moves or spending/wealth channels and are not scored as a valuation view. These are Mr. Gramlich ("The stock market is coming back"), Mr. Olson (business community waiting to see whether "the stock market is going to recognize" the recovery), Ms. Minehan on state revenues and on wealth-to-spending comparisons, Mr. Moskow on "negative wealth effects", and Chairman Greenspan on equity extraction from housing.

Rule applied: a stated valuation or concern view is scored on the table. One participant states it explicitly and the others only report price moves, so confidence is Moderate at most, with a wider band. Score 38, UNC +-8, Moderate.
