# Blind check, 1996-03-26: CREDIT_SPREADS and EQUITY_VALUATION

Document type: ordinary meeting (policy-stance go-around and a vote to hold at 5-1/4 percent with a symmetric directive). Read to the last line, including the vote read-out. The text was readable, with no garbled passages affecting these two dimensions.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses spread or risk-premium wording about credit instruments. Long-term yield talk (Broaddus: "really spectacular rise in both intermediate- and long-term interest rates", "inflation premium"; Melzer: long-bond yield "increased more than 50 basis points") concerns Treasury yields and inflation expectations, not credit spreads, and is not used. Under clarification section 3 (third bullet), availability and lending language can support a score with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10. Evidence rests on availability:

- Hoenig: "loans are readily available at our banks to both commercial and other borrowers."
- Phillips: "We seem to have plenty of credit availability. The banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion."
- Supporting but weaker: Melzer "Loan demand, especially for commercial loans, is still strong"; McDonough "demand for nearly all types of loans has strengthened". These are demand statements, so they are not counted as availability evidence.

Two participants (Hoenig, Phillips) give availability evidence, and both point to ample credit. This is the "ample credit availability" row (16-30) of the table, but the evidence is indirect and thin, so I score on the favorable side, short of the extreme. Score 33, UNC +-12, Confidence Low. The score rests on availability, not on spread wording.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Participants state valuation concerns, so the stated-view rule applies (clarification section 2, first bullet).

- Greenspan: "The market is probably high by any objective measure that we can find. We need a lot of different assumptions to argue that it will stay there." Also: "What I find particularly bothersome is that history suggests more often than not that stock prices remain high for a protracted period of time when they should not." He calls it "the second and more disturbing issue" and warns of a "very significant contraction in stock prices".
- Yellen: "I am particularly concerned at this stage about the possibility of a significant stock market correction. The current level of stock prices is not impossible, but it is increasingly difficult to justify in terms of fundamentals."
- Lindsey: names "the level of prices in capital markets" as an unresolved issue, with the investment boom and "rising prices in the equity markets... feeding on one another" and a "booming capital market" making "equity capital very cheap".
- Phillips mentions "high stock prices" only in passing, as a spending channel. Greenspan's "wealth effect" remarks are spending-channel material and are not scored.

Three participants (Greenspan, Yellen, Lindsey) support an overvaluation concern. Greenspan's "high by any objective measure" and Yellen's "increasingly difficult to justify" amount to "stretched/rich" (16-30 on the table), tempered because the wording stops short of "bubble" or "froth" and Lindsey's remark is only implicit. Score 25, UNC +-7, Confidence High.
