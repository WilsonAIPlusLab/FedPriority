# Blind check, 1995-03-28 (EQUITY_VALUATION, CREDIT_SPREADS)

Read the full file to the last line (policy go-around, symmetry/asymmetry round, vote read-out of 11 Yes). Only participant words were used.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant uses spread, premium or credit-risk wording. The only "premium" is President Jordan's question "Some liquidity premium in there?", which is about the Treasury term structure, not credit spreads. Bond yields are discussed (Broaddus: "bond rates have come down"; Melzer: "decline in long-term bond yields"), but these are Treasury yields and inflation expectations, so they are not evidence on credit spreads.

The only material is availability and cost of credit language, which the clarifications allow with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10:
- Governor Phillips: "The cost of capital in the aggregate is still relatively low" and "Balance sheets are stronger on the corporate side"; "we don't have any very significant balance sheet strains at the household level."
- Governor Kelley: "I don't think we can call the real rates high by any reasonable standard."
- President Hoenig: "recent national bank lending data are consistent with an economy that is still growing at a fairly strong pace."
- Chairman Greenspan: "C&I loans have flattened out in the last two weeks after spiking up for a considerable period." This is about loan demand and is not read as tightening.
- Governor Lindsey (about the 1993 episode): "they could borrow at 3 percent and lend at 6 percent". This is a historical carry-trade remark, not a current spread level.

Rule applied: clarification section 3, bullet 3. The score rests on cost and availability of credit only, with no spread wording. The direction is mildly below 50 (benign, ample credit, strong corporate balance sheets). Support is thin: one participant (Phillips) speaks to cost and balance sheets directly, and two or three others touch lending conditions only indirectly. Score 42, UNC +-12, Low confidence.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Participants state a valuation concern view, so it is scored on the table (clarification section 2, bullet 1):
- Vice Chairman McDonough: "The stock market is very strong indeed"; "the strong stock market and the strong bond market would seem to reflect the notion... that we have in fact achieved the soft landing"; "they have it wrong by being euphoric on the top side."
- Chairman Greenspan: "the downside risks are basically coming from the possibility of significant increases in stock and bond prices... wealth effects that were unsustainable and potentially creating bubbles. Ironically, the real danger is that things may get too good"; "an element of euphoria about a soft landing."
- Governor Lindsey: "if what we fear is a bubble, we should not... go asymmetric"; going asymmetric "would only strengthen the conviction of the market and maybe actually exacerbate the bubble."
- President Stern: "what we are seeing in the financial markets now is a consequence of unsustainable euphoria for one reason or another. But it may be on the other hand that the markets are telling us something significant."
- President Melzer: financial market participants "have all rushed to one side of the boat."
- Governor Kelley: "the stronger stock market and bond market... I would expect that confidence will continue to stay high." This is a wealth and confidence channel only and is not counted.

At least five participants (McDonough, Greenspan, Lindsey, Stern, Melzer) voice a concern that markets, including equities, are euphoric and carry bubble risk. This is a valuation or concern view and is not a bare price move. It reads as elevated or somewhat rich, not an outright stated bubble. McDonough also allows "the financial markets may have it right", and Stern is explicitly undecided. The concern is also about stocks and bonds together. Direction: above fair value (high valuation, low end of the scale). Score 32, UNC +-8, Moderate confidence.
