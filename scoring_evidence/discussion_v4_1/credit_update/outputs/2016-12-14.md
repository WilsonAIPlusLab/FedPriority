# Blind check, 2016-12-14: CREDIT_SPREADS and EQUITY_VALUATION

Whole file read to the last line (vote read-out: ten Yes votes, no dissent). Policy-vote meeting, so not special-topic.

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant states a corporate or credit spread level, and none says "spreads" widened or narrowed. The words that bear on the dimension are about risk premiums, leverage, financial conditions and lending, so the clarification rule for non-spread language applies (rule 3, third bullet), with CONF Low and UNC of at least +-10. The score rests partly on a risk-premium direction and partly on availability and lending language.

- Vice Chairman Dudley, on the post-election tightening of financial conditions: "That was an increase in risk aversion. That was an increase in the risk premiums that were embedded in financial asset prices, and I don't think that's happening this time." This says risk premiums are not rising. It is a direction statement with no level, and it leans slightly below 50.
- Governor Brainard: "all of them highlighted a low level of leverage among investors," and market participants are "quite complacent" about China. Both point to calm risk pricing.
- Mester: "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions, improved household balance sheets" and "the accommodative financial conditions we have had for quite some time". Availability and conditions language, below 50.
- Lacker: "Several bank CEOs noted a flurry of borrowing requests." Bank lending demand is strong. This is not spread wording.
- Counter-evidence: Mester, relaying a director, notes CMBS refinancing problems "in light of the new rules on risk retention and tighter underwriting standards by lenders." It is a sector-specific tightness and is reported second-hand. Evans and Dudley say financial conditions have tightened, but through rates and the dollar, not credit spreads.

Support comes from about four participants (Dudley, Brainard, Mester, Lacker), all indirectly and no one on spreads. Net reading: credit conditions are somewhat easy, with risk premiums not rising. Score 45 (a modest side-of-50 call), CONF Low, UNC +-10, resting on risk-premium direction and availability language and not on a stated spread level.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Participants do state valuation views on equities, so the score goes on the table.

- Lacker states the clearest concern. Directors use "exuberance" and "euphoria", and "one director called the euphoria 'irrational.'" He adds, "It seems plausible that outcomes will not be quite as beneficial to economic growth and earnings as the move in equity values would imply. So I think it's worth taking the recent outbreak of exuberance with a few grains of salt." This is a modest above-fair-value view, which is the "somewhat elevated" band of the table (31-45).
- Mester: expectations "seem to be skewed toward putting too much weight on very positive outcomes and too little weight on poor outcomes"; she mentions "animal spirits". Kashkari: "markets could give back these gains as quickly as they acquired them." Both support caution about the post-election rally, without using valuation words.
- Counter-evidence: Bullard reads the rally as "revaluation of the U.S. corporate sector" from lower expected corporate tax rates ("I don't see the equity rally, so far, as reflecting changing fundamentals"). This explains the move as a repricing for taxes and is neutral to mildly concerned. Rosengren notes "the stock market has reached new highs" as a bare move.
- Dudley says the tightening in conditions reflects "greater optimism" and he is "not really concerned". This is not about valuation.

Support: Lacker (explicit), with Mester and Kashkari (softer), and Bullard as a partial offset. Score 42 (modestly above fair value), CONF Moderate, UNC +-8.
