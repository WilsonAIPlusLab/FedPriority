## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- INFLATION_PRESSURE 38: "price inflation generally had remained subdued, with little evidence of acceleration"; "members saw little prospect that inflation would gather significant momentum"; "underlying inflation was expected to stay quite subdued"; offset by "several" noting the risk of rising inflation "was still present" and "a few" worried about M2. Below target-pressure side, with tight labor as a counterweight.
- OUTPUT_GAP 55: "relatively robust economic growth", "very tight labor markets", "remarkably rapid pace", offset by "ample industrial capacity". No explicit above/below potential wording; modestly above (labor tightness and strong demand).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 70: "persistence of very tight labor markets across the nation"; "escalating increases in labor compensation in a number of areas"; "shortages of skilled construction workers in many areas". Directive reports UE 4.5 percent. Very tight.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62: consumer spending "on a solid uptrend ... remarkably rapid pace"; business investment "extraordinary pace" now moderating; "very strong construction activity in many parts of the country"; expected "considerable moderation". Above trend but expected to slow.
- PRODUCTIVITY 60: "an apparently more rapid trend of productivity gains" listed as a favorable factor. Brief, one mention.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 65: "decidedly less hospitable financial conditions"; "tightening credit availability"; "credit conditions were likely to remain tighter"; "members did not believe" a credit crunch likely, though "some members" could not rule it out. Somewhat tight to tight.
- TREASURY_STRESS 48: "Strong demands for safety and liquidity had driven down yields on U.S. Treasury securities". Yield move from flight to quality, no dysfunction described. Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 58: "spreads of private rates over Treasury rates had gapped higher"; "especially large for lower-grade borrowers"; "many banks had tightened their credit standards and terms"; "many members" said risk spreads "previously had embodied an overly optimistic assessment" (so prior level was too low). No stated level; mostly direction plus availability, so Low confidence, UNC +-10.
- EQUITY_VALUATION 52: "declined substantially further"; "many members" saw "some correction in these markets as a positive development" after "overly optimistic assessment"; also decline "likely to damp the growth of consumer spending". Valuation view is correction from rich toward more normal, balanced against the sharp fall; mid-range.
- BANKING_STRESS 54: only "many banks had tightened their credit standards and terms"; no discussion of bank capital or health. Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A: only "the dollar's earlier appreciation" mentioned as a lingering price-restraining factor; no current dollar movement discussed by participants.
- INFLATION_GOODS 30: "Declining commodity and other import prices would be restraining prices"; "generally declining commodity and other import prices".
- INFLATION_SERVICES N/A: not discussed separately.
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A: housing discussed as activity only, not prices or rents.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 40: "inflation expectations remained damped"; "restraining prices and inflation expectations for a while".
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 31: "all the members endorsed" a 25bp cut; "A small majority favored" an easing-tilted directive; "many members saw the need for a cautious policy action"; "several" stressed inflation risk "still present". Moderate dovish: easing action by all, cautious size, easing bias.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY 18: "all the members supported" the cut; "Votes against this action: None."; "All the members also indicated that they could accept" the easing bias. Minor differences only on symmetric vs asymmetric directive and caution on size.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 72: extensive discussion of global turmoil, risk spreads, credit tightness, equity decline, "credit crunch" possibility, and the policy rationale ("insurance against ... worsening in financial conditions"); the appended item on Long-Term Capital Management adds to it. Prominent topic.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 28: directive with bias toward ease ("a somewhat lower federal funds rate would be acceptable"); "readiness to respond promptly" to threats; some said unanticipated developments would drive actions. Easing lean.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 68: "abnormally high volatility and very substantial uncertainty, it was impossible to predict"; "How much weaker was a major uncertainty in the economic outlook"; net exports "subject to a great deal of uncertainty"; "sense of unease and uncertainty". High.
