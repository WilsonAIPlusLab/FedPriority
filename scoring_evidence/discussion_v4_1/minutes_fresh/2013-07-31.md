## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +-6 High. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective"; "a number of others ... viewed the low inflation readings as largely reflecting persistently deficient aggregate demand ... could remain below 2 percent for a protracted period"; "A few" expect a quick return to 2 percent (minority). Below target, mixed on persistence.

OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6 Moderate. "growth ... somewhat below their earlier expectations" (a number); "persistently deficient aggregate demand" (a number); labor market "remained weak". Below potential, slack remains.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +-6 High. "unemployment rate remained elevated"; "low readings on the participation rate and the employment-to-population ratio ... high incidence of ... part time for economic reasons ... generally seen as indicating that overall labor market conditions remained weak"; payrolls "solid". Level is slack.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 +-6 Moderate. "Household spending and business fixed investment continued to advance"; consumer spending "other than motor vehicles was relatively soft"; business outlook "mixed"; "a few" doubt the wealth effect; fiscal restraint held back spending; housing strengthening. Net slightly below trend.

PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-10 Low. "weak gains in productivity"; one participant thought "sluggish productivity performance was likely to persist". Below trend, briefly discussed.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 50 +-8 Moderate. "some participants felt ... overall financial market conditions had tightened significantly, importantly reflecting larger term premiums" versus "Several others ... judged that the rise in rates was likely to exert relatively little restraint, or that the increase in equity prices and easing in bank lending standards would largely offset"; "improving credit availability". Offsetting views, neutral.

TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +-10 Low. Only rate/yield moves: "increases in U.S. longer-term interest rates", "volatile at times", driven by policy expectations; no dysfunction described. Normal functioning.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +-10 Low. No spread, yield or premium wording on credit; rests on availability: "banks' increasing willingness to make mortgage loans", "easing in bank lending standards", "improving credit availability". Somewhat favorable, so below 50.

EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A. Equity prices appear only as a wealth/spending channel and as an offset to higher rates ("increased equity ... prices", "increase in equity prices ... largely offset"); no valuation view.

BANKING_STRESS: N/A. Banks mentioned only as lenders (mortgage willingness, lending standards); no discussion of bank health or stress.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. Not discussed by participants.

INFLATION_GOODS: N/A. No goods-price discussion.
INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. No services-price discussion.
INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. House prices discussed only as housing-sector activity, not shelter inflation.
INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47. Vote record: "longer-run inflation expectations were stable" and "well anchored"; inflation expected back toward 2 percent over the medium term. Roughly anchored at or just under target.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 +-7 Moderate. "almost all Committee members agreed that a change in the purchase program was not yet appropriate"; "highly accommodative stance ... was warranted"; asset purchases continued at $85 billion per month; "A few members emphasized ... being patient" versus "a few others ... it might soon be time to slow somewhat the pace of purchases"; one dissent for a more explicit taper signal; "several participants were willing to contemplate lowering the unemployment threshold". Majority maintains maximum accommodation with a small taper-leaning minority.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-6 High. One formal dissent (Ms. George, wanted a more explicit signal of reduced purchases); "a range of views were expressed regarding the cumulative improvement in the labor market"; a few versus a few on timing of tapering; 11 votes in favor. One dissent plus notable but moderate differences on pace.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 Moderate. A full paragraph on market volatility, term premiums, financial conditions tightening, and "unwinding of unsustainable speculative positions"; "heightened financial market uncertainty about the path of monetary policy". Several participants address it, framed mostly as financial conditions and communication.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 20 +-8 Moderate. "reaffirmed its view that a highly accommodative stance ... will remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends"; thresholds kept ("at least as long as the unemployment rate remains above 6½ percent"); several willing to lower the threshold; ideas for added dovish guidance. Offsetting contingent taper plan ("reduce the pace of purchases in measured steps and conclude ... around the middle of 2014") keeps it from the strongest easing commitment.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 Moderate. "A number of participants ... somewhat less confident about a near-term pickup"; "uncertainty about the effects of the federal spending sequestration"; "heightened financial market uncertainty about the path of monetary policy". Somewhat elevated, mostly specific named factors.
