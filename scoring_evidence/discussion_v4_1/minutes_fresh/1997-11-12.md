## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: "persistence of subdued increases in prices"; "inflation had remained relatively subdued"; "risks remained in the direction of rising price inflation"; "all agreed that the risks remained tilted toward rising inflation". Current level subdued, risk tilted up, so below 50 but not deflationary. Score 40.
- OUTPUT_GAP: "already tight resources"; "unsustainable pace of domestic demand and rising resource utilization" (other members); "growing considerably more rapidly than the sustainable rate" (Broaddus, one member). Above potential. Score 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "low and falling rates of unemployment"; "tight labor markets throughout the nation"; "demand for many types of workers exceeded the supply in many regions"; "a number of members reported... held back by the scarcity of labor"; bonuses to attract workers. Unemployment 4.7 percent is in the directive. Score 67.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: "strength in all major sectors"; "robust economic expansion"; "ongoing surge in [capital] spending"; "private domestic demand... considerable strength". Score 68.
- PRODUCTIVITY: "apparently sharp increase in productivity in the second and third quarters... encouraging", but "too early to judge the persistence". Score 66.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "broadly supportive financial conditions"; "overall financial conditions remained quite supportive despite the recent market turmoil and high real short-term interest rates"; "ready availability of finance on attractive terms". Supportive (bright line below 50). Score 33.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Not discussed by participants.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread wording. Availability only: "Credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available on favorable terms, perhaps overly so" (some members). Score rests on availability, Low confidence, UNC at least 10. Score 28.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A. Equities appear only as a wealth and spending channel ("very large gains in stock market wealth", "a substantial decline in equity values would reduce household wealth"). No valuation view stated.
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A. Not discussed.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "additional appreciation of the dollar... would have adverse implications for net exports" (some members); "lagged response to earlier appreciation of the dollar". Appreciation theme, concern about further strength, no current-level statement. Score 58, Low.
- INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A. Not discussed.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: only Broaddus's "recent apparent reduction in inflationary expectations". Slightly below anchored. Score 45.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: "all but one member endorsed... an unchanged policy stance"; "Other members stressed that the unsustainable pace of domestic demand... seemed to call for a near-term tightening" but also "a tightening of U.S. monetary policy risked an oversized reaction"; "next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming". One member wanted a modest tightening. Hold with a tightening bias. Score 56.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Broaddus, in favor of tightening); two camps on emphasis (some on favorable productivity and cost trends and Asian risk, others on unsustainable demand), but all but one agreed on holding. Score 32.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Asian turmoil and "global financial markets had not yet settled down"; "markets still skittish"; "uncertainty about asset values"; financial conditions "overly so" supportive. Moderate attention, mostly as a risk channel. Score 42.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: "retain... the existing asymmetry toward restraint"; "next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing". Mild tightening bias. Score 66.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: "two important influences that were injecting new uncertainties"; "the ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asia was unknown"; "too early to judge" productivity; "to an uncertain extent"; "in light of the uncertainties about the economic outlook"; "subject to considerable debate". Elevated. Score 55.
