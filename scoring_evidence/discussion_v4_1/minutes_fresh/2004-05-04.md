## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- INFLATION_PRESSURE 46: "recent data pointing to jumps in consumer and producer prices"; "Many members indicated that the surprisingly large advances had substantially reduced the odds of further disinflation"; "most members saw low inflation as the most likely outcome"; "With inflation low and resource use slack"; "the risks to the goal of price stability had moved into balance"; "some members saw underlying inflation as relatively stable". Upward pressure is noted, but the level is still described as low. Slightly below target.
- OUTPUT_GAP 40: "considerable slack seemed to remain in the labor market overall"; "make appreciable headway in narrowing margins of unutilized resources"; "resource use slack"; "probable persistence of slack ... for at least several more quarters". Below potential and narrowing.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 38: "After a protracted period of meager gains in employment"; "hiring was continuing to pick up"; "a number of members cited reports of difficulties in hiring within certain job families"; "considerable slack seemed to remain in the labor market overall, and wage gains on the whole were moderate". Improving but still slack.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62: "consumer spending was rising at a brisk rate"; "business fixed investment was growing vigorously"; "accelerating considerably, especially for equipment and software". Offsets: "nonresidential structures remained sluggish"; "some of the considerable caution ... apparently lingered". Above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY 65: "strong productivity gains and corresponding downward pressures on unit labor costs"; "recent substantial gains in productivity would be extended". Above trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 45: "accommodative financial conditions" supporting households; but "the appreciable rise in real long-term interest rates ... implied that financial market conditions had already tightened on balance". Mixed; bright-line (accommodative) keeps it below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS N/A: only a rise in long-term yields mentioned, no market functioning discussion.
- CREDIT_SPREADS N/A: no spread, premium, lending or credit-availability wording by participants.
- EQUITY_VALUATION N/A: equities not discussed.
- BANKING_STRESS N/A: banking system not discussed ("generally sound balance sheets" refers to households).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A: dollar/FX not discussed.
- INFLATION_GOODS 62: "surcharges for higher energy and steel prices were being added to base prices for certain goods"; "earlier price discounts had been canceled"; "jumps in the prices of energy and non-oil imports". Value only.
- INFLATION_SERVICES N/A: not discussed separately.
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A: house price increases mentioned only as housing activity, not shelter inflation.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47: "longer-term inflation expectations that apparently remained contained" (said by "some members" as a restraining factor). Value only.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 56: "all of the members favored maintenance of the existing target of 1 percent"; "the time at which policy firming appropriately would commence might be closer than previously had seemed most probable"; "the reference to patience was no longer warranted"; against: "some members referred to the possibility that growth could falter". Hold with a tightening bias.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY 14: "Vote against this action: None."; "All of the members agreed" on dropping "patient" and accepting "measured"; "A number of policymakers were concerned" that "measured" could constrain future adjustments, while "others ... saw substantial benefits". Unanimous with a modest wording debate.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 10: only passing references to accommodative conditions, mortgage rates and the tightening from higher long-term real rates; "an apparent breakdown in fiscal discipline" is fiscal, not financial stability.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66: "with policy tightening likely to begin sooner than previously expected, the reference to patience was no longer warranted"; "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured". Start of normalization language, with the pace stated as gradual.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 40: "increased their uncertainty about prospective price trends"; "the extent to which these developments signaled an upturn in underlying inflation was unclear"; "inflation predictions based on estimated output or employment gaps were subject to considerable error"; risks "roughly equal". Somewhat elevated, mainly on inflation.
