## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document type: standard minutes discussion with a policy vote (25bp hike, unanimous). Not special-topic.

- INFLATION_PRESSURE 56: "concerns about near-term inflation pressures had eased somewhat"; indirect effects "muted"; but "many participants remained concerned that elevated energy prices could put pressure on core inflation"; "some firms were successfully passing at least a portion of their increased costs"; "resource markets could tighten further and inflation pressures build". Modestly above target, easing, with risk skewed up.
- OUTPUT_GAP 51: "in the view of a number of participants, the economy was possibly producing in the neighborhood of its potential"; fed funds "likely within a broad range ... consistent with output remaining close to potential" (some members); "possible increases in resource utilization". Near potential, bright line 48-52.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 52: "further advances in employment and income"; "measures of labor compensation showed only moderate gains"; "resource markets could tighten further". Little direct labor discussion; near balanced.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60: "consumer spending outside the auto sector appeared to have remained vigorous"; "Business investment spending had accelerated some since midyear"; "spending apparently retaining considerable momentum"; offset: housing "tentative signs that activity was beginning to slow", light vehicle sales slowed. Net above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY 65: "strong growth in productivity were holding down business costs"; "further substantial gains in productivity were helping to contain cost and price pressures".
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36: "Supportive financial conditions" (abroad); "the sustained period of favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand" (Committee). Supportive/favorable means below 50 (bright line). Phrasing is general, so UNC wider.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, Treasury market not discussed.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A, no spread, yield, premium or credit availability wording by participants.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A, equities not mentioned (asset prices only in a general policy context).
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A, banking system not discussed.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57: "the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar owing to global demands for dollar assets"; appreciation so above 50, mentioned in passing, no concern attached.
- INFLATION_GOODS: N/A (energy price pass-through to core noted but no goods-specific category view).
- INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A, not discussed.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A, housing discussed as activity/prices of homes, not shelter inflation.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 54: "inflation expectations, which had jumped after the hurricanes, had fallen back"; "inflation and long-term inflation expectations remained contained"; "inflation expectations could rise if monetary policy were not seen as responding". Roughly anchored with an upside risk.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 64: "all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 4-1/4 percent"; "Committee members generally anticipated that policy would likely need to be firmed further"; but "most members" saw the "number of additional firming steps ... probably would not be large", "some members" thought "measured" no longer necessary, policy no longer characterised as accommodative.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY 14: "Votes against this action: None"; "all members" favored the hike; only mild difference: "Views differed on how much further tightening might be required"; some wanted to drop "measured".
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 18: passing references only: "favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand", housing market "significant market effects" from damping investor/speculator demand.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66: "some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed"; "number of additional firming steps required probably would not be large"; "policy will respond to changes in economic prospects as needed". Mild-to-moderate tightening bias, near the end of cycle.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 38: "members thought that the policy outlook was becoming considerably less certain"; "effects were especially difficult to judge"; otherwise standard caveats and "risks ... roughly in balance". Mostly policy-path uncertainty.
