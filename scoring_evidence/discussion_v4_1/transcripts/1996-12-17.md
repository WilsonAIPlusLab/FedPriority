# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1996-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript: opening remarks and minutes approval, members' questions during the Desk/staff Q&A, a full economic go-around (about 17 participants), the policy round (Chairman's recommendation, then each member), and a unanimous roll call. Staff turns are absent, so some Chair/member turns appear as responses to missing questions. Two or three passages have words blanked (steel-company names), which does not affect scoring.

**Era context.** December 1996, a long expansion with low unemployment and moderate inflation. I scored only what participants said. The bulk of the discussion is regional reports, a view that the Greenbook forecast is acceptable, tight labor markets, the stock market ("irrational exuberance"), and the meaning of an asymmetric directive.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hoenig, Moskow, Stern, Broaddus, Melzer, Boehne: tight labor markets, and upside inflation risk that varies in strength. Melzer and Broaddus want inflation caps or targets.
- Parry: slight excess demand in 1997, and some tightening may be needed next year, but nothing now.
- Minehan: wage pressure anecdotes and leverage/liquidity worries in mutual funds.
- Guynn, Jordan, McTeer, Rivlin, Phillips, Meyer, Yellen, Kelley: growth near trend, a "nearly ideal" economy, inflation benign but at risk, and vigilance.
- Lindsey, Yellen, Rivlin, Phillips: stock-market overvaluation risk.
- Greenspan: a long analysis of wage and profit margins, and a view that "the next move will be up rather than down," so asymmetric "B" (unchanged rate, tightening bias).

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above the comfort zone, with the upside tilt rising. Core CPI is near 2.75 to 3 percent; Melzer notes a half-point rise in 1996. Several call inflation "benign" or "tamed," and Broaddus says the true rate is nearer 2 percent after CPI bias. Wages are accelerating "very modestly." Above midpoint, modest: 55.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is at or slightly above potential. Rivlin says growth is "very close to potential." Parry sees "a slight degree of excess demand." Moskow sees output "at or slightly above potential." Meyer says growth is at trend, starting from full employment. Slightly above, within the near-potential band: 52.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor markets are described as tight almost universally ("exceptionally tight," "undeniably tight," "labor shortages"). The unemployment rate is about 5.2 to 5.4 percent, with the staff and Meyer suggesting it is below NAIRU. The rubric UE band alone would give a low 50s score, but the language is strong. Above midpoint: 60.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Holiday sales are robust, business fixed investment has outpaced projections, and commercial construction is strengthening. Offsets are consumer debt constraints, housing cooling, and a softer GM/auto picture. Net slightly above trend: 55.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned only in passing and in conflicting ways (Melzer "weak productivity growth"; Jordan "productivity psychology"; Phillips "maybe enough productivity improvement"; Jordan says the measurement is flawed). No clear directional view: 48 with low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants cite buoyant markets, relatively low external financing costs, lower mortgage rates, and stock gains. Lindsey expects continued "excessive credit expansion." Accommodative, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied): 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed by members, apart from dollar-asset sales and Desk operations. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are not discussed. Credit availability is (Lindsey on excessive credit expansion; Jordan on banks tightening for retail construction; Greenspan on a consumer-credit ceiling). I score on the loose-credit side of 50 with low confidence and a wide band (level rule: no stated high level, so not above 50): 38.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: A major theme. "Irrational exuberance," the stock market as "a significant continuing risk," valuations "aggressive," a "cold shower" from the Chairman. Valuation concern is high, so a low score: 18.
- BANKING_STRESS: Little discussion. Melzer reports banks with "high profits" and only a slight rise in non-performing loans; Jordan reports bankers tightening on retail space lending. Sound with minor concerns, below 50: 32, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Greenspan says the dollar is "holding up remarkably well"; Yellen cites "the stronger dollar" as a drag on net exports; Lindsey expects more appreciation; McTeer calls the dollar price reassuring. Appreciation, so above 50: 62.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: energy and food are rising (Phillips), steel and materials prices flat to slightly higher, imports cheap (Lindsey). Roughly stable: 50.
- Services: N/A. Housing: only a regional Boston shelter-cost remark, so N/A.
- Expectations: Broaddus uses bond rates and surveys to say expectations are still around 3.5 percent and "some distance" from price stability. Melzer fears credibility loss. Above anchored: 62.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: unchanged funds rate at 5.25 percent with an asymmetric tightening directive. Greenspan: "the next move will be up rather than down." Melzer favors a modest increase. Broaddus would have been "less than heated" if a snug had been proposed. Parry says tightening may be needed later. Some (Broaddus, Meyer, Kelley) note risks have become more balanced. Lean hawkish hold: 59.
- Dissent: unanimous roll call (12 yes, no dissent). Differences are minor: Melzer's preference for tightening, Broaddus's mild preference, the meaning of asymmetry (Jordan, Rivlin, Minehan), and whether to set multi-year inflation paths (Melzer, Broaddus, Jordan favor; Meyer against a path). Near-consensus with reservations: 17.
- Financial stability emphasis: Several participants address the stock market and credit at length (Minehan on mutual-fund leverage and margin requirements, Lindsey on three "bears," Yellen, Rivlin, Phillips), but it is not the dominant topic. Moderate: 42.
- Forward guidance: Asymmetric language toward tightening, and the Vice Chair says it signals a strong consensus on upside risks without implying a near-term move. Mild-to-moderate tightening bias: 65.
- Uncertainty: Parry notes "considerable uncertainty" about activity; Jordan "puzzled" and doubts the output and productivity measurement; Meyer notes forecast ranges are very narrow and NAIRU is uncertain; Stern finds risks hard to assess. Overall, normal to somewhat elevated: 36.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 52: slightly above potential language is satisfied (> 50; Parry's "slight excess demand").
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38: accommodative/supportive, below 50, satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 38: no stated high level, below 50, satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 32: sound, below 50, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62: appreciation, above 50, satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 52 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 60: a gap of 8, within 15. Participants see tight labor markets without excess demand in product markets (Meyer).
2. OUTPUT_GAP 52 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 55: a gap of 3, fine.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, which is fine in normal times.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 55 vs sub-components (goods 50, expectations 62): consistent.

**N/A justification.** Treasury market functioning, services and housing inflation were not raised by participants at the national level.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Melzer, "CPI inflation is up 1/2 percentage point this year over last year, and I think the risks are that it could rise further." Phillips, "'benign' and 'tamed' are the more recent adjectives of choice."
- Summary: modestly above comfort, with an upward tilt but benign core measures.
- Direction: above neutral because inflation is near 3 percent with rising risks.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Rivlin, "a nearly ideal economy that is growing at a rate very close to potential." Parry, "a slight degree of excess demand."
- Summary: at or marginally above potential.
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "labor markets remain exceptionally tight." Yellen, "labor markets are undeniably tight."
- Summary: Tight labor markets are reported by nearly everyone, with unemployment around 5.2 to 5.4 percent.
- Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Phillips, "business fixed investment has consistently outpaced the Greenbook projections." Hoenig, "Retail sales have been robust."
- Summary: solid, slightly above trend, with housing and consumer-debt offsets.
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 48 +-10 (Low).**
- Evidence: Melzer, "weak productivity growth." Jordan (director report), "productivity psychology."
- Summary: conflicting passing remarks.
- Direction: roughly at neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Phillips, "the external financing cost of capital has been relatively low." Lindsey, "another year to go on the excessive credit expansion."
- Summary: supportive conditions with easy credit and rising equities.
- Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-12 (Low).**
- Evidence: Lindsey, "excessive credit expansion." Jordan, bankers "tightening up on credit" for retail space construction.
- Summary: Spreads are not discussed; credit is described as ample overall.
- Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 18 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "irrational exuberance in asset markets"; stock prices "aggressive." Lindsey, "a very good year for irrational exuberance."
- Summary: several members see equities as overvalued.
- Direction: valuation concern is high (score low).

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Melzer, "District banks continue to record high profits with only a slight increase in nonperforming loans."
- Summary: banks look sound, with scattered tightening in retail real estate lending.
- Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "the dollar is holding up remarkably well." Yellen, "the stronger dollar."
- Summary: the dollar is firm or appreciating.
- Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 50. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62.**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "long-term inflation expectations are still on the order of 3-1/2 percent today." Phillips, "energy and food are still experiencing price increases."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 59 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "a far greater likelihood that the next move will be up rather than down." Melzer, "I would favor a modest increase in the degree of reserve restraint."
- Summary: unanimous hold with a tightening bias.
- Direction: lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: unanimous roll call. Broaddus, "if you had recommended a slight snugging, my opposition would have been less than heated."
- Summary: minor reservations and different views on strategy, no dissents.
- Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Minehan, concern over "the higher and higher degree of leverage in financial markets." Lindsey, three "bears" for exuberance.
- Summary: several participants discuss asset prices and credit meaningfully, though not as the main topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: McDonough, "it is very likely that our next move will be up and that asymmetry should be used to indicate... the risks to the forecast are on the up side." Greenspan, "I accept your definition of asymmetry."
- Summary: tightening bias with no near-term action implied.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 36 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Parry, "considerable uncertainty at this time about the prospective strength of economic activity." Jordan, "I find the uncertainties... to be something more than I can deal with."
- Summary: normal caveats with some emphasis on measurement and NAIRU uncertainty.
