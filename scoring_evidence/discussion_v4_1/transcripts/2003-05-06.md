# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2003-05-06

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: a short opening Q&A (members' questions to staff), the economic round-table, the Chairman's closing assessment, and the policy go-around on a draft statement. Staff turns are removed and ignored. One vote read-out at the end: 12 "Yes". (An early "No" by Broaddus on the swap-renewal motion is procedural and is not a policy dissent.)

**Era context.** Spring 2003, just after the Iraq war ended. Slow recovery, weak labor market, very low and falling core inflation, deflation worry, funds rate already low. This context is not used to override the text. The scores below rest on what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Nearly everyone reports sluggish activity, weak hiring, reluctant capital spending and a modest post-war improvement in tone and in financial markets.
- Many (Parry, Minehan, Ferguson, Bernanke, Gramlich, Kohn, Poole, Bies, Olson) see a wide output gap and falling inflation. Several say they could have voted to ease today.
- Hoenig, McTeer, Santomero, Guynn, Broaddus, Stern and McDonough prefer to wait until June. Guynn and Broaddus see deflation as not yet the foremost risk, and call it a close call.
- The Chairman says the Committee would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless activity picks up substantially, but not today, because markets are fragile and not expecting a move.
- The draft statement splits the risks: growth risk and a downside inflation risk, combined as "weighted toward weakness".

**Dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as well below target and falling. Core PCE is about 1 percent, "disinflation," 35 percent staff-estimated deflation probability, "inflation has fallen to dangerously low levels" (Gramlich), "risks decidedly downward" (Bernanke). Meaningful deflation risk, which maps to 11-25. Score 20.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. "output gap is wider than expected" (Kohn); "output gap even into 2005" (Minehan); "growth ... below the potential rate" (Parry). Large and persistent slack, so well below 50. Score 28.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Slack. "labor markets remain quite slack" (Minehan); "hours worked still contracting"; unemployment rising and 5.6 percent forecast for end-2004 (Gramlich). Score 32.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Consumers have held up, but business investment is weak and "no postwar bounce" (Poole, CVS/Wal-Mart, Pianalto). Net below trend. Score 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Strong. "productivity remains strong" (Minehan); the Chairman says gains were "greater than the published numbers"; Stern's contacts say it "has some way to go". Above trend. Score 63.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as improved and supportive: "more accommodative financial conditions" (Kohn), the cost of capital has decreased, and the Chairman cites a "very significant drop" in rates on BBB and higher-yield debt. They also call the improvement fragile and not matched by real activity. Below 50 (bright line: accommodative). Score 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads narrowed a lot (Moskow, Kohn "more than retraced their run-up of last summer", Chairman), but a stated level is given by McDonough ("still broad by historical standards") and Ferguson ("still relatively wide by historical standards"). The level reading puts the score above 50. Minehan also doubts that the narrowing is fully benign. Modest, above 50, with mixed evidence. Score 55.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity rally discussed as price and sentiment, with fragility, but no valuation (rich or cheap) commentary. Default band. Score 50, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Guynn: "bank balance sheet and capital positions are favorable", "not a concern to banking supervision"; Olson: asset quality "remained stable"; Stern: credit quality "doesn't seem to be a concern". Healthy, so below 50. Score 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar weakening: McDonough "dollar weakness has continued"; Kohn "a lower dollar"; Guynn "fall in the value of the dollar". Below 50 (bright line). Score 30.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods: relative price declines in manufactured goods, commodities down (Guynn, Gramlich), Pianalto's firms are still cutting prices. Score 28. Services: no clean services-specific comment, N/A. Housing: owners' equivalent rent and rental housing values are falling (Gramlich, Chairman), though measurement is questioned. Score 32. Expectations: "for the most part inflation expectations have been stable" (Kohn), no measure signals deflation (Guynn), with a risk of drifting lower. Score 42.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: Policy was held, but the forward lean is clearly toward ease. A sizeable group said they could have voted to ease now (Parry strongly, Minehan, Bies, Olson, Hoenig, McTeer, Kohn). The Chairman himself says to move lower unless the economy picks up. Poole says an easing is likely. Nobody argued for tightening. A hold with a strong easing bias leans moderate dovish. Score 32.
- Dissent: The vote is unanimous with no formal dissents. There are reservations about the "taken together" sentence (Santomero, McTeer, Pianalto, Guynn, Broaddus) and Moskow's alternative (balanced risks). Parry and Minehan prefer easing today. Differences are about timing and wording, not about the economics. Score 30.
- Financial stability emphasis: Financial markets, credit spreads and their fragility are central to the timing argument (Chairman, McDonough, Stern, McTeer). Banking and credit quality get brief mention. Moderate. Score 42.
- Forward guidance: The statement signals downside risk, and the Chairman says the burden of proof falls on those who would not move in June. An explicit easing bias with data dependence. Score 22.
- Uncertainty: "too soon to tell" (Guynn, Hoenig, Santomero, Kohn "the extent and timing of the pickup are uncertain", Chairman "very chancy", "unsettled period"). Elevated but not extreme. Score 55.

**Polarity checks.** Higher inflation pressure means more inflation, and the evidence says low, so the score is low. Higher credit spreads means more stress, and the evidence is "still wide by historical standards", so a score slightly above 50 is consistent. Higher financial-conditions score means tighter, and the evidence says easier, so a score below 50 is consistent. Higher dollar score means stronger, and the evidence says weaker, so a score below 50 is consistent. Higher lean score means hawkish, and the evidence says easing bias, so the score is low.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (supportive/accommodative) satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS above 50 on the stated level satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 28 and LABOR 32 agree. OUTPUT_GAP 28 and CONSUMPTION 38 differ by 10, within tolerance. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 55 sit on opposite sides of 50 by a modest margin. This reflects the text: conditions have eased and spreads have narrowed, but members call the level still wide by historical standards. Treasury market N/A. Inflation 20 is consistent with sub-components at 28-42 (expectations are anchored, while goods and rents are soft).

**N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market not discussed by members. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-specific price comment.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 20 ±6 (High).** Gramlich: "inflation has fallen to dangerously low levels"; Bernanke: "the risks to our inflation objective are decidedly downward." Low and falling inflation with real deflation risk. Below neutral because inflation is well below target.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 28 ±6 (High).** Kohn: "The output gap is wider than expected"; Minehan: "an output gap even into 2005." Substantial slack. Below neutral.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 32 ±6 (High).** Minehan: "labor markets remain quite slack"; Chairman: employment data "awful". Slack labor market. Below neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38 ±7 (High).** Poole: "no sign at all of a postwar bounce"; Pianalto: "aversion to business investment". Consumers hold up, investment weak. Below trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 63 ±8 (Moderate).** Minehan: "productivity remains strong"; Chairman: structural productivity gains "greater than the published numbers". Above trend.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Kohn: "more accommodative financial conditions"; Chairman: "financial data have improved quite dramatically." Easier, though called fragile. Below neutral.
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 55 ±8 (Moderate).** McDonough: "spreads have narrowed, but they're still broad by historical standards"; Ferguson: "still relatively wide by historical standards". Level reading puts it above 50. Kohn offsets: spreads "more than retraced their run-up". Slightly above neutral.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 50 ±8 (Low).** McDonough: "the makings of a breakout, but ... unstable". Price and sentiment talk, no valuation call. Default neutral.
- **BANKING_STRESS 30 ±7 (Moderate).** Guynn: "bank balance sheets and capital positions are favorable"; Olson: asset quality "has remained stable". Healthy. Below neutral.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 ±7 (Moderate).** McDonough: "The dollar weakness has continued"; Kohn: "a lower dollar". Depreciation. Below neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 28, INFLATION_HOUSING 32, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 42; INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Guynn: prices falling for manufactured goods "as relative price declines"; Gramlich: declines in rental housing value; Kohn: expectations "stable" but could fall.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 32 ±6 (High).** Chairman: "we probably would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless we see a fairly substantial pickup"; Parry: "a good case could be made for a further easing of policy today." Hold today, clear easing bias. Dovish side.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 30 ±7 (High).** Unanimous vote. Moskow: "I don't feel strongly enough to dissent"; reservations by several members over the "taken together" sentence. Broad agreement with disagreement over timing and wording.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 42 ±8 (Moderate).** Chairman: "the recent strengthening of the stock market ... is a potentially fragile situation"; McDonough: "very, very dangerous to surprise the market". Several members address market fragility in the timing argument.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 22 ±7 (High).** Chairman: absent signs of improvement by June, "the burden of proof will be on those who think we should not move"; Poole: the language signals "we are very likely to be easing policy". Easing bias with data dependence.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 55 ±8 (Moderate).** Guynn: "it's just too soon to tell"; Chairman: "a very chancy situation." Elevated, post-war data scarce, but not extreme.
