# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - MEETING 2016-09-21 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: economic go-round (day 1), policy go-round (day 2), plus members' questions in the staff Q&A. Staff turns are removed, so some answers are missing. The vote read-out is also missing (a bare "Brian." line). The dissent count comes from Vice Chairman Dudley's own remark that "we're going to have three dissents on the side of tightening." There is no garbled passage that affects scoring. A few lines are pure procedure (officer selection, foreign currency documents), which I ignore.

**Era context.** This is a mid-expansion meeting about a year after liftoff (December 2015), with a funds rate still near the bottom. The text itself describes the following:
- The unemployment rate is 4.9 percent.
- Core PCE inflation is 1.6 percent.
- GDP growth was weak in the first half and is expected to pick up.
- The debate is whether to raise rates now or in December.

I score from what participants say, not from what I know of the meeting.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hold now, wait for evidence (Chair Yellen, Dudley, Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, Evans):
  - Some labor slack remains.
  - Inflation is below target and rising slowly.
  - Downside risks are asymmetric because of the effective lower bound.
  - Kashkari prefers alternative A (a more dovish statement).
- Raise now (Williams, Rosengren, Lacker, George, Mester): these five all back alternative C.
- Raise now if the choice were theirs (Kaplan): he would have raised today. He supported B only because the Committee was going that way.
- Supports B but sees the case for acting now as nearly as strong as the case for waiting (Fischer).
- Supports B and wants a clear December signal (Harker, Lockhart).
- Supports B for a different reason (Bullard): the policy path should be very flat.

Dudley says explicitly that the vote will see three dissents on the side of tightening.

**Dimension-by-dimension direction (before numbers).**

- *Inflation pressure.* Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target and rising only slowly.
  - Core PCE is 1.6 percent.
  - Yellen: "remarkably few signs of rising cost pressures."
  - Powell: progress "at a snail's pace."
  - Evans: "firmly entrenched low-inflation psychology."
  - Core CPI above 2 percent (Mester, George, Fischer) and some wage and input-cost increases pull the score up a little.
  - Fischer says "close to our 2 percent target."
  - Overall this is below the midpoint, in the "below target, contained" band, about 40.
- *Output gap.* The view is split around "at potential."
  - Williams: "output and unemployment gaps now closed."
  - Mester and Powell: close to full employment.
  - Yellen, Dudley, Tarullo, Evans, Kashkari and Brainard: some slack remains.
  - Growth is described as "just barely above trend," and one forecast has a modest overshoot next year.
  - Net this is at potential, 48.
  - Bright line: "near potential" requires 48-52, which is satisfied.
- *Labor tightness.* Participants see the labor market as near full employment.
  - Evidence of tightness: Lacker ("continued tightness"), Lockhart ("widespread wage pressure"), Harker ("rising crescendo of complaints"), George ("tightening labor market").
  - Evidence of slack: Dudley, Tarullo, Yellen and Kashkari say some slack remains.
  - Unemployment is 4.9 percent against natural-rate estimates of about 4.7-5 percent. Score 51.
- *Consumption and investment.* The view is consumption solid, business investment weak.
  - Consumption is solid and the second half is expected to rebound.
  - Business fixed investment is weak or cautious (Lockhart, Kaplan, Evans, Harker, Mester), and the first half had weak GDP.
  - Net this is slightly below trend, 46.
- *Productivity.* This is widely discussed as low or very low (Fischer, Lacker, Bullard, Harker, Williams, Mester). Score 28.
- *Financial conditions.* Participants call these accommodative or eased.
  - Powell: "significant loosening in financial conditions."
  - Mester: "accommodative."
  - Dudley: "financial conditions are accommodative."
  - Williams: "easier than in June."
  - This is below 50 (bright line satisfied), 36.
- *Credit spreads.* The level is described as low.
  - Mester: "low corporate bond spreads."
  - Powell: "narrower credit spreads."
  - Williams: "notable decrease in the spread between high- and low-grade corporate debt," possibly search-for-yield.
  - Evans: debt markets "highly receptive."
  - The level reading is below 50 (bright line satisfied), 35.
- *Treasury stress.* N/A.
  - Dudley discusses LIBOR and money-fund reform at length but explicitly judges it low in consequence.
  - Lockhart asks about Japanese hedged buying of the long end.
  - There is no description of Treasury market dysfunction.
- *Equity valuation.* Only price moves are mentioned (Williams, Powell: stocks higher).
  - Kaplan raises overallocation to risk assets and carry trades; George raises record corporate leverage and buybacks.
  - That is concern about risk-taking, not an equity valuation call. Default is 45-55, with a small tilt to richness, 46, Low confidence.
- *Banking stress.* Banks are mentioned only indirectly.
  - Cleveland banking readings are positive.
  - Brainard flags euro-area bank profitability.
  - Rosengren cites "continued European banking problems."
  - Kaplan flags Chinese non-performing loans.
  - US banking is not stressed. Score 38 (below 50), Low confidence.
- *Dollar.* There was a big appreciation since 2014 that is still a headwind (Brainard, Rosengren).
  - Recently the dollar is "stabilized" (Brainard), "little changed" (Yellen, Williams) or "weaker" (Powell, since December).
  - Direction is flat, so 50.
- *Inflation sub-components.*
  - Goods: George says "core goods inflation was not a drag" and Mester cites rising input and finished-goods prices. Stabilizing, 46.
  - Services: N/A.
  - Housing: N/A. The CPI/PCE rent wedge is staff-style content in the Chair's remarks, not scored.
  - Expectations: market-based measures and Michigan are at or near lows (Brainard, Kashkari, Evans, Powell). SPF and Cleveland measures are stable (Mester). Slightly below anchored, 40.

**Tier 3 check in words.**
- *Policy lean.* The outcome is a hold with a strong December tightening signal.
  - Five participants wanted to tighten now and three dissented formally.
  - Kaplan, Fischer, Harker and Lockhart are near-tightening.
  - Against them, the Chair, Dudley, Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, Evans and Kashkari want patience.
  - Kashkari leans toward A, which would be more dovish.
  - Hold with a tightening bias, 57.
- *Dissent.* Three formal dissents, all toward tightening. Distinct camps argue about the economics (full employment and overshoot risk versus slack and asymmetric risk). The tone is respectful and the statement text was widely accepted as the compromise, so the intensity sits in the 61-75 band at its low end. Score 62.
- *Financial stability attention.* Several participants address it meaningfully.
  - Kaplan: carry trade and imbalances.
  - George: corporate leverage.
  - Mester: risks "contained."
  - Dudley: savings, pensions and money-market reform.
  - Evans and Williams: search-for-yield.
  - It is not the core of the meeting, which is about the labor market and overshoot risk. Score 38.
- *Forward guidance.* The "for the time being" language is explicitly calendar-based.
  - Yellen: a "December rate hike will, in all likelihood, be warranted."
  - Participants also stress a "gradual" path.
  - Mild-to-moderate tightening signal, 64.
- *Uncertainty.* Many participants stress humility about the natural rate, r*, slack and historical relationships.
  - Mester: "remain humble."
  - Tarullo: the economy's structure "quite different."
  - Evans: "uncertainty seems high."
  - Brainard: uncertainty about slack.
  - Most also say risks are "roughly balanced," and the basic outlook is not disputed. Elevated but not extreme, 50.

**Polarity checks.** Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence says conditions are easier, so the score is below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and spreads are low, so below 50. Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and inflation is below target, so below 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and US banks are fine, so below 50. All consistent.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 48: "near potential," within 48-52.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 (<50): satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 35 (<50, level reading): satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50): satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 50: no net direction, so no bright-line trigger.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51) are within 3.
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (46) are within 2.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) and CREDIT_SPREADS (35) co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, which is fine in a normal-times meeting.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (40) vs sub-components (goods 46, expectations 40): consistent.

**N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: no Treasury market dysfunction is described.
- INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed by members as such.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Brainard: "core inflation has not yet convincingly broken out of the 1.4 to 1.6 percent range"; Powell: "it's happening at a snail's pace"; Evans: "firmly entrenched low-inflation psychology."
- Summary: Inflation is below target and rising slowly, with Mester and George noting core CPI above 2 percent.
- Direction: Below neutral because most participants describe underlying inflation as below 2 percent with little emerging pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 +-5 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Williams: "output and unemployment gaps now closed"; Dudley: "growing only very modestly above trend"; Yellen: "some slack still remains."
- Summary: The Committee is split between "at potential" and "slightly below."
- Direction: At or just below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Lacker: "continued tightness in labor markets"; Lockhart: "widespread wage pressure"; Yellen: "a moderately tight labor market would help speed the return to 2 percent inflation," with "some slack still remains."
- Summary: The labor market is near full employment, with participants disagreeing on how much slack is left.
- Direction: Near neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Mester: "consumer spending... remains solid... The trouble spot continues to be weak business investment spending"; Evans: "I haven't spoken with anyone who talks about robust expansion plans."
- Summary: Consumption is solid and investment is weak, netting to slightly below trend.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 28 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Fischer: "extremely low rate of productivity growth"; Harker: "My main concern is the low productivity growth."
- Summary: Productivity is described as low by many participants.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Powell: "significant loosening in financial conditions"; Mester: "Financial conditions are accommodative."
- Summary: Conditions are eased and supportive.
- Direction: Below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Mester: "low corporate bond spreads"; Powell: "narrower credit spreads."
- Summary: Spreads are described as low or narrowed, with a possible search-for-yield tilt.
- Direction: Below neutral (low level).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Powell: "higher stock prices"; Kaplan: "overallocation to risk assets."
- Summary: Participants mention price moves and risk-taking, but give no explicit valuation view.
- Direction: Near neutral, with a small tilt to richness.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-10 (Low).**
- Evidence: Mester: "construction, freight, and banking continue to show positive readings"; Rosengren: "continued European banking problems."
- Summary: US banks are not a concern, and foreign bank stress is only noted.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 50 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Brainard: "stabilization of the dollar"; Yellen: dollar "little changed from where they stood last fall."
- Summary: The dollar is stable recently after large earlier appreciation.
- Direction: At neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 46.** George: "core goods inflation was not a drag on the overall CPI."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Brainard: "10-year inflation compensation is less than 1.5 percent"; Mester: "reasonably stable."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-5 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Yellen: "a December rate hike will, in all likelihood, be warranted"; Lacker: "an increase in our policy rates is appropriate today."
- Summary: A hold with a tightening bias, with five participants wanting to tighten now.
- Direction: Above neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 62 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Dudley: "we're going to have three dissents on the side of tightening"; Mester: "express my nonconsensus view."
- Summary: Three formal dissents and clear camps, with a respectful tone.
- Direction: Well above the low-dissent range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Kaplan: "the carry trade... overallocation to risk assets"; George: "nonfinancial corporate sector has increased leverage."
- Summary: Several participants address financial stability, but it is not the focus of the meeting.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Yellen: "'for the time being' is essentially a calendar-based reference"; Dudley: "the market is going to react... as making December highly likely."
- Summary: The statement leans forward to a December hike, within a gradual path.
- Direction: Above neutral (tightening signal).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Mester: "policymakers should remain humble"; Evans: "uncertainty seems high."
- Summary: Many participants stress uncertainty over r*, slack and historical relationships, but most call risks roughly balanced.
- Direction: Elevated.
