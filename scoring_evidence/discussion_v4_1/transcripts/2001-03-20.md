# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion transcript, meeting 2001-03-20

Note on evidence: to respect copyright limits, evidence below is given as attributed paraphrase rather than verbatim quotation.

## Part 1: Thinking Process

1. Document type. Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A turns by members, the economic go-around, the Chairman's policy proposal, the policy go-around, a debate over press-statement wording, and the vote read-out. Text is readable; some names are redacted and some staff passages removed, but nothing is garbled enough to change a score.

2. Era context. Early 2001: a sharp slowdown after very rapid growth, a large fall in technology equities and investment, an inventory correction, and a prior 50 bp cut in January. I score what members say.

3. Speakers. Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, Governors Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, and Presidents Parry, Minehan, Jordan, Santomero, Moskow, McTeer, Stern, Guynn, Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole. Most describe a mixed picture: manufacturing and high-tech weak, consumption, housing and employment steadier, equities sharply lower, downside risks. Poole, Jordan, Hoenig, Parry stress caution about over-easing; McTeer, Gramlich, Meyer, Ferguson stress downside risks and the case to ease.

4. Core dimensions, direction first.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Members see inflation as contained, near or slightly below target. Parry cites core PCE around 2 percent and edging lower; Kelley calls inflation quiescent; Hoenig and Guynn describe modest price pressure apart from energy and health care; Moskow notes readings a bit above expectations; Stern notes pockets of faster compensation growth. Slightly below midpoint.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Parry says output growth stays below potential through the horizon; Gramlich worries about a growing output gap; Ferguson fears growth well below potential for years. Below 50, bright line satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Still fairly tight in level but easing. Santomero reports district unemployment near 4 percent and skilled-labor shortages; Stern reports tight markets; Moskow says Midwest labor markets eased considerably; Hoenig says supply is less tight but not in excess. Blue Chip unemployment near 4.5 percent is called close to NAIRU. Slightly above midpoint.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption has held up better than expected (Minehan, Gramlich, Ferguson), but investment, capital plans and manufacturing are weak (Parry, Moskow, Guynn, Ferguson, Greenspan). Net below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Greenspan, McDonough, Hoenig and Gramlich describe the productivity trend as strong and intact; Ferguson worries a high-tech capital slowdown could erode it. Above trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Meyer says broader conditions have not eased because lower equity prices and a stronger dollar offset the rate cuts; Ferguson cites a falling stock market and banks tightening terms; Moskow reports harder credit; Poole and Broaddus say credit is somewhat tighter but available; Jordan sees no credit access problem. Mildly tight. No member describes conditions as accommodative, so the bright line is not triggered.
- TREASURY_STRESS: only a passing mention of the 10-year yield level (Broaddus) and the curve; no discussion of market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Moskow cites a loan officer survey showing tighter standards and wider spreads in the Midwest; Minehan notes tighter credit markets. Direction only, no level, so per the v4.1 rule 52-55 with Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Heavily discussed. Broaddus calls the decline dramatic but orderly; Parry sees part of it as a return to more rational valuations after historically high ones; Gramlich notes prices are near their level of two years earlier. Sharp but orderly correction with high concern.
- BANKING_STRESS: Hoenig lists sound financial institutions as a strength; Ferguson mentions banks tightening; Broaddus says this is not a typical past-slowdown credit squeeze. Healthy with minor tightening; Low confidence; below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Discussed by Jordan, Greenspan, McDonough, Parry, Meyer. The dollar has appreciated (attributed to a productivity differential and relative growth expectations) and Parry and Meyer see it as a drag. Above 50.
- Inflation sub-components: no category-level assessment, only energy and health-care cost anecdotes. All N/A.

5. Polarity verification. OUTPUT_GAP below 50 = below potential (match). LABOR above 50 = tight (match). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 = tighter (match). EQUITY above 50 = more equity concern (match). BANKING below 50 = healthy (match). DOLLAR above 50 = stronger (match).

6. Tier 3 check. Policy lean: the Chair proposes 50 bp with risks weighted toward weakness; all voters back it. McTeer would do more; Poole prefers 75 bp with a balanced-risk statement; Hoenig, Parry, Jordan, Moskow and others warn against more than 50 bp or against over-easing. Net moderate dovish. Disagreement: zero dissents, unanimous roll call; sizeable disagreement over whether the statement should hint at an intermeeting move (Minehan, Broaddus, Guynn, Jordan, Meyer, Moskow, McTeer, Hoenig, Poole uneasy or opposed; McDonough, Ferguson in favor; Gramlich and Kelley reluctantly accepting). It is tactical, not about the economic assessment. Financial stability: stock-market decline, wealth effects, moral hazard, credit tightening and foreign financial fragility feature in most remarks and in the Chair's policy argument. Forward guidance: weakness-weighted risks, readiness to act before the next meeting; the word "especially" was dropped. Uncertainty: Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson, Greenspan (false dawn), Hoenig, McDonough all stress it.

7. Bright-line check. All satisfied as above; CREDIT_SPREADS at 55 is within the direction-only band.

8. Coherence. OUTPUT_GAP 40 vs LABOR 54: 14 points, within tolerance (labor lags output; unemployment still low). OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: both 40. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 55: co-move. Treasury N/A consistent with normal times. Inflation sub-components N/A, no conflict.

9. N/A justification. TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning discussion. Inflation goods/services/housing/expectations: not assessed by category.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

INFLATION_PRESSURE 45 +-8 [Moderate]. Evidence: Kelley calls inflation quiescent and likely to stay so; Parry projects core PCE near 2 percent and slightly lower next year. Summary: contained, near or a bit below target, with energy and health-care pockets. Below neutral.

OUTPUT_GAP 40 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Parry says output stays below potential throughout the horizon; Gramlich worries about a widening output gap. Summary: growth below potential, slack building. Below neutral.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS 54 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Stern reports continued tight labor markets; Moskow reports markedly easier markets in the Midwest. Summary: low unemployment but loosening. Slightly above neutral.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Minehan is surprised that big-ticket spending persists despite weak confidence; Greenspan reports capital projects being shelved. Summary: resilient consumption, weak investment and manufacturing. Below neutral.

PRODUCTIVITY 62 +-8 [Moderate]. Evidence: Hoenig cites solid productivity trends; McDonough and Greenspan say the higher productivity trend remains in place. Summary: strong structural productivity, with some worry about capital deepening. Above neutral.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Meyer says conditions have not eased because equity declines and a stronger dollar offset policy cuts; Ferguson cites less accommodative conditions from equities and tighter bank terms. Summary: mildly tight though credit remains available. Above neutral.

TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

CREDIT_SPREADS 55 +-8 [Low]. Evidence: Moskow reports a loan officer survey with tighter standards and wider spreads; Poole says credit is somewhat tighter but readily available. Summary: direction toward wider spreads, level unstated. Slightly above neutral on the direction-only rule.

EQUITY_VALUATION 66 +-8 [Moderate]. Evidence: Broaddus calls the decline dramatic but orderly after one of the worst weeks in memory; Parry sees part of it as a return to more rational valuations. Summary: sharp correction, high concern, no disorder. Above neutral.

BANKING_STRESS 38 +-8 [Low]. Evidence: Hoenig cites sound financial institutions; Ferguson notes banks tightening terms. Summary: banks sound with some tightening. Below neutral.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH 64 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Parry says the stronger dollar could slow output; McDonough says the productivity differential has lifted the dollar against the euro. Summary: appreciation seen as a headwind. Above neutral.

INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING, EXPECTATIONS: N/A.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 30 +-6 [High]. Evidence: Greenspan proposes a 50 bp cut; McTeer urges doing more sooner. Summary: clear majority for easing by 50 bp with weakness-tilted risks, a few urging restraint. Dovish.

DISSENT_INTENSITY 38 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: the roll call is unanimous; Poole supports the action though 75 bp is his first choice; Broaddus has strong reservations about the intermeeting-move language. Summary: no dissents, but many reservations on communication and one differing preferred option. Moderate.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 65 +-8 [Moderate]. Evidence: Greenspan argues that policy cannot target stock prices and discusses moral hazard; Meyer stresses the equity and dollar offset to easing. Summary: equity decline, credit conditions and foreign financial fragility are prominent across the discussion.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 28 +-7 [Moderate]. Evidence: Santomero backs keeping the weakness-weighted risk language; Greenspan wants to signal readiness to act if needed. Summary: easing lean with data dependence, with Poole and Jordan pushing back. Below neutral.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 66 +-8 [Moderate]. Evidence: Kelley stresses fundamental uncertainty about whether an upturn is under way; Greenspan raises the risk of a false dawn. Summary: many members emphasize mixed data and unpredictable equity and confidence effects. High.
