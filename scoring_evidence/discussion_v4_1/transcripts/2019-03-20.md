# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2019-03-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two days (a first-day session and a "March 20 Session"). It holds: a balance-sheet normalization go-around and operating-framework Q&A; the economic go-around; a Q&A on an asymmetric-loss-function/policy-rule briefing; the policy go-round on "alternative B"; a communications/dot-plot wrap-up; and a vote read-out (all ten voters Yes). Staff turns are removed, so some questions to staff appear without the answers. Passages from the staff are ignored. Some extraction artifacts (short orphan "Yes." lines, a few cut-off sentences) were seen; none obscured scoring content.

**Era context.** Per the text only: the Committee has raised rates to a 2.25-2.50 range, has "pivoted" to a patient stance, growth is slowing from 2018's pace, Q1 GDP tracking about 1 percent, unemployment about 3.8 percent, core PCE inflation about 1.8-1.9 percent, and financial conditions have loosened after a Q4 tightening.

**Speakers.** Chair Powell, Vice Chair Williams, Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles, Bowman, and Presidents Rosengren, Harker, Daly, Mester, Bullard, Barkin, Kaplan, Evans, George, Bostic, Kashkari. Nearly all speak in both go-arounds.

**Dimension directions (stated before numbers).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint (target). Core PCE is "1.8", "1.9 percent", "muted", "slightly below target"; Powell sees "slippage", Brainard "underlying trend inflation ... slightly below the Committee's 2 percent objective". Some (Barkin, Quarles, Mester, Bowman) call it "basically at target". Wages are rising but with no pass-through. Magnitude: modest, about 10-12 points below 50, so around 38-40.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is slowing to trend (Daly, Williams, Barkin "moderating toward trend"; Powell "near or a bit above trend"), unemployment is below u* (Powell: "well below estimates of the natural rate"; Clarida: "an output gap that is modestly positive"; Bowman). Slightly above potential, so MUST be >50. Score 53.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment about 3.8 percent, "below 4 percent", job openings at record, "hard to find workers" (Kaplan, Barkin, Mester). One soft payroll print (+20k) is mostly dismissed. Tight, above 55 per the UE anchor. Score 64.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending softened sharply in Q1 (retail sales, autos, capex orders; Brainard, Rosengren, Bowman); many discount it and see solid fundamentals (Barkin, Bostic, Quarles, George). Net below trend but not contracting. Score 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Clarida ("upside surprises in ... productivity"), Mester ("strengthened last year ... too soon to tell"), Quarles ("step-up ... to almost 2 percent"), Daly (trend 1.2 percent, a bit faster than since 2010), Barkin, Kashkari. Modestly above recent trend. Score 56.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loosened, "reversal of the tightening" (Brainard, Bowman, Mester); Bowman: "broadly supportive of spending". Below 50 per bright line. Score 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield levels, a flat curve and Treasury supply/term premium (Clarida), plus reserve-demand/funds-rate volatility. No dysfunction, so normal functioning. Score 40, Low.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only: "credit spreads that are tighter" (Clarida), "spreads on high-yield corporate bonds moving down a further 30 basis points", "reversed more than half of their recent widening" (Brainard), "narrowed" (Williams). No explicit level statement. Per the v4.1 rule, direction only puts the score within 45-55 on the side of the move (narrowing), Low confidence. Credit-quality worries (Kaplan, Mester) concern leverage rather than spread level. Score 46.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Brainard: "asset valuations are again starting to look rich ... historically elevated range"; Mester: CRE "lofty"; most others just note equities recovered. Modest overvaluation concern, in the 31-45 band. Score 38, Low-Moderate.
- BANKING_STRESS: Kaplan: "lenders do not appear to be dramatically more leveraged ... CLO structure ... resilient"; Bowman: ag NPLs "well below levels observed during the 1980s" but drifting up; Mester: leveraged lending risks and whether banks would lend in stress. Sound with pockets of concern, below 50. Score 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a question to staff (Bullard) and a passing reference to last year's dollar appreciation feeding into core prices (George). Not specifically discussed, so N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: goods (George: goods inflation "moderating to typical levels") score 42; services (Daly: health-care services inflation held at "historically low levels") score 38; housing not discussed as shelter inflation, so N/A; expectations (many: below target, "edged below 2 percent", drifting down, Powell concerned about credibility) score 33.

**Tier 3 check (in words).**

- Policy lean: Unanimous hold with "patient" language. No one advocates a hike now; the dots flatten to no hikes in 2019 (Daly, Kaplan, Evans, George, Williams, Brainard). A minority (Bostic, Quarles, Mester, Rosengren, Harker) still expect one or more hikes and want options kept open; Evans wants a shallower path, Kaplan and Mester raise possible easing; Bullard says the decision will be read as "dovish"; ending balance-sheet runoff is read as mildly accommodative. Lean dovish-to-balanced.
- Dissent: Zero dissents, unanimous vote. Real but civil differences: Evans/Brainard (shallow path, no hikes for a long time) versus Bostic/Quarles/Mester (more hikes likely), taper timing (resolved overnight), floor of reserves ($1.0 vs $1.2 trillion), asymmetric loss function versus a lower u*. No sharp exchanges. Broad agreement, some emphasis differences.
- Financial stability emphasis: Meaningful. Kaplan on corporate debt/leveraged lending (a long passage and again in the Q&A), Brainard on rich valuations and the CCyB, Rosengren, Mester, Bullard, Evans debate whether it belongs in the framework, George on leveraged corporates, Quarles/Bostic on market reactions to the taper. A large share of day one is balance-sheet and market-reaction plumbing, which is financial conditions but framed as operational. Moderate.
- Forward guidance: Patient/hold, "no presumption about the direction of our next policy move" (Powell); several want to drop "patient" soon to keep either direction open (Barkin, George, Brainard, Kashkari); Bostic and Quarles warn against being read as done. Mild easing lean with data dependence.
- Uncertainty: "highly uncertain" outlook (Evans), "cloudy" (Mester), uncertain r*/u*, Brexit, trade, China, Europe, reserve demand "highly uncertain" (Powell), Kashkari "we just don't know". Elevated, but with a confident base case.

**Polarity checks.** OUTPUT_GAP >50 with "output gap modestly positive" and unemployment below natural rate: consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS <50 with "supportive/eased": consistent. BANKING_STRESS <50 with "resilient/not dramatically more leveraged": consistent. CREDIT_SPREADS: narrowing with no level statement, 46 (inside 45-55, narrowing side). EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more stress; overvaluation concern is a low score by the rubric, so 38 matches "historically elevated, rich". INFLATION: below target gives <50.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 64 (11 apart; participants say unemployment below u* yet growth only at trend, and the output-gap bright line keeps it near 50). OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION 44 (9 apart; Q1 softness is seen as temporary and trend is flat). FC 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 46: both on the loose/normal side, only mildly different. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal. INFLATION parent 38 vs sub-components 42/38/33 and low expectations: consistent.

**N/A justifications.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH (not discussed by members beyond a question to staff and a passing past reference); INFLATION_HOUSING (no shelter-price discussion).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 38 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Brainard: "Underlying trend inflation continues to be running slightly below the Committee's 2 percent objective." Bullard: "Inflation continues to be quite muted." Summary: core PCE about 1.8-1.9, expectations low, no pass-through from wages. Direction: below neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP 53 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Clarida: "an inflation gap measured by core PCE that is modestly negative and an output gap that is modestly positive." Powell: "unemployment well below estimates of the natural rate." Summary: growth near trend, labor utilization above sustainable. Direction: slightly above neutral (bright line satisfied).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 64 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Mester: "the unemployment rate is low, and the job openings rate is at its highest level since the series started in 2000." Kaplan: "Two-thirds of our surveyed firms report difficulty in hiring workers." Summary: unemployment about 3.8 percent, below natural rate; one weak payroll print seen as noise. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Bowman: "domestic spending growth has slowed down markedly this quarter." Barkin: "The fundamentals supporting continued growth in consumer spending remain strong." Summary: Q1 softness in retail, autos, capex orders, with most expecting a rebound. Direction: somewhat below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 56 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Quarles: "I am very encouraged by the step-up in labor productivity growth to almost 2 percent last year." Mester: "too soon to tell whether the pickup will be sustained." Summary: recent upside surprise, trend viewed as modest. Direction: modestly above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Bowman: "conditions appear broadly supportive of spending." Brainard: "a near-complete reversal of the financial tightening we saw late last year." Summary: eased after Q4 tightening, in part due to the patient stance. Direction: below neutral (loose/supportive).

**TREASURY_STRESS 40 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Clarida: "the bond market has seemed to forget that it has a lot of Treasury supply to absorb." Bullard: spread "fairly constant at a low level." Summary: only yield and curve commentary, no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 46 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Clarida: "credit spreads that are tighter ... than in December." Brainard: high-yield spreads "moving down a further 30 basis points", having "reversed more than half of their recent widening." Summary: direction is narrowing, level not stated; per v4.1 stays in 45-55 on the narrowing side. Direction: just below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 38 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Brainard: "asset valuations are again starting to look rich ... historically elevated range." Rosengren: "U.S. and Chinese equity prices have recovered smartly." Summary: one clear valuation-concern voice, most others note the rebound only. Direction: modestly rich (concern about overvaluation).

**BANKING_STRESS 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kaplan: "lenders do not appear to be dramatically more leveraged." Mester: "elevated levels of leveraged loans going to less creditworthy borrowers." Summary: banks sound, with pockets of concern in leveraged lending and agriculture. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.** Not discussed by members.

**INFLATION_GOODS 42.** George: "goods price inflation is moderating to typical levels." **INFLATION_SERVICES 38.** Daly: health-care services price inflation "at historically low levels." **INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 33.** Powell: "a bit more sympathetic to the notion that inflation expectations may have edged below 2 percent."; Evans: "inflation expectations ... stuck below our target."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 42 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Barkin: "It's hard to make the case for anything other than holding rates steady." Bullard: "I think the likely interpretation of today's decision will be 'dovish.'" Summary: unanimous hold, flatter dots, balance-sheet runoff ending, with a hawkish minority wanting hikes kept on the table (Bostic, Quarles). Direction: lean dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 ±6 (High).** Evidence: vote read-out, ten Yes votes; Powell: "I sense strong support around the table for alternative B." Summary: no dissents; differences in rate-path views and framework ideas are aired cordially. Direction: near-consensus with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 47 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kaplan: "I'm increasingly concerned about this excess imbalance building." Brainard: "financial imbalances have played a much more important role ... than has an inflation-induced tightening." Summary: several participants engage meaningfully (corporate debt, valuations, CCyB, framework role), plus market-reaction concerns around the balance sheet. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 42 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell: "remain on hold with no presumption about the direction of our next policy move." Brainard: "patience is only the right policy in one direction." Summary: patient hold, dots flatter, wish to avoid locked-in guidance in either direction. Direction: slightly easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 52 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Evans: "The outlook is still highly uncertain." Kashkari: "the path ahead is really unclear." Summary: many cite global, trade, Brexit, u*/r* and reserve-demand uncertainty, but most also state a confident base case. Direction: elevated.
