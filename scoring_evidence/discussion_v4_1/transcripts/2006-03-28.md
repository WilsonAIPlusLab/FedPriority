# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) - 2006-03-28 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Document type: two-day transcript, economic go-around (Day 1 + Day 2 morning), policy go-around, vote read-out. Not special-topic (has a policy vote and a stance go-around).
- Policy lean: moderately hawkish. Everyone voting backs a 25bp rise (to 4 3/4) with "some further policy firming may be needed"; the debate is over how soon to signal a stop (Hoenig, Minehan, Pianalto, Guynn, Yellen want less forward commitment; Kohn, Moskow, Lacker, Stone, Bies, Fisher, Poole lean to more tightening).
- Dissent: zero formal dissents (11-0 roll call). Reservations are about wording and the stopping point (Hoenig would prefer alternative A and a stop signal but would not dissent; Minehan likewise), so dissent is low but not minimal.
- Conditions: strong growth (Q1 rebound, ~4 1/2%; growth above potential near term), UE 4.8% near full employment with skilled-labor shortages, strong productivity, housing cooling; core inflation benign (about 1.8-2.1%) but at the upper end of members' comfort zones with a slight upside risk.
- Financial conditions described as supportive/liquid (Geithner, Bies, Minehan); credit spreads and risk premiums low; banks healthy (Olson). Treasury stress N/A; housing/mortgage and liquidity discussed as context.
- Forward guidance: mild tightening bias, with explicit committee effort to avoid signalling more than one further move and to exit the guidance later.
- Uncertainty: normal-to-somewhat-elevated (housing, pass-through, NAIRU, term premium), no "unusual" uncertainty.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-6 Moderate-High.** Evidence: Yellen "core PCE prices are up 1.8 percent... core CPI just 2.1 percent"; Geithner "underlying inflation at the upper end of the inferred inflation preference"; Bernanke "very modest upside risk." Center of gravity: recent readings benign, but members place core at the upper end of comfort ranges with a slight upside tilt. Inflation is modestly above/at the top of the comfort zone, so above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 57 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Moskow "operating with very little resource slack... growth will likely exceed potential"; Bernanke "the economy is, if anything, growing more quickly than potential"; Yellen "pretty close to, not beyond, full employment." Center: slightly above potential near term, converging to potential. Above neutral (bright line: "exceed potential" implies >50).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen "unemployment rate already at 4.8 percent"; Stone "economy is expected to remain near full employment, with labor markets tight"; Lacker "worker shortages... chatter has picked up noticeably." Center: UE 4.8%, shortages mainly for skilled workers, wage growth picking up but benign overall. Slightly tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 Moderate-High.** Evidence: Fisher retailers "demand is amazing"; Kroszner "Consumption remains solid... business investment still appears to be reasonably strong"; Warsh capex "may well surprise to the upside". Housing is the offset (Fisher cancellation rate 32 percent; Kohn "appreciable drop-off in the demand for housing"). Net above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Minehan "continued strong productivity growth... a powerful buffer"; Geithner "productivity growth is likely to remain quite high." Above trend as perceived.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Geithner "overall financial conditions seem pretty supportive of the expansion"; Bies "tremendous amount of liquidity in the financial markets"; Minehan "very favorable financing conditions." Counterpoint: Yellen/Hoenig note lagged effect of tightening, long rates not up. Below 50 (bright line satisfied).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only term-premium and yield-curve level discussed; no market functioning commentary by members.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Geithner "Equity prices and credit spreads suggest considerable confidence... sustainability of this present configuration of low risk premiums"; Bies "We do not hear anything from corporations about difficulty in financing." Level described as low; below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-10 Low.** Evidence: Geithner "Equity prices... suggest considerable confidence in the prospect for growth." No valuation view stated; default 45-55 range (V4.1.1), low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS: 25 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Olson "Asset quality remains very good, above levels that historical experience would suggest they could maintain"; Bies "bankers clearly are looking for loan growth." Healthy, below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 55 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Kohn "the dollar has risen on balance"; Geithner forecast "conditioned on little movement in the dollar." Mild appreciation, brief mention; above 50 per bright line.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42.** Geithner "recent weakness in core goods prices in the United States may not be sustained."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 48.** Geithner "Service price inflation in the United States has shown signs of slowing."
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Housing discussed as activity/wealth, not shelter inflation.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Stern "inflation expectations remain very well anchored"; Warsh TIPS "contained, while... higher than ideal"; Lacker "expected CPI and the term spreads are running around 2 1/2 percent... high to me."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Kohn "I'm convinced we should lean hard against any potential increases in inflation"; Poole "highly probable... we will want another 25 basis points"; Hoenig "we are about where we need to be." All voters back 25bp; a minority wants a signalled stop soon; a larger group sees at least one more move. Majority favors tightening, with a mixed forward lean, so moderate hawkish (reduced from the mid-60s by the stop-soon camp).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +-6 High.** Evidence: roll call 11-0 "Yes"; Hoenig "I wouldn't dissent over it if I were a voting member"; Minehan "I wouldn't dissent over the language." No dissents; differences on wording and ending point among many speakers.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Bies on liquidity as "an inflationary risk"; Geithner on "low risk premiums"; Warsh on LBO/IPO and investment-grade premium risks; housing/mortgage rate discussion. Several mention, limited focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: statement language "some further policy firming may be needed"; Bernanke "we may choose to signal a halt or even to not move in May"; Minehan wants to say "We are done" in May or June. Mild tightening bias retained but deliberately flexible.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Kohn "how wide those confidence intervals are, how little we know about price and output determination"; Bernanke on housing "The range of possible outcomes is quite wide." Standard-plus caveats; no "unusual" uncertainty.
