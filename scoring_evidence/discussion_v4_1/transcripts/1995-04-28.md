# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, 1995-04-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, early (1995) format. The input holds only the Chairman's and members' turns around the staff briefings: Hoenig asks about Mexican reserves, Greenspan asks about intervention, Lindsey asks about consumer loan growth, Greenspan asks about the quarterly pattern of real PCE, and Blinder asks how much of the Q1 export weakness is due to Mexico. Greenspan then gives a report on the G-7 meeting and the Interim Committee. Moskow asks about a visitor to Chicago. The session ends with "END OF SESSION". The text contains no economic go-around, no policy go-around, no directive and no vote read-out. It is either truncated or the go-around was not captured. Several turns are fragments in which staff answers were removed, and some sentences are cut (for example "the end result was that," and "we had to confront the question who had argued..."). Most of the text is questions and an international-policy report, so the document is very thin for scoring. All scores get Low confidence and wide bands, and the document is flagged as an anomaly.

**Era context.** Spring 1995, after the Fed's 1994 tightening cycle, with the Mexican crisis and a weak dollar in the background. I did not let this override the text. Only what is said in the document is used.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (G-7 report, a few questions), Hoenig (Mexican reserves), Lindsey (consumer loans), Blinder (exports and Mexico), Moskow (a visitor).

**Dimensions.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: not discussed by any member. The text contains no mention of prices or inflation as a condition. N/A.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Greenspan refers to "the weakness in the economy that we were observing" and to inventories that "may effect some softening in the economy". Blinder notes that exports fell in Q1, from 15-20 percent growth to roughly zero, and that Mexico explains much of it. Participants therefore see activity as softening, which points below the midpoint. Nothing is said in terms of potential or slack. The bright-line rule is satisfied by a score below 50. Mild softening supports 5-10 points below the midpoint, but the evidence is thin, so about 43 with wide bands.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: not discussed. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Greenspan's "weakness in the economy" and "softening" from inventories, plus Blinder's export drop, imply below-trend spending. Lindsey's 14 percent consumer loan growth point sits in a question and is a staff-data item, so it carries no weight. Greenspan's question about the Q2 pickup in PCE is a question and offers no member view. Score slightly below the midpoint, about 42, Low confidence.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: the conditions are barely described. Greenspan argues that long-term nominal rates have not risen despite the dollar's weakness, and that "real long-term rates must have been going down". This implies somewhat easy long-term financing conditions, so the score is below 50. The bright-line rule (easy means below 50) is satisfied. It is an argument about the dollar rather than a conditions assessment, so Low confidence, about 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: not discussed as market functioning. Only yield levels appear in Greenspan's argument. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: not discussed. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: not discussed. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: not discussed. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: the dollar is the central topic. Greenspan describes "the weakening long-term forward exchange rate for the dollar" and says that after a 50bp rise "the dollar would jump a couple of pfennings but it would then start to depreciate". He also says the move "may be a bubble comparable to that in the latter part of 1984 and early 1985", and G-7 partners call the result "intolerable". Participants therefore see the dollar as weak and depreciating. The bright-line rule requires a score below 50. This is notable depreciation, so about 25, with Moderate-to-Low confidence. The only substantive evidence is Greenspan's report, so Low.
- Inflation sub-components: none discussed. N/A.

**Tier 3.**

- Policy lean: there is no policy go-around. The only policy-relevant statements are Greenspan's report that raising interest rates to support the dollar would be "quite inappropriate", that small rate changes "do not accomplish very much", and that interest rates cannot fix structural problems. These concern the exchange-rate role of rates, not the stance on domestic conditions. There is no stated easing or tightening bias. Balanced, with a very low-confidence reading of about 48, since the weakness comments carry a slight softness.
- Dissent: there is no vote, no dissent and no disagreement. Greenspan describes a collegial G-7. Near-consensus by absence of any dissent. Low, about 10, Low confidence because the vote is missing.
- Financial stability emphasis: only the exchange-market and Mexico topics. These are brief and international. Roughly 12.
- Forward guidance: no signal. 50, Low confidence.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan emphasizes that "our inability to forecast exchange rates" shows the adjustment process is "far greater than anything we can get our hands around", that "we have to be careful about being very sure about any of these explanations" and that there is "no good way of coming at this". This is genuine uncertainty, but narrow in scope (exchange rates and the reasons for softening). Elevated for this topic only. About 45, Low confidence.

**Polarity checks.** OUTPUT_GAP, CONSUMPTION and DOLLAR_STRENGTH all point to weak or depreciating readings, so lower scores. Dollar depreciation is below 50, as required. Easy long-term real rates give FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (43) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42) are within 15 points. LABOR_TIGHTNESS is N/A, so that pair cannot be checked. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and CREDIT_SPREADS: credit spreads are N/A. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A. Inflation parent is N/A and has no sub-components to compare.

**N/A justification.** Inflation, labor, productivity, Treasury stress, credit spreads, equity valuation, banking stress and the four inflation sub-components were not raised by participants in the available text.
