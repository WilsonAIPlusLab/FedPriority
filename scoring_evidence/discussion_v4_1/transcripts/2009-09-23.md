## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document: member and participant discussion, two-day meeting (afternoon Q&A and unstructured talk, economic go-around, policy go-around on statement alternative B, straw votes, roll call 10 Yes, 0 No). Not special-topic. Staff turns are removed, so the member questions to staff appear without the answers.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-6, High.** Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation for the past year is 1.4 percent... well below"; "disinflation... being borne out". Yellen: "Core inflation is currently below my price stability goal"; Evans: "likely to stay below... 1½ percent". Kohn: "core inflation continues to edge lower". Dudley: "risks seem very much on the downside". Bernanke: "Inflation remains relatively low... modest downward trend in the core". Offsetting minority (Plosser: "only upside risks to inflation" in the medium term; Hoenig; Lacker) concerns future risk, not current pressure. Below target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 27 +-5, High.** Yellen: "both labor and product markets have a considerable amount of slack". Kohn: "considerable labor and capital resources looking to... be put back to work". Dudley: "ample slack in the economy". Bernanke: "very high level of slack". Plosser: slack of "minus 3 percent" in his model vs "minus 6 or 7 percent" in the Greenbook. Far below potential, recovery just starting (bright line satisfied).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 +-4, High.** Evans: unemployment "just about 10 percent"; "remaining above 9 percent". Tarullo: "employment situation continues to be dismal"; "nowhere near achieving the maximum employment". Yellen: "jobless recovery... persistently high unemployment". Dudley: "payrolls are still falling, and the unemployment rate is still rising". Bernanke: "very weak, but may be close to the bottom". High unemployment; the level governs.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-7, Moderate.** Yellen: "Consumer spending appears to have bottomed out". Lacker: "marked improvements in consumer spending, equipment and software, and residential investment". Fisher: "retail sales remain anemic"; CEOs "only commit to vital and minimal cap ex in 2010". Kohn: "record low capacity utilization... will hold back investment". Bernanke: consumption "restrain[ed]" by weak labor, tight credit, fading stimulus. Rebounding from a low level but still below trend; the net is below 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +-8, Moderate.** Kohn: "rapid productivity growth, unit labor costs are falling very, very fast". Fisher: businesses "wringing productivity... out of their workers"; "productivity has held up rather well throughout this recession". Yellen: "productivity improvements in the downturn". Bernanke: "Cost-cutting has contributed to productivity gains. The sustainability... is unclear". Above trend, with doubts about durability.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 +-9, Moderate.** Markets improved: Plosser "signs of financial stress... have greatly abated"; Kohn "continued improvement in financial markets"; Bernanke "better financial conditions" and falling Treasury yields "supportive of recovery". Credit side still tight: Kohn "Bank credit to households and small businesses remains very tight"; Dudley "Small businesses and commercial real estate sectors are particularly starved for credit"; Bernanke "Bank credit remains tight". Easy in markets and tight in bank credit net to roughly neutral. The bright-line words "supportive" and "tight" each apply to different segments.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8, Moderate.** Bernanke: "decline in longer-term Treasury yields is somewhat puzzling, but at least for now it is supportive of recovery". Lacker and Plosser: yields fell despite upbeat data. Warsh: Treasury markets "incredibly supportive of massive issuance... infinite demand", with a fiscal-sustainability worry about the future. Yield moves and concern about future supply only; no dysfunction. Normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 +-12, Low.** Rests mainly on availability (V4.1.1 rule 3). Kohn: "higher equity prices and lower spreads" (market spreads narrowed, direction only, no level). Kohn also: "spreads on business loan rates over base rates... tightened further"; "Credit card spreads and terms have been tightening". Dudley: "starved for credit". Tarullo: "continuing dysfunction in some other parts of credit markets". Duke: "very low demand... pricing is still significantly better". Narrowing market spreads (below 50) are outweighed by clearly tight bank availability and wide loan pricing (above 50). Wide UNC for that reason.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-8, Moderate.** Fisher: "greatest stock market rally coming out of a recession in history"; worried about "pretty healthy equity market numbers going forward" without top-line growth. Warsh: "still a ton of momentum"; "strategically short, or at least strategically nervous"; "Zero rates seem to be having some hand in driving this reflation in asset prices". Bernanke: "I don't really see any major bubbles at this point". Modest valuation concern and momentum worries, not stretched.

**BANKING_STRESS: 64 +-7, High.** Dudley: "the banking system is still impaired. Bank credit outstanding has been declining". Rosengren: "nonperforming loans that are growing faster than reserves at many banks". Bernanke: "credit risks in the banking system remain substantial"; banks "just going to skirt by in terms of their ability to maintain adequate capital". Duke: "Commercial real estate is still the big wild card". Significant but post-crisis stress (not "healthy"; bright line satisfied).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 32 +-7, Moderate.** Warsh: "the foreign exchange value of the dollar is down"; "if the dollar continues to weaken". Bernanke: "the dollar is weakening"; "The decline in the dollar we've been seeing has been relatively moderate". Lockhart: dollar "stabilized in the last few days, but two or three weeks ago it was under pretty severe pressure". Depreciation (bright line satisfied), moderate in size.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42.** Dudley: "core goods inflation seems likely to follow" services down. Plosser: manufacturers' "prices paid and received... firmed a bit". Bernanke/Warsh: commodity prices up. Goods prices are soft in core, with commodities firming.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 30.** Dudley: "Core services inflation has come down a lot". Bernanke: "substantial slowdown in services prices". Kohn: compensation "clearly downward".

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 30.** Bernanke: "substantial slowdown in services prices, including rents". A passing remark; no other member comment on shelter inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Pianalto and Lyon: expectations "stable" or "well anchored". Kohn: "quite stable". Plosser: surveys "in the 3 percent range", expects them to rise. Bullard: "suspicious of how anchored". Yellen: "inflation expectations have actually drifted down". Bernanke: stability "may be disguising a great deal of uncertainty". Anchored near target on balance, with dispersed concerns on both sides.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +-6, High.** The Committee keeps the near-zero rate with "extended period" language and completes the full $1¼ trillion of MBS purchases (straw vote 10 to 5 to drop "up to"). Yellen: "I see a strong case on policy grounds for further monetary stimulus". Dudley: "we should commit to doing that $1¼ trillion". Kohn: "I don't think we need to tighten in any way". Bernanke: "I don't think that scaling back stimulus significantly at this meeting is warranted". Hawkish minority: Plosser ("slight variant of alternative C", "time to" exit "extended period"), Hoenig ("I would prefer alternative C"), Lacker (wary of more MBS). Majority hold maximum accommodation with ongoing QE, and the hawkish camp is a minority.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-8, Moderate.** Roll call: 10 Yes, no dissent among voters. Disagreement is nonetheless real. Evans: "the most disparate set of commentary I've heard on policy alternatives". Yellen: "I can support alternative B today, but I do so reluctantly". Tarullo: "not without some reluctance". Plosser and Hoenig (non-voters this year) prefer C. Disagreement over the "up to" wording (straw vote 5 to 10, "four in favor... four in favor"), over slack versus expectations as drivers of inflation (Plosser and Lacker vs Evans, Yellen and Kohn). Tone is collegial, so the score stays at the low end of the moderate band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8, Moderate.** Long threads on bank credit and capital (Duke, Kohn, Dudley, Rosengren, Tarullo), asset-price momentum and Treasury fiscal risk (Warsh), balance-sheet losses and exit, and the Chairman's remarks on bubbles ("I don't really see any major bubbles") and a possible disorderly dollar decline that "might lead to concerns about financial stability". Much of the meeting concerns the Fed's balance sheet and MBS purchases, which is not itself financial stability, so the attention is moderate to substantial but does not dominate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-6, Moderate.** "Extended period" language is retained (Kohn: "it's way too early to signal anything"; Hoenig "uneasy with the phrase"). Yellen proposes the alternative A wording, "low resource utilization and subdued inflation are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels", to keep the funds-rate path low. Bernanke: "keep this possibility alive if the situation warrants" of doing more; Tarullo and Bullard want it clear the Fed will respond if conditions deteriorate. Easing lean (extended low rates, MBS completed by end of Q1) with a taper signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-7, High.** Lockhart: "substantial uncertainty... vulnerable to relapse". Pianalto: "uncertain and cautious"; "very fragile". Fisher: "Our tail risks are almost undefinable". Bullard: "masking a real uncertainty"; "we don't have models or a lot of experience with them". Tarullo: relapse, "grind it out" and rejuvenation "all remain plausible". Bernanke: "considerable uncertainty about whether final demand growth will be sustained". Warsh: "market prices might be as unclear as they've been in a very long time". Broad and genuine uncertainty, though less extreme than at the trough.
