## Part 2: Evidence and scores

INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 +-6 High. Core PCE 1.5% and below target; "inflation... the slumbering giant, or perhaps it is a mouse" (Fischer); "core PCE prices had increased only 1.5 percent... considerable shortfall below the Committee's 2 percent objective" (Brainard); "significant shortfall below our 2 percent target" (Kashkari); "disconcerting" (Evans); Dudley, Kaplan, Mester, Williams, Yellen see soft prints as largely idiosyncratic/transitory with a return to 2 percent expected (forecast, not scored). Several worry; only Rosengren/Kaplan expect upward pressure later.

OUTPUT_GAP 54 +-5 High. "well above estimates of potential growth" (Fischer); "real GDP growth will be above its potential rate over the next two years... overheated economy" (Rosengren); "overshot by a significant amount our maximum employment mandate" (Williams); "moderate but above-trend growth" (Powell); "at or somewhat beyond maximum employment" (Mester). Offsets: Bullard sees growth only at trend; Yellen "labor utilization is currently close to its sustainable longer-run level... merely warm, not hot"; "capital utilization rates... below historical averages" (Yellen). Above-potential language is present, so score is above 50, modestly.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS 62 +-5 High. UE 4.3%; "labor markets are tight... Unemployment is well below estimates of its natural rate" (Mullinix); "hot labor market" (Williams); "JOLTS job openings rate is at its historical high" (Mester); "labor market is clearly tighter than it was" (Kashkari). Tempered: wage growth subdued, Yellen "warm", natural rate estimates 4.5-4.8.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50 +-5 Moderate. "overall economy is continuing to expand at a moderate pace... business investment appears to be expanding at a solid pace" (Yellen); "economic growth is sluggish to moderate" (Kashkari); "growth in consumer spending has bounced back after the Q1 dip" (Yellen); auto sales slowing and large firms curtailing capex on tax uncertainty (Bostic, Mullinix). Net at trend.

PRODUCTIVITY 27 +-7 Moderate. "Productivity growth remains disappointing" (Fischer); "productivity growth rising from its anemic level" (Mester); "decade of slow productivity growth" (Williams); "relatively weak productivity growth in recent years" (Bullard).

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 25 +-5 High. "financial conditions, broadly defined, have continued to ease and are at a very accommodative level" and "extremely accommodative" (Dudley); "Financial conditions also remain supportive of growth... easy financial conditions" (George); "still highly accommodative financial conditions" (Fischer); "strong accommodative conditions in financial, housing, and other markets" (Bostic). Bright-line (accommodative, so below 50) satisfied. Bullard and Kaplan question how much to read into the FCI but do not dispute that conditions are easy.

TREASURY_STRESS 42 +-8 Low. Participants discuss yields only as a puzzle, not as dysfunction: "10-year Treasury rate... approximately 2.2 percent" (Kaplan); "puzzled by the low level of long-term Treasury yields" (Dudley); debt-ceiling timing worries concern policy timing, not market functioning. Normal functioning (36-50), Low confidence because no direct market-functioning comment.

CREDIT_SPREADS 28 +-8 Moderate. "credit spreads have been relatively stable recently, they're also very narrow" (Dudley); "corporate bond spreads have narrowed" (George); "tighter credit spreads" (Powell); "corporate bond yields are down significantly, more than a percentage point for... high-yield" (Fischer). Stated narrow level, so below 50.

EQUITY_VALUATION 36 +-6 High. "equity market valuations... appear somewhat elevated" but "don't seem to be near the dizzying heights of 1999 and 2000" (Brainard); "frothiness... in financial market valuations" (Dudley); "Sectors of both the stock market and real estate markets are arguably becoming frothier" (Rosengren); "elevated asset prices of equities, real estate, and corporate bonds" (Williams); "low equity premium" (Mester); "higher equity P/Es" (Powell). Bullard argues post-election equity moves reflect corporate-tax-reform repricing rather than a bubble. Modestly above fair value.

BANKING_STRESS 30 +-7 Moderate. "the large banks at the center of the system are much better capitalized" (Brainard); "loan-to-value ratios for commercial real estate lending are at... much more sensible levels" (Mester); pockets watched: "rising levels of corporate debt, CRE lending, and rising delinquency rates in subprime auto" (Brainard). Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 +-7 Moderate. "the dollar has weakened" (Dudley); "dollar come down" since November (Williams); "exchange rate of the dollar about unchanged over the past 18 months" (Fischer); "lingering effect of a depreciated dollar" (Rosengren); "the dollar is still quite elevated" (Yellen, level). Direction recently weaker, so below 50; Yellen's level comment keeps it near stable.

INFLATION_GOODS N/A. Import prices mentioned only as a supporting force (George, Bostic, Brainard); no goods-price assessment.
INFLATION_SERVICES 36. Cell phone service plan prices fell sharply ("10 percent decline in the price of cell phone services over the past three months", Dudley; Mester, Kaplan on telecom); prescription drugs too. Treated as idiosyncratic declines in services prices.
INFLATION_HOUSING 42. "rent increases are slowing in much of the District, and rents are declining in Pittsburgh" (Mester); "house prices seem fairly well aligned with rents" (Brainard). Thin evidence.
INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 36. "inflation expectations that anchor our projections remain too low" (Evans); "survey-based... near the very bottom of their historical ranges" (Kashkari); NY Fed three-year expectations at 16-month low and "something more serious" (Dudley); "worry about the possibility of erosion in our nominal anchor" (Bostic). Counterweights: Mester, Powell, Kaplan describe them as stable.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 62 +-6 Moderate. Eight of nine voters back a 25bp hike plus the balance-sheet runoff plan ("appropriate to tighten", Dudley; "next steps toward removing accommodation", Williams); Rosengren and Williams want faster than the Tealbook; Dudley says "we have more to do, not less". Tempered by Evans, Powell, Bostic, Brainard favoring skipping September or delaying, Bullard "current level of the policy rate is... about right", and Kashkari dissenting for no hike. Moderate hawkish, with inflation shortfall limiting the lean.

DISSENT_INTENSITY 38 +-6 Moderate. One formal dissent: Kashkari voted No ("I support alternative A... tension between faith and data"). Bullard ("not a great day for the Committee"; "Damn the torpedoes" normalization) and Evans voted Yes with strong reservations. Open disagreement on balance-sheet start (July per Fischer, Rosengren, George, Mester vs September per Williams, Brainard, Dudley, Yellen) and on the inflation outlook. Courteous throughout; no sharp exchanges apart from one joke by Dudley.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 58 +-6 Moderate. Many raise it: "lean against some of the 'frothiness'" (Dudley); "financial risks building... very high asset prices and other forms of financial excess" (Powell); "financial stability risk could be building" (Mester); "very low levels of... the VIX... complacency" (Williams); "complacency" (Fischer); Rosengren on financial-stability implications of low unemployment; Brainard's detailed review; Bullard's challenge to the FCI as a rationale. A recurring theme and used as an argument for tightening, but not the dominant topic.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66 +-7 Moderate. Balance-sheet reduction announced with start "later this year"/September ("announce the start of our portfolio normalization program in, say, September", Yellen); gradual hikes to continue ("Continue on the gradual normalization path unless there is material evidence against doing so", Mester). Moderating: new "monitoring inflation developments closely" wording; Fischer wants "less forward guidance"; Powell/Evans/Bostic expect a possible pause until December. Tightening bias, tempered.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 36 +-7 Moderate. Risks called "roughly balanced" (Williams, Powell, Mester, Yellen); Dudley lowered stated uncertainty to "broadly similar". But "we're very uncertain about the natural rate of unemployment" (Kashkari), "we don't really understand inflation dynamics very well" (Kashkari), fiscal/political uncertainty (Fischer, Mester, George, Bostic), and "not wise to have a lot of conviction" about the natural rate (Powell). Somewhat elevated on specific factors, not general.
