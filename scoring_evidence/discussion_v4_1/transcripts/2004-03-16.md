# v4.1 Discussion Score: 2004-03-16 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed): the Chairman's questions to the staff, the economic go-around (Moskow through Bies, with Kohn, Gramlich, Geithner, Olson, Ferguson, Bernanke interleaved), the Chairman's policy opening, a policy/statement round (Poole through Olson), the vote read-out (all Yes), and a closing procedural discussion of the minutes (ignored as procedure). A few lines are garbled or cut (Greenspan's replies to staff, a "red line" chart remark) but no passage relevant to scoring is unreadable.

**Era context.** Spring 2004. Participants describe a recovery that is strong in output and investment, a weak labor market (very soft February payrolls, "jobless" recovery), very low core inflation (about 1 percent) with growing anecdotes of cost and price pressure, a federal funds rate held at a very low level with "patience" language, and worry that low rates are fostering reach-for-yield behavior. Score is taken from what the text says.

**Speakers and views.**
- Moskow: strong household spending, hesitant hiring, output growth above potential, slack gone next year, "don't need to be in a hurry".
- McTeer: pricing pressure beginning, "tipping point" toward higher inflation, but no policy shift yet. Overbuilt Dallas offices, falling rents.
- Minehan: strong growth, soft labor market, rising prices in shelter and commodities, "very accommodative" policy raising leverage and financial-instability risk, "interest rates too low for too long" is the bigger concern; wants the statement to show a willingness to move up.
- Broaddus: widening output and employment gap, disinflation risk underweighted, wants an easing-bias statement (extreme dovish outlier).
- Poole: demand on the high side, inflation shocks all in the same direction, wants balanced risks. Parry: data moderately weaker, core near price stability, supports no change.
- Santomero, Guynn, Hoenig, Stern, Pianalto: positive growth, price anecdotes growing (Guynn, Hoenig, Pianalto, Stern most), several prefer balanced risks but accept no change.
- Kohn, Ferguson, Bernanke, Bies, Gramlich, Geithner, Olson: patience, larger output gap, downside tilt retained; Geithner flags that the patience signal is holding expectations down too far and should be dialed back soon; Bernanke proposes a financial stability report.
- Greenspan: rapid productivity slowing, reach-for-yield "probably one of the most important issues" that will eventually move the Committee toward tightening, but no tightening soon; leave the inflation-risk sentence unchanged. Outcome is no change in the stance or the balance-of-risks sentence; the vote is unanimous.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see core inflation as below the target zone and low (Parry "close to price stability", core PCE 1 percent; Kohn "from an extraordinarily low level"; Bies "1 percent is a low number"), but with rising commodity/energy/insurance/health-care pressure and several (McTeer, Pianalto, Guynn, Hoenig, Poole) saying the disinflation is bottoming out. Net: below 50, modestly, with upward drift. About 43.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as below potential. Greenbook gap widened (Broaddus, Kohn "somewhat larger estimated output gap", Ferguson "output gap is probably up"). Moskow says growth is above potential and will eliminate slack next year, but the current level is still slack. Below 50, "slack remains" language: about 40.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Weak payrolls, rising employment gap, participation falling, unemployment in the mid-5s (Parry: below 5.5 by Q4; Minehan: low 5s by 2005). Offsetting: skill shortages, small firms hiring. Below full employment: about 37.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Household spending "robust" (Moskow), consumption "moderate pace" (Santomero), equipment and software spending "quite strong" (Kohn), "strong" (Stern, Pianalto), though slightly below expectations and housing/autos softening (Ferguson). Above trend but cooling: about 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Repeatedly described as very rapid, structurally higher (Ferguson, Geithner, Pianalto, Stern, Poole question on the staff's drop to 1.6 percent). Greenspan notes it is beginning to slow. Well above trend: about 72.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative financial conditions generally" and "very favorable financial conditions" (Geithner), "very accommodative stance" with leverage building (Minehan), investors "awash with liquidity" (Moskow), reach for yield. Bright line requires < 50: about 27.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only the Chairman's chart questions on custody holdings and Guynn's comment on Japanese/Chinese holdings; no dysfunction. N/A (topic not raised as stress by participants).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level language points low: Bernanke "narrowing of risk spreads on corporate bonds...investors reaching for yield", Kohn "risk spreads in some markets" beyond justification, but also "domestic risk spreads aren't obviously too low for the beginning of an expansion" and "widening in a few risk spreads". Level reading: below normal but mixed, bright line < 50: about 36, low confidence on size.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price moves (Kohn "decline in equity prices"; Ferguson "equity wealth...may start to soften"), no valuation judgment. Default neutral: 48, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Kohn "capital in most intermediaries is high, and the system is resilient", Bies "credit quality has improved", Olson C&I loans growing and banks "very aggressive" lending. Healthy, bright line < 50: about 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Mixed: much language refers to the weaker dollar that boosted orders and tourism (Moskow, Guynn, Parry, Minehan "bound to depreciate a bit further"), but in the intermeeting period the dollar rose (Kohn "dollar's rise is rather surprising", Bernanke "downward trend in the dollar has been arrested"). Net near stable with slight weakness leaning: 46. Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS, commodity/metals/energy/steel prices up sharply with some pass-through, but retail goods prices still falling (Poole Wal-Mart -1 percent): 56. SERVICES, insurance, health care, security, trucking, auditing fees rising (Guynn, Poole): 54. HOUSING, shelter up 6.3 percent in Boston (Minehan), house prices elevated vs rents (Kohn) but Texas rents falling (McTeer): 52. EXPECTATIONS, Blue Chip 2.25-2.5 percent (Parry), TIPS and "resiliency of inflationary expectations" (Minehan), otherwise not a focus: 50.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Unanimous decision to hold. Patience retained, inflation-risk sentence leaning to the downside retained. Camps: Broaddus wants an easing bias; Kohn, Bies, Ferguson, Bernanke, Gramlich, Olson, Geithner, Greenspan keep the downside tilt; Poole, Minehan, Stern, Guynn, Hoenig, McTeer, Pianalto, Santomero mildly prefer balanced. Net hold with a mild easing bias, with hawkish undercurrent for later: about 46.
- Dissent: zero formal dissents, unanimous roll call. But about eight participants stated a preference for balanced risks while accepting no change, Broaddus took an outlier easing position, and there were open differences on the output gap and inflation risks (Minehan vs Kohn/Broaddus). Broad agreement with notable reservations: about 32.
- Financial stability: Bernanke's whole speech (financial stability reports), Minehan, McTeer (foreclosures when rates rise), Guynn (real estate flipping), Kohn (extended section on asset-price distortions), Geithner, Greenspan ("one of the most important issues"). Several address at length, not dominant relative to the economic go-around: about 60.
- Forward guidance: statement keeps "patient" and the downside sentence; Greenspan says reach-for-yield will eventually move toward tightening and that they can "sit with patience for a considerable period"; Geithner and Minehan want to prepare the dial-back. Mild easing/neutral posture with tightening in the background: about 38.
- Uncertainty: employment weakness repeatedly called a puzzle ("I wish we understood this better"), Gramlich praising uncertainty bands, Kohn on uncertainty about potential growth and the gap, Poole's Wal-Mart contact "more confusing" than in ten years, Greenspan "just too close to call"; but also confident growth views (Stern, Guynn, Hoenig). Moderately elevated: about 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 40 < 50 with "slack"/"gap widened" language (satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 27 < 50 with "accommodative" (satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 36 < 50 on level reading (narrow/not too low, no stated high level; satisfied). BANKING_STRESS 28 < 50 "resilient" (satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 46: appreciation described only for the intermeeting period; the net reading is stable-to-weak, and the stated rise is acknowledged but not dominant; score kept below 50 as a net judgment, flagged low confidence. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Polarity checks.** LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter; evidence is slack, so below 50, ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence is accommodative, so low, ok. BANKING_STRESS: healthy means low, ok. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: hold with easing-tilted statement and a small easing-bias outlier, ok slightly below 50.

Divergence is real, not a polarity error.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 27 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 36: both on the loose side, consistent.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A in normal times, consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant describes Treasury market functioning problems; only custody-holding mechanics and a question about foreign holdings. All others have at least some discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Parry, "The most recent data on core inflation continue to indicate that we are close to price stability... core PCE price inflation in 2004 remains at 1 percent." Pianalto, "this long period of disinflation we've been living through is bottoming out."
- Center of gravity: Low measured inflation with growing price-pressure anecdotes and a few members worried about the upside.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe core inflation near 1 percent, only beginning to firm.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 40 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kohn, "a somewhat larger estimated output gap last quarter and this quarter and one that is closing more slowly than we'd like." Moskow, "output growth to remain above potential, eliminating resource slack sometime next year."
- Center of gravity: Slack persists and widened in the staff projection even though growth is strong.
- Direction: below neutral because the gap is negative and not yet closing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 37 ±6 (High)**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "the slack in that market is not diminishing but increasing." Parry, unemployment "would fall below 5.5 percent by the fourth quarter."
- Center of gravity: Weak hiring and rising employment gap with isolated skill shortages.
- Direction: below neutral because unemployment is in the mid-5s and payrolls are soft.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±6 (High)**
- Evidence: Moskow, "Household spending generally remains robust." Kohn, "business spending on capital equipment remains quite strong, with no signs of tapering off."
- Center of gravity: Strong consumer and equipment spending, slightly under expectations.
- Direction: above neutral because spending is described as robust and strong.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±8 (High)**
- Evidence: Santomero, "Productivity simply cannot continue to grow at the extremely rapid rate we have been seeing." Geithner, "structural acceleration of productivity growth."
- Center of gravity: Productivity gains are far above historical trend, though slowing from the peak.
- Direction: above neutral because gains are repeatedly called rapid and structural.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 27 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Geithner, "accommodative financial conditions generally" and "very favorable financial conditions". Minehan, "current very accommodative stance of monetary policy... has increased leverage across all markets."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are loose, with reach-for-yield concern.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because members call conditions accommodative and favorable.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Justification: No stress discussed; only custody-holding mechanics.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 ±10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "whether the narrowing of risk spreads on corporate bonds is justified by reduced default risk or is instead the result of investors reaching for yield." Kohn, "Domestic risk spreads aren't obviously too low for the beginning of an economic expansion", alongside "widening in a few risk spreads."
- Center of gravity: Spreads viewed as narrow-to-normal, with a small recent widening.
- Direction: below neutral on level; the widening is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the decline in equity prices"; Ferguson, equity wealth "may start to soften".
- Center of gravity: Price moves cited, no valuation view.
- Direction: at neutral because there is no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the capital in most intermediaries is high, and the system is resilient." Bies, "credit quality has improved."
- Center of gravity: Banks viewed as healthy and starting to lend again.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is called resilient and well-capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 46 ±8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Kohn, "The dollar's rise is rather surprising." Minehan, "The dollar seems bound to depreciate a bit further." Bernanke, "the downward trend in the dollar has been arrested."
- Center of gravity: Earlier weakness recently halted or partly reversed; mixed.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because most commentary references the weaker dollar over the past year, with the latest move up.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 56.** Evidence: Moskow, import prices "up sharply for products such as energy, paper fiber, and most metals"; Poole, general-merchandise prices "falling at a rate of about 1 percent". Moderate.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 54.** Evidence: Guynn, insurance and health care "on that list for some time", auditing and trucking fees up. Moderate.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 52.** Evidence: Minehan, Boston "6.3 percent increase in shelter costs"; McTeer, "Apartment rents have fallen". Mixed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.** Evidence: Parry, Blue Chip "around 2¼ or 2½ percent"; Minehan, "resiliency of inflationary expectations". Anchored, with mild upside concern.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the virtues of patience". Minehan, "we need to begin to reflect in our statements a willingness to move the funds rate up." Broaddus, "somewhat greater accommodation may be appropriate in the interim."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold with patience retained and a downside-leaning inflation-risk sentence, hawkish voices wanting balance later and one dovish outlier.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the retained statement keeps an easing-leaning tilt.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: unanimous roll call (all Yes). Stern, "a mild preference for going to balance"; Broaddus, "I may be an outlier even in the Kohn camp."
- Center of gravity: Zero formal dissents but visible differences over the wording of inflation risk and the gap.
- Direction: above the unanimity floor because of many stated preferences and a clear outlier.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "Many of the questions and comments we receive... have to do with perceived and actual risks to financial stability." Greenspan, reaching for higher yields "is probably one of the most important issues confronting us."
- Center of gravity: Recurrent theme across several speakers, with a proposal for a stability report, though secondary to the economic outlook.
- Direction: moderately above typical attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we can sit with patience for a considerable period!" and the statement left unchanged; Geithner, "we need to consider thinking about doing it soon".
- Center of gravity: Patience retained and the downside inflation sentence unchanged, with some members preparing to turn.
- Direction: below neutral because the signal remains patient.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Moskow, "I wish we understood this better." Kohn, "Uncertainty in the current situation seems greatest about the rate of growth of potential GDP."
- Center of gravity: The weak hiring puzzle and gap uncertainty are widely noted but the growth outlook is held with confidence by many.
- Direction: elevated above normal caveats, short of high.
