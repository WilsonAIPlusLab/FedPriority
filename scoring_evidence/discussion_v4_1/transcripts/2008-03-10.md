# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting dated 2008-03-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, v2 input (staff turns removed). It opens with the Chairman's framing, then members' questions to staff, then a go-round of comments, then vote read-outs. The meeting is a short-notice, evening, special-purpose meeting. Its two items are a proposed Term Securities Lending Facility (TSLF) and an expansion and extension of the ECB and SNB currency swap lines. There is no federal funds rate decision and no standard economic go-around. Participants say only a little about the real economy and inflation, in passing. This is an emergency/special-topic meeting. It is not a pure special-topic meeting with no assessment, because members describe financial conditions at length, so I score what is said and use N/A for the rest.

**Era context.** The Chairman says the financial crisis is "now in its eighth month". I do not let the date set any score. Every score below rests on the text.

**Speakers.**
- Bernanke: frames "significant deterioration in term funding markets", "self-feeding liquidity dynamics", and a "deep and abiding financial crisis, a serious slowdown, and inflation pressures".
- Kohn: reports from Basel that liquidity has dried up, there is "no price discovery", and spreads have "widened everywhere".
- Yellen: "systemic risk is large, and it's escalating very quickly".
- Rosengren: "Market conditions have deteriorated and are likely getting worse."
- Warsh: describes a "rolling capital call".
- Geithner: "the best of a bunch of bad options."
- Lacker (non-voting): opposes the TSLF on principle.
- Hoenig, Fisher, Plosser, Kroszner, Lockhart, Evans: support or raise reservations about moral hazard, regulatory reach, slippery slope and exit strategy.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as slightly above the midpoint. The only evidence is Bernanke's "inflation pressures" in a list of problems and Evans's reference to "inflation risks that come with" funds-rate adjustments. It is a passing mention, so confidence is Low and the band is wide.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as below potential. Bernanke: "the economic outlook has worsened quite significantly", "a serious slowdown", and "increased expectations of recession". There are no further details. The bright line (below potential means < 50) is satisfied with a score of 38. A slowdown with recession expectations is not a deep contraction, so I do not go below the 30s.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor markets are never mentioned, so N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: There is no discussion of spending or investment. "Serious slowdown" is a general phrase, and Lockhart's housing comment is about housing finance. N/A, with the slowdown already captured in OUTPUT_GAP.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed, N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as much tighter than normal, bordering on crisis. Evidence: "dysfunctional", "no price discovery", "vicious spiral", "systemic risk is large, and it's escalating very quickly", "financial stability is truly at stake". They stop short of "credit frozen", because the facilities are meant to prevent that. Bright line (tight means > 50) satisfied at 85.
- TREASURY_STRESS: US Treasuries are not discussed. Kohn mentions widening spreads among European government bonds, which is not US Treasury functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Participants say the level is very wide. Kohn: "risk spreads were way out of whack", "Spreads have widened everywhere". Kroszner: "risk spreads have blown out again". Yellen: "rapid escalation we're seeing in these spreads". Fisher notes that pre-crisis spreads were "fantastically low". This is a level reading of very wide spreads, not just a direction, and 83 satisfies the level rule (> 50). Spreads are not described as a complete lending freeze.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Lacker's tech-stock remark is an analogy about rationalizing intervention, not a valuation view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Stress is high, but it falls on broker-dealers and leveraged institutions more than on commercial banks. Evidence: Geithner's "an institution that is deteriorating rapidly", "primary supervisors... limits around their capacity to understand in real time", Fisher on Bear Stearns, Kohn's "Liquidity and solvency were becoming intertwined", Yellen's "liquidity problems are escalating into solvency problems", and Kohn's report that banks are refusing to advance funds. Bright line (stressed means > 50) satisfied. I score 80, short of "multiple failures".
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar funding and swap lines are discussed, but no one comments on the dollar's value or direction. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: nothing on goods, services, housing or expectations. All N/A.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: There is no funds-rate decision. The actions are liquidity-easing facilities. Bernanke says "We are coming to the limits of our monetary policy capability". Yellen says "monetary policy efforts have been seriously thwarted". Evans refers to "funds rate accommodation" and hopes for "fewer adjustments in the funds rate". The lean is dovish: support for extraordinary easing of liquidity, with no tightening voice and no inflation-driven hawkish argument. Lacker's opposition is against balance-sheet intervention, not a rate stance. I put it at moderate dovish, about 32, with a wide band because no policy rate vote took place.
- Disagreement: All three roll-call votes show only "Yes" from the nine voters, so there are zero formal dissents. Lacker, who is not on the roll, explicitly opposes. Hoenig, Plosser, Fisher, Kroszner and Kohn voice substantive reservations (moral hazard, crossing a line, exit strategy, limited supervisory reach). The disagreement is partly about framework (Lacker: no market failure). The vote is unanimous and the supporters are largely reluctant. That puts it in the "zero dissents but several reluctant supporters" band, about 35.
- Financial-stability attention: This is the whole meeting, so it is in the top band.
- Forward guidance: No explicit rate signal. Bernanke says "Whether we will have to take additional steps, we'll come back to you". Kroszner says "We may have to respond very boldly". Geithner expects more pressure to do more. This points to a readiness to ease and extend support further, with data dependence. I score it as an easing lean, about 33, with a wide band.
- Uncertainty: Very high. Geithner: "Where do we go from here? If only we knew", "none of the options out there beyond this look good". Kroszner: "I don't really understand why some of the risk spreads have blown out again". Bernanke: "almost unprecedented combination". Many participants stress that they are "not 100 percent convinced", "dubious", or that effects are "very hard to know". I put it at about 82.

**Polarity checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: higher means more above potential. The evidence says slowdown and recession, so below potential, and 38 matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter. The evidence says dysfunction and spiral, so tight, and 85 matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. The evidence says very wide spreads, and 83 matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. The evidence says deteriorating dealers and a solvency spiral, and 80 matches.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure. "Inflation pressures" are named alongside a slowdown, so slightly above 50, and 57 matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP (38, below potential, satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (85, tight, satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS (83, high level, satisfied). BANKING_STRESS (80, stressed, satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A, so the rules are not triggered.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 +-10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "a deep and abiding financial crisis, a serious slowdown, and inflation pressures". Evans: "the inflation risks that come with those adjustments might be more limited".
- Summary: Inflation is named as one of three problems but never quantified or argued over.
- Direction: above neutral, modestly, because "inflation pressures" is a worry, not a calm "near target" description.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-8, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the economic outlook has worsened quite significantly"; "increased expectations of recession". Geithner: "even in a world where there is less uncertainty about the macro outlook".
- Summary: Participants see weak activity and recession risk, with no detail on the size of the gap.
- Direction: below neutral because a "serious slowdown" and recession expectations imply slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Labor markets not mentioned.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: N/A.** Spending and investment not discussed.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 85 +-5, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Kohn: "There is really no price discovery. There is aggressive deleveraging and a flight to safety and soundness." Yellen: "systemic risk is large, and it's escalating very quickly."
- Summary: Nearly every participant describes markets as dysfunctional and in a downward spiral.
- Direction: above neutral (tight) because markets are described as dysfunctional and funding is impaired.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** US Treasury market not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 83 +-6, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Kohn: "the risk spreads were way out of whack with anything that might possibly happen to the economy"; "Spreads have widened everywhere." Kroszner: "some of the risk spreads have blown out again in the last week or two."
- Summary: Spreads are described as very wide, not just widening. Fisher's remark that pre-crisis spreads were "fantastically low" confirms that the current level is far above that benchmark.
- Direction: above neutral because the stated level is very wide and access to term funding is impaired. This is a level reading.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** No valuation view stated.

**BANKING_STRESS: 80 +-7, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Liquidity and solvency were becoming intertwined." Yellen: "liquidity problems are escalating into solvency problems." Geithner: "an institution that is deteriorating rapidly".
- Summary: Participants see the dealer and leveraged-institution sector under serious solvency pressure. Commercial banks are mentioned less directly.
- Direction: above neutral because solvency and funding stress are explicitly invoked.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Dollar funding is discussed, the exchange rate is not.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 +-9, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "We are coming to the limits of our monetary policy capability." Yellen: "Our monetary policy efforts have been seriously thwarted by the spread-widening." Evans: hopes for "fewer adjustments in the funds rate".
- Summary: Participants endorse extraordinary liquidity support. No one argues for tighter policy. Lacker's objection is to balance-sheet intervention, not to the rate stance. No policy-rate vote occurred.
- Direction: below neutral (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +-8, Moderate confidence.**
- Evidence: Lacker: "I oppose, respectfully, this measure". Plosser: "I'm a little dubious." Kroszner: "I share with Governor Kohn some of the struggles over it." All nine roll-call votes are "Yes".
- Summary: Zero formal dissents among voters, but one clear non-voting opponent on framework grounds, and a string of reluctant supporters.
- Direction: above the near-consensus range because of real reservations and Lacker's principled objection, below 46 because no voting member dissents.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 96 +-3, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Yellen: "financial stability is truly at stake here". Bernanke: "whether there are actions we can take, other than monetary policy, to break or mitigate this adverse dynamic."
- Summary: The entire meeting is about financial stability and market functioning.
- Direction: very high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 33 +-10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Whether we will have to take additional steps, we'll come back to you if we think we do or if you think we do." Kroszner: "We may have to respond very boldly in ways that we haven't done before."
- Summary: There is no rate signal. The forward signal is readiness for more easing-type action.
- Direction: below neutral (easing lean).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 82 +-6, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Geithner: "Where do we go from here? If only we knew." Kroszner: "I don't really understand why some of the risk spreads have blown out again."
- Summary: Many participants say outcomes are hard to know, the effect of the actions is unclear, and the combination of problems is "almost unprecedented".
- Direction: very high.
