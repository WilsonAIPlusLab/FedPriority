# FOMC Member Discussion v4.1 Scoring: meeting 2007-06-28 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It holds the Q&A with staff (members' questions only), the economic go-around (day 1), the policy go-around on the statement (day 2, alternative B), a long communications and projections discussion (horizon, total vs core inflation, histograms, fed funds path, minutes timing, statement governance), and a vote read-out. The vote was 10 Yes, no dissent. Staff turns are removed. Extraction looks clean: only a few unattributed fragments (e.g., repeated "MR. LACKER." turns with no question text), none garbled in a way that blocks scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2007: Committee holds at 5.25 percent after a soft first quarter, with a housing correction and subprime stress under way. I score only what members said.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Yellen: activity solid, core inflation tame but partly transitory, labor and product markets "somewhat tight today", policy "modestly restrictive", housing a "600-pound gorilla", inflation risks significant.
- Moskow, Lacker, Plosser, Pianalto, Hoenig, Warsh, Kroszner, Minehan, Geithner: inflation is the predominant risk, the core improvement is likely partly transitory, headline is high, and resource utilization is high.
- Fisher, Poole, Lockhart: more cautious on growth (soft anecdotes, housing the biggest risk).
- Kohn: economy "very near its potential", core around 2, "high levels of resource utilization".
- Mishkin: risks balanced, a "rebalancing" under way.
- Stern: sound growth, moderately restrictive policy.
- Plosser: wants a tighter path (+50bp in his forecast) and a lower inflation goal.
- Geithner and Warsh: financial markets at a "delicate moment".
- Bernanke summary: growth moderate, labor market strong, inflation risks to the upside and predominant.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core PCE is about 2.0 and edging down, but members call it partly transitory, headline is stubbornly high, resource utilization is high, and several lean toward a 1.5 to 2 goal. Members report "the predominant risk remains that inflation will not moderate". Modest-to-moderate pressure, so 60. The transitory-forecast language is not netted out.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as near to slightly above potential. Kohn: "producing very near its potential"; the statement cites "high level of resource utilization"; Yellen has markets "somewhat tight today" with forecast growth slightly below potential. Gap is at or a little above zero, so 53. This is above 50, consistent with the "high resource utilization" language and the Yellen and Kohn remarks (Kohn's "very near" is a 48-52 phrase). Net 53.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as tight. "Labor markets continue to be strong", shortages (Moskow, Hoenig, Minehan, Poole), unemployment in the "mid to low 4s" (Bernanke), "somewhat tight today" (Yellen). Level ~4.5 percent with shortage language gives 63.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending at about trend. Consumption "steady but not exuberant", investment "back to some modest trend", exports strong, but residential investment is a large drag and Fisher and Poole report flat anecdotes. Net 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Yellen, Plosser, Kohn and Kroszner. Productivity has been "disappointing" and "significantly below" the staff's 2.5 percent, with hopes that revisions will lift it. Below trend, so 38.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Warsh: "still supportive of growth, but somewhat less so"; Bernanke: "slightly restrictive but probably not substantially"; Moskow: financing for risky projects "readily available at quite favorable rates and terms". Supportive but tightening. Bright-line (supportive means below 50) satisfied. Score 41.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Discussed (Warsh): 10-year up 30 to 50bp, MOVE "fairly reasonable" over five years, term premiums "returning to more-normal levels". No dysfunction. Score 41.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The text gives level information. Spreads have widened sharply in CDS and loans (Warsh: IG CDS +7bp, HY CDS +20bp, loan CDS +65bp; Kroszner: "very significant increase in spreads"), but this is a repricing from very compressed levels: Moskow reports "few covenants", Warsh says negotiating balance is "more balanced than they have been in a very long time", and terms are only "slightly tougher". Level still below normal, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Score 42 with wide band because the move is large and fast.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Brief. "Continued ebullience of equity markets" (Minehan), strong equities (Plosser), Kohn sees downside risk to equity prices because the market's profit forecast far exceeds the staff's. Mild valuation concern. Score 40, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Plosser: "financial sector remains healthy". Pockets: Poole (banks pressuring builders, "turn off the credit spigot"), Hoenig (rising nonperforming real estate loans, farmland), Geithner (hedge fund and dealer losses). System sound with stress pockets. Score 38 (below 50 per the bright-line).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Minehan cites "the falling dollar"; Kroszner cites "lagged pass-through of the previous declines in the dollar". Weakening direction, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Score 36, low confidence, because the remarks are brief.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher = more of the named thing: labor 63 says tighter (matches "strong", shortages); financial conditions 41 says looser/supportive (matches "supportive"); credit spreads 42 says below-normal stress (matches loose terms despite widening); banking 38 says low stress (matches "healthy"); dollar 36 says weaker (matches "falling dollar").

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold at 5.25 percent. The statement keeps "predominant concern is inflation" and members repeatedly want no easing. Some members want more restraint (Plosser's forecast has +50bp; Lacker wants to be positioned to act; Hoenig finds inflation above acceptable). Others (Geithner) say no need to tighten further. Hold with a tightening bias, so 55.
- Dissent: zero dissents, 10 Yes. Policy view is broadly shared. There is real disagreement about the inflation objective (1.5 vs 2; Lacker and Plosser vs Mishkin, Minehan), the communications design (Lacker's objection to releasing projections without an objective; histograms; fed funds path; governance vote), and some disagreement on growth (Fisher's pessimism vs Plosser and Mishkin). Mostly about tactics and framework, not the policy action. Score 22.
- Financial stability attention: Bear Stearns hedge funds and CDO valuation opened the meeting (Fisher, Minehan, Geithner, Kohn); Geithner and Warsh gave extended financial-market risk assessments; Kroszner covered subprime guidance; Minehan "concerned about financial stability". Several address it meaningfully, though the meeting is dominated by the macro and communications items. Score 52.
- Forward guidance: statement keeps the inflation-predominant-risk language and "future policy adjustments depend on the evolution of the outlook". Mild tightening bias but explicitly data-dependent; markets' removal of cuts is welcomed. Score 57.
- Uncertainty: housing and financial-market risk acknowledged as significant (Yellen "appreciable angst"; Geithner "delicate moment"), natural rate uncertain (Bernanke, Kohn), but Lockhart calls it "a normal amount of uncertainty", Poole notes the forecast has barely changed. Normal to somewhat elevated. Score 34.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (>50 with "high resource utilization", and not below-potential language; Kohn "very near potential" bounds it at the low end). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 41 (<50, "supportive"). CREDIT_SPREADS 42 (<50, level favorable). BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50, "healthy"). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 36 (<50, "falling"). TREASURY_STRESS 41 (no dysfunction, 36-50 range). All satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 63: gap 10, within 15 (tight labor with growth slightly below potential). OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47: gap 6, ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 41 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 42: co-move. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury near normal, ok. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 vs sub-components (goods 58, housing 55, expectations 55): parent a bit above the sub-average, reflecting high headline energy and food pressure that is not in the sub-components; acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES is N/A: only a passing Lacker district remark that services price trends are moderate, with no real national discussion by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Pianalto: "the predominant risk going forward is that inflation will fail to moderate as expected"; Bernanke summary: "general agreement that the risks to inflation remain to the upside and remain the predominant concern".
- Center-of-gravity: core is about 2 and edging down but viewed as partly transitory, headline is high, and utilization is high.
- Direction: above neutral because members see inflation as modestly elevated with upside risks.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 ±5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "The economy is producing very near its potential"; statement text read by Bernanke: "the high level of resource utilization has the potential to sustain those pressures".
- Center-of-gravity: at or slightly above potential.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because of high utilization language, though several see growth slightly below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the labor market continues to be strong... unfilled demands for highly skilled workers and with some signs of wage pressures"; Hoenig: "significant tightening of our labor markets".
- Center-of-gravity: unemployment in the low-to-mid 4s with widespread shortages.
- Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Consumption seems likely to grow at a steady but not exuberant level"; Kohn: capital spending "pretty flat or a modest upward tilt".
- Center-of-gravity: trend-like net spending; strong exports and a recovering investment offset by the housing drag.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "recent productivity data have been disappointing"; Kohn: "productivity over the past five quarters has been growing significantly below the staff's estimate of 2½ percent".
- Center-of-gravity: weak recent productivity, with some hope of upward revision.
- Direction: below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 41 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "financial conditions are still supportive of growth, but somewhat less so"; Moskow: financing for high-risk projects "continues to be readily available at quite favorable rates and terms".
- Center-of-gravity: tightening from easy levels, still supportive.
- Direction: below neutral (looser than neutral).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 41 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "Term premiums I would characterize as returning to more-normal levels"; the MOVE index is "still fairly reasonable" over five years.
- Center-of-gravity: yields up, orderly.
- Direction: at normal, no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh: spreads "widened... high-yield CDS spreads have widened about 20; a relatively new index of loan CDS spreads has widened about 65 basis points" and "negotiations... significantly more balanced than they have been in a very long time"; Moskow: loans "made with few covenants".
- Center-of-gravity: sharp widening from very compressed levels, level still favorable.
- Direction: below neutral by level; widening is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "the continued ebullience of equity markets"; Kohn: "I also continue to see a downside risk to equity prices".
- Center-of-gravity: mild concern that valuations run ahead of profit forecasts.
- Direction: somewhat above fair value, so below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "I think the financial sector remains healthy—healthier now than it was perhaps in the early '90s"; Hoenig: "some increases in nonperforming real estate loans".
- Center-of-gravity: sound system with pockets of stress (builders, hedge funds, farmland).
- Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 36 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "the falling dollar"; Kroszner: "lagged pass-through effects of the previous declines in the dollar".
- Center-of-gravity: dollar weaker, mentioned only in passing.
- Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58 [value only]** Plosser: "prices for industrial goods continue to increase"; Lacker: manufacturing price acceleration; metals and commodity cost pressure (Minehan).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 55** Bernanke: "decided step-down in the past three or four months in the shelter component"; Kroszner: owners' equivalent rent "may come back up". Elevated but easing.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55** Hoenig: "somewhat concerned with the recent uptick in longer-term inflation expectations"; Kroszner: expectations "reasonably well contained".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "we have to position ourselves to do something about inflation when we are more certain"; Yellen: "policy is well positioned, and we should leave it exactly where it is".
- Center-of-gravity: unanimous hold, with an inflation-tilted risk assessment.
- Direction: slightly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out shows all ten voters Yes; Mishkin: "the difference between the hawks and the doves here is extremely slight"; Lacker: projections without an agreed objective are "a poor and ineffective substitute".
- Center-of-gravity: no policy dissent; disagreement over the inflation goal and communications design.
- Direction: low, with framework differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner: "With financial markets, we are at a delicate moment. The losses in subprime are still working their way through the system"; Minehan: "we also need to be concerned about financial stability".
- Center-of-gravity: several members address structured-credit and hedge-fund risk substantively, but it is not the dominant item.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: statement retained: "the Committee's predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate as expected. Future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook"; Geithner: "we need more time before we can justify shifting to a more balanced risk assessment".
- Center-of-gravity: no easing signal; inflation tilt kept; explicit data dependence.
- Direction: mildly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 34 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lockhart: "a normal amount of uncertainty"; Yellen: the housing risk "causes me appreciable angst".
- Center-of-gravity: normal caveats plus real concern about housing and financial markets.
- Direction: somewhat elevated, on the low side.
