# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) - 2004-11-10

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Document type: full transcript discussion. It has an economic go-around, a policy go-around and a roll call. It also opens with comments on Desk operations, the meeting-date memo and the dollar. It is NOT special-topic.
- Policy lean: a unanimous 25bp increase (to 2 percent) with the B-prime statement, which keeps "measured" and says nothing about December. The Chairman, Geithner, Lacker, Hoenig, Minehan, Santomero and Gramlich lean toward continued removal of accommodation and a positive real rate. Kohn, Bernanke, Yellen and Ferguson want room to signal pauses. Overall: moderate hawkish, about 64.
- Dissent: 0 formal dissents (12 of 12 Yes in the vote read-out). The non-voter Yellen preferred alternative B to B-prime. Kohn and Bernanke differ with the Chairman and Minehan on the cost skew and on communications. The Chairman notes "the first indication ... of an increased variance of opinion." Score: 34.
- Conditions: participants mostly describe slack ("way to go before the output gap is fully closed", "labor supply remains ample", "large pool of underutilized resources"), so OUTPUT_GAP is below 50. Growth is described as at or slightly above trend. Inflation is low and well contained, with cost-pressure anecdotes and upside risks from oil and the dollar. Financial conditions are accommodative (Kohn, Stern). The dollar is depreciating.
- Credit spreads: Kohn gives only a direction ("narrowing of credit spreads"). Stern's lenders say credit is "too easy", which is availability language. Score 40, Low confidence.
- Equity: Kohn reports only a price move ("rise in equity prices"), so the default range applies (47, Low). Banking stress and housing-shelter inflation are N/A.
- Uncertainty: elevated. The Chairman, Poole, Santomero and Ferguson stress wider distributions and risks that are hard to reconcile. Score: 50.
- Bright-line and coherence checks pass. OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS are both 42. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) and CREDIT_SPREADS (40) are both below 50. The parent INFLATION_PRESSURE (40) sits below the goods sub-component because members describe core inflation as low overall.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Yellen: "core inflation and longer-term inflation expectations are well contained." Kohn: "low inflation seem to have become even more entrenched." Santomero: "inflation remains low." Bernanke: "core inflation will remain low and stable."
- Center of gravity: inflation is low and below a 2 percent-type benchmark (core PCE cited around 1.5 percent). There are cost-pressure anecdotes (Moskow, Minehan "hyperinflation" outlier, Santomero pricing power) and upside risks (Geithner, Gramlich), and Lacker says "the disinflation risk continues to abate."
- Direction: below neutral, because members describe inflation as low and contained.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High]
- Evidence: Moskow: "the economy has a way to go before the output gap is fully closed." Kohn: "the output gap has not closed much at all since early this year." Yellen: "slack still remaining in the economy." Bernanke: "the economy still has some room to grow."
- Center of gravity: slack remains, but growth is at or slightly above trend and the gap is closing slowly. Guynn's "no significant output gaps" is a minority view.
- Direction: below neutral. Under the bright-line rule, "slack remains" means the score must be below 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Yellen: "labor supply remains ample, and we hear little about worker shortages." Bernanke: "The employment-to-population ratio remains low. The number of people working part time for economic reasons remains high." Against this, Moskow: "it is no longer as easy to find qualified workers." Poole: the software labor market is "brutally more competitive."
- Center of gravity: the market is below full employment and improving after the strong October report, with skilled-worker shortages in pockets.
- Direction: below neutral, because slack and modest wage growth dominate.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 51 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Moskow: "Consumer spending has rebounded." Guynn: "Consumer spending remains solid, and investment spending continues to make a good contribution." Against this, Lacker: "continuing weakness in business investment spending relative to what we might have expected." Moskow and Minehan cite business caution tied to Sarbanes-Oxley.
- Center of gravity: solid consumption and strong housing, offset by cautious business investment. The net is about at trend.
- Direction: at neutral.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 55 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Chairman: "a very substantial and surprising growth in productivity," but the structural rate is uncertain (2.75 vs 1.75 percent). Geithner: "productivity growth slowing somewhat before returning to ... around 2½ percent." Bies and Lacker expect slower short-run productivity. Pianalto expects productivity "higher than in the staff's baseline."
- Center of gravity: the trend is still seen as above the historical norm, but it is decelerating and uncertain.
- Direction: modestly above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Kohn: "financial conditions broadly defined have become even more accommodative on balance since our last meeting." Stern (reporting housing lenders): "very favorable financial conditions ... credit conditions had become too easy."
- Center of gravity: conditions are loose and accommodative. Policy itself is called "extremely accommodative" (Guynn), but that describes policy, not conditions, and was not used as evidence here.
- Direction: below neutral. Under the bright-line rule, "accommodative" means the score must be below 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low]
- Evidence: Olson: "longer-term interest rates have actually declined, particularly the ten-year Treasury yields." The Chairman discusses TIPS signals, mentioning liquidity only in passing.
- Center of gravity: only yield movements are discussed, with no dysfunction.
- Direction: below 50 (normal functioning).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±10 [Low]
- Evidence: Kohn: "a narrowing of credit spreads" (direction only). Stern: lenders "felt that credit conditions had become too easy," with "lower down-payment requirements" (availability). Geithner: the dollar decline came "without significant negative effects on risk premiums."
- Center of gravity: the score rests partly on availability language. Spreads are narrowing and credit is described as easy.
- Direction: below neutral. No stated wide level; narrowing plus easy availability.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±6 [Low]
- Evidence: Kohn: "the rise in equity prices." Ferguson only conditionally: "if asset prices continue to rise inappropriately."
- Center of gravity: a price move with no valuation view, so the rubric default applies on the side of the rise.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
- Banking-system health is not discussed. Stern's lenders' remark about "potential difficulties going forward" and Holcomb's note on Texas commercial loan growth are anecdotes about credit, not assessments of the banking system.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 28 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Kohn: "The dollar is already under downward pressure." Minehan: "a somewhat faster pace of dollar depreciation." Gramlich: "The dollar has dropped since our last meeting." Yellen: "the dollar has fallen somewhat."
- Center of gravity: depreciation is widely noted. Kohn's "still-elevated dollar" (level) tempers the score slightly.
- Direction: below 50. Under the bright-line rule, depreciation means the score must be below 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 55
- Evidence: Moskow: suppliers "finding it somewhat easier to pass higher costs along," and "the weaker dollar is raising the wholesale prices of imported goods." Geithner: "Core finished goods in the PPI ... still showing fairly high rates of increase." Against this, Pianalto: "Retail prices and wage growth remain subdued." Bernanke: "non-energy commodity prices have declined."

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 48
- Evidence: Holcomb: malpractice premiums "dropped by 8 to 17 percent ... potential to restrain medical cost inflation." This is thin, regional evidence.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- House-price appreciation is mentioned (Santomero, Stern) as asset prices. Rents and shelter costs are not discussed.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52
- Evidence: Lacker: "inflation and inflation expectations well anchored." Kohn: "firmly anchored inflation expectations." Against this, Geithner: "Inflation expectations at the one-year and up to the five-year horizon have moved up again materially." Holcomb: long-term expectations "anchored at or above 2.5 percent."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Chairman: "we are far better positioned to get the funds rate up as fast as we can this year." Hoenig: "as long as the rate is below ... neutral ... I don't think we should be pausing." Against this, Kohn: "we're going to have to signal the markets ... that we are not on a treadmill." Bernanke: "we should probably begin soon to signal to the public that pauses may occur."
- Center of gravity: the hike is unanimous and the statement gives no pause hint. Most members favor continued removal of accommodation, while a sizable group wants to prepare for pauses.
- Direction: above neutral (tightening).

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 34 ±7 [High]
- Evidence: the vote read-out shows all 12 voters Yes. Yellen (non-voter): "My own preference would be to move today but to adopt alternative B rather than B′." Chairman: "the first indication in this Committee of an increased variance of opinion." Bernanke: "I disagree with President Minehan; I think we should be trying to provide guidance."
- Center of gravity: no formal dissents, with two or three reservations about the statement and communications.
- Direction: below neutral, in the moderate-differences range.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Kohn: "The odds on a disorderly decline are very, very low, but they're not zero." Geithner: "the vulnerability ... of a substantial change in the terms on which nonresidents are willing to finance us." Stern: housing "bubble" concerns and "credit pendulum had swung too far." Chairman: "We could face problems of balance sheet losses if the change is rapid."
- Center of gravity: several participants meaningfully address dollar/external-financing risk, housing credit and balance-sheet risk.
- Direction: moderate attention.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: the "measured" pace language is retained (Gramlich quotes it). Chairman: "The best thing that can happen is for the market to perceive that we are implying another rate increase in December." Against this, many want to retire "measured" soon to allow pauses (Moskow, Bies, Kohn, Gramlich).
- Center of gravity: there is a mild tightening bias with no pause hint now, but the forward signal is expected to soften.
- Direction: above neutral.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±8 [High]
- Evidence: Poole: "My own probability distribution is considerably more spread out." Ferguson: "the risks and uncertainties around the forecast are quite large, and I find them very difficult to reconcile." Chairman: "with the increased uncertainties ... our forecast is going to be wrong." Santomero: "a good deal of uncertainty on both sides." Against this, Geithner: "risks as roughly balanced." Kohn: "compression in the range of risks."
- Center of gravity: several participants emphasize unusually wide uncertainty about productivity, the dollar and the policy path.
- Direction: elevated.
