# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion, transcript, meeting 1995-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds the Chair's and members' questions on the Mexican swap line and Domestic Desk operations, a few questions to the staff, the round of District and national economic reports, a staff (Kohn) presentation of "opportunistic versus deliberate" strategies followed by members' questions on it, the Chairman's long statement, the policy go-around, and a roll-call read-out. Procedural and swap-line items are ignored. Staff content is ignored.

**Era context.** Late 1995: a mature expansion after a strong third quarter (about 5 percent growth cited), a weaker fourth quarter, CPI inflation near 3 percent, unemployment 5.5-5.6 percent, a fall in long-term yields and a strong stock market, and a government shutdown and budget standoff. The scoring below rests on what the speakers said.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), McDonough, Lindsey, Boehne, Parry, Minehan, Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Moskow, McTeer, Hoenig, Guynn, Kelley, Yellen, Phillips, Jordan, Blinder.

**Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- Inflation pressure: Participants see inflation as contained, about 3 percent CPI, "remarkably subdued" (Boehne), "remarkably well contained" (Kelley), "more progress than we have a right to have expected" (Phillips). Hawks (Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan) say 3 percent is not price stability, which is a complaint about the level rather than about pressure building. Expectations are described as falling. Net: at to slightly above the midpoint, near 50 (3 percent CPI above a stability goal, but no building pressure).
- Output gap: Participants see the economy at potential. Hoenig: "slowing to potential," resources "at or near capacity." Broaddus: "at least in the neighborhood of potential." Moskow: "near its potential." Kelley: "growth near potential." Yellen sees a later bias to below-trend growth. Score 48-52 band, so 49.
- Labor: Many Districts report tight labor markets and shortages (Melzer, Stern, Parry, Moskow, Hoenig). Others say shortages are scattered or abating (Guynn, Phillips) and wages are not consistently under pressure. Unemployment is 5.5-5.6 percent, "full employment" in the rubric's near-NAIRU band, though the tight anecdotes pull up. Score 48.
- Consumption and investment: Consumers are cautious and holiday retail is weak or "horrible" in many Districts (McDonough, Boehne, Minehan, McTeer, Jordan). Business fixed investment is strong but decelerating. Housing is mixed and supported by lower rates. Inventories are building. Net slightly below trend, 45.
- Productivity: Greenspan argues technology and capital turnover are raising efficiency and that measured productivity is understated. Stern: "productivity has done better than anticipated." Kelley: "Productivity is still growing very nicely." Modestly above trend, 57.

**Financial dimensions.**
- Financial conditions: Participants say credit is "quite ample" (Kelley) and "readily available" (Melzer). Long rates have fallen sharply, stocks are strong, and the cost of capital is low (Phillips). The real funds rate is called restrictive, but that is policy, not conditions. Conditions are supportive, so below 50. Score 38.
- Treasury stress: The bond market is discussed only in terms of rally and yield level (long bond 6.22 percent, "down 10/32"). There is no dysfunction. Score 40, low confidence.
- Credit spreads: Spreads are not discussed. Credit availability is described as ample, and there is no widening. Score 36 in the "ample credit availability" band, low confidence.
- Equity valuation: McDonough says the stock market "by any historical standard is overvalued." Greenspan says "we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble" and notes a decline in real equity premiums. Minehan echoes the bubble worry. Phillips is "not quite as pessimistic." Score 22, elevated valuation concern.
- Banking stress: The only direct remark is Melzer's "District banks continue to post record profits," with loan demand healthy. That implies low stress. Score 30, low confidence.
- Dollar: Yellen mentions the earlier depreciation of the dollar boosting 1996 exports and a stable dollar assumed after that. McTeer mentions the weak peso. Brief, past depreciation, assumed stable. Score 40, low confidence.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: soft steel prices (Moskow), no net movement in input or final prices (Guynn), competition precluding output price increases (Minehan), commodities well behaved (Kelley). Score 40. Services: not discussed, N/A. Housing: not discussed, N/A. Expectations: Parry and Yellen report expectations down 50 basis points, while Melzer says long-run forecasts of 3 percent persist and "3 percent inflation expectations are too high." Net near 48.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Chairman proposes a 25 basis point cut with a symmetric directive and no discount rate change. A majority supports it, several reluctantly (Minehan, Moskow, Hoenig, Melzer would "prefer alternative B"). Stern and Broaddus favor no change. Jordan wants "a little more aggressive." Parry notes that a reversal may be needed in 1996. Moderately dovish for the Committee, softened by the reluctance and the caution. Score 37.
- Dissent: The vote is unanimous among the 11 voters, so there are no formal dissents. Minehan says "I don't think I will dissent." Moskow says he does not feel strongly enough to dissent. Hoenig and Melzer accept it reluctantly. Stern and Broaddus (non-voters) favor holding. There is also a framework split of opportunistic versus deliberate and a split over the Chairman's "new world" hypothesis (Minehan, Blinder, Melzer not signing on). Several reservations, no dissents, differences of substance on the strategy. Score 36.
- Financial stability emphasis: The bubble worry is raised by the Chairman, McDonough, Minehan and Boehne. Stock-wealth effects on consumption are discussed at length (Lindsey, Yellen, Greenspan). It is context for the decision, not the main topic. Score 30.
- Forward guidance: The directive is symmetric. Greenspan says "it is conceivable that we may have to go lower" but that no judgment is needed now. Parry says the cut may have to be reversed. Lindsey says a backup in long rates would make further easing difficult. Mild easing lean, data dependent. Score 42.
- Uncertainty: Boehne: "pivotal periods where people are less certain." Broaddus: the situation is "unusually uncertain." Yellen: a "difficult and tricky judgment call." Greenspan's long-run hypothesis is explicitly uncertain. Others are fairly confident in a Greenbook-like path. Score 42.

**Polarity checks.** Higher output gap = more above potential; the evidence says at potential, so about 50. Higher financial conditions = tighter; the evidence says ample credit and low long rates, so below 50. Higher equity score = more equity stress or concern about valuation per the rubric's table, where overvaluation or bubble sits at the low end (1-30); the evidence says overvalued, so the score is low. Higher credit-spreads = more stress; the evidence says ample credit, so below 50. Hawkish/dovish: a cut is dovish, so below 50.

**Bright-line check.** Output gap in 48-52 (at or near potential). Financial conditions below 50 ("supportive," ample credit). Credit spreads below 50 (ample availability, no widening). Banking stress below 50 (record profits). Dollar: past depreciation, now stable, below 50 (40). Treasury: no dysfunction, 40.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. Output gap 49 and labor 48 are within 15 points of each other.
2. Output gap 49 and consumption/investment 45 are within 15 points.
3. Financial conditions 38 and credit spreads 36 co-move.
4. Financial conditions 38 and Treasury stress 40 are fine in a normal-market period.
5. Inflation pressure 50 and expectations 48 and goods 40: consistent with a modest weighted average, with the 3 percent level pulling the parent up slightly.

**N/A justification.** Inflation services and housing were not discussed by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Boehne, "price pressures remain remarkably subdued"; Broaddus, 3 percent "is not price stability." Summary: contained but at a level the hawks consider too high. Direction: at neutral because the low-pressure reports are balanced by the stated 3 percent level.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 49 +-4 (High).** Evidence: Hoenig, "an economy slowing to potential"; Broaddus, "in the neighborhood of potential GDP." Summary: the economy is seen at potential. Direction: at neutral (within 48-52).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Stern, "labor markets in the District are tight"; Phillips, "At 5.6 percent unemployment... wage rates have not consistently been under pressure." Summary: unemployment near the natural rate with scattered shortages. Direction: slightly below neutral to neutral because the level is 5.5-5.6 percent and wages are not under consistent pressure.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Minehan, "consumers remained wary and uncertain"; McDonough, "business fixed investment has held up extraordinarily well." Summary: soft holiday retail offset by strong but slowing investment. Direction: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley, "Productivity is still growing very nicely"; Stern, "productivity has done better than anticipated." Summary: several members and the Chairman see productivity better than measured. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley, "Credit availability still seems to be quite ample"; Phillips, "the cost of capital is low." Summary: credit is ample and long rates have fallen. Direction: below neutral (loose and supportive).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan, "The cash market for the long bond is at 6.22 percent"; Melzer, "The rally in longer-term bonds is mostly good news." Summary: yield movements only, with no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Melzer, "Aggregate credit remains readily available"; Kelley, "Credit availability... quite ample." Summary: ample credit and no stress, spreads not discussed directly. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 22 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "the stock market by any historical standard is overvalued"; Greenspan, "we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble." Summary: valuations seen as stretched, with Phillips a mild dissenter. Direction: well below neutral on the rubric's overvaluation scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Melzer, "District banks continue to post record profits." Summary: one clear statement of healthy banks. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Yellen, "the lagged effects of the depreciation of the dollar, which should stimulate exports in 1996"; "a stable dollar." Summary: past depreciation, now assumed stable. Direction: below neutral (modest weakness to stable).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 40.** Evidence: Moskow, steel "prices are soft"; Guynn, "no net movement in either input or final goods prices."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Evidence: Yellen, expectations "fallen about 1/2 percentage point"; Melzer, "3 percent inflation expectations are too high."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we rightfully should be moving the funds rate lower... I would go 25 basis points now"; Minehan, "my basic inclination would be not to change policy." Summary: a majority supports a modest cut, several reluctantly. Direction: below 50 (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "I don't feel strongly enough to dissent"; Minehan, "I don't think I will dissent over 25 basis points." Summary: a unanimous vote hides several reluctant supporters and a real strategy split, with no formal dissents. Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "The real danger is that we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble"; Boehne, "the asset inflation problem, the bubble effect, is one that we ought to take into account." Summary: a few members and the Chair give it meaningful attention as context. Direction: limited discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I would go symmetrical... it is conceivable that we may have to go lower"; Parry, the cut "may have to be followed by a move in the other direction." Summary: symmetric directive with a mild easing lean. Direction: below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Broaddus, "the current situation and the very short-term outlook are unusually uncertain"; Yellen, "a difficult and tricky judgment call." Summary: several members flag elevated uncertainty, others are confident. Direction: somewhat elevated.
