# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2002-12-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: Q&A turns during the staff briefings, the economic go-around (Jordan, Guynn, Santomero, Minehan, Broaddus, Moskow, Parry, Stern, McTeer, Kohn, Hoenig, Gramlich, McDonough, Olson, Poole, Ferguson, Bies, Bernanke), a short policy round (Chairman recommends holding at 1 1/4 percent with the balanced-risks assessment), and a vote read-out. Staff answers are removed, so many questions appear without answers. I ignored them. No garbled passages were found that affect scoring.

**Era context.** Late 2002. The fed funds rate is 1 1/4 percent after a 50 bp cut at the previous meeting. Unemployment is 6.0 percent. Participants discuss a "soft spot", a possible jobless recovery, deflation risk, strong productivity, Iraq and other geopolitical risk, corporate-governance fallout, and likely fiscal stimulus. I scored from the text only.

**Speakers and views.**
- Regional presidents report soft but slightly improving conditions, cautious capex, strong housing, weak commercial real estate and slack labor markets.
- Kohn, Ferguson, Bernanke, Poole and Jordan are cautiously optimistic and stress improved financial markets and narrower spreads.
- Broaddus sees risks skewed to the downside.
- Gramlich flags a possible future "punch bowl" problem.
- Everyone supports holding at 1 1/4 percent.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Minehan says "by any measure, inflation continues quite low". Kohn cites "an ongoing lack of pricing power". Gramlich says "we may get too much disinflation". Parry's core PCE forecast is 1 1/2 percent next year. Deflation risk is a live topic but not acute. Score about 28.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Minehan sees "considerable excess capacity". Gramlich notes unemployment is projected above NAIRU throughout. Guynn and Broaddus report excess capacity in many industries. Bright line (below potential means < 50): satisfied. Score about 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Unemployment is 6 percent (Broaddus), with a "jobless recovery" and "slack" labor markets (Hoenig, Moskow). The 5.5-7 percent band gives 31-45. Score about 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly below trend. Consumers are cautious and price conscious, and holiday sales are modest. Housing is strong. Business capex is restrained but there are early signs of an E&S pickup (Kohn). Net about 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend. Poole cites "a year of remarkable numbers", Bies says "Productivity has been very strong", and Broaddus and Parry cite strong gains and a "productivity surge". Score about 72.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Looser than neutral. Policy is described as accommodative (Ferguson, Hoenig), equities hold gains, spreads have narrowed, and issuance has resumed. There is a counterweight: spreads are still elevated and banks are tightening standards. Bright line (accommodative means < 50): satisfied. Score about 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as a market-functioning topic. Olson mentions the ten-year only as a refinancing correlate. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction is narrowing (Kohn "bond spreads have continued to drop substantially"; Minehan "narrowed slightly"; Moskow "decline in risk spreads"). The level is stated once by Ferguson: "Spreads, while still elevated... have come off their earlier highs". Level governs, so the score is just above 50. Bright line (stated elevated level means > 50): satisfied. Score 56, with moderate uncertainty because the level language is thin.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss price moves, volatility and recovery from the October lows, but give no valuation view. The default is 45-55. Score 52, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Guynn says "bank credit quality is holding firm" and Ferguson says household credit quality is "basically stable". McDonough notes bankers "tightening up on their lending standards" and some rising delinquencies. Jordan's bankers cite narrow margins. Healthy with minor concerns. Bright line (sound means < 50): satisfied. Score about 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed by members. N/A.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Hold at 1 1/4 percent with balanced risks. Dovish-leaning voices are Broaddus (downside risk, wants a signal) and Minehan (too much excess capacity). Hawkish-side voices are Poole and Bernanke (upside risks) and Gramlich (future tightening). The net is balanced with a slight dovish tilt: 46.
- Disagreement: The vote is unanimous, with no dissents. Broaddus calls his own view "an outlier" on risks, and Minehan voices a mild reservation. Score 9.
- Financial stability: Several participants discuss financial conditions meaningfully (Kohn, Ferguson, Bernanke, Minehan, Moskow). Poole mentions the UK housing bubble. It is context for the outlook, not the focus. Score 42.
- Forward guidance: Balanced risks are retained. Broaddus worries the market doubts the Greenbook assumption of a hold until mid-2004 and wants a signal. Ferguson says "we should be prepared to be flexible... aggressive in either direction". Near neutral with a slight easing tilt: 46.
- Uncertainty: Geopolitical, fiscal and productivity uncertainty are cited (Santomero, Moskow, Broaddus, Ferguson "haze or uncertainty"; Kohn "plenty of downside risks"). Many are also fairly comfortable with the central forecast. Elevated but not extreme: 50.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence says low, so the score is below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and spreads are narrowing but still described as elevated, so the score is just above 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and banks are described as sound, so the score is below 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and conditions are accommodative and improving, so the score is below 50.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 33 and LABOR 33 move together.
- OUTPUT_GAP 33 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 42 differ by 9, which is within the 15-point tolerance.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 and CREDIT_SPREADS 56 differ somewhat. Policy and equities are accommodative, while the spread level is still described as elevated. This is explained by the mixed evidence and not contradictory.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.
- Inflation sub-components are not discussed, so there is no parent mismatch.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH and all four inflation sub-components were not raised by participants. Poole's UK house prices and the Latin America comments are not about US inflation or the dollar.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "by any measure, inflation continues quite low"; Gramlich, "we may get too much disinflation". Summary: Low inflation with deflation-risk discussion. Direction: below neutral because core PCE is around 1 1/2 percent with no pricing power.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Minehan, "plagued by considerable excess capacity"; Gramlich, projected unemployment "in excess of NAIRU throughout the recovery". Summary: Meaningful slack. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, "rise in the unemployment rate from 5.7 to 6 percent"; McTeer, "basically jobless recovery". Summary: Slack labor market. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Santomero, "consumers continue to be cautious and price conscious"; Kohn, "pickup in equipment and software spending has already been under way". Summary: Soft consumers and capex with housing strength and early investment pickup. Direction: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Poole, "a year of remarkable numbers"; Bies, "Productivity has been very strong". Summary: Strongly above trend. Direction: above.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Ferguson, "financial conditions much more consistent with... the baseline", with "Accommodative monetary policy"; Kohn, equity gains held and long-term financing facilitated. Summary: Improving and generally supportive. Direction: looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Ferguson, "Spreads, while still elevated... certainly have come off their earlier highs"; Kohn, "bond spreads have continued to drop substantially". Summary: Narrowing from high levels but still described as elevated. Direction: just above normal on the stated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Kohn, equity prices "generally have held the gains registered since early October"; Ferguson, volatility "declined noticeably from the October peak". Summary: Price and volatility commentary only, with no valuation view. Direction: near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn, "bank credit quality is holding firm"; McDonough, bankers "tightening up on their lending standards" with delinquencies up "some". Summary: Sound system with minor concerns. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**Inflation sub-components: all N/A.** Not discussed by participants.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Chairman Greenspan, "stay where we are at 1 1/4 percent... retain the balanced risks assessment"; Broaddus, view on downside risks "clearly an outlier". Summary: A unanimous hold with balanced risks, a slight dovish tilt from Broaddus and Minehan, and upside-risk comments from Poole and Bernanke. Direction: just below balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 9 +-4 (High).** Evidence: all participants say "I support your recommendation", and the roll call shows 12 yes votes. Summary: A near-consensus meeting with mild differences on risk balance. Direction: very low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn's long passage on risk premiums and issuance; Bernanke, "keep a close eye on the risk premiums and other measures of stress". Summary: Several members address market conditions meaningfully, but as outlook context. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 46 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Ferguson, "prepared to be flexible going forward"; Broaddus, signal that "we're at least aware of these risks". Summary: Neutral and data dependent, with a mild easing bias. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "no one knows how the situation in Iraq and the Middle East will play out"; Broaddus, "there is a lot of uncertainty" on productivity. Summary: Several participants cite geopolitical, fiscal and productivity uncertainty, while many are comfortable with the central forecast. Direction: elevated but not extreme.
