# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2014-04-30 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) a long first session on monetary policy normalization (operating framework: ON RRP, IOER, term deposits, the funds rate as target), mostly members' questions to staff plus a go-round on seven staff questions; (b) a short financial-developments / financial-stability question round, including an informal go-round on financial-stability worries; (c) the economic go-round (Williams, Rosengren, Fisher, Lockhart, Lacker on day 1; Bullard through Powell on day 2) with the Chair's summary of it; (d) the policy go-round on alternative B and a unanimous roll-call. Block (a) is a special topic with no economic assessment, so it informs only the financial-stability, uncertainty, forward-guidance and dissent dimensions. The economic dimensions come from (b) and (c).

**2. Era context.** Spring 2014, QE3 being tapered by $10B per meeting, funds rate near zero, unemployment 6.7%, first-quarter GDP just reported very weak (weather), core inflation well under 2%. I did not let this override the text. The text agrees with it.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Yellen (opening, summary of the economic round, summary of the policy round), Vice Chairman Dudley, Governors Tarullo, Stein, Powell, and Presidents Rosengren, Fisher, Bullard, Evans, Lacker, Kocherlakota, Plosser, Williams, Lockhart, Pianalto, George.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (target) because core PCE is "just 1.3 percent" over six months (Evans), "inflation continues to run low but has stabilized" (Bullard), Kocherlakota sees PCE inflation "below target until 2018". Several (Pianalto, George, Dudley, Plosser) see it stabilizing and drifting up, and CPI firmed in March. Stabilization is a forecast and a small improvement, not elevated pressure. Magnitude: "well below target", "painfully slow progress" fits 26-40 band. Score about 31.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as below potential because "considerable labor market slack that could be reduced through higher levels of aggregate demand" (Tarullo), Yellen's summary of "substantial remaining slack", and Evans: growth needs "highly accommodative policy". Rosengren says growth is "poised to grow above potential" this year, which is a growth-rate statement, and the level gap is still negative. Powell sees growth in the high 2s as "pretty close to the speed limit". Score 38.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Unemployment 6.7% (Plosser), wages muted (Williams: comp at about 2% vs a normal 3.5-4%), high part-time for economic reasons and long-term unemployment, "weak" labor demand (Dudley). Local pockets of tightness (Fisher's labor shortages in energy and IT, George's wage pressures, Bakken 1.5% unemployment) are noted but called "small pockets" (Kocherlakota). UE 5.5-7% band, improving trend does not raise the level score. Score 36.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Near but slightly below trend. Q1 was a weather-driven disappointment, March rebounded ("retail sales, manufacturing, consumer confidence" Powell), growth of about 3% expected. Housing is a "significant disappointment" (Powell), business fixed investment fell in Q1 and is "unusually weak relative to fundamentals" (Dudley). Strong auto sales and optimistic CEOs offset. Net 46.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing mentions (Williams, Lockhart: "even as productivity growth improves"). No assessment. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as loose/supportive: "financial conditions on balance" unchanged (Williams), "reach-for-yield behavior", low volatility ("incredibly low volatility environment", Dudley), "froth in fixed-income markets" (Powell), leveraged-loan terms deteriorating (Yellen summary). Concern is about excess ease and a future tightening at liftoff, not current tightness. Bright line: loose means below 50. Score 31.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields are mentioned only as unchanged (Dudley) or in a forward-rate decomposition (Bullard). The ON RRP / money-fund run-risk talk is hypothetical and about the operating framework. No dysfunction discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is described as low: Fisher "low spreads on triple-C or junk credit", Stein "corporate bond spreads have been coming down / narrowing", Yellen's summary links this to reach for yield. Direction plus stated low level means below 50 under the level rule. Score 25.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuations are seen as rich: Fisher "significant, if not hypervaluation", Dudley "high stock market valuation relative to book" and low vol supporting asset valuations "above average", Powell on private-equity prices "getting stretched". Only a few speakers, mostly mixed ("not as bad as 2007"). Score 30.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks appear only through leverage-ratio/FDIC costs for arbitrage, Plosser's note that bankers are more optimistic, and Pianalto on tight mortgage standards. No assessment of bank health or stress. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a mention of the yen's decline (Evans) and Hong Kong's peg. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more credit stress; evidence says spreads are low/narrow, so less stress; score 25 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says loose; 31 matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more stress/undervaluation concern; evidence is overvaluation concern, so below 50; 30 matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Unanimous support for alternative B: another $10B taper, forward guidance unchanged. Dovish voices: Evans ("we need to be accommodative"), Rosengren ("maintain a highly accommodative stance"; risks on the downside), Kocherlakota (inflation under target to 2018), Williams. More hawkish voices: Plosser (raise "sooner than in the Tealbook", shrink the balance sheet before lift-off, rates may need to move faster), George (concerned about unusually low rates for an extended period), Lacker (mild), Lockhart wants caution against too-early liftoff. Chair says liftoff likely "sometime next year". Majority holds accommodation while tapering: lean dovish to balanced. Score 40.
- Disagreement: Zero dissents in the roll call (9 yes). Reservations: Plosser and George on paragraph 6 language and r*, Kocherlakota on the communications framework, Lacker on an unspecified statement portion. The normalization go-round has real differences (Bullard LIFO / repo target vs Lacker/Kocherlakota IOER-only vs Dudley/Powell floor system) but they are tactical and openly tentative. Economic assessments are very similar ("my outlook is unchanged"). Score 24.
- Financial stability: Large share of text. The ON RRP run-risk debate (Kocherlakota, Dudley, Stein, Bullard "first-order concern", Plosser), a briefing Q&A on leveraged lending, low volatility, private equity (Fisher, Powell, Lockhart), a go-round on housing exuberance abroad (Rosengren, Bullard, Evans), and policy-round comments (Stein on implied volatility, Dudley on the dots and low vol). Yellen's summary lists financial-stability risks. Prominent but does not dominate the economic assessment. Score 66.
- Forward guidance: Statement keeps "below normal for some time" language; a slow taper continues; Williams wants guidance signalling "highly accommodative future path". Offsetting: Plosser and George want a clearer, less dovish reaction function, Yellen says liftoff likely next year and normalization communications in September. Net: easing lean with gradual-normalization planning. Score 25.
- Uncertainty: Many "tentative"/"learning by doing" remarks on normalization, "high degree of uncertainty about how market rates will respond" (Rosengren), "we cannot know exactly" (Yellen); on the economy, residual uncertainty about the post-weather trend (Dudley, Lockhart, Evans) and external risks (China, Ukraine). Yet forecasts are largely unchanged and described as in the Tealbook's ballpark. Elevated-ish but not unusual. Score 40.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 (< 50, slack/below potential language): OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 31 (loose/supportive): OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 25 (stated low level): OK. BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, so no violation.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs LABOR 36: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 46: gap of 8, within 15; consumption is near trend because of the rebound, while slack remains in labor. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 31 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 25: co-move, both loose. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 31 vs sub-components (goods 38, services 42, housing 56, expectations 48): weighted average about 43; parent is lower because participants repeatedly describe the headline outcome as "well below target" while sub-component evidence is mostly about expected firming. Parent is anchored to the explicit "1.3 percent run rate" language; acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment, only passing mentions. TREASURY_STRESS: no market-function discussion beyond unchanged yields. BANKING_STRESS: bank health not discussed. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: FX not discussed. INFLATION_SERVICES etc. are scored below only because individual members gave specific price observations.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 31 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: "the Tealbook's estimate over the past six months is still just 1.3 percent. That's really not much to get excited about." Bullard: "Inflation continues to run low but has stabilized."
- Summary: Inflation is well below target, with stabilization and expected gradual rise.
- Direction: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because members describe a core run rate far under 2% and "painfully slow progress".

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "there remains considerable labor market slack that could be reduced through higher levels of aggregate demand." Evans: growth "needs the continued support of highly accommodative policy."
- Summary: Slack persists though closing as growth returns to about 3%.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 36 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "The unemployment rate remained at a steady 6.7 percent." Williams: compensation has "fallen well short" of the 3.5-4% pace, "most measures centering around 2 percent."
- Summary: Unemployment elevated, wages muted, improving with pockets of shortages only.
- Direction: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because of the 6.7% rate, part-time and long-term unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "Housing has become a significant disappointment"; "I see economic growth rebounding... growth in the high 2 percent range." Dudley: business fixed investment "declined" in Q1 despite strong fundamentals.
- Summary: Weather-hit Q1 then rebound to roughly trend, with housing and capex soft.
- Direction: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because housing and investment lag while consumption is solid.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A** (not assessed).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 31 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "The risk of continued rising froth in fixed-income markets, followed by a correction..." Dudley: "we're living through this incredibly low volatility environment."
- Summary: Conditions are loose and accommodative, with worry about complacency and a liftoff tightening.
- Direction: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because markets are described as calm, spreads low and risk appetite elevated.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no market-functioning discussion).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "when you talk about low spreads on triple-C or junk credit, bear in mind that it's a low spread over an extremely low historical nominal yield." Stein: "Corporate bond spreads have been coming down."
- Summary: Spreads are low and compressing, with leveraged-loan terms deteriorating.
- Direction: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because the stated level is low (tight).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +-9 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "this period of what I consider significant, if not hypervaluation." Dudley: low volatility "supports asset valuations that are probably going to be above average."
- Summary: Valuations seen as rich, voiced mainly by a few members.
- Direction: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral because members see overvaluation, not depressed prices.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** (bank health not discussed).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** (not discussed).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Evans: auto contacts see "softer pricing"; "steelmakers were grousing about low prices." Dudley: ex-petroleum import prices were "unusually soft," base effects.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 42.** Dudley: prices for "medical goods and services... were unusually weak" but are expected to reverse; wages subdued.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 56.** George: "shelter costs in the CPI are up 2.7 percent... the fastest pace in over five years." Dudley: owners' equivalent rent firming; Bullard notes multifamily supply pressuring rents.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** George: "continued stable longer-term inflation expectations."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "we should maintain a highly accommodative stance for monetary policy." Plosser: "we will need to consider raising the federal funds rate sooner than in the Tealbook."
- Summary: Unanimous continuation of the taper with accommodation; a hawkish minority wants earlier balance-sheet shrinkage and clearer rate guidance.
- Direction: Lean is slightly dovish, since policy remains highly accommodative and most want no change beyond the taper.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll call: nine "Yes", no dissent. Kocherlakota: "I continue to have concerns about this framework"; Plosser on paragraph 6: "I still have them."
- Summary: Unanimous vote with scattered reservations and tactical differences on normalization design.
- Direction: Disagreement is low-to-modest.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 66 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Bullard: financial stability "is a first-order concern for the Committee." Yellen's summary: "financial-stability risks in that we're seeing evidence of reach-for-yield behavior in fixed-income markets, including leveraged loan markets."
- Summary: Prominent theme in the framework debate, the briefing Q&A and the policy round, though not rivaling the economic assessment.
- Direction: Attention is high regardless of direction.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 25 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "forward guidance that signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate." George on paragraph 6: rates "below" the long-run level "for some time", asking for clarity.
- Summary: Guidance stays accommodative and gradual; the push for clarity and normalization planning (liftoff "sometime next year") adds only a mild hawkish counterweight.
- Direction: Guidance is on the easing side of neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "there's a high degree of uncertainty about how market rates will respond to our setting of these administered rates." Dudley: "I do think there is some uncertainty until we actually see that trend materialize."
- Summary: Real uncertainty about normalization mechanics and the post-weather trend, but forecasts largely unchanged.
- Direction: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated.
