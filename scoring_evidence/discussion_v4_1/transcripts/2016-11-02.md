# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 scoring: meeting of 2016-11-02 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion, two days. Staff turns are removed, so only member questions remain. The content is, in order: (a) a long session on the long-run monetary policy implementation framework (floor vs corridor, policy rate choice, balance sheet size, liquidity facilities); (b) members' questions in the financial developments and financial stability segments, plus a financial-stability comment round; (c) the economic go-round; (d) the Chair's wrap-up; (e) the policy round on day two; (f) the vote read-out. The framework session is not an economic assessment and is ignored for the economy dimensions. I use it only for a few remarks on financial stability and on the policy outlook.

**Era context (from the text only).** Policy rate near the lower bound, a single hike about eleven months earlier, core PCE inflation 1.7% and rising slowly, unemployment about 5%. Members discuss a possible December hike. The choice is alternative B (hold, with language signalling a likely December hike) against alternative C (hike now).

**Speakers.**
- Hawkish voices:
  - Williams, Mester and George favor C (hike now).
  - Rosengren would have moved earlier and says delay is costly.
  - Lacker says the data "strengthened the case for a rate increase".
  - Harker says "remain committed to the need for a rate hike this year".
  - Kaplan says it is time to remove some accommodation, "if not today, then very soon".
- Cautious voices:
  - Brainard says wait for more evidence and that she sees no evidence of a hot economy.
  - Tarullo says the case is "not yet compelling", though it is "arguable" that a modest removal is approaching.
  - Evans would not advocate a December hike on inflation-expectations grounds, but accepts it because of communication and credibility.
  - Kashkari wants to see more evidence on labor force participation and inflation.
  - Fischer says "I don't think it makes sense to change the interest rate this month", but also "we should very soon take the second step".
- Bullard supports B and a December hike, but wants guidance that further 2017 moves are not anticipated.
- Chair Yellen says a December increase is appropriate if the data come in about as the staff expects.
- Powell, Dudley and Lockhart support B. Powell calls December "probably appropriate". Dudley calls a hike "likely".

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE.
  - Participants see inflation as slightly below the 2% target but moving up.
  - Evidence: core PCE 1.7% (Mester, Yellen, Powell), headline lower but rising on energy, "inflation still running below 2 percent" (Yellen), "inflation still below our target" (Dudley), "persistent shortfalls" (Brainard).
  - This sits at 41-49, slightly below target, "edging toward target". Score 44.
- OUTPUT_GAP.
  - Members mostly describe growth "modestly above" trend but resource use as near full employment.
  - Yellen says "some modest amount of slack remains"; Evans sees "a bit more resource slack"; Dudley sees "some excess slack"; Mester says "essentially at full employment"; Rosengren says "above-potential" growth ahead; Harker says "little to no slack".
  - The level is about at potential. Bright-line "near potential" gives 48-52. Score 49.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS.
  - Unemployment is about 5% (Rosengren puts the natural rate at 4.7%).
  - Payrolls average about 180k per month, wage growth is picking up (the Atlanta Fed tracker is above 3%, ECI about 2.3%, AHE about 2.6%), worker shortage reports are increasing, but Yellen sees "few signs of overheating".
  - UE of about 4.5-5% is the near-NAIRU band at 46-52, nudged up for the shortage and wage reports. Score 52.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.
  - Consumer spending is described as "solid"/"moderate" but slowed in Q3 and was softer than the staff expected. Residential construction has "stalled". Business investment is "surprisingly soft" and "continues to decline" (Kashkari), with a tentative turn.
  - The net is slightly below trend. Score 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY.
  - Discussed. Powell: "productivity growth to remain weak". Yellen: "anemic". Fischer: "extremely low rate of productivity growth", "negative productivity growth ... for several quarters". Harker: "lack of productivity growth".
  - Well below the historical trend. Score 27.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.
  - Members describe markets as "tranquil" (Williams) and "low corporate bond spreads are supportive" (Mester). They cite low interest rates, "overly accommodative policy creating financial stability concerns" (Harker), and valuation pressures "moderate" (Fischer).
  - Counter-evidence is limited: the LIBOR rise lifted some borrowing costs by 25bp (Evans), and Dudley recalls the January turbulence.
  - Conditions are supportive/accommodative. Bright-line gives below 50. Score 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS.
  - Members touch on term premium and yield outlook (Williams, Rosengren, Kaplan) and on ON RRP and repo plumbing in the framework session. No one describes Treasury market dysfunction.
  - Not discussed as a stress topic. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS.
  - Only Mester gives a level: "low corporate bond spreads". Rosengren and Lacker probe "reach-for-yield" valuations through staff charts, which supports compressed spreads.
  - A stated low level is below 50 under the bright-line rule. Score 33, Low confidence (essentially one participant).
- EQUITY_VALUATION.
  - Members touch on equities lightly. Lockhart worries about runs in ETFs, Dudley expects an election-related equity selloff in tail cases, Fischer notes asset prices were supported by low rates, Mester notes CRE somewhat overvalued and bubble risk, Rosengren and Lacker probe valuation charts.
  - The view is mildly elevated, with no strong concern. Score 42, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS.
  - Mixed signals. Dudley and Fischer discuss Deutsche Bank at length: "undercapitalization", "very fragile", "risk of an abrupt loss of confidence", with spillover risk to Credit Suisse and Barclays. Powell cites "weak European banks".
  - Against that, Dudley says "the financial system is a lot stronger today than it was in 2008", and Brainard says capital at the largest banks has doubled. Fischer rates US vulnerability "moderate". Lacker mentions banks pulling back from CRE.
  - Stress pockets sit abroad while the US system is sound. This is the "neutral/mixed" band. Score 50. The bright-line is not violated: stress language is applied to one named foreign bank and soundness language to the US system.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH.
  - Dollar discussed. "The dollar has been stable for some time" and "still elevated" (Yellen), the disinflationary effect of the stronger dollar is waning (Tarullo), Williams worries about appreciation risk from hikes, Powell cites "a dollar highly sensitive to expectations", Brainard cites "elevated sensitivity of the exchange rate".
  - Stable at a high level, with upside risk. Score 52 (46-54 band, at the upper end).

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Yellen says core import prices are "now rising ... rather than falling" and spot oil is up. Mester says nonpetroleum import prices have moved up. Direction is modest upward pressure from a low base. Score 53.
- SERVICES: Only passing references (nonmarket services, weak health-care prices). N/A.
- HOUSING: Housing activity is discussed but not shelter inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Members say expectations are "stubbornly low" (Brainard), "extremely low by historical standards" (Kashkari), and the Michigan measure is at a record low (several members, including Evans, Mester, Powell). Market measures have ticked up from low levels. Mester says "reasonably stable". Below-target anchoring. Score 38.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the decision is a hold under B, but the statement signals a likely December hike. The Chair, Powell, Dudley, Bullard, Rosengren, Lacker, Harker, Kaplan, Williams, Mester and George all expect or want a hike by December. Brainard, Tarullo, Evans, Kashkari and Fischer (for this meeting) want to wait or go cautiously. This is a majority for tightening with the current action a hold, and the gradual-path caveats pull it down from 65+. Score 62.
- Dissent: two formal dissents (George and Mester, both for C, i.e. the hawkish side). Additional expressed reservations: Williams, who prefers C but supports B, and Rosengren, Lacker, Harker and Kaplan, who would have moved earlier. Others have reservations on the cautious side (Tarullo, Evans, Brainard, Kashkari). There is an assessment-level disagreement about running the economy "hot" (Yellen, Powell, Williams vs Mester, George, Lacker). Distinct camps, 2 dissents, disagreement extends to assessment. Score 52.
- Financial stability attention: a dedicated session, with Rosengren on money market fund reform, Dudley and Fischer at length on Deutsche Bank, Mester on search-for-yield and bubbles, Harker and Kaplan on imbalances in the policy round, Brainard on risk-taking, and Dudley on the market reaction to a hike. This is substantial but not dominant. Score 56.
- Forward guidance: the signal is a December hike if data are in line, on a gradual and shallow path (Brainard, Kaplan, Bullard). The tone is mild-to-moderate tightening. Score 70.
- Uncertainty: "uncertainty about future nonmonetary policies", the election, "treacherous waters" (Fischer), "we don't know what is going to happen" on Deutsche Bank, uncertainty about r*, participation and the natural rate (Powell, Yellen, Tarullo calling his view an "intuition"), Williams on "windshield still fogged". Plenty of hedging, but forecasts are described as broadly on track and "risks balanced" (Mester, Lockhart). Somewhat elevated. Score 44.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 49 is within 48-52 (at/near potential), and no "above potential" level language is used for the gap.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 is below 50 ("supportive", "low spreads", "overly accommodative").
- CREDIT_SPREADS 33 is below 50 (stated low level).
- BANKING_STRESS 50: mixed, with no blanket sound/stressed statement applying to the US system.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 52 is above 50: "elevated", stable, with appreciation risk.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, with no dysfunction described.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; evidence says slightly below target, so it is below 50 (matches). Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; evidence says supportive, so it is below 50 (matches). Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress; mixed evidence gives 50 (matches). Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN means more hawkish; most members want a hike soon, so it is above 50 (matches).

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 49 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 52 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44: all within 15 points. Spending is softer than the labor market, as members noted.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 33: co-move.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is N/A, which is consistent with normal conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 vs sub-components (goods 53, expectations 38): consistent with a slightly-below-target parent.

**N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: no participant describes Treasury market functioning problems.
- INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no participant gives a services or shelter price assessment.

**Text quality.** There are hard line breaks and some gaps where staff answers were removed. Nothing is garbled to the point that meaning is unclear. Roughly the first third of the document (the framework session) is not an economic assessment and was set aside.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "inflation still running below 2 percent"; Mester, "Core PCE inflation, measured year over year, is now 1.7 percent".
- Summary: inflation is slightly below target and rising.
- Direction confirmation: inflation pressure is below neutral because core PCE is at 1.7% and members describe "persistent shortfalls" while noting a gradual rise.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 49 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Mester, "we are essentially at full employment"; Yellen, "some modest amount of slack remains".
- Summary: the economy is near potential, with growth modestly above trend.
- Direction confirmation: the gap is at neutral (slightly below on some views, slightly above on others).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 ±4 [High]**
- Evidence: Harker, "little to no slack in the labor market"; Yellen, "I see few signs of overheating in the labor market. Reports of worker shortages have increased".
- Summary: unemployment is about 5%, near estimated full employment, with firming wages.
- Direction confirmation: labor tightness is at or slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Growth in consumer spending slowed appreciably ... business investment continued to be surprisingly soft"; Kashkari, "domestic demand came in weak and fixed investment continues to decline".
- Summary: moderate consumption and soft investment net to slightly below trend.
- Direction confirmation: spending is slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 27 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "productivity growth continues to be anemic"; Harker, "My main worry remains the lack of productivity growth".
- Summary: productivity growth is described as very weak.
- Direction confirmation: productivity growth is well below its historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams, "financial markets are tranquil"; Mester, "low corporate bond spreads are supportive of increased investment spending".
- Summary: markets are calm and supportive, with the accommodative stance seen as feeding some imbalances.
- Direction confirmation: conditions are looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not raised as a market-functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Mester, "low corporate bond spreads are supportive of increased investment spending".
- Summary: the one explicit level statement is that spreads are low.
- Direction confirmation: spreads are below normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fischer, we "reduced the interest rate to get asset prices up ... the higher level to which prices have gotten"; Dudley, a party sweep "would lead to an equity market selloff".
- Summary: asset valuations are seen as somewhat elevated, with no alarm.
- Direction confirmation: valuation concern is slightly above fair value (a lower score means more valuation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, Deutsche Bank is "very fragile ... undercapitalization", yet "the financial system is a lot stronger today than it was in 2008"; Brainard, "Capital levels have doubled at the largest banks".
- Summary: stress is concentrated in European banks while the US system is sound.
- Direction confirmation: banking stress is at neutral because the pockets of stress offset a sound US system.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 ±5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the dollar has been stable for some time" and "with the dollar still elevated"; Williams, risk that the dollar "may appreciate even more".
- Summary: the dollar is stable at a high level with appreciation risk.
- Direction confirmation: dollar strength is slightly above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 53 [value only].** Evidence: Yellen, "core import prices are now rising at a moderate rate rather than falling". Direction: modestly rising from a low base.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38 [value only].** Evidence: Brainard, "stubbornly low for eight years"; Kashkari, "extremely low by historical standards". Direction: below-target anchoring, with a recent small uptick in market measures.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "it would be appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate in December"; Williams, "I favor alternative C".
- Summary: a hold today, but a clear majority leans toward tightening in December, with a gradual path.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 52 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Mester, "I realize that I will likely be in the minority today"; the vote read-out shows George and Mester voting No on B.
- Summary: two hawkish dissents, plus camps differing on timing and on the "run hot" strategy.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is above moderate and at the notable-disagreement band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 56 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "the failure of a firm the size and complexity of Deutsche Bank would have significant consequences"; Mester, risks "from search-for-yield behavior and potential asset price bubbles".
- Summary: a dedicated discussion by several members, but not the dominant theme.
- Direction confirmation: attention is moderate to prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "it would take something quite surprising to merit leaving the federal funds rate unchanged in December"; Brainard, "the rate path forward is likely to remain gradual and shallow".
- Summary: a clear signal of a December hike on a gradual path.
- Direction confirmation: the forward signal is hawkish but tempered by gradualism.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 44 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fischer, "such a great deal of uncertainty about future nonmonetary economic policies" and "we will be navigating treacherous waters"; Tarullo, "this is ... more an intuition than anything else".
- Summary: somewhat elevated uncertainty about the election, the natural rate, participation and foreign banks, but baseline forecasts are broadly agreed.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is somewhat elevated.
