# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 scoring: meeting 1996-09-24 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It opens with brief questions to the staff (EMU/French budget, payroll data), then the economic go-around (about 17 participants), then the Chairman's policy statement, the policy go-around, the vote read-out, and an unrelated afternoon portion on foreign-currency investment duration and the domestic portfolio's liquidity. The afternoon portion has no economic assessment and is ignored for scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1990s expansion, unemployment around 5.1-5.4 percent, core CPI about 2.6-3 percent and drifting down, strong payrolls, a stock market rally. The Committee is weighing a first tightening against holding. I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Greenspan: wage acceleration is real and labor markets are tighter; profit margins are holding, so productivity is probably understated; the probability of having to move is "still above 50 percent" but falling. He recommends no change ("B") with an asymmetric directive and says 25bp would not be enough if the Committee does move.
- Hawkish voices: Parry (explicit call to raise 25bp), Broaddus (tighten today, 1/2 point), Minehan (25 or 50bp), Melzer ("take out an insurance policy"), Hoenig (prefers to move now), Guynn (a modest increase is desirable now or soon), Stern (formal dissent for 25bp). Moskow, Kelley, Jordan and Rivlin voice upside inflation risks.
- Cautious or "close call" voices: Meyer (close call, Taylor rule says policy is about right), Yellen (could support 25bp, favors a routine response function), Lindsey (the academic case is to move, but he supports the Chairman), Phillips, Kelley, Boehne, McDonough.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see current measured inflation as benign and falling (core CPI 2.6 percent, Meyer; "price discipline," McDonough). They also see underlying pressure as building: wages accelerating, "inflationary danger zone" (Yellen, Rivlin, Broaddus), a "risk of inflation... predominantly on the up side" (Guynn). Net: above 50, modestly. 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: the economy is "very fully utilized" (Kelley), "somewhat above its long-term potential" (Parry), "2.7 percent... probably above the sustainable pace" (Lindsey). Meyer says growth is near trend, and growth is moderating. Above 50, slightly. 56.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "by anyone's estimate, labor markets are tight" (Minehan); "unemployment clearly below the natural rate" (Parry); "impossible" to find entry-level workers (Yellen); August unemployment 5.1 percent. In the language of this era's participants this is clearly tight, so I score from their language and the level (unemployment near the bottom of any NAIRU range) and not from the rubric's modern 4.5-5 percent table. 63.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: strong first half, third quarter softer (retail sales, final sales about 1 percent per Meyer), but housing, capex and autos remain solid, and some doubt the slowdown. Near or slightly above trend. 55.
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed at length. Greenspan argues productivity is well above the measured figures (1.5 percent or more, corporate 2.6 percent over four quarters). Jordan reports 5-7 percent gains at a parts supplier and "productivity is really quite good." Phillips, Boehne and Minehan see it as understated. Above historical trend. 63.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "readily available credit, and a booming equity market" (Melzer); "banks are continuing to support growth with credit availability" (Phillips); long rates up and "a bit more monetary restraint provided by markets" (Phillips); Lindsey and Greenspan on a stock bubble. Accommodative/easy, so below 50. 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: only Lindsey's remark that the bond market is optimistic about the elections, and a Yellen remark that longer rates rose on rumors. The afternoon portfolio-liquidity talk is operational. No dysfunction discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread talk. "Readily available credit" (Melzer) and bank credit availability (Phillips) imply ample availability, which is a below-normal-stress reading. Limited and indirect evidence. 38, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Lindsey calls an "emerging bubble" with IPO activity, speculative stocks and a 12-year high in earnings expectations. Greenspan: "I recognize that there is a stock market bubble problem." Hoenig notes a "speculative bent." Strong overvaluation concern, so a low score. 20.
- BANKING_STRESS: "banks are doing quite well. They are making money!" (Phillips); the securities industry is "very healthy" (McDonough). Counterweight: rising delinquencies and charge-offs (Jordan), "some decline in credit quality" (Guynn). Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50. 36.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. The opening exchange is about the deutsche mark and EMU politics, not dollar strength. Phillips says only that net exports are a "bit of a drag."

**5. Polarity checks.** Equity: higher means more equity stress or concern, and the bubble concern is overvaluation, which the rubric puts at 1-15 and 16-30, so a low number matches. Financial conditions: easy credit means below 50, matched. Banking: healthy means low stress, matched. Labor: tight means high, matched.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: the Committee holds (B, asymmetric toward tightening). A substantial group, including four or five who are not voting this year, argues for an immediate 25-50bp increase. Greenspan says the odds of having to move are above 50 percent. That is a hold with a tightening bias, leaning to the upper part of that band. 60.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Stern, for 25bp). Hoenig said he would prefer to move but would accept the recommendation. Parry, Broaddus, Minehan and Melzer argued explicitly for tightening and Guynn said a modest increase is desirable. Meyer, Yellen and Lindsey called it a close call. Minehan pushed back on McDonough's appeal for unity. The disagreement is about timing and risk, not the economic diagnosis, which is largely shared. 45.
- Financial stability: Lindsey's long bubble and bond-market-optimism passage, Greenspan's reply on bubbles and margin requirements, and Hoenig's note on speculation. Most members do not address it. Limited-to-moderate. 25.
- Forward guidance: the directive stays asymmetric toward tightening; Greenspan says no move may be needed before year-end, but the bias is retained and he says Committee needs 50-75bp if it acts. Mild tightening bias. 64.
- Uncertainty: the Phillips curve and NAIRU are questioned (Meyer, Yellen), data are "foggy" and "confusing" (Greenspan, Minehan), and Stern says "it pays to be humble." Rivlin says the uncertainty is overdone and most members state firm risk assessments. Somewhat elevated, normal-plus. 40.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 56 (above potential language, above 50, satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (accommodative/easy language, below 50, satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (ample availability, no wide-level language, below 50, satisfied). BANKING_STRESS 36 (healthy, below 50, satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs LABOR 63: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 55: aligned. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move. Treasury stress is N/A. Inflation pressure 58 is above the expectations sub-score (50, anchored) because the sub-score sits at the neutral anchor while wage-driven pressure is what pushes the parent above 50. Goods, services and housing inflation are not discussed in a way that supports a score.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning discussion. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no dollar discussion by members. INFLATION_GOODS and INFLATION_SERVICES: only scattered price anecdotes (steel, building materials, apparel) that are mixed and not framed as category trends. INFLATION_HOUSING: only commercial rents and housing activity, not shelter inflation. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is scored: Kelley ("inflationary expectations are flat"), Yellen ("no evidence of an uptick in inflationary expectations"), Melzer (a risk that the "acceptable" rate drifts up).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Yellen: "the risk of an increase in inflation has definitely risen... an inflationary danger zone." Meyer: core CPI "declined to 2.6 percent in August... Not only has inflation not begun to rise."
Summary: measured inflation is subdued but members see wage-driven pressure building.
Direction: above neutral because the participants' dominant concern is latent upward pressure, though actual readings are benign.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 ±6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Parry: "operating at a level somewhat above its long-term potential." Kelley: "the economy is very fully utilized."
Summary: slightly above potential, with growth moderating toward trend.
Direction: above neutral because utilization is described as above potential or above sustainable.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: Minehan: "By anyone's estimate, labor markets are tight." Yellen: it is now "impossible" to find qualified entry-level workers.
Summary: nearly every member reports tight and tightening labor markets, with unemployment at 5.1 percent.
Direction: above neutral because tightness is described as increasing across districts.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 ±6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Meyer: final sales up "only 1 percent in the third quarter compared to 4 percent in the previous quarter." Phillips: business fixed investment "poised for substantial growth."
Summary: strong but moderating spending, near or slightly above trend.
Direction: slightly above neutral because housing and capex stay solid despite a soft third quarter.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Greenspan: productivity growth is "a lot stronger than the 1 percent or less" in the official data. Jordan: "that says to me that productivity is really quite good."
Summary: members see productivity gains as better than measured, supporting profits and restraining prices.
Direction: above neutral because gains are described as stronger than trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Melzer: "money growth near the top end of the Committee's ranges, readily available credit, and a booming equity market." Phillips: "Banks are continuing to support growth with credit availability."
Summary: easy credit and strong equities, partly offset by higher long rates.
Direction: below neutral because conditions are described as easy and supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
No participant describes Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±10 [Low]**
Evidence: Melzer: "readily available credit." Phillips: banks supporting growth "with credit availability."
Summary: no spread levels cited; availability is described as ample.
Direction: below neutral because credit is described as readily available and no wide-level language appears.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 20 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Lindsey: "this emerging bubble is nonetheless real." Greenspan: "I recognize that there is a stock market bubble problem at this point."
Summary: the Chairman and a Governor see stretched valuations, with Hoenig noting speculative behavior.
Direction: low (concern about overvaluation) because members describe a bubble.

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Phillips: "banks are doing quite well. They are making money!" Jordan: "rising delinquencies, late payments, bankruptcy filings, and therefore more charge-offs."
Summary: a healthy system with minor credit-quality concerns.
Direction: below neutral because the system is called healthy and profitable.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A**
Not discussed by members as dollar strength.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A**
Anecdotes are mixed and not framed as category trends.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50**
Evidence: Kelley: "Inflationary expectations are flat." Yellen: "we have yet to see any evidence of an uptick in inflationary expectations."
Summary: anchored, with Melzer worrying about drift.
Direction: at neutral because expectations are called stable.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Greenspan: "I would therefore conclude... that we can stay at 'B,' no change," retaining "an asymmetric directive." Parry: "it would be prudent to raise interest rates 25 basis points at this meeting."
Summary: the Committee holds but with a tightening bias, and a large minority wants to move now.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 45 ±7 [High]**
Evidence: vote read-out shows Stern "No." Hoenig: "If I were voting, I would say that I prefer to move now." Minehan: "I think the only place where we do disagree is in our degree of willingness to wait."
Summary: one formal dissent plus several explicit advocates of tightening, with agreement on the diagnosis and disagreement on timing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Lindsey: "What worries me more is that our luck is about to run out in the financial markets." Greenspan: bubbles are hard to manage with monetary policy and margin requirements are an option.
Summary: one extended presentation and a Chairman response, with scattered mentions elsewhere.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 ±6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Greenspan: the probability of having to move "though declining, is still above 50 percent." Rivlin: "If we get adverse information in the next few weeks, I think we should be prepared to reconsider."
Summary: the asymmetric directive and the Chairman's stated odds signal a likely move, but not an imminent one.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Meyer: "there is more uncertainty about the degree of labor market tightness." Stern: "it pays to be humble about our ability to forecast with accuracy."
Summary: normal-plus uncertainty about NAIRU and the slowdown, with several members expressing firm risk views.
