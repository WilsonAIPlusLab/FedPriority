# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 — Meeting 2016-01-27 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two sessions. The input holds: organizational items; a debate on amending the longer-run-goals "consensus statement" (one dissent, Bullard); questions on the ON RRP facility cap; the financial-stability session; the economic go-round (all 17 participants, day 1 and the start of day 2); the Chair's wrap-up; the policy go-round on "alternative B"; a fan-chart discussion; and the vote read-out (10 Yes, no dissent on the policy action). Staff briefings are removed; only the participants' questions remain, and I ignore them as content.

**2. Era context.** First meeting after the first rate increase (liftoff). Participants discuss a sharp equity selloff, wider credit spreads, a further oil decline, a stronger dollar, China worries, weak Q4 GDP alongside strong payrolls, and low inflation expectations. This backdrop is context only. Every score below comes from what members say.

**3. Speakers and views.** Williams, Lacker, Mester, George, Harker and Kashkari are fairly sanguine about the baseline. Rosengren, Lockhart, Evans, Brainard, Tarullo, Dudley, Powell and Fischer stress downside risks. Bullard is "watch and wait" and worries about falling inflation expectations. Kaplan focuses on oil and the equity P/E. The Chair says real activity looks like "a tale of two economies" and that the financial tightening is equivalent to at least 50 bp of funds-rate increases.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target and likely to stay low longer (oil, dollar, falling breakevens). Brainard: "persistently under target" and "well short of our 2 percent target". Dudley: inflation "likely to stay lower for longer". Fischer says core is near 1.3 and headline well below 1. Nobody sees deflation spiral risk or an outright price decline. Many still expect eventual return to target. Below 50, in the 26-40 band: 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Mixed. Williams says growth will be "above-trend" and "overshoot full employment". Lacker says underutilization is "statistically indistinguishable from zero". Fischer says the labor market is "near maximum sustainable employment". Against that, Q4 GDP was about 0.5 percent and Tarullo is less optimistic than the median. Brainard says there is still "a continued take-up in labor market slack". Participants see the economy as roughly at potential, slightly below on activity: 47.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Strong. Unemployment is a bit above 5 percent, close to the longer-run level. Williams: labor market "very close to our maximum employment goal". Mester: "tightening labor markets have spread beyond construction and skilled trades". Lockhart: "tightness is spreading". Kashkari reports Twin Cities unemployment of 3.2 percent. Wage growth is modest but picking up. Near NAIRU with tightening features: 53.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend in the near term. Yellen says consumer spending rose only 1.5 percent in Q4, "disappointing, to say the very least". Brainard says business investment shows "tepid gains" and drilling and mining investment fell sharply. Manufacturing is weak. Many expect a rebound to moderate growth. Net below trend: 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Williams (negative productivity quarters; recently "so weak"), Lacker (low productivity growth likely to continue) and Dudley ("poor productivity growth"). Below trend: 33.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as tighter. Powell: "significant tightening in financial conditions ... the equivalent of three or four tightenings". Brainard: "tightened considerably". Evans: "the credit environment has tightened by more than simply our 25 basis point move". Above 50: 62.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No participant describes Treasury market dysfunction. ON RRP and money-market discussion is about the facility design, not Treasury stress. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Powell: "Credit spreads have increased further, especially for lower-quality issuers"; in his bearish narrative, "Spreads have blown out". Evans reports "new issuance of high-yield debt has essentially shut down" and a similar contraction in leveraged loans. Dudley: spreads "widened considerably", and "the price of leverage may be going up", after years of "narrow credit spreads". Brainard: "greater strains in corporate bond markets". Most explicit level evidence is for high yield and energy (blown out, access shut). Investment-grade level is not stated. Level reading: wide-to-somewhat-wide: 62, moderate confidence, because the level is partly inferred from "blown out" and the issuance shutdown.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Selloff of about 7 percent is widely discussed, with valuation commentary. Kaplan: a P/E of 16 is "a relatively full multiple ... sort of in the range", and a return to a normal P/E is "not a bad thing". Dudley: "not that equities are overvalued relative to bonds. Equity risk premiums are not unusually narrow." Lacker: the decline is "way out of proportion" to fundamentals. Fischer: high q will decline as policy normalizes. Roughly fair value, with some concern about the disorderly selloff: 54.
- BANKING_STRESS: Not a central topic. Powell notes "banks are tightening credit standards for C&I and CRE loans". Yellen cites the SLOOS tightening. George notes squeezed net interest margins at community banks. Harker says bank lending is strong (loan growth above 10 percent). Rosengren raises a possible CRE bubble. No stress in the banking system itself, but lending standards are tightening: about neutral, 47, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciation, which is above 50 under the bright-line rule. Powell: "broad trade-weighted dollar is up 2½ percent". Brainard: real dollar up about 20 percent since mid-2014. Evans: "the trade-weighted dollar has risen 25 percent since the summer of 2014, and that's a heavy lift for tradable goods sectors". Notable strength weighing on exports: 72.
- INFLATION sub-components: GOODS: falling oil and non-energy import prices pushing goods prices down (Dudley, Powell, Fischer): 25. SERVICES and HOUSING: not discussed, N/A. EXPECTATIONS: market-based measures at historic lows, survey measures at or near lows (Yellen, Evans, Bullard, Brainard); Lockhart, Mester and Harker say still anchored; Dudley says "less well anchored". Risk of unanchoring to the downside: 32.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress. The evidence says widened, wide for high yield, and access reduced. That implies more stress, so above 50 fits.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher = stronger dollar. The evidence is appreciation, so above 50 fits.
- BANKING_STRESS: the evidence is a healthy system with some tightening of standards, so near 50 or below fits.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: the evidence is tightening, so above 50 fits.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: the evidence is low inflation, so below 50 fits.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds the rate unchanged (alternative B) after the December liftoff. The statement adds that the Committee is monitoring global developments and their implications for the balance of risks. Most participants favor a cautious pause. Several (Brainard, Evans, Tarullo, Dudley, Lockhart, Harker) signal that a March move is unlikely or that the bar is higher. Williams, Fischer and Evans want a shallower path. Others (Mester, George, Lacker, Kaplan) keep the case for a gradually rising path. Nobody argues for a cut. That is a hold with a mild dovish tilt: 40.
- Disagreement: The policy vote was unanimous (10 Yes). The one formal dissent (Bullard) was on the wording of the longer-run-goals statement, not on the rate decision. Tarullo prefers alternative A language and goes along "a little bit reluctantly". Lockhart, Mester, Lacker and Williams would rather not shift the risk-balance wording; Brainard and Tarullo want it acknowledged. The fan-chart item splits the table: a straw poll showed discomfort, and Lockhart, Tarullo and Kaplan voiced reservations. The economic assessments themselves are broadly similar. Low-to-moderate: 32.
- Financial stability emphasis: There is a dedicated financial-stability session (Fischer, Rosengren, Kaplan, George, Lockhart) and ON RRP financial-stability risk comments (Williams, Lockhart, Dudley). Tightening financial conditions dominate the go-round and policy round. Prominent: 68.
- Forward guidance tone: Participants favor data dependence, "agnostic" about March, and no pre-set path. Fischer: the trajectory "may be flatter than I had expected". Mixed: gradual normalization is still intended, but the implied signal is softer than in December: 42.
- Uncertainty: Many emphasize it. Fischer: "somewhat more uncertain ... they may neither resolve themselves". Tarullo: "anybody who thinks they know what is going on [in China] is fooling themselves." Kashkari: "uncertainty has risen". Mester: "we're living in uncertain times". The fan-chart debate is itself about uncertainty. Elevated to high, but short of crisis language: 66.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 47: no "above potential" language is used as the main reading and there is no "below potential" bright-line phrase. The range 48-52 is for "at potential" language. Participants mostly say "near maximum employment" with mixed spending, and Brainard mentions residual slack. 47 sits just below that range because the activity data are weak and some slack remains. The score is slightly below 50 and consistent with the mixed language.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 62: tightening language, so above 50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 62: wide or "blown out" levels, so above 50. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 47: "healthy" or "sound" language is not strong here, but nothing says "stressed" either. Neutral. Satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 72: appreciation, above 50. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53) differ by 6, which is within 15. Strong labor data against weak activity is the "tale of two economies".
2. OUTPUT_GAP (47) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40) differ by 7, within 15.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) and CREDIT_SPREADS (62) co-move.
4. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, which is fine in a non-dysfunction setting.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) is consistent with goods (25) and expectations (32).

**9. N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: no participant describes Treasury market functioning problems.
- INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as separate components.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±6 [High].**
Evidence: Brainard, "inflation, which was already soft and coming in persistently under target"; Dudley, "inflation seems likely to stay lower for longer, due to lower energy prices, a stronger dollar".
Center of gravity: Inflation is well below target and expected to stay low longer, but most still see a return to 2 percent.
Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe persistently low inflation and falling expectations.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 47 ±6 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Williams, "labor market is very close to our maximum employment goal"; Lacker, underutilization is "statistically indistinguishable from zero".
Center of gravity: Labor market near full employment but activity weak (Q4 GDP about 0.5 percent), and some slack is still cited.
Direction confirmation: The output gap is slightly below neutral because activity is soft and some residual slack remains, despite near-full employment.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 ±5 [High].**
Evidence: Mester, "Reports of tightening labor markets have spread beyond construction and skilled trades"; Lockhart, "tightness is spreading. Firms reported increased wage pressures".
Center of gravity: Labor market at or near full employment with spreading shortages, unemployment about 5 percent, modest wage acceleration.
Direction confirmation: Labor tightness is slightly above neutral because of reported shortages and nearness to the natural rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±6 [High].**
Evidence: Yellen, consumer spending rose only "1½ percent last quarter, down from a 3 percent rate"; Brainard, business investment shows "only tepid gains".
Center of gravity: Q4 spending and investment disappointed, with energy-sector investment sharply down, though a moderate rebound is expected.
Direction confirmation: Spending is below trend because the data are weak and several sectors are retrenching.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 33 ±8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Lacker, labor productivity "will grow at about the same low rate it has grown over the last few years"; Dudley, "poor productivity growth".
Center of gravity: Productivity growth is seen as weak and likely to stay low.
Direction confirmation: Productivity is below trend because participants call it low and weak.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 ±6 [High].**
Evidence: Powell, "significant tightening in financial conditions estimated by credible external economists as the equivalent of three or four tightenings"; Evans, "the credit environment has tightened by more than simply our 25 basis point move".
Center of gravity: Considerable tightening via equities, credit spreads, dollar and volatility since December.
Direction confirmation: Conditions are tighter than neutral because members repeatedly say conditions have tightened considerably.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a market functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 ±8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Powell, "Spreads have blown out, and banks are tightening credit standards"; Evans, "new issuance of high-yield debt has essentially shut down".
Center of gravity: Spreads widened considerably, with high-yield and leveraged-loan access curtailed; Dudley notes spreads had been narrow before and the repricing of leverage may persist. Level is inferred for high yield; investment grade is not stated.
Direction confirmation: Spreads are above neutral because the stated levels and access conditions are wide and strained, not only that they widened.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 54 ±8 [High].**
Evidence: Kaplan, "16, in my career ... is a relatively full multiple ... right in the middle of 1,840 to 1,920"; Lacker, "the sudden decline in equity prices seems way out of proportion with any tangible change in the outlook".
Center of gravity: Members see valuations as roughly fair to returning to normal, with concern about the speed and cause of the selloff.
Direction confirmation: Slightly above neutral because the selloff is a notable concern but valuation commentary is mostly "normalizing".

**BANKING_STRESS: 47 ±8 [Low].**
Evidence: Powell, "banks are tightening credit standards for C&I and CRE loans"; George, low rates and the flat yield curve compress community bank margins.
Center of gravity: No banking-system stress is described; tightening standards and margin pressure balance healthy lending (Harker, over 10 percent loan growth).
Direction confirmation: Near neutral because signals are mixed and the system is not described as stressed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 72 ±6 [High].**
Evidence: Brainard, "the exchange rate in fact has strengthened in each of the six quarters since mid-2014, for a cumulative increase of nearly 20 percent in real effective terms"; Evans, "the trade-weighted dollar has risen 25 percent since the summer of 2014".
Center of gravity: Notable further appreciation weighing on manufacturing and exports.
Direction confirmation: Above neutral because the dollar has strengthened substantially.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 25.** Falling oil and import prices are holding goods prices down (Dudley: "lower energy prices and non-energy imports"). Value only.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 32.** Evans, "I've been uncomfortable for a while with the continued low levels of inflation breakevens"; Lockhart and Mester, "well anchored". Mixed, with downside unanchoring risk. Value only.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 ±6 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Brainard, "I think it's unlikely that we would see a set of data that would give me enough reassurance ... to justify an increase in the federal funds rate in March"; Mester, "the economy will evolve in a way that warrants a gradually rising path of policy rates".
Center of gravity: Hold with a shallower expected path and a higher bar for March; a minority keeps a firmer gradual-hike view; nobody argues for easing.
Direction confirmation: Below 50 because the table leans toward patience and a flatter path.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Policy vote 10-0; Bullard dissented on the goals-statement wording ("I'm dissenting on the adoption of this statement as written"); Tarullo accepts alternative B "a little bit reluctantly".
Center of gravity: One formal dissent on a non-rate item, reservations on risk wording and fan charts, broad agreement on the policy action and outlook.
Direction confirmation: Low-to-moderate disagreement because the vote was unanimous and the economic assessments largely align.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68 ±8 [High].**
Evidence: Fischer on the QS reports, "bang, it shows up"; Rosengren, "possible commercial real estate bubble in real time"; plus extensive talk of financial tightening.
Center of gravity: A dedicated session plus continuous attention to market conditions in the economic and policy rounds.
Direction confirmation: Attention is high because a separate session and most go-round speakers discuss financial conditions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 ±7 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Lockhart, "agnostic about a March policy action"; Fischer, "the trajectory of rate increases may be flatter than I had expected in December".
Center of gravity: Data-dependent, no pre-set path, gradual normalization maintained but softened.
Direction confirmation: Slightly below neutral because the signal is cautious and a flatter path is implied.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±7 [High].**
Evidence: Tarullo on China, "anybody who thinks they know what is going on there is fooling themselves right now"; Kashkari, "uncertainty has risen. We don't know how hard a landing China will have".
Center of gravity: Many emphasize elevated uncertainty and downside risks, though baselines are mostly unchanged.
Direction confirmation: High because multiple participants stress unusual uncertainty without crisis-level language.
