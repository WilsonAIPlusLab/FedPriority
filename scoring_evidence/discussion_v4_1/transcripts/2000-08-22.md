# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2000-08-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening Q&A with the Desk (Treasury paydown, foreign official holdings, euro reserve diversification), members' questions to the staff, the economic go-around (Moskow through Kelley), then the Chairman's policy view, a short policy go-around, and the vote read-out. Staff turns are removed, so a few passages have missing words (for example, "lead the to understand what we want them to do"). I scored only member content.

**2. Era context.** Summer 2000, after 175bp of tightening since mid-1999. The backdrop is a long expansion, a productivity surge, tight labor markets and a rise in energy prices. The text itself describes a slowing in consumer and housing demand, with business investment still strong.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan, McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Meyer, Kelley, Moskow, Parry, Guynn, Hoenig, Minehan, Broaddus, Stern, McTeer, Poole, Jordan, Santomero. All describe a slowing but strong economy, very tight labor markets, remarkable productivity, modest core inflation and risks tilted toward higher inflation. Differences are about how much weight to put on productivity. Minehan, Guynn and Poole are more worried about inflation; Gramlich, Stern and McTeer are more relaxed. All favor no change, and the bias toward higher inflation is retained.

**4. Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core is "modest" (Hoenig), the 3-month core PCE is low (Greenspan), and there is "no acceleration" (Stern). But headline CPI "starts with a 3" (Minehan), energy is tight, "inflation is creeping up" (Kelley), and the balance of risks is higher inflation. About 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see activity as modestly above potential but closing. Meyer says unemployment is below the short-run NAIRU. Greenspan says "the gap between aggregate supply and demand is beginning to close". Moskow says "if demand expands further beyond potential output". Above 50, about 56 (bright line satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight, with some easing. Unemployment is about 3% in the Tenth District, below 3% in New England, 4.1% in Texas, and 2.5% in Virginia. Ferguson says the job market went "from super hot to sort of warm". Greenspan calls the labor market "the overwhelming problem". About 72.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net is above trend. Consumers and housing are slowing but remain solid. Capital spending "continues to boom" (Greenspan, Guynn). The slowdown is clear, though, so about 58.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Far above trend. Broaddus: "amazing", "fastest since 1983". Meyer celebrates the acceleration. Poole and Minehan caution about extrapolating it. About 82.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Ferguson: conditions "have in fact eased a little" and real rates fell. Hoenig: "policy right now is tight". Banks are tightening standards (Ferguson, McTeer, Jordan). Net near neutral with a slight easy lean, 47.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only the opening Q&A touches Treasury paydown and foreign holdings. There is no dysfunction. McDonough raises a hypothetical "seriously disturbed" market. Normal market, 40, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are not discussed. The only related content is credit availability: banks tightening standards and loan demand slackening (Guynn, McTeer, Jordan, Ferguson), and investment-grade real rates up about 1 point (Ferguson). Level information is absent, so near neutral with a slight lean toward less availability, 52, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Kelley says "stock market and consumer debt are dangerously high". Parry notes the "correction in stock prices this year". McTeer says "stalled stock market" and Ferguson says "rising gradually". Modest overvaluation concern, 40, Low-Moderate.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are reported cautious, with tighter standards and weaker loan demand. These are cyclical and there is no capital concern. No "healthy/sound" language is used. 44, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: McTeer: "the dollar remains strong". Parry wants a higher dollar path. Greenspan: "if the dollar continues to strengthen". Jordan hears no competitiveness complaints. Strong and stable-to-appreciating, above 50, 62.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher means more of the named construct. LABOR_TIGHTNESS is high because unemployment is very low. EQUITY_VALUATION: a low score means overvaluation concern (Kelley), so 40 is consistent. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above 50 because the dollar is described as strong.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: all favor no change and keep the "risks toward higher inflation" language. Hoenig wants to broaden or soften the labor-market reference, and Gramlich and McTeer lean toward patience, while Minehan, Guynn, Meyer and Poole stress the inflation risk. This is a hold with a tightening bias, mildly hawkish.
- Dissent: no dissent, with all the voting members listed answering Yes. The differences are over the productivity outlook, the NAIRU, and how to word the bias statement. Gramlich says the FOMC "may be making a mistake" with its NAIRU view, but he supports the recommendation.
- Financial stability: limited. Ferguson on financial conditions, Kelley on stock market and debt, bank lending reports, and the opening Treasury and foreign-holdings discussion.
- Forward guidance: the retained bias statement ("balance of risks toward higher inflation") with explicit readiness to act. Mild tightening signal.
- Uncertainty: productivity is repeatedly flagged as unpredictable (Broaddus: "totally unprecedented"; Meyer: "a lot of uncertainty"; Poole; Jordan on humility about forecasting). Elevated but not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 with "beyond potential" and "gap closing" language. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Ferguson's "eased" and the tight-lending reports offset, so the score is near neutral. CREDIT_SPREADS is within 45-55 with no level information. BANKING_STRESS has no healthy/stressed wording, so the rule is not triggered. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above 50. TREASURY_STRESS has no dysfunction, so it is below 55.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs LABOR 72: the gap is 16, slightly over the 15-point guide. Labor is scored on the unemployment-rate level (strong anchor), while the output gap is moderated by the slowing and the closing gap. OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 58: consistent. FC 47 vs CREDIT 52: consistent. TREASURY is near normal. INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 vs sub-components (goods ~50, services ~58, housing ~55, expectations ~47): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** None of the five Schema A financial dimensions is N/A; the weak ones are scored with Low confidence. PRODUCTIVITY is heavily discussed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "core inflation pressures obviously remain modest". Minehan, "Does anybody really like the fact that the overall CPI number now starts with a 3". Greenspan, "The acceleration of price inflation is essentially still a forecast."
- Summary: Modest core, rising headline and energy, risks up.
- Direction: Above neutral because the risks are tilted up and headline is elevated, tempered by low recent core.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "The gap between aggregate supply and demand is beginning to close." Meyer, unemployment "still below the staff's estimate of ... the short-run or effective NAIRU."
- Summary: Still some excess demand, narrowing.
- Direction: Above potential per the bright-line rule.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan, "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight, with several New England states ... below 3 percent". Greenspan, "the labor market still is the overwhelming problem".
- Summary: Very tight with early signs of easing.
- Direction: Well above neutral because unemployment is about 3-4%.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "overall capital investment continues to boom"; also "a marked slowdown in the growth of consumer demand". Guynn, "the continued strength in business investment".
- Summary: Consumer and housing cooling, investment strong, net above trend.
- Direction: Above trend because of the booming investment, offset by the slowdown in consumption.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 82 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "the fastest productivity growth since 1983". Mr. Ferguson and Mr. Meyer, "In Celebration of Accelerating Productivity".
- Summary: Exceptional gains, with debate about persistence.
- Direction: Far above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 47 ±7 [Low]**
- Evidence: Ferguson, "financial conditions have in fact eased a little since our last meeting". Hoenig, "policy right now is tight". McTeer, "tighter lending standards that banks are reporting".
- Summary: Slight easing in rates offset by tighter bank lending.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral to neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan on the paydown of Treasury debt; McDonough, "if we have a U.S. Treasury market that is seriously disturbed" (hypothetical).
- Summary: Orderly market, concern only about future supply and foreign demand.
- Direction: Normal functioning, below 50.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 52 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Ferguson, "banks are reporting a tightening of standards and a firming of terms on business loans". Guynn, "falloff in loan demand".
- Summary: No spread levels; availability somewhat tighter.
- Direction: Marginally above neutral on credit availability only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kelley, "the stock market and consumer debt are dangerously high". Ferguson, equities "rising gradually ... as opposed to at a torrid pace".
- Summary: Mild overvaluation concern from one governor, with no broad view.
- Direction: Below neutral, meaning concern about high valuations.

**BANKING_STRESS: 44 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Jordan, bankers "have really tightened conditions by demanding more equity". Guynn, a bank "cancelled its most recent loan review meeting because of no agenda".
- Summary: Cautious lending, weak demand, no capital problems.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: McTeer, "the dollar remains strong". Parry, a "stronger dollar" is likely given growth prospects.
- Summary: Strong dollar with some expectation of further strength; Greenbook assumes depreciation.
- Direction: Above 50 for strengthening.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 52 (oil up; pricing power limited; tech prices falling, per McTeer, Santomero). SERVICES 58 (health care costs, wages). HOUSING 55 (strong rents in New York City, house prices up in California, but the housing sector is slowing). EXPECTATIONS 47 (Santomero, Ferguson, Gramlich: long-run expectations "quite stable").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we ought to stay where we are ... it would be highly risky ... to depart from the bias". Gramlich, "comfortable just watching the situation".
- Summary: Hold with a tightening bias.
- Direction: Mildly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out shows all Yes. Hoenig on the labor-market wording, Jordan on the bias rationale, Gramlich's mistaken-NAIRU concern.
- Summary: Unanimity with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 20 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson on financial conditions and bank lending; Kelley on the stock market and debt; the opening Treasury and foreign-holdings discussion.
- Summary: Passing mentions, not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Santomero, "the balance of risks appears to remain on the side of higher inflation ... we should respond". Moskow, "we have to be prepared to act very quickly".
- Summary: Retaining the higher-inflation statement is a mild tightening signal while waiting.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "totally unprecedented ... a lot of room for doubt and uncertainty". Meyer, "a lot of uncertainty about how it all works out".
- Summary: Elevated, centered on the productivity trend, but members express considerable conviction on policy.
