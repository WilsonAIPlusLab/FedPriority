# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion transcript, meeting 2014-03-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It contains members' questions during staff Q&A, the economic go-round, a short SEP/communications exchange, the policy go-round on "alternative B" (a $10 billion taper, replacing the 6.5 percent unemployment threshold with qualitative forward guidance, new paragraphs 6 and 7), and a vote read-out. Not a special-topic meeting. Length is ample (over 30,000 words), so confidence can be High where several members speak. Pure procedure, drafting wording and staff Q&A are ignored.

**2. Era context (not used to override the text).** Early 2014, a recovery with unusually cold weather and QE tapering underway. Scores come from what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Yellen (Chair), Dudley, Williams, Plosser, Rosengren, Evans, Lockhart, Fisher, Pianalto, Lacker, Bullard, George, Kocherlakota, Tarullo, Stein, Powell.
- Dovish or accommodative camp: Rosengren (significant slack, highly accommodative policy for a considerable time), Evans (wants an inflation floor and thresholds retained), Kocherlakota (dissents, wants stronger guidance), Lockhart (may need more accommodation if inflation risk plays out), Pianalto, Williams, Tarullo, Powell (labor slack understated), Yellen.
- Hawkish tilt: Plosser (rates may need to rise faster; wants symmetric language), Bullard (committee "calibrated to a mediocre economy"), Lacker, George (policy rules signal a shift away from zero), Fisher (financial excess, though he supports B).
- Financial-stability voices: Fisher, Stein, Powell, Bullard, George, with Yellen's summary acknowledging it.

**4. Directional statements for core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as well below the 2 percent target. Williams: 12-month PCE "stubbornly" about 1 percent. Evans: "far below our target." Yellen: "very uncomfortably low level." Most expect a slow return, so the score sits clearly below 50. About 1 percent PCE with "great concern" about under-shooting fits the 11-40 band, upper end of "very low". Chosen: 28.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Rosengren: "significant slack in labor markets"; Yellen: "still a lot of slack"; Evans: slack nearly eliminated only by end of 2016. Bright-line (slack remains means below 50) satisfied. Chosen: 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE 6.7 percent, U-6 elevated, wage growth "tepid", part-time for economic reasons high. A few counterpoints exist (Fisher's Texas, Lacker and Bullard on participation, George's improvement), but the center says slack. Level 6.7 percent places it in the 31-45 band. Chosen: 35.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Weather-hit first quarter (Rosengren: growth about 2 percent even without weather), housing "soft patch", Q4 consumption revised down, but business optimism and expected 3 percent growth. Fisher says firms are buying back shares rather than investing. Net slightly below trend. Chosen: 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Yellen (1.1 percent since 2010, trend may have shifted down), Lacker (no compelling evidence of a pickup), Lockhart ("modestly improving"), Powell (productivity about 1.5 percent long run). Below historical trend. Chosen: 38.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Dudley: "buoyant financial conditions." Powell: "mixed but trending toward ebullience." Stein: credit "meltup." Fisher: record margin debt and high valuations. Some tightness is mentioned (mortgage credit via FICO, Dudley), but the weight is on easy conditions. Bright-line (accommodative or easy means below 50) satisfied. Chosen: 28.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only questions on repo volumes and Rosengren's remark that the 10-year is stable. No dysfunction discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Fisher: high-yield spread 280 bp "lowest since 2007." Stein: CDX HY about 320, "230 basis points below average", "close" to the 2006-07 boom level of 290, though "not historically low". George: "fierce competition among lenders resulting in questionable terms." Stated level is below average and compressed, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Chosen: 24.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher gives a detailed overvaluation case (market cap to GDP 1.25, highest since 2000; Tobin's q highest since 2001; record margin debt). Powell: "P/Es are not unsustainably high" and profit margins are unknowable. Bullard sympathetic to Fisher. Valuation concern is real but mixed; low number means overvaluation concern. Chosen: 30.
- BANKING_STRESS: No assessment of banking-system health. Mentions: European banks more exposed to EMEs (Rosengren), rural bankers see farm stress (George), lender competition (George). These are not an assessment of US bank soundness. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.** Credit spreads: higher means more credit stress; members say narrow spreads, so low score (matches). Equity: higher means more stress/undervaluation concern; members worry about overvaluation, so low score (matches). Financial conditions: higher means tighter; members say loose/buoyant, so low (matches).

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: A further $10 billion taper, replacement of numerical thresholds with qualitative guidance emphasizing "considerable time" and a low path after liftoff. Nearly everyone supports B. Several (Rosengren, Evans, Lockhart, Williams, Kocherlakota) want accommodation preserved or strengthened; a tighter-leaning minority (Plosser, Bullard, Lacker, George) wants flexibility or earlier normalization but all but one still vote yes. Overall lean dovish-to-balanced. Chosen: 40.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Kocherlakota, a dovish dissent). Reservations: Plosser, Lockhart, Lacker, Fisher on paragraph 6; Evans prefers keeping thresholds and strengthening B but supports on the whole; extended SEP dot-plot dispute. Most disagreement is about wording and communications, with some about slack and inflation risk. Fits "1 dissent, 2-3 reservations", 31-45. Chosen: 38.
- Financial stability: Fisher gave a long data-heavy presentation and returned to it in the policy round; Stein devoted most of his remarks to credit markets; Powell and Bullard and George raised it; Yellen's summary named it. Prominent among several members but not the core of the meeting. Chosen: 52.
- Forward guidance: Thresholds dropped, replaced by qualitative guidance. "Considerable time" after QE ends, rates below normal even after goals met, paragraph 7 stressing continuity. Members say it is an update in language, not a policy change. Easing-lean with data dependence; lower for longer. Chosen: 25.
- Uncertainty: Weather clouds the data, labor slack is "even more uncertain than usual" (Powell), Kocherlakota "highly uncertain" about credibility, Dudley "huge uncertainty" two and a half years out, geopolitical risks (Ukraine, China). Yellen: "a lot of uncertainty" from weather. Somewhat elevated but not extreme; most members present confident modal forecasts. Chosen: 40.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 (below, ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 (accommodative, ok). CREDIT_SPREADS 24 (narrow, ok). BANKING_STRESS N/A. DOLLAR N/A. TREASURY N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs LABOR 35: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs CONSUMPTION 44: 8 apart, fine. FC 28 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 24: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: N/A in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 28 vs sub-components: services about 36 (benign health care and non-priced services), housing about 58 (OER rising), expectations about 46; weighted average is a little above 28 but core PCE is repeatedly described as about 1 percent, so I keep the parent where it is, based on direct statements.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not assessed by participants as conditions. INFLATION_GOODS: only scattered, conflicting remarks on input and commodity prices (falling metals prices per Evans, rising grain and commodity indexes per Fisher and Pianalto), no clear goods-inflation assessment.

**Garbled text.** None material. Questions in the staff Q&A appear without the staff answers; ignored.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 (High)
- Evidence: Williams: "The 12-month overall and core PCE inflation rates have stubbornly remained at about 1 percent for almost a year." Evans: "We are still running far below our target." Yellen: inflation "stabilized but at a very uncomfortably low level."
- Summary: Near-universal agreement that inflation is well below target and expected to return only slowly.
- Direction: Below neutral because PCE is about 1 percent versus 2 percent target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 36 ±6 (High)
- Evidence: Rosengren: "significant slack in labor markets." Evans: growth "enough to eliminate almost all the slack in the economy by the end of 2016." Yellen: "still a lot of slack in the labor market."
- Summary: Economy is below potential and closing the gap gradually.
- Direction: Below neutral because slack remains.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±5 (High)
- Evidence: Rosengren: unemployment at 6.7 percent, "well above my estimate of full employment", 7.2 million part-time for economic reasons. Powell: "the unemployment gap more likely understates slack than overstates it."
- Summary: Labor market improving but with notable slack; wage growth tepid.
- Direction: Below neutral because unemployment is above members' natural-rate estimates (5.2-5.8 percent) and wage growth is weak.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 ±6 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Rosengren: growth would have been "roughly only 2 percent even in the absence of weather problems." Dudley: "downward revisions to consumer spending" and housing "taken a greater hit" than expected. Many members: business confidence "positive."
- Summary: Spending soft in Q1, partly weather, with underlying moderate growth and a housing soft patch.
- Direction: Slightly below trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 38 ±8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Yellen: "productivity in the nonfarm business sector has grown at an annual average pace of just 1.1 percent" since 2010. Lacker: "I don't really see compelling evidence" for a productivity pickup.
- Summary: Productivity growth viewed as below historical trend with some members doubting an acceleration.
- Direction: Below neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 ±6 (High)
- Evidence: Dudley: consumer spending and investment "supported by buoyant financial conditions." Powell: "mixed but trending toward ebullience." Stein: market in the middle of a "meltup."
- Summary: Conditions viewed as loose and getting looser, prompting stability concern.
- Direction: Below neutral (accommodative/easy).

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Treasury market functioning not assessed by participants beyond a passing yield remark and Q&A questions.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 24 ±6 (High)
- Evidence: Fisher: "The spread between high-yield corporates and 10-year U.S. Treasuries was just 280 basis points... the lowest since the lows in 2007." Stein: CDX high yield "about 230 basis points below average"; "None of these measures suggest historically low levels."
- Summary: Spreads are well below average and erosion in terms continues, though not yet at record lows.
- Direction: Below neutral (compressed level; the move is narrowing).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 ±8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Fisher: market cap to GDP "1.25... highest since the third quarter of 2000", margin debt at record highs. Powell: "P/Es are not unsustainably high"; profit margins question unresolved.
- Summary: Substantial overvaluation worry from one prominent member, tempered by others.
- Direction: Below neutral (valuation concern, not undervaluation).

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
- No participant assesses US bank soundness; remarks on European bank exposure and lender competition are not a health assessment.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- Not discussed.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS: N/A (conflicting, scattered input-price remarks).
- INFLATION_SERVICES: 36. Dudley: "very benign trend for some nonpriced services, health-care price trends have been unusually subdued."
- INFLATION_HOUSING: 58. Pianalto: CPI higher "attributable to increases in OER inflation"; Dudley: home prices "almost certainly going to feed through more into owners' equivalent rent."
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46. Dudley: "Inflation expectations are well anchored"; Kocherlakota: uncertain "about the credibility of the 2 percent inflation target"; Yellen: don't take "too much comfort" from anchored measures.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 ±6 (High)
- Evidence: Rosengren: "ample justification for maintaining a highly accommodative monetary policy for a considerable time." Plosser: "interest rates may need to move up faster than we currently anticipate after purchases end." Williams: "signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate."
- Summary: Continued measured taper and strong accommodation; dovish pull outweighs a hawkish minority wanting flexibility.
- Direction: Lean dovish.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±6 (High)
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "I have decided to vote against alternative B." Plosser, Lacker, Lockhart object to the second sentence of paragraph 6; Evans: "my biggest concern... looks like a tightening."
- Summary: One dovish dissent plus several reservations, mostly on communication.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 ±8 (High)
- Evidence: Fisher's detailed equity and credit metrics; Stein: "the fuse has been lit"; Powell: "financial instability emerges in the form of unsustainably exuberant financial conditions"; Yellen: "interesting discussion of potential financial-stability risks."
- Summary: Several participants addressed it at length, though it was not the focus of the decision.
- Direction: Moderate, near the top of that band.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 25 ±8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Williams: guidance "signals a highly accommodative future path"; Tarullo and Powell back paragraph 6 on rates below normal after goals are met; Rosengren: liftoff "toward the end of 2015" after "a considerable time."
- Summary: Thresholds dropped but members stress continuity and lower-for-longer after liftoff.
- Direction: Easing lean.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Powell: "the level of slack is even more uncertain than usual." Dudley: "there's huge uncertainty about what's going to happen two and a half years out." Yellen: "a lot of uncertainty thrown into this assessment by the severe winter weather."
- Summary: Weather, labor slack and geopolitical risk add elevated uncertainty, though most hold firm modal forecasts.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated.
