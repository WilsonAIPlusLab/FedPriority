# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1995-11-15

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It opens with a long procedural block: swap-line renewals, the Japanese bank dollar-funding arrangement, a contingency plan for Treasury securities in the event of a federal debt default, and members' questions to the staff. Then come the district-by-district economic roundtable, a short exchange on a Taylor-rule chart, the Chairman's policy recommendation, the policy go-around on the directive, and the roll call. I score only participants' views on conditions and policy and ignore pure procedure (swap renewals, minutes approval, the Desk's coupon-pass mechanics). No garbled passages affected scoring. Some staff answers are missing, so a few member turns are bare questions.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Mid-1995. Participants refer to the funds rate at 5-3/4 percent after a quarter-point cut in July, a very strong Q3 GDP print (about 4 percent), unemployment of 5.5 percent, and CPI inflation around 3 percent. A government shutdown and budget impasse is under way.

**Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: the economy is "soggy" but not deteriorating, and "as close to middle ground as one ever sees it." He recommends no change and is agnostic on the directive. Some "somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996" is likely, and a move in December becomes more compelling if softness continues.
- Hawkish or upside-risk voices: Hoenig, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Minehan, and Moskow in part. They see the economy "a bit beyond capacity," inflation stuck near 3 percent, and upside risk. Melzer says "strongly prefer a symmetric directive" and warns that asymmetry would signal comfort with 3 percent inflation.
- Easing-leaning voices: Forrestal ("inclined to move right now"), Blinder (a small cut is supportable, but he would "very much favor an asymmetric directive toward ease"), McDonough (ease is appropriate, asymmetric directive preferred), Yellen (real rate too high, downside risk, asymmetry preferred), and Lindsey (cut today, the only "No" vote). Boehne expects a downward adjustment "within the next several months."
- Jordan: the funds rate is too high relative to a softening Fourth District, but he lacks a criterion to cut with a 3 percent inflation forecast. He says leave it alone.
- Heavily downside-risk themes: the government shutdown ("train wreck"), consumer debt (Lindsey at length, with Kelley and Blinder), and a G-7 slowdown.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above price stability. The forecast is a persistent 3 percent ("no further progress toward price stability until 1998"). Recent news was favorable, with subdued wages and ECI, but "core CPI is likely to rise this year over last." Hoenig, Parry, Broaddus, and Melzer worry about it. Jordan, Stern, and Yellen see a tilt to the benign side. Net: modestly above target, so 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Hoenig says "the economy continuing to operate a bit beyond capacity." Kelley cites "very high utilization rates." Broaddus puts the path "very close to potential and conceivably even a little above it." Parry sees "some resource constraints." Against this, Greenspan's "sogginess" and the lower NAIRU are largely about the pace of growth. The bright line (above-potential language) requires > 50, so 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Stern says "very, very tight across the District." Moskow reports district unemployment of 3.3 to 4.8 percent and "labor markets remain tight." Phillips cites 5.5 percent national unemployment, "a bit better than expected," with a NAIRU possibly below 6 percent. Minehan's New England is mixed and Boehne reports "anemic" employment gains. Unemployment is a bit below the estimated NAIRU, so labor is slightly tight, and wages are restrained. Score 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q3 was very strong and business investment is "quite robust" (Broaddus). Housing is picking up (Forrestal, Broaddus, McTeer). Retail sales, autos, and consumer confidence are soft (Boehne, McDonough, Moskow, Minehan), and Lindsey stresses consumer-debt fragility. The consensus is moderate growth of about 2 to 2-1/2 percent. Net: at trend, 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Brief but real discussion. Jordan welcomes higher productivity growth and human-capital investment. Blinder notes labor productivity is "still growing at 1 percent a year" with TFP flat. Phillips says "recent productivity numbers are above trend, unless" chain weights are used. Mixed, near trend: 50 (Low confidence).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants describe markets as supportive. Phillips cites stock prices and bond prices up, a flatter yield curve, a steadier dollar, and "cost of capital still favors business spending." Stern says "credit is readily available." Minehan cites "low interest rates and booming financial markets." Kelley says the real funds rate has not put "much of a damper on credit extensions or the stock market or the housing market." Many see the real funds rate (about 3 percent) as high, but that is a policy-stance statement, not market conditions. Bright line (supportive/easy) requires < 50, so 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The only content is the contingency discussion about Treasury securities in a default scenario and Blinder's question about November 15 maturities trading at a discount (answer removed; a Desk participant adds that "There does not seem to be a distortion in the November 30 coupons"). This is concern about a possible event, not functioning breakdown. Score 51 with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No direct spread discussion. Evidence on level: credit "readily available" (Stern), and bankers' remarks that "loan margins generally had narrowed and competitive pressures had intensified" (Broaddus). Level is easy, so below 50: 40, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stock gains are discussed mostly for wealth effects (Lindsey's distribution-of-dividends argument), plus "booming financial markets" (Minehan). There is no explicit overvaluation concern, so the default is 45-55: 47 (Low confidence).
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Broaddus says bankers report "loan quality was deteriorating" (six or seven of the bankers present). Melzer notes a consumer delinquency increase that "remains well below the level attained before the last recession." Hoenig says bank credit growth picked up. Minehan says large-bank loan growth slowed. Greenspan raises the Japanese banks' presence in US markets as a latent risk. Healthy with minor concerns: 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only Phillips's "the dollar has been steadier" over the intermeeting period (plus yen and peso side remarks). Stable and not a concern: 48 (Low confidence).
- Inflation sub-components. Goods: Moskow cites soft nonagricultural commodity prices (steel scrap, paper board, gypsum), Jordan notes the lack of commodity-price reports, and Moskow cites sharp grain price increases that may lift food prices. Net 46. Services and housing: not specifically discussed, N/A. Expectations: Melzer says the public expects inflation to accelerate (Philadelphia survey 3.5 percent; Michigan 4 percent) and "We simply do not have the credibility"; others see long rates falling. Modestly unanchored upward relative to a price-stability target: 60.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: hold with a mild easing tilt in the discussion. Five or six participants (Forrestal, Blinder, McDonough, Yellen, Lindsey, plus Boehne's "within several months") expect or want lower rates, while Hoenig, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Minehan, Moskow, Stern, and Kelley want a symmetric directive and stress inflation. Net: hold, slightly dovish tilt, 43.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Lindsey, who wanted an immediate cut). Several expressed reservations on symmetry (Blinder, McDonough, Yellen preferred asymmetry toward ease; Forrestal not a voter this meeting but would "move right now"). Real disagreement exists on the risk assessment (down side vs. up side) and on the Greenbook's reading of the Q3 data. Score 37.
- Financial stability attention: a meaningful but minority share of the discussion. It covers the Japanese bank funding arrangement, the Treasury default contingency and moral hazard (Minehan explicitly frames it as "financial stability"), Lindsey's extended consumer-balance-sheet argument, and bankers' loan-quality comments. Most of the discussion is real-economy and inflation. Score 27.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive adopted, with Greenspan flagging a somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996 and a more compelling case for a move in December if softness persists. Easing lean with data dependence: 40.
- Uncertainty: Phillips ("all scratching our heads"), Yellen ("the level of uncertainty has increased"), Stern ("a fair amount of uncertainty"), McDonough ("nobody knows or is absolutely sure why"), Boehne ("enough uncertainty"), Greenspan ("it's not clear which way it is going"), and "conflictive" data (Broaddus). Several participants, with budget standoff uncertainty on top. Elevated but not extreme: 55.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 (> 50, consistent with "beyond capacity / above it" language).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (< 50, "supportive / readily available" language).
- CREDIT_SPREADS 40 (< 50, level from "readily available" and "margins narrowed").
- BANKING_STRESS 40 (< 50, healthy with minor concerns).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 48 ("steadier", no appreciation or depreciation stated, so no bright-line trigger).
- TREASURY_STRESS 51 (no actual dysfunction described, so it stays under 60).

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; evidence of modestly above-target inflation gives a score above 50, which matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; evidence of supportive markets gives a score below 50, which matches. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN means a more hawkish stance; evidence of a hold with an easing tilt gives a score slightly below 50, which matches. Higher FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE means a more hawkish signal; evidence of a cut possibly ahead gives a score below 50, which matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 54 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 54: aligned.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 54 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50: a 4-point gap, so no conflict.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 and CREDIT_SPREADS 40: aligned (easy conditions, narrow spreads).
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 and TREASURY_STRESS 51: normal times, with Treasury stress only reflecting the default contingency. Consistent with the guide's "near 40-50 in normal times."
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 and sub-components: goods 46 and expectations 60. The parent is consistent with persistent 3 percent and the expectations concern, with soft goods prices pulling slightly the other way.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed by participants as price categories (the housing remarks concern activity, not shelter inflation). All other financial dimensions had at least some participant content and are scored with Low or Moderate confidence.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "we are not likely to see a real easing in inflation below that 3 percent mark." Broaddus: "In this Greenbook, it stays almost exactly constant at 3 percent." Counterweight, Stern: "despite tight labor markets... we have not seen any acceleration of inflation."
- Summary: Persistent ~3 percent inflation with an upside bias for a bloc, partly offset by benign recent news.
- Direction: Above neutral because the forecast shows no progress toward price stability.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "the economy continuing to operate a bit beyond capacity, by most measures." Broaddus: path "may be very close to potential and conceivably even a little above it."
- Summary: Modestly above potential on resource-use language, while growth is slowing.
- Direction: Above neutral because participants refer to operating beyond capacity.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Stern: "labor markets are very, very tight across the District and across skill and experience levels." Moskow: "Unemployment rates in our District range between 3.3 and 4.8 percent."
- Summary: Tight in several districts, 5.5 percent nationally and below the estimated NAIRU, with wage pressures restrained.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because unemployment is below the NAIRU estimate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "Business investment, especially in computing equipment... is still quite robust." Boehne: "it just does not have the feel of a 4 to 5 percent growth rate."
- Summary: Strong Q3 and investment offset by soft retail sales, autos, and confidence, netting to moderate trend growth.
- Direction: At neutral because strength and softness offset.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Phillips: "The recent productivity numbers are above trend, unless one has made the mental conversion already to chain weights." Blinder: "Labor productivity is still growing at 1 percent a year, right?"
- Summary: Brief, mixed discussion near trend.
- Direction: At neutral because the evidence is contested.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Phillips: "Stock prices are up; bond prices are up; the yield curve has flattened... The cost of capital still favors business spending on investment." Stern: "credit is readily available."
- Summary: Markets and credit availability are supportive, even though the real funds rate is thought to be high.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because conditions are described as supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 51 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Blinder: some Treasury securities maturing November 15 "started to sell at some discount." Reply: "There does not seem to be a distortion in the November 30 coupons."
- Summary: Contingency planning for default, with no actual market dysfunction reported.
- Direction: At neutral because it is concern about a possible event, not functioning breakdown.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Stern: "There has been a striking lack of reports of difficulty in finding credit." Broaddus: bankers said "loan margins generally had narrowed and competitive pressures had intensified."
- Summary: Easy credit with narrowed margins, but no explicit spread discussion.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is easy.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "booming financial markets." Lindsey: stock gains are mostly "wasted" on people unlikely to spend them (a wealth-effect argument, not a valuation concern).
- Summary: Strong equity markets noted without valuation commentary.
- Direction: Near neutral because there is no explicit valuation view.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Broaddus: bankers "expressed the view that loan quality was deteriorating in our region." Melzer: "consumer loan delinquency rate... remains well below the level attained before the last recession."
- Summary: Healthy system with minor credit-quality concerns.
- Direction: Below neutral because the system is described as sound with minor concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Phillips: "the dollar has been steadier."
- Summary: A single remark describing stability.
- Direction: At neutral because the dollar is described as steady.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 46** (Moskow: "Nonagricultural commodity prices continue to be soft"; he also notes grain prices at multi-year highs).

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A; INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 60** (Melzer: "Households in the Michigan survey on average expect inflation to accelerate to about a 4 percent pace next year"; "We simply do not have the credibility").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 43 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "no change today strikes me as the most appropriate response... at some point we should be looking toward a somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996." Forrestal: "I would therefore be inclined to move right now." Melzer: "I strongly prefer a symmetric directive."
- Summary: A hold, with several participants favoring a cut or an asymmetric easing directive and a hawkish bloc insisting on symmetry.
- Direction: Slightly dovish because the easing-leaning camp is larger than the tightening-leaning one, and no one argues for a hike.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 37 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll call: Lindsey No, ten Yes. Lindsey: "I would support a reduction in the funds rate today." McDonough and Blinder prefer an asymmetric directive; Yellen prefers asymmetry but says symmetry is acceptable.
- Summary: One formal dissent plus several reservations on the directive.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement because of one dissent and disagreement over risks.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 27 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "it's entirely appropriate to come at it from the point of view of financial stability." Lindsey on consumer debt: "the problems... on the liability side of the household balance sheet are real."
- Summary: A limited but non-trivial share of the discussion on Japanese bank funding, default contingency, and household balance sheets.
- Direction: Limited because most participants focus on growth and inflation.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "if this economy fails to pick up and continues to be soggy into the next meeting, the evidence in favor of a move at that time would probably become more compelling." Committee adopted a symmetric directive.
- Summary: Neutral directive, but the Chair signals a possible cut ahead.
- Direction: Below neutral because the signal points to easing, tempered by the hawkish insistence on symmetry.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Phillips: "now we are all scratching our heads." Yellen: "from my perspective the level of uncertainty has increased."
- Summary: Several participants stress confusing data, a poorly understood benign-inflation episode, and budget standoff risk.
- Direction: Elevated because multiple participants emphasize it.
