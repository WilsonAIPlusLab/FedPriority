# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2007-08-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed): staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, the policy go-around on the statement wording (alternatives A/B), the Chairman's summary, and a vote read-out. Farewell remarks and communications housekeeping at the start are procedure and are ignored.

**Era context (not used to override text).** Mid-2007. Members discuss a housing downturn, a sudden repricing of risk in subprime, CDO/CLO, leveraged-loan and commercial paper markets, a downward revision to productivity and potential growth (NIPA benchmark revisions), and recent favorable core inflation readings. The policy rate is 5 1/4 percent.

**Speakers.** Chairman Bernanke; Vice Chairman Geithner; Governors Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin; Presidents Moskow, Yellen, Fisher, Pianalto, Stern, Hoenig, Plosser, Lockhart, Rosengren, Poole, Lacker. Views: nearly everyone expects moderate growth, sees downside risks to growth up (housing plus credit-market turmoil), sees inflation readings as encouraging but with upside risks, and wants to hold the funds rate. Disagreement is over statement language (keep or drop "predominant", where to put the downside-risk phrase, "volatile" vs "risk premiums", paragraph 3 wording) and over how much to signal.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as slightly above the midpoint. Core PCE is tame (four months of readings below 2 percent, Lacker; "quite tame", Yellen), so current prints are favorable and near or just below target, but members stress upside risks (reversal of transitory factors, tight labor markets, unit labor costs, commodity/energy pass-through, "tight" utilization per Kohn) and the Chairman says "risks to inflation remain to the upside." Net a little above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy near potential, a touch below on growth but with tight utilization. Growth is "a little below potential for a few quarters" (Kohn), "moderately below" (Yellen), "near potential" (Hoenig, Lockhart), while Kohn says "Utilization is tight" and potential has been marked down. Bright-line: no "above potential" language that dominates, no clear "slack remains"; unemployment edging up only slightly. Score in the 48-50 band, slightly below 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above the midpoint. Fisher: widespread shortages, unemployment 3.5 to 4.1 percent in Texas metros; Lacker: "increasingly taut"; Hoenig: shortage of skilled labor; Plosser: tight labor markets, builder cannot hire; Stern: "labor market overall is strong"; Kroszner: "very robust labor markets." Offsets: Poole sees no labor pressure, Bernanke says the labor market has "marginally softened" with unemployment about 25bp above its low, Rosengren expects unemployment drift up. National unemployment is implied near 4.5 to 5 percent (Kohn NAIRU 4 3/4). Moderately tight.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below the midpoint. Housing is "significantly worse", Q2 consumption soft and retail slowing, business investment trimmed because of uncertainty, offset by solid incomes, exports and non-residential strength. Growth around 2 percent, below a trend that itself was lowered. Net somewhat below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Below trend. NIPA revisions lower structural productivity (Kroszner: compounded growth "down to 1.4 percent from 1.8 percent"; Yellen: "slower structural productivity growth"; Plosser, Stern, Bernanke discuss). Clearly discussed, modestly to well below historical trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tighter than neutral. Risk premiums up, credit standards tightening, MBS market illiquid, CP rollover problems, "Some conduits of credit are simply closed or frozen" (Bernanke); Kohn: conditions "have tightened noticeably." Offsets: cost of capital for high-quality borrowers little changed (Plosser, Kroszner), lower Treasury yields and dollar, banks have funds. Bright-line (tight/restrictive/strained gives >50) is satisfied. Somewhat tight, not crisis.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market is discussed (steep fall in risk-free rates, flight to safety/liquidity in the two-year, Poole; Yellen on declining yields; Hoenig expects yields to move back up). These are yield moves and flight to quality, not Treasury-market dysfunction. Score in the normal band.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (level reading): Rosengren says spreads on riskier corporate bonds "have widened from unusually low levels and are still relatively narrow compared with earlier periods of significant financial disruption." Kroszner says risk premiums have "simply moved close to historical levels from record lows" in many markets, above historical averages in some. Pianalto and Kohn report widening across a broad array of instruments; Lockhart cites non-price rationing and CP rollover risk; Warsh reports 10 percent discounts on leveraged loans and high yield and 15 to 20 percent on second-lien. The level is about normal to somewhat wide, with availability worse than the price level. No uniform "elevated/wide" statement and no "compressed" statement. Score slightly above normal.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity declines noted (Yellen "drop in equity prices"; Warsh "about $1 trillion in market capital" lost; Rosengren on homebuilder equities) without any valuation judgment. Per the gotcha, default to mid-range, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Below the midpoint. Poole: "bank capital is not impaired"; Kroszner: major banks have very high capital ratios, thick cushions, strong earnings, challenge to earnings not capital; Kohn: "Well-capitalized banks"; Plosser: banks not in subprime. Minor concerns: Warsh on dispersion and write-downs at some banks, Hoenig on bank CRE exposure, Rosengren on Countrywide-type lenders. Bright-line (sound/well-capitalized gives <50) satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar is described as declining (Yellen "decline in the dollar", Kohn "recent declines in the dollar", Rosengren "a falling dollar", Kroszner "a lower dollar", Bernanke "lower dollar"). Bright-line (depreciation gives <50) satisfied. Modest depreciation, mentioned as offset to growth and an inflation risk.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: Fisher on paper products (double-digit increase requests), shipping, Wal-Mart comps driven by inflation; Lacker on manufacturers' prices rising; energy/commodity pass-through (Hoenig, Kroszner). Moderately above stable. Services: Fisher "still price inflation in the service sector", Lacker services price pressure picked up; but Bernanke/Yellen note tame core. Slightly above stable. Housing: owners' equivalent rent is cited as a factor that has been damping core and is ebbing (Yellen, Lacker), with Bernanke flagging possible renting-driven rent increases as a risk; house prices falling. Roughly stable to slightly easing shelter inflation. Expectations: "well-anchored" around 2 percent (Yellen, Mishkin, Kohn, Geithner), but Lacker and Plosser say expectations remain above where they would like. Near anchored at target, small upward tilt.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Vote is unanimous to hold at 5 1/4 percent with the existing "predominant policy concern" inflation language plus a new clause that downside risks to growth have increased. Several members (Geithner, Yellen, Kohn, Rosengren, Warsh) want flexibility to ease or expect easing later; Fisher, Poole, Lacker, Moskow, Hoenig stress inflation and avoiding any hint of a cut. Hold, balanced to a shade dovish in risk framing; the retained inflation emphasis keeps it from being clearly dovish. Slightly below 50.
- Dissent: Zero formal dissents (10 of 10 yes). Disagreement is over wording and signaling: "predominant" (Geithner, Yellen, Fisher, Rosengren would drop; Warsh, Kohn, Kroszner, Mishkin, Moskow keep), location of downside-risk clause (Poole, Plosser, Yellen vs Kohn), Lacker and Moskow wanting no downside-risk phrase in the statement, Mishkin on the inflation objective and "Fed put" debate (Kohn vs Mishkin vs Lacker exchange). Extends a little into risk assessment (Kohn: "go ahead and act" if turbulence persists vs Lacker/Poole against cuts). Broad agreement with several reservations: low-to-moderate.
- Financial stability emphasis: The market turmoil is the headline topic. The Desk report opened the meeting and the questions to it, and nearly every participant addresses credit markets, liquidity, CP, CDO/CLO, ratings, bank capital; the Chairman and Warsh/Kohn/Kroszner devote long passages; policy go-around centers on how to signal about markets and moral hazard. Dominant, rivaling the economic assessment.
- Forward guidance: Statement keeps inflation as the predominant concern but adds downside growth risk; Fisher, Poole, Lacker, Stern, Mishkin explicitly do not want to hint at cuts, while Geithner/Kohn/Yellen want flexibility and accept market pricing of cuts; Kroszner expects a "slight increase in expectations of a cut." Signal is neutral to slightly easing-leaning with data dependence.
- Uncertainty: High. Kroszner "uncertainty has gone up dramatically"; Stern "very difficult, to put it mildly, to assess"; Lacker "awfully uncertain"; Lockhart "great deal of uncertainty at our September meeting"; Kohn "this tail of distribution is a lot fatter"; Warsh "less confident" about subprime. Many emphasize difficulty, but the baseline view (moderate growth) is held with some conviction and several (Poole, Hoenig, Plosser) downplay the effect on the real economy. High but not extreme.

**Polarity verification.** INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher = more pressure; evidence is mildly favorable current prints with upside risks, score a little above 50, matches. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: evidence is shortages and strong labor markets, score above 50, matches. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: evidence is depreciation, score below 50, matches. BANKING_STRESS: evidence is sound/well-capitalized, so low stress, below 50, matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence is spreads up from record lows to about historical norms, with some wide pockets and worse availability, score a little above 50, matches level reading. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: evidence is tightening, score above 50, matches. PRODUCTIVITY: evidence is revised-down growth, score below 50, matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP: no one-sided phrase; score 49 (48-52 band for near potential with tight utilization versus modestly below-potential growth) is acceptable. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS >50 (tight/restrictive language). CREDIT_SPREADS: no stated high level and no compressed level; mid-to-slightly-above, level governs. BANKING_STRESS <50. DOLLAR <50. TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction described for Treasuries, 36-50 band.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (49) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): 9 points apart, within 15. Labor is tight while growth is slightly below a lowered potential.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (49) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43): within 15.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (56): both above 50, co-move.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS (45): flight-to-quality yields, no dysfunction; consistent with normal-times treatment, though some CP/MBS illiquidity elsewhere.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (54) vs sub-components (goods 57, services 55, housing 46, expectations 51): weighted average is about 52 to 54, consistent.

**N/A justification.** None of the 20 dimensions is N/A; all were discussed by members. Equity valuation is given a low-confidence mid-range score because only price declines, not valuation views, were discussed.

**Text quality.** Staff answers and Dudley's briefing are removed, so some member questions lack their replies; some speaker turns are fragments ("Right.", "Thank you"). The vote read-out is complete (10 yes). No passages were garbled enough to hinder scoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 +-6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Lacker: "four months of readings below 2"; Chairman: "risks to inflation remain to the upside."
- Center of gravity: Current core prints are favorable and near/just under target, but most members stress upside risks (transitory damping fading, tight labor, unit labor costs).
- Direction: Inflation pressure is slightly above neutral because upside risks and tight utilization outweigh the tame recent readings.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 49 +-6 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Kohn: "growth a little below potential for a few quarters" but "Utilization is tight"; Hoenig: growth "near potential."
- Center of gravity: Economy near a lowered potential, slightly below on growth, with tight utilization.
- Direction: Output gap is at or marginally below neutral because growth is a touch under (lowered) potential while resource use stays tight.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +-6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Fisher: "widespread labor shortages, for both unskilled and skilled labor"; Stern: "labor market overall... is strong."
- Center of gravity: Many Districts report shortages and wage pressure, tempered by Poole's slack trucking contacts and the Chairman's "marginally softened."
- Direction: Labor market is above neutral because most participants describe it as strong and tight.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-6 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Moskow: retailers expect "spending will be softer than in the first half"; Pianalto trims business fixed investment.
- Center of gravity: Housing is a heavy drag, Q2 consumption was soft and investment is being trimmed on uncertainty, partly offset by solid incomes and exports.
- Direction: Spending is below trend because housing weakness and caution outweigh solid income-driven consumption.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 36 +-7 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Kroszner: productivity growth "down to 1.4 percent from 1.8 percent"; Yellen: revisions "suggest slower structural productivity growth."
- Center of gravity: NIPA revisions point to lower structural productivity, though participants split on how permanent.
- Direction: Productivity is below trend because members describe it as revised down and slower than previously believed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 +-7 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Kohn: "financial conditions have tightened noticeably"; Bernanke: "Some conduits of credit are simply closed or frozen."
- Center of gravity: Sharp repricing of risk and tighter credit and underwriting, partly cushioned by low Treasury yields and unchanged costs for top-rated borrowers.
- Direction: Financial conditions are tighter than neutral because members repeatedly describe tightening credit and restrictive effects.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +-8 (Low confidence)
- Evidence: Poole: the two-year is "a flight to safety" / "flight to liquidity"; Yellen: "steep reduction we have seen in risk-free rates."
- Center of gravity: Yields fell on flight to safety and weaker growth expectations; no impairment of Treasury market functioning described.
- Direction: Treasury stress is at/below neutral because yield moves and safe-haven demand are not dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 +-8 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Rosengren: spreads "widened from unusually low levels and are still relatively narrow compared with earlier periods"; Kroszner: premiums "moved close to historical levels from record lows."
- Center of gravity: Spread level is roughly back to historical norms, with wider pockets (leveraged loans, high yield, second liens) and worse availability, so slightly above normal.
- Direction: Credit stress is slightly above neutral because the level is about normal to somewhat wide with availability constrained; widening from record lows alone was not allowed to push the score higher.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +-8 (Low confidence)
- Evidence: Warsh: "lost about $1 trillion in market capital in the past twenty trading days"; Yellen: "drop in equity prices."
- Center of gravity: Price declines noted as a wealth-effect channel, with no valuation view offered.
- Direction: Equity concern is near neutral because only price moves, not valuation judgments, were discussed.

### BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-7 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Poole: "bank capital is not impaired"; Kroszner: major banks have "very high... capital-to-asset ratios" and "thick capital cushions."
- Center of gravity: Banking system is viewed as well-capitalized and a stabilizer, with some dispersion and earnings/write-down concerns.
- Direction: Banking stress is below neutral because participants call banks well-capitalized and profitable.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 33 +-7 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Kohn: "recent declines in the dollar will reinforce" export demand; Rosengren: "a falling dollar" as an inflation risk.
- Center of gravity: Modest dollar depreciation cited several times as a growth offset and inflation risk.
- Direction: Dollar strength is below neutral because members describe the dollar as declining.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 57 (value only)
- Evidence: Fisher: paper producers "asking for double-digit increases"; Lacker: manufacturers' prices up "for both raw materials and finished goods."

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 55 (value only)
- Evidence: Fisher: "still price inflation in the service sector"; Lacker: services price pressures "have picked up."

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 46 (value only)
- Evidence: Yellen expects "ebbing" of owners' equivalent rent effects; Lacker cites OER as a damping factor; Bernanke flags a rent-driven upside risk if homeowners move back to renting.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 51 (value only)
- Evidence: Yellen: "well-anchored inflation expectations"; Lacker: expectations "remain above where I'd like them to be."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 47 +-6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Poole: "I believe we should keep it steady"; Kohn: "we could end up coming out of this and easing policy somewhere down the road."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold at 5 1/4 percent, inflation still named the predominant concern, but growth downside risks up and several members want flexibility to ease; others resist any hint.
- Direction: Lean is a shade dovish of balanced because the hold is paired with softened asymmetry and expected easing, though inflation vigilance is retained.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Vote read-out: all ten voters "Yes"; Yellen on "predominant": "we don't care; we remain totally focused on inflation."
- Center of gravity: No dissent; sustained but friendly disagreement over statement wording, signaling and the Fed-put/moral-hazard question.
- Direction: Disagreement is low because the vote is unanimous and differences are about emphasis and tactics rather than economics.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 80 +-6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Warsh: "the much-anticipated repricing of risk is upon us"; Chairman: "the markets right now are not functioning normally."
- Center of gravity: Credit-market turmoil dominates the economic go-around, the policy debate, and the questions to the Desk.
- Direction: Attention is very high because financial-market stress rivals the real-economy assessment in the discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 46 +-7 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Poole: "we ought not to give any hint that we are trying to suggest a policy change"; Geithner: "some softening of the asymmetry in our assessment of the balance of risks."
- Center of gravity: Members deliberately avoid signaling cuts yet keep "predominant" inflation concern and add downside-risk language, keeping a flexible, data-dependent posture with a slight easing tilt.
- Direction: Forward signal is near neutral and slightly easing-leaning because added downside-risk wording and flexibility offset the retained inflation-first language.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-7 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Kroszner: "uncertainty has gone up dramatically"; Stern: "very difficult, to put it mildly, to assess its effects."
- Center of gravity: Many members stress hard-to-assess market dynamics, fat tails and a stale information set, while the baseline of moderate growth is held.
- Direction: Uncertainty is high because multiple participants emphasize unusual difficulty and wide scenario ranges.
