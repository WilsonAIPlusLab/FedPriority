## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document: two-day meeting transcript. Normalization-tools session (ON RRP cap, term RRP testing, minutes language), economic go-around, policy go-around on alternatives A/B/C, vote at end (10 Yes, no No votes). Not special-topic: it has a policy-stance discussion and a policy vote.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 29 (+-5, High)
- Brainard: inflation "remains stubbornly low relative to our 2 percent target"; core PCE "1.3 percent". Evans: outlook "nowhere near satisfactory"; "only slowly crawling back up to 2 percent sometime in 2018 or later". Yellen: "readings on inflation are likely to remain uncomfortably low"; "12-month change ... just 0.2 percent" (total). Rosengren: lowered core to "1.2 percent". Most expect a return to 2 percent as energy and dollar effects fade (Lacker, Fischer, Powell), but the current reading is clearly below target.
- Below target, not deflationary.

OUTPUT_GAP: 42 (+-5, High)
- Yellen: "a good bit of remaining slack"; "all but two of you ... continue to think that some slack remains". Brainard: "some slack along several margins". Mixed with Mester ("nearing our goal of maximum employment", growth "above trend") and Bullard ("above-trend growth"). Net slightly below potential. Bright-line "slack remains" gives below 50.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 45 (+-5, High)
- Fischer: "closing in on full employment"; Mester: "We are nearing our goal of maximum employment"; unemployment about 5.5 percent (Lacker: "from 8.3 percent to 5.5 percent"). Yellen: "we still have a ways to go"; wage growth "sluggish ... around 2 percent". Kocherlakota: "compensation pressures remain, at best, moderate". Evans: "anemic wage growth". Strong payrolls ("280,000", Mester, Brainard) but level of tightness is near-but-not-at NAIRU.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 (+-6, High)
- Yellen: "Many of you cited consumer spending as a bright spot", but "three disappointing retail sales reports", "housing market remains quite sluggish", "nonresidential business investment seems as though it's weakening further". Tarullo: "Business fixed investment is still sluggish". Brainard: other components "quite tepid". Lacker is the main voice for "fairly robust trend in consumer spending". Net slightly below trend.

PRODUCTIVITY: 30 (+-6, Moderate)
- Fischer: "productivity growth has fallen dramatically"; Williams: "Continued weak productivity growth"; Rosengren: "continued disappointing productivity numbers"; Lacker: "unlikely to accelerate significantly".

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 46 (+-8, Low)
- Williams: "fairly dramatic shift in financial conditions" (stronger dollar, lower long rates). Brainard: dollar appreciation "exerting a significant tightening effect"; Evans: dollar "somewhat like a monetary policy tightening". Offsetting: Dudley: "very low level of bond term premiums", "search for yield"; Kocherlakota: "signs of excessive risk-taking are not widespread". No explicit "accommodative/tight" wording about conditions overall; mixed, so near neutral.

TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Members discuss term premiums and Treasury sales hypotheticals only; no market-functioning comment.

CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A
- No participant states spreads, yields-premiums on credit, or credit availability/lending terms beyond a passing farm-lending remark (George, financing needs) that is demand, not availability.

EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A
- Equities not discussed with a valuation view or price move (Tarullo mentions 1990s stock market only as history).

BANKING_STRESS: N/A
- No assessment of bank health; Holcomb: bankers "report few negative effects from lower energy prices so far" is a single regional aside.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 72 (+-5, High)
- Dudley: dollar "up about nearly 15 percent over its level last summer"; "shifting from a risk to a reality". Brainard: "broad nominal dollar has appreciated more than 15 percent since June". Powell: "significant headwind and threatens to be an even bigger one". Tarullo: "dollar strengthening continues beyond what's projected". Notable broad appreciation with exports hit.

INFLATION_GOODS: 30
- Dudley: "core goods prices are falling ... as the dollar strength continues to show through"; Yellen: apparel prices held down by import content.

INFLATION_SERVICES: 48
- Dudley: "Core services prices are rising about 2 percent"; Yellen: nonenergy services "little changed over the past year".

INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- No participant discusses rents or shelter inflation.

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40
- Survey measures "anchored" (George, Mester, Lacker: "well anchored", Dudley "broadly stable"); market measures "near historical lows" (Kocherlakota), "loss of credibility" concern (Yellen summary "for some of you"); Kocherlakota: "significant risk of inflation expectations sliding permanently below 2 percent". Anchored on surveys but tilted below target on market measures.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 51 (+-6, Moderate)
- Decision: hold at zero, drop "patient" (alternative B), "June ... cannot be ruled out" (Yellen). Hawkish pull: Lacker "we should raise rates in June"; George "appropriate to lift off in June"; Holcomb "liftoff in June is quite likely to be appropriate"; Bullard "policy settings ... increasingly out of step". Dovish pull: Kocherlakota backs alternative A (more accommodation) and calls removal of "patient" "a tightening"; Evans "liftoff should be delayed until the first quarter of 2016"; Tarullo liftoff "December". Yellen: "cautious and deliberate approach to tightening". Balanced with a slight tightening bias on the removal of "patient".

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 (+-7, Moderate)
- Vote record: ten Yes on alternative B, no dissents. But Kocherlakota (a non-voter this year) argues against B and for A: "I'm opposed to such a move at this time"; Lacker raises a wording conflict ("internal tension"); liftoff views range from June (Lacker, George, Holcomb) to 2016 (Evans, Kocherlakota). Tools session: Lacker "declin[es] to support term testing", near-even show of hands on "fairly soon" (Yellen: "pretty evenly divided"), Tarullo and Williams contest language. Disagreements are mostly about timing and tactics, not about the statement.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 (+-8, Moderate)
- Tarullo: ON RRP as "safe haven to which money markets could run" and "financial-stability risks arise" over time; Holcomb: "financial-stability risks associated with term RRPs seem likely to be lower"; Evans, Williams, Lockhart, Bullard weigh financial stability against rate control. Dudley: low term premiums "represents an important source of uncertainty and risk ... financial stability". Kocherlakota: "I see no risks that are material for the macroeconomy". A sizeable part of the tools session but not the main subject of the economic go-around.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 (+-6, Moderate)
- Removal of "patient" with new conditional wording ("further improvement in the labor market and ... reasonably confident that inflation will move back"). Lockhart: "liftoff is approaching"; Fischer: "not unlikely that we'll want to raise rates in June". Offsets: Yellen "I don't view the removal of 'patient' as a signal that liftoff in June is particularly likely"; Powell "policy should remain highly accommodative for quite a while longer"; Brainard: economic projections "point to a later and more gradual path". Mild tightening bias with a data-dependent, gradual path.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 (+-7, Moderate)
- Powell: "very notable how much uncertainty there is around the inflation creation process as well as the natural rate of unemployment"; Rosengren: "great uncertainty around key variables"; Evans: inflation outlook "highly uncertain"; Brainard: "considerable uncertainty regarding the magnitude of import price pass-through", "outlook for inflation is more uncertain"; Lockhart: "lots of elements of uncertainty", "particularly tough time to read the underlying trends"; Fischer: "two significant puzzles". Mester: "Reasonable confidence should not require high confidence." Multiple participants stress uncertainty about slack and inflation, but most still give a modal path.
