# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1993-03-23 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, early-1990s format. It opens with a procedural item (naming of the FOMC "minutes"), then holds members' questions to the Desk and staff, a District-by-District economic go-around, a policy round on a 3 percent funds rate with a symmetric versus asymmetric directive choice, and the roll call. Procedure is ignored for scoring.

**Era context.** Spring 1993: recovery under way, funds rate at 3 percent for months, unemployment around 7 percent, a recent jump in CPI and PPI prints, long-term rates falling, a new fiscal package pending. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Reserve Bank presidents (Boehne, Forrestal, Keehn, Parry, Broaddus, McTeer, Hoenig, Syron, Stern, Jordan, Melzer) report moderate, improving District growth, plentiful labor, cautious hiring, firming materials prices (steel, lumber, scrap), and worry about fiscal and trade policy.
- Mullins, Corrigan, Angell, Lindsey, Phillips and Broaddus stress the inflation upturn, with Mullins citing core CPI near 4 percent over six months.
- Angell and Lindsey want tightening now. Greenspan says the funds rate cannot stay at 3 percent without court trouble but that the moment is not this meeting, and he proposes "B" symmetric.
- Forrestal, Syron, Boehne, Hoenig, Stern, Keehn, Kelley, LaWare, McTeer and Phillips favor holding.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly to clearly above the midpoint. Core CPI near 4 percent for six months, rising commodity and materials prices, and spot price talk in the Districts push it up. Underlying fundamentals (labor costs, slack) are called benign and some call the prints an aberration. Angell says "3 percent is terrible". Net about 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Parry says "real GDP remaining below potential". Mullins says "ample slack in the economy both on the labor side and in terms of capacity utilization". Boehne says "slack in labor markets". Bright-line: below potential gives a score under 50. About 40.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Forrestal says "labor supplies are still plentiful", national unemployment is about 7 percent (Lindsey), and hiring is cautious. Lindsey and Stern note spot shortages, and Lindsey says the rate outside California is "closer to NAIRU". Net about 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: At trend. Moderate growth around 3 percent, with strong steel, trucks and capital goods but cautious consumers and a February retail air pocket. Net about 52.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Greenspan says it "is picking up in a fairly substantial way, and I suspect it is basically real". Mullins says wage increases are unlikely to swamp productivity gains. Brief, so a modest above-trend score of 58 with low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (looser). Mullins cites the "benign capital market environment" and "the lowest interest rates in several decades", and Phillips says the financial sector is "well positioned" to provide capital. Greenspan counters that credit growth is "extraordinarily restrained". Bright-line: accommodative or supportive means a score under 50. Net about 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The bond rally and its vulnerability are discussed, but nobody describes dysfunction. McDonough says the market is "in pretty good shape to ... absorb" bad news. Rate moves are not dysfunction, so about 38.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Mullins notes "tightening of quality spreads" in investment grade and below. Syron mentions mutual fund managers' "concerns about credit quality spreads" and the "reach for yields". No explicit level is stated, but the context implies narrow and compressed spreads. The rule for direction-only text says 45-55 on the side of the move, so I use 44 with low confidence (a slightly below-normal reading).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Mullins says stock averages are "within whispering distance of their all-time highs". Greenspan says "the stock market ... is high". Lindsey calls markets "overextended". This is modest valuation concern, about 38.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Greenspan says "a still severely impaired financial system", while Phillips says the financial sector is well positioned, Boehne and Syron report rising loan demand, and Syron describes some banks eager for acquisitions. Net neutral, 50 with low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Angell calls the dollar "tepid", and Lindsey says it is weaker than it should be and unchanged despite relative US strength. The earlier weakening against the mark is noted. Below the midpoint, about 38.
- Inflation sub-components. GOODS: commodity, lumber, steel and scrap prices rising sharply, about 68. SERVICES: not discussed. HOUSING: only lumber and lot costs, which are construction inputs, not shelter inflation, so N/A. EXPECTATIONS: members attribute the bond rally to lower inflation expectations, while Mullins notes survey expectations "remain high" and Lindsey reports a changed attitude. Net near neutral, 48.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the vote is to hold at "B" with a symmetric directive. Greenspan states the funds rate "cannot" stay at 3 percent indefinitely and that "we have to be" ahead of the curve on the upside. Broaddus and Melzer say a tilt or a move may be needed soon, while most others want to wait. This is a hold with a tightening bias, so lean hawkish at about 60.
- Dissent: two formal dissents (Angell for "C", Lindsey for a small rise), both in the hawkish direction. Disagreement is mostly about timing and tactics, with some framework differences (Angell's price level targeting, Jordan on the aggregates). About 48.
- Financial stability: the bond market's vulnerability, equity levels, credit quality spreads and the impaired credit system feature in the Chair's central argument and in several members' remarks, but this is not the focus of the meeting. About 30.
- Forward guidance: a symmetric directive, with "vigilance" language and the Chair's expectation of a move, possibly quickly. Mild tightening bias, about 60.
- Uncertainty: many members cite conflicting signals. Parry says there is "considerable uncertainty", Corrigan has a "nagging sense of unease", LaWare says it is like "23 unknowns", and Greenspan says this cycle "is different from anything any of us has seen". Elevated, about 55.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 40, below 50, matches "below potential" and "ample slack".
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40, below 50, matches accommodative and benign language.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 44, below 50, matches "tightening of quality spreads"; the rule for direction-only text is followed.
- BANKING_STRESS 50 is a mixed neutral reading.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 38 matches weakness and "tepid".
- TREASURY_STRESS 38 has no dysfunction language.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (34) are within 15 points and both below 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52) differ by 12, which is acceptable because activity is at a moderate trend while slack persists.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) and CREDIT_SPREADS (44) co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is near the normal range.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) is consistent with goods at 68 and expectations at 48.

**N/A justification.** SERVICES and HOUSING inflation were not discussed by members (lumber and lot costs are building inputs). All other dimensions were addressed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 (High).** Mullins: "in three out of the past five months core CPI has been 1/2 percent". Boehne: "recent price numbers strike me more as an aberration". Summary: the near-term prints are clearly high but fundamentals are called benign, so modestly to clearly above neutral. Direction: above neutral because of the six-month 4 percent core readings.
- **OUTPUT_GAP: 40 ±6 (High).** Mullins: "There is ample slack in the economy". Parry: "real GDP remaining below potential". Summary: slack is acknowledged widely. Direction: below neutral.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 34 ±6 (High).** Forrestal: "Labor supplies are still plentiful". Lindsey: the rate outside California is 6.7 percent, "a lot closer to NAIRU than our 7 percent sounds". Summary: unemployment is high, with scattered local tightening. Direction: below neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±6 (Moderate).** Phillips: "it's not surging, but it's at least continuing at a reputable pace". Keehn: bookings and truck, auto and equipment production are up. Summary: moderate growth with strong industrial pockets and a cautious consumer. Direction: near neutral.
- **PRODUCTIVITY: 58 ±10 (Low).** Greenspan: "Productivity is picking up in a fairly substantial way". Summary: one clear remark plus an aside. Direction: above neutral.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±8 (Moderate).** Mullins: "the lowest interest rates in several decades". Phillips: the financial sector "appears well positioned to continue to provide capital". Counterweight: Greenspan on restrained credit. Summary: market conditions are easy even though credit aggregates are weak. Direction: below neutral (looser).
- **TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 (Low).** McDonough: markets are "in pretty good shape to get some unpleasant news and absorb it very well". Corrigan: the rally is "vulnerable". Summary: orderly with a reversal risk, no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS: 44 ±8 (Low).** Mullins: "We have seen tightening of quality spreads". Syron: "concerns about credit quality spreads". Summary: spreads are tightening and probably narrow, but no explicit level is stated. Direction: slightly below neutral.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Mullins: stock averages "within whispering distance of their all-time highs". Greenspan: "the stock market, which is high at this particular stage". Summary: high but not a bubble concern. Direction: modest valuation concern.
- **BANKING_STRESS: 50 ±10 (Low).** Greenspan: "a still severely impaired financial system". Phillips: the financial sector is "well positioned". Summary: mixed signals. Direction: neutral.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Angell: "a somewhat tepid foreign exchange value of the dollar". Lindsey: "the dollar unchanged should be sending out alarm bells". Summary: the dollar is soft or flat against expectations. Direction: below neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS: 68.** Angell on commodity indexes; Keehn and Jordan on steel and lumber; Melzer on scrap steel up close to 20 percent in two months.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
- **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Stern and others say the rate decline reflects "more favorable expectations about future inflation". Mullins: "Expectations survey data remain high".
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 (High).** Greenspan: "we can't stay here" and "we should be ahead of the curve on the up side". Lindsey: "a small rise ... in the fed funds rate". Summary: hold now, with a clear tightening bias and two members wanting to tighten. Direction: hawkish.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 ±7 (High).** Two formal dissents (Angell and Lindsey) in the same direction, with broad agreement among the others on holding. Summary: distinct but small camps, mainly on timing.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±8 (Moderate).** Bond-market vulnerability, credit conditions and equity levels are discussed by several members as context for the policy call. Not the focus of the meeting.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60 ±8 (Moderate).** Symmetric directive, but Greenspan says "the bias is [to move]" and Broaddus raises a tilt toward tightening next time. Mild tightening bias.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 (Moderate).** LaWare: "like trying to solve an equation with 23 unknowns". Greenspan: "this business cycle is different from anything any of us has seen". Elevated, with genuine mixed signals.
