# v4.1 discussion score: meeting 2006-08-08 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff-Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, the policy go-around with a statement-language discussion, an afternoon session on communications strategy (no conditions content), and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. The input is long (about 3,300 lines); I read all of it. No garbled passages that affect scoring (a few hard line breaks only).

**2. Era context.** Mid-2006: a tightening cycle of seventeen consecutive 25bp moves has taken the funds rate to 5.25%. Housing is correcting, growth is slowing, and core inflation is elevated. Taken only from the text: the decision is whether to pause or hike once more. This is scored from what participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Hawkish voices: Poole (broad-based inflation, but votes to hold), Moskow (prefers a hike), Lacker (dissents, wants a hike and statement D), Plosser (prefers a hike), Minehan (would hike), Fisher (accepts a pause only with hawkish language), Warsh, Stern.
- Pause, with upside inflation risk: Yellen, Kohn, Geithner, Kroszner, Bies, Guynn, Pianalto, Hoenig.
- The Chairman recommends a "hawkish pause" (alternative B, with section 3 of C).
- Vote: Lacker is the only No.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as clearly above objective and broad-based. Core PCE is 2.4%, at or above 2% since April 2004, with 83% of components rising (Fisher). Bernanke: "I certainly share that concern." Plosser: "unacceptably high inflation." Yellen: "well above my comfort zone." Some mitigation: Kohn, Geithner and Guynn expect gradual moderation. Clearly above target, with broadening pressures, puts this in the 61-75 band. Score about 68.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants say the economy is slowing to "near or a bit below potential" (Moskow), "slightly below its potential" (Yellen), "fluctuating around potential" (Lacker), or "slowing to potential or slightly below" (Bernanke, Geithner). Pianalto wants more slack than the Greenbook. Others (Lacker, Minehan, Kohn) say resource utilization is still high. Net: at potential, 48-52. Score 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 4.7-4.8%, near the NAIRU (Yellen: "just a bit below standard estimates"). Skilled workers are hard to find (Minehan, Plosser, Lacker, Guynn), and compensation and ULC are accelerating, but job growth has slowed. That is full employment with tight pockets, so slightly above 50. Score 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: The net picture is slowing. Housing is falling sharply (residential investment -14% annual rate) and consumption is soft with an energy squeeze (Kohn, Hoenig, Guynn). Business fixed investment is "pretty good" and Warsh is bullish. Growth is near or a little below trend, below the earlier torrid pace. Score 45.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed explicitly. Revised down somewhat (Yellen: structural 2.7% in the Greenbook, about 1/4 point lower; Bies: lower productivity with faster-rising labor costs), but still "very strong" (Kohn) and "still seems strong" (Geithner). Slightly below earlier views but near or above trend. Score 50, Moderate.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants call conditions accommodative or not restrictive. Moskow: "I talked to no one who thought that conditions are restrictive... financing is readily available." Minehan: "Financial markets remain accommodative... more accommodative than before." Plosser: policy not particularly restrictive. Hoenig says policy is "moderately restrictive", and Kohn argues a hold will not loosen conditions. Bright-line: accommodative means below 50. Score 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Warsh and Geithner talk about the Treasury rally and the curve inversion, which is yield and pricing commentary only. There is no dysfunction. Score 42, Low confidence, since the Treasury market is touched only as a pricing signal.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Moskow: "Risk spreads are low." Warsh: "credit spreads low." Geithner sees only "a little" moderation of exuberance in credit markets, and Fisher reports "continued high appetites for risk." Level is low, so below 50 (bright-line). Score 30.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Warsh covers earnings and profits and Fisher mentions buyout liquidity, but there is no valuation view or concern. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Guynn's bankers call credit quality "unsustainably" or "embarrassingly" good. Fisher mentions a "flush banking system." Bies notes some subprime delinquency rises and Warsh says delinquencies are encouraging. Healthy with minor subprime concern, so below 50. Score 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Poole ("Dollar depreciation would tend to promote higher prices"), Bernanke ("the declining dollar") and Geithner ("the dollar falling only a little") all describe modest depreciation. Below 50 (bright-line). Score 37, Low confidence (passing mentions).

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: Bernanke says goods inflation went from negative in 2005 to slightly positive in 2006 (apparel, used cars), and Poole lists many goods categories rising. Score 56.
- Services: Bernanke says non-energy, non-shelter services inflation "has been generally flat", while Warsh says non-energy services trends are "not our friend". Score 54.
- Housing: Shelter and OER are a main driver (48 of 71bp of the acceleration; Kroszner and Kohn also cite rents). Kroszner and Kohn expect it to be transitory, but the level is high. Score 72.
- Expectations: Mostly "well anchored" or "contained" (Stern, Yellen, Kroszner, Kohn). Lacker argues the levels are not reassuring, Warsh says "wobbly", and Moskow and Plosser fear drift. Anchored with an upside worry. Score 56.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: the decision is to hold at 5.25% with a tightening bias, a "hawkish pause." Moskow, Plosser, Minehan and Lacker favor a hike (Fisher accepts a pause only under conditions). Guynn and Pianalto say they would not dissent against a hike. The statement adds "some inflation risks remain." Hold with a tightening bias is the 51-60 band, pushed up by the strong inflation concern. Score 60.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Lacker). Three or four nonvoting presidents said they would hike (Minehan, Moskow, Plosser, and Fisher partly). Two reluctant pause supporters. The debate is civil and the economics are broadly shared (inflation concern, slowing growth), so the disagreement is about tactics. Score 38.
- Financial stability: limited. Housing, financial conditions, asset-price adjustment (Geithner), and subprime (Bies) come up, but not as a focus. Score 27.
- Forward guidance: "extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed"; "not the end of the process"; Geithner and Bernanke say a further increase is more likely than not. This is a mild tightening bias. Score 66.
- Uncertainty: "toughest decision" (Minehan, Kroszner), "highly uncertain" inflation outlook (Yellen), wide error bands (Guynn), "higher variance" (Bernanke), the Phillips curve breaking down. Much of it is genuine, and it is elevated but not extreme. Score 56.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 satisfies the "near potential" rule. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 is below 50 (accommodative). CREDIT_SPREADS 30 is below 50 (spreads low). BANKING_STRESS 32 is below 50 (healthy). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 37 is below 50 (depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS 42 is a normal-functioning score with no dysfunction.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 and LABOR 54 (4 apart, fine). OUTPUT_GAP 50 and CONS_INV 45 (5 apart, fine). FC 36 and CREDIT_SPREADS 30 both loose (consistent). FC and TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, Treasury near 40-45, fine. INFLATION_PRESSURE 68 versus sub-components (56, 54, 72, 56): the parent sits above the average because the broad-based, above-target character is described directly by many members, and the sub-component averages are dragged down by flat services. Mild divergence, noted.

**9. N/A.** EQUITY_VALUATION is not discussed as a valuation topic.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 68 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Moskow, "the latest reading on core PCE inflation is 2.4 percent. That's pretty high"; Plosser, "unacceptably high inflation seems likely to be around for a while". Center of gravity: broad-based, above comfort zone, expected to persist for months. Direction: above neutral because most members call inflation too high and broadening.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 50 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Lacker, "resources are approximately fully utilized"; Yellen, "growth to continue to run slightly below its potential rate". Center of gravity: slowing toward potential with views split between slightly below and at. Direction: at neutral.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 54 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "unemployment rate has risen to 4.8 percent, just a bit below standard estimates of the NAIRU"; Plosser, "difficulty in filling positions". Center of gravity: near NAIRU with tight skill pockets and accelerating wages. Direction: slightly above neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 45 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kohn, "Private domestic final purchases increased at a rate of only 2 percent"; Warsh, "consumer spending looked quite strong to most of them". Center of gravity: housing and consumption slowing, business investment firm. Direction: slightly below trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 50 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "structural productivity growth of 2.7 percent, about 1/4 percentage point slower"; Kohn, "Productivity growth remains very strong". Center of gravity: modest downward revision to a still solid trend. Direction: at neutral.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Moskow, "I talked to no one who thought that conditions are restrictive"; Minehan, "Financial markets remain accommodative". Center of gravity: loose to neutral, with real rates not high. Direction: below neutral (looser).
- **TREASURY_STRESS 42 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Warsh, "The Treasury markets have convinced themselves... of the prospects of lower growth, lower rates". Center of gravity: orderly repricing, no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 30 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Moskow, "Risk spreads are low"; Warsh, "credit spreads low". Center of gravity: spreads low with ample credit. Direction: below neutral by the stated level.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION N/A.** Not discussed as valuation.
- **BANKING_STRESS 32 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Guynn, credit quality "'unsustainably' or 'embarrassingly' good"; Bies, "subprime borrowers especially are having more difficulties". Center of gravity: sound with minor subprime worry. Direction: below neutral.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 37 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Geithner, "the dollar falling only a little"; Bernanke, "the effects of the declining dollar". Center of gravity: modest depreciation mentioned in passing. Direction: below neutral by the depreciation rule.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 56, INFLATION_SERVICES 54, INFLATION_HOUSING 72, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 56.** Evidence: Bernanke, goods "from a negative number in '05 to a slightly positive number in '06" and services "generally flat"; Bernanke, "of the 71 basis point increase in the core CPI... 48 basis points were associated with the shelter increase"; Stern, expectations "well anchored", versus Warsh, "a bit more wobbly than we would like".
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 60 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Bernanke, "I characterize this as a hawkish pause"; Lacker, "I favor a 25 basis point increase". Center of gravity: hold with a clear tightening bias and a minority wanting a hike. Direction: above balanced.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 38 +-8 [High].** Evidence: Lacker, "I... favor them enough to vote accordingly"; Guynn, "I do not intend to dissent today if the consensus is on the side of tightening". Center of gravity: one dissent plus several nonvoting hike preferences, with a shared assessment. Direction: moderate differences about tactics.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 27 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Geithner, "the rise in asset prices and residential investment and the leverage... may lead to a process of adjustment in asset prices"; Moskow, "Third, what about financial conditions?". Center of gravity: financial conditions and housing as context for policy, not the focus.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66 +-7 [High].** Evidence: statement, "The extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed"; Geithner, "We have to signal that inflation remains the predominant risk and that we may have more tightening to do." Center of gravity: pause explicitly not a stop. Direction: tightening signal.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 56 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "I view the inflation outlook as highly uncertain"; Bernanke, "our forecast may have a higher variance now". Center of gravity: widespread "close call" language and model doubts, with firm views on the inflation direction. Direction: elevated, not extreme.

Polarity checks: higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more inflation, and the evidence says more; FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 = looser, and the evidence says accommodative; CREDIT_SPREADS 30 = less stress, and the evidence says spreads low; BANKING_STRESS 32 = less stress, and the evidence says healthy.
