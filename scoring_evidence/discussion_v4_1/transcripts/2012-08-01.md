# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2012-08-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed, so some questions to staff appear without answers). It spans a policy-rules special topic, questions on financial markets and the economy, the economic go-round, a pre-policy summary by the Chairman, a policy go-round on Alternatives A and B, a vote read-out, and a final session on an experimental "consensus forecast". Only the economic go-round, the policy go-round and scattered remarks about conditions carry scorable content. The rules and consensus-forecast sessions are procedural or methodological and were ignored except where they reveal views on conditions or policy. The text is clean enough to read, apart from page headers and orphan one-line questions.

**2. Era context.** Summer 2012: a slow recovery with the policy rate at the zero bound, a $3 trillion balance sheet, the Maturity Extension Program (MEP) in place, "late 2014" calendar guidance, and the Committee weighing a further large-scale asset purchase (LSAP) program (Alternative A) versus holding with a forward-leaning statement (Alternative B). The backdrop is Europe, the fiscal cliff, a drought and a housing bottoming. The scores below come from what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Tarullo frames five propositions: the economy is "bogged down", downside risks are weightier, and a few good data points will not change that.
- Lockhart, Pianalto, George and Plosser describe moderate growth with uncertainty-driven caution.
- Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen, Raskin and Tarullo want more accommodation soon.
- Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Bullard, George, Pianalto and Stein have reservations about LSAP efficacy and costs.
- Kocherlakota argues for a supply-shock reading and less slack.
- Stein gives a detailed credit-spread analysis.
- Dudley, Duke and Powell give banking and sector color.
- Bernanke argues that slack is real and demand is the main problem.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- **INFLATION_PRESSURE.** Participants see inflation as stable, at or slightly below the 2 percent target (Pianalto "2 percent or a bit less", Powell "1.8 percent, low and under control", Williams and Yellen "below 2 percent"). Fisher: "trimmed mean...just shy of 2 percent". Kocherlakota and Lacker expect a rise, and Plosser, Fisher and George flag longer-term balance-sheet risk. The drought adds some food price pressure. The level is slightly below target and not concerning, so about 44.
- **OUTPUT_GAP.** Below potential. Bernanke: "output is below potential". Powell: "a material output gap". Tarullo and Evans: "bogged down", "headwinds". Kocherlakota, Lacker, Plosser (growth near potential) and Bullard (lower trend) dispute the size. The center of gravity is a clearly negative gap, with debate about its size, so about 36.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Unemployment is 8.2 percent ("unacceptably high", Raskin, Evans). Kocherlakota thinks there is less slack, but even his view is about early-2004 slack. Progress is described as "excruciatingly slow". Score in the 16-30 band, at 25.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.** Consumer spending and private final sales slowed appreciably (Raskin, Bernanke). Business capex and hiring are being deferred (Lockhart, Evans, Fisher, Plosser). Offsets are autos, housing and energy investment, with durables "more or less normal" per Bernanke. This is below trend, so 38.
- **PRODUCTIVITY.** There is lots of talk of trend or potential growth, structural damage and "lower trend", but almost none of productivity growth itself. Evans mentions capex being "productivity enhancing" in passing. N/A.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.** The picture is mixed. Cost and availability of capital are not a problem (Fisher). Banks are easing standards (Pianalto). Bernanke's summary: spreads "above pre-crisis norms that are consistent with fairly accommodative policy". Stein: conditions "somewhat more accommodative" than spreads suggest. Williams: indexes show "little additional progress" and sit nearer long-term averages, with "tight credit" a drag. Europe is whipsawing markets. The bright line (accommodative means below 50) holds. Score 44.
- **TREASURY_STRESS.** Treasuries come up as safe-haven demand and "moneyness" (Stein), and as capacity and market-functioning concerns about further LSAPs (Pianalto, Duke, Tarullo). Nobody describes actual dysfunction. Market functioning is a prospective cost, not a present problem. Score in the normal range, 36, with Low confidence.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS.** Level reading. Stein gives AA spreads at 114 bp versus a pre-crisis norm of 63, and B-rated at 617 bp versus 456, and calls them "somewhat above the typical values we saw before the crisis". Bernanke's summary says spreads "remain above pre-crisis norms". Stein adds that junk spreads look compressed relative to AA, which tempers the picture. The stated level is above average, so the score must be above 50. Score 57.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION.** Equities appear only as wealth-effect channel talk (Fisher: policy "lifted stock prices"), mutual fund outflows, and investors "underweight equities" (Powell). There is no valuation-concern commentary, so the default range applies: 48, Low confidence.
- **BANKING_STRESS.** US banks are "stronger, better capitalized, and more liquid" (Dudley, Duke, Bernanke summary). There are minor concerns about net interest margins, CRE rated "fair to poor", and deposit flight risk (Duke, Bullard). European bank stress is a separate theme. This is healthy with minor concerns, so 32. The bright line (healthy means below 50) holds.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** Dudley: "The dollar has strengthened", which implies softer exports. Powell: investors are "long the dollar". LSAP-induced dollar weakness is hypothetical (Yellen, Bernanke). Direction is mildly stronger, so above 50 and modest: 57, Low-to-Moderate confidence.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence is slightly below target, so below 50. Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential, and the evidence is below potential, so below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means a tighter market, and the evidence is 8.2 percent unemployment, so low. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter. The evidence is accommodative policy and ample capital with elevated spreads, so slightly below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and spreads are above pre-crisis norms, so above 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and banks are healthy, so below 50. All match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- **Policy lean.** The meeting holds (Alternative B) after the June MEP extension. Strong easing voices are Evans ("wholeheartedly"), Williams ("strongly favor alternative A"), Rosengren, Yellen ("take strong action at September"), Tarullo, Kocherlakota (supportive if made credible, wants a balanced approach) and Dudley (default to move in September). Many others defer: Lockhart (prefers open-ended LSAP, wait until September), Duke (full support for QE, wait), Raskin, Powell and Stein (open-minded). A hawkish minority opposes: Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Bullard (sees no urgency), George and Pianalto. The decision is to wait, with a forward-leaning statement. The QE debate is itself the dovish signal, with the decision deferred, so about 28.
- **Dissent.** One formal dissent: Lacker voted No on Alternative B, a hawkish dissent against the forward-guidance language. Eleven voted Yes. Beyond that there are open camps on LSAP efficacy and costs (Plosser, Fisher, Bullard vs Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen). There is also disagreement about slack and the supply versus demand diagnosis (Kocherlakota and Lacker vs Bernanke, Powell and Stein). Reservations on the vote from Raskin ("not without reservations") and Yellen. Williams says "our delay is only making matters worse". Distinct camps, 1 formal dissent, and disagreement that extends to the assessment give a score of about 50.
- **Financial stability attention.** Europe is a major theme (Bullard, Dudley, Williams), alongside Stein's lengthy credit-spread risk-premium analysis, concerns about market functioning and LSAP capacity, banks and deposits, and stability risks of prolonged low rates (Pianalto, George, Bullard on savers, Powell's "grab bag of stability issues"). It is not the dominant topic: the main focus is the macro outlook and policy design. Moderate, about 48.
- **Forward guidance.** The statement keeps "late 2014" (mid-2015 struck) and strengthens the easing language ("will provide additional accommodation as needed"). Several want it forward-leaning, and many want a September action. Lacker and Plosser object to calendar guidance on principle, but the signal that results is easing. Strong easing bias with some threshold and conditionality talk: about 11.
- **Uncertainty.** Very heavily emphasized. Tarullo: "unknown unknowns". Lacker: "staggering amount of uncertainty". Lockhart: "businesses...institutionalizing" uncertainty. Yellen: "enormous uncertainty". Bernanke: "a great deal of uncertainty". Plosser: "uncharted territory". There are wide fan charts, model uncertainty, and the fiscal cliff and Europe. Many emphasize it, though not quite crisis-level, so about 66.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: below potential, score below 50. Satisfied (36).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" language, score below 50. Satisfied (44).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: stated level above pre-crisis norms, score above 50. Satisfied (57). Direction words were not used to set the side.
- BANKING_STRESS: "stronger, better capitalized", score below 50. Satisfied (32).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "dollar has strengthened", score above 50. Satisfied (57).
- TREASURY_STRESS: no actual dysfunction described, score within 36-50. Satisfied (36).

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25): an 11 point gap, within 15, and consistent because unemployment at 8.2 percent is a hard anchor for labor while the gap carries more dispute (Kocherlakota, Plosser).
- OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38): consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (44) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (57): the spreads are moderately above pre-crisis norms while overall conditions are described as accommodative because of low rates, easing standards and cheap issuance. Mild divergence, explained by the policy-driven rate level and Stein's point that spreads overstate tightness.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal, as expected with no dysfunction.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (44) vs sub-components: goods (55, food and energy), services N/A, housing N/A, expectations (50). The parent is below the goods score because core and wage pressure are subdued and headline inflation is slightly below target. Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.**
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed only as trend or potential growth, not productivity growth.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-price commentary. Wages and compensation costs are mentioned only as "subdued", which is not enough.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices and a passing remark on rents (Rosengren) are not shelter-inflation commentary.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Powell, "I continue to see U.S. inflation, at 1.8 percent, as low and under control". Pianalto, "stable rate of underlying inflation of 2 percent or a bit less".
- Center-of-gravity: Most participants see inflation stable and just below target, with a few warning of upside risk; this is slightly below target and not concerning.
- Direction: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation runs "just below 2 percent" (Powell, Williams, Yellen).

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "the basic result that output is below potential". Powell, "there's a material output gap".
- Center-of-gravity: The majority see sizable slack; Kocherlakota, Lacker and Plosser see less but still not above potential.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because the economy is "bogged down" below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Raskin, "unemployment rate still above 8 percent". Yellen, "excruciatingly slow progress in lowering unemployment".
- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 8.2 percent with weak job growth; the dispute is about structural versus cyclical slack, not about the level.
- Direction: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is far above the sustainable rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Raskin, private domestic final sales "falling to its lowest growth rate in nearly two years". Evans, "any further expansion plans face a very high hurdle".
- Center-of-gravity: Slowing spending and deferred capex, partly offset by housing, autos and energy.
- Direction: Below neutral because spending is soft and below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 ±7 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Fisher, "I don't hear complaints about the cost of capital, nor do I hear concerns about the availability of capital". Williams, financial conditions indexes "generally nearer their long-term average levels".
- Center-of-gravity: Policy-driven low rates and easing bank standards make conditions accommodative, tempered by European stress and spreads above pre-crisis norms.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (looser) because capital is cheap and available.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 36 ±8 (Low confidence)**
- Evidence: Stein, "if there is powerful safe-haven demand and a scarcity of Treasuries...downward pressure on Treasury yields". Pianalto, further LSAPs "could have more adverse effects on market functioning".
- Center-of-gravity: Safe-haven yield compression and prospective market-functioning concerns; no actual dysfunction.
- Direction: In the normal range because yield declines reflect safe-haven demand and there is no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 57 ±6 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Stein, "the spread on the AA-rated to Treasuries...is 114 basis points versus a pre-crisis norm of 63". Bernanke's summary, "Credit spreads remain above pre-crisis norms".
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads sit moderately above pre-crisis norms, though Stein notes junk spreads look relatively compressed and issuance is healthy.
- Direction: Above neutral because the stated level is above the pre-crisis norm.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8 (Low confidence)**
- Evidence: Fisher, "our policy has obviously lifted stock prices". Powell, investors "underweight or short equities".
- Center-of-gravity: Equities appear only as a wealth-effect channel and positioning, with no valuation concern.
- Direction: Near neutral because there is no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 ±6 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "The U.S. banking system is also in better shape. It has more capital, it has more liquidity". Bernanke's summary, banks "stronger, better capitalized, and more liquid".
- Center-of-gravity: Sound US banks with margin compression and CRE caution; European bank stress is external.
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±6 (Low confidence)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "The dollar has strengthened, which suggests that U.S. export growth...is probably going to continue to soften". Powell, investors "long the dollar".
- Center-of-gravity: A modest dollar appreciation noted by a couple of participants.
- Direction: Above neutral because the dollar has strengthened.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55 (food and energy)**
- Evidence: George, drought effects on "food price inflation should emerge in two waves". Fisher, "very low expectation of significant sustainable upward price pressure on oil".
- Center-of-gravity: Rising food prices from the drought, flat-to-soft energy; stable overall.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50**
- Evidence: Yellen, "well-anchored inflation expectations". Bullard, "inflation and inflation expectations...pretty close to our target". George, breakevens "consistent with our stated 2 percent goal".
- Center-of-gravity: Anchored at target, with a few upside-risk worries.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Evans, "I favor alternative A today wholeheartedly". Yellen, "unless the outlook over the next six weeks improves substantially, we should prepare to take strong action at the September meeting". Counterweight: Plosser, "I see no further need for another round of policy accommodation".
- Center-of-gravity: A QE3 debate with a majority leaning toward action in September and a forward-leaning hold now; a hawkish minority resists.
- Direction: Dovish because the central question is how much more to ease.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 ±8 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: The vote read-out shows "President Lacker No" with eleven Yes. Kocherlakota on the consensus forecast: it "seems inconsistent with a balanced approach". Raskin, "My support wouldn't be without reservations".
- Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent, real camps on LSAP efficacy and on the slack/supply-versus-demand diagnosis, and several reservations among supporters.
- Direction: Notable disagreement, above the one-dissent baseline because it extends to the economic assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Stein devotes his go-round to credit risk premiums, "you might want to think about it from a financial stability perspective". Pianalto, "potential risk to financial stability that would be associated with a protracted period of very low interest rates".
- Center-of-gravity: Europe, spreads, market functioning and low-rate stability risks are discussed by many, but the meeting centers on the macro outlook and policy design.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 11 ±5 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Chairman Bernanke, on the final statement, "get rid of 'mid-2015'" but keep "provide additional accommodation as needed" and "keep the forward-leaning aspect". Yellen, the language is "sufficiently forward leaning to indicate our intention to act unless we do see significant improvement".
- Center-of-gravity: Calendar guidance to late 2014 retained and a bias toward additional accommodation signaled, with a September action expected unless the outlook improves.
- Direction: Easing signal; Lacker and Plosser object to the calendar form, not its direction.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±7 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Lacker, "A staggering amount of uncertainty associated with point forecasts of the economy". Bernanke, "a great deal of uncertainty here, a great deal of uncertainty". Plosser, "We are in uncharted territory".
- Center-of-gravity: Many participants stress Europe, the fiscal cliff, model uncertainty, and businesses frozen by lack of visibility; not Knightian but high.
