# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 1996-08-20 (transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). Contains the opening procedural items (minutes approval, Desk operations, swap-line/reverse-RP discussion), members' questions in the staff Q&A, the regional go-around, the Chairman's summary and policy proposal, the policy go-around, and the vote read-out. Staff turns are removed, so some members' questions and rejoinders appear without the staff answers. The text looks complete and readable. One passage (the Vice Chairman's remarks on swap lines, a few Greenspan lines on that topic) has small gaps from extraction, but these are procedural and do not affect scoring.

**Era context.** Mid-1996. Expansion in its sixth year, unemployment about 5.4-5.5%, fed funds at 5-1/4% after easing steps in the prior winter, long rates up and then down, Q2 growth very strong. The text says the same: strong Q2, slowing signs in July, tight labor markets, rising ECI, CPI near 3% (core about 2.85%), broad chain measures about 2%.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hawks wanting to tighten now: Broaddus (50bp, or 25bp as fallback), Melzer (50bp), Parry (at least 25bp), Hoenig (at least 25bp), Minehan (25bp, "course correction"), Stern (25bp, voted No).
- Holders with asymmetric "B" directive: Greenspan (proposes; says 25bp would not do much and a move would have to be 50-75bp, but a reversal of trend while the economy slows is undesirable), McDonough, Boehne, Lindsey, Jordan, Guynn, Kelley, Rivlin, McTeer, Phillips, Yellen, Meyer, Moskow.
- Doves/optimists on inflation: Meyer (NAIRU maybe about 5.5%, broad inflation about 2%), Kelley ("economic nirvana"), Rivlin, Guynn, McDonough (board view that cost pressures will not pass through).

**Dimension directions in words.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as somewhat above the comfort zone and facing upside risk. CPI is about 3% and core CPI about 2.85%, ECI wage pickup, minimum wage and food prices push up. Meyer and Phillips stress that broad measures are about 2% and improving, and Kelley notes it is "flat to down" so far. Melzer calls the current level "already unacceptable". Net modestly above midpoint, 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Parry says activity "somewhat above its long-run potential"; Moskow says "aggregate demand will exceed potential output"; Rivlin says in July growth was above potential and now the economy is "probably growing at about what we think is potential"; Kelley says "at or very close to capacity". Net slightly above potential, so bright line requires >50: 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Nearly every president reports tight labor markets, wage pickup, shortages of skilled workers (Minehan, Melzer, Moskow, Hoenig, Stern); unemployment about 5.4% and many judge it at or below NAIRU (staff, Yellen, Stern). The rubric UE band for 5.5% would give a low score, but participants uniformly describe "tight" with wage pressure, "below natural rate" per many. Compromise: 58.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Very strong Q2, moderating in July (autos, retail, housing softening, durable goods decelerating), but "level of activity still very high". Net just above trend: 55.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Debated. Anecdotes of strong gains (Stern, Guynn, Kelley, Phillips) versus data showing about 1% or lower (Meyer's half percent). Mixed, near trend: 47.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Phillips: "Financial markets generally continue to support growth", ample bank credit, decline in long rates; Melzer: policy accommodative; Minehan: buoyant equity markets. Offsets: Jordan's credit quality deterioration and Melzer's SLOS tightening of standards. "Supportive" means below 50: 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only long-term yield movements and the Chair's question to the Desk about dollar versus yields (procedural/staff Q&A). No dysfunction discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spread talk. Only credit availability: "banks generally appear to be providing ample credit" (Phillips) versus tightening standards (Melzer) and worsening credit quality (Jordan). Level not stated; slight lean below normal, Low confidence: 43.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Phillips says equity markets are "a quite reasonable source of capital" and a further correction would be "healthy"; Yellen says valuations rest on "highly optimistic" earnings expectations with a risk of "very significant correction"; Jordan says speculative-excess concern vanished after the Nasdaq adjustment. Valuation concern modest: 40.
- BANKING_STRESS: Jordan reports higher delinquencies, bankruptcies, rising chargeoffs, expected lower bank profitability for 1997; Stern and Lindsey discuss credit card/small business borrowing risk; Phillips says banks ample credit and adjusting terms; Fisher/Minehan note bank balance sheet restructuring. Mixed, near neutral and slightly below on the system-soundness side: 46. Bright line not triggered (no "well-capitalized" or "stressed" wording).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Lindsey: "The dollar tends to lose when the mark appreciates"; weaker dollar risk; Greenspan notes dollar weakened on some days despite rising rates; Kelley mentions a "lower dollar" would be needed for pricing power (not in view). Mild weakening: 42, Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services, housing not discussed as categories. Food and energy are mentioned in passing (Moskow, Phillips) but not scored as a category. Expectations are touched only indirectly (Guynn, Melzer, "built into expectations"), not assessed. All N/A.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Outcome is hold with asymmetric directive (tightening bias); 6 of 19 speakers want to tighten now; most of the rest say tightening is likely "when rather than whether" (Rivlin) or hold while awaiting data. Hold with tightening bias: 56.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Stern, wants 25bp). Five more non-voting presidents prefer tightening now (Broaddus, Melzer, Parry, Hoenig, Minehan), and Jordan contests the Greenbook framework sharply but supports the hold. Camps differ on policy timing more than on the economic assessment (Meyer and Yellen on NAIRU versus the staff). One dissent plus visible camps: 42.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Limited. Credit card/small-business borrowing (Stern, Lindsey, Minehan), credit quality (Jordan), equity valuation (Yellen, Phillips), EMU speculation risk (Lindsey). Mostly side topics in a 1996 meeting centered on inflation and labor: 15.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Asymmetric directive toward restraint; Moskow says Sept and Nov are "crucial decision points"; Meyer, Yellen, Phillips say tightening is called for if demand fails to slow or inflation rises; Guynn is comfortable with asymmetry. Mild to moderate tightening bias: 63.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Yellen: "fog of uncertainty", "level of uncertainty ... is extremely high"; Meyer: still uncertain on NAIRU, "knife edge"; Phillips: "more data but not more information"; Boehne: "far from clear"; Greenspan: "forecast error ranges easily encompass either scenario". Many participants stress it, though some language is standard for the era: 58.

**Polarity checks.** Labor 58: higher = tighter; evidence says tight, so consistent. Financial conditions 40: supportive, so below 50 consistent. Dollar 42: weakening, below 50, consistent. Banking stress 46: mixed, neutral. Equity 40: modest valuation concern on the high-valuation side, below 50 consistent with the rubric direction (higher = more stress/undervaluation).

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP >50 (55) satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS "supportive" <50 (40) satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS: no level stated as high; 43 is below 50, consistent with "ample credit". BANKING_STRESS: no trigger phrase. DOLLAR: weakening gives <50 (42) satisfied. TREASURY: N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 58 vs CONSUMPTION 55: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 43: co-move. Treasury N/A fine. Parent inflation score has no sub-components to reconcile.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS (no market-function discussion), INFLATION_GOODS/SERVICES/HOUSING/EXPECTATIONS (not discussed as categories).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Broaddus: "the pressure on resources is still likely to push the trend rate of inflation up a bit, perhaps to above 3 percent"; Meyer: broad measures "about 2 percent over the past year".
Center of gravity: Current inflation moderate with a clearly seen upside trend and wage pressures.
Direction: above neutral because most members expect inflation to rise and see it as already uncomfortable, though broad measures are benign.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Parry: "level of activity will remain somewhat above its long-run potential"; Moskow: "aggregate demand will exceed potential output".
Center of gravity: Economy modestly above to at potential and slowing.
Direction: above neutral because several state the economy is above potential/capacity while others say "about at potential".

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +-6 [High]**
Evidence: Melzer: "Tight labor markets continue to be the rule in the Eighth District"; Minehan: employers "find it hard to hire skilled and in some cases even clerical workers".
Center of gravity: Tight markets, wage acceleration, unemployment about 5.4% regarded by many as at or below NAIRU.
Direction: above neutral because tightness is reported across nearly all districts.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Broaddus: "a moderation from a very robust rate of expansion in the second quarter"; Moskow: "Growth in consumer spending slowed considerably in July".
Center of gravity: Strong but decelerating spending and investment.
Direction: above neutral because the level is high even as momentum fades.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 47 +-8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Stern: business community talks of "tremendous productivity improvements"; Meyer: "Productivity growth over the last three years has been very low, below 1 percent."
Center of gravity: Official data weak, anecdotes strong; members split.
Direction: roughly neutral/slightly below because measured trend is weak but many believe it is understated.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Phillips: "Financial markets generally continue to support growth"; "Banks generally appear to be providing ample credit".
Center of gravity: Supportive conditions with some credit quality concerns.
Direction: below neutral because conditions are described as supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a market-functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 43 +-10 [Low]**
Evidence: Phillips: banks "providing ample credit"; Melzer: "tightening of lending standards" in the Senior Loan Officer Survey.
Center of gravity: No spread talk; credit availability slightly favorable.
Direction: below neutral because the only level-type language is ample availability.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Yellen: "Present market valuations appear to be based on earnings expectations that are highly optimistic"; Phillips: "equity markets remain a quite reasonable source of capital".
Center of gravity: Valuations somewhat rich but not alarming after the summer correction.
Direction: below neutral (valuation concern side).

**BANKING_STRESS: 46 +-8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Jordan: "slower payments, higher delinquencies, increased bankruptcy filings, and larger allowances for chargeoffs"; Phillips: banks adjusting terms with ample credit.
Center of gravity: Emerging consumer credit-quality concerns inside a generally sound system.
Direction: roughly neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 +-8 [Low]**
Evidence: Lindsey: "The dollar tends to lose when the mark appreciates"; Greenspan: "the dollar weakened as a result" on some days.
Center of gravity: Mild weakness and risk of more.
Direction: below neutral because the discussion is about dollar weakening.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** Not discussed as categories.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 +-6 [High]**
Evidence: Greenspan: "if we do choose alternative B ... I trust we will continue our asymmetric bias"; Broaddus: "I still favor some tightening ... 50 basis point move".
Center of gravity: Hold with a tightening bias; a sizable minority wants to tighten now.
Direction: above neutral because the stance is a hold with an upward bias and six speakers want an immediate rise.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-6 [High]**
Evidence: Roll call: "President Stern No"; Jordan: "I do not think that we would be correct in starting off on an installment plan now".
Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus visible disagreement among non-voters, differing mainly on timing and the NAIRU reading.
Direction: moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 15 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Stern on small-business credit-card borrowing; Yellen on risk of "a very significant correction" in stocks.
Center of gravity: Several side comments but not a focus.
Direction: low attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Rivlin: "it is a question of when rather than whether"; Moskow: "the September and the November meetings very crucial decision points".
Center of gravity: Asymmetric directive and widely shared expectation of eventual tightening.
Direction: above neutral (tightening signal).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Yellen: "the level of uncertainty about the future course of inflation ... is extremely high"; Greenspan: "Current forecast error ranges easily encompass either scenario".
Center of gravity: Many members stress two unresolved questions (slowdown, NAIRU), though in familiar terms.
Direction: elevated.
