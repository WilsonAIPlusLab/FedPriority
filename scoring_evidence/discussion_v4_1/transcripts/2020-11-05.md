# Part 2: Evidence and Scores (v4.1 rubric + V4.1.1 clarifications; SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

Document type: two-day meeting transcript (member turns only; staff turns removed). Contents: a non-decisional asset-purchase round, a financial-stability round, an economic-outlook round, SEP communications comments, and the policy go-round with a roll call (10 Yes, 0 No) on alternative B. It is not special-topic: there is a policy-stance go-round and a policy vote. Staff-reported content was ignored.

## Economic conditions

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-6 High
- Powell: "Inflation has moved up a bit but remains below our 2 percent objective"; Quarles: "PCE inflation obviously is still well below our 2 percent target"; Clarida: core PCE "now running at a 1.5 percent annual rate"; Feldman: "little pressure on inflation"; Daly: "inflation is barely back to its pre-pandemic levels."
- Offsetting: Mester/Brainard note durable-goods (used car) inflation strong, but services and rents are soft. Net: below target, no broad pressure.

OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-6 High
- Mester: "the level of real GDP remains 3½ percent below its peak"; "levels of output and employment are still far below their pre-pandemic levels"; Quarles: "we are still far from meeting our full employment goal"; Evans: "closing resource gaps over the near term."
- Below potential but recovering rapidly: below 50 per the bright-line rule.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 +-6 High
- Quarles: unemployment "7.9 percent—that's the 90th percentile of the postwar monthly distribution"; Mester: payroll employment "7 percent below its level in February"; Brainard: "true unemployment rate would be 10.7 percent."
- Isolated hiring difficulty (Mester, Barkin: "biggest shortages... are in labor") does not offset a UE near 8%. Below full employment.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-8 Moderate
- Clarida: Q3 "very robust 33 percent annual rate", consumption "up 40 percent"; Barkin: "residential market has been extremely robust... Retail sales have been buoyant."
- Offsets: Brainard: services "well below pre-pandemic levels", outlook "weaker"; Bostic: "strategic investment plans have been shelved"; Kaplan: "recent weakness... in consumer spending." Net around trend: strong goods, housing and equipment against weak services and structures.

PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
- Only passing firm-level remarks (Mester on workforce variability); no participant view on productivity growth.

## Financial conditions

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-8 Moderate
- Williams: "quite favorable financial conditions in capital markets", but "banks have tightened lending conditions"; Brainard: financing "for residential real estate and large corporations... remain generally accommodative, bank lending conditions have tightened further"; George: "accommodative financial conditions in place"; Kaplan: "Loose conditions in financial markets."
- Accommodative overall (below 50); partly offset by bank tightening (SLOOS) and VIX touching 40 (Brainard).

TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 Moderate
- Mester: Desk indicators "suggest that these markets have essentially returned to normal functioning"; Bostic: markets "functioning"; Quarles: "market functioning and liquidity obviously have generally returned to normal" but "continued vulnerability in the Treasury market."
- March dysfunction (Brainard, Quarles, Mester) is retrospective; present functioning normal with latent fragility. Score in the normal range.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 +-9 Low-to-Moderate (reported as Moderate)
- Quarles states a level: "valuations on corporate bonds and equity are near the middle of their historical distribution"; downgrades "reached pre-COVID-event levels."
- Williams: "corporate bond spreads come way down since March" (direction), but "lending conditions have tightened considerably, according to the SLOOS" (availability). Level near normal; bank availability pushes slightly up, narrowed capital-market spreads push down. UNC kept at +-9 because part of this rests on lending-availability language.

EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-7 Moderate
- Quarles: valuations of corporate bonds "and equity are near the middle of their historical distribution"; Rosengren: "stock prices have slumped"; Brainard: stock market "down, on net, over the intermeeting period"; Bowman cites staff "increased asset valuation pressures."
- Valuation view is roughly fair value; no bubble or distress language.

BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-8 Moderate
- Mester: "the financial system has come through this shock much better than expected"; Quarles: "I see banks and households remaining resilient"; "CET1 capital ratios and loan loss provisions are now materially above" prior levels.
- Counter-view: Feldman: "We remain concerned about the condition of large banks"; Mester: "levels of nonperforming loans and insolvencies can be expected to rise"; Rosengren/Brainard on tightening credit; Quarles on leverage-ratio constraints. Resilient now, concerns ahead: below 50.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- No participant discusses the dollar or FX.

## Inflation sub-components (value only)

INFLATION_GOODS: 68
- Mester: "pushed up durable goods inflation to its highest level since 1995"; Bostic: used auto prices "rising at a record annualized rate of 75 percent"; Brainard: durable prices "rose notably more than expected."

INFLATION_SERVICES: 32
- Mester: "services price inflation to be at the lower part of its range over the past 10 years"; Brainard: "services price inflation remains soft"; Quarles: "Inflation within the services sector... has been soft."

INFLATION_HOUSING: 30
- Bostic: rent inflation "has slowed more than ½ percentage point since February"; Brainard: "a slowdown of housing services inflation"; George: risk of "a sizable decline in rents and housing inflation"; Williams: "dramatic drops in rents."

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44
- Quarles: "Market-based inflation expectations have also returned to their pre-COVID range, and the staff's broader measures... stable"; Brainard: longer-term readings "mixed", market-based "closer to pre-pandemic levels"; Williams/Brainard/Daly: expectations shifted up since the framework. Roughly anchored, a bit under target.

## Discussion-specific

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 27 +-8 High
- Powell: "our highly accommodative policy stance as appropriate... Policy... is in a good place for now"; "solid support for alt-B as written." Williams: "maintain a highly accommodative monetary policy stance for quite some time." Barkin: "keep our pedal to the metal"; Evans: "cannot be shy about aggressively using all of our tools."
- Asset purchases continue at the current pace, with many ready to add accommodation if the outlook worsens (Rosengren, Kaplan, Feldman, Powell: "forceful strengthening of our asset purchase program"). Hawkish-side offsets are about eventual, not current, moves: Bostic would scale back purchases "somewhat"; Bowman and Quarles on eventual gradual slowing; Harker on eventual unwinding. Majority maintains maximum accommodation: strong-dovish band, lifted for the tapering talk.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-7 High
- Roll call: 10 Yes, 0 No on alternative B ("solid support for alt-B as written", Powell). Harker: "I support alternative B as written, but I still have a few concerns" (wants "or"/drop maximum-employment reference); Bullard on SEP; Bostic "question the efficacy" of purchases; George prefers date-based guidance; Bostic/Barkin sceptical of purchases.
- Differences on asset-purchase design and communication, in a non-decisional discussion; no formal dissent and no dispute on the policy stance.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68 +-7 High
- A full agenda item on the Financial Stability Report with about nine speakers at length (Mester, Rosengren, Feldman, George, Kaplan, Quarles, Daly, Brainard, plus Williams in the policy round); bank capital, nonbank fragilities, Treasury market, climate risk. Reserves/bank balance-sheet capacity and risk-taking also featured in the asset-purchase round (Quarles, Bowman, Barkin).
- Prominent topic, not dominant over the economic assessment.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-6 High
- Outcome-based rate guidance (September) is repeatedly endorsed: Brainard: "pledging to provide accommodation until shortfalls from maximum employment have been eliminated and average inflation of 2 percent has been achieved"; Powell: "very strongly committed to using our very powerful tools until the job is well and truly done"; asset purchases "at least at the current level until we reach some state-based goal."
- Mild hawkish drift only on the balance sheet: Bowman "begin signaling a move toward a gradual slowing in the pace of our asset purchases" in H1 2021; Quarles prefers an earlier taper date than markets expect; Harker "scaling back in 2021". Strong easing commitment on rates dominates.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 75 +-7 High
- Clarida: "the economic outlook is remarkably uncertain and really more uncertain than I would've thought"; Williams: "an extraordinarily high degree of uncertainty"; Harker: "an uncertainty that we have never dealt with"; Evans: "We are flying blind with regard to many such questions"; Mester: "adds considerable uncertainty"; Barkin: "a wider range of outcomes than normal."
- Many participants emphasize deep uncertainty (virus, fiscal, election) while baseline views stay fairly confident (Bullard, Quarles); high but below the March 2020 level.

## Coherence checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (33) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (27): 6 apart, consistent. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (46): 13 apart, explained by strong goods/housing spending in a still-depressed-output economy.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (48): both near-neutral to slightly loose; bank tightening vs capital-market ease explains the gap. TREASURY_STRESS in the normal range, consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (33): goods high (68) against services/housing soft (32/30) and expectations near anchored (44); parent below target is consistent.
