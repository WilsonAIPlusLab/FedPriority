# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 scoring: transcript discussion, meeting 2010-06-23

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). The text has four blocks. (a) A day-one balance-sheet discussion: Q&A with the Desk, then a go-round on MBS coupon swaps, Treasury redemption options 1-4 and MBS sales. (b) Questions after the staff chart show, then the economic go-around. (c) The Chair's summary, followed by his remarks on labor-market hysteresis and on symmetry of more versus less stimulus. (d) The policy go-round on alternatives A and B, statement edits, and a vote read-out of 9-1, with Hoenig voting No. No garbled passages affect scoring. Staff answers are missing, so some Q&A is one-sided, but the member content is clear.

**Era context.** June 2010. The recovery is about a year old, the funds rate is at zero, and the Fed holds a large balance sheet (MBS purchases). The European sovereign debt turmoil appeared during the intermeeting period. These notes are for orientation only; the scores below rest on what members say.

**Speakers.** All participants speak: Bernanke, Dudley, Kohn, Warsh, Duke, Tarullo, and the Reserve Bank presidents (Lockhart, Plosser, Rosengren, Pianalto, Fisher, Evans, Yellen, Bullard, Hoenig, Kocherlakota, Lacker). Common view: a moderate recovery with a weaker modal forecast and higher downside risk from Europe. Labor slack is large and inflation is low and falling, with some deflation concern (Rosengren, Pianalto, Kocherlakota, Kohn). Hawkish dissent comes from Hoenig. Lacker and Plosser say tightening will come earlier than the Tealbook assumes, and Fisher wants to keep the option of MBS sales. Dudley, Kohn, Yellen, Rosengren and Pianalto want contingency planning for more easing.

**Dimension direction statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as well below the midpoint (below target, disinflation). Pianalto: median CPI at 0.5 percent y/y, an "all-time low". Kohn has headline inflation near 1 percent for three years. Yellen calls it "undesirably low". Bernanke: "upside risks to inflation are now very, very low". Concern is about disinflation or deflation, not acute deflation. Warsh sees no downward price spiral.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below the midpoint. Yellen: "substantial slack"; economy "far below maximum sustainable employment". Kohn: "a long, slow climb out of a very deep hole". Evans: slack outweighs the anchor of expectations. Growth is moderate, so the gap is closing slowly. Bright-line rule (below potential, so <50) is satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: well below the midpoint. Unemployment is 9.7 percent (Kocherlakota). Yellen expects about 9.5 percent at year-end and 46 percent of the unemployed out of work 27 weeks or more. Bernanke: recovering "painfully slowly".
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: somewhat below trend or moderate. Household spending is "moderately strong" (Bernanke) but flat or modest per retailers (Yellen). Equipment and software spending is strong but expected to slow. Housing is weak after the tax credit, and business capex for expansion is weak (Fisher, Warsh). Net is slightly below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed by Yellen at length, with Bernanke and Fisher touching it. Productivity gains have been extraordinary and may be the "new normal"; the staff expects a sharp slowdown. Above trend as perceived now.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: tighter than at the previous meeting but not tight in absolute terms. Bernanke: "financial conditions became less supportive". Dudley: "Financial conditions have tightened". Kohn: cost of capital up and LIBOR up. Offsets: record-low mortgage rates and Treasury yields, and Fisher says conditions are "much better" than when the MBS program began. Slightly tight direction, 55-65 band.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction. Yields fell on flight to safety, which is not stress. Bernanke notes that in Treasuries "there are essentially no fails". Low score, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (level reading): the level is mixed. Bernanke: "somewhat tighter corporate credit". Duke: "unusually high spreads for C&I loans seem to have peaked", and spreads "are not as wide as they were". Evans: funding available for quality borrowers, while construction loans are hard to get. No stated historically wide or tight level for market spreads. Slightly above normal, 55.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: price decline with little valuation commentary. Bullard: equities "declined precipitously", seen as temporary. Warsh: retail investors stepping back, assets about 10 percent off peaks. Default 45-55, nudged up for the decline and fear.
- BANKING_STRESS: improving. Duke: delinquencies have peaked, classified assets are leveling off, the FDIC problem list is growing more slowly, and bankers believe they have "more than enough capital". Concerns: CRE (Lockhart), bank credit "extraordinarily weak" (Kohn), Warsh expecting weaker big-bank Q2 profits and tough term funding, Europe exposure, Basel and FASB uncertainty. Healthy with concerns, so below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: dollar appreciating on safe haven flows, mentioned by Bullard, Kohn, Dudley, Yellen, Rosengren, Bernanke. Above 50 per bright-line.
- INFLATION sub-components: Goods are weakly addressed (commodity and energy prices moderating, import prices tame, Fisher's transport-cost and Chinese-price remarks offset by no pass-through). Services get one passing remark (Bernanke: public-service fees are among the few areas with increases). Housing inflation not discussed, so N/A. Expectations are described as stable or well anchored with some downside drift (Pianalto, Kohn, Kocherlakota).

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: stay at zero (alternative B) with "extended period" kept. Several want contingency planning for more easing (Dudley, Kohn, Yellen, Rosengren, Pianalto, Kocherlakota, Evans). Kohn would cut the rate if above zero and floats cutting IOER. A hawkish minority argues for dropping the language or opening asset sales (Hoenig; Lacker and Plosser on balance sheet and earlier tightening; Bullard and Warsh partly hawkish on specifics). Majority is dovish hold with an easing bias but no easing action, around 30.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Hoenig). Visible disagreement over MBS sales, Treasury maturity shortening and "extended period" is mostly about tactics. Economic assessment is shared. Mild-moderate, in the typical one-dissent range with a bit extra for the balance-sheet debate.
- Financial stability emphasis: Europe, money market fund exposure, bank stress tests, Basel, a staff financial stability section and memos praised by several, Hoenig's low-rates-for-long imbalances concern with a long Bernanke reply, and Warsh's financial-market risk list. Prominent, short of dominant.
- Forward guidance: "extended period" retained and defended (Yellen, Evans, Warsh); Kohn fears it will last an "extended period"; some want to strengthen it (Kocherlakota). Hoenig wants it removed, and normalization planning continues. Net low-for-long, easing-leaning.
- Uncertainty: widely cited as elevated because of Europe (Lockhart "uncertainty is a whole lot greater", Evans "uncertainty is high", Kocherlakota "increased", Kohn, Pianalto, Warsh "very, very unsettled"). Modal forecasts are largely intact, Plosser pushes back on gloom, and Bullard sees muted risk. Elevated, not extreme.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; my evidence is low and falling inflation, so the score is below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter; unemployment is near 10 percent, so the score is very low. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; conditions are described as tightened, so the score is above 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress; slightly wider spreads at a modestly elevated level put it above 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress; improving credit and sound capital put it below 50. Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means stronger dollar; appreciation puts it above 50. All consistent.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential, so <50 (32). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS tightened, so >50 (57). CREDIT_SPREADS: a level is stated only as "unusually high" C&I spreads that have peaked, plus "somewhat tighter corporate credit", so >50 (55). BANKING_STRESS: bankers are well-capitalized and delinquencies have peaked, so <50 (42). DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciation, so >50 (62). TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, so <55 (30).

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (32) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (18): a 14-point gap. Unemployment near 10 percent with long-term unemployment at record levels is more extreme than the product-market gap described, and several members suspect partly structural unemployment, which makes the labor score extra low.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (32) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42): 10 points; moderate growth is closing the gap slowly.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (57) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (55): co-move.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS (30): stress limited to risk assets, with Treasuries functioning normally.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (24) vs sub-components (goods 36, services 40, expectations 42): the parent is lower because members emphasize core and trimmed-mean readings at record lows and deflation risk. The sub-component scores are thin evidence and sit near neutral, so the parent is not the weighted average; this is explained rather than forced.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_HOUSING: members never discuss rents or shelter prices. All other dimensions have at least some participant content.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Pianalto: median CPI "dropped to 0.5 percent on a year-over-year basis in May, and that's an all-time low." Yellen: "inflation is undesirably low and trending lower."
- Summary: broad disinflation with deflation risk rising, but not a spiral.
- Direction: inflation pressure is below neutral because core measures are at record lows and many members forecast inflation under target for years.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "The economy's substantial slack will continue to weigh on inflation." Kohn: "a long, slow climb out of a very deep hole."
- Summary: large slack closing slowly in a moderate recovery.
- Direction: the output gap is below neutral because members describe substantial slack in labor and product markets.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "In May, national unemployment was 9.7 percent." Yellen: 46 percent of the unemployed out of work 27 weeks or more, "an extremely disconcerting situation".
- Summary: very high unemployment with painfully slow improvement.
- Direction: labor is far below neutral tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: retailers report spending "flat or growing at only a very modest pace." Tarullo: consumer spending, manufacturing and equipment and software "remain solid".
- Summary: moderate net spending, with equipment strong, housing weak, and a slowdown expected.
- Direction: spending is slightly below trend because strengths are offset by housing and a weak outlook for capex.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "extraordinary productivity gains of 2009 and early this year"; contacts say gains are "the new normal". Bernanke: "The staff predicts low productivity growth from here."
- Summary: recent productivity gains were strong, with debate over whether they persist.
- Direction: perceived productivity growth is above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "financial conditions became less supportive". Dudley: "Financial conditions have tightened, and tail risks have increased."
- Summary: conditions tightened during the intermeeting period because of Europe, but remain accommodative in level terms (low yields and mortgage rates).
- Direction: financial conditions are somewhat above neutral (tighter) because members note a tightening, offset by still-low rates.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "In the Treasury market ... there are essentially no fails in that market." Bullard: flight-to-safety effect on Treasury yields.
- Summary: Treasuries are functioning normally, with yields down on safe-haven demand.
- Direction: Treasury stress is below neutral because no dysfunction is described. Evidence is thin.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "somewhat tighter corporate credit". Duke: "the unusually high spreads for C&I loans seem to have peaked even for smaller loans."
- Summary: spreads somewhat wider than normal and edging wider in markets, while bank loan spreads are easing from elevated levels.
- Direction: spreads are slightly above neutral because the level is described as high but past peak; the evidence is scattered.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "U.S. equity prices declined precipitously during the intermeeting period", seen as temporary. Warsh: retail investors "stepping back from the equity markets".
- Summary: a sharp decline with fear but no valuation judgment.
- Direction: equity valuation stress is slightly above neutral because of the drop and risk aversion, without distress language.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Duke: "Delinquency rates appear to have peaked in all loan categories except commercial real estate." Warsh: big-bank Q2 profits likely "below expectations and meaningfully so".
- Summary: credit quality is improving and capital seen as ample, with CRE, profit, funding and regulatory risks.
- Direction: banking stress is below neutral because members describe a sound and improving system with specific concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: the weaker outlook "appreciated the dollar". Dudley: "depreciation of the euro versus the dollar".
- Summary: dollar strengthened on safe-haven flows and is seen as a drag on exports and prices.
- Direction: dollar strength is above neutral because members report appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36 [value only]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "prices of globally traded commodities have moderated". Fisher: input prices up modestly "very little pass-through to consumer prices."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 40 [value only]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: the few price increases were in "public services, such as public transportation and education". Thin evidence.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
- Not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 [value only]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "inflation expectations remain well anchored". Pianalto: the Cleveland model shows "a drop of over 15 basis points at all horizons less than 10 years."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "if the nominal funds rate were above zero, I would be advocating that we reduce it." Hoenig: "it's very important that we take this 'extended period' language out."
- Summary: policy held at zero with easing contingency planning favored by most; hawkish minority led by Hoenig, with Lacker and Plosser leaning toward earlier tightening.
- Direction: policy lean is below neutral (dovish) because the majority favors maintaining accommodation and considering more.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 37 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: vote read-out "President Hoenig No"; Hoenig on dropping "extended period" while Yellen "strongly support[s] maintaining" it.
- Summary: one formal dissent plus a spirited but civil balance-sheet debate, with agreement on the economic assessment.
- Direction: disagreement is moderate, above the minimum because of the dissent and the multi-sided asset-sales debate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren on money market funds and European bank exposure; Bernanke's long reply on imbalances: "the potential risk to financial stability from low interest rates over a long period of time."
- Summary: European turmoil, bank stress tests, regulation and leverage risks all get sustained attention.
- Direction: attention is prominent, though economic assessment still dominates.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 25 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "I strongly support maintaining the 'extended period' language." Kohn: "I fear the 'extended period' is going to be appropriate for an extended period."
- Summary: low-for-long retained with possible strengthening; only mild offset from balance-sheet normalization planning.
- Direction: the forward signal is below neutral (easing-leaning).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lockhart: "risk is higher, and uncertainty is a whole lot greater." Warsh: the euro zone situation "remains very, very unsettled."
- Summary: many members stress elevated uncertainty from Europe and tail risks, while central forecasts are intact.
- Direction: uncertainty is elevated, well above normal, short of the crisis range.
