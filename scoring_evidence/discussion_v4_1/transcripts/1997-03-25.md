# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript (meeting dated 1997-03-25)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A questions, a full economic go-around by Reserve Bank presidents and governors, the Chairman's policy statement, a policy go-around, a drafting exchange on the press announcement, and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed. The text reads cleanly and I saw no garbled passages.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1990s expansion (the speakers refer to the seventh year of the expansion). Participants see a strong economy, a very low unemployment rate, low and edging-down measured inflation, and a debate about whether to tighten after a long hold. Scores below rest on what the speakers say.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chair McDonough, Governors Meyer, Kelley, Phillips and Rivlin, and presidents Broaddus, Jordan, Hoenig, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Guynn, Boehne, Stern, Melzer and McTeer. The Chairman proposes +25bp with a symmetric directive. All 10 voters say Yes.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation as subdued, flat to edging down ("inflation is flat to down" - Kelley; "remarkably quiescent for now" - Boehne; no pricing power). They also see a clearly rising risk of acceleration (pipeline, wage, commodity pressures; Phillips: "moved further into the inflation alert zone"). I score what is reported now: near neutral, at 50, with a wide band because the risk talk pulls the other way.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants describe an economy at full utilization, growing above potential ("well beyond anybody's estimate of potential" - Kelley; "above-potential growth" - Minehan; "excess demand pressures most likely are building" - Parry). Greenspan: "not at this stage moving into... an overheated boom." Above 50, modestly.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Tight. "Exceedingly tight" (Broaddus), "labor shortages" (Moskow, Stern), regional rates of 3-4.3%, national unemployment projected near 5%, Greenspan "tightness in labor markets is increasing... running out of qualified people." The national rate is around 5%, which the rubric puts at 46-52, but the language is strongly tight, so I place it at the lower edge of the tight range.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Consumer confidence at its highest since 1965 (Broaddus), consumption burst, wealth effects, business investment "unusually robust" (McDonough), construction "rising sharply." Above trend but not a boom (Greenspan), so 60s.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length. Greenspan: productivity "badly underestimated and indeed may actually be accelerating"; Jordan: "favorable productivity surprise"; steel firm 27% over three years. Above trend, 60s. Stern doubts how widespread it is.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Stern: "financial conditions remain very comfortable." Melzer: "continuing easy credit market." Greenspan: falling risk premiums and a low cost of capital. Loose, so below 50 (bright line satisfied).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only long rates rising with the dollar and bond market anticipation of policy are mentioned (Meyer, Greenspan). That is no dysfunction. N/A because the Treasury market is not discussed as a market-functioning topic.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No direct spread talk. There is level evidence of easy credit terms: "slippage of underwriting standards" (Boehne), construction "lending standards... have disappeared" (Jordan), Greenspan's falling risk premiums. Level below normal, so below 50 (bright line satisfied). The evidence is indirect, so confidence is Low/Moderate.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stock run-up and wealth effects are discussed. Greenspan's valuation comment is: "high market values are better than low market values. What we are trying to avoid is bubbles that break." He also says the market's growth projections for high tech "are unrealistic." Mild overvaluation concern, so a bit below 50 on this scale (lower means more overvaluation concern).
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are not stressed, but there are minor concerns: margins eroding, standards slipping, developers "forgetting the lessons of the 1980s" (Guynn), a card issuer's problems (Boehne), slight rise in consumer delinquencies (McDonough). "Healthy with minor concerns," so below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Participants note a strong or firming dollar (Broaddus "strong dollar"; Parry "high dollar"; Greenspan "the dollar has been firming"; Meyer: long rates "risen along with the dollar"). Above 50, modestly.

**5. Polarity checks.** FC and credit spreads: loose conditions imply low scores. Equity: overvaluation concern implies a low score on this scale, and concern is mild. Banking: sound with minor concerns implies a score under 50. Dollar: firming implies over 50. Labor: tight implies high. All consistent.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: Unanimous decision to tighten 25bp, the first move after a long hold. Several wanted more (Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Jordan implicitly) and several would have preferred an asymmetric directive. Greenspan says better-than-even odds of another move. The Chair also argued against 50bp. Moderate hawkish, just above 60.
- Dissent: Zero formal dissents, 10-0 vote. Differences are about the size of the move (25 vs 50) and the directive (symmetric vs asymmetric), each accepted. Rivlin and Kelley stress growth wording, and Greenspan notes the gap between the Beigebook and the table. Broad agreement with some reservations, 20s.
- Financial stability: Several participants raise it as context (lending standards, risk premiums, wealth effects, bubbles, developers, credit card issuer). It is neither prominent nor dominant. Limited to moderate, around 30.
- Forward guidance: The directive is symmetric, yet the Chair says this will probably not be the last tightening, with a possible telephone-conference move. Mild tightening bias, mid-60s.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "what we do not know"; Boehne "uncomfortable uncertainty"; Stern "uncertain about the outlook... effectiveness of policy"; Parry "raises uncertainty about the future"; Meyer notes NAIRU and utilization uncertainty. Elevated but not extreme, because most speakers still show conviction on the upside risk. About 42.

**7. Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 with "above-potential" language: satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 with "comfortable/easy": satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 on level evidence: satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 ("healthy with minor concerns"): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (firming/strong): satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR (60) differ by 3. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION (64) differ by 7. FC (35) and CREDIT_SPREADS (33) co-move. Treasury is N/A. Inflation (50) versus the sub-components: only goods is scored (48), and it is consistent.

**9. N/A.** Treasury: not discussed as a functioning issue. Services, housing and expectations inflation: services and shelter not discussed (only health costs ending a positive shock and commercial rents). Expectations: only credibility worries (Broaddus, Melzer), no measured expectations. Goods inflation is scored from the mixed commodity and steel price talk.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Kelley: "inflation is flat to down according to many statistical series." Phillips: "we have moved further into the inflation alert zone."
- Center of gravity: current inflation is benign while latent pressures are building, so neutral.
- Direction: at neutral because reported inflation is low while concern is about the future.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 57 ±6 (High)**
- Kelley: growth "well beyond anybody's estimate of potential in an economy whose resources already seem to be fully utilized." Minehan: "robust, above-potential growth."
- Center of gravity: fully utilized and moving above potential, though Greenspan says short of overheating.
- Direction: above neutral because above-potential growth and fully utilized resources.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±6 (High)**
- Broaddus: "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight." Greenspan: "the tightness in labor markets is increasing."
- Center of gravity: widespread shortages and rising participation, with national unemployment near 5%.
- Direction: above neutral because shortages and low unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 64 ±6 (High)**
- Broaddus: consumer confidence "at its highest level since 1965." McDonough: "business fixed investment seems unusually robust for this late stage."
- Center of gravity: strong consumer and investment demand, though short of a boom.
- Direction: above neutral because robust spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "productivity is badly underestimated and indeed may actually be accelerating." Jordan: "We are experiencing a favorable productivity surprise."
- Center of gravity: a Chair-led view of accelerating productivity, with some doubt on breadth (Stern).
- Direction: above neutral because gains are seen as stronger than trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Stern: "financial conditions remain very comfortable." Melzer: "a continuing easy credit market."
- Center of gravity: loose financing and low risk premiums, with little tightening seen.
- Direction: below neutral (loose) because comfortable and easy.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Not discussed as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±9 (Low)**
- Boehne: "slippage of underwriting standards"; Greenspan: "decline in risk premiums."
- Center of gravity: risk premiums low and standards loose, but spreads themselves are not discussed.
- Direction: below neutral because low premiums and easy terms.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 ±9 (Low)**
- Greenspan: "high market values are better than low market values. What we are trying to avoid is bubbles that break." Also that high-tech earnings forecasts "are unrealistic."
- Center of gravity: a mild overvaluation worry, mostly framed as wealth effects.
- Direction: below neutral (overvaluation concern) because bubble risk is mentioned but accepted.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Jordan: construction "lending standards... have disappeared"; Guynn: developers "forgetting the lessons of the 1980s."
- Center of gravity: sound banks with margin erosion and lax standards as the concerns.
- Direction: below neutral because healthy with minor concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "the dollar has been firming." Broaddus: "the strong dollar" damping prices.
- Center of gravity: a strong, firming dollar restraining exports and prices.
- Direction: above neutral because strength.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 48: mixed (steel increases "50/50 chance of sticking", newsprint plunging, hog prices up, energy easing). SERVICES N/A. HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 66 ±6 (High)**
- Greenspan: "we clearly need at this stage... to move off the dime." Parry: "I would support a 25 basis point increase... strongly prefer... asymmetric toward further tightening."
- Center of gravity: unanimous start of tightening, with a hawkish minority wanting more.
- Direction: hawkish because consensus to tighten.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±6 (High)**
- Vote 10-0 Yes; Hoenig/Broaddus/Moskow/Parry "prefer asymmetry"; Parry and Broaddus lean toward a larger move.
- Center of gravity: unanimity with preference differences on size and wording.
- Direction: low because unanimous vote and acceptance of the Chair's proposal.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "we recognize that that can create unusual problems within balance sheets." Boehne and Jordan on lending standards.
- Center of gravity: several brief mentions, with the Chair's risk-premium discussion the longest.
- Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "the odds are better than 50/50 that the move we are considering will not be our last tightening move."
- Center of gravity: a symmetric directive with a flagged probable follow-up move.
- Direction: tightening bias tempered by the symmetric directive.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±8 (Moderate)**
- McDonough: "I think... concentrate on what we do not know." Stern: "We are uncertain about the outlook; we are uncertain about the effectiveness of policy."
- Center of gravity: several voices on uncertainty (productivity, NAIRU, effects of policy) but with directional conviction.
- Direction: somewhat elevated.
