# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2007-12-11

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds the opening on the term auction facility (TAF) and the Swiss swap, members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic go-around, the policy go-around, and the vote read-out. Stand-alone staff content is ignored. Members' references to Greenbook or Bluebook numbers are used only where the member adopts them as their own view.

**Era context.** Late 2007. Financial turmoil is renewed, housing is falling, and the Committee has already cut 75bp over two meetings. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.**
- Pessimistic on growth, favouring more easing: Yellen, Rosengren, Lockhart, Mishkin, Geithner (moderately), Kohn (moderately).
- Cautious on inflation or sanguine on growth: Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker, Fisher, Poole (partly), Evans, Pianalto.
- Financial-market focus: Warsh, Kroszner, Stern, Kohn, Geithner.
- The Chairman summarises. He sees a fundamental softening, is "conflicted", and proposes 25bp.

**Inflation pressure.** Participants see it as modestly above the midpoint, with a split. Core is near or just under 2 percent (Yellen, Rosengren, Mishkin, Geithner, Stern: contained or benign). Headline is high and food, energy and commodity costs are rising (Plosser: core PCE 2.26 percent over three months, "more-generalized inflationary pressures"; Fisher: "we are understating inflationary pressures"; Hoenig; Lacker: "overall inflation is well above our targets"; Kohn: risk still to the upside but "considerably less"). Netting these gives about 57.

**Output gap.** Participants see activity below potential. Growth is expected below potential for several quarters (Geithner, Yellen, Hoenig: "will grow below its potential"; Evans: gap "less than ½ percentage point"; Kohn: 25bp "moves the unemployment rate above full employment"). Yellen says resource utilisation is "near its equilibrium level" now. This is a small, emerging negative gap, so about 43.

**Labor tightness.** The labor market is described as "pretty solid" and "unemployment remains low" (Bernanke, Evans). Hoenig and Fisher (Texas unemployment 4.1 percent) report tight local markets. Lacker: "still fairly tight". Yellen expects slack to develop, with unemployment rising to about 5 percent. This is near NAIRU, so about 51.

**Consumption and investment.** Participants see it as below trend and weakening. Consumption was "dead in the water" or "flat" in September and October (Yellen, Evans, Kohn, Lacker). Housing is collapsing, capital spending is softening, and sentiment is weak. There are offsets: Fisher's retail anecdotes, Poole's electronics, Hoenig and Plosser on business investment and net exports. Net is about 38.

**Productivity.** Only a passing question from Stern ("a very bad productivity number"), with no member view. N/A.

**Financial conditions.** Participants see them as tighter and strained. Plosser: "financial market conditions … have clearly deteriorated". Kohn: "a net tightening of credit conditions". Geithner: "tighter financial conditions". Stern, Warsh and Kroszner describe markets as dysfunctional. Some on the other side say credit to Main Street is not yet constrained (Plosser, Evans, Lacker, Kroszner on investment-grade firms). The score is above 50 (bright line), at about 69.

**Treasury stress.** Treasury functioning is barely discussed. Kohn and Bernanke note falling Treasury yields. Warsh notes a preference for Treasuries over agencies. Geithner notes "large spikes in demand for liquid risk-free assets". None of this is Treasury-market dysfunction, only yield moves and flight to safety. I score 42 at Low confidence.

**Credit spreads.** The level is stated high. Poole: "spreads remain wide". Rosengren: term lending rates "elevated" and lower-grade risk premia "now becoming quite elevated". Kohn: corporate spreads widened enough to raise borrowing costs, and forward LIBOR-OIS is "substantially higher". Lacker says investment-grade costs are low. The level reading puts the score above 50, at about 68.

**Equity valuation.** Equities are mentioned only as price moves: a 5 percent fall (Yellen), a "correction" with a partial rebound conditional on expected easing (Bernanke), and "compelling equity risk premiums" for opportunistic buyers (Warsh). There is no clear valuation view. Default is the neutral band, 52 at Low confidence.

**Banking stress.** Mixed. Banks came in with a lot of capital, and most are "in pretty good shape" (Yellen, Geithner, Mishkin, Plosser). Against that, there are big write-downs ($75bn, Bernanke), balance-sheet pressure from SIVs and leveraged loans, rising criticized loans, "capital … under pressure" (Yellen), and CRE worries at smaller banks. Bernanke does "not expect insolvency or near insolvency". This is emerging stress with a sound core, so about 57.

**Dollar strength.** Discussed as depreciation: "weaker dollar" boosting exports (Lacker, Evans), "dollar depreciation" as an inflation risk (Hoenig), "the dollar … a little fragile" (Bernanke), and recent stabilisation. Direction is weakening, so the score is below 50 (bright line), at about 30.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: energy, food, commodity and import prices are discussed widely (Fisher, Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker). About 65.
- Services: only a passing "some services" from Hoenig. N/A.
- Housing: members discuss house prices, not shelter or rent inflation. N/A.
- Expectations: split. Plosser says "fragile". Pianalto says "tugging on their anchor". Hoenig, Lacker, Kroszner and Fisher are concerned. Mishkin, Geithner and Rosengren say anchored. About 57.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean. Most favour a 25bp cut, a clear easing action. Rosengren, Yellen, Lockhart and Mishkin prefer 50bp. A hawkish tail (Hoenig would rather hold; Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Poole stress inflation and limits on reversing) holds the cut to 25bp. This is moderate dovish, about 35.
- Dissent. There is one formal policy dissent (Rosengren, for 50bp) and one dissent on the Swiss swap (Poole). Three others (Yellen, Lockhart, Mishkin) preferred 50bp but supported 25bp. Hoenig, Lacker, Plosser and Fisher voice reluctance or wanted less easing. There are distinct camps, and the disagreement extends to the assessment (recession odds of 40-50 percent versus "weather these forces"; benign inflation versus fragile expectations). There is no heated personal exchange, however. About 47.
- Financial stability emphasis. It is the dominant theme: the TAF and swap opening, most of the go-around on credit and liquidity, and the "fever and infection" framing. About 86.
- Forward guidance. Members want flexibility ("nimble"), the balance-of-risks sentence is dropped, and Bernanke says "a good chance that we may have to move further". Lacker, Plosser and Fisher warn about reversal and inflation. This is an easing lean with data dependence, about 37.
- Uncertainty. Many members stress it. Lockhart: "extremely difficult to read". Fisher: "I'm confused". Pianalto: "can't assess with any useful precision". Poole: "more variance than usual". Mishkin: recession probability "nearing 50 percent". Bernanke: "recognize the uncertainty". Not Knightian, but high, about 72.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 is below 50 for "below potential". OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 69 is above 50 for "tightened/strained". OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 68 is above 50 for "wide/elevated". OK.
- BANKING_STRESS 57 reflects emerging stress with no "troubled" language; the sound-capital language is balanced by the stress language. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 is below 50 for depreciation. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS 42 involves no dysfunction. OK.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and participants see modestly more than 2 percent headline: matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means a tighter market, and participants see near full employment: matches. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN means tighter policy, and the action is a cut: score below 50, matches.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 versus LABOR 51: 8 apart, fine. Unemployment is still low while growth is below potential.
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 versus CONSUMPTION 38: 5 apart, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 69 versus CREDIT_SPREADS 68: co-move, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is near normal, with flight to safety but no dysfunction. Fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 versus goods 65 and expectations 57: consistent.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY (no member view), INFLATION_SERVICES (passing mention only), INFLATION_HOUSING (house prices discussed, shelter inflation not).

**Text quality.** There is some garbling (sentence fragments where staff answers were removed, and run-on joins), but nothing that obscures member views. Some lines carry apparent extraction splicing (a Greenbook quote by Fisher; the Poole/Lockhart exchange), which does not affect the scores.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±6 [Moderate].**
- Plosser: "headline inflation rates have risen sharply … perhaps there are more-generalized inflationary pressures."
- Mishkin: "I really do not see the substantial upside risk that a lot of people are talking about."
- Summary: core is near 2 percent while headline and input costs are up and the camps split, so net modestly above target.
- Direction: above neutral because headline, energy, food and commodity pressures are noted by roughly half the table.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±6 [Moderate].**
- Hoenig: "the economy, though it will grow below its potential".
- Yellen: "growth below potential throughout next year", with "resource utilization near its equilibrium level" today.
- Summary: a small and widening shortfall.
- Direction: below neutral because growth is below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 ±6 [Moderate].**
- Bernanke: "Unemployment remains low."
- Hoenig: "labor markets are, in fact, tight across much of the District."
- Yellen: unemployment "to rise to about 5 percent."
- Summary: near full employment, with a slowing trend and regional tightness.
- Direction: at neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±6 [High].**
- Yellen: "real consumer spending in September and October was dead in the water."
- Evans: "PCE was basically flat in September and October."
- Summary: consumption has flattened, housing is collapsing and capital spending is softening, with some positive anecdotes.
- Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** No member view expressed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 69 ±7 [High].**
- Kohn: "a number of indicators point to a net tightening of credit conditions across a range of borrowing sources."
- Plosser: "financial market conditions … have clearly deteriorated in recent weeks."
- Summary: conditions are tighter and strained, though Main Street access is mostly intact.
- Direction: above neutral (tighter).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Low].**
- Warsh: "a very meaningful preference for Treasuries as opposed to agencies."
- Geithner: "large spikes in demand for liquid risk-free assets."
- Summary: flight to safety and falling yields, not dysfunction.
- Direction: near normal.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 68 ±7 [High].**
- Poole: "spreads remain wide."
- Kohn: "spreads on corporate bonds have widened sufficiently to actually increase borrowing costs for both investment-grade and junk-bond issuers."
- Summary: wide and still widening, with term funding spreads severe.
- Direction: above neutral on level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Low].**
- Yellen: "broad stock indexes are down nearly 5 percent."
- Warsh: "extremely compelling equity risk premiums".
- Summary: price moves with no valuation commentary, so default.
- Direction: near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 57 ±7 [Moderate].**
- Bernanke: "I do not expect insolvency or near insolvency among major financial institutions", set against "$75 billion in write-downs", with criticized loans rising from 26 to 28 percent.
- Mishkin: "banks having good balance sheets."
- Summary: sound capital base but emerging stress and pressure.
- Direction: modestly above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 ±6 [Moderate].**
- Lacker: "the weaker dollar is providing a boost to manufacturing exports."
- Hoenig: "dollar depreciation."
- Summary: the dollar has been weakening, with recent stabilisation.
- Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 65.** Energy, food, commodity and import prices (Fisher on Frito-Lay and Wal-Mart cost increases, Plosser's prices-paid surveys).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57.** Plosser: "fragile". Pianalto: "tugging on their anchor just a bit". Mishkin: anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 35 ±6 [High].**
- Bernanke: "I recommend 25 basis points and alternative B", having said that if the choice were between 0 and 50 "I would be very tempted to do 50."
- Hoenig: "I would support maintaining the rate … Thus, I can accept the 25 basis point cut."
- Summary: a majority supports easing; four wanted more and a few pull back.
- Direction: dovish (below 50).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 47 ±7 [High].**
- Rosengren: "my strong preference is for the decisive action reflected in alternative A", then a "No" vote on the policy action.
- Poole: "I vote against the swap."
- Summary: one policy dissent, one swap dissent, many reservations either side, and an assessment gap on growth and inflation, but cordial.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 86 ±5 [High].**
- Geithner: "illiquidity itself can threaten market functioning and the economy."
- Stern: "the main development since the last FOMC meeting has been the renewed deterioration in some of the financial markets."
- Summary: the TAF, swap and credit risk dominate the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 37 ±8 [Moderate].**
- Bernanke: "there is a good chance that we may have to move further at subsequent meetings … we signal our flexibility, our nimbleness."
- Lacker: "I would not be surprised if we want to reduce the funds rate another ¼ point at the end of January."
- Summary: easing lean with data dependence, tempered by reversal and inflation caveats.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 ±7 [High].**
- Lockhart: "The current situation is extremely difficult to read."
- Pianalto: "I simply can't assess with any useful precision how to weigh the risks."
- Summary: many members emphasise unusual uncertainty and a wide scenario range.
