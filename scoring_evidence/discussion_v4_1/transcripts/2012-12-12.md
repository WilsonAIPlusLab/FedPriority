# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2012-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It has the staff Q&A (members' questions), the financial-stability remarks, the economic go-round (Williams through Powell, then the Chair's summary and cost-benefit framework), the policy go-round on "alternative B" (Dudley first, Powell last), and a roll-call vote read-out. Score the participants' views only. Staff-quoted content (Tealbook figures, memo results) is ignored unless a member adopts it as their own view.

**2. Era context.** Late 2012, a gradual recovery with the unemployment rate near 7.7 percent. The Committee is deciding whether to continue open-ended asset purchases ($45B Treasuries after the maturity extension program ends, plus $40B MBS) and whether to replace the calendar-date guidance with numeric thresholds (6½ percent unemployment, 2½ percent projected inflation). The fiscal cliff dominates the outlook. This backdrop is context only; scores come from what members say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish/supportive core: Dudley, Williams, Yellen, Rosengren, Evans, Kocherlakota (wants even lower thresholds), Raskin, Tarullo, Bernanke.
- Supporters with reservations: Lockhart ("not free of angst"), Pianalto ("very reluctantly"), Duke, Stein ("fairly serious reservations"), Powell ("despite my misgivings").
- Opposed to more purchases: Lacker (voted No), Plosser, Fisher, George (non-voters at the table). Bullard wants a slower $35B pace but supports thresholds.
- Economic assessment is broadly shared (slow recovery, fiscal-cliff drag, tame inflation). Tarullo says there is "not a significant divergence in expectations". The splits are on policy and costs.

**4. Dimension-by-dimension directional signals (words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint/target. Evidence: Bernanke "inflation remains relatively tame"; Dudley "I don't see any meaningful signs of inflation pressure"; Kocherlakota says inflation "averaged only 1.3 percent per year" and underlying pressures are "subdued". Plosser, George and Lacker worry about future upside, but nobody reports current pressure. Roughly 1.6-1.7 percent inflation, "below target" and "contained" means 26-40. Score 34.
- OUTPUT_GAP: BELOW the midpoint. Evans "demand is very low in our economy"; Tarullo "the case for insufficient aggregate demand is still a pretty strong one"; Bernanke "an economy that is obviously underperforming". Lacker says "we may not be that far from a balanced growth path", a minority view. Bright-line: slack language gives < 50. Score 34.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: BELOW the midpoint. Unemployment is 7.7 percent, U-6 is 14.4 percent, participation is falling, hiring and quits are depressed (Yellen, Tarullo, Raskin). Lacker and Fisher report worker shortages in niches, and Fisher's Texas district is at 6½ percent. The national picture is a UE > 7% market with slack: 16-30 range. Score 28.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: BELOW trend on net. Consumption is "softer than expected" (Evans); real disposable income is weak (Raskin); business investment is held back ("engines are just idling", Fisher; Yellen, Williams, Powell). Housing and autos are strong (Duke, Dudley, George). Net 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only a passing remark (Dudley: Q4 productivity growth "will be really lousy" because of the drought and the hurricane, and unit labor costs are benign). It is a transitory comment, so Low confidence. Score 42.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Looser than neutral. Dudley "financial conditions are accommodative right now"; mortgage rates "historically low" (Yellen, Bernanke); credit availability gradually improving (Dudley, Duke). Offsets: tight credit for many households and mortgage borrowers (Williams, Tarullo, Bernanke's summary). Bright-line: accommodative gives < 50. Score 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Little stress. Bernanke "We are not seeing any signs of market dysfunction"; Dudley says $45B a month was done "without any negative impact on market function". Yellen notes term premiums at unprecedented lows and an exit-risk concern, but that is not dysfunction. Score 27.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is below normal/favourable. Fisher cites record investment-grade issuance and very low coupons; Pianalto and Bernanke note reach for yield and easier covenants; Rosengren notes the primary-secondary mortgage spread has widened (a mortgage-market capacity issue, not a general credit-stress signal). Level reading gives < 50. Score 36.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Little valuation commentary. Powell: "The stock market is pricing in a really high risk premium"; Dudley expects stocks to fall in a bad cliff outcome; Raskin notes equity gains. No bubble concern, a mild "depressed/high risk premium" view. Low confidence, 53.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy. Dudley "The banking system is stronger than it was a few years ago"; Bernanke's summary "banks are financially stronger". Worries are about interest-rate-risk exposure and money market funds, not solvency. Bright-line: sound gives < 50. Score 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Discussed mostly as a policy channel (Williams: the dollar depreciated on LSAP announcements). Powell expects "a strong dollar" in a messy fiscal outcome and global risk-off, which is a mild forward appreciation signal. Thin and forward-looking, so Low confidence. Score 54.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS N/A. SERVICES: Bernanke notes services ex energy and shelter fell from a 2 percent to a 1 percent rate (weak, scored 33). HOUSING: house prices are discussed, not rents or shelter inflation, so N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Pianalto "anchored below 2 percent"; Bernanke "remained anchored"; Lockhart sees a 2 percent business expectation as "not material"; George notes TIPS near the upper limit of their historical range; Plosser warns of risk. Score 47.

**5. Polarity verification.** INFLATION: higher = more pressure; evidence is "tame", so below 50 matches. OUTPUT_GAP: higher = more above potential; evidence is slack, so below 50. LABOR: higher = tighter; evidence is slack, so below 50. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence is accommodative, so below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence is favourable levels, so below 50. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress; evidence is "stronger", so below 50. DOLLAR: appreciation expectation, so slightly above 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: A clear majority supports continuing and enlarging QE ($85B a month, labelled QE3 continuation), a QE-era posture (anchor 15-25). Pull toward a less dovish stance: Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, George oppose; Pianalto, Bullard, Duke, Stein and Powell want smaller or slower purchases or want to rein in expectations; Plosser wants to start normalizing by end-2013. Net 24.
- Disagreement: one formal dissent (Lacker, No on the statement). Three non-voting presidents oppose more purchases. Five voters support only with reservations, and Bullard (a non-voter) wants $35B. The disagreement is mostly about costs and efficacy and about thresholds, not the economic assessment. The meeting has no sharp personal exchanges. Score about 47.
- Financial stability attention: There is a dedicated financial-stability presentation and go-round, and financial stability and costs of the balance sheet recur in Pianalto, Yellen, Stein, Powell, Duke, Kocherlakota and Bernanke ("the most difficult" cost). This is prominent but the meeting is chiefly about policy design. Score 62.
- Forward guidance: Numeric thresholds (6½ percent unemployment, 2½ percent inflation) replacing mid-2015 dates, with rates low "for a considerable time" after purchases end; Kocherlakota wants even lower thresholds. This is Evans-rule-type guidance (anchor 3-8). Offsets: members and the Chair discuss tapering, cost and efficacy reviews, and rein-in of balance-sheet expectations, but these are about asset purchases rather than rate liftoff. Score 9.
- Uncertainty: Fiscal-cliff and policy uncertainty is the central theme ("enormous uncertainty", Tarullo; Dudley's range from minus 2 to plus 4 percent growth; business "paralyzed by uncertainty", Williams). Members also stress model and forecast error (Plosser), uncertainty about efficacy and costs of LSAPs (Bernanke, Powell, Williams) and about labor-market structure (Tarullo, Yellen). Many emphasize it, but it is mostly of the known fiscal type. Score 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 satisfied (slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 satisfied (accommodative). CREDIT_SPREADS: level reading gives < 50, with no member describing spreads as wide in the general credit sense. BANKING_STRESS < 50 satisfied. DOLLAR > 50 satisfied (mild appreciation expectation). TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction reported, so 27 (< 60).

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs LABOR 28: gap of 6, consistent. OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36: consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 36: co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs TREASURY_STRESS 27: calm Treasury market, loose conditions, consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 34 vs sub-components (services 33, expectations 47): consistent given expectations sit near target while realized inflation is below.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS: not discussed by members. INFLATION_HOUSING: house-price (asset) discussion only, no rent or shelter inflation commentary. Other dimensions have at least some member commentary.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 34 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "I don't see any meaningful signs of inflation pressure, either incipient or manifest." Kocherlakota, "inflation has averaged only 1.3 percent per year."
- Summary: Members see realized and projected inflation below target with no wage pressure; a few worry about later upside.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is below neutral because inflation is running under 2 percent and compensation is weak.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans, "this situation must be symptomatic of the fact that demand is very low in our economy." Tarullo, "the case for insufficient aggregate demand is still a pretty strong one."
- Summary: Most members see persistent slack; Lacker alone argues the economy is near a balanced path.
- Direction: The output gap is below neutral because the economy is operating below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "I still see nothing in the data that convinces me that it is definitively on the mend." Tarullo, U-6 "is still up at 14.4 percent, very high historically."
- Summary: Unemployment near 7.7 percent, falling participation and weak hiring dominate, offset only by niche shortage anecdotes from Lacker and Fisher.
- Direction: Labor tightness is below neutral because slack remains large.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans, "consumption has been softer than expected"; Fisher, businesses' "engines are just idling." Duke, "continued signs of momentum in the housing recovery."
- Summary: Weak income, a consumption dip and idled business investment outweigh strong housing and autos.
- Direction: Spending is below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 +-12 [Low]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "productivity growth will be really lousy due to the drought and the hurricane."
- Summary: A single transitory remark, with unit labor costs described as benign.
- Direction: Productivity is modestly below trend in the near term, on thin evidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions are accommodative right now." Bernanke, "many households do still face tight credit."
- Summary: Markets and mortgage rates are easy, with pockets of tight household and mortgage credit.
- Direction: Financial conditions are below neutral (looser) because policy-driven easing dominates.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 27 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "We are not seeing any signs of market dysfunction." Dudley, purchases made "without any negative impact on market function."
- Summary: The market is functioning normally; only exit-related yield-spike worries are raised.
- Direction: Treasury stress is below neutral because there is no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 +-10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher, "we did break through the record at $1.025 trillion in investment-grade credits." Rosengren, the primary-secondary mortgage spread "has widened" because of lender capacity constraints.
- Summary: Corporate credit is abundant and cheap; the mortgage-spread widening is a narrow, capacity-driven issue.
- Direction: Spreads are below normal in level (level governs), so the score sits under 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 53 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Powell, "The stock market is pricing in a really high risk premium."
- Summary: Little valuation discussion; the one explicit view is a high risk premium and no bubble concern.
- Direction: Equity concern is roughly neutral to slightly above because valuations are described as risk-premium-depressed, not rich.

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "The banking system is stronger than it was a few years ago." Duke, banks have incentives to "increase capacity, lower rates, and ease standards."
- Summary: Banks are seen as sound; concerns are interest-rate-risk exposure and money market funds.
- Direction: Banking stress is below neutral because banks are healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Powell, "the dollar should remain strong." Williams, the dollar "typically depreciated in response to surprise expansionary monetary policy announcements."
- Summary: Mostly a policy-channel discussion; the one outlook statement points to a firm dollar.
- Direction: The dollar is modestly above neutral given the expected strength in a risk-off fiscal fight.

**INFLATION_GOODS: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 33.** Bernanke, services ex energy and shelter "declined from a 2 percent rate over the last 12 months to a 1 percent rate over the last three months."
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Only house-price asset commentary.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Pianalto, expectations "anchored below 2 percent even beyond the 20-year horizon"; George, TIPS expectations "near the upper limit of their historical range."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "I consider it imperative that we continue our longer-term asset purchases at the current rate of $85 billion per month." Rosengren, "Additional asset purchases are needed to shorten the time we continue to miss on both elements of our mandate."
- Summary: A QE-era majority backs continued large purchases; Lacker, Plosser, Fisher and George dissent or oppose, and several supporters want to rein in the program.
- Direction: The lean is dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 47 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President Lacker No" with eleven other voters Yes. Pianalto, "I can very reluctantly support alternative B."
- Summary: One formal dissent, three non-voters opposing, and five voters with explicit reservations, but a shared economic assessment and civil exchanges.
- Direction: Disagreement is moderate to notable, centered on policy costs and thresholds.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 63 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Pianalto, "it is important to me that we put more emphasis on financial stability." Bernanke, "The fourth cost is financial stability, which I think is the most difficult."
- Summary: A dedicated financial-stability session plus recurring cost and risk arguments in the policy round make it a prominent but not dominant topic.
- Direction: Attention is moderately high.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 9 +-4 [High]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota, the thresholds mean the Committee "is highly unlikely to begin to raise the fed funds rate as long as inflation expectations over the medium term remain below 2½ percent and the unemployment rate remains above 6½ percent." Statement text: low rates "for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends."
- Summary: Threshold-based easing commitment, Evans-rule style, with only asset-purchase caveats on the hawkish side.
- Direction: The forward signal is strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "I think the range of outcomes is actually very wide... minus 2 percent... plus 4 percent." Tarullo, "the enormous uncertainty due to the fiscal cliff, which is just holding people back."
- Summary: Many members stress fiscal-cliff, efficacy, cost and labor-structure uncertainty.
- Direction: Uncertainty is high.
