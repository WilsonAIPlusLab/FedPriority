# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1997-09-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript: staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around (all Reserve Bank presidents and governors), the Chairman's summary, and the policy round (Alternative B with asymmetric language). Unanimous roll call (10 Yes). Staff turns are removed; I ignore Greenbook content that members merely cite (the 100bp tightening and 20% stock correction are staff assumptions, used only as the object of members' comments).

**Era context.** 1997, late-cycle expansion, "new paradigm" debate. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.** Hawkish-leaning on timing: Broaddus ("tap the brakes sooner"), Melzer (prefers C, "why not adopt a tighter stance in 1997"), Jordan ("we are probably late"), Minehan (would have supported a small move now; over 50% odds in November), Meyer ("cautiously preemptive"), Moskow, Stern, McDonough (firm at the next meeting). More patient: Rivlin ("less inflation-prone economy"), Phillips ("time to let things develop"), McTeer, Parry (leave unchanged, could support insurance), Kelley (steady as she goes, asymmetric, high alert). Greenspan: do not move today, "easily a 50/50 chance" of a move in November, "B" with continued asymmetry.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Realized inflation is low and falling ("inflation has fallen further", Parry; "no evidence of any pipeline inflation", Greenspan; CPI 1.6% annualized, Melzer; "for all intents and purposes... price stability"). But members see upside risks and building pressures (tight labor, bonuses, some price increases sticking). Participants see current inflation pressure slightly below the midpoint with upside risk: 44.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy above potential: Phillips "Resource utilization has to be getting rather taut"; Meyer "growth is likely to remain above trend", unemployment below NAIRU; Broaddus "capacity utilization approaching the level associated with rising inflation". Bright line: above 50. 61.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Nearly all describe very tight labor markets (Broaddus "as tight as at any time since I have been in the Federal Reserve"; Moskow regional UE below 4%; Melzer "stretched to the limit"; Greenspan "fundamental disequilibrium"). UE nationally 4.8%, heading to 4.5%. Participant language is stronger than the UE anchor alone, so 68.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Greenspan "extraordinary acceleration in capital investment"; Phillips "incredibly impressive"; consumer strength in Q3 (Moskow, Kelley "consumers are very confident"). Some districts note housing/high-end softness. 66.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Heavily discussed; Greenspan cites manufacturing productivity near 7-1/2% annualized, Jordan/Minehan/Rivlin cite gains; Greenspan and McDonough doubt a lasting acceleration, Hoenig skeptical. Above trend as perceived: 62, wide band.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Meyer "supportive financial conditions"; Kelley "abundant liquidity", "plenty of liquidity at reasonable rates", household wealth rising; Phillips strong equity and debt markets; Jordan sees policy as accommodative relative to returns. Bright line: supportive/easy, below 50. 33.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as a functioning issue (only the yield curve as a not-pressured indicator by Kelley, and bond market reaction to testimony). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only indirect: Jordan reports bankers' "increasingly skimpy pricing in C&I lending", "everyone is chasing deals"; McTeer "downward pressure on rates but mostly on terms". Level below normal: 30, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members mention an expected decline "we have been expecting for so long" (Minehan), risk of a market break (Kelley, Broaddus), strong equity markets (Phillips), rising wealth. Valuation concern (stretched), though not explicit "overvalued" language: 33, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips: financial balance sheets "in quite good shape"; Melzer double-digit bank loan growth; Jordan "banks have plenty of money to lend". Mexico's banks "vulnerable" is foreign. Healthy, low stress: 27, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar described as high/appreciated and restraining inflation (Parry, Minehan, Guynn "stronger dollar" hitting tourism, Phillips "strong dollar", Hoenig). Bright line: above 50. 67.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods: firms hold the line on prices, commodity prices eased, a few price increases (paper, railroads): 40. Services: no direct price discussion beyond McDonough's wage/service-sector comment, N/A. Housing: real estate prices "increasing as yet only modestly", rents rising in some places (Minehan, Boehne): 52. Expectations: "stable" (Hoenig), "inflation psychology considerably reduced" (Phillips), long-term expectations down (Melzer): 40.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Committee unanimously holds the rate but with an asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive; the Chairman says a November move is a 50/50 prospect; several would move sooner (Melzer, Minehan, Jordan, Broaddus). Hold with a tightening bias, leaning to the upper part of that band: 60.
- Dissent: Zero dissents, unanimous vote. Differences are about timing of the move (Melzer prefers C, Jordan says "probably late", Minehan would have moved; Rivlin and Phillips want more evidence) and about the new-paradigm argument. 21.
- Financial stability emphasis: Stock-market correction risk, asset valuations, money and credit growth and lending terms come up, but as context to the macro story. 24.
- Forward guidance: Chairman and many members signal that a tightening in November is likely and urge public preparation. Clearly pointing to tightening but conditional on data: 70.
- Uncertainty: "we are still guessing" (Greenspan), new-paradigm vs old-model debate, "we don't know where the limits are" (Greenspan), Boehne "we just do not know where in the middle". Multiple participants stress it, though conviction on direction of risk is shared. 52.

**Polarity check.** Higher OUTPUT_GAP = more above potential: evidence above potential, score 61, consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter: evidence supportive/easy, score 33, consistent. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress: evidence loose terms, score 30, consistent. DOLLAR higher = stronger: evidence strong dollar, 67, consistent. BANKING_STRESS higher = more stress: healthy banks, 27, consistent.

**Cross-checks.** OUTPUT_GAP 61 vs LABOR 68 (7 apart, fine). OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 66 (5 apart). FC 33 and CREDIT_SPREADS 30 co-move (both loose). FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A, fine. INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 vs sub-components (goods 40, housing 52, expectations 40): consistent.

**Magnitude checks.** Labor 68 reflects "as tight as at any time", "severe"-type language in many districts, above the "tight" band 53-65 but not "very tight/unsustainable wage growth" band beyond 80 since wages are still modest.

**N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS (no participant discussion of Treasury market functioning), INFLATION_SERVICES (no direct discussion of services price inflation).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry "inflation has fallen further"; Greenspan "there is no evidence to suggest that there is any pipeline inflation". Center of gravity: low, falling realized inflation with upside risk perceived. Direction: below neutral because current readings are favorable even though risks are on the upside.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 61 +-7 [High].** Evidence: Phillips "Resource utilization has to be getting rather taut"; Meyer "growth is likely to remain above trend for some time". Summary: economy running above sustainable pace with unemployment below NAIRU estimates. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Broaddus "labor markets in much of our District are as tight as at any time since I have been in the Federal Reserve"; Melzer "labor markets are stretched to the limit". Summary: near-universal reports of shortages and bonuses. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 66 +-7 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan "extraordinary acceleration in capital investment"; Phillips "Business fixed investment is incredibly impressive". Summary: strong investment and consumer spending. Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-9 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan "manufacturing productivity appears to be rising at an annual rate of about 7-1/2 percent"; Rivlin "apparent increase in productivity growth". Summary: perceived above trend though some doubt it is durable. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Meyer "supportive financial conditions"; Kelley "abundant liquidity in the economy". Summary: easy credit and rising wealth. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-10 [Low].** Evidence: Jordan relaying bankers on "increasingly skimpy pricing in their C&I lending"; McTeer "downward pressure on rates but mostly on terms". Summary: ample credit and loose pricing, though no explicit spread levels. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-10 [Low].** Evidence: Minehan "the decline in the stock market that we have been expecting for so long"; Kelley "household wealth continues to rise". Summary: members expect a correction, implying stretched valuation. Direction: below midpoint on this scale (valuation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 27 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Phillips "balance sheets for both financial and nonfinancial corporations seem to be in quite good shape"; Jordan "banks have plenty of money to lend". Summary: healthy banks. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 67 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry "At its present level, the dollar is a restraining influence"; Minehan "the appreciated dollar". Summary: dollar high after appreciation. Direction: above neutral.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 40 (Minehan "continue... to hold the line on prices"); SERVICES N/A; HOUSING 52 (Minehan "Prices... increasing as yet only modestly"); EXPECTATIONS 40 (Hoenig "inflation expectations that continue to be stable").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-5 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan "I am putting on the table a 'B' directive with continued asymmetry"; Melzer "I would prefer alternative C". Summary: hold with a clear tightening bias and several favoring earlier action. Direction: hawkish side of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 21 +-6 [High].** Evidence: unanimous roll call; Rivlin "a little less convinced than you that we will have to move in November". Summary: unanimous, with differences over timing and the paradigm. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 24 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Broaddus on the stock market reaction and being "behind the curve"; Jordan on bank credit and money growth. Summary: asset prices and credit appear mainly as context. Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan "the probability that we will be forced to move at the next meeting has gone up quite considerably"; McDonough "seize an opportunity... lay out the line of thought". Summary: members signal a probable November tightening. Direction: tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan "we are still guessing"; Boehne "We just do not know where in the middle at this point". Summary: repeated acknowledgement of not understanding the new behavior of inflation and productivity. Direction: elevated.
