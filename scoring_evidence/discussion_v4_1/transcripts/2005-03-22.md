## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document treated as a standard (not special-topic) meeting: it has a policy go-around, a statement-language debate and a roll-call vote (12 Yes, 0 No).

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +-6 (Moderate-High)**
- Hoenig: "core inflation elevated"; core "drifted higher last year" and "unexpectedly higher"; "wage pressures are emerging". Santomero: "relatively sharp acceleration in core consumer good prices". Bies: "core inflation move modestly higher"; "price pressures coming from both the sales and the cost sides". Minehan: "doubling in the rate of core inflation". Guynn: "both goods and services inflation are now showing signs of picking up".
- Offsets: Bernanke "mini-inflation scare"; "Other determinants of inflation... largely under control"; Yellen core PCE "just over 1½ percent"; Lacker "core PCE inflation to return to near 1½ percent". Kohn: "price pressures have been a bit more intense than anticipated".
- Modestly above-to-clearly-building pressure, core still below 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 47 +-5 (Moderate)**
- Hoenig: "the output gap and labor market slack are diminishing"; "output close to potential". Lacker: "the output gap is still on track to continue diminishing". Kohn: "the narrowing output gap". Gramlich: "output gaps tighten". Hoenig also says "growth above trend", but the gap is described as narrowing, not closed.
- Slightly below potential and closing (bright line: no "above potential" level claim).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Santomero: "unemployment rate now under 5.5 percent... current pace of employment growth is probably about right"; Guynn: District unemployment "edged down further... to 5 percent"; Minehan: "Skilled workers are becoming difficult to find". Kohn: "wage and compensation growth remain essentially flat, indicating to me that slack in labor markets persists". Moskow: hiring news "more subdued". Hoenig: "Wage pressures are emerging".
- Near NAIRU, tightening but with flat aggregate wages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +-6 (High)**
- Poole: "very broad-based and robust recovery. Business fixed investment is taking hold and taking the lead." Bies: "Retail sales excluding autos have grown at over a 9 percent annual rate"; "Business investment has been strong". Kohn: "household spending rising briskly". Gramlich: "Housing and consumption growth remain strong. The feared pothole in business investment never materialized". Santomero: "growing at a solid pace".
- Above trend, with weak autos and nonresidential construction as small offsets.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: "evidence of even greater strength in actual and structural productivity growth than previously assumed". Kohn: "productivity growth was stronger in the fourth quarter... than anticipated". Pianalto: "productivity growth surprises of the last couple of years may persist". Bernanke: "Still-strong productivity growth". Bies: "extraordinary pace of productivity growth has slowed in the past year" but "still very strong from an historical perspective".
- Above trend, with some slowing noted.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Minehan: "Overall credit and financial conditions are supportive, if not encouraging, to spending and growth." Kohn: "low long-term rates... depressed real level would represent a net stimulus"; "business borrowers who are facing very low risk premiums". Geithner: "lower risk premia across financial markets". Yellen: long rates "30 to 60 basis points below the fundamentals-consistent level". Offset: bond rates up "just under 40 basis points" this intermeeting period.
- Supportive/accommodative, so below 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +-10 (Low)**
- Yellen on term premium: "term premium... may remain as much as 60 basis points below normal"; Geithner: "remarkable reduction in uncertainty about monetary policy expectations"; Ferguson: declines in "implied volatilities and... spreads over Treasuries". Yields and term premium were discussed; no dysfunction or liquidity problem raised by anyone. Scored in the calm/low-volatility range with low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Guynn: a "BBB credit... was offered what amounted to AAA credit financing, at 20 basis points over Libor"; "potential underpricing of risk"; lenders "taking more risk in search of returns". Kohn: "very low risk premiums". Geithner: "lower risk premia across financial markets". Ferguson: spreads over Treasuries had declined. Stated level is low, so below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A** Equities appear only as a wealth-effect or funding channel (Greenspan on capital gains; Holcomb on tangible vs. financial assets); no valuation view or price-move report.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Guynn: bankers "report continued consumer loan growth, high asset quality, and low past-dues". Lacker: bank CEO says "business is as strong as train smoke". Poole: "C&I loans... now positive and accelerating". Minor concerns: Guynn on competition and risk-taking, Olson on lenders tightening interest-only loans. Healthy, so below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-6 (High)**
- Santomero: "the dollar has declined considerably". Moskow: "the declining dollar is showing through to higher import prices". Bernanke: "The weak dollar"; Gramlich: "The dollar has fallen already... fears that it will fall further". Depreciation, so below 50.

**Inflation sub-components**
- INFLATION_GOODS 58: Santomero "relatively sharp acceleration in core consumer good prices"; Guynn "surge in core goods inflation... used car prices"; offset by Bernanke "Prices of imported consumer goods have also been remarkably tame" and auto prices falling 0.9 percent in February.
- INFLATION_SERVICES 52: Guynn "both goods and services inflation are now showing signs of picking up"; Bernanke "Inflation in services is stable".
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A: house prices and speculation are discussed as asset and financial-stability topics; no rent or shelter inflation discussion.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 58: Lacker "TIPS five-year inflation compensation... increased by about a third of a percentage point"; "expectations, although they have been creeping up, are not yet a problem". Ferguson: "long-term inflation expectations continue to be well anchored". Kohn: "rise in near-term inflation expectations"; longer-run not increased.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 68 +-6 (High)**
- All 12 voters back +25 bp. Policy is described as "quite accommodative" (Yellen, Hoenig, Santomero). Hoenig: "begin moving more aggressively"; "drop any reference to measured pace". Greenspan: "if we were to become a bit more aggressive in tightening... the damage... would not be all that great"; the loss function favors tightening. Gramlich: "risk of falling behind the curve has increased. We have to keep raising rates." Poole: majority see inflation risks "tilted to the upside". Restraint: most prefer 25 bp, not 50 (Poole, Kohn, Yellen, Ferguson). The majority favors continued tightening, with a hawkish tilt in the risk language.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +-6 (High)**
- Unanimous vote; roll call 12 Yes. Differences are about wording: drop vs. keep "measured pace" (Hoenig, Minehan, Bies vs. Ferguson, Gramlich), and the "appropriate policy action" and balance-of-risk language (Minehan, Moskow, Gramlich, Guynn, Lacker, Yellen). Hoenig leans toward faster action. Minehan: "I'm not real happy with that." Greenspan deferred the wording to May. Few differences on the economy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Housing speculation: Guynn "housing speculation and the potential underpricing of risk"; Olson on the "day trading" and "real estate bubbles" item for June; Lacker "bubble conditions"; Holcomb on a "paradigm shift... toward tangible assets". Yellen at length on term premia and bond-rate risk; Ferguson on "carry trades"; Geithner on "household debt... net international position". Several participants address it; the main focus remains the economy and policy language.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 +-6 (Moderate)**
- The statement keeps "measured pace" but adds inflation-pressure language and "appropriate monetary policy action" (Bernanke: "our forecast is conditional on continued tightening"). Geithner: "signaling more tightening to come"; Kohn: could "deviate from the measured pace with a larger firming action... as soon as the next meeting". Greenspan: "we have to start moving in the direction where our previous conversation left us". Mild-to-moderate tightening bias, with the wording change deferred to May.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 33 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Poole: "This expansion is about as surprise-free as we ever see". Standard caveats: Hoenig "quantifying the extent of the inflation risk is difficult"; Ferguson "uncertainties regarding the supply side"; Minehan "difficult for any model to correctly anticipate"; Kohn "our ignorance of what drives those prices"; Greenspan "ability to forecast where we're going to be on May 3 is not all that terrific". Supply-side and oil caveats are voiced, but forecast confidence is generally high and the Greenbook baseline is accepted. Somewhat above normal.
