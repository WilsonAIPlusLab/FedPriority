# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 1995-05-23 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of participants' turns (Chair, Vice Chair, governors, presidents), staff turns removed. It opens with procedure (minutes approval, desk-operations questions, ratification votes), then members' questions to the staff, a round table of District and national assessments, a short policy go-around, and a vote read-out (all 11 voting members: Yes). The text contains PDF-extraction artifacts (page markers, a few joined words); nothing was garbled enough to obscure meaning.

**2. Era context (not allowed to override the text).** Mid-1995 after a year of tightening. Members describe a slowdown ("soft landing" talk), an inventory correction centred on autos, falling long rates, a weaker dollar, and an uptick in core CPI.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Broaddus: two risks, near-term cumulative downside and later inflation buildup; "real GDP currently is above its longer-term potential level."
- Forrestal, Boehne, Stern, Moskow, Parry: slowing, soft landing; inflation uptick seen as cyclical/temporary.
- Melzer: "the central concern of this Committee should be inflation"; CPI 4.9% annualized in April; "moderately tight" policy is appropriate. Hoenig, Minehan, Parry: upside risks to inflation.
- McDonough: growth risk to the upside, inflation "appreciably" above the Greenbook, "substantial uncertainty."
- Kelley, Phillips: wait-and-see.
- Lindsey: "I am going to be a minority"; credit terms easing too much; policy "a little tighter than we want it to be"; urges "earlier action"; fiscal contraction expected.
- Yellen, Blinder: soft/bumpy landing; downside risks near term; inflation contained, favourable compensation surprise.
- McTeer: risks "somewhat unbalanced to the down side," markets have already eased.
- Greenspan: B-symmetric; more nervous about an asset price bubble than product prices; inflation "not acceptable."

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: members see inflation above a price-stability goal, with core CPI accelerating and CPI forecast near 3-3.6%. Several call it cyclical and temporary; Melzer and Minehan are more worried, Yellen sees downside inflation risks. Above midpoint, modestly to clearly: 61.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Broaddus says GDP is above potential and Yellen says the economy "may have overshot potential output." Growth is now below trend and capacity utilization is falling, so the gap is closing. Slightly above 50: 53 (bright line satisfied, >50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "labor markets remain tight" (Moskow, Stern, Melzer), unemployment 4-4.5% in several Districts, but 6.3-6.8% in the NY area and wage pressures are restrained. National rate not stated. Near and slightly above NAIRU: 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumer durables, autos and housing weak, retail disappointing, final sales slowing sharply (McTeer 5.7% to 1.8%); business investment, nonresidential construction and exports strong. Net slightly below trend: 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: touched on only by Greenspan (cyclical productivity slowdown from fixed costs) and Yellen (favourable supply shock in compensation). Weak evidence: 45, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: stock market near record highs, long rates and mortgage rates down, credit widely available, banks competing aggressively, credit terms easing (Lindsey, Minehan, Phillips). Accommodative/supportive, so below 50: 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: the bond rally and flatter yield curve are discussed only as price moves, with no dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: credit "widely available," terms easing "too much" (Lindsey), the California spread is "well below the peak" after Orange County. Level below normal: 33. Evidence is indirect, so moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Phillips says P/E ratios are "not dramatically out of line"; Greenspan fears the stock market "going straight up and then straight down" and an asset price bubble. Mild-to-moderate overvaluation concern: 40.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips says banks can support growth and there is no credit bottleneck; Lindsey worries about loose underwriting later, not impairment. Healthy with a forward concern: 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: mostly depreciation (Forrestal, Minehan, Hoenig, Parry, "weaker dollar," "shock to the dollar"); Phillips mentions "the dollar has rallied." Weakness dominates: 30.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: intermediate-material prices rising (Broaddus, Parry) but steel and gypsum increases being rolled back (Moskow, Jordan): 55. Services: not discussed, N/A. Housing: not discussed as inflation, N/A. Expectations: Melzer says markets expect 3% or more over 5-10 years; Yellen says "inflationary expectations have not risen"; Phillips hopes long rates reflect lower expectations. Mixed: 52.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION means more pressure and members' evidence implies modestly above target, so a score above 50 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: easy/supportive evidence maps to <50, which matches. DOLLAR: depreciation maps to <50, which matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: low-spread, easy-availability language maps to <50, which matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold with symmetric directive. Mild hawkish voices (Melzer, Minehan, Hoenig, Parry, Stern) are offset by mild dovish voices (Lindsey wants earlier easing, McTeer, Blinder, Broaddus on preemptive easing). Net balanced: 50.
- Dissent: no dissents, all 11 Yes. Lindsey explicitly self-identifies as a minority and differs on the prescription (earlier easing) but still supports B-symmetric. Differences are in emphasis (inflation vs. downside): 24.
- Financial stability: Greenspan discusses asset price bubbles at some length, Lindsey on credit-term easing and consumer debt, McTeer on the yield curve and Japan trade. Several participants, but not dominant: 32.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive, "wait-and-see," "keep powder dry," Parry cautious about lower rates, Kelley "at least one meeting" from a decision. No directional signal: 50.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "substantial uncertainty," Hoenig "considerable uncertainty," Parry "some uncertainty," Greenspan on unpredictable path; others fairly confident in the soft landing. Moderate: 42.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (>50, "above potential"; satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 (<50, easy/supportive; satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 33 (<50, level reading from easy availability; satisfied). BANKING_STRESS 32 (<50, healthy; satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 (<50, depreciation; satisfied). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 54: consistent. OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 43: a 10-point difference, within the 15-point tolerance; levels are above potential but flows are now decelerating. FC 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 33: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A. INFLATION 61 vs sub-components (goods 55, expectations 52): somewhat above them, since the parent also reflects core CPI and wage and price commentary at the headline level; modest gap, acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only yield-level moves mentioned. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no member addresses services or shelter prices.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 61 +-8 (Moderate-High confidence; Moderate listed)**
- Evidence: Melzer: "The annualized growth rate of the CPI was 4.9 percent in April"; Broaddus: "recent acceleration in the core inflation rate." Counter: Boehne "temporary cyclical upturn."
- Summary: Core inflation is rising above what members want, but most view it as cyclical.
- Direction: above neutral because members cite accelerating core CPI and forecasts of 3% to 3.6%.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "the level of real GDP currently is above its longer-term potential level"; Yellen: "the economy may have overshot potential output."
- Summary: Slightly above potential but narrowing as growth slows below trend.
- Direction: above neutral because members say output is above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "labor markets remain tight, with the District's unemployment rate at its lowest level in over 15 years"; McDonough: unemployment 6.8% in New York.
- Summary: Tight in most Districts, loose in the Northeast, without broad wage pressure.
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: McTeer: "Growth in real final sales went from 5.7 percent to 1.8 percent"; Phillips: "slowdown is well under way... led by housing and consumer durables," with business investment a cushion.
- Summary: Spending decelerating sharply with strong capital goods partly offsetting.
- Direction: below neutral because consumer and housing components are weak.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 45 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "expected slowdown in productivity that occurs as a consequence of fixed costs not being spread"; Yellen: compensation restraint a "favorable supply shock."
- Summary: Only a cyclical productivity dip mentioned.
- Direction: slightly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Minehan: "credit is widely available, with numerous banks calling in search of new lending opportunities"; Lindsey: "a lot of easing of credit terms."
- Summary: Easy credit, high stock prices and falling long rates mean supportive conditions.
- Direction: below neutral (looser than normal).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only yield-level moves; no functioning concerns raised.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-9 (Low-Moderate)**
- Evidence: Lindsey: "credit terms are easing"; Parry: California spread "remained well below the peak."
- Summary: Spread levels are implied to be below normal by easy availability and easing terms; no spread numbers cited.
- Direction: below neutral because of the stated high availability and easing, not the spread direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-10 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Phillips: "price-earnings ratios are not dramatically out of line with historical averages"; Greenspan: "the stock market is going to go straight up and then straight down."
- Summary: Current valuations are seen as acceptable, with a looming bubble concern from the Chair.
- Direction: somewhat on the overvaluation side of neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Phillips: "Banks and other lenders appear to be able to support growth as needed"; Lindsey: bankers say competitors ease "too much."
- Summary: Banks are healthy and competitive; the worry is future loose underwriting.
- Direction: below neutral, low stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Forrestal: "the dollar's weakness against the deutschemark"; Minehan: "weaker dollar." Contrary: Phillips "The dollar has rallied."
- Summary: Mostly depreciation, helping exports and adding to inflation worries.
- Direction: below neutral because depreciation dominates.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55.** Evidence: Broaddus "continuing sharp increases in the prices of many key industrial materials"; Moskow/Jordan: steel increases rolled back.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Evidence: Melzer: markets expect "inflation at or above its current level for horizons as long as five or ten years"; Yellen: "inflationary expectations have not risen."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 50 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "just do 'B' symmetric and wait"; Lindsey: "monetary policy is probably a little tighter than we want it to be"; Melzer: "moderately tight monetary policy... is appropriate."
- Summary: Hold with symmetric bias; mild hawkish and mild dovish camps balance.
- Direction: balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: all Yes; Lindsey: "I think I am going to be a minority here."
- Summary: Unanimous vote with emphasis differences on inflation versus downside risk and one member urging earlier easing.
- Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "how we should respond to asset price bubbles"; Lindsey on consumer debt and easing credit terms.
- Summary: Several participants raise credit and asset-price issues, mostly as a future concern.
- Direction: limited-to-moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kelley: "at least one meeting, maybe a number of meetings away" from a decision; Phillips: "keep the monetary policy powder dry."
- Summary: Symmetric directive, wait-and-see, no directional signal.
- Direction: neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McDonough: "substantial uncertainty about the forecast so that an open mind is very much indicated"; Hoenig: "subject to considerable uncertainty."
- Summary: Several note forecast uncertainty about the soft landing and fiscal policy, but most are fairly confident.
- Direction: somewhat elevated.
