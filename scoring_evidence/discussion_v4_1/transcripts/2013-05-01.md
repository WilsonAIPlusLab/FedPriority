# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion transcript, meeting dated 2013-05-01 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It contains: members' questions during the staff Q&A, the full economic go-around (Lockhart through Powell, then a Chairman summary and Chairman comments), the policy go-around on statement alternatives A/B/C (including a proposed extra sentence by Governor Duke and a straw vote), a further go-around on exit-strategy principles, and a vote read-out. The vote read-out shows 11 Yes, 1 No (President George). No garbled passages affected scoring. Page markers and hard line breaks were ignored.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2013. Policy rate at the zero bound, an open-ended asset purchase program (about $85bn per month) with unemployment and inflation thresholds for the funds rate. I scored only what participants say. They describe moderate growth, unemployment around 7.5%, PCE inflation near 1%, fiscal drag from sequestration and the payroll tax increase, and a housing recovery.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Doves/accommodationists: Kocherlakota (wants more accommodation, lower threshold to 5.5%), Rosengren (large misses on both mandates), Evans (low inflation "already here," downside risks), Yellen (employment gap about 4.2 million), Raskin, Tarullo (any tapering signal would be "dissonant").
- Middle/pro-calibration: Stein and Powell (argue for tapering in June to regain control of communications), Bullard (patience; small moves either way), Williams, Dudley, Bernanke (very comfortable with high accommodation, but wants "vary/adjust" language).
- Hawkish side: Plosser (end LSAPs by year-end, taper), Fisher (begin tapering now), George (the dissenter; wants steps now signalling midyear tapering), Lacker (shift away from MBS), Lockhart, Pianalto and Duke (smaller or shorter program, signal firmer).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Evidence: PCE inflation near 1%, "particularly low readings" (Bullard), "I think that day is already here" (Evans). Several expect transitory factors to fade, which does not raise the current score. 28.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Yellen's calculation of the unemployment gap plus a participation gap, Rosengren's "large misses." "Slack remains" type language, so it must be below 50. Fiscal drag and moderate growth, with no overheating. 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Loose. Unemployment about 7.6%, employment-population ratio down, "unemployment well above full employment" (Rosengren), "just aren't enough jobs" (Raskin). Scattered reports of skilled-worker shortages (Lacker, Lockhart) do not change the level. By the rubric UE above 7% gives 16-30. 27.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly below trend on net. Consumer spending and housing are resilient (Plosser, Powell, Tarullo), but business investment is "leveling off" (Tarullo), cautious (Lockhart, Evans), and the saving rate is thin. Sequestration and the payroll tax are a drag. 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Barely discussed. Raskin says structural productivity may have slowed; Fisher says contacts are driving productivity and cost containment. Low confidence, modestly below trend. 42.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see them as accommodative (Bernanke: "financial conditions are easier"; Stein: "credit conditions are very accommodative"; Evans: "no one indicated that financial conditions were worse"). Bright-line says below 50. Mortgage credit supply is still tight in places (Bernanke), which keeps it from going very low. 29.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield moves are discussed (the 10-year falling to about 1.7%, the reversal, the JGB moves). Members do not discuss Treasury market dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level below normal and favorable. Stein cites SLOOS easing "near record levels" and record leveraged lending. Fisher cites covenant-lite near its 2007 peak and CCC access rising. Bernanke says spreads have come down in Europe and CRE terms are easing. Level reading: compressed and risk-on, so below 50. 28.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Asset values are up and called wealth-effect support (Stein, George). Fisher says QE is "unambiguously inflating asset prices" and Plosser sees buoyancy and reach for yield, but nobody calls valuations stretched in a concrete way. Mild valuation concern, low confidence. 43.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy. Duke: credit losses "at or below historic norms," delinquency transitions approaching pre-crisis levels, intense competition for loans. Bernanke mentions better credit conditions in the banking sector. Dudley's concerns are about European banks, not U.S. banks. Below 50. 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only touched through yen depreciation: "yen depreciated 2.6 percent against the dollar," from 88 to 99 (Rosengren), which means dollar appreciation against the yen. Not discussed as a concern. Low confidence, just above 50. 54.

**5. Polarity checks.** Inflation: higher means more pressure; the evidence says low pressure, so below 50. Labor: higher means tighter; the evidence says loose, so low. Credit spreads: higher means more stress; the evidence says compressed or favorable, so low. Banking stress: healthy means low. All match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee votes to hold the asset purchase pace (alternative B). The debate is about when and whether to taper, not about expanding. The doves (Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Evans, Yellen, Raskin, Tarullo) want more or unchanged accommodation. The hawkish group (Plosser, Fisher, George, Lacker, Pianalto, Duke, Lockhart partly) wants to start tapering in June or sooner. The Chairman says "I remain very comfortable, personally, with the high level of accommodation." This is the QE era with a taper debate, so moderate dovish: 34.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (George, who wanted a taper signal). Bernanke says "a little more dissonance today than usual." Tarullo says "there is a lot of disagreement around the table." The Duke-language straw vote was 8 to 7. The groups differ on economics (risk skew, labor slack) and on costs and benefits of QE. Distinct camps, one dissent: 48.
- Financial stability emphasis: Several participants devote real attention (Fisher on covenant-lite and CCC, Plosser and George on reach for yield and imbalances, Kocherlakota's tail-risk memo, Stein and Powell on MBS sales and financial stability, Dudley and Rosengren on exit risks). The focus is mainly QE and communications, not stress. Moderate: 48.
- Forward guidance: Threshold-based guidance remains. Duke's added sentence signals a readiness to vary purchases up or down. Some want to flag June tapering, but the statement stays accommodative. Strong easing commitment with a mild step toward a "dial" signal: 15.
- Uncertainty: Bernanke: "Uncertainty remains high: Is this a new normal?" Dudley: "uncertainty about the near-term outlook is quite high." Tarullo: "pretty murky." Yellen: natural rate and participation are "fraught with uncertainty." Fisher: "fog of uncertainty." Elevated, multiple participants: 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (slack language): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (accommodative): satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 (level favorable and compressed): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 (healthy): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (appreciation against the yen): satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, so no violation.

- OUTPUT_GAP (36) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (44): differ by 8; consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (29) and CREDIT_SPREADS (28): co-move.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A in normal times; consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) and sub-components: goods 33, housing 58, expectations 42. The average is above the parent. I keep the parent lower because the members' own headline focus is PCE near 1%, and housing and house-price items are not core inflation concerns for them.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only yield levels and moves discussed, no market functioning issue raised. ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Bullard: "headline PCE inflation rate measured from one year earlier is approaching 1 percent... particularly low readings." Evans: "I think that day is already here. After all, year-over-year PCE inflation is 1.0 percent."
- Summary: Inflation is seen as well below the 2% target and worrying on the low side.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe inflation near 1% and fear it falling further.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "the overall shortfall of employment from its maximum sustainable level... currently stands at about 4.2 million." Rosengren: "we will continue to have large misses for both elements of our mandate."
- Summary: Substantial slack, with growth moderate and held back by fiscal drag.
- Direction: Below neutral because members speak of resource slack and shortfalls.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Raskin: "the level of the unemployment rate remains too high mainly because there just aren't enough jobs." Rosengren: "unemployment well above full employment."
- Summary: Unemployment around 7.6% with a large participation gap; a few skill shortages do not change the picture.
- Direction: Below neutral because slack is large.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "capital expenditures now seem to be leveling off... Consumer spending and confidence have held up, really, remarkably well considering how weak income growth has been." Evans: business contacts "downright sour."
- Summary: Resilient consumption and housing offset by cautious investment and fiscal drag; net slightly below trend.
- Direction: Below neutral because investment is soft and consumer strength is not seen as sustainable.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Raskin: "structural productivity may have slowed as middle-income workers are displaced." Fisher: "Every single contact I talked to continues to drive productivity and cost containment."
- Summary: Brief, mixed mentions, tilted toward a slower trend.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because of the structural slowdown comment.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 29 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I think financial conditions are easier." Stein: "credit conditions are very accommodative."
- Summary: Conditions are broadly easy, with mortgage credit supply still restrained.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because members call conditions easy and accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Evidence: Only yield moves were raised (Lacker, Rosengren on the 10-year). No dysfunction discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Stein: for C&I lending, "the percentages reporting easing their terms or the spreads are really near record levels." Fisher: cov-lite issuance "fast approaching its June 2007 peak."
- Summary: Spreads and terms are seen as favorable and risk-on, with some members worried about excess.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is compressed and terms are easy.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Fisher: "quantitative easing is unambiguously inflating asset prices." Stein: "significant increases in asset values—in house prices and stock prices."
- Summary: Higher equity prices are treated mostly as wealth support, with mild froth concern from a minority.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (modestly elevated valuation, per rubric) because of the froth comments, but no concrete overvaluation claim.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Duke: "Credit losses are now at or below historic norms, and, even in mortgages, the transition rates into delinquency are approaching pre-crisis levels."
- Summary: U.S. banks are healthy and competing for loans.
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 +-6 (Low)**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "The yen depreciated 2.6 percent against the dollar... from 88 to 99 to the dollar."
- Summary: The only FX content is dollar appreciation against the yen after the BOJ move; not framed as a concern.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because the dollar rose against the yen.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 33.** Evidence: Bernanke summary: "Food prices have recently been weak, as have the prices of other commodities and raw materials"; Fisher: grain prices down up to 10%.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed as such.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Evidence: Williams: "House price appreciation has also been more brisk than I had expected"; Bernanke: lumber up about 35%, construction costs modest.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Evans: models show three-year-ahead expectations "below our 2 percent long-run objective"; Bernanke summary: five-by-five breakevens "closer to target"; Raskin: "If inflation expectations changed at all, they've drifted down."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 34 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I remain very comfortable, personally, with the high level of accommodation, and I don't see any basis for changing that in this meeting." Powell: "I believe that we ought to take the next plausible opportunity reduce the pace of purchases."
- Summary: The Committee holds the asset purchase program; the debate is when to taper, with a sizable hawkish minority and a vocal dovish wing.
- Direction: Dovish-leaning because purchases continue at full pace and many argue for more or no less accommodation.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 +-8 (High)**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "Boy, there is a lot of disagreement around the table." Vote read-out: George "No." Straw vote on the Duke sentence: 8 in favor, 7 opposed.
- Summary: One formal dissent, plus camps that disagree on the outlook, on QE costs and benefits, and on communication.
- Direction: Above the "broad agreement" band because of the dissent and visible camps, but short of significant division since 11 of 12 voted yes and most supported alternative B.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: George: "Evidence of financial imbalances has emerged." Powell: on MBS sales, "This, to me, is the very place where financial stability could erupt."
- Summary: Several participants address it meaningfully, mainly as a QE cost and an exit-design issue, not as a main theme.
- Direction: Attention is moderate; it is not tied to direction.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 15 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Evans: policy aims "to reduce the unemployment rate below 6½ percent as soon as possible." Duke's added sentence on being "prepared to vary" the pace up or down.
- Summary: Threshold guidance plus open-ended purchases stays in place, with an incremental signal that purchases could be adjusted either way.
- Direction: Easing-biased because the thresholds and purchases remain in force.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "uncertainty about the near-term outlook is quite high." Bernanke: "Uncertainty remains high: Is this a new normal?"
- Summary: Several participants emphasize unusually high uncertainty about near-term data, labor slack and the fiscal drag.
- Direction: Elevated because multiple participants stress it, though not at crisis levels.
