# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2001-12-11

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: the members' questions to staff, the economic go-around (Parry through Olson), a short exchange on the Bluebook "perfect foresight" simulation, then the policy go-around, the vote read-out and a brief statement discussion. Staff answers are removed, so the Q&A shows only fragments such as "Right." and "Yes." Those fragments carry no scoreable content and I ignored them.

**2. Era context.** Winter 2001. The country is in a recession that was deepened by the 9/11 attacks. The funds rate has been cut 450bp this year to 2 percent. The proposal on the table is a 25bp cut (down from the earlier 50bp steps) with the balance-of-risks language kept toward weakness. I scored only what members said.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Weak-economy, risks-down camp: Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Parry, Ferguson, Gramlich, Meyer, Santomero. All describe recession, rising unemployment, disinflation and downside risks.
- Stabilization camp: Stern, Guynn, Hoenig, McTeer, Jordan. They see stabilization or an emerging recovery, and policy that is already accommodative.
- Chairman: sees "deflationary forces" still in play and argues the risk of doing nothing is too high.
- Poole: close to a trough; wants to start an orderly process toward a changed stance.
- Vice Chairman McDonough, Bies, Olson: briefly concur.

**4. Core dimension directions (stated before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Evidence: Bies, "very little pressure on inflation"; Minehan, "inflation is quiescent if not declining"; Broaddus, core PCE about 1.5 percent and heading lower; Poole, identical Wal-Mart items 16 percent cheaper; Greenspan, firms' ability to pass through cost increases is "virtually nonexistent". Meyer is the one voice putting some weight on a supply shock damping the disinflation. Score is in the low 20s to high 20s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Evidence: "the economy is clearly in recession" (Moskow); "excess capacity"; Gramlich, unemployment peaking at 6.4 percent "a long way from full employment"; Meyer, unemployment still above his 5-1/4 percent NAIRU at the end of 2003. Slack remains, so the score is below 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. The unemployment rate is rising "fastest ... since 1982" (Broaddus), the November report was weak, and many say further increases are "baked in the cake" (Stern). A few pockets of tightness are reported (Jordan: health care "hard-to-fill openings"; Hoenig: lowest share of firms reporting hiring difficulty, the lowest in the survey's history). Net is clear slack, consistent with an unemployment rate in the 5.5-6.5 percent range.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend overall. The consumer is the mainstay (record auto sales on zero-percent financing, holiday sales "not as weak as feared"), but the auto surge is temporary and now fading (Greenspan: "slumped very sharply in the first 10 days of December"), and capital spending, construction and exports are weak. Netted slightly below the midpoint.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Parry calls Q3 growth "robust"; Gramlich, "holding up remarkably well for a recession"; Greenspan, output per hour better than anticipated. Meyer notes the staff trend estimate falling to about 1-1/2 percent next year. Net is modestly above trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants call policy "accommodative" (Hoenig, Guynn), credit spreads have narrowed, equities are up and markets are optimistic. Offsetting: mortgage and Treasury yields have risen almost 1 point (Greenspan), and small businesses report "a lack of bank financing except on a fully secured basis" (Minehan). The bright-line rule for "accommodative" requires below 50. Mildly loose.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning was discussed in the opening exchange (Greenspan on the 10-year yield and MBS duration; Minehan on bond-market volatility and "a market that is not as deep"). The comments concern yield and volatility moves, not dysfunction. Normal range, with thin evidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Broaddus mentions it ("credit spreads have narrowed"). That is direction only, with no level given. Per the v4.1 rule: 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Narrowing puts it just under 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: The scale is higher = more stress or concern. Here the concern is overvaluation (Minehan: "high P/E ratios ... euphoria"; Greenspan: standing pat "requires that the stock market remain overvalued"; Meyer: "perception that there is some overvaluation today"). That belongs in the elevated-valuation (low-score) range. Moderately low.
- BANKING_STRESS: Moskow, "we have not seen any adverse spillover to the banking sector, as bankers have limited the excess use of leverage"; Minehan, "the health of our banking system". Sound, so below 50, with a minor note on tight lending to small businesses. Briefly discussed only.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: McDonough only: the current account deficit "can be financed ... with very little if any weakening of the dollar". Roughly stable; one passing mention, so Low confidence.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more of the named construct. Inflation is low, so a low score matches. Slack is high, so low OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS match. For EQUITY_VALUATION, higher means more distress or undervaluation concern, while the participants' worry is overvaluation, so the score sits below 50. For CREDIT_SPREADS, narrowing with no level stated means a score just under 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the outcome is a 25bp cut with risks still weighted to weakness. Broaddus would still prefer 50bp, and Gramlich and Meyer see room for easing. Against that, Stern, Guynn, Jordan, Hoenig and McTeer prefer to pause, and Hoenig dissents. Poole, Meyer, Bies and others want the easing to be signaled as nearly over. The majority favors a modest, final-looking easing, so it is moderately dovish at the mild end.
- Disagreement: one formal dissent (Hoenig, preferring no change). Four more members state a preference for pausing but accept the cut (Stern, Guynn, McTeer; Jordan agrees with Stern, though Stern, Guynn and Jordan do not vote this year in the roll shown). Broaddus preferred a larger cut. The debate is mostly about tactics and insurance rather than the economic assessment. The score fits "1 dissent plus several reservations."
- Financial stability attention: members discuss stock-market valuation, mortgage rates and MBS duration, bond-market depth, spreads and bank lending, but as part of an economy-centered debate. Moderate attention.
- Forward guidance: the statement keeps the weakness bias, but several members and the Chairman want the language to signal the situation is "more mixed", with Poole's "two-thirds chance" of no change next time. The signal is easing bias fading, so still below 50.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan, "I don't know that and I don't think anybody can know that"; Ferguson on how hard it is "to identify with great certainty turning points"; Stern, "timing is always a matter of considerable uncertainty"; Santomero, error bands; Greenspan on the unemployment shock as "indeterminate". Elevated, although several members speak with conviction.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50: yes (recession and slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50: yes ("accommodative"). CREDIT_SPREADS: direction only, 46, inside 45-55 as required. BANKING_STRESS below 50: yes. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no appreciation is claimed, so near 47 is acceptable. TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, so it stays in the 36-50 band.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 36: within 15 points. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: within 15 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 with CREDIT_SPREADS 46: both near neutral to slightly loose, consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, near 45, fine. INFLATION_PRESSURE 26 vs sub-components (goods 22, expectations 38): the two scored sub-components average about 30, consistent with 26 given the services and housing sub-components are not scored.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no member discusses services prices. INFLATION_HOUSING: only commercial real estate rents and housing activity are discussed, not shelter inflation. Dollar and Treasury items are scored with Low confidence because each is only briefly raised.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

### Economic conditions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 26 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Bies, "I find very little pressure on inflation at the moment"; Minehan, "inflation is quiescent if not declining"; Broaddus, "there are broader reasons to be concerned about disinflation."
- Summary: Participants see low and falling inflation, with some talk of deflationary forces.
- Direction: below neutral because core inflation is around 1-1/2 percent and members worry about disinflation.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Moskow, "the economy is clearly in recession"; Gramlich, unemployment peaking at 6.4 percent "a long way from full employment by anybody's definition."
- Summary: The economy is in recession, with slack expected to persist well into 2003.
- Direction: below potential because of recession and projected slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 36 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, the rise in unemployment "has been the fastest increase in a long time--since 1982"; Stern, further increases "almost baked in the cake."
- Summary: Labor markets are slackening, with a few pockets of hiring difficulty (Jordan: hard-to-fill health-care openings).
- Direction: below neutral because unemployment is elevated and still rising.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan, motor vehicle surge shows consumer "resiliency" but "real income fell sharply"; Broaddus, "a very significant further drop in spending on equipment and software."
- Summary: A temporary consumer surge on strong auto incentives, offset by weak business investment, construction and exports.
- Direction: below trend, in the sluggish range, with the consumer holding the net up.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Parry, "the 1-1/2 percent increase is robust considering the decline in real output"; Gramlich, "productivity is holding up remarkably well for a recession."
- Summary: Productivity is seen as above trend for a recession, with a small caveat from Meyer on a staff deceleration.
- Direction: above neutral because several members call recent gains strong.

### Financial conditions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "A 2 percent fed funds rate is stimulative"; Broaddus, "stock market is up, ... credit spreads have narrowed"; Minehan, small businesses report "a lack of bank financing except on a fully secured basis."
- Summary: Markets and policy are described as accommodative, partly offset by higher long-term and mortgage rates and tight small-business bank credit.
- Direction: below neutral (looser), consistent with the "accommodative" bright line.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 45 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan, bond market "not as deep because of corporate supply conditions"; Greenspan, "this correlation between the 10-year Treasury yield and duration in the mortgage-backed securities market bizarre."
- Summary: The yield jump and volatility are discussed as odd but not as dysfunction.
- Direction: near neutral, normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 46 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "credit spreads have narrowed."
- Summary: One passing mention of narrowing, with no level given. Per the v4.1 rule, direction only places the score at 45-55 on the side of the move.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because of narrowing, with the level unknown.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 36 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan, "the very euphoria of the stock market right now is a risk in and of itself"; Greenspan, standing pat "requires that the stock market remain overvalued for a protracted period."
- Summary: Several members see valuations as stretched against dismal profits, with Greenspan allowing it could be a new valuation regime.
- Direction: in the elevated-valuation (below 50) range.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Moskow, "we have not seen any adverse spillover to the banking sector"; Minehan, "the health of our banking system."
- Summary: Banks are viewed as sound, with only a minor note on tight bank credit for small firms.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are called healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 47 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: McDonough, the deficit "can be financed ... with very little if any weakening of the dollar."
- Summary: A single remark implying a stable dollar.
- Direction: roughly neutral.

### Inflation sub-components

**INFLATION_GOODS: 22.** Evidence: Poole, identical Wal-Mart items "16 percent lower this year"; Moskow, "many retailers are offering sizable price discounts." Falling goods prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed as price inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38.** Evidence: Parry, Michigan one-year expectations fell to 0.4 percent then rebounded to 1.6 percent; Jordan, longer-term "measures ... don't seem to have moved at all." Short-term expectations are low, longer-term unchanged.

### Discussion-specific

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 35 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "move the funds rate down another 25 basis points ... and stay asymmetric to the downside"; Broaddus, "a 50 basis point cut is the best option"; Stern, "I would prefer to leave policy unchanged today."
- Summary: A majority backs a moderate 25bp cut with a downside bias, a minority wants to pause, and a few want a larger cut.
- Direction: dovish because the action is easing, but the cut is small and framed as near the end of easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, "President Hoenig No"; McTeer, "I'd like to associate myself with the comments of Gary, Jerry, Jack, and Tom."
- Summary: One formal dissent plus several members who would have preferred to pause and one who would have gone to 50bp; the debate is largely about tactics.
- Direction: moderate disagreement in the 31-45 band (one dissent plus several reservations).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan on stock-market euphoria and P/E ratios; Greenspan on MBS duration and mortgage rates feeding home-equity extraction.
- Summary: Several participants address market valuation, rates and credit meaningfully, but the debate is centered on the real economy.
- Direction: moderate discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole, "a two-thirds chance that we will not change the funds rate at the next meeting"; Bies, "beginning to signal that this may be one of our last easing actions."
- Summary: The statement keeps the weakness bias but is meant to signal that easing is winding down; no member wants an explicit tightening signal.
- Direction: below neutral, but with a fading easing bias and data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I don't know that and I don't think anybody can know that"; Ferguson, "extraordinarily difficult to identify with great certainty turning points."
- Summary: Many members stress hard-to-time turning points and shock risks (terrorism, fiscal stimulus, labor shock), though others speak with more confidence.
- Direction: elevated.
