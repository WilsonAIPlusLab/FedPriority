# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1998-05-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening business, member questions during the staff Q&A (securities lending, Japan, the forecast), the economic go-around, a short post-break policy section (Chairman's recommendation followed by a policy go-around) and the vote read-out. Staff turns are removed, so many members' questions appear without the answers. I score members' views only.

**2. Era context.** Spring 1998: very strong growth with low measured inflation, a very tight labor market, a booming stock market, Asian crisis, and a "virtuous cycle" productivity debate. This is context only; scores come from what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, Governors Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, Phillips, Rivlin, and Presidents Boehne, Broaddus, Guynn, Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Poole, Stern. Governor Phillips is attending her last meeting.

**4. Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Realized inflation is described as low and well contained (Phillips "well contained, even falling"; McTeer "GDP deflator lowest since 1964", PPI non-increases; Poole "price pressures ... isolated"). Against that, many see rising upside risk: core CPI on a consistent basis rising toward 2.7-3 percent (Meyer, Moskow, McDonough), wage acceleration, 3-month core CPI 2.8 percent. Participants see current pressure as somewhat below the midpoint, with risk tilting up. The score is for the current level, so I put it just below 50, not high.
- OUTPUT_GAP: "Employment and output growth appear unsustainably strong" (Boehne); GDP growth about 3 percent "above estimates of a sustainable trend" (Moskow); "growth is extraordinarily strong" (Phillips). Above potential, so the score must be above 50. Hoenig notes capacity utilization is below its historical average, and many stress the supply side is accommodating demand, so only moderately above.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 4.3 percent unemployment, "extremely tight by all measures" (Moskow), "extremely tight" (Broaddus), many anecdotes. The unemployment-rate anchor says well above 55. Wage growth is moderate, which keeps it from the top of the range.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: "extraordinary current strength in consumer demand" (Broaddus), "impressive momentum in domestic demand" (Meyer), "blown the doors off" (Guynn). Well above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Debated. Greenspan sees a "marked and discontinuous pickup" since 1995; Rivlin, Ferguson and Kelley see an upshift; Meyer, McDonough, Minehan and Hoenig are skeptical. Net: above trend in the center of gravity.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Accommodative" (Stern, Minehan, Ferguson on the credit aggregates), "very supportive" (Meyer), "readily available credit" (Guynn). Bright line: must be below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed by members as a market-function issue (only the staff-facing securities-lending questions). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Boehne cites a "narrowing of spreads" and "narrow risk spreads between Treasuries and corporate bonds including junk bonds"; Jordan and Broaddus note lax lending and abundant money. The stated level is narrow, so below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan says he thinks the market is "significantly overvalued"; Rivlin says "clearly too high"; the bubble is discussed at length. Elevated valuation concern, so low on this scale. Greenspan and Phillips doubt whether a bubble can be identified or policy can address it, which moderates the number.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed and thin. McDonough says "the banking sector looks rather good"; Guynn says "examiners have not yet uncovered any serious problems"; Boehne cites consumer bankruptcies and subprime lending; Jordan cites questionable lending. Japan's banking system is troubled, but that is foreign. Healthy domestically with minor concerns, so below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "the strong dollar" (McTeer), "a higher dollar" (Parry), "long cumulative appreciation of the dollar" (Meyer), "rising dollar" (Rivlin). Appreciation, so above 50, modestly.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS: Moskow "cannot raise prices because of fierce competition", McTeer soft commodities, so below 50. SERVICES: Moskow "increases in consumer prices are being driven by the prices of services", Phillips "domestic service ... seeing some price increases", so a little above 50. HOUSING: only commercial rents and home prices appear in passing, with no consumer shelter inflation, so N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Poole "solid, perhaps even rock solid, expectations ... of continuing low inflation" but fragile, Parry "diminished", so slightly below 50.

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter; members say accommodative, so the score is below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress; narrow spreads, so below 50. EQUITY_VALUATION is on the "more concern" scale where overvaluation maps to low numbers; Greenspan and Rivlin say overvalued, so low. BANKING_STRESS higher = more stress; "looks rather good" means low.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Chairman recommends no change to the funds rate with an asymmetric directive toward tightening; the vote is 10-2 for it. Many members, including Greenspan, Ferguson, Meyer, Moskow, Kelley and Gramlich, expect tightening soon. Poole and Jordan dissent in favor of tightening now. Broaddus, Guynn and Minehan would have preferred a move today and accepted holding. Rivlin, Parry, Hoenig, Boehne, Stern and McDonough are comfortable waiting, with McDonough calling a hike "extremely ill advised". The current action is a hold and the forward lean is hawkish, with a clear hawkish minority. Lean hawkish: a hold with a tightening bias, lifted by the pro-hike voices.
- Disagreement: Two formal dissents, both hawkish. Several others prefer to tighten but concede. The disagreement is mainly about timing and the cost of waiting, and partly about productivity and inflation. Moderate-to-notable.
- Financial stability attention: The stock-market bubble question takes up a major block (Phillips, Rivlin, Gramlich, Greenspan, Meyer, Jordan, McTeer). Boehne lists financial excesses in detail, Minehan and Jordan describe asset-price and credit-quality risks, and the Asia/Japan fragility is central. A prominent topic.
- Forward guidance: Asymmetric directive, "likely will have to tighten" (Kelley), "before the summer is out" (Ferguson), "maintaining asymmetry toward tightening is essential" (Greenspan). Mild-to-moderate tightening bias.
- Uncertainty: Frequent explicit humility and uncertainty (Kelley "too much uncertainty", Stern "so much uncertainty", Boehne "hard analysis will not give us the answer", Gramlich on NAIRU, Greenspan on productivity and Japan, Meyer "knife edge"). Elevated, not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 (above sustainable): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (accommodative): satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 (narrow level stated): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 (healthy): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (appreciation): satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (63) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (77): a 14-point gap, within tolerance. The unemployment-rate anchor drives labor; output is held down by the supply-side offsets and Hoenig's below-average capacity utilization.
- OUTPUT_GAP (63) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (68): consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (26): co-move, both loose.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (43) vs sub-components (goods about 36, services about 55, expectations about 44): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member comments on Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: no consumer shelter-price discussion; commercial rents and home prices came up only as asset-price or boom signs.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +-7, Moderate-to-High confidence (given as Moderate).**
- Evidence: Phillips: "by almost any measure inflation appears to be well contained, even falling." Meyer: "annualized inflation rates over three-month and six-month intervals exhibit quite a pronounced upward trend ... the risk of higher inflation has escalated."
- Center of gravity: Realized inflation is low and benign, with rising upside risk on wages and a drifting-up CPI.
- Direction: Below neutral (current level), because most members describe prices as contained even while warning about the outlook.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 63 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Boehne: "Employment and output growth appear unsustainably strong." Moskow: GDP growth "above estimates of a sustainable trend."
- Center of gravity: Demand is running well ahead of sustainable growth and supply is being stretched, though supply responses are strong.
- Direction: Above neutral, because growth is above sustainable and labor is very tight.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 77 +-5, High.**
- Evidence: Moskow: "Labor markets are extremely tight by all measures." Broaddus: "Everybody agrees that the labor markets are extremely tight"; April unemployment at 4.3 percent.
- Center of gravity: Unanimous description of an extremely tight labor market with a 4.3 percent unemployment rate.
- Direction: Above neutral, because unemployment is far below prior NAIRU estimates and workers are scarce.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "the extraordinary current strength in consumer demand." Guynn: "Had it not been for the drag from net exports, we would have blown the doors off."
- Center of gravity: Domestic demand is very strong and repeatedly surprising to the upside, offset only by net exports and a possible inventory correction.
- Direction: Above trend, because growth exceeded forecasts consistently.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "a marked and discontinuous pickup in productivity growth that started in 1995." Meyer: "recent robust productivity growth can be explained by ... a more modest increase in productivity and a normal cyclical rebound."
- Center of gravity: The Chair and several governors see an upward shift, with skeptics, so the net view is above trend but contested.
- Direction: Above neutral, because the leadership view and the staff estimate are higher trend growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Stern: "what I take to be quite accommodative financial market conditions." Meyer: "financial conditions appear to be very supportive."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are broadly described as easy, with plentiful credit, rapid money growth and rising asset prices.
- Direction: Below neutral (loose), because members call conditions accommodative and credit freely available.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed by members as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 26 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Boehne: "narrow risk spreads between Treasuries and corporate bonds including junk bonds." Boehne also cites "the narrowing of spreads, strong competition for lending that has led to an easing in credit terms."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are narrow with easy credit terms, a theme raised by a few members.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress), because the stated level is narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 20 +-8, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I conclude that the stock market is significantly overvalued, which I do." Rivlin: "the stock market, which clearly is too high."
- Center of gravity: Extended and heavy discussion of a possible bubble, with a leading view that valuations are stretched but that policy cannot sensibly target them.
- Direction: Stretched valuations, so low on this scale, because several members call the market overvalued.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-10, Low-to-Moderate (given as Low).**
- Evidence: McDonough: "the banking sector looks rather good." Guynn: "our examiners have not yet uncovered any serious problems."
- Center of gravity: Domestic banks are described as sound, with some concern about lax lending and consumer credit, and Japan's banks are a separate worry.
- Direction: Below neutral, because banks are sound with minor concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: McTeer: "given the strong dollar, the flat yield curve, and soft commodity prices." Meyer: "the long cumulative appreciation of the dollar should continue [to weigh]."
- Center of gravity: Members repeatedly cite a strong or rising dollar as a drag on net exports and a disinflationary force.
- Direction: Above neutral, because the dollar has appreciated.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36.** Moskow: goods producers "cannot raise prices because of fierce competition"; McTeer cites soft commodities and PPI non-increases.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 55.** Moskow: "increases in consumer prices are being driven by the prices of services."
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Poole: "solid, perhaps even rock solid, expectations ... of continuing low inflation," though fragile.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "maintaining asymmetry toward tightening is essential." Jordan: "we need to do something now or ... the toothpaste is already out of the tube."
- Center of gravity: The decision is to hold, but with a tightening bias, two hawkish dissents and several more members who would have preferred to move.
- Direction: Hawkish, because no one wants easing and a sizable group favors tightening soon, though the action is a hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 +-7, High.**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "That is a 10 to 2 vote" (Jordan and Poole dissenting). Poole: "if we do not act today ... we will be getting ourselves further behind." Rivlin: "I do not share the view of Presidents Poole and Minehan that there is much of a risk of waiting too long."
- Center of gravity: Two formal dissents plus open disagreement on the cost of waiting, with others deferring reluctantly.
- Direction: Notable disagreement, because there are two dissents, but the debate is mostly about timing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Boehne: "The risks in the financial sector also are tilted in the direction of excess." Jordan: "we do not see people making decisions based on the assumption that asset prices will [not] rise forever."
- Center of gravity: Stock-market bubble, asset prices, lax lending and international financial fragility take up a large share of the discussion.
- Direction: Prominent topic, because many members and the Chair's remarks address it at length.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +-7, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "maintaining asymmetry toward tightening is essential." Ferguson: "it probably will be appropriate to move before the summer is out."
- Center of gravity: Asymmetric directive and widespread expectation of a coming tightening, with timing left open.
- Direction: Hawkish, because the signal is for higher rates, though not immediately.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Kelley: "I think there is too much uncertainty to say that we are sitting tight." Stern: "so much uncertainty that I conclude we should not do very much."
- Center of gravity: Many members stress uncertainty over productivity, NAIRU, inventories and Asia, and ask for humility.
- Direction: Elevated, because of repeated emphasis by many speakers, but without extreme language.
