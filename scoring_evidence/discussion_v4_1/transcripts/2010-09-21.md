# FOMC member-discussion scoring, v4.1 rubric: transcript discussion, meeting 2010-09-21

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). It holds every member turn in order: a procedural opening on how the minutes are drafted, members' questions to staff (staff answers removed), the economic go-round, the Chairman's summary and policy remarks, the policy round on draft statements (alternatives A1/A2, B, C), a closing wording negotiation, and the roll-call read-out. Pure procedure (minutes drafting, Deputy Secretary appointment, meeting logistics) is ignored. Chairman Bernanke's quoted Greenbook/Tealbook numbers are staff-derived and are not used as evidence; his own judgments are.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2010: unemployment about 9.6%, core inflation about 1%, the policy rate at the zero bound, a large balance sheet, and a debate about further asset purchases and about changing the policy framework. I take this only as background. All scores come from what the participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Doves, who want more accommodation soon: Yellen (supports B, prefers A1), Evans (liquidity trap, wants a price-level objective and aggressive action), Dudley (supports B, prefers A at the next meeting, "being late is far worse than being early"), Rosengren (B now, A likely needed), Kocherlakota (worried about disinflation, B with softer wording), and Bernanke, who says the Committee is "getting very close" to acting.
- Middle, who support B with a softer paragraph 4: Bullard, Pianalto, Lockhart, Tarullo, Duke ("further purchases... are warranted" but wants a framework), Warsh (sees little benefit from $500bn and low deflation risk, supports B with "if").
- Hawkish or sceptical: Hoenig (prefers C, objects to "extended period" and to B, the lone "No" vote), Lacker (against moving, against signalling November action, deflation not a present danger), Plosser (against a large-scale asset purchase program, wants to back off), Fisher (sides with Lacker and Plosser on the statement, sceptical that accommodation creates US jobs).

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Bullard says "about 1 percent", Rosengren "core CPI... just below 1 percent", Evans "significantly underrunning price stability", Yellen "inflation is running below desired levels", Dudley "not that far from outright deflation". Lacker alone says 1.5% is "just fine", and Warsh and Plosser call deflation risk low. Some firms report rising input prices (Fisher). That is the 26-40 band, toward the lower end given the repeated "too low" language. 27.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Yellen: "enormous resource slack, which I expect will persist for years" and about 3 points of excess unemployment. Evans: "very large margin of slack". Tarullo: absence of demand is the main factor. Counter-view: Plosser, Warsh and Lacker stress structural factors, a higher NAIRU and a lower potential. Even so they do not say the economy is at potential. 32.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well below the midpoint. Unemployment is about 9.6%, Rosengren says near a 25-year low for hard-to-fill jobs, Kocherlakota cites an employment-to-population ratio of 58.5%, and Yellen reports an abundance of qualified applicants. Structural arguments raise the natural rate to 6-8% but still leave unemployment well above it. 20.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Yellen: "anemic retail sales, decelerating investment". Lacker: consumption about 2% with equipment investment "briskly" expanding. Plosser: retail showed some strength. Fisher: consumers are resilient but price-sensitive. Housing is very weak (Duke, Lockhart). Hiring and capex are held back (Pianalto, Evans). Net: sluggish. 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Barely discussed. Fisher says firms are "focused laser-like on enhancing productivity" and Evans notes "substantial gains in productivity" but from cost cutting, which "hardly seems to bode well" for further gains. Gains now, no trend view. 53, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed but accommodative on net. Plosser: "generous supply of liquidity... markets appear to be in good working order". Fisher: "even better access to credit... abundant liquidity". Hoenig: bankers have "more deposits than we can deploy". Warsh: spreads "continue to be narrow" and issuance impressive. Dudley: a tightening followed by a return of risk appetite, "broadly unchanged", though "significant constraints on credit availability remain". Duke: loans for expansion are hard to get. Small borrowers and mortgages stay tighter. The bright-line rule needs below 50 for "accommodative". 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Bullard and Warsh ask whether large purchases could impair Treasury market functioning, but only hypothetically. No member says current functioning is impaired. N/A, since no member characterises the current state.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Warsh says "Credit spreads continue to be narrow" and "corporate issuance continues to be impressive". Fisher's contacts report abundant credit, and non-investment-grade financing is "plentiful" (Hoenig). Narrow means below 50. Dudley mentions widened sovereign spreads in Ireland, which is not corporate credit. 36.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price direction ("Equity prices are up", Warsh) and Yellen's mention of "higher equity prices" as a policy channel. No valuation view. Default neutral. 49, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Below the midpoint, with pockets. Duke: delinquencies and charge-offs "remain elevated but, with the exception of commercial real estate, are improving"; CRE construction still deteriorating. Dudley: investors are confident large banks can meet Basel standards. Chairman's summary: credit problems "mostly peaked, except in CRE". Healthy with minor concerns. 42.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Mentioned only as a possible policy side-effect (Bullard on competitive devaluation, Yellen on "a lower dollar" as a channel) and in Fisher's question to staff, whose answer is removed. No member describes the dollar's recent movement. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure; evidence says low inflation; score low. Match.
- OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS: slack means low scores. Match.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress; "improving... healthy" means below 50. Match.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "narrow" means below 50. Match.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; the dominant language is "accommodative/abundant", so below 50. Match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Nobody argues for tightening. Policy is held (alternative B, rate near zero, reinvestment of MBS paydowns). A majority and the Chair lean to further easing (QE) at the next meeting unless data improve markedly. A minority (Lacker, Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, with Warsh sceptical of the benefits) oppose signalling or doing it. Because this is a QE-era debate about how much more to buy and when, the lean is in the dovish band. 28.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (Hoenig). Reservations about statement wording from Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Warsh, Kocherlakota, Bullard and Lockhart. Open disagreement on the economics: structural versus cyclical unemployment (Plosser, Lacker, Warsh versus Yellen, Evans, Tarullo), the size of deflation risk, and whether policy has any traction. Lacker also challenges the dual-mandate framing. The exchanges are courteous and the vote is 9-1. Notable disagreement, distinct camps, one dissent. 50.
- Financial stability attention: A moderate share of the discussion. The Chairman cites staff financial-stability memos. Hoenig on farmland lending at 80% loan-to-value and banks reaching for yield. Warsh on money funds and Europe, "dry tinder". Dudley on Europe, municipal bonds and Basel. Duke on the banking sector. Rosengren on European CDS. It is context and risk, not the focus. 38.
- Forward guidance: The draft statement keeps "extended period" and says the Committee "is prepared to provide additional accommodation" (softened to "if needed"). Dudley wants the market to see about 60% odds of a move in November, Bullard expects 90-95% market odds. This is a clear easing bias with a hint of action. 14.
- Uncertainty: Many stress it. Pianalto's contacts are "more certain" about slower growth. Kocherlakota says households and firms "are right to be uncertain" and he himself is "uncertain". Bullard: "uncharted territory", "our models do not admit" the Japan scenario. Duke has her own area of uncertainty. Chairman: "genuine uncertainty", "no safe option". Dudley: "difficult to judge the risk". Others (Lacker, Warsh, Hoenig) are fairly confident. High but not extreme. 58.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 (below 50, slack language). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (accommodative language, below 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 36 (narrow, below 50). BANKING_STRESS 42 (sound/improving, below 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 32 against LABOR 20: a 12-point gap, within 15. Labor is lower because unemployment is far above even the raised NAIRU estimates, while some participants see lower potential output, which narrows the output gap relative to the labor gap.
- OUTPUT_GAP 32 against CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: a 6-point gap. Fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 against CREDIT_SPREADS 36: close. Fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS against TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is N/A, which is consistent with normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 27 against sub-components: expectations 33 (slipping down, "well below 2 percent") and goods 45 (some import and commodity price increases but little pricing power). The parent sits below the sub-components because the headline focus is core and services disinflation (not scored, see below). Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH: see above; no member characterised current conditions.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: only Plosser gives a services figure, and the transcript text for it is garbled ("services component is up only about 9 percent", which cannot be right alongside 2.1% and 2¾% for the other components). I do not guess. The remaining remarks are general ("wage gains very limited").
- INFLATION_HOUSING: no member addresses shelter or rent inflation (the Chairman only mentions measures that exclude OER in passing).

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "core CPI is currently just below 1 percent and is expected to remain far below 2 percent". Evans, "inflation significantly underrunning price stability". Lacker (the one dissenting voice on this) calls 1.5% "just fine".
- Center of gravity: Below target and disinflationary, with deflation worry but not a spiral.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because nearly all members describe inflation of about 1% and projected to stay low.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "enormous resource slack, which I expect will persist for years". Evans, "that still leaves a very large margin of slack".
- Center of gravity: Large slack in the median view, tempered by structuralist members who raise the NAIRU.
- Direction confirmation: The output gap is below neutral because members consistently describe slack and an economy below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 +-4 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, hard-to-fill jobs at "near the 25-year low". Kocherlakota, employment-to-population "has fallen to 58.5 percent". Yellen, "an abundance of qualified workers, not a shortage".
- Center of gravity: Very loose labor market with unemployment about 9.6%.
- Direction confirmation: Labor is below neutral because of high unemployment and weak hiring.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "anemic retail sales, decelerating investment". Lacker, consumer spending "about a 2 percent" and equipment investment "expanding briskly".
- Center of gravity: Sluggish spending, housing weak, business investment cautious.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is below trend because of high saving, deleveraging and withheld hiring and capex.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 53 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fisher, interlocutors "focused laser-like on enhancing productivity". Evans, "substantial gains in productivity" from cost-cutting, which does not "bode well for further strong future productivity gains".
- Center of gravity: Recent gains noted, no trend view.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is slightly above neutral on near-term gains, but the evidence is thin and discounted by Evans.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Plosser, "a generous supply of liquidity... financial markets appear to be in good working order". Dudley, "financial conditions are broadly unchanged since the last meeting" after a tightening and a return of risk appetite, with "significant constraints on credit availability" remaining.
- Center of gravity: Broadly supportive for large firms and markets, tighter for small borrowers, housing and bank credit.
- Direction confirmation: Conditions are below neutral (looser) because the dominant description is abundant liquidity and ready credit access.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Evidence: Only hypothetical questions on market functioning under large purchases (Bullard, Warsh); no assessment of current functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "Credit spreads continue to be narrow, and... corporate issuance continues to be impressive". Hoenig, non-investment-grade firms find "financing plentiful".
- Center of gravity: Spreads narrow and issuance strong.
- Direction confirmation: Spreads are below neutral because members state a narrow level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 49 +-6 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "Equity prices are up." Yellen cites "higher equity prices" as a policy channel.
- Center of gravity: Price direction only, no valuation concern.
- Direction confirmation: Roughly neutral, since no valuation view is expressed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Duke, "delinquencies, nonperformers, and charge-offs remain elevated but, with the exception of commercial real estate, are improving". Dudley, "Investors appear to be confident about the ability of large banks to meet the standards".
- Center of gravity: Recovering banks with a CRE problem and weak loan demand.
- Direction confirmation: Stress is below neutral because the sector is described as improving and able to meet capital standards.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A**
- Evidence: Only policy-side mentions (Bullard, Yellen); no description of recent dollar moves.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 45**
- Evidence: Fisher, retailers report "rising input prices from imported goods" but "insufficient... pricing power". Chairman, price pressures on "some commodities and imported goods may be rising".
- Direction: Slightly below stable, since pricing power is limited.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** (the only figure, Plosser's, is garbled in the text; no other member gives one.)

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** (not discussed.)

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 33**
- Evidence: Bullard, five-year TIPS breakeven "about 1.2 percent, somewhat low", Cleveland curve "significantly lower". Dudley, expectations "less well anchored to the downside".
- Direction: Below target and drifting down, though Lacker sees them as unchanged and Warsh discounts the TIPS signal.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "I believe we should provide additional monetary policy accommodation". Bernanke, "we are getting very close to the point... where we should make good our obligation to ensure that nominal quantities... are at levels consistent with a healthy recovery". Opposing: Lacker, "I don't favor moving now"; Plosser, against a "large-scale LSAP program".
- Center of gravity: Hold now with a strong majority tilt toward further easing in November.
- Direction confirmation: Lean is dovish because the debate is how much more to buy and when, with only a minority against.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll call has Hoenig "No"; Hoenig, "I object to alternative B". Lacker, "my deeper objection... we really don't care about unemployment, and we really don't care about inflation". Yellen versus Plosser and Lacker on whether the problem is demand or structure.
- Center of gravity: Distinct camps on strategy and part of the economics, one formal dissent, courteous tone, 9-1 vote.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is above the typical one-dissent meeting because it reaches the assessment of slack, deflation risk and the mandate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, farmland loans "80 percent of the value" and banks reaching for yield; Warsh, "a lot of dry tinder... money market mutual fund industry"; Dudley on Europe, municipals and Basel.
- Center of gravity: Several members raise financial risks as context; not the centre of the discussion.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is moderate and the topic is secondary to the monetary-policy framework.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Draft B, "is prepared to provide additional accommodation if needed", with "extended period" retained (Hoenig objects to this). Dudley wants "about 60 percent probability" of a November move; Bullard fears markets will price "0.9 or 0.95".
- Center of gravity: Strong easing bias signalling likely asset purchases, softened by "if".
- Direction confirmation: The signal is easing, so far below 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bullard, "We are obviously in uncharted territory". Kocherlakota, "these issues leave me uncertain". Bernanke, "no safe option... genuine uncertainty about the most recent economic data".
- Center of gravity: Elevated and widely voiced uncertainty about structure, transmission and the data, with some members confident.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is well above normal because many members stress it and standard models are questioned.
