# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1994-11-15 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, early (1994) format. It contains the members' staff Q&A (questions only), a full economic go-around, a policy go-around with a 75 bp proposal from the Chairman, a long discussion of foreign exchange intervention and swap lines, and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed. Intervention/swap-line material is mostly procedural/institutional and is used only where it speaks to conditions (dollar, market credibility) or to disagreement.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1994 tightening cycle: the funds rate has already been raised 175 bp since February; the Greenbook assumes a further 150 bp. Backdrop is a strong expansion with inflation fears. I am scoring what the text says, not recalled history.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- McDonough: growth stronger than expected, NAIRU about 6 percent and unemployment below it, October hourly earnings +0.7 percent, inflation expectations high, banks' lending standards slipping and spreads low. Calls for a decisive move.
- Minehan: region soft but nation overshot, "behind the curve", risks on the up side, move sharply.
- Forrestal, McTeer, Parry, Hoenig, Boehne, Broaddus, Stern, Melzer, Moskow: strong momentum, tightening labor markets, price and wage pressures building, upside inflation risk, credibility concerns. Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Phillips and Jordan favour a multiyear inflation target.
- Lindsey, Kelley, LaWare, Phillips: strong economy, inflation teakettle warming; LaWare wants more progress on inflation; Phillips: markets functioning well, equity correction orderly, CPI above 3 percent.
- Jordan: skeptical that growth means inflation; concerned about the "another 50 each meeting" pattern; data-quality concerns.
- Yellen, Blinder: economy at or past potential but lags mean serious hard-landing risk from the Greenbook's extra 150 bp; both prefer 50 over 75, Yellen can live with 75, Blinder does not dissent.
- Greenspan: economy has momentum (inventories), "behind the curve", recommends 75 bp with a symmetric directive and no commitment on December.

**4. Core dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. CPI "well above a 3 percent rate" (Phillips), "inflation would be on an upward trajectory" (Parry), "upturn in inflation seems inevitable" (Hoenig), wage and price pressures building in many Districts. But Yellen sees limited upside risk, Jordan and Stern see no widespread price pressures, and Blinder calls 3 percent "an awfully good position". The reports are of early warning signs and a rising trend rather than a surge. Score about 64 (clearly above, rising, not yet broad).
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above midpoint. "Past the point of full noninflationary utilization" (Moskow), "growth above potential for at least the next three quarters" (Hoenig), "carry the economy past potential output" (Yellen), "pretty near and maybe right at or even a little beyond capacity" (Blinder). Modestly above potential: 61.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above midpoint on participants' language: unemployment "below the NAIRU" (McDonough), "markets ... extremely tight" (Parry), "everybody who wants to work is working" (Stern), shortages (Moskow, Jordan, Hoenig), 5.8 percent national unemployment (Melzer). The 5.8 percent level alone would point to the 31-45 band in the rubric table, but participants uniformly place it below the then-estimated NAIRU of about 6 percent, so I take the participants' view: 60.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong: "extremely strong expansion" (Kelley), "interest-sensitive areas ... very little response" (McDonough), 4 to 4.5 percent growth, investment strong. Some regional softness (housing slowing, New England). 66.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched on only in passing (Jordan's question about 6 percent manufacturing productivity; McDonough: help from productivity lower after payroll revision; Phillips: "productivity increases have resumed"). Mixed and brief: 52, Low.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Easing lending terms and aggressive bank lending (McDonough, Boehne, Yellen) and markets "functioning quite well" (Phillips) point loose; cost of capital higher, 175 bp of tightening and a big run-up in long rates point tighter, but "not inhibiting investment" (Phillips). Net slightly below neutral: 47.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Bond market discussed only as yields and inflation expectations (Fisher Q&A, McDonough yield spread, Blinder on long rates, Greenspan's tail risk of a bond run). No dysfunction reported; Phillips: markets more depth, less volatile. Normal functioning: 42, Low.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only direct level statement: McDonough, "The spreads are sufficiently low now" on bank lending, with covenants slipping; Boehne: lending terms easing. Level below normal: 36.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan: stock market "still a little rich although off its price/earnings ratios"; Phillips: "one of the most orderly corrections"; worry about a stock reaction (Greenspan, Yellen, Blinder). Valuation view roughly fair to slightly rich with some downside concern: 47, Low.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are healthy but risk-taking is a concern: aggressive lending, "lending standards ... slipping excessively" (McDonough), bad loans made in good times (Boehne), smooth shift to bank financing (Phillips). Low stress side, with a minor concern: 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has been weak: Fisher's report (via Chairman's questions), "recent decline in the dollar" (Yellen), "weakness of the dollar" (McTeer), Greenspan's risk of "a run on the dollar", intervention in support of the dollar. Depreciation: 27.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS: input and materials price pressure (Melzer, Moskow steel 6-8 percent, Forrestal, imported goods prices +6.9 percent rate) but retailers resist (Jordan "impossible"): 60. SERVICES: not discussed: N/A. HOUSING: only Parry's "expect a pickup in housing cost inflation" against weak California prices: 52. EXPECTATIONS: "inflationary expectations are high" (McDonough), expected 3.5 percent (Broaddus), credibility lost (Broaddus, Melzer); Jordan notes Michigan survey expectations falling: 68.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more inflation / more above potential / tighter labor / stronger spending / tighter conditions / more credit stress / more banking stress / stronger dollar. Evidence: inflation rising (above), economy past potential (above), labor tight (above), spending strong (above), conditions mixed-to-easy lending (slightly below), spreads low (below), banks sound (below), dollar weak (below). All consistent.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: nearly all members back a 75 bp funds rate increase (largest move of the cycle), many say "behind the curve" and want to be ahead of expectations. Against that, many resist the Greenbook's further 150 bp and want December left open; the directive is symmetric. Strong but not very strong hawkish: 72.
- Dissent: unanimous vote on the policy action (read-out all Yes). Reservations: Blinder (long, pointed case for 50, declines to dissent), Boehne (50), Yellen (prefers 50, can live with 75), Stern, Jordan (trap/coasting remarks), Lindsey and Parry disagree on intervention; Broaddus voted No on the swap renewal in the roll call (a non-policy vote, noted in the transcript's raw roll but the read-out lists Yes; I treat the intervention debate as tactical disagreement). Moderate differences, no formal policy dissent: 35.
- Financial stability/conditions attention: not a focus per se, but market reaction, credibility, the dollar, bond and equity risk, lending standards and a lengthy FX intervention discussion take a real share of time. Limited-to-moderate: 33.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive, no commitment; Greenspan puts December between no change and 50; Lindsey hopes for 0; Blinder says he won't go further; Parry wanted asymmetry. Slight hawkish tilt only from the assumption that more may come: 52.
- Uncertainty: standard-to-somewhat elevated: lags of policy (Yellen, Blinder), forecast skepticism, data quality (Jordan), market psychology "nobody really knows" (Blinder), Phillips's list of open uncertainties. Not framed as unusual: 36.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 61 (>50, above-potential language): OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 47 (<50; no members describe conditions as tight, easy lending cited): OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 36 (<50, level stated "sufficiently low"): OK. BANKING_STRESS 32 (<50): OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 27 (<50, depreciation): OK. TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction, within 36-50): OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 61 vs LABOR 60 vs CONSUMPTION 66: within 15. FC 47 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 36: moderate gap; FC reflects higher rates and cost of capital while spreads/lending are loose, so no contradiction (not tight FC with compressed spreads). FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, near 40-50. INFLATION_PRESSURE 64 vs sub-components (goods 60, housing 52, expectations 68): consistent.

**9. N/A.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-price discussion by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 64 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Phillips, "We are well above a 3 percent rate at current levels"; Hoenig, "an upturn in inflation seems inevitable." Center of gravity: rising, clearly above target, early rather than broad. Above neutral because members report building wage and price pressure and an upward inflation trend.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 61 +-6 High.** Evidence: Moskow, "past the point of full noninflationary utilization of productive resources"; Hoenig, "real GDP growth to remain above potential for at least the next three quarters." Above neutral: economy past potential.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 60 +-8 High.** Evidence: McDonough, "the rate of unemployment in our view is below the NAIRU"; Stern, "everybody who wants to work is working." Above neutral, tempered by 5.8 percent unemployment level.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 66 +-6 High.** Evidence: Kelley, "an extremely strong expansion"; McDonough, "interest-sensitive areas ... show very little response to higher interest rates." Above trend, strong momentum.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 52 +-10 Low.** Evidence: Phillips, "Productivity increases have resumed"; McDonough, "the help that we thought we would get from productivity is much lower." Roughly at trend; mixed and brief.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 47 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Phillips, cost of capital "has increased ... But it does not appear to be inhibiting investment"; Yellen, "easier lending terms have offset somewhat the impact of higher interest rates." Slightly below neutral: easy lending offsets higher rates.
- **TREASURY_STRESS 42 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Phillips, "The markets are not as volatile as they were earlier in the year. There is more depth." Normal functioning; yields discussed only as expectations.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 36 +-8 Low.** Evidence: McDonough, "The spreads are sufficiently low now that it is not clear ... they are getting an adequate return"; Boehne, "lending terms are easing." Below normal level.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 47 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Greenspan, "the stock market, in my judgment, is still a little rich"; Phillips, "one of the most orderly corrections." Roughly fair, some concern about reaction.
- **BANKING_STRESS 32 +-8 Low.** Evidence: McDonough, lending standards "slipping excessively"; Phillips, "smooth transition from credit crunch to the direct issue market and to bank financing." Banks sound; risk-taking noted, so low stress.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 27 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "The recent decline in the dollar exacerbates this concern"; McTeer, "the weakness of the dollar in the foreign exchange markets." Depreciation: below neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 60, INFLATION_SERVICES N/A, INFLATION_HOUSING 52, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 68.** Evidence: Moskow, steel increases "6 to 8 percent range"; Parry, contacts "expect a pickup in housing cost inflation"; McDonough, "inflationary expectations are high and ... our tightening to date has not reduced those expectations."
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 72 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "interest rates need to be raised considerably above current levels ... support ... 75 basis point increase"; Hoenig, "the sooner we move on this, the less we will have to move later." Strong hawkish on the action; tempered by wide resistance to a further 150 bp and the symmetric directive.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 35 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Blinder, "My personal preference is strongly for 50 ... I thought hard about whether I should dissent ... I finally decided that I won't"; Yellen, "I would favor 50 today ... but I can live with 75." No policy dissent; several reservations; sharp but tactical FX intervention exchanges.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 33 +-10 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, risk of "a run on the dollar, a run on the bond market, and a significant decline in stock prices"; McDonough on slipping lending standards. Market reaction and the dollar matter to the decision, but the discussion is mainly about the real economy and inflation.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 52 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, December "somewhere between no change and 50 basis points"; Lindsey, "the expectation that December will be 0." Symmetric directive, no commitment; neutral to slight tightening tilt.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 36 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Blinder, "nobody really knows about market psychology"; Yellen on lags and hard-landing risk. Somewhat elevated but not framed as unusual.
