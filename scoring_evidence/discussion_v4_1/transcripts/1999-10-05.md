# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 scoring, meeting 1999-10-05 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening procedural items, members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic go-around, the policy go-around (the Chairman opens with a long analytical statement and recommends "B" asymmetric), a drafting exchange on the press statement, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. The text is clean; one passage in Poole's UPS remarks (lines ~755-758) has dropped numbers, which affects no score.

**Era context.** Autumn 1999: very strong expansion, two 25bp tightenings already done (June, August), unemployment about 4.2%, core CPI about 1.9% y/y, rapid productivity gains, Y2K liquidity worries, stock market down about 10% since August, long rates up. I take the levels from what members say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish or tightening-leaning: Broaddus (wants to "consider seriously a further preemptive action"), Jordan (skeptical that policy is calibrated), Hoenig (wants 25bp now), Meyer (risks tilted to rising inflation), Gramlich (more comfortable with a higher funds rate; may have to act in November), Kelley, Minehan, Guynn (each could have tightened today), Moskow (risks tilted to the upside), Ferguson (risks slightly skewed to the upside), Parry (the time for raising rates is nearer).
- Dovish or patient: McDonough ("declare victory", the inflation concern is "Don Quixote's windmills"), McTeer (no inflation in the pipeline, wants to suspend symmetry announcements), Stern (relatively optimistic on productivity), Boehne (risks balanced, wait-and-see).
- Chairman Greenspan: the models are obsolete because they assume flat trend productivity, and the second derivative of productivity may be positive. He is unsure whether the economy is overheated and wants to hold ("B") but with an asymmetric tightening bias. He says November is still open. He does not think the Fed is behind the curve and sees the next big risk as a stock-market crash.
- Outcome: unanimous vote for "B" (no change) with an asymmetric directive, announced publicly.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current measured inflation as benign (core CPI 1.9%, "remarkably benign" compensation), but report firming input costs, health costs, pipeline pressures and a Greenbook path of rising inflation. Slightly above the midpoint, because pressures are building but not yet present. About 54.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Broaddus: "aggregate demand is continuing to outstrip the growth of potential GDP". Meyer: "above-trend growth", "high utilization rates". Must be above 50, so about 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight, with local unemployment of 2-3% and "unemployable being employed". National UE of 4.2% is under the 4.5% anchor. 1999 anchor 65-75, so about 74.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Well above trend. Words used: "binge", "extraordinary strength", "truly robust", investment "soared", "frothy". About 72.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Heavily discussed and seen as accelerating (3.2% over four quarters against an assumed 1% trend; "second derivative positive"). Significantly above trend, about 74.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Meyer says "still favorable financial conditions even with a tightening", but stocks are down about 10%, corporate yields are up "significantly more than Treasuries", yield and credit spreads have picked up, and Boehne notes "more edginess". Meyer's phrase counts as "supportive" under the bright line, so I stay below 50 with a wide band. About 47.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No member describes dysfunction. Peter Fisher's Y2K liquidity facilities are discussed only in staff Q&A fragments with the staff answers removed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only (Minehan: "widening credit spreads"; McTeer: "noticeable pickup in yield spreads"). No level stated. Per the v4.1 rule, 52-55 with Low confidence. 53.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Parry sees the market at "very high levels" and vulnerable to a "much larger correction". Minehan cites "unrealistic asset prices". Greenspan refers to "a bubble that can't persist". Elevated or bubble-type concern, partly offset by the "productivity explains values" argument. About 28.
- BANKING_STRESS: Discussed lightly. McDonough: "few delinquencies", though standards are tightening. Stern expects some agricultural credit deterioration and bankers "chasing the marginal customer". Sound with minor concerns, so below 50: 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Members describe a "modest drop in the dollar", "dollar ... appears to be weakening", "has begun to reverse". Gramlich expects a large decline. McTeer expects it to stay strong. Net depreciation, so below 50: 38.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: commodity prices are firming, import prices rebounding, oil up; the core PPI for finished goods is down (McTeer). 55.
- Services: Jordan's tuition and marina anecdotes, health costs. 55.
- Housing: rising home prices and rents (Boston, Manhattan, NJ). 60.
- Expectations: Poole says "expectations remain very, very solid". Anchored, 48.

**Tier 3 check.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Decision is hold with an explicit tightening bias. Roughly half the table said they could have tightened now, and Hoenig said he wanted to. A few are firmly patient (McDonough, McTeer, Stern, Boehne). This is "hold steady with tightening bias, approaching time to act," so 60.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero dissents and a unanimous vote. But real differences: Hoenig would hike now, McDonough says declare victory, and there was a 5-4 split among current members on announcing asymmetry. Sharp-ish drafting exchange on the labor-market wording (Hoenig, Guynn, Meyer, McTeer). Broad agreement with notable emphasis differences, 30.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Asset prices and a possible stock-market correction (Parry, Minehan, Greenspan), debt levels and margin credit (Hoenig, Minehan), credit-crunch worry (Jordan), Y2K liquidity (questions, Jordan). Several participants touch on it, but the focus is inflation and productivity. 35.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Asymmetric directive, published, plus the statement "especially alert ... to the potential for cost increases significantly in excess of productivity". Greenspan says the Fed should not signal it is through for the year. Mild-to-moderate tightening signal, 68.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Greenspan: "we really don't know", models "increasingly obsolete", "very unusual period". Broaddus: "very, very uncertain" on productivity. Ferguson, Meyer (a maximin strategy) and Boehne stress it as well. The uncertainty is genuine and central to the policy choice, though most have a view on the risk direction. 60.

**Polarity checks.** Higher OUTPUT_GAP = more above potential; evidence says demand outstrips potential, so above 50 (matches). Higher LABOR = tighter; very tight (matches). Higher DOLLAR = stronger; evidence is weakening so below 50 (matches). EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more stress/undervaluation concern, so overvaluation concern is below 50 (matches). BANKING_STRESS: sound means below 50 (matches). CREDIT_SPREADS: direction only, so within 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence (matches).

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 (>50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 47 (<50, supported by "favorable financial conditions"). CREDIT_SPREADS 53 (direction-only rule). BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 38 (<50, depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs LABOR 74 (12 apart, fine), vs CONSUMPTION 72 (10, fine). FC 47 vs spreads 53 co-move loosely; both near neutral, with the direction of change tightening but levels not stated. Parent inflation 54 vs sub-components (55, 55, 60, 48): consistent. Treasury stress N/A.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant discusses Treasury market functioning.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer: "The recent data for compensation and core CPI have been remarkably benign"; Minehan: "all the temporary factors that acted to restrain price increases ... are now slipping away". Summary: current inflation is benign, but pressures are building. Direction: slightly above neutral because members see rising pipeline and cost pressures though core CPI is under 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 62 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus: "growth of aggregate demand is continuing to outstrip the growth of potential GDP"; Meyer: "above-trend growth". Summary: demand is running above potential. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 74 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Minehan: "Labor markets in the region remain extremely tight"; McDonough: "4.2 percent unemployment rate, we have the unemployable being employed". Summary: very tight nationwide. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 72 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus: "household spending 'binge'"; Gramlich: "investment soared in the third quarter". Summary: spending well above trend. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 74 ±8 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan: "nonfarm productivity rising in the last four quarters to a rate of 3.2 percent"; Guynn: "large productivity gains are very likely still ahead of us". Summary: productivity growth is far above the historical trend and seen as accelerating. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 47 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Meyer: "still favorable financial conditions even with a tightening"; McTeer: "stock market indexes have fallen by about 10 percent ... noticeable pickup in yield spreads". Summary: conditions are still supportive but tightening at the margin. Direction: slightly below neutral (bright-line rule on "favorable").

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 ±6 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan: "widening credit spreads"; Greenspan: corporate bond yields "up significantly more than Treasuries". Summary: spreads are widening, but no level is given. Direction: slightly above neutral by the direction-only rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 28 ±10 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry: "a much larger stock market correction from today's very high levels"; Greenspan: "the existence of a bubble that can't persist". Summary: valuations are seen as elevated and vulnerable. Direction: below neutral (overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: McDonough: "Banks report steady loan demand, increasingly tight credit standards, and few delinquencies"; Stern: "I expect to see some deterioration in credit quality at the banks, which is partly related to agriculture". Summary: sound system with minor concerns. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley: "the dollar has ceased to strengthen and has begun to reverse"; Parry: "the modest drop in the dollar". Summary: modest depreciation, with larger declines expected by some. Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55; INFLATION_SERVICES: 55; INFLATION_HOUSING: 60; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Evidence: Meyer: "a rebound in nonoil import prices ... upward trend in nonoil commodity prices"; Jordan on tuition: "increases ... quite sharp"; Boston and Manhattan real estate; Poole: "expectations remain very, very solid".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Hoenig: "we should move the funds rate up ¼ point now"; Greenspan: "I would favor asymmetry". Summary: hold, with a tightening bias and many members ready to act. Direction: above neutral (lean hawkish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Roll call, ten yes votes; Hoenig prefers a symmetric directive and an immediate hike; McDonough: "declare victory". Summary: unanimous vote, but different views on tightening now and on how to disclose. Direction: low-to-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Jordan: "we will have a credit crunch"; Hoenig: "Margin credit is rising to record levels". Summary: several members raise asset prices and debt, but it is not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan: "I would prefer not to give a message that we are necessarily through for the year"; statement: "especially alert ... to the potential for cost increases". Summary: published tightening bias. Direction: hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan: "I come to the basic conclusion that we really don't know at this stage"; Broaddus: "we're still just very, very uncertain about what's happening to the second derivative of productivity growth". Summary: broad, genuine uncertainty about productivity and the models.
