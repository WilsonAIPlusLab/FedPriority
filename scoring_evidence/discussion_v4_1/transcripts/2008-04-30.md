# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2008-04-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two sessions (afternoon of day 1, morning of day 2). Staff turns are stripped, so some member questions and follow-ups have no visible answers. Content in order: (a) liquidity-facility package (TAF, swap lines, TSLF collateral expansion to AAA ABS) with a straw vote; (b) questions to staff; (c) the economic go-around; (d) the policy go-around on alternatives B (cut 25bp to 2%, soft-pause language) and C (hold); (e) a long, separate discussion of interest on reserves (operational framework, not conditions; ignored for scoring except as procedure); (f) a vote read-out. The read-out shows 8 Yes and 2 No (Fisher, Plosser) on a 25bp cut to about 2 percent.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2008, after months of financial turmoil, a prior string of large rate cuts, and the Bear Stearns episode. I score only what participants say. Participants describe a weak economy, tight credit, and a financial system that has improved but is still fragile. Headline inflation is high on oil and commodities.

**3. Speakers.** Hawkish/hold camp: Plosser (inflation credibility, M2/MZM, negative real rate), Fisher (CEO anecdotes, inflation feedback loop), Hoenig, Lacker, Bullard, Evans (all prefer a hold, though Evans, Hoenig, Lacker and Bullard do not vote). Cut-with-pause camp: Kohn, Yellen, Stern, Rosengren, Kroszner, Pianalto, Lockhart, Warsh (reluctantly), Mishkin ("on the fence"), Geithner, Bernanke. Rosengren is the most dovish and says it is "not at all clear" the Committee should pause after this meeting.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as clearly above the midpoint. Headline CPI is 4 percent and PCE 3.4 percent (Plosser); Hoenig says concern is "the highest level I've been involved with in the last decade"; Fisher cites broad CEO price-increase anecdotes. Core is described as moderate or "close to acceptable" (Stern) and about 2 percent (Rosengren), and Kohn, Geithner, Rosengren and Mishkin stress that it is mostly relative-price and commodity driven. Net: clearly above target but with core contained. 66.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. "We still appear to be in a recession" (Lacker), "a noticeable output gap opening this year" (Evans), "unemployment remains well above the NAIRU" through 2010 (Rosengren), "increasing resource slack" (Evans). A mild recession is the modal view, and Bernanke calls himself "reasonably confident that we are in a recession." About 33. The bright line (below potential means under 50) is satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Payrolls are falling (average of 77,000 a month in Q1 per Lacker; about 100,000 per Rosengren), unemployment is up 0.3pt and "still relatively low" (Lacker), heading to just over 5.5 percent (Yellen). Wages are "moderate" and "nothing is going on there" (Yellen). The level of unemployment is only modestly elevated but the direction is clearly loosening and the language is of a slack labor market. 42.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below the midpoint. "Consumer spending... slowed to a crawl" (Lacker), "retail sales, sentiment, consumer spending... generally soft" (Bernanke summary), tenants' and credit-card data weak (Warsh, Kroszner), capex plans deferred (Plosser, Yellen). Some offsets (exports, energy, rebates pending). 33.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not discussed as a perceived-growth topic (a CEO quote and Evans's 2003 aside are passing). N/A.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tight. "Credit conditions have turned quite restrictive" (Yellen), "very tight credit conditions in many sectors" and "60 or 70" mph headwinds (Kohn), securitization shut, SLOOS tightening, LIBOR-OIS wide. Offsets: markets "improved substantially" (Lockhart, Geithner), "financial conditions have improved in the past month" (Bernanke). Hoenig dissents on how much credit is restricted. Above 50, about 67. Bright line (tight means over 50) satisfied. A few members (Evans, Lacker) call policy "accommodative", but that is policy, not conditions; Kohn and Yellen explicitly say the real funds rate misleads about conditions.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only touched. Bernanke says Treasuries trade "at lower spreads and with less volatility" since the TSLF; Rosengren says Treasuries and repo "are trading in more-normal ranges." The TSLF discussion implies earlier stress now eased. No dysfunction described. Mid-normal, about 50, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level governs. Kohn: spreads "retraced only a small portion of the run-up... still very, very high by historical standards"; Kroszner: "jumbo spreads are still extremely wide," CDS "very high"; Bernanke's auto-ABS example shows AAA spreads at 195bp versus 10bp. Geithner and Rosengren say spreads have come in. Lacker says spreads are within past-recession bounds, a minority view. Level is high, above 50, about 72. Bright line satisfied (stated high level means over 50; narrowing alone does not change the side).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price moves ("stock market has moved up", equity inflow into financials), no valuation commentary. Default band, 50, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Above 50. "Banks that are contending with capital-depleting losses and illiquid assets" (Yellen), National City recapitalization (Pianalto), smaller banks and construction exposures "under significant duress" (Rosengren), only about one third of projected losses recognized (Bernanke), "banking system broken" (Kohn). Offsets: large capital raises ($115 billion), Bullard's district banks "in good shape". About 67. Bright line satisfied (no "sound/healthy" characterization of the system overall).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Mixed. The dollar has risen during the intermeeting period ("The dollar appreciated," Bernanke; "dollar stronger on net," Geithner) but the dominant theme is the longer-term depreciation and the risk of a sharper fall (Fisher, Lockhart, Warsh, Bernanke "anxiety about the dollar"). Under the direction rule, the discussion is mostly of weakness with a recent rally; score below 50, about 40, Moderate confidence with a wide band.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Hoenig: "significant inflation pressure from goods prices, especially imported goods prices"; widespread commodity pass-through anecdotes (Fisher, Pianalto). 70.
- SERVICES: Only Hoenig's remark that the recent moderation "is coming mainly from some softness in service prices", expected not to last. Little else. 46.
- HOUSING: Kohn: vacant units "will continue to put downward pressure on rent increases." 38.
- EXPECTATIONS: Michigan one-year 4.8 percent, "inflation psychology" (Hoenig), "just barely" credible (Plosser), index "at the top of its range" (Fisher); against that, long-run expectations "reasonably well anchored" (Mishkin, Rosengren), TIPS breakevens down. Rising near term, anchored long term. 64.
- Parent check: parent 66 versus goods 70, services 46, housing 38, expectations 64. The parent is weighted toward headline and goods/commodity concern and expectations; the sub-components with low values (services, housing) were each raised by one speaker only, so the parent is acceptable.

**7. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: A 25bp cut, a small step, with explicit signals of a near-end to easing. Six participants prefer a hold (Plosser, Fisher voting; Evans, Hoenig, Lacker, Bullard non-voting), with strong inflation-credibility language. Eight or so support the cut, several reluctantly (Warsh "without the conviction", Lockhart, Stern, Mishkin on the fence), and Rosengren and Yellen argue growth risks warrant more. This is the "hold-ish with mild easing" zone; weighting the small cut against the large hold camp and the heavy inflation rhetoric gives about 44.
- Dissent: Two formal dissents (Fisher, Plosser) against a 25bp cut, plus four other participants who argued for hold in the go-around. Disagreement extends to assessment: how to measure the stance (real funds rate versus financial conditions), relative prices versus inflation, credibility. Geithner calls the gap "a surprise." Camps are distinct, though the tone is collegial and everyone agrees that easing is near its end. About 52.
- Financial stability emphasis: Very high. The whole first segment is liquidity facilities and TAF/TSLF/swap lines; Bernanke gives the "third inning" losses analysis; banks, LIBOR-OIS, securitization, Bear Stearns, capital raising, and fragility recur from nearly every speaker. 75.
- Forward guidance: Alternative B drops downside-risk language and "timely manner", adds "continue to monitor", described as a "hawkish soft pause" (Geithner) and "a stronger indication of a pause" (Bernanke). Not a tightening signal, but a clear shift from easing to hold, data dependent. Rosengren and Mishkin stress flexibility to ease more. About 56.
- Uncertainty: Repeated, strong language. "Unusually high degree of uncertainty" (Yellen), "Uncertainty is huge. We are sailing in a fog in uncharted waters" (Kohn), "very high uncertainty" (Mishkin), "unusually high" (Geithner), "very high" (Lockhart citing the Greenbook), Warsh "significant uncertainty". Counterweights: some members see tail risks falling and are fairly confident in their view. About 66.

**8. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 (<50, slack language): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 67 (tight language): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 72 (stated high level): ok. BANKING_STRESS 67 (stress language): ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 (depreciation theme, recent rally noted; direction ambiguous, flagged with wide band): ok. TREASURY_STRESS 50 (no dysfunction described; normal): ok.

**9. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says above-target: 66 matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter; evidence says loosening: 42 matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter; evidence says tight: 67 matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress; evidence says spreads still very high: 72 matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress; evidence says capital-depleted but recapitalizing: 67 matches.

**10. Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 33 versus LABOR 42: diverge by 9 (<15). Labor is higher because unemployment is only at about 5 percent and is called "relatively low", while the output gap is expected to widen.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 33 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 33: aligned.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 67 versus CREDIT_SPREADS 72: co-move, consistent (both high).
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 67 versus TREASURY_STRESS 50: Treasury market is described as normalizing; per the rubric, in normal times Treasury stress sits near 40-50 regardless. Consistent.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE versus sub-components: discussed above.

**11. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed as a growth topic. All other dimensions have at least some participant discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 66 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "year-over-year CPI inflation was 4 percent in March, and year-over-year PCE inflation was 3.4 percent"; Hoenig: "Concern about inflation has escalated to the highest level I've been involved with in the last decade."
- Summary: Headline inflation is clearly above target and broad commodity cost pass-through is widely reported, but core is moderate and several members call the problem relative prices.
- Direction: Above neutral because headline is well above the objective and risks are "somewhat more to the upside" (Bernanke).

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "we still appear to be in a recession"; Rosengren: "the unemployment rate remains well above the NAIRU even at the end of 2010."
- Summary: A mild recession with a gap opening this year and persisting.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe slack and recession.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "Payroll employment has continued to decline, falling an average of 77,000 per month in the first quarter. Unemployment is rising, although it is still relatively low"; Yellen: unemployment "increases to just over 5 1/2 percent by the end of this year."
- Summary: Unemployment near 5 percent and rising with falling payrolls: loosening, somewhat below balanced.
- Direction: Below neutral because jobs are being lost and unemployment is rising toward 5.5 percent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "Consumer spending, like business spending, appears to have slowed to a crawl"; Warsh: consumer spending in March "was as bad as anyone... could have envisioned."
- Summary: Consumer and business spending are weak, with rebates and exports as partial offsets.
- Direction: Below neutral because of widespread weakness and deferred capex.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed as a perception of productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 67 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "credit conditions have turned quite restrictive"; Kohn: "We have very tight credit conditions in many sectors of the market."
- Summary: Conditions have improved from the March trough but remain tight and fragile, with funding markets and securitization impaired.
- Direction: Above neutral (tighter) because credit availability and terms are restrictive; improvement is partial.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Treasuries are trading at lower spreads and with less volatility since we began these activities"; Rosengren: "Treasury securities and repurchase agreements are trading in more-normal ranges."
- Summary: Brief mentions describe normalization, with no dysfunction.
- Direction: At neutral (normal functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 72 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "they are still very, very high by historical standards"; Kroszner: "The jumbo spreads are still extremely wide."
- Summary: Spreads have narrowed from peaks but remain at stressed levels; a minority (Lacker) views them as in line with past recessions.
- Direction: Above neutral because the stated level is high, regardless of the recent narrowing.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "The stock market has moved up"; Geithner: "additional wave of inflow of equity into the financial system."
- Summary: Price moves noted with no valuation view.
- Direction: At neutral (default for price-move-only commentary).

**BANKING_STRESS: 67 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "banks that are contending with capital-depleting losses and illiquid assets"; Rosengren: smaller institutions with construction exposure "are now experiencing significant duress."
- Summary: Banks are recapitalizing but losses are only a third recognized, with commercial real estate the next problem.
- Direction: Above neutral because of impaired capital and rising delinquencies, only partly offset by capital raising.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "The dollar appreciated during the intermeeting period, but longer-term depreciation... remain another source of pressure on inflation"; Lockhart: "concern about the value of the dollar, notwithstanding the recent rally."
- Summary: Recent bounce, but the discussion is dominated by prior depreciation and fear of a further fall.
- Direction: Below neutral because the main thrust is weakness and downside dollar risk; the recent appreciation keeps it near 40 rather than lower.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 70.** Hoenig: "significant inflation pressure from goods prices, especially imported goods prices"; many commodity pass-through anecdotes (Fisher, Pianalto).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 46.** Hoenig: recent moderation "coming mainly from some softness in service prices", expected not to continue.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 38.** Kohn: "vacant housing units will continue to put downward pressure on rent increases."
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 64.** Plosser: Michigan one-year expectations "4.8 percent"; Mishkin: "long-run inflation expectations are still reasonably well anchored around 2 percent."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 44 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "make the case for why we should stand pat today"; Pianalto: "a modest reduction... is prudent. But I also strongly support the language that indicates we're very close to, if not at, a pausing point."
- Summary: A 25bp cut with a soft-pause signal, two voting dissents and four more members for a hold, against heavy inflation-credibility rhetoric.
- Direction: Slightly dovish (below 50) because the action is easing and the majority backs it, though the forward lean is hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 52 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "I am in favor of a pause... that's how I plan to vote"; Geithner: "the extent of the gap in this Committee in how we think about the way to measure the stance of monetary policy."
- Summary: Two formal No votes, four more non-voters preferring a hold, and disagreement extending to how to assess the stance and relative-price shocks, though cordial and with agreement that easing is ending.
- Direction: Moderately high because of distinct camps and assessment-level disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 75 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "we are probably closer to the third inning" on credit losses; Kroszner: "Liquidity and solvency issues are ultimately related, but there is a real concern that many of the money market mutual funds... will just walk away."
- Summary: Liquidity facilities and the fragile financial system occupy a major part of the meeting alongside the economic assessment.
- Direction: High attention (prominent to dominant topic).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner: "a modest recalibration of policy with a hawkish soft pause"; Bernanke: "we removed any reference to downside risk to growth... 'continue to monitor'... watchful waiting."
- Summary: The statement shifts from easing mode to a data-dependent pause; no tightening promised, but the easing bias is removed.
- Direction: Slightly hawkish of neutral because the signal is that easing is ending and inflation risks are being highlighted.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Uncertainty is huge. We are sailing in a fog in uncharted waters"; Yellen: "unusually high degree of uncertainty that we are facing."
- Summary: Many participants stress unusually high uncertainty over growth, credit, and commodity prices, with some tail-risk reduction.
- Direction: High (61-75 band) because many participants emphasize it and standard stance measures are questioned.
