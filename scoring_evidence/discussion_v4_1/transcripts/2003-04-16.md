# v4.1 Scoring: Member Discussion (transcript), meeting 2003-04-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, with staff turns removed. It holds the Chair's procedural remarks, a few members' questions during the staff Q&A (Ferguson and Minehan on SARS, Parry on the second-half forecast), a "somewhat truncated roundtable" (Greenspan's own words), and a short closing exchange on whether to act now. There is no formal policy go-around, no directive and no vote. Roughly 5,500 words, so the Low-confidence rule for very short discussions does not apply. The roundtable is mostly District anecdotes, with only a few macro-level views.

**2. Era context.** The text puts the meeting just after the start of the Iraq war and the oil-price decline. Members also mention the SARS outbreak, weak growth ("2 percent growth rut" in H1), low and falling inflation, and Bernanke's call to keep "potential further easing on the table". I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Greenspan: markets have behaved well because tail risks shrank, not because of good news; no positive forces yet; no policy move before May 6.
- Regional presidents Minehan, Santomero, Pianalto, Broaddus, Guynn, Moskow, Poole, Stern, Hoenig, McTeer and Parry: mostly "flat", "sluggish", "muddling along", with weak capex and soft but stabilizing labor.
- Governors Kohn, Ferguson, Bernanke, Bies, Olson and Gramlich: sluggish growth, low resource utilization, falling inflation, SARS and fiscal uncertainty; Gramlich says sit tight.
- McDonough: the Second District's mood depends on pending Wall Street settlements.

**4. Directional statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Bernanke gives core PCE at 1.4%, core CPI at 1.2% and market-based core PCE at 0.9%, "all falling", with "reasonable likelihood of further disinflation". Kohn cites "low inflation". Only these two address it, so it is a strong signal from few voices. About 27.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Kohn: "low level of resource utilization". Bernanke: "considerable economic slack going into 2005" even on the projected growth. Broad "sluggish" District reports support this. About 34. No "above potential" language, so the bright-line rule is met.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Broaddus: "labor markets are soft". Moskow: "Labor demand is still soft". Parry: layoffs and reluctance to hire. Guynn: subdued employment growth expected. Hoenig: layoff announcements slowed. Stabilizing, but at a slack level. About 35.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below the midpoint. Capex is weak and being postponed (Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Minehan); consumers are "fragile" (Parry) or moving forward at a "relatively sluggish pace" (Moskow). Housing is strong (Broaddus, Hoenig, Pianalto refinancing) and some nondurables are fine. Net is mildly sluggish. About 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned once. Guynn cites "productivity growth and reports about the ability to function with fewer workers" as a reason for subdued job growth. That implies decent productivity, but it is thin, so about 57 with Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Slightly accommodative. Greenspan says markets have been "behaving so well". Kohn cites the "rise in equity prices". Pianalto: banks "flush with deposits" and refinancing at "lower interest rates". Hoenig: banks have "good liquidity". Nobody describes tight credit, and loan demand is weak rather than supply-constrained. About 41, Moderate, with a wide band.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not raised by a participant. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No member gives a level or direction for spreads. Working-capital borrowing and loan demand are demand-side comments, not spread comments. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Price moves only (Kohn's "rise in equity prices", Greenspan on markets buoyed, Bies on earnings "at the expected levels"). There is no valuation commentary, so I use the default of 45-55. 48, Low.
- BANKING_STRESS: Bankers "flush with deposits" with margins squeezed (Pianalto), "good liquidity" (Hoenig), steady loan growth. This is healthy to mildly pressured on margins. The bright-line rule requires < 50. 36, Low confidence because the discussion is brief.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Minehan only: firms "affected to some extent... by the decline in the dollar". Depreciation, so < 50. 38, Low.
- Inflation sub-components: none discussed separately. Bernanke's "stabilizing inflation at a desirable level" is not an expectations reading. All N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence is disinflation, so below 50: matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and the evidence is liquidity and health, so below 50: matches. Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means a stronger dollar, and the evidence is a decline, so below 50: matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence is supportive, so below 50: matches. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH means more hawkish, and the evidence is hold with an easing option kept open, so just under 50: matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds, with no action before May 6. Gramlich: "sit tight for now" with a "heart-to-heart talk in May". Greenspan: "would not make a policy move" before May 6 but leaves open acting then. Moskow: "current stance of monetary policy is fine". Bernanke explicitly wants "potential further easing on the table", as insurance and to stop inflation falling to about 1%. McTeer reports "zero sentiment for easing" from his directors and agrees "at the margin". Overall this is hold with a mild easing bias. About 42.
- Disagreement: There is no vote and no dissent. All members present endorse waiting and dropping the weekly calls ("unanimity"). Bernanke and McTeer differ slightly on whether easing is useful, but this is only an emphasis difference. About 12.
- Financial stability: Greenspan spends a paragraph on market behavior and tail-risk compression. McDonough raises the Wall Street settlements. Bies raises earnings. Banks and liquidity get passing mentions. There is no stability-risk focus, so this is limited. About 22.
- Forward guidance: The signal is neutral to mildly easing-leaning. Wait for data, reassess on May 6, and keep easing available. About 42.
- Uncertainty: Minehan reports contacts with "a great deal of uncertainty" and "no one had a sense of confidence". Parry: "Uncertainty continues to hamper business spending". Olson: "still in a period of uncertainty" on fiscal policy. Greenspan: "a very unusual situation" in which everyone forecasts a Q3 acceleration but nobody has evidence of it. Ferguson: forecasts keep pushing out the rebound. Others (Pianalto, Guynn, Stern, Moskow) sound steadier. This is elevated but not extreme. About 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP is below potential and < 50. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is supportive and < 50. BANKING_STRESS is healthy and < 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is a decline and < 50. CREDIT_SPREADS and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A (not discussed).

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs LABOR 35: consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: consistent (gap of 4, within 15).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads are N/A, so no check applies.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, so no check applies.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-components: all sub-components are N/A; the parent rests on Bernanke's and Kohn's aggregate statements.

**9. N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS: not raised by any participant.
- INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: no component-level price commentary.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: no discussion of expectations.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "The twelve-month inflation rate as measured by the core PCE deflator is currently 1.4 percent... All of these inflation measures have been falling" and "a reasonable likelihood of further disinflation". Kohn: "Given the low level of resource utilization and low inflation".
- Summary: Low, falling inflation, with disinflation the stated concern, but raised by only two members.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe inflation of about 1% and falling, with a worry about going lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "low level of resource utilization... this 2 percent growth rut". Bernanke: "there will still be considerable economic slack going into 2005".
- Summary: Slack is seen as persisting even through the projected recovery.
- Direction: Below potential (bright-line satisfied).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "labor markets... are soft... not softening significantly further". Moskow: "Labor demand is still soft but does not appear to be eroding further". Parry: "reports of layoffs, and firms appear reluctant to add to their payrolls".
- Summary: A soft labor market that has stabilized but shows no tightening.
- Direction: Below neutral because of continued layoffs and reluctance to hire.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Parry: "Consumer spending can be described as fragile" and "Capital investment that can be postponed still is being postponed". Moskow: "Consumer spending still seems to be moving forward, though at a relatively sluggish pace".
- Summary: Weak capex and sluggish consumers, partly offset by strong housing and refinancing.
- Direction: Below trend because capex is weak and consumption is sluggish.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Guynn: "productivity growth and reports about the ability to function with fewer workers".
- Summary: One passing mention that implies healthy productivity and subdued hiring.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because productivity gains are cited as letting firms get by with fewer workers.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 41 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the financial markets have been behaving so well recently". Pianalto: bankers "flush with deposits"; commercial borrowers "taking advantage of the lower interest rates". Hoenig: banks "have good liquidity".
- Summary: Markets are buoyant, rates are low and credit is available, though demand for loans is weak.
- Direction: Below neutral (easier) because markets are rallying and liquidity is ample.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** The Treasury market was not discussed by participants.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** No member commented on spread levels or direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "the rise in equity prices". Bies: "corporate earnings are coming in at the expected levels".
- Summary: Price and earnings remarks only, with no valuation view, so the default band applies.
- Direction: At neutral because there is no valuation concern either way.

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Pianalto: "flush with deposits and... net interest margins are being squeezed". Hoenig: "some steady loan growth but loan demand is not strong at all. So they have good liquidity".
- Summary: Banks are liquid and healthy, with only margin pressure and weak loan demand.
- Direction: Below neutral because the descriptions are of liquid, sound banks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "Firms are affected to some extent... by the decline in the dollar".
- Summary: A single remark on dollar depreciation.
- Direction: Below neutral because the dollar is described as having declined.

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** None discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 42 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Gramlich: "it doesn't seem sensible to do anything today... we ought to have a heart-to-heart talk in May". Bernanke: "I hope that we will keep potential further easing on the table".
- Summary: A hold with easing kept open, against McTeer's view that easing "might send out the wrong signal".
- Direction: Slightly dovish of neutral because the only active policy proposal is to keep easing available.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I presume there is unanimity on that insight and recommendation." Hoenig: "Excellent presumption."
- Summary: Every participant who spoke endorsed waiting until May 6, with only a mild difference over whether easing would help.
- Direction: Near consensus: no dissent, with only emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 22 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan on markets: "the significant diminution of the tail ends of the probability distributions". McDonough on Wall Street settlements that "remove the fog of uncertainty".
- Summary: A few members treat financial markets as context for the outlook, not as a risk in themselves.
- Direction: Limited attention: a few mentions, with markets as backdrop.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "not taking any action now but leaving open the possibility that we may want to do something on May 6". Moskow: "the current stance of monetary policy is fine until May 6, and we can reconsider it at that point".
- Summary: Data-dependent waiting, with the open option leaning toward easing.
- Direction: Slightly dovish of neutral because the open option is easing and nobody signals tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "a great deal of uncertainty... No one had a sense of confidence in projections". Greenspan: "almost across the board everybody who is forecasting is projecting an acceleration... Nobody has any evidence that the acceleration has started. So it's a very unusual situation."
- Summary: Elevated uncertainty, though some members sound steady.
- Direction: Above the normal range because several members stress unusual uncertainty and the lack of evidence for the forecast pickup.
