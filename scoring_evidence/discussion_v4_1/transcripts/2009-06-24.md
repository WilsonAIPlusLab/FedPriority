# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2009-06-24 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. The input has two parts. The first is the afternoon session (day 1): members' questions to the staff on the balance sheet, exit tools, liquidity facilities and large-scale asset purchase (LSAP) strategy, followed by a free discussion of those three topics and votes on the facilities. The second is the next-morning economic go-round, the Chairman's summary, the policy round, and the vote read-out. Staff turns were removed, so many questions appear without their answers. I found no garbled passages.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2009, the Great Recession. The economy is bottoming out after a deep contraction, the policy rate is at the zero bound, and LSAPs ($1.25T agency MBS, $300B Treasuries, agency debt) are under way. The text is scored for what participants say, not for what I recall about this period.

**3. Speakers.**
- Bernanke chairs and summarizes.
- Dovish or accommodation-minded voices: Yellen, Rosengren, Kohn, Tarullo, Duke (concerned about the dual mandate), Dudley (cautious on exit signals), Lockhart, Pianalto.
- Hawkish or exit-minded voices: Plosser (wants rate hikes "by early next year at the latest"), Lacker, Fisher, Hoenig, Warsh, Stern, Bullard (partly), Evans (balanced).
- The economic picture is widely shared: a bottoming but fragile economy, a very large output gap, very high unemployment and slow recovery. The main disagreement is over inflation risks further out, the meaning of the output gap, and what LSAPs are for.

**4. Core dimensions (direction in words before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2% objective and drifting lower. Core PCE is about 1.5%. Kohn forecasts "inflation falling below my 2 percent objective". Yellen: core inflation "drift lower... below the 2 percent". Rosengren: inflation "well below my target". Deflation worries have faded, and the Chair's summary says inflation is "well controlled in the near term". A minority (Plosser, Lacker, Warsh) see upside risk in the outyears and anecdotal inflation worries. The current level is below target but not deflationary, so the score is below 50, around 35.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see a very large gap. Yellen cites a "GDP gap of about 5½ percent". Kohn: "sizable output gap... by almost any measure". Tarullo: "the number is clearly large". Plosser, Bullard and Evans question measurement but not the sign. The score is well below 50, about 20. The bright-line rule is satisfied (below potential, below 50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is near 9.5% and heading to 10%. May payrolls were -345k. Slack is severe and part-time work is unprecedented. Score about 16, which is the 2009 anchor range.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption has stopped falling, but the Chair says it is "not robust". Capital spending is "very soft" and housing shows only "a few signs of life". Savings are rising and consumers are liquidity constrained. This is below trend and stabilizing, so about 29.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Kohn ("surprisingly rapid"), Yellen (TFP "on or actually above trend") and the Chairman ("surprisingly high"). The score is above trend, around 62. Confidence is moderate because the staff reads it as partly cyclical.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Conditions improved a lot, but participants see them as still tight. Kohn: "risk premiums remain quite high by historical standards... costs of debt and equity capital are elevated". Stern: "continued strained credit conditions". Duke: "credit crunch is still to come". Bank lending is still declining. They are well off the crisis peak but not accommodative and not neutral, so somewhat tight, about 60. Bright-line: no "accommodative" or "easy" language, so above 50 is consistent.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members mention higher Treasury yields (Warsh: "benign and less benign reasons") and the Chair attributes the rise to a reversal of flight-to-quality flows. Warsh says the Treasury market "was working well". No dysfunction is described, so the score is in the normal range, about 40.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads have narrowed (Yellen, Kohn, Lacker: BBB yields "just below 8 percent, off from... 10 percent"). The level is still described as high: Kohn says risk premiums "remain quite high by historical standards" and bank lending spreads remain "elevated". Level governs under v4.1, so the score is above 50, about 64.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members discuss the roughly 30% rebound in equities. Warsh worries markets are "not discriminating" and "lemmings", and that prices have not found a sustainable equilibrium. Dudley says markets are pricing out tail risk. The concern is that prices are ahead of fundamentals, with no extreme bubble language. Score about 43, Low confidence, because the valuation view is mixed and brief.
- BANKING_STRESS: Stress tests were seen as successful and capital was raised (Warsh, Duke, Chair). But Tarullo cites bank closures growing "longer each week" and CRE problems. Duke, Kohn and Rosengren expect mounting losses and constrained balance sheets. Banks are neither healthy nor in crisis, so the stress is emerging to significant, about 62. Bright-line: no "healthy" or "sound" language, and the score is above 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only brief mentions. Yellen lists "a lower dollar" among recent market moves. Dudley raises a dollar collapse as a low-probability risk. A modest depreciation gives a score below 50, about 38, Low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence says below target, so a score below 50 matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means a tighter market, and the evidence says very slack, so a low score matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and the evidence says elevated but narrowing, so a mid-60s score matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and the evidence says an emerging-to-significant level, so a score in the low 60s matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence says somewhat tight, so a score in the low 60s matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds the funds rate at zero and keeps LSAPs on the existing schedule (Alternative B), with no expansion. The hawkish camp (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Hoenig, Warsh) opposes expansion and some want scaling back or earlier hikes. The dovish camp (Yellen, Rosengren, Kohn, Tarullo, Duke) sees a case for more stimulus but does not push for it now. Ongoing QE is itself a dovish signal. The result is a hold with a mild easing bias and an active balance sheet, so dovish-leaning, about 39.
- Disagreement: The vote is unanimous with zero dissents (per the vote read-out and the Chairman's remarks). There is real disagreement in the discussion on the output gap and Phillips curve (Plosser, Bullard vs Kohn, Yellen), LSAP objectives (Warsh vs Rosengren, Kohn), Fed bills, whether to include the exit sentence, and the timing of tightening. Tarullo expresses discomfort at an "disconnect" between the economic analysis and policy. Lacker says he "would have terminated them all". Still, all vote for B. This is moderate differences without dissents, about 34.
- Financial stability: Roughly the whole first day concerns facilities, the balance sheet and exit, and members also discuss bank health, CRE, credit supply, market improvement and fragility in the go-around. It is a prominent topic and not dominant over the real-economy assessment. About 62.
- Forward guidance: "Exceptionally low levels... for an extended period" stays. Most want the exit-strategy sentence dropped so the statement does not signal tightening, and the Chair agrees. Several (Plosser, Warsh, Hoenig) want to prepare for earlier exit, and the deflation-concern sentence is dropped, which is read as somewhat more hawkish. Overall this is a low-for-long lean with data dependence. About 30.
- Uncertainty: Stern says that near turning points "there's always a tremendous amount of uncertainty". Bullard: "tremendous amount of uncertainty". Multiple members cite the output gap range (minus 2-3% to minus 7-8%), the wide inflation range, LSAP mechanisms "we don't know" (Plosser), and the fragile recovery. Evans notes reduced downside risk since March. The result is elevated but not extreme, about 58.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50: yes. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 with no accommodative language: consistent. CREDIT_SPREADS above 50 on a stated high level: yes. BANKING_STRESS above 50 with no "healthy" language: yes. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 on depreciation: yes. TREASURY_STRESS in the 36-50 band, no dysfunction: yes.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 20 vs LABOR 16: consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP 20 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 29: the difference is 9 points, within the allowed range, because spending has stabilized while the gap remains very large.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 60 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 64: they co-move.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: acceptable, because there is no Treasury dysfunction.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 35 vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 50: expectations are described as anchored (near target) while actual inflation is low. No sub-components are scored other than expectations.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING are N/A. Members mention commodity and energy prices and tobacco taxes only in passing, and do not describe goods, services or shelter inflation as categories. PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS, EQUITY_VALUATION and DOLLAR_STRENGTH were each discussed at least briefly, so they are scored, with lower confidence where the discussion was thin.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

### Core economic

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "inflation falling below my 2 percent objective over the next few years". Bernanke (summary): inflation "likely to be well controlled in the near term".
- Summary: Core inflation near 1.5% and expected to drift lower, with upside concern held by a minority about the outyears.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is below neutral because the main view is slack-driven disinflation, offset by rising energy prices and a few hawks.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 20 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "With a GDP gap of about 5½ percent, the economy is in a deep hole." Tarullo: "the number is clearly large."
- Summary: Nearly everyone agrees the gap is very large, even those skeptical of how to measure it.
- Direction: The output gap is below neutral (below potential) because of the deep slack in labor and product markets.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 +-4 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "345,000 nonfarm jobs lost in May... unemployment rate is soaring". Tarullo: "inexorable rise towards double digits."
- Summary: Unemployment about 9.5% and rising, with job losses and widespread part-time work.
- Direction: The labor market is below neutral (very loose) because of the unemployment level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 29 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke (summary): consumer spending "no longer declining, is not robust either"; capital investment "remains very soft". Yellen: consumers "passing up bulk purchases".
- Summary: Spending has stabilized at a depressed level, with capex postponed and consumers liquidity-constrained.
- Direction: Spending is below neutral (below trend) because of weak labor, wealth and credit conditions.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Productivity growth has been surprisingly rapid." Yellen: productivity "on or actually above trend".
- Summary: Several members note strong productivity, though the staff view is cyclical.
- Direction: Productivity is above neutral because participants call it surprisingly high.

### Financial

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "risk premiums remain quite high by historical standards. The costs of debt and equity capital are elevated." Stern: "continued strained credit conditions".
- Summary: Conditions have improved but remain tighter than normal, with weak bank lending and reliance on government support.
- Direction: Financial conditions are above neutral (tight) because participants stress that the improvement is partial and contingent.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh: Treasury market "was working well before we got in it". Bernanke: rate rise reflects "a stronger outlook and a reversal of the flight-to-quality flows".
- Summary: Yields rose on normal market forces with no dysfunction described.
- Direction: Treasury stress is below neutral because participants describe orderly functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 64 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "risk premiums remain quite high by historical standards"; Lacker: "Triple-B corporate bond yields are just below 8 percent, off from... 10 percent."
- Summary: Spreads are narrowing, but members describe the level as still elevated.
- Direction: Credit spreads are above neutral because of the stated high level, with the narrowing noted as direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "asset prices... not yet having found a new sustainable equilibrium"; markets "don't seem to be discriminating".
- Summary: Members see the rally as getting somewhat ahead of fundamentals, without bubble language.
- Direction: Equity concern is slightly below neutral, in the direction of modest overvaluation worry, because prices have rebounded fast.

**BANKING_STRESS: 62 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "the list of banks slated for closure grows longer each week". Duke: "I believe the credit crunch is still to come."
- Summary: The stress tests and capital raises reduced systemic risk, but CRE and consumer losses, constrained balance sheets and failures continue.
- Direction: Banking stress is above neutral because of rising losses and failures, despite improved capital.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "falling risk spreads on bonds, higher stock prices, higher Treasury rates, and a lower dollar".
- Summary: Only passing mention of a weaker dollar.
- Direction: Dollar strength is below neutral because the dollar is described as lower.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS: N/A (not discussed as a category).
- INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50. Pianalto: expectations "reasonable to characterize them as anchored". Bullard: TIPS compensation "below our inflation target". Lacker: "measures of inflation expectations have increased noticeably". Anchored at about target, with worries on both sides.

### Discussion-specific

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 39 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "not much need to change our policy stance today, so we should keep our programs in place". Yellen: "a strong case exists for further monetary stimulus", but "I wouldn't push for an expansion now." Plosser: "begin raising rates by early next year at the latest".
- Summary: Hold at zero with ongoing LSAPs and no expansion, with a hawkish minority and a dovish minority and a mild easing bias in the middle.
- Direction: The lean is below neutral because the stance is accommodative and the QE program continues.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 34 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: The vote read-out shows ten Yes votes and none against. Tarullo: "somewhat discomfited by... a disconnect". Lacker: "I would have terminated them all."
- Summary: No dissents, but real camps on the output gap, LSAP rationale and the exit sentence.
- Direction: Disagreement is moderate because there are no formal dissents but substantive differences are aired.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Chair opens the first day with "balance sheet, reserve management, exit strategy". Duke, Kohn and Tarullo discuss bank health, credit supply and CRE at length.
- Summary: A large share of the meeting concerns facilities, balance sheet and financial market conditions, though the real economy remains central.
- Direction: Emphasis is above neutral because it is prominent without dominating.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "even if they've changed their expectations for future interest rates, we have not, at least not appreciably." Bernanke: "I would recommend dropping the last sentence" (the exit sentence).
- Summary: "Extended period" is retained and the exit sentence is dropped to avoid signalling tightening, while the deflation concern is removed.
- Direction: Guidance is below neutral because the signal is low for longer, with a hawkish minority wanting earlier exit.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Stern: "there's always a tremendous amount of uncertainty" at turning points. Bullard: "a tremendous amount of uncertainty about where the economy will be going".
- Summary: Several members stress uncertainty about the gap, inflation and LSAP effects, though downside tail risk has narrowed since March (Evans).
- Direction: Uncertainty is above neutral (elevated) because many cite measurement and outlook doubts, short of extreme.
