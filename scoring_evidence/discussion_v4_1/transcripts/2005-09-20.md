# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion transcript, meeting 2005-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: members' questions to staff (staff answers removed), the round-table go-around of conditions and policy, the Chairman's recommendation, a policy go-around, and a vote read-out. The text is readable and I found no garbled passages. The staff Q&A opening is fragmentary (questions only), which I treat as procedure.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Participants talk about Hurricane Katrina and the Rita threat, energy and gasoline prices near $3/gal, the Greenbook forecast, a long run of 25bp funds-rate increases ("ten consecutive") under a "measured pace" statement, and the choice between a 25bp increase and a pause.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), Geithner (Vice Chair), Governors Ferguson, Kohn, Olson, Bies, and Presidents Moskow, Guynn, Santomero, Fisher, Yellen, Minehan, Lacker, Stern, Hoenig, Poole, Pianalto. Nearly all favor a 25bp increase. Olson (voting governor) wants to hold, and Poole (non-voter) prefers a pause.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. Core PCE is projected at 2.25-2.5% in 2006, which Lacker calls "appreciably beyond a range I view as consistent with price stability." Energy pass-through, cost increases passed on to customers, and wage anecdotes are widespread. Recent core readings are called well behaved, and Yellen sees core as "well contained." Score about 65.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy at or slightly above potential. Santomero: "GDP growth slightly higher than potential" and "a whisker from potential." Ferguson: "appeared to be growing above potential." Geithner: "growing slightly above trend." Kohn: "high level of resource utilization." Minehan: growth "around potential." Bright line (any "above potential" language means more than 50) is satisfied at 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as modestly tight. Santomero: "little or no slack remaining." Moskow, Guynn, Minehan and Stern report shortages and wage increases. Yellen calls wage measures "remarkably subdued." Score 58.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net is near trend. Pre-Katrina spending was solid, but there is weak capex, retrenchment from high gasoline prices, and bifurcated consumers. Score 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mixed. Geithner says "solid productivity growth," Minehan says "Productivity growth has slowed," and Moskow says outsized gains cannot continue. Score 48.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Looser than neutral. "Favorable financial conditions" (Geithner, Kohn, Bies), "so much liquidity" (Moskow), "ample liquidity" (Bies), Minehan "supportive of growth, if a bit too complacent." Bright line (supportive means less than 50) is satisfied at 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. The only Treasury item is the opening Desk question about futures delivery and open interest, and the staff answers are removed. No member describes dysfunction.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A. No member mentions spreads. Greenspan's "term premiums down" is not a credit spread.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A. Only a theoretical aside by Ferguson.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only a light touch. Stern says credit card "charge-offs are at a 10-year low," and Minehan says bank customers are "doing great." Healthy side, low confidence. Score 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods are scored from energy, petrochemical, building-material and distribution cost pass-through. Services and housing are N/A (house-price talk is not shelter inflation). Expectations are scored from the Michigan jump, "somewhat less contained" (Ferguson), TIPS stable (Yellen, Kohn), and Greenspan's erosion worry.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: A clear majority and the Chair favor another 25bp increase and the "measured pace," with the Chair stressing that the real risk imbalance is on inflation and wanting a "buffer." Some want to rethink the language. Score 68.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (Olson, "very much a minority view"). Poole prefers no change. Everyone else agrees on the action. Differences on economics are modest, and the debate is mostly about tactics (pause versus not). Score 32.
- Financial stability: Financial conditions are mentioned as context (favorable, liquidity, complacency, housing, fiscal and term-premium worries), but this is not a focus. Score 28.
- Forward guidance: Most signal continued tightening at a measured pace. Greenspan wants the "alternative B" language. Guynn and Poole want stronger inflation wording. Fisher, Stern, Hoenig and Ferguson raise rethinking "measured pace" and approaching neutral. Score 62.
- Uncertainty: Many say "more uncertain than usual" (Kohn), "uncharted territory" (Olson), and "level of uncertainty is going to be rising" (Greenspan), and note the output and price sides are hard to read. Most still argue for decisive action. Score 64.

**Polarity checks.** Inflation: more pressure means a higher score, and the evidence says above target, which gives more than 50. Hawkish lean: higher means more hawkish, and the evidence says tighten, which gives more than 50. Financial conditions: higher means tighter, and the evidence says supportive and ample liquidity, which gives less than 50. Banking stress: higher means more stress, and the evidence says low charge-offs, which gives less than 50.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 and LABOR 58 differ by less than 15. OUTPUT_GAP 54 and CONSUMPTION 50 are consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 pairs with CREDIT_SPREADS N/A, so there is nothing to conflict. TREASURY is N/A. The parent inflation score (65) is consistent with the sub-components (goods 68, expectations 58).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not addressed by members in a scorable way.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 65 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Lacker, "would put inflation appreciably beyond a range I view as consistent with price stability"; Moskow, "the outlook has deteriorated." Center of gravity: core inflation outlook above comfort range driven by energy pass-through, with a few members seeing core as well behaved. Above neutral because most members stress upside inflation risk.

**OUTPUT_GAP 54 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Santomero, "slightly higher than potential... a whisker from potential"; Ferguson, "growing above potential." Center of gravity: the economy sits at or slightly above potential, with the gap closing. Above neutral per the bright-line rule.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 58 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Santomero, "little or no slack remaining"; Guynn, "shortages in several markets." Center of gravity: tight labor with shortage anecdotes but subdued wage indexes. Above neutral because slack is gone.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Santomero, "Consumers continued to spend at a good pace"; Kohn, "weakness in capital spending." Center of gravity: solid consumer spending offset by weak capex and an energy-price drag. At neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 48 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Geithner, "solid productivity growth"; Minehan, "Productivity growth has slowed." Center of gravity: mixed views on productivity. At neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Geithner, "favorable financial conditions"; Bies, "ample liquidity in financial markets." Center of gravity: accommodative conditions per several members. Below neutral because conditions are supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A; CREDIT_SPREADS N/A; EQUITY_VALUATION N/A; DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.** Not addressed by members.

**BANKING_STRESS 30 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Stern, "charge-offs are at a 10-year low"; Minehan, "most bank customers seemed to be doing great." Center of gravity: low credit stress, thin evidence. Below neutral because the system looks healthy.

**INFLATION_GOODS 68** (Guynn, "prices for petrochemical feeder stock... up 20 to 40 percent"). **INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 58** (Ferguson, near-term expectations rising and longer-term "somewhat less contained"; Yellen, TIPS stable).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 68 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I would opt for a 25 basis point increase"; Bies, "risks of higher inflation to me right now are much greater than the risks to economic growth." Center of gravity: a large majority backs continued measured tightening, with one dissent and one non-voting pause advocate. Above neutral because the majority favors tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 32 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Olson, "I continue to support alternative A... very much a minority view"; Poole, "I retain my preference for no change." Center of gravity: one formal dissent (Olson) and one non-voting pause preference, with broad agreement otherwise. Moderate-low because only tactical differences remain.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 28 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "a bit too complacent about risk"; Greenspan, "inflation premiums are going to build into long-term interest rates." Center of gravity: financial conditions are context only. Below the midpoint because attention is limited.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 62 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Poole, "the highly likely need for more restraint in the future"; Geithner, "the slope of the expected fed funds rate should remain materially positive." Center of gravity: continued tightening signal, with some calls to rethink "measured pace." Above neutral because the signal is tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 64 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Olson, "We are in uncharted territory"; Kohn, "the outlook is more uncertain than usual." Center of gravity: widespread, genuine uncertainty about the Katrina and energy effects, though members still act decisively. High because many emphasize difficulty.
