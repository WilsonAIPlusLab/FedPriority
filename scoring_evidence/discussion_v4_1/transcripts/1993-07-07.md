# v4.1 / V4.1.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 1993-07-07 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed (staff answers to member questions are missing, so some member questions appear without their answers). The file runs to the last line, which holds the vote read-out. It covers four items:
- the M2+ memo discussion;
- the Desk reports and chart-show questions;
- the economic go-around;
- the long-run monetary ranges, the policy directive vote, and a long discussion of leaks.

**Special-topic?** No. There is a go-around on conditions, a policy stance discussion (hold, symmetric vs asymmetric directive) and a roll call on the directive. Under V4.1.1 rule 1 the Tier 3 dimensions are scored from what was said and the vote record. The M2 and leak sections are not conditions evidence and are ignored for the condition dimensions.

**Era context.** Summer 1993: a modest recovery, a fiscal package and health-care reform pending, a recent move to an asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive, and an uptick in core CPI earlier in the year.

**Speakers.**
- Forrestal, Parry, Keehn, McTeer, Boehne, Hoenig, Stern, Oltman, Syron, LaWare, Phillips, Kelley and Lindsey describe the economy as modest growth under fiscal and health-care uncertainty, with downside risks.
- Broaddus, Mullins, Melzer, Jordan and Angell stress the inflation problem and the weight of expectations.
- Mullins gives the most detailed data review. Angell is the hawkish outlier.

**Directional statements, by dimension.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as above the objective. Mullins says core CPI averaged about 4% for eight months versus 2-1/2% before, and calls it "a long blip" across broad measures. Broaddus calls the core CPI rise "troubling". Angell's and the staff's 3.3% is described as a failure to restrain. Offsetting this, Forrestal, Keehn, Boehne and Oltman report "virtually nonexistent" price pressure in their districts, and the latest CPI and PPI were favorable. Somewhat to modestly above target, so 59 to 62.
- OUTPUT_GAP: members speak of "an environment of slack and slow growth" (Mullins) and "use of resources is way down" (Angell). Forrestal says "enough slack to support growth somewhat above potential for a while". Below potential, so I set about 40. The bright-line rule holds: only below-potential language is used.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: California unemployment is 9.1% (Parry), jobs grow at a modest pace (about 161,000 per month per Mullins), and Phillips says job creation is "barely keeping up with the demographics". Layoff announcements and a reduced propensity to hire are mentioned. Slack remains, so about 36.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: moderate growth of 2.5 to 3%. Autos are strong, but consumer spending is "losing momentum" (Forrestal), manufacturing is sluggish, and capital spending is held back by uncertainty (Keehn). Mullins calls it "the most reliable component" but decelerating. Net slightly below trend, about 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Jordan describes the "extraordinary increase in the utilization of technology". Angell cites "real productivity gains". Phillips says investment is focused on productivity and efficiency, and Keehn cites the steel contract. Above trend, about 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: two forces pull against each other. On the loose side are the "lowest mortgage rates in 21 years" (Mullins), "very stimulative" policy (Melzer) and negative real rates (Angell, Phillips). On the tight side, the credit crunch is "alive and well" for small business (Forrestal, McTeer), though banks are "beating the bushes" for borrowers. Net about neutral, 47. This is conditions, not the rate decision.
- TREASURY_STRESS: the only bond-market content is Mullins on the long rate flirting with 7% and then setting record lows, which is yield movement tied to inflation fears. No dysfunction. I score 40 with Low confidence rather than N/A, because the bond market was explicitly discussed.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread, yield-premium or risk-premium wording. Only availability language (V4.1.1 rule 3): small-business credit "very difficult" (Forrestal), improving access (Hoenig), weak loan demand, higher collateral demands (Oltman). It is mixed and slightly tight, so 53 with Low confidence and UNC +-12. This rests on availability, not spreads.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: no valuation view. Syron relays fund managers' worry about a "market correction" and mutual fund flows, and Angell fears stocks and junk bonds "could really fall off the table" if rates must catch up. This is vulnerability talk, not a valuation call. Slight overvaluation concern, 43, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: banks are discussed only as lenders (cautious standards, shrinking intermediation per McTeer). There is no capital or solvency language. A neutral 50 with Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: McDonough says the fundamentals point toward "a gradually strengthening yen and a weakening dollar", but the dollar might "strengthen a little" after the Japanese elections, and there is no conviction against European currencies. Mixed to stable. Score 44, Low confidence.
- INFLATION sub-components. Goods: Keehn reports purchased materials costs declining, while Melzer and Phillips report cement and lumber spikes described as short-term supply problems. Net 50. Services and housing are not discussed, so N/A. Expectations: Phillips sees a "ratcheting up" of expectations, Stern says "expectations are not working for us", and Mullins and Syron report businesses treating 3 to 3-1/2% as the norm. Anchored at an elevated level, so 65.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean. The Committee holds the funds rate unchanged and keeps the asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive. Angell dissented by voting No on the directive; his stated reason is that a 1/4-point rise is needed to raise real rates. Melzer says "we're going to have to get that rope back in", Jordan wants a correction, and Parry and Phillips want to stay the course, with Phillips noting that a change "would signal a lack of concern about inflation". Several (Forrestal, Hoenig, Keehn, Oltman, Boehne) would prefer symmetry in principle but accept asymmetry, and others such as LaWare, McTeer and Lindsey stress downside risks. Hold with a tightening bias, so slightly hawkish at 57.
- Disagreement. There is one formal dissent (Angell, on the directive vote). The M2 range votes were unanimous, though preferences differed. The economic views were broadly similar, with some sharp exchange on the leak topic (Keehn and Angell), which is not about policy. Moderate-low, 30.
- Financial stability attention. Several members raise credit availability and Syron raises mutual fund fragility, but all briefly. Brief to limited, 17.
- Forward guidance. The asymmetric directive was retained, and several members stressed the need to act promptly if inflation emerges (Syron, Melzer, Broaddus). Mild tightening bias, 61.
- Uncertainty. Many repeat fiscal, health-care and tax uncertainty as depressing spending. Oltman says "repeat the theme of uncertainty", Keehn says "extreme uncertainty" on M2, and Parry says the inflation outlook is "very uncertain". The risks themselves are described fairly clearly as downside for activity and upside for inflation, so elevated but not extreme: 58.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more inflation, and the evidence says above target, so the score is above 50. Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential, and the evidence says slack, so it is below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter, and the evidence says unemployment of roughly 7% and above, so it is below 50. Higher HAWKISH means a tighter stance, and the evidence says hold with a tightening bias and one hawkish dissent, so slightly above 50.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP is below 50 given slack language.
- CREDIT_SPREADS has no level wording, so it sits in the 45-55 band on availability, with Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS has no "sound" or "stressed" wording, so it is neutral.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: the expected direction is mixed (stronger yen, perhaps a little stronger dollar), so it is near and below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS has no dysfunction.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is neutral because the loose and tight signals offset, and no one calls conditions "accommodative" or "tight".

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR (36): consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43): consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (47) and CREDIT_SPREADS (53): within 6 points and co-moving, both near neutral.
- The Treasury stress score is not in tension with financial conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) is consistent with goods (50) and expectations (65), and no services or housing sub-scores are available.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed them. No dimension is N/A beyond that.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Mullins, "for eight months now... core CPI has averaged roughly 4 percent versus about 2-1/2 percent for the previous six months"; Keehn, "I really don't sense any significant upward pressures on prices on a broad basis." Center of gravity: inflation is clearly above where members want it, but the latest data are better and districts report little pressure. Inflation pressure is above neutral because core measures run 3.5 to 4% and the forecasts keep rising.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Mullins, "in an environment of slack and slow growth"; Angell, "use of resources is way down". Center of gravity: the economy still operates below potential despite a slow recovery. Output gap is below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 36 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, California unemployment "raising the unemployment rate to 9.1 percent"; Phillips, job creation is "barely keeping up with the demographics". Center of gravity: modest job growth that does not close slack. Labor is below neutral because unemployment is elevated.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Forrestal, "Consumer spending had been quite strong but it appears to be losing momentum"; Mullins, "capital spending... has been the most reliable component of the recovery". Center of gravity: moderate growth with uncertainty restraining business spending. Spending is slightly below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Angell, "We're in a period where we have some real productivity gains"; Jordan, "the extraordinary increase in the utilization of technology". Center of gravity: several members see strong technology-driven productivity gains. Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 47 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Mullins, "the lowest mortgage rates in 21 years"; Forrestal, "a lot of complaints about the famous credit crunch". Center of gravity: low rates against persistent small-business credit tightness. Conditions are about neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Mullins, "the long bond again flirted with 7 percent... the long rates retrenched to new record lows". Center of gravity: orderly yield moves around inflation news, with no dysfunction. Normal functioning, below the midpoint.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 +-12, Low.** This rests on availability, not spreads (V4.1.1 rule 3). Evidence: Forrestal, "difficulty people have getting credit, particularly small business people"; Hoenig, "much less the case than it was a few months ago". Center of gravity: availability is improving but still tight for small borrowers. Slightly above neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Syron, "concern about all this money flowing into mutual funds and about what will happen when we get a little market correction"; Angell, "those investments in common stocks and junk bonds could really fall off the table". Center of gravity: a mild vulnerability concern, not a stated valuation view. Slightly toward overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 50 +-10, Low.** Evidence: McTeer, "the banking system is shrinking; it's becoming less important as an intermediary"; Oltman, bankers say they are "beating the bushes" for borrowers. Center of gravity: lending behavior only, with no capital or solvency language. Neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 44 +-8, Low.** Evidence: McDonough, "fundamentals... point toward a gradually strengthening yen and a weakening dollar"; "the dollar would strengthen a little" after the Japanese election. Center of gravity: a mixed or stable picture with a weak-dollar lean against the yen. Slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 50.** Evidence: Keehn, purchased-materials cost forecast revised to a decline of 0.9%; Melzer, "the price of cement, which has gone up about 50 percent", "a short-term supply problem". Mixed.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 65.** Evidence: Phillips, "a ratcheting up in inflationary expectations in the consumer confidence indexes"; Stern, "I don't have a sense that expectations are working for us". Anchored at an elevated level, above target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-6, High.** Evidence: Phillips, "best staying with an asymmetric toward tightening directive"; Angell, "This is the time for us to move real interest rates back at least to zero." Center of gravity: unchanged rates with a retained tightening bias and one dissent for tightening. Slightly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-7, High.** Evidence: the vote record shows Governor Angell voting No on the directive (one dissent) and unanimous votes on the 1993 and 1994 M2 ranges; Angell, "I'm clearly not in tune with the other members of the FOMC." Center of gravity: one formal dissent with broad agreement on economics. Low-moderate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 17 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Forrestal and McTeer on the credit crunch; Syron on fund managers' fragility concerns. Center of gravity: brief mentions, mostly of credit availability. Low attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 61 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Syron, keep "the signal that we'd react relatively quickly"; Melzer, "we're going to have to get that rope back in at some point". Center of gravity: the tightening-bias directive is kept, with calls to be ready to move. Mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Oltman, "I have to repeat the theme of uncertainty"; Keehn, "this period of extreme uncertainty". Center of gravity: many members stress fiscal and health-care uncertainty and an uncertain inflation outlook, though risks are still characterized. Elevated.
