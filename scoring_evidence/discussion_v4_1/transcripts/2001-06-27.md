# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2001-06-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It contains (a) members' questions and remarks in a special session on productivity and a capital-overhang discussion (day 1), (b) the economic go-around (day 2 morning), (c) questions on the Bluebook/policy options, (d) the Chairman's recommendation and the policy go-around, (e) a statement-wording exchange, and (f) the vote read-out (9 Yes, 1 No: Poole). Many turns are bare questions whose staff answers were removed, so some passages are only half a conversation.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2001, after 250 bp of easing in the year (275 bp after this meeting), a manufacturing/high-tech downturn, weak equipment investment, strong dollar, tax cut stimulus coming. Scored from what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan; VC McDonough; Governors Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer; Presidents Jordan, Guynn, Moskow, Parry, McTeer, Santomero, Minehan, Stern, Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole. Broad view: economy stalled/"bumping along the bottom", housing and autos resilient, business investment and manufacturing very weak, labor market loosening from very tight, inflation contained but some (Poole, Hoenig, Guynn, Moskow, Jordan, Parry) warn of future inflation risk from too much ease. Policy: 25 bp cut with downside-risk wording retained, and an intent to signal that easing is near its end.

**4. Directional statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as slightly below/at the midpoint: Greenspan says "very little in the way of pricing power" and core PCE "unchanged"; Hoenig "price pressures should be contained"; McDonough "wage pressures ... subdued"; Parry core PCE "just over 2 percent" and "higher than I'd like". Offsetting: Poole "inflation risks are rising", health costs, oil futures. Net slightly below 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Slightly below potential. Capacity utilization near recession lows (Kelley, Ferguson), growth stalled, unemployment rising; but Meyer says the slowdown "began with output above potential" and Kelley says the economy still operates at "a very high level", with the unemployment gap "near zero" by 2002. Net modestly below 50 (bright line: slack/below-trend language, so < 50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Loosening from very tight levels. UE figures cited about 4.3-5% (NY 4.3, Texas 4.5, NC ~5), "historically low level" rising (Kelley), claims mid-400,000s (Ferguson), "labor markets have loosened further" (Broaddus), some shortages persist (Jordan). Near NAIRU, easing: slightly below 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption and housing "holding up" (Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole), autos strong; business equipment/high-tech investment very weak ("dramatic weakening in equipment investment"), capital overhang, GDP growth near 0-1%. Net clearly below trend, about 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Extensively discussed. Structural productivity "around 2-1/2 percent ... an elevated level" (Meyer), "new and more favorable productivity track" (Stern); possible deceleration and wide confidence bands. Above trend, with doubts: about 60.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Policy "clearly accommodative" (Gramlich), "credit markets are accommodative" (Poole), "ample liquidity" (Jordan), Minehan "financial markets seem less than accommodative to excess"; long rates have not fallen; equity capital losses weigh. Bright line: accommodative gives < 50. About 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members discuss long-term rates, forward rates, TIPS spread, but no dysfunction at all. Normal functioning, scored low with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level favorable: "Neither the price nor the availability of credit is a problem" (Jordan); "Funds for qualified borrowers continue to be available but credit quality concerns remain" (Guynn); "firms that are reasonably creditworthy have no difficulty borrowing" (Poole). Below normal: about 38. Low-Moderate confidence because spread levels are not directly stated.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Poole: "by conventional measures the stock market is still generously priced"; Kelley "remains quite elevated overall"; Greenspan questions double-digit earnings estimates. Valuation view modestly above fair value: about 35.
- BANKING_STRESS: Guynn bank examiners "still expressing confidence" but bankruptcies rising; Minehan loan deterioration "begun to taper off", confined to telecom syndicated loans. Healthy with minor concerns: about 42 (< 50 satisfied).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Strong dollar, "the dollar keeps rising above expectations" (Parry), "strong dollar" cutting tourism/exports (Guynn, Ferguson, Broaddus). Appreciation, > 50: about 62.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services, housing not framed as category pressures by members (health-cost remarks are firm-cost anecdotes; housing inflation comments are single regional notes): N/A. Expectations: Greenspan "inflation expectations if anything falling", Poole "holding steady", Gramlich sees no evidence of rising expectations; Stern/Moskow caution about keeping them low. Anchored: about 47.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION = more pressure; evidence says contained: below 50, OK. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress; evidence says healthy: below 50, OK. Higher DOLLAR = stronger; evidence says appreciation: above 50, OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: accommodative gives below 50, OK. EQUITY_VALUATION: the rubric scale puts "somewhat elevated" valuations at 31-45, consistent with Poole's "generously priced".

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: A 25 bp cut (smaller than prior 50 bp steps) with downside-risk wording retained. Most favor modest further ease with a "now finishing" signal; a hawkish group (Poole, Hoenig, Guynn, Jordan, Moskow, Parry, Broaddus, Stern, Kelley) stresses inflation/overshooting risk, while Ferguson, McTeer, Gramlich, McDonough, Santomero see more downside. McTeer wanted continued reduction. Moderate dovish, tempered: 40.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Poole, on the risk-statement wording and excess ease); reluctant support from Hoenig ("would have stopped the easing process by now"), Kelley ("reluctantly"), Guynn ("wish I could be more comfortable"), Stern ("do not disagree strongly"); disagreement on tactics and the interpretation of the balance-of-risks language more than on the outlook. Moderate differences: 40.
- Financial stability emphasis: Some discussion of bond-market vs output-forecaster schism (Gramlich), capital losses and earnings (Greenspan), equity valuations (Poole), Argentina/Latin America instability, bankruptcies and bank loan quality, but economy and productivity dominate. Limited-to-moderate: 33.
- Forward guidance: Statement keeps risks weighted toward weakness (easing bias) but the stated aim is to signal easing is "completing"/"close to end", with readiness to reverse quickly. Mildly easing-leaning but near the end: 40.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan "we don't in fact know what the distribution of probabilities is" and "very great uncertainties"; Moskow "great deal of uncertainty"; Ferguson "distinctly murky"; Minehan "increasing uncertainty on the downside"; Poole "unique situation", Broaddus "confidence interval ... fairly wide" on productivity. Elevated, not extreme: 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (slack/low utilization framing; pairs with "above potential at onset" noted but current level below): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (accommodative): satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 (level favorable): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS < 50: satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50: satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS 36-50, no dysfunction: satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 43 vs LABOR 47: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 43 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36: 7 apart, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal, no crisis. INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 consistent with expectations 47 (only scored sub-component).

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: members give only scattered firm-level or single-region anecdotes (health costs, Denver housing inflation slowing, Philadelphia prices), no category-level characterization of price pressure.

**Text problems.** Many member turns are bare questions or short replies with the staff answers removed, so the exchanges are one-sided; the first line of the file has a speaker with no remarks; a few page markers and an unattributed line ("MS JOHNSON" answer) appear inside a member turn. No garbling that prevents scoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "There is very little in the way of pricing power in the economy"; Hoenig: "Price pressures should be contained for the time being." Summary: current inflation is seen as contained and softening, with forward risks from stimulus offsetting only partly. Direction: below neutral because most members describe soft prices and easing wage pressure while a hawkish minority (Poole: "inflation risks are rising") worry about the future.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kelley: capacity utilization "at a near-recession level, unemployment is rising"; Meyer: slowdown "began with output above potential" and the unemployment gap is "near zero" by 2002. Summary: growth has stalled and slack is opening, though from a high-activity level. Direction: below neutral because utilization is low and unemployment rising.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kelley: unemployment "rising from its recent historically low level"; Broaddus: "District labor markets have loosened further overall" (NC unemployment 3.6 to just over 5 percent). Summary: labor market near NAIRU and loosening. Direction: slightly below neutral because it is easing, with UE around 4.5%.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Broaddus: "dramatic weakening in equipment investment ... household spending still seems to be holding up better than might have been expected"; Moskow: "Some firms are simply refusing to buy any more computer equipment". Summary: investment collapse outweighs resilient housing/autos/consumption. Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Meyer: "structural productivity growth is around 2-1/2 percent and that represents an elevated level"; Stern: "inclined to believe that the U.S. economy is on a new and more favorable productivity track". Summary: productivity growth seen as above the 1970s-90s trend though possibly decelerating, with measurement doubts. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Gramlich: "now policy is clearly accommodative"; Poole: "credit markets are accommodative. The amount of funds being raised is ample". Summary: easy credit and liquidity, partly offset by equity losses and sticky long rates. Direction: below neutral (loose side).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Poole: "the ten-year bond rate today is about the same as it was in December"; Greenspan on TIPS/nominal spread. Summary: only yield and expectation discussion, no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning, low-moderate.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Jordan: "Neither the price nor the availability of credit is a problem for the smaller companies"; Guynn: "Funds for qualified borrowers continue to be available but credit quality concerns remain." Summary: credit available at the level of normal-to-favorable, with quality concerns confined to weaker borrowers. Direction: below neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Poole: "by conventional measures the stock market is still generously priced"; Kelley: stock market "remains quite elevated overall". Summary: valuations still rich despite declines. Direction: modestly above fair value (low score on this stress scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Guynn: "our bank examiners are still expressing confidence in our banks' ability to deal with the present level of stress"; Minehan: loan deterioration "has begun to taper off". Summary: sound banks with pockets of credit quality worry. Direction: below neutral (healthy).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry: "the dollar keeps rising above expectations"; Guynn: "the strong dollar and higher fuel prices are both seen as cutting into business". Summary: dollar strong and appreciating, a headwind to exports. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** No category-level discussion.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Evidence: Greenspan: "inflation expectations if anything falling at this point"; Poole: "inflation expectations are holding steady". Summary: anchored. Direction: at/just below target anchoring.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan: "I think we can afford to reduce the rate another 25 basis points. I would hope that will be the end of it"; Poole: "policy is on an excessively expansionary course." Summary: majority backs a smaller final-ish easing with caution, a hawkish minority resists. Direction: dovish side of neutral, tempered.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Poole: "I do not support the recommendation"; Hoenig: "if I had my preference, I would have stopped the easing process by now." Summary: one formal dissent plus several reluctant supporters, disagreement mostly about signaling and degree of ease. Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 33 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Gramlich on the schism between "output forecasters and the financial forecasters"; Greenspan: "very dramatic capital losses. These losses are weighing on the system". Summary: financial conditions are a recurring but secondary theme. Direction: limited-to-moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: McDonough: "a fond hope ... that by the next meeting we can issue a statement that the risks have evened out"; Greenspan: "we will signal to the market that we are now in the process of completing our easing actions." Summary: downside-risk wording retained but explicit signal that easing is ending and reversal is possible. Direction: easing lean tapering to neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "we don't in fact know what the distribution of probabilities is, and I think that we face a degree of risk that we are not able to assess fully"; Moskow: "a great deal of uncertainty remains about the outlook." Summary: many members stress an unusual cycle and wide risks, though not extreme. Direction: elevated.
