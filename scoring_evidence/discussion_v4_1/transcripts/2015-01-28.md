# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 2015-01-28 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion from a transcript, staff turns removed. It runs over two sessions. It opens with organizational business, the consensus statement, and open-market-operations authorizations. Then comes a long operational go-round on liftoff tools (ON RRP cap, term RRP testing, IOER). Then a financial-stability Q&A, the economic go-round, the liftoff-timing and pace go-round with statement language, and the policy go-round on alternative B. It ends with a vote read-out. I score participants' views on conditions and policy, and I ignore pure procedure and tool mechanics except where they bear on financial stability emphasis and policy lean. Staff answers are absent. Some member turns in the Q&A are orphaned questions or "Thanks" lines with no answer, so I did not rely on them.

**2. Era context.** The text puts this at the end of January 2015. The policy rate is at the lower bound. The labor market has improved a lot and growth is strong. Oil has fallen about 50 percent and the dollar has appreciated. Headline inflation is near zero and core is soft. Market inflation compensation has fallen and long-term yields are low. The Committee is deciding when to drop "patient" and when to lift off. None of this overrides what the text says.

**3. Speakers.** All of the following speak about conditions or policy.
- Chair Yellen, Vice Chair Dudley, Governors Fischer, Tarullo, Powell and Brainard.
- Presidents Fisher, Lacker, Williams, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Lockhart, Mester, Plosser, Bullard, Evans and George.
- Broad pattern: nearly everyone is optimistic on real activity, and views on inflation and on the speed of normalization diverge.
- Doves and cautious: Kocherlakota (wants more accommodation, credibility of the inflation target), Evans, Rosengren, Lockhart, Brainard, Tarullo, Dudley (patience, June at risk).
- Tilting hawkish: Plosser (prefers alternative C, policy "overly accommodative"), Lacker, Bullard, Fisher, George, Mester, Williams, Powell (June or September).

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as well below the 2 percent target. Headline is near zero or negative on energy. Core is about 1 percent, and midyear PCE is expected below 1.5 percent. Most call the energy and dollar drag transitory, so this is below the midpoint. The language is "somewhat less satisfactory", "below 1½ percent", and worry about deflation in some regions. That fits roughly 25-35, so I use 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: this is a split. Fisher says the gap is "close to being closed". Powell says slack will be gone by year-end. Williams expects full employment by year-end. Yellen, Tarullo, Evans, Dudley and Rosengren stress remaining slack and a lower natural rate. Growth is above trend, but the gap is still slightly negative and closing. Per the bright-line rule, "slack remains" means below 50, so I use 44.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: the unemployment rate is 5.6 percent. Participants say it is near estimates of the natural rate (about 5 to 5.5), with wages still tepid and hidden slack in part-time work and participation. The score is below 50 and close to it, at 45.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumer spending is brisk (Lacker cites about 4 percent growth), confidence is up and GDP is about 3 percent. Business investment is positive apart from oil and gas drilling, which is a drag. This is above trend, at 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: only passing mentions with no assessment, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: they are broadly accommodative. Long yields are very low, credit standards are easing, and Powell sees no bubble conditions. Yellen says conditions "may remain more accommodative than we intend". The strong dollar is a partial tightening offset (Evans, Yellen). Accommodative means below 50, so 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: yields and the term premium are discussed at length, but only as a puzzling low level. Nobody describes market dysfunction. Under the rule that yield moves alone are not dysfunction, I score normal functioning at 40. Confidence is Low because functioning itself is not discussed.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads are not named. Credit availability and quality are described as favorable (Lacker, Mester, Plosser), with a caution on subprime auto from Fisher. That is below normal on a level reading, at 38 with Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: only Yellen's remark about "boosting equity prices", Bullard's warning about bubbles if unemployment undershoots, and Powell's "don't see" bubble conditions. This is a price and concern mention without valuation analysis, so I default to 44 with Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: banks are described as hedged, little exposed to oil, healthy and stable (Fisher, Mester, Plosser, George). That is low stress, at 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: the dollar is up about 10 percent since summer and members fear more. This is strong appreciation and a notable headwind, at 72.
- Inflation sub-components: goods get a score because dollar and import-price pressure and energy declines are explicitly cited (25). Services and housing inflation are not assessed, so N/A. Inflation expectations are heavily discussed (34).

**5. Polarity checks.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure. The evidence says well below target, so the score is below 50. This matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means tighter. The evidence says a little slack and an unemployment rate near the natural rate. Below 50 and near it matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. The evidence says banks are sound. Below 50 matches.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciation means above 50. Matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. Easy credit means below 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter. Accommodative means below 50. Matches.

**6. Tier 3 in words.**
- *Policy lean.* The decision is to hold at the lower bound with alternative B, keeping "patient" in the formal statement. Most participants anticipate liftoff in mid-2015 or later, at a gradual pace. A meaningful group wants to wait (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Lockhart, Brainard, Tarullo). Another group wants earlier action (Plosser, Lacker, Bullard, Fisher, and partly George, Mester, Williams and Powell). The Chair says liftoff "could come as soon as the June meeting" but stresses caution. This is about balanced with a slight tightening bias, so 52.
- *Dissent.* The policy vote is unanimous, 10 to 0 on alternative B. Plosser says alternative C fits better, Lacker says April would not be too soon, and Kocherlakota (not a voter) argues for more accommodation. Lacker formally dissents on two foreign-currency authorizations, which is ritual and not about monetary policy. He and Fisher are also opposed on term RRP testing. There are distinct camps on timing and on the weight of low inflation, with sharp but collegial exchanges. That is 40.
- *Financial stability attention.* Much of the RRP discussion is framed around financial stability and footprint. Tarullo gives it extended treatment. There is a financial-stability Q&A (Fisher on subprime auto, Rosengren on global shocks, George on structural vulnerabilities). Bullard raises bubbles, Tarullo ties liftoff motivation to financial stability, and Brainard calls for vigilance. It is a moderate to prominent topic alongside the macro discussion, at 56.
- *Forward guidance.* The discussion is about when to remove "patient", with June or September liftoff on the table, data dependence and a gradual path. The actual statement keeps patience. The signal is a mild tightening bias with strong data dependence and caution, at 52.
- *Uncertainty.* Participants repeatedly call the environment "highly uncertain". They describe a puzzling disconnect between the real economy and wages and prices, a conundrum in long yields, uncertainty about the natural rate, and risks abroad (Greece, Ukraine, China). This is elevated but not extreme, at 56.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 is below 50 under "slack remains".
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 is below 50 under "accommodative".
- CREDIT_SPREADS 38 is below 50. No high level is stated and favorable credit is described.
- BANKING_STRESS 28 is below 50 under "sound".
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 72 is above 50 under appreciation.
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 is within 36-50, with no dysfunction described.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 45 are within 1 point.
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60 differ by 16. The reason is that growth is above trend and strong (a flow and momentum measure), while the level of the gap is still slightly negative (a stock). Growth is above trend while resources are still being absorbed, so I accept the small divergence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 38 co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 sits in the normal range with accommodative conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 matches expectations at 34 and goods at 25, with services and housing unscored.

**9. N/A.**
- PRODUCTIVITY: not assessed.
- INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no assessment of services or shelter prices, only a passing mention of services price inflation by Dudley.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±6, High.**
- Evidence: Williams: "the drop in energy prices has pushed down headline year-over-year inflation numbers to zero and even into negative territory." Yellen: "I am concerned that, at midyear, PCE inflation will be running below 1½ percent on a 12-month basis."
- Summary: inflation is well below target, with a transitory energy and dollar drag expected to fade.
- Direction: below neutral because most members describe headline near zero and core around 1 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 ±5, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Fisher: "I believe it is close to being closed." Tarullo: "continued substantial slack in the labor market."
- Summary: growth is above trend and the gap is closing, but members still describe residual slack.
- Direction: slightly below potential because slack remains per many members.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 45 ±5, High.**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "5.6 percent U-3 measure of unemployment." Fischer: "the unemployment rate is now very close to our estimates of the natural rate."
- Summary: the labor market is nearly at the natural rate with hidden slack and tepid wages.
- Direction: slightly below neutral at 5.6 percent unemployment with slack in participation, part-time work and wages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±6, High.**
- Evidence: Lacker: "consumption growth at about 4 percent this year." Fisher: "solidly above-trend growth path."
- Summary: consumer spending is strong, and investment is positive despite the oil and gas drag.
- Direction: above trend because spending, confidence and GDP growth near 3 percent are described as strong.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Only passing references with no assessment.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±7, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Yellen: "financial conditions may remain more accommodative than we intend." Mester: "credit standards are easing and credit demand is rising."
- Summary: accommodative overall, with the strong dollar as a partial tightening offset.
- Direction: below neutral because low long yields and easing credit are described as accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8, Low.**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the 10-year Treasury has declined close to 1 percentage point over the past year." Dudley on a possible bond revaluation in Germany and Japan.
- Summary: yields are low and puzzling, but nobody describes dysfunction.
- Direction: normal functioning because yield moves alone are not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±8, Low.**
- Evidence: Lacker (citing a banker): "loan demand and credit quality are now better than at any time since 2007." Plosser: "Bank lending on C&I loans was particularly robust."
- Summary: credit is available, with an isolated subprime auto concern from Fisher. Spreads are not discussed directly.
- Direction: below neutral because credit is described as favorable.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 ±8, Low.**
- Evidence: Powell: "I have been concerned that there would be bubble conditions... I don't see that at all." Yellen: low yields "may also be a factor boosting equity prices."
- Summary: no valuation concern is voiced, and bubble risk is mentioned only as a worry for the future (Bullard).
- Direction: roughly fair to slightly elevated, with no particular concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 ±6, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Fisher: "most of the middle to larger-sized banks we have contacted say their energy customers are hedged." Plosser: "the greater stability of our financial system currently."
- Summary: banks are sound with limited oil exposure.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 72 ±5, High.**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar has increased about 10 percent" since last summer. Dudley: "I am worried that the dollar's strength will not just persist but intensify."
- Summary: broad appreciation, seen as a headwind for net exports and inflation, with the risk of more.
- Direction: above neutral because the dollar has strengthened.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 25, Low.** Mester: "The appreciation of the dollar is showing up in lower input prices." Evans expects soft core and "pass-through from energy prices". Falling import, energy and commodity prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not assessed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Housing activity is discussed, not shelter inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 34.** Kocherlakota: "downside risk to the credibility of our inflation target." Lacker: longer-run expectations "have been quite stable." Market compensation is at lows and surveys are stable, with many worried about downward drift.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 52 ±6, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Yellen: "liftoff could come as soon as the June meeting." Kocherlakota: "I recommend that we commit to keeping the federal funds rate in its current range until the one-to-two-year-ahead outlook for inflation has returned to 2 percent."
- Summary: hold now, with mid-2015 liftoff widely expected at a gradual pace. A sizeable minority urges waiting, and a sizeable group favors earlier action.
- Direction: roughly balanced with a slight tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Lacker: "I will respectfully dissent from the foreign currency authorization directives." The policy vote on alternative B is 10 to 0. Plosser: "alternative C aligns better with my assessment of appropriate policy."
- Summary: no policy dissent, but camps differ on timing and on how much weight to put on low inflation. The only formal dissents are Lacker's on procedural foreign-currency items.
- Direction: moderate differences without a formal policy dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 56 ±8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "I began this exercise... with a strong set of financial stability concerns." Fisher flags "subprime auto loans".
- Summary: financial stability is a recurring theme in the RRP discussion and in the Q&A, but alongside the macro and policy discussion.
- Direction: moderate to prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 ±8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Mester: "I do think the suggestions in the staff memo for how the language might evolve in advance of liftoff provide a useful approach." Yellen: the trajectory is data dependent and "could easily be steeper or flatter" than the SEP paths.
- Summary: members discuss signaling liftoff while keeping options open, with a gradual and data-dependent path.
- Direction: mild tightening bias offset by emphasis on caution and flexibility.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 56 ±7, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Lockhart: "a puzzling and confusing disconnect between the strong real economy performance... and wage and price trends." Yellen: "such huge uncertainty about how real activity and inflation will evolve."
- Summary: many members stress uncertainty over inflation, the natural rate, long yields and global risks.
- Direction: elevated, not extreme.
