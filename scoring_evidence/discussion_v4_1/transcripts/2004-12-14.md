# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (+V4.1.1) — 2004-12-14 (transcript discussion)

Part 1: omitted in this run

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE — 46 ±6 (High)**
- Hoenig: "inflation remains modest, although there are some signs that it may be rising ... Core PCE inflation is 1½ percent ... core CPI inflation is 2 percent"; Stern: "I do expect inflation to remain low, but ... a little less confident"; Kohn: "so far the odds favor our success"; Yellen: risks "balanced", downside risk from productivity. Upside risks cited by many (Poole "tilted slightly to the upside"; Santomero "somewhat more inflation risk"; Geithner "somewhat greater probability of a higher inflation outcome"; Guynn "gives me pause").
- Measured inflation described as low/modest and below the comfort level, with broadening upside-risk talk; slightly below midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP — 44 ±4 (High)**
- Moskow: "still some meaningful resource slack in the economy"; Kohn: "a gradual further erosion of slack"; Hoenig: "growth remains above trend ... we will gradually approach our long-run potential GDP"; Geithner: "a smaller output gap—with the gap perhaps now within the margin of error"; Minehan: "excess capacity gradually disappearing".
- Slack remains but is closing (bright line: below 50).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS — 42 ±5 (High)**
- Moskow: "Labor market conditions are improving, although they're still not robust"; Santomero: "there likely is still some slack in labor markets, this is being worked off"; Kohn: wages moderate "tends to confirm that there is, indeed, slack in labor markets"; Hoenig: "sluggish employment growth ... wage pressures do remain slight". Pockets of tightness: Minehan "highly skilled labor remains in short supply"; Poole "beginning to tighten at the upper skill levels"; Stern "some skills are now in short supply".
- Below full employment with slack being absorbed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT — 60 ±5 (High)**
- Minehan: "consumer spending and business investment show considerable strength overall"; Lacker: "Consumer spending has advanced at a pretty solid trend rate ... capital spending has been fairly brisk, and housing keeps coming in on the strong side"; Bies (read): consumer spending "grew by a healthy 5.1 percent annual rate". Offsets: Ferguson on weak high-tech investment ("percentage of NFIB survey respondents planning to make capital expenditures is near the low end"); Santomero "nonresidential construction remains soft".
- Net above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY — 60 ±7 (High)**
- Yellen: "labor productivity growth ... has averaged nearly 4 percent—about 1½ percentage points above its pace over 1996-2000"; "2.7 percent Greenbook estimate of structural productivity growth as reasonable" with "significant upside productivity potential"; Pianalto "more optimistic than the baseline ... about productivity". Counter: Geithner "productivity growth slowing significantly"; Ferguson worried about "a slowing in capital deepening"; Kohn "only one quarter of slower productivity growth after about half a dozen quarters of growth above the presumed trend".
- Trend seen as above historical norm, with recent slowing debated.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — 28 ±6 (High)**
- Minehan: "Financial markets are supportive of continuing growth, with equity markets turning up and longer-term interest rates and spreads low ... liquidity abounds"; Kohn: "overall easing of financial conditions over the intermeeting period", "continued low intermediate- and long-term interest rates"; Bies: "Long-term interest rates are still low by historical standards"; Guynn: "unusually low mortgage rates".
- Supportive/easy (bright line: below 50).

**TREASURY_STRESS — 35 ±8 (Low)**
- Kohn: "I also find the recent behavior of bond yields hard to understand ... long-term yields have actually declined"; Ferguson: "two- to ten-year nominal spread has fallen ... to around 120 basis points". Only yield levels/curve shape; no dysfunction mentioned.
- Normal, quiet functioning; yield moves are not stress.

**CREDIT_SPREADS — 25 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Minehan: "the narrowness of credit spreads suggest that liquidity abounds"; Minehan (Q&A): "spreads are narrower ... market participants see less risk"; Olson (availability): C&I growth "reflects a greater appetite for risk-taking in the lending portfolio".
- Level stated as narrow/low (bright line: below 50).

**EQUITY_VALUATION — 43 ±8 (Low)**
- Poole: "maybe even very early signs of exuberance when you look at the recovery of IPOs and at merger and acquisition activity"; Minehan: "equity markets turning up". Other mentions (Kohn, Hoenig, Bies) are wealth-channel only.
- Price rise plus a tentative froth remark; mildly below midpoint.

**BANKING_STRESS — 35 ±10 (Low)**
- Olson: C&I lending growing with "a greater appetite for risk-taking", banks "have taken on more M&A lending", condo construction lending "absolutely unsustainable"; Guynn: "more of our bankers are reporting some increase in business lending".
- No stress described; banks expanding and taking more risk; below midpoint.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH — 25 ±5 (High)**
- Minehan: "the value of the dollar has declined. It may be likely to fall more"; Hoenig: "the dollar continues on balance to depreciate"; Yellen: "the decline in the dollar"; Santomero: "the depreciation of the dollar".
- Depreciation (bright line: below 50).

**INFLATION_GOODS — 60**
- Santomero: "some reports suggest increasing industrial goods price pressure"; Hoenig: "a net positive percentage of firms raised output prices" for the twelfth month; Kohn: "more-rapid increases in PPIs"; Lacker: firms "increasingly saying that they can pass through price increases".

**INFLATION_SERVICES — N/A**
- Members do not discuss services-price inflation as such (only shipping-cost anecdotes and wage costs).

**INFLATION_HOUSING — N/A**
- House-price run-up discussed (Kohn, Guynn) as an asset-price issue; residential rents/shelter costs not discussed (only declining commercial rents, Santomero).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS — 58**
- Guynn: expectations "while reasonably well anchored, have drifted up steadily ... to nearly 2¾ percent"; Lacker: survey and TIPS measures "between 2½ and 3 percent ... noticeably greater than we think"; Geithner: "sticking at a level somewhat above what we consider comfortable"; Holcomb: "a bit higher than preferred". Counter: Kohn "remained remarkably stable"; Ferguson five-to-ten-year "well contained".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN — 72 ±5 (High)**
- Unanimous support for alternative C, a 25 bp increase. Santomero: "prudent to continue on our upward path"; Hoenig: raise "by more than assumed in the Greenbook"; Guynn: "a considerable way to go to get back to a more neutral stance"; Holcomb: "this is not the time to pause"; Bernanke: "erring slightly on the side of tightness". Tempering: Kohn "don't think we should be planning on moving up faster than the market anticipates"; Yellen stresses downside inflation risks.
- Consensus for continued tightening at a measured pace.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY — 18 ±6 (High)**
- Policy roll call: all voters "Yes". Only wording reservations (Guynn: "moderate pace" doesn't describe the conversation; Minehan would prefer a "middle of the road" sentence 3), plus Hoenig wanting a faster pace and the Yellen/Ferguson productivity-risk difference.
- Broad agreement, no dissents.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS — 42 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Minehan: "the proliferation of hedge funds and the narrowness of credit spreads suggest that liquidity abounds, which could have its obvious downside"; Kohn: house prices "astonishing ... a much fatter tail on the side of a considerable softness", bond yields, asset-price policy approach; Bernanke: "A panicky flight from the dollar ... would be very harmful"; Guynn: house-price run-up "deserve[s] further study"; Olson: condo lending; Santomero: CRE prices "inconsistent with the underlying fundamentals".
- Several participants address it meaningfully, not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE — 72 ±6 (High)**
- "Measured pace" retained with minimal statement changes; Poole: "the expectation I think we all have that another 25 basis points may be appropriate at the next meeting"; Geithner: "a significant positive slope to the path of the fed funds rate"; Guynn: "I hope we will not try to signal that we may soon pause"; Moskow: "too early to signal an imminent pause". Kohn wants future flexibility ("taking a break from time to time").
- Clear signal of further gradual increases.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED — 40 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "our forecasting is somewhat befogged"; Moskow: "a great deal of uncertainty about the size of resource gaps"; Minehan: "a very unpleasant, though possibly unforecastable, surprise"; Kohn: "a lot of uncertainty about structural productivity"; Pianalto: "more challenging than usual"; Gramlich: "there's an iceberg out there". Offsets: Poole "the expansion is not fragile"; Yellen "more confident"; Stern "things have been turning out pretty much as we expected".
- Somewhat elevated, specific to productivity/slack and imbalances.
