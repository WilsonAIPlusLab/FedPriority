# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2005-05-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, a break, then the policy go-around (Chair's recommendation, then each member's view on the rate and the statement language), the roll-call read-out. Staff turns are absent; a few member turns that are only procedural (Lacker on Table 1 process, the Guynn/Poole/Gramlich exchange on how minutes and statement relate, sports banter) are ignored for conditions scoring but count as evidence for the communication/forward-guidance and dissent dimensions. No garbled passage prevented scoring. Only the last two speakers' remarks in the staff-presentation gap (Reinhart, Stockton) are missing by design.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2005, mid-way through a measured tightening cycle. A "soft patch" in activity (weak March/April spending, durable goods, investment) coincided with rising core inflation and high energy prices. The decision is a 25bp hike, with debate over keeping "measured pace" and the balance-of-risks sentence. I score what members say, not what I recall.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair; recommends +25bp, alternative B, argues soft patch is energy-driven and the last tightening move is usually a mistake), Geithner (Vice Chair; less confident, risks to inflation higher), Kohn, Gramlich, Olson, Bies, Ferguson (governors); presidents Moskow, Lacker, Minehan, Guynn, Stern, Fisher (new), Yellen, Pianalto, Hoenig, Santomero, Poole.

**4. Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see core inflation as above the comfortable level and rising (Lacker: core PCE 2.9% over three months; Bies: core PCE 2.6%, core CPI 3.3% over three months; Hoenig: "above comfortable levels"; Yellen: "uncomfortably and unexpectedly high"). Counterweights: 12-month rates still mid-1s to ~2%, many call the rise energy pass-through and transitory, wages restrained, pricing power mixed. Net above the midpoint, clearly but not extremely: about 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: the Greenbook gap is "near 1 percent" through 2006 (Lacker); Kohn "appreciable slack remains" and capacity utilization below its long-run average; Hoenig "sizable slack"; Yellen "at least some, possibly considerable, slack"; Stern "general lack of supply-side constraints". Against this, Guynn sees output "near or even above potential" and Moskow growth "above potential" in H2, which is forecast, not current. Net below potential: 44.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Yellen and Kohn see slack in labor markets; Poole "almost no case" of labor pressure except skilled niches; Stern "gradually tightening"; Hoenig labor market slack diminishing; Fisher oil-sector job tightness; Greenspan argues unemployment implies some tightness. Net slightly below tight: 46.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: soft patch; spending and durable goods weak in Feb-April, investment deceleration "disappointing" (Lacker, Kohn), consumer confidence slumped; yet growth still around 3.5% and investment fundamentals "strong". Net a bit below trend: 46.
- PRODUCTIVITY: "underlying trend in productivity continue[s] to support solid growth" (Moskow), "underlying trend in productivity is also solid" (Yellen), Geithner structural productivity strong; Greenspan sees a slowdown risk. Modestly above trend: 57.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: accommodative/supportive (Hoenig "highly accommodative"; Kohn "quite accommodative... a little tighter than they have been"; Bies "very supportive"; Stern "comfortable"; Geithner "credit conditions favorable"). Slightly tighter at the margin from risk repricing. Below 50: 29.
- TREASURY_STRESS: only yield-curve/TIPS/futures-path references, no dysfunction discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan: "risk spreads have widened from their low points, they still remain quite low"; Greenspan: last meeting the Committee wanted to confront "spreads were getting too narrow"; Geithner "modest widening"; Kohn "risk spreads have risen"; Olson notes debt spreads widening (GM/Ford downgrade discussion). Level stated as low, direction widening: level governs, below 50: 32.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: stock volatility and declines noted (Geithner, Kohn, Gramlich "sluggish") with no valuation-concern language; Geithner calls the repricing "welcome and healthy". Default region: 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: Olson: asset quality improved, "very little in the way of troubled assets", marginal credits may be rewritten; Hoenig anecdote on thin-equity condo lending. Healthy with minor concerns: 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: one passing reference by Gramlich ("The dollar drop may help"), implying recent depreciation. Bright line: depreciation gives < 50. Thin: 40, Low confidence.
- Sub-components: Goods (Bies: goods prices went from declining ~3% to about unchanged; Guynn: negative goods contribution reversed; Fisher retailer "prices have not risen; they've just stopped falling") roughly stable to firming: 52. Services (Guynn "nearly 3 percent"; Bies "a bit above 2 percent"): 58. Housing (Guynn: rental equivalence among the largest contributors): 58. Expectations (Moskow "fairly well anchored, some measures crept up slightly"; Geithner some measures above desirable; Kohn compensation declined): 56.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says above comfortable: matches. OUTPUT_GAP: evidence says slack remains, so below 50: matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says accommodative, so below 50: matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence says low level despite widening, so below 50: matches. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress; evidence says healthy, so below 50: matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: +25bp at this meeting, supported by every voter; "measured pace" retained; the broad center (Kohn) sees measured increases as best guess and rates "too low". Wings: Poole and Moskow open to faster; Gramlich, Minehan, Pianalto, Ferguson wary of overshooting and near a pause; Hoenig wants faster removal of accommodation. A continuing-tightening consensus tempered by softness: 62.
- Dissent: zero dissents and unanimous roll call (11 voters yes). But clear expressed reservations about the language: Minehan, Pianalto, Fisher, Poole (alternative D), Hoenig on dropping "measured"/shortening, Gramlich wanting column A, Lacker on process, Guynn uneasy. Disagreement is about communication, not about the rate. Moderate differences, reluctant supporters but all said they would support: 33.
- Financial stability emphasis: scattered: Minehan on "measured pace" encouraging inappropriate risk-taking and spreads; Hoenig and Guynn on condo/real-estate speculation; Olson on bank asset quality and debt spreads; Geithner/Kohn on risk repricing; Bies on SOX market reaction; Greenspan on spreads too narrow. Several mention it, none makes it the focus: 27.
- Forward guidance: Greenspan and most keep "measured pace" and the signal that rates will keep rising, but many want flexibility, a pause is priced by markets, and balance-of-risks sentence is to be retired later. Tightening signal with built-in flexibility: 64.
- Uncertainty: very widespread: Minehan ("vision of the future is now a bit cloudier"), Guynn ("think hard, very hard, about how much we really know"), Poole ("uncertainty ... has been rising", "length of our discussion" as evidence), Geithner ("less confident"), Kohn ("elevated the level of uncertainty ... as many of us have remarked"), Santomero (dispersion of paths wider), Ferguson ("quite certain that the baseline will be wrong"), Pianalto, Yellen. Central forecast still shared. Elevated, multiple participants: 60.

**7. Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP 44 (<50, slack language): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 29 (accommodative): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 32 (stated low level): ok. BANKING_STRESS 33 (healthy): ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 (depreciation mention): ok. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs LABOR 46 vs CONSUMPTION 46: within 15. FC 29 vs spreads 32: co-move. FC vs Treasury: N/A. Parent inflation 60 vs sub-components (52, 58, 58, 56, mean about 56): parent is somewhat higher because members weight the headline core readings; consistent within a few points.

**9. N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant describes Treasury market functioning, only yield-curve and futures-path expectations.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Yellen "Readings on overall and especially core inflation have been uncomfortably and unexpectedly high"; Hoenig core inflation "is above comfortable levels". Summary: core inflation clearly rising and above comfort, though many call it energy-driven and transitory. Direction: above neutral because core PCE ~2.6-2.9% over three months.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Kohn "appreciable slack remains in labor markets. Capacity utilization in manufacturing is still below its long-run average"; Lacker, Greenbook gap "near 1 percent to the end of 2006". Summary: members see a modest remaining gap expected to close slowly. Direction: below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 46 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen "at least some, and possibly considerable, slack remains in labor markets"; Poole "In almost no case ... does anyone report any pressures in the labor market". Summary: modest slack, wages restrained, pockets of skilled-labor tightness. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Lacker "deceleration in capital spending is particularly disappointing"; Moskow "Business spending continues to be solid". Summary: soft patch in spending and investment around a still-solid 3.5% growth trend. Direction: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Moskow "the underlying trend in productivity continue to support solid growth"; Yellen "The underlying trend in productivity is also solid". Summary: productivity seen as solid/above trend, with Greenspan flagging possible slowdown. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 29 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Hoenig "financial conditions that are, perhaps, highly accommodative"; Kohn "Financial conditions remain quite accommodative ... a little tighter than they have been". Summary: accommodative, marginally tighter on risk repricing. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No participant discussion of Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan "risk spreads have widened from their low points, they still remain quite low"; Geithner "modest widening in credit spreads". Summary: widening from compressed levels but still low. Direction: below neutral because the level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Geithner "the volatility in equity prices ... absorbed reasonably well"; Gramlich "The stock market is sluggish despite pretty decent earnings". Summary: price moves noted without valuation concern. Direction: at neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Olson "there is still very little in the way of troubled assets out there, particularly in C&I loans"; Olson "marginal credits may, in fact, be rewritten". Summary: healthy with minor watch-points. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-10 [Low].** Evidence: Gramlich "The dollar drop may help". Summary: single passing mention of a decline. Direction: below neutral (depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52.** Bies "goods prices have gone from declining at an almost 3 percent pace ... to being about unchanged now". **INFLATION_SERVICES: 58.** Guynn "services inflation, which has been rising at nearly 3 percent for the past year". **INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Guynn "the rental equivalence measure for housing" among the largest contributors. **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 56.** Moskow "some measures have crept up slightly"; Geithner "some measures of expectations ... are above the desirable level".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Kohn "we ought to tell the market that we still think rates are too low and will have to rise"; Greenspan "go 25 basis points, and maintain the language". Summary: broad consensus for another 25bp and continued measured tightening, with some wings each way. Direction: above balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-7 [High].** Evidence: unanimous roll call; Minehan "I'm starting to feel like Alice in Wonderland with this statement", Poole "I have an alternative D". Summary: no dissents, but several reluctant supporters and an open language/process dispute. Direction: moderate differences, short of a formal split.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 27 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan "encourage inappropriate risk-taking"; Hoenig on a condo deal with "$3 million of equity". Summary: several scattered mentions, never a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kohn "a measured pace of increase in the funds rate is still our best guess"; Yellen "the likely direction of the federal funds rate is up". Summary: tightening signal retained, flexibility and exit from the guidance debated. Direction: above neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-7 [High].** Evidence: Guynn "I heard an awful lot of uncertainty today"; Ferguson "quite certain that the baseline will be wrong; I just don't know in which direction". Summary: multiple members emphasize elevated uncertainty, but a shared central forecast remains.
