# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting date 2012-10-24

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Participant-only transcript discussion (staff turns removed). The file covers two sessions: (a) a long discussion of forward-guidance "thresholds", (b) the market/economic go-round, (c) the Oct 24 policy go-round on alternatives A/B/C, and (d) a discussion of the experimental consensus forecast and the SEP. A vote read-out closes the file. Staff answers are stripped, so some member questions appear without replies. That does not affect the scoring.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2012: unemployment about 7.8-7.9%, open-ended MBS purchases (about $85bn per month including the MEP) announced in September, calendar guidance of mid-2015, fiscal cliff and euro-area risk looming. I score what participants say, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Chair Bernanke: growth about 2%, core inflation "a bit below our target", output gap about 4%, risks balanced on inflation, downside risks on growth.
- Doves (Yellen, Williams, Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Raskin, Dudley, Tarullo, Stein) favor maintaining or extending accommodation and quantitative thresholds. Kocherlakota wants 6%/2.5%.
- Hawkish or skeptical (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, George, Pianalto, Powell, Lockhart, Bullard on thresholds) worry about inflation expectations, exit and financial stability. Only Lacker votes No.
- Economy: household sector strengthening (consumer sentiment, autos, housing); business sector weak (capex and hiring held back by uncertainty); Europe and China slowing.

**4. Directional statements (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as slightly below the 2% objective (Bernanke: core run rates 0.93% and 1.28%; Fisher: "a little bit below our 2 percent"; Raskin: core about 1.25%). A few see upside risk (Lacker, George). Below the midpoint, not concerning.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Bernanke cites a gap of 4.0%. Plosser questions the size of the gap but not its sign. "Substantial slack": about 30.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Loose. Unemployment about 7.8%; "a long way from maximum employment" (Raskin); "many years before we are back to full employment" (Rosengren).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net below trend. Households improving, business investment weak. GDP growth of about 2% is described as modest/sluggish.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched by Bernanke's forecast-error exercise (TFP growth lower than expected) and Plosser (potential output revised down). A brief mention, so below trend, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Supportive/accommodative. Lower mortgage and bond rates, higher stock prices, weaker dollar, strong demand for high yield; Powell's "reach for yield". Offsets: mortgage credit remains tight for lower-quality borrowers, risk premiums "remain high" (Bernanke summary). Net below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield reactions and prospective market-functioning caveats (Stein, Pianalto). No dysfunction. Normal market functioning, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Powell: high-yield and CCC deals on "bubble terms of 2005 to 2007", demand "far in excess of supply". MBS spreads compressed about 40bp. Dudley: Morgan Stanley CDS down to 200bp from 600bp. Against that, Kocherlakota and Bernanke say risk premiums remain high. Level is below normal for corporate credit, so below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Mixed and little valuation commentary. Kocherlakota: equity risk premium highest in 20 years while some observers think prices are "inexplicably high". Fisher notes the stock market is down 3.2% since the last meeting. Plosser reports concern about M&A multiples. Default near 50, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: "Pressures on large U.S. financial institutions have moderated" (Dudley, Bernanke summary); earnings and returns on equity still depressed; community banks "responsible" underwriters (Duke). Healthy with minor concerns, below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar fell after the September announcement (Rosengren: "weakening the dollar"; Yellen: "exchange rate declined"; Bernanke summary: "the dollar fell"). Weakening, so below 50.
- INFLATION sub-components: Goods are touched on (gas price spike reversing, commodity ex-energy flat, drought-related food increase, meat prices damped), net stable. Services and housing/shelter inflation are not discussed, so N/A. Expectations are discussed extensively: Cleveland measure anchored below 2%, TIPS breakevens up, Plosser and George concerned, Dudley and Yellen comfortable. Anchored at about target.

**5. Polarity verification.** INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure; evidence says slightly below target, so below 50 (matches). LABOR_TIGHTNESS: evidence says slack, so low (matches). DOLLAR: higher means strength; evidence says depreciation, so below 50 (matches). BANKING_STRESS: evidence says moderated, healthier, so below 50 (matches). CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; evidence says compressed or bubbly high-yield terms, so below 50 (matches).

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Alternative B (maintain open-ended QE3, mid-2015 guidance, "considerable time" language). No change today, but many doves (Yellen, Williams, Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Raskin) expect or want more purchases in December. Hawkish dissent from Lacker (no more purchases, no MBS). Plosser, Fisher, George, Powell, Pianalto voice concerns about more purchases. QE-era "how much more" debate puts this in the 20s.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Lacker). Reservations: Fisher ("was not supportive of QE3"), Plosser ("opposed"), George and Powell (concerns). Distinct camps on qualitative vs quantitative thresholds. Disagreement is about tactics and about QE efficacy, partly about assessment. Moderate, mid-40s.
- Financial stability: Several participants address it meaningfully (Fisher on froth and junk issuance, Powell on bubble terms and duration, Stein, George, Pianalto, Kocherlakota on instability, Plosser on exit, Duke on mortgage credit, Europe). Moderate to prominent but not dominant.
- Forward guidance: Strongest easing commitment category (date-based "through mid-2015", threshold 6.5%/2.5% proposals, "considerable time" after recovery). No tightening signal; thresholds discussed are accommodation-preserving.
- Uncertainty: Fiscal cliff, Europe, China, labor-force participation, natural rate and output gap measurement. "Uncertainty is rampant" (Fisher), "great deal of uncertainty" (Kocherlakota), "puzzling... jury is still out" (Yellen), "uncharted territory" (Duke). Elevated.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential gives under 50 (31, OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative/supportive gives under 50 (36, OK). CREDIT_SPREADS level reading is tight/compressed, under 50 (34, OK). BANKING_STRESS moderated/sound gives under 50 (40, OK). DOLLAR_STRENGTH depreciation gives under 50 (40, OK). TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, within 36-50 (38, OK).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 31 vs LABOR 26 (5 apart, OK). OUTPUT_GAP 31 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 41 (10 apart, OK; households stronger than the gap alone suggests). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 34 (co-move, OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS 38: normal times, consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 41 vs sub-components: goods 50, expectations 52, services and housing N/A. The parent sits below them because the core and PCE readings cited are below target.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no participant discusses services or shelter price inflation (house-price and rent comments are about the housing market, not shelter inflation).

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 41 ±5 [High]**
- Bernanke: "core inflation does remain a bit below our target"; Fisher: "a little bit below our 2 percent longer-term target; so inflation isn't the issue."
- Center of gravity: inflation at or slightly under 2% with contained core. Direction: below neutral because core run rates are about 1% to 1.3% and most see risks balanced.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 31 ±5 [High]**
- Bernanke: "the output gap would be 3.7 percent. The current estimate is 4.0 percent"; Rosengren: "it will still be many years before we are back to full employment."
- Center of gravity: substantial slack persists. Direction: below potential because a roughly 4% gap and "slack" are cited repeatedly.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 26 ±5 [High]**
- Raskin: "we are a long way from maximum employment"; Yellen: "I have seen no evidence of any substantial improvement in the outlook for the labor market"; Bernanke: output "unemployment... 8.0 percent."
- Center of gravity: unemployment about 7.8% with weak payroll gains. Direction: loose, well below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 41 ±6 [High]**
- Yellen: "Spending has picked up noticeably... data pertaining to the business sector reveals disconcerting trends"; Lacker: "business spending has been in the doldrums."
- Center of gravity: household strength is offset by weak capex. Direction: below trend overall (about 2% GDP).

**PRODUCTIVITY: 33 ±10 [Low]**
- Bernanke: potential-output shortfall "about half from TFP growth"; "if productivity was lower than expected, then unit labor costs were higher than expected." Plosser: "potential output was revised down."
- Center of gravity: productivity growth disappointing. Direction: below trend, only briefly addressed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Rosengren: policy is "lowering mortgage and bond rates, weakening the dollar, and increasing stock prices"; Powell: "deals are being done in the high-yield market on terms that are very similar to the bubble terms of 2005 to 2007."
- Center of gravity: broadly supportive, with pockets of tight mortgage credit and high risk premia. Direction: below neutral (accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low]**
- Yellen: "Nominal Treasury yields, in contrast, did not fall... I do not necessarily interpret this as a negative signal"; Stein: constraint of "not overly degrading market functioning in either the Treasury or the MBS market."
- Center of gravity: orderly Treasury market, with only prospective functioning caveats. Direction: normal functioning, no dysfunction cited.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 34 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Powell: "demand for leveraged loans and high-yield bonds is far in excess of supply"; Stein: "40-plus basis points on the option-adjusted spread of mortgages to Treasuries" move.
- Center of gravity: spreads compressed in corporate high-yield and mortgages, though Kocherlakota/Bernanke note risk premia generally remain high. Direction: below neutral by level reading.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Low]**
- Kocherlakota: "some observers view equity prices as being inexplicably high, [but] the equity risk premium is actually higher now than at any point in the past 20 years"; Fisher: "the stock market is actually down since we previously met by 3.2 percent."
- Center of gravity: no clear valuation view; opposing observations offset. Direction: about neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Dudley: "the pressures on some of the large, vulnerable U.S. financial institutions do appear to have moderated"; Dudley: Morgan Stanley CDS "about 200 basis points from levels that were over 600."
- Center of gravity: improving bank health with depressed returns and Europe tail risk. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Rosengren: policy affected "weakening the dollar"; Yellen: "the exchange rate declined following the announcement."
- Center of gravity: modest dollar depreciation after the September action. Direction: below neutral (weakening).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 50 [Moderate]**: Bernanke: gas prices "largely reversed"; commodity index "flat for the year"; George: drought food increase "moderate." Net stable.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**: Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**: Shelter/rent inflation not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52 ±5 [Moderate]**
- Pianalto: "expectations are anchored below 2 percent"; Plosser: "breakevens are near the high end of their historical ranges"; George: concern "further balance sheet expansion... could push longer-term expected inflation still higher."
- Center of gravity: anchored near target with a mild upward drift in market measures. Direction: about neutral to slightly above.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 27 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Williams: "we will need to continue our purchases of longer-term Treasury securities well past the end of the year"; Lacker: "I cannot support additional asset purchases at this time."
- Center of gravity: majority backs maximum accommodation with more QE expected, offset by a hawkish dissent and several skeptics. Direction: dovish; QE-era band with some hawkish resistance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 45 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Vote: Lacker No (one formal dissent). Plosser: "I was opposed to the new round of asset purchases... I can live with alternative B"; Fisher: "I was not supportive of QE3."
- Center of gravity: one dissent, several reluctant supporters, clear camps on thresholds. Direction: moderate to notable disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Powell: "we look like we are blowing a fixed-income duration bubble right across the credit spectrum"; Fisher: "We may be getting a little bit of froth"; Pianalto: larger LSAP "could present financial stability risks."
- Center of gravity: several participants address it meaningfully, not dominant. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 ±4 [Moderate]**
- Yellen: "I think we should continue to communicate that we will maintain a highly accommodative stance of policy for a considerable period after the economic recovery strengthens"; Tarullo/Dudley/Yellen/Raskin: back 6.5%/2.5% thresholds that preserve the mid-2015 accommodation.
- Center of gravity: strongest easing commitment (date or thresholds, "lower for longer"). Direction: far on the easing side; some members (Lacker) object to the "considerable time" language.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Fisher: "uncertainty is rampant and is hampering decisionmaking"; Kocherlakota: "I see a great deal of uncertainty about our rate of progress to our dual mandate objectives"; Bernanke summary: "Uncertainty and risks remain high."
- Center of gravity: many participants stress fiscal cliff, Europe/China and measurement uncertainty. Direction: elevated.
