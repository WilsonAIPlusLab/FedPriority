# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2004-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening procedural remarks, members' questions during the staff Q&A (reserve-market operations, current-account/exchange-rate exchange), the economic go-around, Chairman's policy remarks, the policy go-around ("I support...") and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. I scored only members' own views. The Chairman's retirement tribute and the Committee-procedure items are ignored. The text has some broken lines from extraction but nothing garbled enough to hide meaning.

**Era context.** Third year of a recovery in which policy has been removing accommodation at a "measured pace" (funds rate 1.5% going to 1.75%). Backdrop in the text: a spring/summer "soft patch", oil near $50, the Greenbook writing down growth, and low core inflation. The scores follow what members say, not this backdrop.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), Geithner (Vice Chair), Governors Bernanke, Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kohn, Olson, and Presidents Moskow, Poole, McTeer, Minehan, Pianalto, Lacker, Stern, Santomero, Yellen, Hoenig, Guynn. Hoenig, Gramlich and others also spoke in the exchange-rate discussion.

**Dimension reasoning.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. McTeer notes core PCE projections "under 2 percent, with many... closer to 1 percent" and compares the pattern to the 2003 deflation worry. Yellen says the run-up "reflected temporary factors"; Bernanke says core inflation is moderating; Geithner sees core PCE at 1 to 1.5%. Against this, Guynn notes outside forecasts with an "upward tilt" and Santomero is "not as sanguine" as the Greenbook. Some input costs (steel, energy) and health care costs are high. Net: below target, not alarming. Score 31.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output below potential. Moskow: "output gap to remain sizable"; Minehan: "gaps will not close until... mid-2006"; Lacker and Ferguson: gap closes by end-2006; Yellen: "slack remaining in labor and product markets". The bright line (< 50) is satisfied. Moskow's "modestly faster than potential" growth describes closing the gap, not a level above potential. Score 37.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment "in the low to mid-5s" (Minehan) and 5.2% in the Third District. Members stress weak job growth ("not yet shown the kinds of gains") and "continued labor market slack" (Minehan). Offsets are skill shortages (Yellen, Santomero) and wage gains "a bit higher" (Moskow). Guynn says 100,000 jobs a month would hold unemployment steady. The level is below full employment but near it. Score 42.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net near trend or a little below. Consumer spending bounced back and equipment investment is helped by partial expensing, but members repeat "business caution", reluctance to invest (McTeer, Kohn, Ferguson) and a growth shortfall relative to the stimulus (Kohn). Growth of 3.5 to 4% is described as "solid but somewhat less vigorous than we had hoped" (Bernanke). Score 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Pianalto calls productivity gains "the most remarkable aspect of this expansion"; Minehan says it is "relatively strong"; Geithner says "still-impressive average of the '90s surge"; Stern cites productivity as a reason for optimism. The Chairman thinks it is "probably slowing at this stage". Net: above trend. Score 60.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Yellen: "persistently easy financial conditions"; Kohn: "very supportive financial conditions"; Hoenig's director: low rates and available capital make financing easy. Long rates have fallen (Gramlich). The bright line (< 50) is satisfied. Score 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants do not discuss dysfunction in the Treasury market. Long-term yield movements are only mentioned as yield levels. The reserve-market and funds-rate-deviation exchange concerns the Desk's mechanics, not Treasury market function. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The only direct mention is Bies: more rating upgrades "one of the reasons we've seen credit spreads come in" (narrowing, no level stated). Bies and Hoenig also say capital is easily available and affordable. That points to below-normal stress. Direction alone would only support 45 to 50; the availability language is modest level information. Score 42, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only Yellen's passing "increase in equity prices" and Moskow's "financial markets are not pricing in heightened risk". No valuation view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Kohn: "I'm not really worried about the financial system... it's pretty robust". Bies: more upgrades than downgrades, companies stronger. Brief. The bright line (sound means < 50) is satisfied. Score 30, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Participants describe the dollar as "roughly stable all year" (Bernanke) and "broadly stable" (Geithner), after an earlier decline (Minehan: "the decline in the dollar, however small"; Yellen: "the decline in the dollar... over the past year"). The long exchange discussion is mostly about the risk of a future dollar fall (Gramlich: "if the dollar started slipping"; Greenspan: prefers euro-dollar at 145 instead of 122). Direction is stable to modestly weaker, so below 50. Score 40, Moderate confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS: high steel and energy input costs but firms "unable to pass higher costs on" (Moskow): 47. SERVICES: health care costs mentioned as an exception but no real services-price discussion: N/A. HOUSING: only house-price appreciation, not shelter inflation: N/A. EXPECTATIONS: "long-term inflation expectations remain steady" (Santomero), "well anchored" (McTeer): 48.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Every voter supports a 25bp hike and an unchanged statement ("measured pace"). Many flag that a pause may soon be appropriate (McTeer, Minehan, Yellen, Ferguson, Kohn "burden of proof... should not be unusually high" for skipping, Gramlich). Guynn and Santomero lean to continued withdrawal, and Greenspan leans against signaling a pause. A hike with real caution about the next step: lean hawkish to moderate hawkish, 58.
- Dissent: There were no dissents and all participants voted yes. Differences are about emphasis on a possible pause and on the exchange-rate and reserve-operations tangents (Greenspan versus Kohn/Minehan/Geithner on the Desk, a mild exchange). Minor emphasis differences only: 16.
- Financial stability: Extended discussion of the unsustainable current-account deficit and a disorderly dollar, plus Greenspan's concern about duration, the carry trade and mortgage refinancing, Minehan's financial-stability remark and Guynn's concern about "unintended consequences and distortions". It is a meaningful topic but not dominant: 45.
- Forward guidance: The signal is to keep removing accommodation at a measured pace, with Greenspan's explicit "lean against conveying the presumption that we will pause", though flexibility and a possible pause are widely mentioned. Mild tightening bias: 62.
- Uncertainty: Kohn ("another way of labeling our ignorance", "we just don't have full understanding"), Geithner (greatest sources of uncertainty), Ferguson ("range of uncertainty is still quite large"), Bernanke ("very tentative"), Minehan (employment "more a hope than a reality"). Against this, Poole ("less happened"), Guynn ("ho-hum") and Pianalto (risks balanced and narrowed). Mixed but several emphasize uncertainty: 50.

**Polarity checks.** Higher = more of the construct. OUTPUT_GAP 37 (below potential, matches evidence of slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 (accommodative, matches). BANKING_STRESS 30 (sound, matches). DOLLAR 40 (flat/weaker, matches). CREDIT_SPREADS 42 (favorable, matches). INFLATION 31 (below target, matches).

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (slack): met. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (easy/supportive): met. CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 (availability favorable, spreads "come in"): met, with low confidence because no level was stated. BANKING_STRESS < 50: met. DOLLAR < 50 (depreciation/stable, no appreciation): met. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 37 and LABOR 42 differ by 5. OUTPUT_GAP 37 and CONSUMPTION 47 differ by 10 (within 15; spending is near trend while the level of output is still below potential). FC 30 and CREDIT_SPREADS 42 co-move (both below 50). The Treasury link is not applicable. INFLATION 31 is consistent with goods 47 and expectations 48 (the parent is lower because core measures are well below target); the goods and expectations scores reflect mixed or steady readings, not stress.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, EQUITY_VALUATION, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed in any scorable way by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 31 ±6 [High].** Evidence: McTeer: "all of the inflation projections are for core PCE to come in under 2 percent, with many of the projections centered closer to 1 percent"; Yellen: "the run-up in inflation last winter reflected temporary factors." Summary: inflation is seen as low and below target with a few hawkish caveats. Direction: below neutral because members describe low, moderating core inflation.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 37 ±5 [High].** Evidence: Moskow: "our forecast is for the output gap to remain sizable for some time"; Minehan: gaps "will not close until later in the forecast time period—mid-2006". Summary: slack remains and closes only in 2006. Direction: below neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Minehan: "continued labor market slack... unemployment in the low to mid-5s"; Yellen: "we still haven't seen real strength in job growth". Summary: slack with unemployment above 5%, tempered by skill shortages. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Bernanke: recovery "solid but somewhat less vigorous than we had hoped"; Kohn: shortfall "in business investment spending... 'business caution.'" Summary: growth near trend, with weak business investment offsetting rebounding consumers. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Pianalto: "the ability of businesses to continue to squeeze out more production with the same number of workers"; Geithner: "still-impressive average of the '90s surge." Summary: productivity is seen as strong. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Yellen: "persistently easy financial conditions"; Kohn: "very supportive financial conditions." Summary: members see easy, supportive conditions. Direction: below neutral (looser than normal).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a market-function issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Bies: ratings upgrades are "one of the reasons we've seen credit spreads come in"; Bies: "keep capital within easy access and affordable." Summary: spreads narrowing and capital readily available, but no explicit level. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Only passing mentions of price movement.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Kohn: "I'm not really worried about the financial system... it's pretty robust"; Bies: "companies are stronger." Summary: the system is sound; brief. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bernanke: "The dollar has been roughly stable all year"; Minehan: "the decline in the dollar, however small, that has already occurred." Summary: stable to modestly weaker, with concern about a future slide. Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 47.** Moskow: high steel and input costs but "most firms have been unable to pass higher costs on to consumers."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Santomero: "long-term inflation expectations remain steady."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan: "I recommend a 25 basis point increase"; Kohn: "Tightening today is called for," but with a low burden of proof for skipping. Summary: unanimous support for another measured hike, with many flagging a possible pause. Direction: above neutral (lean hawkish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 16 ±5 [High].** Evidence: every participant "I support your recommendation"; vote read-out all Yes. Summary: no dissents and only emphasis differences (pause timing, Guynn's hawkish caveat, the Desk-procedure exchange). Direction: near consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Geithner: external imbalance leaves us "more vulnerable to a shock to confidence"; Greenspan: carry trade and duration could "create some significant collateral damage in the balance sheets of American businesses." Summary: a meaningful subtopic (external imbalance, mortgage duration) but not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "we have to lean against conveying the presumption that we will pause"; Pianalto: "continue with our plan to gradually remove our policy accommodation." Summary: measured further tightening signaled, with flexibility. Direction: above neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kohn: "we just don't have full understanding of what is damping the response of the economy"; Ferguson: "the range of uncertainty is still quite large." Summary: several members stress unexplained weakness, while others call the period predictable. Direction: moderately elevated.
