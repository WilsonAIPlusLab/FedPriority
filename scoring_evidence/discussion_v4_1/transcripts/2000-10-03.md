# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2000-10-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It opens with a foreign-exchange intervention discussion (ratification of euro purchases; Poole, Broaddus, Greenspan, McDonough, McTeer), then staff Q&A, the economic go-around (Parry, Guynn, Broaddus, Santomero, McTeer, Moskow, Jordan, Hoenig, Minehan, Stern, McDonough, Poole, Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley), the Chairman's summary, the policy go-around (everyone supports no change, retain the inflation-tilted balance-of-risks statement), and a late SOMA/Treasury-debt-paydown discussion. Several staff answers and some questions are missing, so some passages are fragmentary (e.g., "You are saying that ?"). The vote read-out is unanimous.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2000. The funds rate is 6-1/2 percent after a tightening cycle that began June 1999; growth is slowing from a very strong pace; unemployment is about 4 percent; oil and energy prices are high. I score the text, not my recall of the period.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Nearly all describe a moderating economy. Retail and housing are softer, and manufacturing is mixed.
- Labor markets are still very tight (Broaddus, Moskow, Stern, Minehan, Guynn, Poole).
- Core inflation is flat to creeping up (Parry, Santomero, Guynn), with headline CPI about 3-1/2 percent from energy (Poole).
- Most see inflation risks as still tilted up, and want to keep the tilt. Hoenig would accept a balanced statement. Poole sees "about equal probability" the next move is up or down.
- Productivity is a major theme: Greenbook structural productivity of 2.9 to 3.7 percent (Minehan), the NAIRU simulations (Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer), and Greenspan's skepticism of the productivity numbers.

**4. Directional statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly to clearly above the midpoint. Core is "as high as 2 percent" and "may be rising" (Parry), core "has also shown a moderate acceleration" (Santomero), and headline CPI is around 3-1/2 percent. Offsetting this, core is not accelerating much and some report easing retail prices (Hoenig, McDonough). I score it at 62.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint. Meyer says the economy is moving "from above-trend to below-trend growth", with the unemployment rate "below the short-run NAIRU". Jordan notes the Greenbook has the economy "somewhat above" potential (he is skeptical). Growth is now slowing to or below potential (Ferguson, McDonough), so the level is above potential but narrowing. I score it at 57 (>50 per the bright line).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well above the midpoint. Unemployment is about 4 percent (national 4.1 percent), and participants cite "poaching" with 10 percent wage raises (Broaddus), Moskow's labor shortages, and "labor markets are tight". The rubric says UE below 4% should be above 55, and the language is strong. I score it at 70.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly above trend but decelerating. Retail sales are slowing (Stern, McDonough, Jordan, Santomero), but Gramlich says there is "a very strong investment boom" and Hoenig says "investment spending remains very good". The net is a modest positive, 55.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Well above trend. Members note "recent very large productivity gains" (Guynn), Greenbook structural growth of 3.7 percent, and a "productivity revolution... continuing" (McDonough). Greenspan is skeptical but acknowledges the numbers are accelerating. I score it at 72.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Somewhat tight. Minehan says "Tighter financial conditions are likely a healthy development" and "spreads rising for lower quality credit". Guynn reports "financing terms are now more stringent", and Ferguson says credit conditions tighten. Also a flat or lower stock market and a higher dollar. No one calls conditions accommodative. I score it at 58.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Greenspan's questions on swap spreads and 30-year supply expectations, with no dysfunction. The staff answers are removed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only (Minehan: spreads rising for lower quality credit). No level is stated, so the rule is 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence. I score it at 54.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Price moves and "violent reaction to earnings disappointment" (Santomero), a "flat stock market" (Hoenig), and Greenspan's "bizarre stock price movements". There is no valuation commentary, so I default to the midpoint, 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. McDonough reports "falling delinquency rates--a banking supervisor's dream". Against that, Jordan reports rising late payments and delinquencies in Ohio, narrower margins, and tightening standards, and Guynn says banks and examiners see no telltale CRE trouble. The system is sound with pockets of tightening, so I score it slightly below 50, at 44.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: A stronger dollar is the stated background: "a higher dollar" (Parry), "a stronger dollar" (McTeer, Hoenig), "continued strength of the dollar" (McTeer), set against euro weakness. Appreciation means above 50. I score it at 62.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence of rising core and 3.5% headline matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter, and the evidence matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means wider, and the evidence matches (low-quality spreads rising). Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means stronger, and the evidence matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: unanimous vote for no change at 6-1/2 percent, keeping the inflation-tilted risk statement. Hoenig and Poole voice a balanced or symmetric view, but Poole explicitly argues against changing the language. This is a hold with a tightening bias, so lean hawkish, 56.
- Dissent: zero dissents and a unanimous vote. Mild emphasis differences (Hoenig on balanced risks, Broaddus vs. McTeer on productivity and inflation). A sharp but non-policy exchange on FX intervention (Poole, Broaddus vs. Greenspan and McDonough) was resolved by approving the ratification without objection. Score 20.
- Financial stability: Limited. FX intervention, spreads and leverage (Minehan), credit quality of investors (Ferguson), CRE (Guynn), bank lending terms (Jordan). Context only, 28.
- Forward guidance: The "upward bias" is retained, and Greenspan says "risks clearly remain on the up side." But the near-term message is on hold, with a possible shift to balanced "as we get into next year" (McDonough). Mild tightening bias, 58.
- Uncertainty: Minehan says "more than the usual sense of uncertainty", Greenspan "something very unusual is happening" and "choosing the appropriate forecast is clearly quite problematic", and Meyer notes "considerable uncertainty" about NAIRU. Multiple participants stress it, but not Knightian. Score 58.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 57 (> 50, above potential/trend language present). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 (> 50; no "accommodative" language). CREDIT_SPREADS 54 (direction only, within 45-55, widening side). BANKING_STRESS 44 (< 50, sound). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 (> 50, appreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 57 vs LABOR 70: a 13-point gap, within 15. Labor tightness is about the level of unemployment, while output is already decelerating to or below potential growth. OUTPUT_GAP 57 vs CONSUMPTION 55: aligned. FC 58 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 54: co-moving, both mildly toward tight. Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 62 vs sub-components (goods 57, housing 58, expectations 58): roughly consistent, with wage and energy pressure lifting the parent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning remarks beyond a swap-spread/supply question. INFLATION_SERVICES: no discussion of services prices as such (wage costs and anecdotes only).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 62 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "I don't think we should be content with core inflation measures as high as 2 percent, especially given the likelihood that they may be rising"; Poole, "the rise in the aggregate CPI over the past 12 months is now running around 3-1/2 percent". Summary: core is near or above 2 percent and creeping up, headline is higher on energy, and most see risks tilted up. Direction: above neutral because core and headline are above target and expected to drift higher.

**OUTPUT_GAP 57 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer, "the economy appears to be making a transition from above-trend to below-trend growth" and "unemployment rate is below the short-run NAIRU"; Jordan, the baseline has "the economy... operating somewhat above some notion of our national potential". Summary: the level is modestly above potential while growth cools. Direction: above neutral because the unemployment rate is below NAIRU and members refer to above-trend and above-potential operation.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 70 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, one employer "raised wage rates across the board by about 10 percent to stop... poaching"; Moskow, "problems of finding and retaining quality workers". Summary: unemployment near 4 percent with widespread shortage reports. Direction: well above neutral because of the very low unemployment rate and repeated shortage anecdotes.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 55 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "a significant slowing in consumer spending"; Gramlich, "There is still a very strong investment boom in progress". Summary: spending is cooling from a strong pace while investment stays firm. Direction: slightly above neutral because investment remains strong, partly offset by softer consumption.

**PRODUCTIVITY 72 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Minehan on the Greenbook structural productivity rise "from 2.9 to 3.7 percent"; McDonough, "the productivity revolution is continuing". Summary: participants treat very high productivity growth as the central supply-side fact, even though Greenspan doubts it can keep accelerating. Direction: above neutral because gains are well above the historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "Tighter financial conditions are likely a healthy development"; Guynn, "financing terms are now more stringent, especially for second-tier borrowers". Summary: credit tightening for weaker borrowers, a soft equity market, a stronger dollar and the funds rate at 6-1/2 percent make conditions somewhat tight. Direction: above neutral because members describe tightening, with no mention of accommodative conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** No Treasury market dysfunction discussed by participants.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 54 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "credit markets seem to be distinguishing among borrowers, with spreads rising for lower quality credit". Summary: direction only, with no level stated. Direction: slightly above neutral per the direction-only rule (a clear widening for low-quality credit at 54, bounded to 45-55).

**EQUITY_VALUATION 50 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Santomero, "Equity market investors... violent reaction to earnings disappointment"; Hoenig, "the flat stock market". Summary: price and sentiment remarks with no valuation judgment. Direction: at neutral because there is no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS 44 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "weakening loan demand, tightening credit standards, and falling delinquency rates--a banking supervisor's dream"; Jordan, "proposals are now being turned down that would have been accepted a year ago". Summary: the system looks sound, with some tightening and local delinquency pockets. Direction: below neutral because delinquencies are low overall and no one describes strained banks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "a higher dollar in combination with our past tightening actions"; McTeer, "the continued strength of the dollar". Summary: members treat the dollar as strong and appreciating. Direction: above neutral because of the stated dollar strength (the euro's weakness is the flip side).

**INFLATION_GOODS 57.** Fuel and energy costs are being passed through (Poole on FedEx surcharges; Jordan on diesel), while retail and manufacturers' output prices are steady (Santomero, Hoenig, McDonough).
**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING 58.** Local home prices up 10-15 percent or more (Minehan, McDonough) but decelerating, and rents mixed (Parry).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 58.** Santomero, "inflationary expectations are a real concern going forward"; McDonough sees "considerable risk of the higher energy prices increasing inflation expectations"; offset by steady SPF 10-year readings.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 56 +-5 (High).** Evidence: McDonough, "keep the fed funds at its current level and... retain the statement that the balance of risks is on the inflation side"; Hoenig, "we could go to a more balanced stance. But we can wait." Summary: an unambiguous hold with a retained tightening bias, a small minority leaning toward balanced. Direction: slightly hawkish of neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 +-5 (High).** Evidence: all voting members "support the recommendation"; unanimous roll call. Summary: broad agreement on policy with only mild differences on the risk language and on FX intervention. Direction: low disagreement because there are zero dissents and the only reservations were mild.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 28 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "Corporate leverage is growing"; Ferguson, "about a quarter of it is done by firms that are not investment grade". Summary: a few members raise credit and leverage as context, not as a focus. Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 58 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the risks clearly remain on the up side as far as inflation is concerned"; McDonough, "there will be a question as to whether our assessment of the balance of risks will have to be shifted" next year. Summary: the tightening bias is kept, but no action is signaled soon and a shift to balanced is pending. Direction: mildly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 58 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "more than the usual sense of uncertainty... 13 alternative simulations"; Greenspan, "choosing the appropriate forecast is clearly quite problematic". Summary: many stress uncertainty over oil, productivity and NAIRU, but without Knightian language. Direction: elevated.
