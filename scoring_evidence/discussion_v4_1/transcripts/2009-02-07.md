# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting date 2009-02-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** This is a member/participant transcript, but not a standard go-around. It is a short, Saturday, joint FOMC-Board briefing and Q&A (about 1,690 lines, roughly 17-18k words) in which the Chair walks through a Treasury "financial stabilization plan" that the Treasury Secretary is due to announce two days later. The plan has bank scrubs and stress tests, a convertible capital facility, an "aggregator bank", an FDIC guarantee extension, a Government Investment Board and bank conditions. It also covers the Fed's role: expanding the TALF (asset-backed securities loan facility) from $200 billion to up to $1 trillion, and legislation to let the Fed sterilize balance-sheet expansion. The Chair says at the outset that no formal action is expected. There is no economic go-around, no policy-rate discussion, no policy go-around and no vote. Staff turns are removed, so the Vice Chairman (Dudley, a New York Fed official who speaks as a participant) appears and is treated as a participant.

**2. Era context (not used to override the text).** Depth of the 2008-09 financial crisis. The text itself supports that backdrop: the Chair cites "600,000 jobs lost in January", "the deepest part of the crisis" and the economy falling "into the abyss".

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Bernanke presents the plan. He calls it "tough love" and truth-telling, sees the securitization markets as shut down, and sees deleveraging as bringing down aggregate demand and creating "enormous excess capacity".
- Dudley supplements the Chair and explains the TALF and the messaging.
- Duke, Fisher, Warsh, Pianalto, Bullard and Kohn worry about the market reaction, the vagueness, runs or a "slow bleed" at mid-sized banks, and communication.
- Plosser asks about sterilization and the aggregator bank.
- Lacker is skeptical about incentives in the aggregator bank, TALF loss analysis, and Fed independence and credit policy.
- Hoenig objects that TALF "further misallocate[s] resources" and would prefer to minimize Fed involvement rather than "accelerate it up to $1 trillion".
- Evans, Lockhart, Stern and Tarullo contribute questions or nothing of substance.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: not discussed as conditions. The Chair mentions "encouraging discussions... on an inflation objective" only as a governance point. N/A. Inflation sub-components and expectations: N/A, same reason.
- OUTPUT_GAP: participants (the Chair, the only one to address it) see the economy as far below potential: "enormous excess capacity in the economy", "deepest part of the crisis", "fall into the abyss". Below 50; GFC-trough anchor 15-22. Evidence is brief and from one speaker, so confidence is Low. Score 18.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: participants see the labor market as extremely slack: "600,000 jobs lost in January... an enormously serious economic situation". Far below 50; hemorrhaging jobs. Score 15, Low confidence (one speaker, one data point, but strong language).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: the Chair says deleveraging "bring[s] down aggregate demand"; Hoenig refers to "pain" in the adjustment; Kohn stresses that credit to households and businesses is impaired. There is no direct description of spending. Below 50, a sharp pullback implied. Score 22, Low confidence, wide band.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: participants see conditions as very tight and impaired: "shutdown of the securitization markets", "credit markets are just not working very well", banks cannot raise private equity, a coming CRE finance crunch, and "ease financial conditions" as the goal of TALF. Some offsets: Kohn says the CDS/equity/liquidity dynamic was broken by TLGP and Fed facilities, and Warsh says facility improvements have been seen. Above 50, near crisis. Score 82.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Fisher asks only about the size of net Treasury issuance and its yield-curve effect, and whether the Fed has any implied commitment on the long end. No dysfunction is described. Not meaningfully discussed as stress. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: the Chair describes a "huge bid-asked spread", "huge liquidity premium", expected returns "very high, because of liquidity and risk premia", and shut securitization markets. Kohn mentions CDS spreads. These are stated high levels, so the score must be above 50. Score 80, Low confidence (no spread figures; qualitative).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Pianalto: bank stocks "hit extremely hard" on speculation; Duke: equity prices at "very low levels" trigger public concern; Kohn expects an "extremely negative" equity reaction to the plan. The text expresses concern about depressed bank equity, not a valuation judgment. I score the concern view moderately above neutral and keep confidence Low. Score 66.
- BANKING_STRESS: participants see severe, systemic stress: possible insolvency at the two largest "problem" banks, government majority ownership, a 22-bank stress scrub, Duke's fear of "runs on uninsured liabilities", and Kohn's "slow bleed". This is the opposite of "well-capitalized"; above 50. Score 83.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher means more of the named construct in every case. Banking stress (83): evidence says severe stress, so a high score matches. Credit spreads (80): evidence says very wide, so a high score matches. Output gap (18) and labor (15): evidence says deeply slack, so low scores match. Financial conditions (82): evidence says tight and impaired, so a high score matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: there is no rate discussion. The only policy content is expansion of the balance sheet to "stimulate the economy and ease financial conditions" (TALF up to $1 trillion plus GSE purchases, "very substantially supportive"). This is a QE-style easing and credit-support posture, so strongly dovish. Offsets: Hoenig and Lacker push back, and the sterilization and exit emphasis is a caution, not a tightening signal. No vote, so no endorsement count. Lean about 20.
- Disagreement: no formal vote and no dissent. Hoenig voices a substantive economic objection to TALF expansion, and Lacker questions the incentives in the aggregator bank, TALF loss analysis and Fed independence. Evans retreats from his earlier concern ("feel much better"). Most others concur and focus on communications. Notable differences on framework, no dissents: about 30.
- Financial stability attention: this is the whole subject of the meeting. About 95.
- Forward guidance: no rate guidance at all. The balance-sheet signal is continued credit easing ("up to $1 trillion", "I'm hopeful this would be the last key initiative") with a commitment to unwind and sterilize. The Chair's "inflation objective" aside is not guidance. Mild easing lean, with very low confidence and a wide band.
- Uncertainty: very high. Examples are "ready, fire, aim", "who knows", "we don't know whether it will work", "I've been wrong every time as the economy has deteriorated", "the crisis seems to be going in unpredictable ways", and a "very harsh reaction" to be prepared for. Most of it is about program execution and market reaction, not the macro outlook, so I place it below the March 2009 trough anchor. About 76.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_PRESSURE and the four sub-components, PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH were not raised by participants as conditions assessments. Treasury issuance was raised only as a quantity question.

**Special-topic rule: judgment call, flagged prominently.** The rubric says special-topic meetings (balance sheet, framework, operations) with no standard economic assessment should be scored ALL N/A. This meeting is a special-topic briefing on Treasury's bank plan and TALF/sterilization, with no go-around or policy decision, so the rule is arguably triggered. I did not apply it in full because the transcript does contain direct participant statements on bank health, credit-market functioning, financial conditions and (briefly, from the Chair) the labor market and slack, which map onto the scored dimensions. I therefore scored what the text supports, with Low confidence and wide bands, and flagged the anomaly. If the pipeline wants strict treatment, every line in Part 3 should be masked to N/A except the flag. ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: N/A
Not discussed as a condition. The Chair's passing reference to "an inflation objective" is a governance point.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 18 +-8 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the deleveraging process has a side effect, which is to bring down aggregate demand and to create enormous excess capacity in the economy"; Bernanke: "we are in what you could very well call war time"; "if the Fed stood by and let the economy fall into the abyss".
- Center of gravity: the Chair, the only participant to speak to slack, describes deep and large excess capacity amid the crisis.
- Direction confirmation: output gap is below neutral because "enormous excess capacity" implies far below potential.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 15 +-8 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "there were 600,000 jobs lost in January. This is an enormously serious economic situation, and it requires unusual responses." Repeated: "remember, as I said—600,000 jobs."
- Center of gravity: severe job losses, with massive layoffs implied, set the market near the hemorrhaging end.
- Direction confirmation: labor tightness is far below neutral because the level of slack implied by 600,000 monthly job losses is very large.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 22 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: deleveraging will "bring down aggregate demand"; Hoenig: "there is going to be pain with that [shifting of resources]"; Kohn: "If we want credit flowing to households and businesses, a lot of it has to flow through the securitization market."
- Center of gravity: no direct spending readings, but participants frame demand as contracting under deleveraging and credit shortage.
- Direction confirmation: spending and investment are below trend because aggregate demand is described as falling and credit to households and businesses as impaired.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 82 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "one of the big problems is the shutdown of the securitization markets"; "the credit markets are just not working very well. There are all kinds of informational and capital and other issues". Dudley: TALF is about "help[ing] market functioning improve and eas[ing] financial conditions".
- Center of gravity: several participants describe broken securitization markets, capital-constrained banks and a looming CRE crunch, only partly offset by the effect of Fed facilities.
- Direction confirmation: financial conditions are well above neutral (tight) because credit markets are described as impaired and in need of easing.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
Fisher asked about net issuance and its effect on the yield curve; no market dysfunction was described.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 80 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "such a huge bid–asked spread... this huge liquidity premium"; "rate of expected return is now very high, because of liquidity and risk premia". Kohn: dynamic "between the CDS spreads and the equity market and liquidity".
- Center of gravity: premia are described as very high and securitization as shut, but no spread numbers are given.
- Direction confirmation: spreads are above neutral because the stated level of liquidity and risk premia is "huge" and "very high".

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 66 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Pianalto: bank "stocks are getting hit extremely hard, because of some assumptions that are being made"; Duke: stock prices "particularly to the very low levels that they've reached recently" trigger concern.
- Center of gravity: concern over depressed bank equity and its signaling effect, with no valuation judgment (fair or cheap); scored as concern.
- Direction confirmation: equity concern is above neutral because members worry about sharply depressed bank stock prices and the reaction to the plan.

### BANKING_STRESS: 83 +-6 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "This capital process is going to end up with substantial government minority shares... and majority shares in a few"; Duke: "runs on uninsured liabilities at the banks... violent reaction". Kohn: for marginal institutions, "the 'slow bleed'".
- Center of gravity: many participants discuss insolvent or near-insolvent large banks, forced recapitalization, and run risk.
- Direction confirmation: banking stress is far above neutral because participants discuss possible insolvency, government ownership and run contingency planning.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
Not discussed.

### INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A
Not discussed.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "ways to use our balance sheet to stimulate the economy and ease financial conditions"; "this commitment plus the GSE purchases puts us in a range which is very substantially supportive". Counterpoint, Hoenig: "if we can develop ways that would minimize our involvement, rather than continuing to accelerate it up to $1 trillion, I think we would be well served".
- Center of gravity: the only live policy matter is a large balance-sheet expansion (QE-style credit easing) that most support, with two sceptics; no rate decision.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is dovish because the presence of a major easing programme is the signal and the objections are about risk and independence, not about tightening.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-10 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Hoenig: TALF "does further misallocate resources"; Lacker: "I'm a little puzzled about the incentives in the aggregator bank" and "we might compromise something that might be more workable"; Bernanke: "I'm less concerned than you are".
- Center of gravity: no vote and no formal dissent; two members make substantive objections, while most others endorse the approach and press on communications.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is above near-consensus because two participants raise framework-level objections, but below notable because there are no formal dissents or camps.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 95 +-5 [High confidence]
- Evidence: the entire meeting is the bank stabilization plan (stress scrub, capital facility, aggregator bank, TLGP extension, TALF); Duke: "contingency plan if... there start being runs on uninsured liabilities".
- Center of gravity: financial stability is the subject of the meeting.
- Direction confirmation: emphasis is at the top of the range because the meeting is overwhelmingly about financial stability.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +-15 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I'm hopeful that this would be the last key initiative—but, who knows?"; "up to $1 trillion"; sterilization and unwind assurances ("responsibility to be able to unwind the programs in a timely way").
- Center of gravity: no rate guidance is given; the forward signal is continued balance-sheet support with an exit-readiness caveat.
- Direction confirmation: the tone is below neutral because further credit easing is signaled and no tightening is, but the evidence is indirect.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 76 +-10 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Fisher: "one more uncertain step being taken"; "a 'ready, fire, aim' approach". Bernanke: "I've been wrong every time as the economy has deteriorated". Kohn: "We don't know whether it will work."
- Center of gravity: many participants stress that outcomes of the plan, the market reaction and the economy are unpredictable, mostly regarding execution and communication.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is far above normal because multiple participants emphasize unpredictability, though the focus is the plan more than the macro forecast.
