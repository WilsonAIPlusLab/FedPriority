# FOMC member-discussion scoring (v4.1) - meeting 1993-11-16 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript for a 1993 meeting. Staff turns are removed. About the first 3,500 of roughly 5,000 lines are a long procedural debate on whether and how to release FOMC tapes, transcripts and minutes (the Gonzalez request, FOIA, edited transcript vs MOD, 3 vs 5 year lag). That content is procedure, not conditions or policy, and I ignore it for scoring. It does matter for context: a few speakers (Syron, Keehn) say the disclosure issue is inhibiting candour. Next comes the foreign-currency and swap-line discussion and Desk-operation ratifications, which are also procedural. Then come members' questions to the staff. Then the economic go-around (Hoenig through Lindsey), the Chairman's summary, and the directive go-around. The vote read-out closes the file. The policy content is a short go-around plus "B symmetric" calls.

**Era context.** Late 1993, fed funds at 3 percent, unemployment about 6.8 percent (Syron cites a coming BLS redefinition that would push it above 7 percent), CPI inflation about 3 percent and drifting down, a fourth-quarter growth surge, and a 40bp rise in the long bond. I take only the text from participants.

**Speakers and views.**
- Almost all presidents report improving regional activity: strong housing, retail, autos, steel and manufacturing. Parry reports California weakness. Several (Hoenig, Forrestal, Broaddus, Melzer, Mullins, LaWare, Angell) see 3 percent or more growth, somewhat above the Greenbook.
- Boehne, Syron, Kelley, Phillips and Stern see a sawtooth, 2.5 to 3 percent path.
- On inflation, many say pressures are absent (Stern, Boehne, Keehn, Forrestal, Kelley: "Inflation is still falling"). Others say progress is too slow: Jordan ("unacceptable"), Angell, Melzer, Mullins (core about 3 percent). McDonough fears firms testing price increases early next year.
- On policy, everyone says "B symmetric", but Melzer wants a bias toward restraint, Angell wants the funds rate at 4 percent, Mullins raises moving "on the short end" if the long bond keeps rising, and Broaddus has "sympathy" for Melzer. Kelley says "steady as she goes". Greenspan calls symmetric no-change a "gimme putt".

**Directional statements and dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as about at or slightly below the midpoint. Most say no visible pressure and a falling trend. A minority say the 3 percent level is too high, and some fear upside risk. Net 46, because the level is still above price stability but the tone is disinflation. This is a mixed picture, so I use a wide band.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as slightly below potential. Kelley says "we still have slack in labor markets", Parry cites "labor market slack", and Mullins says "the mean is already a bit above potential" (that is growth, not the level). Melzer says growth was below potential in the first half and is now "at or above". Net 44, which satisfies the bright line (slack mentioned, so below 50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is about 6.8 percent and employment growth is "flat to very modest". There are pockets of shortage (Phoenix construction, parts of Melzer's District). The score is below 50: 36.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong retail, housing and autos, "investment boom" and rapid consumer spending. The concerns are that it outruns income and that the saving rate is low. Above trend but not boom: 59.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mullins ("productivity growth"), Phillips ("productivity kick", falling unit labor costs), Syron (producers focus on productivity). Greenspan's go-around remark that measured productivity could not have fallen is also supportive. Above trend: 60.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Phillips says no "bottlenecks of credit unavailability". McTeer says there is "not much talk of a credit crunch" and banks are aggressively seeking loans. Rates are low, and Mullins and Angell call policy accommodative. Against that, the long bond is up 40bp. Net 38 (supportive, so below 50).
- TREASURY_STRESS: The bond sell-off is discussed (Mullins, Broaddus, Angell) as an orderly repricing on expected growth and inflation, with no dysfunction. Score 44.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are never discussed directly. Only credit availability is (McTeer, Phillips): ample, with no crunch. I score on the availability side, below 50, with Low confidence: 36.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: LaWare says "values there are high enough and the market nervous enough" (a correction risk). Angell is "bullish", with no last bubble yet. Mixed to mildly elevated valuation (below 50 in this polarity): 42, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips says "the financial sector is strong", Melzer reports "higher bank earnings", and McTeer sees banks competing for loans. Parry reports California bank loan shrinkage. Healthy, so below 50: 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed, so N/A.

**Polarity checks.** Higher FC means tighter. Evidence is supportive credit, so FC is below 50, which matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress. Evidence is sound banks, so it is low, which matches. Higher LABOR means tighter. Evidence is 6.8 percent unemployment and slack, so it is low, which matches.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold with a symmetric directive. Several voices (Melzer, Angell, Mullins, McDonough, Jordan) lean toward eventual tightening. Nobody wants easing. Slightly hawkish hold, 54.
- Dissent: the vote read-out is 12 Yes with no formal dissent on policy. Melzer (non-voter) and Angell voice reservations about the stance. Angell dissented orally on the Gonzalez disclosure decision, which is procedural and not counted. 20.
- Financial stability attention: the bond-market backup, equity fragility (LaWare, Angell) and credit availability get moderate but not extended discussion. 1993 baseline is very low, so 24.
- Forward guidance: symmetric, with no commitment, but visible signals (Mullins, McDonough, Melzer) that a move may come. 56.
- Uncertainty: standard caveats ("none of us has a crystal ball", Jordan "the Greenbook is wrong... no idea in which direction", Syron "moods versus reality", Greenspan on noisy data). Normal to somewhat elevated: 32.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 44 (<50, slack mentioned): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (<50, supportive): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 36 (<50, no crunch, availability ample): ok. BANKING_STRESS 30 (<50, strong): ok. DOLLAR N/A. TREASURY 44 (yield moves only, in 36-50): ok.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs LABOR 36: gap of 8, ok. OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs CONSUMPTION 59: gap of 15. The gap is borderline but explainable: spending is strong in growth terms while the level of output is still below potential after a weak recovery. FC 38 vs CREDIT 36: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: normal-times Treasury near 44, fine. INFLATION 46 vs sub-components (goods 43, expectations 52): consistent.

**N/A justification.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no participant discusses the dollar. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: only scattered anecdotes (Tucson home prices, rents), nothing on a sector's price pressure. These are N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 46 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "no inflationary pressure around whatsoever"; Kelley, "Inflation is still falling on a year-over-year basis"; Jordan, progress "unacceptable"; Melzer, a 3 percent CPI "can hardly be viewed as representing price stability". Summary: disinflation under way with no visible pressure, but the 3 percent level is still above what a minority want. Direction: slightly below neutral because most report no pressure and a falling trend.

**OUTPUT_GAP 44 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley, "We still have slack... certainly in labor markets"; Mullins, mean growth "a bit above potential"; Melzer, "growth at or above potential" ahead. Summary: slack remains but is closing. Direction: below neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 36 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Boehne, employment growth "flat to very modest"; Phillips, "Job growth is not strong enough to lower unemployment"; Melzer, "Some areas report shortages of qualified workers". Summary: unemployment elevated at about 6.8 percent with only pockets of tightness. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 59 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Keehn, a retailer's October sales "fantastic"; Mullins, "a very traditional pattern of interest-sensitive spending emerging"; Boehne, consumption is "not sustainable" without income growth. Summary: broadly strong spending and investment with sustainability worries. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 60 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "a productivity kick"; Mullins, "the investment boom and productivity growth". Summary: participants see solid productivity gains. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "not having bottlenecks of credit unavailability"; McTeer, "not much talk of a credit crunch anymore". Summary: supportive conditions offset by the bond backup. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS 44 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mullins, "bond market dynamics... capture those gains before the end of the year"; Broaddus, the "backup in long-term interest rates". Summary: repricing, not dysfunction. Direction: at normal, slightly below midpoint.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 36 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: McTeer, "too many banks were aggressively seeking to fill that demand"; Phillips, no credit bottlenecks. Summary: spreads not discussed; availability ample. Direction: below neutral by availability only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 42 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: LaWare, "the values there are high enough and the market nervous enough"; Angell, "somewhat bullish... haven't had that last boom and bubble". Summary: split views, mildly elevated valuation concern. Direction: slightly below neutral (elevated valuations).

**BANKING_STRESS 30 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "The financial sector is strong"; Melzer, "higher bank earnings". Summary: sound banks, with only California loan shrinkage (Parry). Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS 43.** Evidence: Syron, suppliers expect "2 to 3 to 4 percent reductions in unit prices"; Forrestal, lumber prices up. Net mild softness.
**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** Only scattered anecdotes (Parry, Keehn).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 52.** Evidence: Mullins, "limit the damage of inflationary expectations on the long end"; Broaddus, markets worry inflation will be allowed to rise. Summary: market-implied expectations nudging up, forwards little changed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 54 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Melzer, "alternative 'B' with a bias toward a greater degree of reserve restraint"; Angell, the funds rate "ought to be at 4 percent"; Greenspan, a symmetric no-change is "a gimme putt". Summary: unanimous hold, with several voices leaning toward later tightening and none toward easing. Direction: slightly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: 12 Yes in the vote read-out; Broaddus, "a lot of sympathy for what Tom just said but for now 'B' symmetric". Summary: unanimous on the directive; reservations only from non-voter Melzer and a stated preference by Angell. The disclosure debate and Angell's oral dissent on the Gonzalez answer are procedural and not counted. Direction: broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 24 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: LaWare, "blow an aneurism in this economy through its most fragile part, and that's the financial markets"; Mullins, "we should take these markets seriously". Summary: a few members give the bond/equity markets real attention, though still as context for policy. Direction: limited to moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 56 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mullins, a short-rate move "might be necessary and beneficial"; McDonough, "fairly early next year we will be challenged by some price actions". Summary: symmetric directive with no commitment, but a tilt toward eventual tightening. Direction: slightly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 32 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Jordan, "the Greenbook is wrong. I just have no idea in which direction or how big"; Angell, "none of us has a crystal ball". Summary: ordinary forecast caveats with some doubt about whether the pickup is durable. Direction: normal to somewhat elevated.
