# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2020-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It holds members' questions in the staff Q&A (vaccine, herd immunity, fiscal assumptions, "substantial progress" definitions), the full economic go-around (Daly through Williams, then the Chair's summary), the policy go-around (Clarida through Williams, then the Chair's closing communications points), and a vote read-out. The text appears to span two sessions (the first day's economic go-around and the second day's policy go-around). Staff content (Tealbook figures quoted by members, Desk survey) is ignored except where a member adopts it as their own view. The text read cleanly; I found no garbled passages that affect scoring.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** A pandemic-recession recovery with a resurgent virus wave and vaccines just approved. Policy rate at the lower bound, asset purchases ongoing, decision on outcome-based guidance for the purchases.

**Speakers and views.** Almost every member says the recovery has beaten expectations. Near-term slowdown is expected as virus cases and deaths surge, and fiscal support is uncertain. Vaccines are seen as a strong medium-term positive. Unemployment (6.7 percent; payrolls about 10 million below February; broader measures near 9.5-10 percent) and inflation (about 1 percent, below 2) are far from goals. Many stress scarring, small-business distress and K-shaped hardship (Daly, Bostic, Bowman, Brainard, George, Kaplan). Rosengren raises CRE/NPL and bank solvency trade-offs and wants Main Street restored. Kaplan raises very loose financial conditions, speculation and SPACs. Bullard and Quarles are the optimists and also flag risks of market instability. In the policy round, everyone supports alternative B (outcome-based asset-purchase guidance), with Rosengren preferring A, Bostic wanting "substantial" deleted, and Quarles and George voicing reservations. The Chair stresses patience and "not an itchy trigger finger".

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as clearly below the 2 percent target (about 1 percent for 2020; Quarles: "near 1 percent"; Powell: "pandemic adding to disinflationary pressures"; Brainard: services subdued, housing weak), with expectations of a gradual rise (George, Quarles, market-based breakevens rising). Below 50, in the 26-40 band. Score 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output below potential ("economy remains a long, long way from our goals", payrolls about 10 million below February, "deep hole"; Brainard, Kashkari). However, GDP is recovering faster than expected, Q4 near 5 percent or more, and Quarles/Bullard note rapid recovery. Below 50, scored 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 6.7 percent, broader 9.5-10, participation depressed, lowest quartile at 20 percent. Slack remains. Minor offset: hard-to-fill jobs and worker shortages in some sectors (Barkin, Mester, Bostic). Level reading puts this in 16-35. Score 30.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net mixed. Goods, housing and durables are strong (Brainard, Quarles, Harker), business investment promising, savings high. Against that, high-contact services are weak, a winter pullback is expected (Q1 flat to slightly down per Powell, Kaplan), card spending is mixed (Harker), and the recovery has slowed since September. Net slightly below trend. Score 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing remarks (Bostic "could be good news on the productivity growth front" but he reads it as weak employment; Kaplan on technology disruption). No assessment of productivity growth. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Accommodative" is repeated (Powell: "kept financial conditions accommodative"; Quarles: "still broadly accommodative"; Brainard: "highly accommodative"; Kaplan: "extremely loose"; Williams: "very accommodative"). Small and mid-size firms face tight bank standards, which pulls a little toward neutral. Bright line (<50) satisfied. Score 24.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No member describes Treasury dysfunction. The only functioning comments are that the emergency market-functioning need "has long passed" (Bostic), financial stress indexes are "at normal pre-crisis levels" (Bullard), and a precaution that conditions may not remain calm (Williams, Quarles). Yield-level talk (10-year near 1 percent, Brainard; Williams) is not dysfunction. Normal and quiet, scored low with Low confidence. Score 30.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Brainard: "credit spreads have tightened", "credit markets are back to pre-pandemic levels"; Quarles: plentiful credit for good credits at historically low rates, even speculative-grade and leveraged loans looking through; George: defaults far below expectations. Offset: small firms face tight lending standards. Level is normal to somewhat tight spreads (favorable). Below 50 (bright line satisfied). Score 34.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation commentary exists. Kaplan: disruptors "extremely highly valued", "highly speculative conditions; proliferation of SPACs", late-cycle concerns; Bullard: household wealth near all-time highs; Quarles notes earnings expectations back to 2019 levels. This is modest valuation concern, no distress. Score 36 (above fair value side, rubric polarity: lower = more overvaluation concern).
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed, system sound but with pockets. Barkin: "more confident in the resilience of the banking system", "defaults appear controlled"; Quarles: banks have not cited capital or liquidity as constraints; Mester: keep the system resilient. Against: Rosengren's CRE/CMBS delinquencies, rising NPL risk and "bank solvency risk", with 4013/TDR forbearance masking it. Below 50, sound with minor concerns. Score 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS (Brainard: durable goods prices stopped rising in September; George: durable goods inflation turned positive for the first time in a quarter century, low inventories, supply constraints, Harker/Williams PS5 anecdote) mixed to slightly firm, 52. SERVICES (subdued, depressed prices in COVID-sensitive sectors, Brainard, George, Quarles, Daly) 30. HOUSING (Brainard: continued weakness in tenants' and owners' equivalent rents, 20 percent weight; Quarles expects rents to recover later) 32. EXPECTATIONS (market-based measures rising toward target, survey measures soft, risk of being anchored below target per Mester; Barkin, Bullard, Brainard, Williams) 44.

**Tier 3, in words.**
- Policy lean: No rate change, asset purchases maintained at the current pace and now tied to outcomes. Several members would do more (Rosengren prefers A with longer maturities; Kashkari, Bowman, Mester open to more; Brainard could have supported WAM extension). A few look toward eventual tapering (Barkin, Quarles, Kaplan, Harker), but not now. Maximum accommodation maintained with a slight easing bias. Dovish, about 21.
- Disagreement: Unanimous vote with zero dissents. Reservations are about wording and tactics (Bostic would delete "substantial"; Rosengren prefers A; George and Quarles worry about open-ended guidance; Kaplan about excessive risk-taking) and about the economic outlook (Rosengren more pessimistic, Quarles and Bullard more optimistic). Minor, tactical. About 20.
- Financial stability attention: Moderate. Bullard builds a scenario of health news morphing into financial instability; Rosengren on CRE and bank solvency; Mester on forbearance unwinding and financial stability risks; Kaplan on speculation; Quarles and Williams on market functioning; George on distress. Not the dominant topic. About 42.
- Forward guidance: Outcome-based guidance on both rates and purchases, rate at the lower bound through 2023 in SEPs, liftoff plausible 2024 (Powell), "patient", "not an itchy trigger finger". A mild taper discussion (late 2021 or 2022) is flagged as a future consideration, but the signal is strongly easing/low for long. About 12.
- Uncertainty: Widely emphasized. Williams "highly elevated", Bullard "considerable uncertainty", Harker "tremendous uncertainty", Powell "uncertainty remains quite high" and "wide range", Barkin partly counters with clearer focus. Many members, wide scenarios (vaccine rollout, fiscal package, virus). About 68.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS higher means tighter; evidence says slack, so below 50. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher means tighter; evidence says accommodative, so below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS higher means more stress; evidence is favorable, level normal to somewhat tight, so below 50. EQUITY_VALUATION: mild overvaluation concern is below 50 on this scale (lower means more overvaluation). BANKING_STRESS: sound with pockets of concern, below 50. HAWKISH_DOVISH: higher means more hawkish; evidence says accommodative, so low.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential and score below 50 (pass). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative and score below 50 (pass). CREDIT_SPREADS: level favorable/pre-pandemic, score below 50; "tightened" was treated as direction only (pass). BANKING_STRESS "resilient/sound" below 50 (pass). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS no dysfunction described, score low (pass).

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs LABOR 30: consistent. OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs CONSUMPTION 43: gap of 10, within 15; consumption holds up better because goods and housing are strong. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 24 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 34: both loose/favorable, co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, low. INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 vs sub-components (goods 52, services 30, housing 32, expectations 44): consistent with a weighted average near 33-38; parent held at 30 because members' headline emphasis is "below target, about 1 percent".

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment of productivity growth by members. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member discussed the dollar or FX.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Quarles, "Price inflation will end the year near 1 percent"; Powell, "I continue to see the pandemic as adding to disinflationary pressures. Inflation remains below our 2 percent objective." Center of gravity: inflation is clearly below target with only a gradual rise expected. Direction: below neutral because actual inflation is well under 2 percent, despite some upside-risk remarks.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "the economy remains a long, long way from our goals"; Brainard, "the economy is still in a deep hole". Center of gravity: substantial slack despite a faster-than-expected recovery. Direction: below potential because employment and output remain well short of pre-pandemic and goals.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Quarles, "The labor market remains impaired by any measure"; Brainard, headline 6.7 percent rising to 9.5 percent counting those who left the labor force. Center of gravity: high unemployment and about 10 million jobs missing, with scattered hiring difficulties. Direction: below neutral due to large slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Brainard, "Spending on consumer goods has exceeded pre-pandemic levels, especially on interest-sensitive durables"; Powell, activity "roughly flat or perhaps down in Q1 slightly". Center of gravity: strong goods and housing offset by weak contact services and a winter pullback. Direction: slightly below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** No member assesses productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 24 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Quarles, "Financial conditions are still broadly accommodative"; Kaplan (relaying CEOs), "extremely loose financial conditions overall". Center of gravity: market-based financing is very easy; only small bank-dependent borrowers are tight. Direction: below neutral because conditions are accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Bostic, "the need for those emergency actions has, in my view, long passed"; Bullard, financial stress at "normal pre-crisis levels". Center of gravity: orderly functioning; only precautionary language about future volatility. Direction: below neutral because no dysfunction is described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 34 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Brainard, "credit spreads have tightened" and "credit markets are back to pre-pandemic levels"; Quarles, "those with good credit histories have plentiful access to credit at historically low interest rates". Center of gravity: spreads and access are favorable for large borrowers, tighter for small firms. Direction: below neutral because the stated level is at or better than pre-pandemic.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 36 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kaplan, "highly speculative conditions; proliferation of SPACs"; disruptors "extremely highly valued"; Bullard, "household wealth is near all-time highs". Center of gravity: modest to moderate valuation concern without distress. Direction: below neutral on this scale because valuations are viewed as rich.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Barkin, "more confident in the resilience of the banking system"; Rosengren, "rise in nonperforming loans at banks will further delay the recovery" and need to accept "increased bank solvency risk". Center of gravity: banks sound for now with CRE/NPL risk ahead. Direction: below neutral because the system is described as resilient with banks not capital-constrained.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52.** Evidence: George, "durable goods inflation has turned positive for the first time in a quarter-century"; Brainard, durable goods prices "stopped increasing in September".

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 30.** Evidence: Brainard, "prices on services have remained subdued"; George, "prices of some services remain depressed".

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 32.** Evidence: Brainard, "continued weakness in housing services due to tenants' and owners' equivalent rents".

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Evidence: Quarles, market-based measures "are now above their levels before the onset of the COVID event"; Brainard, "consumer survey-based measures are soft"; Mester, possibility they are "anchored at a level below our inflation objective".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 21 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Evans, "substantial accommodation will still be needed"; Kashkari, "If we see evidence of rising long-term rates or falling inflation expectations or both, I would support doing more". Center of gravity: hold the stance and current purchase pace, strengthen guidance, with some open to doing more. Direction: dovish because the Committee is maintaining maximum accommodation with an easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-5 (High).** Evidence: the roll call is unanimous for alternative B; Rosengren, "I would prefer the language in alternative A"; Bostic, "The language in alternative B works perfectly well if the term 'substantial' is simply deleted." Center of gravity: unanimous, with tactical reservations only. Direction: low disagreement because there are no dissents and the differences are about wording and tools.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bullard, "health news ... may somehow morph into financial instability again"; Mester, "risks to financial stability should never be far from our minds". Center of gravity: several members address it meaningfully, but economic and virus topics dominate. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "we need to not have an itchy trigger finger on backing out of our commitment to support the economy"; Brainard, funds rate "could be at the lower bound through 2023". Center of gravity: outcome-based commitment to keep purchases and rates supportive for years, with taper only as a later possibility. Direction: strongly easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "I continue to view uncertainty about the outlook as being highly elevated"; Bullard, "The shorter-term national outlook is marked by considerable uncertainty." Center of gravity: many members stress a wide range from the virus, vaccine rollout and fiscal outcomes. Direction: high uncertainty, though vaccine news narrows the tails and a few members (Barkin) feel clearer.
