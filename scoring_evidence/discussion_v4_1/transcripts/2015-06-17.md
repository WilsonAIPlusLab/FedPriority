## Part 2: Evidence and scores

Document read to the last line; vote read-out at the end shows ten Yes votes and no dissent. The document has a policy go-round and a vote on alternative B, so it is not special-topic.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 32 +-5 High.** Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation over the past year is currently at 1.2 percent, well below what many of us were forecasting". Kocherlakota: "the balance of risk to inflation remains very much to the downside". Yellen: PCE inflation "back to the neighborhood of 1½ percent" by early next year. Most expect a return to 2 percent only gradually.

**OUTPUT_GAP 44 +-5 Moderate.** Brainard: "With remaining slack, and inflation persistently below our target". Williams: "some slack still being out there in the labor market". Lacker: unemployment "so close to estimates of the natural rate" that differences are not statistically significant. Bullard: "relatively small unemployment and inflation gaps". Slightly below potential, closing; slack-remains language, so under 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 46 +-5 High.** Unemployment 5.5 percent (Lacker, Rosengren). Mester: "we are near our goal of maximum employment". Lockhart: contacts describe labor markets as "tight". Tarullo: "a nontrivial amount of slack left". Near the natural rate, not yet beyond it.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44 +-6 Moderate.** Brainard: "The lackluster pace of household spending is surprising". Yellen: "business investment has been somewhat disappointing". Offsetting: Lacker, "longest sustained improvement in household spending growth", and Yellen, household spending "at a solid pace" in the first half. Net slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 22 +-5 High.** Dudley: "really poor productivity growth over the last four quarters". Brainard: "why productivity growth has been so miserable". Yellen: productivity "risen only ¼ percent on a year-over-year basis".

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 +-7 Moderate.** Holcomb: "accommodative financial conditions will drive the unemployment rate past the natural rate"; she also cites an "artificially favorable financial environment". Rosengren: "we have not tightened financial conditions". Tarullo: "a modest amount of tightening of financial conditions in the United States" from the bund and yield reversal. Yellen: housing credit "so tight". Accommodative overall, with a modest tightening at the margin.

**TREASURY_STRESS 42 +-7 Moderate.** Powell: "volatility in the Treasury securities market has increased in recent months", but the MOVE index is "still below typical volatility levels". Dudley: the yield backup is "warranted"; 10-year yields "not particularly high". Yield moves described as orderly; no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 42 +-12 Low.** No spread wording from members, so this rests on availability and risk-taking language (V4.1.1 rule 3). George: "better-than-expected availability of financing" for energy firms, and "searching for higher yield and the willingness to take on additional risk". Holcomb: "artificially favorable financial environment". Against this, Yellen says housing "credit availability remains so tight". Net slightly below normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION N/A.** Equities appear only as wealth-effect or spending channel (Brainard, Kocherlakota); no valuation or price-move view.

**BANKING_STRESS N/A.** Banks' health is not discussed. Only passing bankers' reports of loan demand (Mester).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 61 +-6 High.** Yellen: "elevated level of the dollar"; staff estimate it takes ¼ percentage point off core inflation. Brainard: "recent increases in the value of the dollar will remain a significant drag on net exports". Mester: "the appreciation of the dollar has slowed". Strong, weighing on exports, but stabilising. Appreciation language, so over 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS 30.** Yellen: "the appreciation of the dollar is depressing import prices". Fischer: "recent declines in core import prices". Energy price pass-through is also cited. Goods and energy prices depressed.

**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** No services price discussion beyond passing "unusually soft medical prices" (George).

**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** No shelter or rent inflation discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 42.** Lacker: "inflation expectations have remained stable". Evans: "this downward drift in the SPF long-run CPI outlook". Kocherlakota: breakevens "remain low by historical standards". Mostly described as stable, with a downside-drift concern from a few members.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 54 +-6 High.** Hold at 0 to ¼ percent (alternative B) with a tightening bias. Lacker: "my preference would be to raise rates at this meeting". Mester: "the economy can support a 25 basis point increase". Williams: "the time for liftoff is fast approaching". Fischer: "we should be willing to move very soon". Dovish end: Kocherlakota, "I like alternative A because it has more accommodation"; Evans, liftoff pushed to "midyear 2016"; Brainard, "watchful waiting". Most (Dudley, Powell, Prichard, Lockhart) see September as likely.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 36 +-6 High.** Unanimous vote (ten Yes, no dissent). Reservations on both sides: Holcomb "lean toward alternative C" but "will support B"; Lacker would raise now but "will not vote against"; George supports C; Kocherlakota prefers A and criticises data dependence; Evans differs on timing. Bullard objects to Dudley's remarks ("a ridiculous characterization"). Disagreement is chiefly about timing and communication, plus some about the economic assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 38 +-8 Moderate.** Holcomb: "imbalances are created that are unsustainable". George: "searching for higher yield and the willingness to take on additional risk". Several members discuss the yield backup and bund reversal (Dudley, Tarullo, Powell), market volatility around liftoff (Mester), and Greece-related "market disruptions" (Brainard). Moderate attention, mostly as market-reaction and communication context.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 50 +-8 Moderate.** The statement stays data dependent: Rosengren, "keep the statement data dependent and not try to have any forward guidance"; Williams, "We should not be reintroducing near-term forward guidance". Some want a hint in July (Lockhart, Mester) and Powell would accept "a little more of a forward lean". C′ "gradual increase" language gets a mixed reception (Holcomb, Lacker, Dudley wary). Yellen leaves September "on the table". Neutral, data-dependent posture with liftoff approaching.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 50 +-7 Moderate.** Powell on Greece: "The range of possible outcomes is wide". Dudley on productivity: "a conundrum that does increase my uncertainty". Evans: "substantial uncertainty" over r*. Yellen: "it's unclear whether real GDP over the rest of the year will be strong enough". Mester: uncertainty "not... exceptionally elevated". Elevated but not extreme.
