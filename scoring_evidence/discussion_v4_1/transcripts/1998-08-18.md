# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) - meeting 1998-08-18 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript for a 1998 meeting: questions to the Desk and staff, a full economic go-around (all voting and non-voting participants, including First Vice President Varvel for Richmond), then a Chairman's summary, a policy go-around on alternative B (no change) and symmetric versus asymmetric directive, and a roll call at the end of the file (read to the last line: 10 Yes, 1 No, President Jordan). This is a normal policy meeting with a vote and a stance discussion, so it is NOT special-topic under V4.1.1 clause 1. Staff turns are removed, so staff answers are missing; I scored only what participants said. One passage (Minehan, the insurance-company contact whose name was dropped) is slightly garbled; the substance (some widening of spreads, slight restraint) is still readable.

**Era context.** The text itself shows: the Asian crisis spreading, Russia in default or restructuring, Japan deflation fears, falling commodity and oil prices, a strong dollar, a recent stock-market correction, very tight labor markets, low measured inflation, strong domestic demand but a Q2 slowdown driven by inventories and the GM strike, and an asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive since March that the Committee is now deciding whether to make symmetric.

**Speakers.** McTeer (wants to ease, "ahead of the curve"), Jordan (wants tighter policy; dissents), Minehan (next move should be up, but cautious on external risks), Poole, Hoenig, Moskow, Guynn, Varvel (upside risks, keep asymmetry), Parry, Stern, Boehne, McDonough, Gramlich, Rivlin, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley (balanced risks, mostly accept symmetry), Greenspan (summary: deflationary pressure rising abroad, data problems, no change, symmetry).

**Directions in words, per dimension.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as slightly below target / benign on measured prices ("news on inflation ... outstanding", GDP price index near 1 percent, core CPI about 2 percent, Boehne "subdued", Greenspan "inflation is low"), but with upside wage-driven risks and some (Jordan, Minehan) seeing a higher median CPI or forecast near 3 percent. Net: modestly below the midpoint, in the 41-49 band; not concerning on current readings.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as modestly above potential in demand and labor terms (Poole "overshot full employment"; Jordan "construction soars to unsustainable levels"; Meyer: growth near trend), though Gramlich and Rivlin say growth is falling below trend and overheating is "slipping into the category of past worries". Above 50 by the bright-line (above-potential language is present and dominant); modest, so mid-50s.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight ("extremely tight", "very tight", highest hiring plans in 22-year survey history, widespread shortages, raiding). Greenspan notes it may have stopped tightening further. Well above 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend: "domestic demand continues to grow strongly", consumer spending surprising on the upside, brisk, construction robust; offset by the expected slowdown and Asia/export drag on manufacturing. Above trend but with a slowdown expected.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed (Parry: somewhat higher trend productivity growth; Greenspan: productivity strong from the income side; Jordan: favorable productivity surprise; Varvel and Ferguson worry about ebbing productivity; Minehan agnostic whether cyclical or structural). Above trend overall but contested.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Tightening elements (stock-market correction, higher risk spreads, reduced market liquidity, "markets measures of monetary policy reflect a restrictive stance" per McTeer) against loose elements (intense lender competition, thinner margins and lighter collateral per Jordan, Minehan's strong C&I lending). Net near neutral, slight tightening drift.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No participant describes Treasury market dysfunction. McDonough's liquidity remark concerns "many other markets", not Treasuries specifically. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Participants give direction only: Greenspan "rise in risk spreads in the last three or four months ... not huge ... consistently higher"; Minehan's contact sees "some widening of spreads related to credit quality". No level is stated. Per V4.1.1 clause 3, 45-55 on the side of the move, CONF Low. Counter-evidence on availability (Jordan: deals at thinner margins, less collateral; Minehan: intense lender competition) keeps it near the middle.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation views are present: Meyer ("some initial overvaluations"), Rivlin (orderly "stair steps down to values more in line with realistic profit expectations"), Jordan (speculative excesses, boom-bust analogy), Kelley/Ferguson (stock market as wealth-effect driver). The correction is seen as orderly and a return from somewhat rich levels. On the table: modestly above fair value, scored 31-45 band.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants discuss bank lending conditions (Jordan, Minehan, Varvel) and Japanese banking reform (McDonough) but give no assessment of US banking-system health or stress. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar strong and rising against most currencies (McTeer "the dollar rising against most currencies"; Parry "high dollar"; McDonough safe-haven flows "making those currencies stronger"; Stern's paper executives want a lower dollar). Above 50 by the bright-line, notable strength.
- Inflation sub-components: goods falling (Jordan: "Goods prices are falling", import and commodity prices falling, Moskow competition limits pricing); services accelerating (Jordan "service prices are accelerating", Ferguson goods/service dichotomy); housing: rents rising significantly (Stern), Manhattan office rents surging (McDonough), Minehan tight real estate; expectations: Parry expects "diminished inflation expectations", no unanchoring noted by anyone, deflation psychology a concern for Hoenig and Greenspan abroad. Not extreme.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The decision is no change (alternative B) with a move from the March tightening-bias (asymmetric) directive to a symmetric one. Most participants support symmetry (McDonough "very strongly", Rivlin, Meyer, McTeer, Ferguson mildly, Gramlich, Stern, Parry, Poole, Boehne); Kelley, Moskow, Guynn, Hoenig, Varvel lean to keeping asymmetry but can accept symmetry. Dovish voices: McTeer (outright easing), Gramlich and Rivlin (more downside risks). Hawkish voices: Jordan (tighten now), Minehan (next move up). Net: hold, bias neutralized after being hawkish; lean dovish-to-balanced, a little under 50.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (Jordan, who wants tightening), plus a non-voting McTeer who argued for easing in the economic round, and a handful of mild reservations about dropping the bias (Kelley, Moskow, Guynn, Hoenig, Varvel). The economic assessments are largely shared (strong domestic demand, Asian drag, tight labor), with differences about the risk balance. One dissent plus several reservations: moderate range.
- Financial-stability attention: High-ish. Global financial turmoil (Asia, Russia, Latin America, contagion), liquidity, spreads, stock-market correction and wealth effects are discussed by many; Jordan makes an explicit financial-stability argument; Minehan flags speculation in financial, credit and real estate markets. It sits alongside, not above, the real-economy assessment.
- Forward guidance: Directive made symmetric; McDonough says next move equally likely up or down; Greenspan summary leaves it at B/symmetry. Neutral on net, with a slight easing flavor relative to the prior tightening bias. Note the disclosure-timing remarks (Parry, Guynn) are about communication process, not direction.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "I do not recall a time when there has been more uncertainty in financial markets"; Greenspan "our data have deserted us" and "no one has a clue" on Y2K; Poole "uncertainties are quite substantial"; Stern "risks look to me to be very large"; Ferguson "fraught with uncertainties", "high-wire act"; Meyer on structural uncertainty and more reactive policy. Several members emphasize unusual uncertainty: high.

**Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress: the evidence is a modest widening, so slightly above 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH higher = more dollar strength: appreciation reported, so above 50. BANKING_STRESS is N/A so no sign issue. EQUITY_VALUATION higher = more stress or undervaluation; participants see mild overvaluation correcting, so below 50 (consistent with the table where elevated valuations sit at 16-45). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: mixed, near 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 (> 50, matches "above"). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 51: no "accommodative"/"tight" characterization by participants as a whole, so near-neutral allowed; McTeer's "restrictive" refers to the policy stance via market measures, not conditions. CREDIT_SPREADS 53: direction-only, CONF Low, in 45-55. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 66 (> 50). BANKING_STRESS and TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 68: 13-point gap, within 15; explained because participants describe labor as very tight in level terms while the output gap is held down by the expected slowdown, Asian drag and Gramlich/Rivlin views of growth already below trend. OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs CONSUMPTION 62: 7 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 51 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 53: co-move. Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 vs sub-components (goods low, services and housing high, expectations a little under target): the parent follows what participants say about overall measured inflation (low, "subdued"), which is why it sits below a simple average of the sub-components; the wage and services pressure is reflected in the upside-risk language rather than the current reading.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant comments on Treasury market functioning. BANKING_STRESS: no participant assesses bank health or stress; loan-standard remarks are about lending terms and competition, and Japanese bank reform is a foreign policy matter.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Parry: "The news on inflation certainly continues to be outstanding." Boehne: "U.S. inflation remains subdued". Offset: Minehan sees inflation "rises to just about 3 percent by the fourth quarter of 1999"; Jordan cites a July median CPI of 3.0 percent.
- Center of gravity: current measured inflation is low and benign, with wage-driven upside risks that members treat as risk rather than present reading.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is slightly below neutral because most members describe measured inflation as low, with upside risk concentrated in the forecast.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Poole: "we probably have overshot full employment". Jordan: "construction soars to unsustainable levels." Counter: Rivlin: "the overheating of the U.S. economy is likely to be slipping into the category of past worries."
- Center of gravity: demand is above sustainable levels in labor and construction, but a slowdown to trend or below is expected.
- Direction confirmation: Output gap is above neutral because above-potential/overshot language dominates, tempered by the expected slowing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Minehan: "labor markets remain extremely tight." Moskow: Manpower hiring plans "at the highest levels in the survey's 22-year history." Stern: "widespread upward pressure on compensation."
- Center of gravity: shortages and wage pressure reported in nearly every District; Greenspan notes tightness has plateaued but at a very tight level.
- Direction confirmation: Labor is tighter than neutral because members report shortages, raiding and accelerating compensation in nearly every District. Unemployment level is not stated precisely (forecasts near 5 percent, "close to NAIRU" per Gramlich), so I stop short of the top of the 66-80 band.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "domestic demand continues to grow strongly." Guynn: "considerable momentum in domestic consumption and investment." Jordan: retailers expect "very strong consumer demand."
- Center of gravity: spending is well above trend in most Districts, partly offset by manufacturing weakness from Asia and an expected slowdown.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is above trend because members repeatedly cite upside surprises in consumption and construction.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Parry: "somewhat higher trend productivity growth". Greenspan: from the income side "productivity growth continues to be quite strong".
- Center of gravity: productivity is seen as running above its old trend, though Varvel and Ferguson fear it is ebbing and Minehan is agnostic.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is above neutral because the favorable-surprise view is the more frequently stated one.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 51 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: risk spreads "consistently higher"; McDonough: "considerably less liquidity" in many markets. Offsetting: Jordan: deals "at ever thinner margins, longer terms, less collateral".
- Center of gravity: modest tightening signs in risk spreads, liquidity and equities against still-easy lending terms.
- Direction confirmation: Conditions are about neutral, very slightly on the tighter side, because the tightening signals and loose lending terms roughly cancel.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (not discussed by participants as a market-functioning issue).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the rise in risk spreads in the last three or four months ... not huge by any means, but they sure are consistently higher." Minehan (contact): "some widening of spreads related to credit quality".
- Center of gravity: direction-only widening; no level stated; per V4.1.1 clause 3, 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence.
- Direction confirmation: Spreads are just above neutral because the only information is a modest widening; the availability evidence (lenders competing intensely) pulls against it.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Meyer: a correction "to reflect some initial overvaluations". Rivlin: orderly corrections "to values more in line with realistic profit expectations".
- Center of gravity: stocks viewed as having been somewhat rich, now correcting in an orderly way, with wealth effects the main concern.
- Direction confirmation: Valuation concern is on the modestly-overvalued side (below 50 on this scale) because members describe overvaluation being corrected, not depressed prices.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** (no participant assesses US banking-system health or stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 66 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: McTeer: "the dollar rising against most currencies". Parry: restrained by "the high dollar". Stern: paper executives talk of "the need ... for a lower dollar."
- Center of gravity: dollar strong and still drawing safe-haven flows, weighing on exports.
- Direction confirmation: Dollar strength is above neutral because members describe broad appreciation and its drag on trade.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 28.** Evidence: Jordan, "Goods prices are falling"; Moskow, competition "constrains their ability to raise prices"; McTeer, "import prices falling".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 62.** Evidence: Jordan, "service prices are accelerating"; Ferguson on strength in services; wage costs passed into prices at some retailers (Moskow).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 66.** Evidence: Stern, rents "moving up significantly"; McDonough, "Office rents surged at a 27 percent annual rate in Manhattan"; Minehan, tighter real estate markets.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Parry expects pressure to be offset by "diminished inflation expectations"; no one reports unanchoring upward; deflation psychology noted by Hoenig and Greenspan (abroad).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "'B', meaning 'no change' ... is the appropriate position"; McDonough: "our next rate move is equally likely to be up or down." Wings: McTeer: "it is time to talk about getting ahead of the curve by easing"; Jordan wants action to restrain money growth.
- Center of gravity: hold, with the tightening bias dropped, a modestly dovish-leaning shift.
- Direction confirmation: Lean is slightly dovish of balanced because the bias moves from tightening to neutral and several members raise easing, though hawkish members (Jordan, Minehan, Hoenig, Kelley, Guynn, Moskow, Varvel) remain.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: Roll call: Jordan No, ten Yes. Jordan: "the more costly mistake to correct will be ... excess demand growth does not in fact slow down." Mild reservations: Kelley "I would prefer to stay with asymmetry"; Moskow "slight preference for an asymmetric directive".
- Center of gravity: one formal dissent from the hawkish side, an easing advocate (McTeer) who does not vote, and several mild reservations on symmetry; economic assessments largely shared.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is moderate (above the consensus band) because of one dissent plus multiple expressed reservations, but not sharp exchanges.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McDonough: "I do not recall a time when there has been more uncertainty in financial markets." Jordan: "the absence of consumer price inflation is not a sufficient condition for financial stability".
- Center of gravity: many participants discuss global financial turmoil, liquidity, spreads and stock-market wealth effects, though the real-economy assessment remains the core.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is moderate to prominent because the topic appears in a large share of statements but does not dominate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 48 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Rivlin: "we are unlikely to change monetary policy anytime soon and ... we may move the fed funds rate up or down"; Meyer: "a symmetric posture is the best choice for now."
- Center of gravity: the directive moves to symmetry, neutral forward signal, mildly softer than the prior tightening bias.
- Direction confirmation: Signal is about neutral, a hair under 50, because the tightening tilt is removed but nobody signals easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: McDonough: "I do not recall a time when there has been more uncertainty in financial markets." Ferguson: "a high-wire act where a move to either side would be quite detrimental". Greenspan: "our data have deserted us".
- Center of gravity: multiple members emphasize unusual uncertainty about Asia, the stock market, data reliability and the domestic slowdown.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is clearly elevated because many participants stress it as unusual, not merely standard caveats.
