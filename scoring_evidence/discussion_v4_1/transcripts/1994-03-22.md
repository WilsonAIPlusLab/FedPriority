# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 1994-03-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It opens with a long discussion of a Mexican swap-line increase (a special topic, not scored), then members' questions to staff, the district/national go-around, a financial-markets and policy discussion led by the Chairman, the policy go-around (25 vs 50 bp, symmetric vs asymmetric directive), a disclosure/announcement discussion, and a vote read-out. The text is readable. A few passages are clipped (names of contacts removed, one-sided Q&A where staff answers are removed), but nothing is garbled enough to affect scores.

**Era context.** Early 1994, an expansion in its third year. The text shows a strong recovery, a recent 25 bp tightening on Feb 4, a sharp bond-market sell-off (30-year up about 120 bp over five months), and a turbulent early March. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Chair Greenspan: the economy "looks terrific"; the main danger is the fragile financial structure after a long period of compressed risk premiums; policy must move to neutrality "as fast as we can", with a 25 bp move now and a 4 to 4-1/2 percent end point. Inflation not yet showing in final prices, which he finds puzzling.
- Hawks: Boehne, Broaddus, Melzer, Parry, Hoenig (inflationary psychology, rising long rates, accommodative real rates; prefer 50 bp). Jordan wants aggressive moves, 50 bp or a discount-rate move.
- Cautious/doves: Kelley ("keeping our powder dry"), LaWare, Phillips, Minehan, Forrestal, McDonough (uncertainty, market fragility, consumer wealth effects, bank rumors).
- Lindsey: financial excess (margin debt, negative real after-tax returns, foreign markets).

**Directional statements (core dimensions).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core CPI is 2.8 percent and still trending down, final-goods and wage pressure is limited (Stern, Keehn, Minehan, Greenspan), but commodity and materials prices, steel, and Broaddus/Melzer/Hoenig anecdotes show building pressure, and capacity is tight. Current level near 3 percent is above a price-stability objective. About 56.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Parry says growth "all but eliminated the gap between actual and potential GDP"; Phillips says "The economy has been running ahead of potential"; Melzer says capacity utilization is at the 1990 peak; Kelley says we may be "further away from this full utilization flash point than we think". Net slightly above to at potential. Bright-line: "ahead of potential" means >50. 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Parry says unemployment is "close to most estimates of the natural rate"; Melzer says conditions are "rapidly approaching" 1990 tightness; Hoenig and McTeer report labor strains and shortages of skilled construction workers; Boehne and Stern see no wage pressure; Minehan says few hire. Unemployment level (about 6.5 percent, not quoted) would argue lower, but participants describe near-natural-rate conditions. 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: strong across autos, heavy trucks, steel at capacity, retail (Keehn comparable store sales up 17 percent), housing, capex plans; offsetting caution from Kelley (consumer debt) and Phillips (low saving rate). Above trend. 65.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Keehn ("very impressive"), Phillips ("strong productivity improvement"), Kelley (substantial improvement possibly secular), Greenspan ("extraordinary productivity increases"). Above trend. 67.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: long rates are up sharply (Broaddus, Kelley "powerful restraint"), markets were turbulent in early March, but Phillips says "the overall cost of capital remains relatively low", banks are positioned to lend, Greenspan says risk premiums remain "quite low", and loan availability is loosening (McTeer "hell yes"). Net slightly looser than neutral. Bright-line: supportive/low-cost language means <50. 45.
- TREASURY_STRESS: the bond sell-off is discussed at length (Greenspan, Broaddus, Phillips) but as a repricing of inflation, growth and portfolio adjustment, not as dysfunction. Only the early-March episode (rumors, rocky markets) hints at strain. Yields alone are not dysfunction. 47.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Greenspan: "We have seen some increase in yield spreads but they are still quite low by any historic standard"; commercial paper/bill spread "gone up moderately... still basically low". Level is low, direction slightly wider. Bright-line: low level means <50. 32.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan's "bubble" language (capital-gains bubble in all financial assets, "prick the bubble in the equity markets", stock market "a little dicey"), Lindsey (record margin debt, foreign markets). Valuation concern is elevated though partly deflated. 30.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips ("Banks are in a strong position"), Hoenig ("Banks financially are strong"), McDonough's account of rumors about J P Morgan and Bankers Trust (AA rated, resolved), Greenspan's concern that the financial system could lose "tensile strength". Banks healthy but the system seen as vulnerable. Bright-line: sound means <50. 37.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member discussion of the dollar's direction beyond an incidental exchange-rate remark in Stern's report on Canadian shoppers and the peso/crawling-peg talk in the swap debate. N/A.

**Sub-components.** GOODS: materials, steel, lumber, plastics up (3-5 percent, up to 15 percent anecdotes); final goods not accelerating. 58. SERVICES: only Keehn's "moderations in the service sector". 44. HOUSING: Greenspan notes owners' equivalent rent up 0.6 percent "way above average" (an acknowledged outlier), plus building cost increases (Forrestal, McTeer). 55. EXPECTATIONS: Broaddus ("inflationary psychology is building"), Melzer ("inflationary expectations have also increased"), Hoenig; countered by Minehan and Kelley. 62.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee tightens 25 bp (to 3.25 percent). Greenspan wants neutrality (4 to 4-1/2 percent) "as fast as we can" and rejects doing nothing. Several members (Boehne, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan; Hoenig and LaWare on the merits) preferred 50 bp. No one argued for easing; the cautious camp still backs 25. Moderate to strong hawkish. 70.
- Dissent: two formal "No" votes (Broaddus, Jordan), both for a more aggressive hike; disagreement on size and on symmetry (Jordan and Hoenig on asymmetry), reluctant supporters (LaWare "reluctantly agree", Kelley "I do not believe that the real economy requires a tightening move"), but common agreement on direction. Plus side disagreements on swap line and disclosure that are not about conditions. 48.
- Financial stability: Greenspan's long analysis of bubbles, mutual funds, portfolio adjustment; McDonough on bank rumors and mutual fund redemptions; Phillips on market vulnerability; Lindsey on margin debt; market effect drives the 25 vs 50 choice. Prominent but not dominant. 66.
- Forward guidance: Greenspan expects to reach 4 percent by midyear moving meeting by meeting; Lindsey (25 in March, May, July); asymmetric directive debated, symmetric chosen with a conference call as an alternative; the Chair does not want to signal a pause. Clear tightening signal. 74.
- Uncertainty: McDonough has "much greater uncertainty"; Stern "confidence interval around any forecast is very wide"; Keehn "toughest call"; Greenspan finds the inflation absence "very puzzling", "something different going on"; Kelley "behaving in some new ways". Many caveats but forecasts broadly agreed. 52.

**Polarity verification.** Higher inflation pressure = more pressure: evidence points modestly above target, matches. Credit spreads: higher = more stress; evidence "still quite low" gives a score below 50, matches. Equity: low score = valuation concern high; evidence of bubble concern, score 30, matches. Banking stress: healthy banks give <50, matches. Hawkish: higher = tighter; hike plus more to come with several wanting 50, matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 (>50 with "ahead of potential"). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 45 (<50 for "relatively low cost of capital"). CREDIT_SPREADS 32 (<50, low level). BANKING_STRESS 37 (<50, "strong"). TREASURY_STRESS 47 (yield moves only, no dysfunction). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 50 (5 apart, fine). OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 65 (10 apart, fine; spending is strong relative to trend while level of slack is near potential). FC 45 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 32 (13 apart, both below 50; FC pulled up by long-rate sell-off). FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal-ish, 47. INFLATION_PRESSURE 56 vs sub-components (58, 44, 55, 62): consistent.

**N/A justification.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: members do not discuss the dollar's value other than incidental exchange-rate remarks about the Canadian dollar and peso in non-policy contexts.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Broaddus: "for the first time in this cycle, really sticking" (3-5 percent supplier price rises); Kelley: "core CPI last month... was the lowest it has been in a long time, at 2.8 percent". Summary: current inflation moderate and not yet accelerating but widely seen as building. Direction: above neutral because core near 3 percent with building materials and commodity pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry: growth "has all but eliminated the gap between actual and potential GDP"; Phillips: "The economy has been running ahead of potential". Summary: near to modestly above potential. Direction: above neutral on "ahead of potential" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry: unemployment "close to most estimates of the natural rate"; Boehne: "few, if any, signs of wage pressures". Summary: near natural rate, tightening but not tight. Direction: at neutral because members see near-NAIRU conditions with isolated shortages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Keehn: "comparable store sales in February were 17 percent higher"; Boehne: "momentum is very well entrenched". Summary: broad strength across autos, housing, capex. Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 67 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Keehn: "productivity improvements, which continue to be very impressive"; Greenspan: "extraordinary productivity increases that are keeping unit labor costs down". Summary: productivity seen as strong. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Phillips: "the overall cost of capital remains relatively low"; Kelley: the long-rate rise "is a powerful restraint". Summary: bond sell-off tightened conditions but credit remains available and risk premia low. Direction: below neutral (still supportive).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 47 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Greenspan: bond market "tried to rally... and got its head cut off"; Broaddus: "one of the biggest backups in long rates in a long time". Summary: large repricing without described dysfunction. Direction: normal.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: yield spreads "are still quite low by any historic standard"; commercial paper/bill spread "still basically low". Summary: spreads widened modestly from a very compressed level. Direction: below neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "prick the bubble in the equity markets"; Lindsey: margin debt at a ratio "not seen since" before the 1987 crash. Summary: valuation concern elevated, partly corrected. Direction: valuation concern high (low score).

**BANKING_STRESS: 37 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Phillips: "Banks are in a strong position to finance further expansion"; McDonough: rumors about two money center banks that required a public statement, "resolved with almost no publicity". Summary: banks healthy, system fragility a worry. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58, INFLATION_SERVICES: 44, INFLATION_HOUSING: 55, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62.** Evidence: Keehn: steel prices, "prices in the manufacturing sector may be firming... offset by moderations in the service sector"; Greenspan: owners' equivalent rent "up .6 percent, which was way above average"; Melzer: "inflationary expectations have also increased".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 +-5 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan: "we have to restore policy to neutrality as fast as we can"; Boehne: "we ought to move in that direction decisively". Summary: majority backs tightening, a sizeable group wants 50 bp, none want easing. Direction: hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 +-7 [High].** Evidence: roll call has Broaddus and Jordan voting No; Melzer: "I favor moving more aggressively... a 50 basis point increase". Summary: two formal dissents on magnitude and sharp debate on 25 vs 50 and on directive symmetry, but common view on direction.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 66 +-7 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan: "I'm worried that we could break the back of this financial system"; McDonough: rumors "could... spiral into a liquidity problem". Summary: market fragility is the main reason for the 25 bp size and a central topic for the Chair, McDonough, Phillips, Lindsey.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 74 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "we are going to 4 percent by midyear, moving only at meetings"; Keehn: "I think we will be moving again". Summary: members expect further hikes at coming meetings, with the symmetric directive chosen mainly to avoid precedent and uncertainty. Direction: tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: McDonough: "I personally have much greater uncertainty"; Stern: "the confidence interval around any forecast is very wide". Summary: several members stress unusual uncertainty about market fragility and the absent inflation response, while forecasts largely agree.
