# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1998-09-29

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion with staff turns removed. Order of content: (a) a long Committee debate on disclosure of the policy "tilt" and the timing of minutes, (b) members' questions in the staff Q&A (Russia/spreads, TIPS spread, Brazil, saving rate), (c) the economic go-around, (d) the policy go-around (25bp cut, symmetric vs asymmetric directive), (e) an afternoon discussion of the Long-Term Capital Management rescue led by the Vice Chair. Some turns appear as bare fragments (answers by staff removed); nothing is garbled enough to hamper scoring.

**Era context.** Autumn 1998, after the Russian default and devaluation, with a global flight to quality, wide credit spreads, and a decline in US equities. I score only what speakers say, not what I recall about outcomes.

**Speakers and views.**
- Nearly every president and governor reports a still-strong domestic economy, "extremely tight" labor markets and benign prices, but a shift in confidence, weakening manufacturing/agriculture/energy, and downside risks from abroad and from financial markets.
- Moskow, Parry, Boehne, Guynn, Hoenig, Gramlich, Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson: risks have shifted to the downside, support a measured easing.
- McTeer: easing earlier and by more ("too little too late").
- Broaddus, Jordan, Minehan, Stern, Poole: caution; skeptical about the urgency, concerned about M2 and inflation credibility, but all support the 25bp cut.
- McDonough, Poole, Ferguson, Gramlich: credit spreads "very wide", credit crunch as a downside risk.
- Greenspan: limited hard data of weakening, but a profound change in risk psychology; recommends 25bp and a mild easing tilt.

**Dimension directions in words (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Moskow says "price pressures remain benign", Stern sees it "very hard to see any inflationary momentum building", McTeer reports deflationary price declines in many sectors, and Parry's forecast has core CPI at about 2 percent and falling. Offsets are very tight labor markets and rising wages (Guynn: 4+ percent), M2 growth (Broaddus, Jordan), and Meyer's "bit higher inflation". Net: modest, contained, 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as modestly above potential now, with the slowdown still prospective. Ferguson: GDP "likely to grow at or above trend for this quarter and the next"; Meyer: "second-half GDP growth still looks to be close to trend"; labor markets "exceptionally tight" (Greenspan). Next year is forecast below trend, but I score the present. Bright-line: "at or above trend" gives above 50; 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Broaddus "extraordinarily tight labor market... workers with even minimal job skills are hard to find"; Stern: Minnesota unemployment at a record low; Gramlich: unemployment "sitting at 4-1/2 percent"; Jordan: "extremely tight", teller pay up. Unemployment of 4.5 percent alone would suggest about 50, but the language is extreme and the unemployment level is below the mid-1990s estimates of NAIRU that Gramlich cites. 68.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Still strong now, weakening ahead. Retail "very strong" (Jordan, Hoenig), autos healthy (Stern, Moskow), housing strong, mortgage lending at records; but manufacturing and capital spending are softening (Broaddus: planned capex slowed sharply; Motorola suspension; Hoenig: firms backing off investment) and Greenspan expects a drop in capital appropriations. Net slightly above trend: 56.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only one mention (Parry: "somewhat higher trend productivity growth"). Weak evidence, slightly above trend: 57, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Conditions have tightened sharply even as Treasury rates fell: spreads "very wide" (McDonough, Poole), banks tightening standards and the syndicated loan market less accommodating (Ferguson, Minehan, Greenspan citing the senior loan officer survey), REIT/CRE financing drying up, equity prices down about 15 percent, credit crunch risk named by several. Offset: low Treasury yields and cheap mortgage credit (Kelley, Poole). Tighter than normal; 65. Bright-line satisfied (no "accommodative" characterisation of overall conditions; the only easy-credit remarks refer to the earlier period).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss Treasury yields and the yield curve and a TIPS/nominal spread curiosity (Parry, Greenspan, Gramlich), and flight to quality. No one describes Treasury market functioning or dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is stated as high. McDonough: "The current spreads are very wide indeed... well beyond a return to normalcy"; Poole: "now much too wide... highly abnormal"; Stern says spreads "have now widened out" from far too narrow; Minehan's contacts see it as partly a return from too-narrow spreads, but the Vice Chair and Poole are explicit that the correction overshot. Stated high level so score above 50: 78. Bright-line satisfied.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members discuss valuation directly. Parry: market "still overvalued according to most models"; Stern/Jordan: the decline was hoped for and necessary; Poole: "a very typical decline"; Greenspan: a $3 trillion loss with a profound wealth and risk-aversion effect; Gramlich expects a further decline. So: correction from overvaluation, orderly-but-significant, concern about spillover but not an undervaluation view. 57.
- BANKING_STRESS: Emerging stress rather than crisis: banks tightening standards, "credit crunch" risk (McDonough, Ferguson), big losses at securities firms, rural banks anticipating loan problems (Hoenig), and the LTCM episode with many bank counterparties exposed and Greenspan stressing exam adequacy. Counterpoints: Hoenig's banks' portfolios "satisfactory", Boehne's bankers' loose terms, Minehan's CEO says settlements unaffected. Note no one says banks are unsound or short of capital. 57.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar described as "strong"/"higher": McTeer "strong dollar" hurting exports and Latin America, Parry "the higher dollar" restraining inflation, Broaddus "stronger dollar". Not discussed in detail. Appreciation implied; 62, moderate-low confidence.
- INFLATION sub-components: GOODS: widespread price declines (McTeer list), negative materials price indexes (Guynn), commodity prices falling; 25. SERVICES: sparse (theme park ticket increases, McTeer's medical costs "on the rise"); 55 with thin evidence. HOUSING: only passing remarks (McTeer: housing costs on the rise; office rents "not rising as rapidly"), not shelter inflation; N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Parry "diminished inflation expectations", Gramlich on a "general downgrading of inflation worries" from the TIPS spread, Broaddus "bond rates show no increase due to heightened inflation expectations"; anchored/below target: 40.
- Productivity and the sub-components are consistent with the parent: goods very low, services modestly firm, expectations low, but tight labor and M2 concerns lift the parent to 38.

**Tier 3 in words.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee unanimously cuts the funds rate 25bp; the majority sees risks to the downside and favors a mild easing tilt (by "the smallest of majorities"). The cut is described as cautious, deliberate, preemptive "insurance"; McTeer would go further; Broaddus, Jordan and Minehan are reluctant and want symmetry; Broaddus and Poole stress the price-stability objective. Moderate dovish with caution: 34.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero formal dissents, unanimous on the 25bp; disagreement on the tilt (roughly even, with several "almost indifferent") and a long disclosure debate with distinct camps (immediate tilt release vs not), though that is procedural. Kelley supports "with considerable hesitation and misgivings"; McTeer wanted a bigger move; Broaddus feels "ahead of the curve". Disagreement is tactical rather than about assessment. 30.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Prominent: spreads, credit crunch risk, risk aversion are central in the go-around of McDonough, Poole, Ferguson, Broaddus, Greenspan, Meyer; the afternoon is devoted to the LTCM rescue and systemic risk (Meyer's systemic-risk study, Broaddus on moral hazard). Rivals the economic assessment: 80.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The tilt toward ease is adopted narrowly but not announced; several expect further cuts "better than 50 percent" (Moskow, Ferguson, Parry), while Greenspan says events will drive and his tilt preference is weak. Easing lean with data dependence: 36.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Poole: "the situation is quite uncertain... the variance of the outlook has certainly increased"; Rivlin: "do not know where the current downslide will end"; Minehan: "very humble about our ability to forecast"; Greenspan/McDonough: LTCM damage "could not be easily ascertained"; Meyer: asymmetric risks, fat tail. Many emphasize, though most economic reports are confident that domestic data remain strong. 63.

**Polarity verification.** Higher = more of the named construct. CREDIT_SPREADS: evidence says high level, so more stress, score above 50: matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: evidence of tightening, above 50: matches. DOLLAR: strong, above 50: matches. BANKING_STRESS: emerging stress, not "sound/healthy" characterization: matches. HAWKISH_DOVISH: easing majority, below 50: matches.

**Coherence checks.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 68: 13 apart (<15). Labor is described in stronger terms than output; growth is slowing toward trend, so output gap is less extended.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 56: aligned.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 65 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 78: both elevated; spreads more extreme than the overall conditions because Treasury rates and mortgage credit are easy.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, no description of Treasury dysfunction (flight to quality only).
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 38 vs sub-components (goods 25, services 55, expectations 40): consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: not described by participants as functioning/dysfunction. INFLATION_HOUSING: no shelter-price inflation discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 38 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Moskow, "price pressures remain benign"; Stern, "very hard to see any inflationary momentum building". Summary: contained prices with tight labor and M2 as offsets. Direction: below neutral because most members see little pass-through and falling commodity/goods prices.

**OUTPUT_GAP 55 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson, "at or above trend for this quarter and the next"; Meyer, second-half GDP "close to trend". Summary: modestly above potential today, with a slowdown forecast. Direction: above neutral because labor markets are exceptionally tight and growth is at/above trend.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 68 +-6, High.** Evidence: Broaddus, "extraordinarily tight labor market conditions... workers with even minimal job skills are hard to find"; Gramlich, unemployment "sitting at 4-1/2 percent". Summary: very tight across nearly every district. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 56 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Jordan, "Our retail sales have been very strong"; Broaddus, new orders and planned capital spending "slowed quite sharply". Summary: strong consumption and housing now, softening manufacturing and investment ahead. Direction: slightly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 57 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Parry, "somewhat higher trend productivity growth". Summary: a single passing mention. Direction: slightly above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 65 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Broaddus, "apparently tighter conditions in loan and other credit markets"; Ferguson, "syndicated loan market appears to have become less accommodating". Summary: credit availability and spreads tightened sharply, partly offset by low Treasury yields. Direction: above neutral because conditions have tightened.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not discussed as a market-functioning matter.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 78 +-6, High.** Evidence: McDonough, "The current spreads are very wide indeed"; Poole, "now much too wide... highly abnormal". Summary: explicitly high level after overshooting a correction from too-narrow spreads. Direction: above neutral on the stated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 57 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "the stock market is still overvalued according to most models"; Poole, "a very typical decline; its size is nothing abnormal". Summary: orderly correction from overvalued levels, with wealth-effect concern. Direction: modestly above neutral on the concern scale.

**BANKING_STRESS 57 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: McDonough, "we could get a credit crunch. I am not forecasting that"; Hoenig, banks' portfolios "satisfactory" but loan problems anticipated. Summary: tightening standards and counterparty losses, with no solvency concern raised. Direction: modestly above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 +-8, Low.** Evidence: McTeer, "a strong dollar continue to batter the Latin American economies"; Parry, "the higher dollar". Summary: dollar treated as strong in passing. Direction: above neutral (appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS 25.** McTeer, price declines in gasoline, petrochemicals, semiconductors, computers, metals, paper, lumber; Guynn, materials price indexes negative.
**INFLATION_SERVICES 55.** Jordan, theme parks raised tickets 6.5 percent; McTeer, medical costs rising (thin evidence).
**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 40.** Parry, "diminished inflation expectations"; Gramlich, TIPS premium reflects "a general downgrading of inflation worries".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 34 +-6, High.** Evidence: Greenspan, "unanimity for a 25 basis point reduction and the smallest of majorities for asymmetry"; Broaddus, "I am no longer in favor of tightening monetary policy". Summary: modest, cautious easing; some want more, a few want symmetry. Direction: below neutral (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 30 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Kelley, will support easing "not without considerable hesitation and misgivings"; Boehne/Stern vs Hoenig/Meyer on tilt release. Summary: unanimous vote, split over tilt and disclosure procedure. Direction: low-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 80 +-7, High.** Evidence: Poole, spreads "highly abnormal"; the LTCM session led by McDonough, "collapse... would create chaotic financial markets". Summary: spreads, credit crunch, risk aversion and LTCM rival the macro assessment. Direction: high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 36 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Moskow, "better than 50 percent probability that we will have to lower rates again"; Greenspan, preference for tilt "a lot less strong" than for the cut. Summary: narrow easing bias, tilt unannounced. Direction: below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 63 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Poole, "the situation is quite uncertain right now. The variance of the outlook has certainly increased"; Rivlin, "do not know where the current downslide will end". Summary: many stress unusual uncertainty and asymmetric risks, but domestic data confidence tempers it. Direction: elevated.
