# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion transcript, meeting 2009-04-29 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It contains the opening staff Q&A (members' questions only), a short open discussion on the balance sheet and exit strategy, the economic go-around (afternoon session), the Chairman's overnight summary, a policy go-round on "alternative A versus B" for the statement, a statement-wording exchange with a straw vote, a transparency report by Governor Kohn, and a roll-call vote read-out. All ten voters are recorded as "Yes". No dissent.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2009, deep recession and financial crisis, funds rate at the zero bound, large-scale asset purchases under way. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Rosengren, Pianalto, Lockhart, Hoenig, Evans, Stern, Fisher, Plosser, Bullard, Lacker: presidents.
- Yellen, Dudley, Kohn, Warsh, Duke, Tarullo, Bernanke: governors, Vice Chairman and Chairman.
- Yellen: very pessimistic on labor, wants alternative A (more Treasury purchases), and voted yes on B.
- Plosser: more optimistic on growth, doubts the size of the output gap, sees upside inflation risk, wants hikes starting late 2009.
- Bullard: wants state-contingent policy.
- Warsh: upside inflation risk.
- Kohn: finds the outcomes "not satisfactory" but backs B.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as BELOW the midpoint.
  - Bernanke's summary: "Most see inflation as remaining low for the foreseeable future."
  - Yellen has core PCE dipping to 1.5% then 1%.
  - Evans has core PCE at about 1.25% in 2010-11.
  - Pianalto has core at 1-1.5%.
  - Kohn: "inflation should continue to drift down."
  - Rosengren still sees a "significant risk that deflation will not be avoided".
  - Deflation risk "moderated" but remains a worry.
  - A minority (Plosser, Warsh, Lacker, Lockhart) stress upside risk in the outyears, which is forecast and not the current level.
  - Gotcha: score the present. Result is about 28, below the 2009 deflation-fear anchor (15-20) because wages and core held up ("resilience in wages and core inflation", Yellen) and deflation risk eased.
- OUTPUT_GAP: BELOW midpoint, deep slack.
  - Kohn: "a large output gap persisting for some time"; "considerable output gap".
  - Yellen: "dramatic increase in slack".
  - Bernanke: "Slack looks to be high and increasing".
  - Plosser, Bullard, Pianalto, Warsh argue the gap is overstated, but none says the economy is at potential. Even Plosser says unemployment at 6.5% is above steady state.
  - Score 22, which respects the below-potential bright line.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: far BELOW midpoint.
  - Yellen: "the data are appalling"; unemployment up 1.7 points in four months; underemployment about 14.5%.
  - Fisher: job cuts exceed hires 10 to 1.
  - Pianalto sees unemployment peaking above 10%. Yellen sees 9.6%.
  - Bernanke: "only the slightest indications of improvement in labor markets".
  - Score about 18.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: BELOW trend, stabilizing at low levels.
  - Kohn: "stability in household spending", "albeit at very low levels".
  - Lacker: capex index remains negative, business spending weak.
  - Warsh and Evans expect capex downside.
  - Inventory liquidation is brisk.
  - Bernanke: "Consumer spending shows some signs of bottoming out".
  - Yellen: subdued consumer spending for an extended period.
  - Net score 30, a little above the Q4-Q1 collapse anchor of 15-20.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Members do not assess productivity growth.
  - Plosser's "negative productivity shock to the financial intermediation sector" is about equilibrium output, not measured productivity.
  - Kohn's remark that the productivity and costs measure was "flat" is a passing mention.
  - N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: TIGHT but improving, so ABOVE 50.
  - Yellen: "Overall, financial conditions have improved."
  - Kohn: "borrowing rates, terms, and conditions for many private borrowers are still very tight".
  - Kohn: "tight credit for a while".
  - Bernanke: "Credit continues to contract"; "Lack of financing for commercial real estate remains a particularly serious issue".
  - Hoenig: banks are "holding back on lending".
  - Bullard: "Time has not been a healer for financial markets."
  - Offsetting: Warsh and Evans report better tone, lower all-in financing costs, equities up.
  - Score 66. Bright line: tight means above 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries come up only as policy (purchases) and as an ex-China concern about a possible sharp rise in yields (Bullard, Fisher quoting Chinese officials). No market dysfunction is described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level governs.
  - Spreads have narrowed, but the stated level is still high.
  - Bullard: stress measures show "only modest improvement relative to very high peaks"; "horrible numbers".
  - Bernanke: "confidence in banks as shown in CDS spreads remains tenuous".
  - Kohn: rates "very tight".
  - Warsh: bank debt at "275 to 325" spreads, called remarkable and encouraging, which is still wide in absolute terms.
  - Evans and Fisher note spreads narrowed "somewhat".
  - Level is elevated, so score above 50: 66.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities are discussed as a rebound.
  - Warsh: a "$3 trillion addition to net worth", an unwinding of fear and risk premium.
  - Bullard: do not put much weight on stock prices, "the guys that brought us the tech bubble".
  - These are price and signal remarks, not valuation-concern remarks, so the default zone of 45-55 applies. Score 52, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Elevated stress.
  - Dudley: "we still do not have the banking system on a sound footing".
  - Yellen: nontrivial probability that common shareholders of major banks are wiped out.
  - Tarullo: institutions "tethered to government guarantees".
  - Lockhart, Hoenig and Duke: severe CRE stress, rising non-accruals.
  - Stern: "two large and very sick institutions".
  - Counterweights: Dudley says banks under the adverse scenario would be "well capitalized", and earnings were better than expected (Duke, Bernanke). Overall stress is high and the stress-test event is a perceived tail risk. Score 72.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar appears only in Fisher's quotation of a Chinese ex-official predicting depreciation. Members state no view on dollar movement. N/A.

**5. Polarity check (ambiguous items).**
- Credit spreads: higher means more credit stress. Evidence says spreads fell but remain at high levels, so the score is above 50 and below the 2008 Q4 anchor of 80-95. Consistent.
- Banking stress: higher means more stress. Evidence is "not on a sound footing", so above 50. Consistent.
- Financial conditions: higher means tighter. Evidence is "still very tight" but improving, so above 50. Consistent.
- Inflation: higher means more pressure. Evidence is low and drifting down, so below 50. Consistent.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds the policy rate at the zero bound and holds asset purchases at the March-announced pace. The balance sheet is expanding massively, so under the QE-era rule the base is dovish. Yellen, with Kohn partly sympathetic, argues for more purchases (alternative A). Bernanke says "more stimulus would be good, as suggested by the Taylor rule". Almost everyone else prefers B (stand pat on amounts). Plosser, Warsh, Stern and Hoenig show hawkish concerns about the balance sheet and inflation risk but support continued expansion or accept existing commitments. Plosser wants tightening by late 2009 to early 2010 in his own forecast. Kohn thinks tightening is not near. Score about 25, the QE-era dovish range, slightly lifted by the "no more" camp.
- Dissent: Zero formal dissents; all ten voters said yes. Yellen preferred A but voted yes. A real split exists on communication (Plosser and Bullard versus Fisher, Kohn, Bernanke) and on the size of the output gap and the inflation outlook, plus a Warsh-Fisher-Hoenig challenge on one statement sentence (straw vote 10 to keep), though the sentence was later reworded. Moderate emphasis differences, no formal dissent, one clear camp (Yellen). Score about 30.
- Financial stability emphasis: Very high. Stress tests dominate the economic go-around (Rosengren, Plosser, Yellen, Lockhart, Evans, Pianalto, Stern, Dudley, Kohn, Warsh, Duke, Tarullo). Credit markets, CRE, TARP, TALF and liquidity programs all feature. Score about 72.
- Forward-guidance tone: Not about funds-rate guidance explicitly. Signals are: no tightening soon (Kohn), balance-sheet exit strategy to be planned in June, statement language retaining "continue to evaluate" the purchases, asset purchases continuing through autumn and year-end. Plosser alone argues for hikes. Easing lean with data dependence, so score about 30.
- Uncertainty: Very high language. Pianalto: "uncertainty around my projection is unusually great". Bullard: actual events "far outstripped" confidence bands. Tarullo: "false dawn" analysis, "low levels of assurance". Stern: "level of uncertainty". Lockhart: "ambiguous". Kohn: "the risks are huge". Yellen: "fraught with peril". Score about 70.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (22): ok.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 for "tight" (66): ok.
- CREDIT_SPREADS above 50 for stated high level (66): ok.
- BANKING_STRESS above 50 for "not sound footing" (72): ok.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 22 vs LABOR 18: within 15.
- OUTPUT_GAP 22 vs CONSUMPTION 30: within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 66 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 66: co-move.
- Treasury stress N/A in a non-dysfunction discussion: allowed.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 28 is consistent with the only sub-component scored, expectations at about 50. Expectations are described as anchored with some upside worry. Parent is low on current prints and a large output gap.

**9. N/A justifications.**
- PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment of productivity growth by members.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no description of Treasury market functioning.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member view on dollar movement.
- INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as separate price categories (only aggregate core and commodity prices mentioned in passing).

**Text problem:** Staff turns are removed, so many members' follow-up questions appear without the answers. A few fragments (for example "all 1 people" in the straw vote) look like extraction artifacts but do not affect scoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-6 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Bernanke summary, "Most see inflation as remaining low for the foreseeable future"; Yellen, "core inflation to dip down to 1½ percent this year and 1 percent next year".
- Summary: Core inflation is low and drifting down with large slack, deflation risk eased but not gone; upside worries (Plosser, Warsh) are forward-looking.
- Direction: Below neutral because inflation is below the 2 percent objective and heading lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 22 +-6 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "a large output gap persisting for some time"; Bernanke, "Slack looks to be high and increasing".
- Summary: Deep slack is the consensus; the dissenting view (Plosser, Bullard, Pianalto) is only that it is smaller.
- Direction: Below potential because virtually all participants describe large slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 +-5 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the data are appalling... unemployment rate has risen 1.7 percentage points over the past four months"; Fisher, "job cuts exceed hires by a 10 to 1 margin".
- Summary: Unemployment near 9 percent heading toward 10, underemployment record-high.
- Direction: Far below tight because of mass layoffs and rising unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 30 +-7 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "stability in household spending and consumption... albeit at very low levels"; Lacker, capital expenditure index "remains negative".
- Summary: Spending has stabilized at depressed levels, investment weak.
- Direction: Below trend, with signs of bottoming.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 66 +-7 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "borrowing rates, terms, and conditions for many private borrowers are still very tight"; Yellen, "Overall, financial conditions have improved."
- Summary: Improving from crisis but credit still contracting and CRE unfunded.
- Direction: Above neutral (tight) because credit remains scarce and bank lending is constrained.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 66 +-8 (Moderate confidence).**
- Evidence: Bullard, "modest improvement relative to very high peaks"; Warsh, issuance at "275 to 325" over Treasuries, described as encouraging.
- Summary: Spreads have narrowed from crisis peaks but the stated level is still elevated.
- Direction: Above neutral because the level is described as still high, not because of direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +-8 (Low confidence).**
- Evidence: Warsh, "$3 trillion addition to the net worth of U.S. households"; Bullard, "I would not put too much weight on stock price movements".
- Summary: Participants discuss the rebound and its signal value, with no valuation judgment.
- Direction: At neutral by the default for price-move-only commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 72 +-6 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Dudley, "we still do not have the banking system on a sound footing"; Stern, "two large and very sick institutions".
- Summary: Stress test, CRE losses and capital constraints dominate; earnings are better but confidence remains fragile.
- Direction: Above neutral because of significant stress and pending stress-test results.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Measures of long-run inflation expectations have also been reasonably stable"; Lacker, expectations "centered near 2 percent".
- Summary: Anchored near target with upside risk concerns from a minority.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 25 +-6 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "I favor alternative A... time to wade in by substantially increasing our purchases of Treasury securities"; Bernanke, "understanding that more stimulus would be good, as suggested by the Taylor rule... better to stand pat on the purchases this time."
- Summary: Zero rate plus ongoing large asset purchases (QE era); majority stands pat on amounts, with one participant seeking more and a few uneasy hawkish voices.
- Direction: Dovish, stand-pat on a very accommodative stance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, all ten "Yes"; Yellen favors A while the rest favor B; Warsh, Fisher and Hoenig challenge a new sentence; Plosser and Bullard push alternate wording.
- Summary: Unanimous vote with a clear minority view and substantive disagreements on the output gap and communication.
- Direction: Low-moderate because there are no formal dissents but real camps on tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 +-7 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "My primary concerns are that the reaction to the stress test is unpredictable"; Kohn, "Financial markets are fragile".
- Summary: Banks, stress tests, funding markets and liquidity programs are central to nearly every economic go-around and much of the policy round.
- Direction: High attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +-9 (Moderate confidence).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "we are not going to be there any time soon" [for tightening]; Plosser, "we begin raising the funds rate by the end of this year".
- Summary: Majority sees no tightening for a long time, purchases continue and exit planning is deferred to June; Plosser is the lone hike advocate.
- Direction: Easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 70 +-7 (High confidence).**
- Evidence: Pianalto, "the level of uncertainty around my projection is unusually great"; Tarullo, "considerable uncertainty whether these are green shoots... or a school of red herring".
- Summary: Many participants emphasize false-dawn risk, model failure and stress-test unpredictability.
- Direction: High, though not at the March-2009 peak since the outlook improved a bit.
