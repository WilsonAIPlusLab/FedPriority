# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 1994-07-06 (transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of member and participant turns only (staff turns removed). It includes members' questions during staff Q&A, a long exchange on foreign-exchange intervention, the economic go-around, a side discussion on monetary-aggregate ranges and whether to release 1996 projections, and the policy go-around with the vote read-out.

**2. Era context (not allowed to override the text).** Mid-1994, in a tightening cycle. Members refer to 125bp of tightening so far, a funds rate around 4.25 percent, and a decision on a possible further move. Unemployment is 6 percent, CPI inflation about 3 percent, and the dollar has weakened against the yen.

**3. Speakers.**
- Hawkish voices: Melzer (50bp plus a discount-rate move now), Broaddus (25 to 50bp now), Hoenig, Kelley, Jordan (inflation worry, but would wait for price data), Parry, Stern (more to do over time).
- Cautious or balanced voices: Blinder ("three bears", balanced risks), Forrestal (symmetry), Laware ("Cassandra", take a breather), Lindsey (opposes asymmetry), Boehne, McTeer.
- Chair Greenspan and Vice Chairman McDonough: hold now because a move would be read as supporting the dollar, with an asymmetric tightening bias and a possible 50bp move in August.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above target and rising. CPI is around 3 percent, which is not price stability. Melzer is "very uneasy" and Broaddus cites the purchasing managers' prices-paid reading at 48 percent. Others (Stern, Boehne, Minehan, Forrestal) say pressures are contained and finished goods cannot be repriced. Net: modestly to clearly above target, about 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy at or slightly above potential. Parry says "most if not all of the slack...has been used up". Hoenig says growth is "slightly above potential". Kelley says "approaching full utilization". Melzer says "unsustainably high". Blinder says "a little bit further to go", and Greenspan says capacity limits are fuzzy. Above 50 and modest: 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 6 percent, which the rubric table would put at 31 to 45. But members say it is at the bottom of the range of NAIRU estimates (Broaddus), with spot shortages of truck drivers and skilled construction workers (Keehn, Forrestal, Melzer) and wage bumps. Others (Blinder, Greenspan) note fewer shortage reports than expected. I settle near NAIRU, slightly tight: 53, with a wide band.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is strong but moderating. Autos, housing and manufacturing are strong, Melzer says growth is "unsustainably high", and several districts report moderation. Above trend: 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Greenspan discusses the GDI/GDP gap and says falling productivity in a rising economy makes no sense. This is a data-quality point, not a level assessment. N/A.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Long rates are up about 2 points (Broaddus). Phillips says markets "survived significant turmoil", with orderly contraction of exposure, bank credit expanded and "no gridlock". Mortgage rates are damping housing. Neither accommodative nor restrictive language is used about the present. Somewhat tighter than a year ago but orderly: 55.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield and volatility remarks (Phillips on bond-market "volatility concerns", Greenspan on 30-year moves). No dysfunction. Normal functioning: 42, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed by participants. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Phillips says stocks are "down for the year" but sideways for three months, and Greenspan says stocks were up that day. There is no valuation commentary, so default to neutral: 51, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips says "bank internal risk management models appear to have worked", "no major U.S. casualties" and "bank credit has expanded". Healthy, so below 50: 35.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar has weakened, notably against the yen (about 98 to 99), and Melzer cites trade-weighted depreciation. Weakening means a score below 50: 24.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Hold at alternative B with an asymmetric tightening bias. A camp (Melzer, Broaddus) wants to tighten now, and many others say more tightening is coming (Phillips, Stern, Hoenig, McDonough, Kelley). The majority holds only because a move would be read as defending the dollar. That is a hold with a tightening bias, leaning to moderate hawkish.
- Dissent: Broaddus voted No on the directive (wanted a move now). Lindsey was recorded No on the directive's operational paragraph but afterwards asked to be recorded as Yes, so it is a withdrawn or retracted dissent. Lindsey and Jordan also voted No on ratifying the foreign-exchange transactions. Members split on symmetric versus asymmetric (Lindsey, Jordan, Stern, Keehn, Minehan, Phillips and Boehne lean symmetric). Members also split on economic risk (Laware and Blinder versus Melzer and Broaddus). Disagreement is mostly about tactics, with some about assessment.
- Financial-stability attention: The exchange-rate and intervention discussion is long, and Greenspan raises the stability of the financial system. Phillips has one explicit paragraph on markets and banks. This is moderate but mostly about FX policy.
- Forward guidance: An asymmetric directive toward tightening. Greenspan floats 50bp in August and a possible conference call. McDonough sees a possible move before the next meeting. That is a clear but not aggressive tightening signal.
- Uncertainty: Phillips cites "more uncertainty around the table than usual". Greenspan says "we don't know how far the unwinding...will go". Laware says "the tea leaves are still very hard to read". Boehne and Parry also stress uncertainty. Elevated but not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: above potential language is mild and the score is above 50. Satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: no accommodative or tight wording about the present. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: healthy language, below 50. Satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: depreciation, below 50. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, below 55. Satisfied.

**8. Coherence checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 53: aligned.
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60: gap of 6, within tolerance. Strong spending is moderating.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads are N/A, so no conflict.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, near 40 to 50. Fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 against sub-components: goods 58 and expectations 66 are consistent. Services and housing are N/A.

**9. N/A justifications.**
- CREDIT_SPREADS: not raised by any participant.
- PRODUCTIVITY: only a data-measurement remark, no assessment of the level.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: not discussed.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: only anecdotes about construction materials and land prices, not shelter inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "makes me very uneasy about the possible path of future inflation. I think the risks are very clearly in that direction". Broaddus, "latest purchasing managers' report...48 percent, which was about double what it was three months ago". Counterweight: Stern, "perhaps the economy is a little less inflation prone than I thought".
- Summary: Inflation is about 3 percent and risks are tilted up, with a minority calling pressures contained.
- Direction: above neutral because the realized rate is above price stability and members expect more pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Parry, "most if not all of the slack in the economy probably has been used up". Hoenig, "growth should remain slightly above potential in the second half".
- Summary: Near to slightly above potential, with the measurement of capacity itself disputed.
- Direction: above neutral because of explicit "slightly above potential" and "slack used up" language, though Blinder and Greenspan doubt how tight things are.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "unemployment rate has already fallen to the bottom of the range of current estimates of the natural rate". Keehn, "an extreme shortage of truck drivers".
- Summary: Unemployment is 6 percent but members place it at NAIRU with spot shortages. Blinder notes fewer tightness reports than expected.
- Direction: slightly above neutral on member language, held down by the 6 percent level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "the economy is still growing at an unsustainably high rate". Keehn, "Midwest economy is continuing to operate at what I think is a comparatively strong pace".
- Summary: Strong spending, with moderation emerging in housing and retail.
- Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**
- Greenspan's GDI/GDP remarks concern data reliability, not a level of productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "markets have survived significant turmoil beginning with the Fed tightening in February...Bank credit has expanded". Broaddus, "30-year bond rate has risen two full percentage points".
- Summary: Conditions have tightened with higher rates but remain orderly.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because of rate increases and a long-rate backup, with no stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "We may still have some volatility concerns about the bond market". Greenspan, "the 30-year bond was down 10/32".
- Summary: Yield moves and mild volatility, with no dysfunction cited.
- Direction: at the normal range, below the stress threshold.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A**
- Not discussed by participants.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 51 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "The stock market is certainly down for the year but it has moved sideways for the last three months".
- Summary: Price moves reported without valuation views.
- Direction: neutral by the default rule.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "Bank internal risk management models appear to have worked...no major U.S. casualties".
- Summary: Banks are described as healthy and resilient. One speaker, with commercial real estate still soft in some districts.
- Direction: below neutral because banking is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 24 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: McDonough, "a very significant weakening of the dollar on no volume...main thing going on...is that we have a very strong Japanese yen". Melzer, "a depreciation going on more broadly on a trade-weighted basis".
- Summary: A weakening dollar that prompted intervention and shaped the policy debate.
- Direction: below neutral because the dollar is depreciating.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58** (value only). Forrestal, "manufacturers are also experiencing pressures in the cost of commodity inputs...not able to pass these price pressures through". Phillips, "pressures in the basic commodities, competition is constraining final goods prices".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Only construction-materials and land-price anecdotes.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 66** (value only). Broaddus, "inflation expectations are stuck at too high a level and they may even be rising". Melzer, "private-sector forecasters ratcheting up their inflation expectations for 1996".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 61 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "I'd move the funds rate up 50 basis points". Greenspan, "I would like to stay at 'B' unchanged, but I'd like to be asymmetric...we might possibly want to do 50 basis points in August".
- Summary: A hold, but with a clear tightening bias, a hawkish minority wanting a move now, and the rest saying the hold is only about the dollar optics.
- Direction: hawkish because the majority expects more tightening, though the current action is a hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, "President Broaddus No" and "Governor Lindsey No*" (later requested to be recorded as Yes). Lindsey and Jordan voted No on the FX ratification.
- Summary: One firm dissent for tightening, one dissent withdrawn, a camp split on symmetry, and sharp but civil exchanges (Broaddus versus Greenspan on intervention, Parry versus Jordan on waiting for CPI).
- Direction: moderate to notable disagreement, mostly on tactics and some on risk assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "the damage it could do to the financial system if we are perceived to be at loggerheads with the Treasury". Phillips on markets and banks surviving turmoil.
- Summary: A long FX discussion and one explicit markets paragraph, but little on financial-stability risk as such.
- Direction: limited to moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I am biased toward the up side...we have to be biased toward restraint". McDonough, "tightening move could well be as much as 50 basis points".
- Summary: An asymmetric directive toward tightening with a conference call option, expecting a possible 50bp in August.
- Direction: hawkish, a clear tightening signal short of a commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "I felt more uncertainty around the table than usual". Laware, "the tea leaves are still very hard to read".
- Summary: Several members stress unusual uncertainty about capacity and the pace of slowing, though many still express firm views.
- Direction: elevated.
