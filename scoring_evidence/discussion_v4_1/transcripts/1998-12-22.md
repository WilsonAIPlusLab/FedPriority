# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 1998-12-22 (transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: a short opening (housekeeping, members' questions during the staff briefings), the economic go-around of Reserve Bank presidents and governors, the Chairman's policy comments, the policy go-around, a go-around on directive wording (options 2 and 3, "in coming months," the money sentence), a go-around on disclosure policy, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed, so the opening shows only members' side of Q&A. The directive-wording and disclosure sections are procedural and I did not score them as conditions. They do bear on forward guidance, dissent and uncertainty.

**2. Era context.** Late 1998, after a fall of global financial turmoil and three funds rate cuts (75bp in total, per Meyer). The funds rate is 4-3/4 percent. I scored only what members say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Greenspan: remarkable, "almost surely unforecastable" economy, no pricing power, labor markets "marginally tighter," equity and wealth effects, risks "widened on both sides." Policy back to balance, so stay put.
- Broaddus: labor markets "extraordinary" tight. Growth to trend will not relieve it. Goods price disinflation will bottom out. Risks skewed to the upside.
- Parry: strong growth, low inflation, upside risks.
- Moskow: benign price pressures, tight labor markets, a more balanced outlook.
- Poole: inflation risks "clearly on the upside." Worried about money growth. Would tighten if not for the shock to the market.
- Minehan: large risks both sides, "room to maneuver ... very small."
- Boehne: wide range of outcomes, low inflation.
- Guynn: risks balanced and symmetric. Be ready to tighten as readily as to ease.
- Stern: excessively elevated equity prices a risk. Accommodative credit and fast monetary growth give pause.
- McTeer: some risks, but worldwide deflationary pressure.
- Jordan: "brown spots" in inflation, froth in equities, money and credit growth must slow, disagreed with the November cut, tightening needed "at some point."
- Hoenig: pronounced upside risks, wants to unwind the easing unless shocks emerge.
- McDonough: risks "very well balanced," watchful waiting.
- Kelley: unsure of the direction of the next move.
- Gramlich: soft-landing conditions look right, comfortable with the stance.
- Ferguson: "murky," risks balanced, "caution" and "vigilance."
- Rivlin: a "hotter" policy instrument with shorter lags, but risks mostly on the downside.
- Meyer: wants vigilance against upside inflation risks later.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint and benign: "price pressures remain benign" (Moskow), "inflation currently remains exceptionally well behaved" (Broaddus), "lack of any pricing power" (Poole, Greenspan), core CPI about 2-1/4 (Parry). Offsetting this are risks that it will rise: Jordan's "brown spots," health care costs, and goods disinflation bottoming out. Current pressure is contained, so about 35.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Economy is above potential/trend. Broaddus: "we may still be above the maximum path on which the economy can grow without an increase in inflation." Meyer: "exceptional tightness now in labor markets," "slowdown to below-trend growth" is desirable. Greenbook-type slowing toward trend is expected. Ample industrial capacity (Parry) and absent inflation limit how far above I go. Modestly to clearly above: 61 (bright line > 50 satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. "Extraordinary tightness" (Broaddus), "extremely tight" (Guynn), "very tight" (Stern, Hoenig), Moskow's district unemployment 3.7%, "3.9 percent unemployment" test (Minehan), labor shortages and 10-12% wage plans (Jordan), "labor markets ... marginally tighter" (Greenspan). Little evidence of wage acceleration. Level governs: 74.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. "Strength of consumer demand has been very impressive" (Moskow), "brisk holiday sales" (Broaddus), "investment has been on a tear" (Kelley), housing strong, C&I demand the "strongest he can recall" (Jordan). Slowing is expected, and business investment is expected to decelerate. Above trend: 66.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. "Robust productivity growth" (Parry), "rather strong productivity gains" (Greenspan), "good productivity growth continued even in manufacturing" (Rivlin). Above trend: 66.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Supportive/accommodative. Meyer: "financial conditions ... continue to be supportive of growth." Stern: "generally accommodative credit conditions." Moskow: credit-availability worries eased, though markets are "still somewhat fragile." Also McDonough: fixed income markets "have not completely returned to normal." Bright line < 50: 38.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Meyer says "private risk spreads have widened," but the "absolute level of private borrowing costs ... is about what it was at the end of last year and only modestly above lows." Poole: "the bond market is functioning normally again; new issues back." Minehan: Wall Street financing dried up in the fall at higher financing rates, but that is past. Spreads are described as having widened, with the level near normal and no explicit "wide" or "elevated" language. Under the rule, direction with little level information gives 52 (side of the move), Low confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. The only Treasury-adjacent talk is Poole on the Desk's indexed-bond purchases (an operations question) and Greenspan's swap-spread queries (staff Q&A). "Fixed income markets" remarks are not Treasury-specific dysfunction.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed with valuation concern. Stern: "excessively elevated equity price." Jordan: "perpetual great expectations ... unrealistic," equity prices cannot rise at double digits. Greenspan: 20 percent annual growth "seems absurd," a bubble is visible only in retrospect. Meyer: "sustainability of current equity prices." Elevated-valuation concern: 27.
- BANKING_STRESS: Minimal direct discussion of US bank health. Minehan: bank C&I lending growth strong. Hoenig: banks "competing very vigorously," margins narrowing. Jordan: bankers worry about overbuilding. Greenspan on Japanese banks hiding losses came in Q&A, and is foreign. Mild signals of a sound system with pockets of concern: 36, Low.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Recent weakness. Kelley: "The dollar is a little weaker." Meyer: "sharper-than-projected depreciation of the dollar" is a risk. Poole: dollar strong "until the last few months." Ferguson: "waning impact of the dollar's appreciation." Modest recent weakness: 40 (bright line < 50).

**5. Polarity verification.** HAWKISH_DOVISH: higher means more hawkish. Evidence is a hold with a symmetric directive and several voices for eventual tightening. That implies slightly above 50, so the score matches. Credit spreads: higher means more stress. Widened spreads with a near-normal level imply slightly above 50, so the score matches. Equity valuation: higher means more stress/concern about equity markets, but the scale puts "elevated valuation" in the 16-30 range. Members' concern is overvaluation, so the score is low (27) per the rubric table.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Unanimous hold at 4-3/4 percent with symmetric "B." The Chairman says policy is "back to balance." Hawkish voices: Poole (would tighten but for market shock), Jordan (disagreed with November cut, tightening "at some point"), Hoenig ("unwinding of our earlier actions"), Broaddus (would be "perfectly happy" with a tightening tilt), Guynn (be ready to tighten), Parry (upside risks greater). No participant argues for easing. Kelley, Gramlich, McDonough and Ferguson are comfortable or neutral. Lean hawkish within a hold: about 55.
- Disagreement: Zero formal dissents, all 11 voters Yes. The policy decision itself is unanimous, with differences about the direction of the next move. Directive wording splits narrowly (small majorities on "in coming months" and the money sentence), and the disclosure policy splits a small majority against a sizable minority, with Kelley going along "with considerable trepidation." Jordan voiced prior disagreement with the November cut. Mostly procedural and tactical disagreement, no dissent on economics: 24.
- Financial stability attention: Moderate. The fall turmoil and credit availability, Brazil, equity valuations and the wealth effect, money and credit growth, asset prices (Jordan, Poole, Stern, Greenspan on bubbles) get several participants' attention, and Greenspan spends an extended passage on wealth effects. Not the dominant topic: 52.
- Forward guidance: Symmetric directive, "watchful waiting" (McDonough), "wait-and-see," many saying be ready to tighten if upside risks materialize, and the disclosure compromise. Neutral with a mild upside-risk tilt: 56.
- Uncertainty: "Murky ... perhaps even murkier" (Ferguson), "unusually wide spectrum of plausible outcomes" (Boehne), "risks are large" (Parry, Minehan), "room to maneuver ... very small" (Minehan), "considerable uncertainty about inflation" (Meyer), "almost surely unforecastable" (Greenspan). Several emphasize it but others are fairly confident of a soft landing: 55.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 61 > 50: above potential/trend language, satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 < 50: "supportive" and "accommodative," satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 52: no "elevated/wide" level stated and direction only, so within 45-55 on the side of the move (widening), satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 36 < 50: no stress language, sound system implied, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 < 50: dollar a little weaker, satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (61) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (74): 13 apart. Within 15. Explained by the extraordinary labor tightness being a level fact, while members see growth heading toward trend, ample capacity and no inflation, so the gap is only modestly to clearly above potential.
- OUTPUT_GAP (61) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (66): within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (52): they diverge by 14. Financial conditions are supportive because absolute borrowing costs and equities have recovered, while risk spreads have widened and remain somewhat unsettled. Explained, and credit spreads are low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, no conflict.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (35) vs sub-components: goods 30, services 55, expectations 40; the weighted average is near the parent score (goods and services dominate), so consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member discusses Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: members discuss housing activity and house prices as asset prices (Poole, Jordan) but not shelter/rent inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Moskow: "Overall, price pressures remain benign." Broaddus: inflation "remains exceptionally well behaved." Counter: Jordan, "I think that we are seeing the brown spots." Center of gravity: current inflation is low with no pricing power, with a rising-risk tail. Direction: inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe it as benign and contained.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 61 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Broaddus: "we may still be above the maximum path on which the economy can grow without an increase in inflation." Meyer: "unwinding of the exceptional tightness now in labor markets." Center of gravity: demand above sustainable pace, expected to slow toward or just below trend. Direction: output gap is above neutral because of "above trend" and "above the maximum path" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 74 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Broaddus: "current extraordinary tightness in labor markets." Guynn: "labor markets remain extremely tight." Center of gravity: near-universal agreement that labor markets are very tight, with wage pressure muted. Direction: labor market is above neutral (tighter) because of shortages and unemployment under 4 percent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 66 ±7 [High].** Evidence: Moskow: "The strength of consumer demand has been very impressive of late." Kelley: "Investment has been on a tear for a long time." Center of gravity: strong consumer and housing, investment strong but expected to moderate. Direction: above neutral because spending is described as strong and above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 66 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry: "robust productivity growth." Greenspan: "rather strong productivity gains." Center of gravity: several members cite strong productivity as restraining inflation. Direction: above neutral because of "strong" and "robust" gains.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Meyer: "financial conditions ... continue to be supportive of growth." Stern: "generally accommodative credit conditions." Center of gravity: conditions eased after the fall turmoil and are supportive, with some fragility left. Direction: below neutral (looser) because of "supportive" and "accommodative."

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No participant discusses Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 52 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Meyer: "private risk spreads have widened in the capital markets, the absolute level of private borrowing costs ... is about what it was at the end of last year." Poole: "The bond market is functioning normally again." Center of gravity: widened but near normal levels, level not stated. Direction: slightly above neutral only because of the widening move, per the direction-only rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 27 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Stern: "excessively elevated equity price." Jordan: equity market driven by "perpetual great expectations," considered "unrealistic." Center of gravity: several members and the Chairman worry about overvaluation and the wealth effect. Direction: valuation concern is on the overvalued side (low scale values).

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 ±10 [Low].** Evidence: Minehan: C&I lending at the largest banks "quite strong." Hoenig: "banks are competing very vigorously ... margins are certainly narrowing." Center of gravity: banking implied sound and active, a few cautions on real estate. Direction: below neutral because no stress is described.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Kelley: "The dollar is a little weaker." Poole: "Until the last few months, the dollar has tended to be strong." Center of gravity: modest recent weakening. Direction: below neutral because the dollar is described as a little weaker.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30.** Evidence: Broaddus: goods inflation "could well bottom out." Moskow: steel prices down about 5%. Lower commodity and goods prices. **INFLATION_SERVICES: 55.** Broaddus: "service price increases"; health care costs rising at a double-digit rate (Guynn); leisure prices (Jordan). **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Parry: "subdued inflation expectations." Poole: "very, very deeply embedded views about price stability."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Hoenig: "I want to be thinking about unwinding our earlier actions unless some real shocks emerge." Poole: "there would be a case for tightening now" but for the market shock. Center of gravity: unanimous hold with no easing advocates and a hawkish tilt among a number of members. Direction: slightly hawkish because the lean toward future tightening outweighs the comfortable-with-stance members.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 ±7 [High].** Evidence: Vote read-out shows all voters Yes. Kelley on disclosure: "I will go along with this suggestion ... with considerable trepidation." Center of gravity: unanimous on policy, with narrow splits on directive wording and disclosure. Direction: low disagreement because no dissents and the policy views converge.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan: "the presumption ... that we can effectively manage a bubble is probably based on a lack of humility." Jordan: "the rate of increase in equity prices must slow." Center of gravity: asset prices, credit/money growth and the fall turmoil get meaningful attention from several participants. Direction: moderate attention, not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: McDonough: "maintain the position of watchful waiting." Guynn: be "as ready to move on the upside to tighten policy as we were very recently to ease." Center of gravity: symmetric stance with an upside-risk leaning. Direction: slightly hawkish because the stance is symmetric but members stress readiness to tighten.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Ferguson: outlook "murky ... perhaps even murkier." Boehne: "unusually wide spectrum of plausible outcomes." Center of gravity: several members stress wide ranges and large risks on both sides, while others are confident in a soft landing. Direction: elevated but not extreme.
