# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2005-12-13

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, the Chairman's summary and policy proposal, the policy go-around (mostly about statement wording), and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed, so the staff answers are missing. I scored only what members say.

**Era context.** The text puts this in a tightening campaign late in the cycle. Members refer to "thirteen meetings" of 25bp moves, the hurricanes, high energy prices, a cooling housing market, and a funds rate heading to about 4.25 to 4.5 percent. The debate is whether the cycle is nearly over and how to word the statement ("accommodative," "measured," "is likely" versus "may"). This backdrop is only context. The scores below rest on what members said.

**Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan and Vice Chairman Geithner. Governors Kohn, Ferguson, Olson and Bies. Presidents Moskow, Hoenig, Guynn, Fisher, Yellen, Minehan, Lacker, Santomero, Pianalto, Poole and Stern. Most describe a solid, productivity-rich expansion with improving core inflation, and Moskow, Guynn and Santomero remain wary of inflation. Moskow, Hoenig and Lacker lean toward more tightening. Stern, Olson, Bies, Pianalto and Yellen see policy as close to done or worry about overshooting. Greenspan argues for tightening on risk-management grounds, citing global liquidity, asset prices and housing speculation.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint, roughly at the edge of comfortable. Core PCE is 1.6 to 1.8 percent (Yellen, Bies), which is benign. Against that, Greenbook core is 2.2 percent in H1 2006 (Lacker), firms are passing on price increases (Guynn, Santomero), and Geithner says "underlying inflation is still running somewhat above what we'd like." Kohn is "a little less concerned." Several see the threat as fading. Net is slightly above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy at or slightly above potential and resource use tightening. Kohn says the economy is "expanding a little more quickly than potential supply," Lacker says "relatively full resource utilization," and Greenspan says "we're running out of spare capacity." Yellen sees growth slowing "toward potential," and Kohn notes it is "not expanding very much above" potential. Slightly above 50 satisfies the bright-line rule.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is about 5 percent with NAIRU around 5 (Yellen). Poole says "close to full employment." Hoenig reports worker shortages, and Moskow reports labor markets "becoming tighter" with wage pressure. Minehan sees slow hiring as a downside risk. Compensation is moderate (Bies, Ferguson). A modestly tight reading at 5 percent unemployment gives 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Solid to strong. Poole says "vigorously solid," Fisher says "the economy is solid," Lacker says consumer spending is "on the strong side" with business investment strong, and Geithner says "Other spending indicators look strong." Housing is cooling. Above trend, but not boom: 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Clearly strong. Yellen says structural growth is "around 3 percent," Bies says "The numbers continue to amaze me," and Pianalto and Minehan agree. Lacker questions the structural estimate, which is a methodological point and not a weak reading. 72.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Members call conditions supportive or easy. Hoenig has "very supportive financial conditions," Ferguson says "financial conditions have been supportive of growth," and Kohn says added demand and equity gains produced "little, if any, offsetting tightening." Greenspan worries that low rates are fueling speculation. The bright line requires below 50. About 28.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries come up only as yields, term premia and forward rates (Greenspan on the 10-year ceiling, Geithner on the term spread). Nobody mentions dysfunction. Yield moves alone are normal functioning, so the score is low-normal, with Low confidence because the topic is only indirect.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kohn says "risk spreads out the yield curve and across risk categories continue to be low." Geithner says credit spreads do not suggest concern. The stated level is low, so the score must be below 50. 24.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity gains are noted (Kohn, Ferguson). Ferguson says the equity risk premium is "above average," so equities are not rich on his view. Greenspan raises speculation and asset-price momentum, plus "the surprisingly high equity premium." Valuation concern is mixed and modest, so the score is a bit below fair value, with Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banks are not discussed. Greenspan discusses Japan's banks, which is foreign and historical. Pianalto reports loan pipelines and competition among lenders, which is only an anecdote. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only Poole's aside about "the strength of the dollar this year," tied to the repatriation tax break. This shows appreciation and is a passing mention. Above 50 per the bright line, with Low confidence.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: Members mention industrial price indexes (Santomero), pass-through of energy and input costs (Guynn), and heavy retail discounting (Pianalto). Net is mildly above stable.
- Services: Only a passing note from Lacker that services prices decelerated. Not a discussion, so N/A.
- Housing: The discussion is about house prices as an asset, not shelter inflation, so N/A.
- Expectations: Mostly "well anchored" or "contained" (Yellen, Lacker, Stern, Ferguson, Greenspan). Moskow worries, and Geithner says TIPS measures have not fallen much. Slightly above target-anchored, about 50.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: All 10 voters back a 25bp hike, and the debate is whether this is near the end. The statement keeps "some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed." Several members (Stern, Olson, Bies, Pianalto, Yellen) lean toward stopping soon. Moskow and Greenspan are hawkish on the path, and Greenspan wants to avoid a bond rally. Current action is a hike and the forward lean is about 1 to 2 more. Moderately hawkish, 63.
- Dissent: The vote is unanimous, with 0 dissents. Reservations concern wording only: Moskow ("an outlier") on "measured," Pianalto and Stern preferring to drop it, and Olson on no presumption of multiple moves. Economic assessments broadly agree. Low to modest, 20.
- Financial stability: Greenspan spends a long stretch on asset prices, speculation and the housing froth. Kohn, Ferguson, Fisher, Yellen and Minehan respond, and Bies discusses long-end risk-taking. Conditions in markets get moderate attention but are not the dominant topic. 45.
- Forward guidance: The statement signals "likely" further firming, "measured," and a one-to-two-move expectation, with January a near certainty and March about an even bet. Members debate softening. A clear but tempered tightening signal, 68.
- Uncertainty: Ferguson cites "known unknowns," Pianalto says even the near-term future is "uncertain" and that the 70 percent band is wide, Kohn sees "a good deal of uncertainty" after January, and Greenspan says "we don't fully understand." Baseline forecasts are held with reasonable confidence. Somewhat elevated, 40.

**Polarity checks.** Productivity high means more productivity, and the text says strong, so it is above 50. Financial conditions: "supportive" means looser, so it is below 50. Credit spreads: low level means below 50. Dollar: appreciation means above 50. Banking: not scored.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 (above potential language, above 50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 (supportive, below 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 24 (low level, below 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 (appreciation, above 50). TREASURY_STRESS 36 (no dysfunction, below 55). BANKING_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 54 agree. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60 is 6 points above OUTPUT_GAP, within tolerance. A solid pace with growth above potential is consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 and CREDIT_SPREADS 24 co-move. Both are loose.
- TREASURY_STRESS 36 is normal and quiet, consistent with loose conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 53 is consistent with goods 55 and expectations 50.

**N/A justification.** BANKING_STRESS: US banks not discussed (Japan banking is Greenspan's historical aside). INFLATION_SERVICES: only a one-clause mention by Lacker. INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices were discussed as assets, not shelter costs.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 53 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "core PCE inflation running at only 1.6 percent over the past six months." Geithner: "underlying inflation is still running somewhat above what we'd like." Guynn: "price increases that are sticking."
- Summary: Core readings are benign but members see an upside risk and some pass-through.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because several members still worry about upside and few call it below target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "expanding a little more quickly than potential supply." Greenspan: "We're running out of spare capacity."
- Summary: Economy at or slightly above potential, with resources tightening.
- Direction: Above neutral because members describe growth faster than potential and tightening resources.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 ±5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole: "We're at close to full employment." Moskow: "labor markets becoming tighter, leading to potential wage pressure."
- Summary: Unemployment near 5 percent with spot shortages and some wage pressure.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because of reported shortages and a drifting-lower unemployment rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Poole: "vigorously solid." Geithner: "Other spending indicators look strong."
- Summary: Spending and business investment are solid to strong, with housing cooling.
- Direction: Above trend because multiple members describe strong demand.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "structural productivity growth is around 3 percent." Bies: "The numbers continue to amaze me."
- Summary: Productivity is clearly above the historical trend with broad agreement.
- Direction: Above the 2 percent trend benchmark because members describe robust gains.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "very supportive financial conditions." Kohn: "little, if any, offsetting tightening in financial conditions."
- Summary: Members see markets and credit as accommodative, even as policy rates rise.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because of supportive conditions and low risk spreads.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 36 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: long rates "run into resistance." Geithner: "narrowing of the term spread."
- Summary: Treasuries are a yield and term-premium topic, with no talk of impaired functioning.
- Direction: Below the stress threshold because yield moves alone are not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 24 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "risk spreads... continue to be low." Geithner: "credit spreads... don't seem to suggest a lot of concern."
- Summary: Spreads are described as low, so the level is compressed.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "The equity risk premium is now above average." Greenspan: "the surprisingly high equity premium."
- Summary: Mixed views, with modest speculation worry but no bubble language about equities.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because there is mild concern about asset-price momentum, offset by the above-average risk premium.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** (US banks not discussed).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Poole: "How much of the strength of the dollar this year might we attribute to the special tax break."
- Summary: A single passing reference to dollar strength this year.
- Direction: Above neutral because the dollar is described as strong.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55.** Santomero: "higher prices for industrial goods"; Pianalto: heavy retail discounting offsets.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.** Yellen: "Survey measures of longer-run inflation expectations are about where they stood before the storms." Moskow: "inflationary expectations could remain elevated."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "we need to tighten to be sure that we're safe from these speculative incursions." Stern: "we will have raised the federal funds rate sufficiently for now."
- Summary: Unanimous 25bp hike, with about one to two more expected and a minority leaning toward stopping.
- Direction: Hawkish because the entire group is tightening, tempered by pause-leaning members.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Moskow: "I guess I'm going to be an outlier here." Vote read-out: all Yes.
- Summary: No dissent; differences concern the wording of "measured" and "likely."
- Direction: Low because the vote is unanimous and assessments are close.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "overall asset prices worldwide... are rising a good deal faster than product prices." Minehan: "low underlying interest rates were encouraging risk-taking."
- Summary: A long Chairman discussion on asset prices and speculation, with several follow-ups.
- Direction: Moderate because several members address it but the economy and wording dominate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "I agree with you that we'll probably have to move in January." Geithner: "very high that we're going to need to move in January and that March is maybe an even bet."
- Summary: "Some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed" is kept despite debate.
- Direction: Tightening-leaning because the likely-more-firming language is retained.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the known-unknown factors around it are to me quite obvious." Pianalto: "the future, even the near-term future, is uncertain."
- Summary: Several flag uncertainty about the path after January, but baselines are held with conviction.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated, not unusual.
