# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript of 2004-08-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It contains Chairman Greenspan's questions in the Desk and staff Q&A (market volatility, oil futures), the economic go-around, Greenspan's policy remarks, the policy go-around (statement wording and the 25bp move), and a vote read-out. The roll call shows 12 Yes votes and no No votes. The text reads cleanly. Hard line breaks are present but nothing is garbled.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Mid-2004. The Committee began raising the funds rate in June and is expected to continue "at a measured pace". The text describes a spring/summer "soft patch" (weak June consumption, a weak July payroll report of 32,000), high and rising oil prices, and a core inflation run-up that has recently moderated.

**Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: the soft patch is exaggerated by noisy employment data and mostly reflects energy plus corporate caution. He proposes "opportunistic disaccommodation" and a 25bp hike, and wants to get back to neutral quickly with a high hurdle for skipping a meeting.
- Moskow, Poole, Santomero, Guynn, Hoenig, Stern, McTeer, Lacker, Gramlich, Bernanke, Bies, Minehan, Pianalto and Olson: the soft patch is temporary, core inflation is contained, and they support continuing at a measured pace. Guynn, McTeer, Pianalto and Bernanke flag residual inflation or upside price risks. Bernanke notes long-run expectations may not be firmly anchored.
- Kohn, Ferguson, Yellen and Geithner: more downside growth risk, a larger output gap, and a more data-dependent pace. Kohn, Ferguson and Yellen openly allow for a pause or skip. Kohn and Ferguson say the gap is closing slowly, and Kohn says "there is slack in the economy".

**Directional statements for core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation pressure as slightly above the midpoint. Headline inflation is "uncomfortable" (McTeer) and cost pass-through persists (Guynn, Pianalto). Core is "well contained", "moderated" and just under 2 percent (Moskow, Geithner, Kohn, Bies). Upside risks are "abating" (Ferguson, Kohn). The net is modestly above target on headline and about at target on core.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below the midpoint. Minehan: "excess capacity remaining at the end of 2005". Kohn: "the output gap has turned out to be larger than anticipated". Ferguson: "economic slack if anything slightly increased". Geithner sees growth returning only "modestly above potential".
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: below the midpoint. The unemployment rate is 5.5% (Santomero), payrolls are weak, and firms squeeze existing staff (Guynn, Greenspan). There are scattered shortages (Moskow, Santomero, Yellen, Hoenig) and wages are well contained (Yellen, Kohn). Kohn: "there is slack in the economy".
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: slightly below trend. There is a soft patch in consumption and a rising saving rate (Kohn, Yellen, Bernanke), with business investment short of cash flow (Greenspan). Offsetting this are strong housing, autos rebounding, and manufacturing and equipment orders (Santomero, Guynn, Moskow).
- PRODUCTIVITY: above trend. "Strong underlying productivity growth" (Moskow), "persistence of a significant advance in productivity" (Stern), and Bernanke's 2.9% and 7.5% manufacturing figures.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: looser than neutral. Kohn: "Interest rates remain low, and credit is readily available." Ferguson: C&I lending is up on "easier terms and conditions". Policy is "highly accommodative" (the real funds rate is negative), but that is policy and not conditions. Equity indexes are down 5-12% (Ferguson) and the implied volatility is "startling" (Greenspan, low). Net: modestly loose.
- TREASURY_STRESS: not discussed as dysfunction. Greenspan and Geithner discuss low rate volatility and fed funds expectations, which is not Treasury market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread levels are cited. Credit is described as readily available with easier terms (Kohn, Ferguson). Corporate bond issuance has picked up (Greenspan). Real A-rated yields are low (Ferguson). Mildly below normal on availability, with Low confidence and no stated spread level.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: stocks are down and Stern says his "concern about equity prices" is partly offset by lower long rates. Bernanke cites a "flat or declining stock market". Greenspan notes very low S&P implied volatility ("too much good news"). This is price direction with little valuation commentary, so I default near 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: Olson reports banking activity is not soft, with a slight concern about consumer-lending overextension and bankruptcies. Lacker reports positive consumer credit quality trends. The banking system is healthy, so below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Geithner mentions only "abrupt change in the willingness of nonresidents to acquire U.S. assets" as a risk. No dollar direction is discussed. N/A.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: all 12 voters back a 25bp hike, with a "measured pace" and Greenspan's "opportunistic disaccommodation" and "high hurdle" to skipping a meeting (Moskow, Gramlich, Lacker, Bernanke, Santomero). Kohn, Ferguson and Yellen want more data dependence, and Poole and Yellen favour wording that would allow a September pause. The result is moderate hawkish: tightening is the consensus action, but pace caution tempers it.
- Disagreement: no dissents. Reservations are about whether to raise the hurdle for skipping (Greenspan, Moskow and Gramlich on one side; Kohn, Ferguson and Yellen on the other) and about adding "sustainable growth" to the statement (Poole and Yellen for; Greenspan and Lacker against). Disagreement is over tactics and emphasis, not over the economy. That points to a low-to-moderate score.
- Financial stability attention: limited. A few mentions of market volatility and expectations (Greenspan, Geithner), credit conditions (Ferguson, Olson) and equity prices (Stern, Ferguson). Not a focus.
- Forward guidance: the default is to keep tightening at each meeting at a measured pace, but data-dependent with an explicit pause option. Mild-to-moderate tightening bias.
- Uncertainty: "a great deal of fog and uncertainty" (Ferguson), "thicker fog" (Geithner), "error bands ... wider" (Guynn), "more uncertainty, and puzzles" (Kohn). Many also assert confidence that the lull is temporary, so this is elevated but not extreme.

**Polarity checks.**
- Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential. Evidence says below potential, so the score is below 50.
- Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter. Evidence says slack with 5.5% unemployment, so the score is below 50.
- Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter. Evidence says loose and available credit, so the score is below 50.
- Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress. Evidence says healthy, so the score is below 50.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP is below 50 and consistent with the "below potential / slack" language.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below 50 given "readily available / low interest rates" (accommodative).
- CREDIT_SPREADS is below 50, with a level-neutral reading. No spread widening or elevated level is stated.
- BANKING_STRESS is below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40) are consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45) are consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) and CREDIT_SPREADS (40) co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, which fits normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (52) is consistent with the sub-components: goods are mixed near 50, services are modestly above (58), and expectations are near anchored (48).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members did not discuss Treasury market functioning. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member discussed the dollar. INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed shelter or rent inflation (housing was discussed only as activity).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McTeer: "Higher oil prices have already pushed headline inflation numbers to uncomfortable levels." Moskow: "core inflation remains well contained." Guynn: "I am not as ready as some to move inflation concerns completely to the back burner."
- Summary: headline is above target and core is near it and moderating, so the net is only a little above the midpoint.
- Direction: inflation pressure is slightly above neutral because headline and pass-through pressure offsets moderating core.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kohn: "the output gap has turned out to be larger than anticipated". Minehan: "excess capacity remaining at the end of 2005".
- Summary: the economy is seen as below potential, with the gap closing slowly.
- Direction: the output gap is below neutral because several members describe persistent slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Santomero: "The unemployment rate fell a tenth to 5.5 percent." Kohn: "there is slack in the economy." Moskow: "scattered labor shortages in a few specialized areas".
- Summary: unemployment is elevated and hiring is weak, with only pockets of shortage.
- Direction: the labor market is looser than neutral because of slack and payroll weakness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "Recent weakness in personal consumption expenditures is particularly worrisome." Guynn: "Investment spending continues at a good pace."
- Summary: a consumption soft patch is partly offset by solid housing, autos and equipment orders.
- Direction: spending is slightly below trend because the weakness in consumer spending outweighs the firm spots.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 68 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "we still have strong underlying productivity growth". Bernanke: "second-quarter productivity growth ... 2.9 percent in the nonforeign business sector and 7.5 percent in manufacturing".
- Summary: productivity is repeatedly cited as strong, above trend.
- Direction: productivity growth is above neutral because multiple members call it strong.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Interest rates remain low, and credit is readily available." Ferguson: C&I lending is up "due as much to easier terms and conditions".
- Summary: credit is available and rates are low, partly offset by lower equity prices.
- Direction: financial conditions are looser than neutral (below 50) because credit is readily available.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (not discussed as dysfunction)

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Kohn: "credit is readily available". Greenspan: "the largest numbers in some time on the issuance of bonds".
- Summary: availability is easy and no spread widening is stated. No level is given, so confidence is Low.
- Direction: spreads are below neutral because credit is easily available. This is not a direction-only call.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Stern: "my concern about equity prices is offset at least to a degree by the decline in ... longer-term interest rates". Ferguson: "Every major domestic stock market index has been down from year-end 2003".
- Summary: equity declines are noted without a clear valuation view, so I default to about fair.
- Direction: equity concern is at about neutral because no explicit over- or under-valuation view is stated.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Olson: "banking activity does not reflect the current softness". Lacker: "positive consumer credit quality trends".
- Summary: banks are described as healthy, with a slight consumer-lending concern.
- Direction: banking stress is below neutral because banks are healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** (no discussion)

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52** (value only). Guynn: core goods prices are a "downward drag", but Moskow, Pianalto and Lacker cite commodity, steel and copper cost pressure with limited pass-through. Mixed.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 58** (value only). Guynn: "were it not for the downward drag of core goods prices, the increase in core service prices would feel more threatening."

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** (not discussed)

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48** (value only). Kohn: "inflation expectations have been well behaved". Bernanke: "not as firmly anchored as we would like". Lacker: expectations "have fallen somewhat since the spring".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "given an unbiased balance of risks ... we should be choosing to raise rates". Moskow: "I think the sooner we get back to neutral, the better off we are."
- Summary: all voters support a 25bp hike at a measured pace. Kohn, Ferguson and Yellen want data dependence, which tempers the score.
- Direction: policy lean is hawkish (above 50), moderate because tightening is the consensus action but the pace is gradual with some pause openness.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: the roll call shows a unanimous vote with 12 Yes votes and no No votes. Yellen: "I agree with Governors Kohn and Ferguson that our decisions should be data-dependent".
- Summary: differences are about statement wording and the pause hurdle, not about the action.
- Direction: disagreement is low because the vote was unanimous and differences are tactical.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the system is less resilient to shocks from unexpected events". Ferguson: "financial market and credit conditions, which are all meant to be forward-looking".
- Summary: a few members address financial markets and credit as context, not as a focus.
- Direction: attention to financial stability is limited, in the 25-35 band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "anything other than moving at every meeting strikes me as something that requires a hurdle of evidence greater than usual." Gramlich: "taking baby steps at each meeting unless we get some jarring new information".
- Summary: the default is continued measured tightening, with an explicit data-dependent pause option held open by Kohn, Ferguson, Yellen and Poole.
- Direction: the forward signal is hawkish, mildly to moderately so.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "Forecasters have been confronting a great deal of fog and uncertainty". Geithner: "given the thicker fog surrounding the near-term outlook".
- Summary: several members stress unusual uncertainty about the soft patch, while others are confident it is temporary.
- Direction: uncertainty is elevated, at the upper end of the "elevated" band.
