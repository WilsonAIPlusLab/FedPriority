# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2009-11-04

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** A member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). The input is the two-day meeting with staff turns removed. It holds a long first-day segment on liquidity facilities (swap lines, reverse repos, term deposits), then the economic go-around, the policy go-around on statement alternatives A/B/C, and a roll-call read-out. The text is clean, with no garbled passages. Staff answers to member questions are absent, so the Q&A is one-sided. That is only a procedural note and does not affect the scores.

**Era context.** Late 2009, with the economy just out of a deep recession. Third-quarter GDP turned positive after four negative quarters. Members also talk about unemployment near 10 percent, core inflation near 1 to 1.5 percent, rates at 0 to 1/4 percent with "extended period" language, and agency MBS purchases about to wind down. I score what participants say, not the backdrop.

**Speakers.** Bernanke, Dudley, Kohn, Warsh, Duke and Tarullo spoke from the Board and New York. Reserve Bank presidents were Lockhart, Plosser, Bullard, Rosengren, Fisher, Lacker, Evans, Yellen, Hoenig, Pianalto and Kocherlakota. Views split into three groups:
- Slack-focused doves: Yellen, Dudley, Kohn, Rosengren, Evans, Pianalto. They expect low inflation and no tightening for a long time.
- Hawkish or exit-oriented voices: Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker, Bullard, Kocherlakota, and partly Fisher. They stress inflation expectations, excess reserves, asset sales and the risk that gap-based views delay action.
- Cautious communicators: Lockhart, Tarullo, Kocherlakota. They objected to the new statement wording, not to the policy stance.

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Core and median inflation are slowing, wages and unit labor costs are falling, and firms have "pricing power is nil" (Fisher). Yellen sees core PCE going to 1 percent. Several flag medium-term upside risk (Bullard, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Warsh) and energy and commodity prices. That keeps the score in the upper 20s to low 30s, not the teens, because near-term deflation fears have "substantially receded" (Tarullo).
- OUTPUT_GAP. Participants see output as well below potential. They cite "substantial resource slack" (Kohn, Evans, Yellen) and "output is well below its trend" (Kocherlakota). Bullard and Plosser argue gaps are mismeasured, but nobody says the economy is at or above potential. Bright-line rule: must be below 50. Score about 27.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Participants see the labor market as extremely slack. Unemployment is "nearly 10 percent", the employment-population ratio is at a 25-year low, and Bernanke says the market is "even weaker than it looks". The score sits in the 2009-10 anchor band, 15 to 22.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Participants see spending as below trend. Q3 consumption was lifted by temporary programs (clunkers, tax credit), household balance sheets are weak, and capex is described as "dead" (Fisher quoting a CEO). There are modest positives in "consumer spending on a moderate upward trend" (Lacker) and the end of inventory liquidation. Net score about 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Participants see productivity growth as strong. Dudley cites the "spike in productivity growth". Kohn cites "surprising strength in productivity". Bernanke cites "sharp gains in productivity" and "impressive" gains. Above trend, so about 70.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. This is mixed. Markets are healing and large firms borrow easily. Yellen says risk spreads have come down. Bank credit remains "quite tight" (Kohn), small firms face a "modest credit crunch" (Evans), and Dudley finds it "slightly disturbing" that conditions have not eased. Bernanke says tightening of lending terms is far greater than the recession alone would explain. Net: somewhat tighter than neutral, about 57.
- TREASURY_STRESS. N/A. No participant describes Treasury market dysfunction. Yellen's term-premium discussion is about fed funds futures, and Lacker's remark was a staff Q&A aside.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Level reading. Bernanke says "bond spreads seem reasonable given expected default rates" and that credit spreads are much narrower. Evans says large firms get funding at "spreads they consider to be closer to normal". Kocherlakota says the jumbo-conforming spread "remains elevated". Small-firm credit is rationed. Level is near normal to slightly wide, so about 54, which is above 50 given the elevated pockets. This is Moderate confidence because there is no explicit statement of the overall level.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Roughly fair. Bernanke says "equity premiums are still above normal, so stock prices don't seem unusually high". Warsh says the March-to-October run looks "substantially complete" and the next $4 trillion will be harder. Kohn reads the recent dip as healthy. Score about 48.
- BANKING_STRESS. Emerging to significant stress. Rosengren cites rising nonperforming loans and CAMELS-3-or-worse assets "high and climbing". Yellen says "a significant share of banks in our District will fail" and mentions "zombie banks". Lockhart expects a CRE drag on regional banks. Offsets are Duke's "a bit more optimistic", with credit costs "approaching a plateau", and Bernanke's "improved" profits and liquidity. Lacker calls CRE distress "relatively manageable". Score about 62.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Net direction is weakening or orderly depreciation over the past year, with a small recent rebound. Bernanke says the safe-haven rise has unwound. Kohn says "some decline in the dollar is a necessary part of the rebalancing". Fisher cites "projected continued dollar weakness". The rule requires a score below 50. Score about 36.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS. Retail deflation and weak pricing (Fisher), against rising energy and import prices (Warsh, Bernanke). Mixed, about 40.
- SERVICES and HOUSING. Not discussed as price categories, so N/A. Wages are discussed but under the headline inflation dimension. House prices are discussed as activity, not shelter inflation.
- EXPECTATIONS. Described as stable or well anchored (Kohn, Evans, Pianalto, Bernanke). Bullard says it is a "bit of an illusion" that they are stable. Plosser notes rising dispersion. Kohn sees possible downside unanchoring. Roughly at target with two-sided risk, about 48.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN. Policy holds at the zero bound under alternative B. The vote is unanimous, with the "extended period" language kept. The majority (Kohn, Dudley, Yellen, Evans, Rosengren) say no tightening until mid-2011 or later. Bernanke says the extended-period language stays "given the macro outlook". A minority (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker, Bullard, Kocherlakota, Fisher) press for earlier exit signals, asset sales or "some time" language. Plosser wants a path to 2.5 percent by end-2010, which is clearly hawkish. The asset-purchase program is winding down but not reversed, and there is no easing, though Rosengren could "conceive of conditions" for more stimulus. Net: maintenance of maximum accommodation with a vocal hawkish minority. Score about 33.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY. There are no formal dissents (10 yes, 0 no in the read-out). The distinct camps disagree over the role of gaps versus expectations in inflation (Plosser and Bullard versus Yellen, Dudley and Kohn), over asset sales first versus reverse repos and term deposits (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Kocherlakota versus Dudley and Bernanke), over swap lines, and over the new statement language (Lockhart, Kocherlakota and Tarullo reservations). Plosser and Hoenig preferred C. Most of this is about tactics and communication, with some economic-framework disagreement. Score about 33.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS. There is a long first-day segment on liquidity facilities and swap lines, plus discussion of bank credit, CRE, the carry trade, dollar risk and asset-price bubbles. Bernanke gives a dedicated section to speculative excess. Not dominant but more than moderate, about 55.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE. The statement keeps "extended period" and adds a list of conditions (resource utilization, inflation trends, inflation expectations). Bernanke frames this as emphasizing conditionality. Dudley reads "extended" as no tightening for six months or more. The push for "some time" was rejected. Low-for-long emphasis, with some hawkish language pressure and an exit-sequencing discussion. Score about 22.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED. Many participants stress uncertainty: Bernanke ("muddled", "considerable uncertainty"), Yellen ("very uncertain"), Tarullo ("considerable uncertainty"), Plosser ("uncertainty abounds"), Bullard ("giant amount of uncertainty"), Warsh ("very humble and uncertain"), Pianalto. Elevated rather than extreme, as the most frightening tail risks have receded (Yellen). Score about 62.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more inflation, and the evidence says low, so below 50: matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter, and the evidence says very slack, so low: matches. BANKING_STRESS above 50 matches the emerging stress described. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 matches weakening. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN below 50 matches maintained accommodation.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP is below 50 (slack language throughout). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above 50, and no member calls conditions "accommodative" overall, though large-firm access is easy. CREDIT_SPREADS is above 50 only slightly, on the "elevated" jumbo spread and small-business rationing, while the main bond-market statements are "reasonable" and "closer to normal". BANKING_STRESS is above 50 and nobody calls the banking system "sound" without qualification. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below 50. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (27), LABOR_TIGHTNESS (18) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (36) are all below 50. The labor-output gap is 9 points, within 15. The consumption-output gap is 9 points, also within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (57) and CREDIT_SPREADS (54) are both above 50 and co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) is consistent with GOODS (40, pulled up by energy), EXPECTATIONS (48) and the low core readings.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants in the relevant sense.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±6 (High).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "reduce core PCE price inflation from about 1½ percent this year to 1 percent in 2010 and 2011". Fisher, "Pricing power is nil."
- Summary: core inflation is low and falling, with upside risk only medium term.
- Direction: below neutral because core is well under 2 percent and wages are decelerating.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 27 ±5 (High).**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota, "output is well below its trend levels". Yellen, "unemployment... far above the NAIRU for a long time".
- Summary: large slack is the working assumption of most members.
- Direction: below neutral because of persistent slack language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 ±4 (High).**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota, "unemployment... extremely high by historical standards at nearly 10 percent". Bernanke, "labor market may be even weaker than it looks".
- Summary: a very weak market is described by nearly every speaker.
- Direction: below neutral because unemployment is near 10 percent with no hiring.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 ±6 (High).**
- Evidence: Fisher, "domestic cap-ex is dead". Lacker, "Consumer spending seems to be on a moderate upward trend."
- Summary: subpar, with temporary props.
- Direction: below trend because of fragile balance sheets and weak business investment.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 70 ±8 (High).**
- Evidence: Dudley, "the spike in productivity growth". Kohn, "the surprising strength in productivity".
- Summary: several members and the Chair see strong cost-saving productivity gains.
- Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 ±7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "bank credit remains quite tight". Yellen, "risk spreads have come down".
- Summary: healing market funding for large firms, but tight bank credit for small ones.
- Direction: above neutral because of tight bank credit and Dudley's concern that conditions have not eased.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 ±7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "Bond spreads seem reasonable given expected default rates". Kocherlakota, "jumbo... remains elevated but has fallen sharply".
- Summary: the overall level is near normal, with elevated pockets.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because of the elevated jumbo spread and small-firm rationing; the narrowing is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "equity premiums are still above normal, so stock prices don't seem unusually high". Warsh, "The one-way upward move by financial markets from March lows... looks substantially complete."
- Summary: no valuation concern in either direction.
- Direction: at neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 62 ±7 (High).**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "nonperforming loans are rising". Yellen, "a significant share of banks in our District will fail".
- Summary: lingering CRE and credit-loss stress, with offsetting improvement in profits.
- Direction: above neutral because of emerging stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 36 ±8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Some decline in the dollar is a necessary part of the rebalancing of global demand." Bernanke, "an orderly depreciation is orderly until it's not".
- Summary: the dollar is seen as drifting weaker, a concern for Fisher, Kohn and Bernanke.
- Direction: below neutral because of depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 40.** Fisher on deflation in retail and food, against Warsh and Bernanke on energy and import prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Kohn, "inflation expectations have been very well anchored". Bullard, "it is a bit of an illusion."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 33 ±7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Kohn, "I had my tightening in mid-2011." Plosser, "it would serve us well if the statement raised the path somewhat."
- Summary: the majority holds maximum accommodation while a vocal minority pushes for earlier exit signals.
- Direction: below neutral because of the unanimous hold at zero and extended-period language.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 ±7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Tarullo, "I certainly wouldn't even dream of opposing alternative B". Hoenig, "I would have some preference for alternative C; but this is not the day."
- Summary: no formal dissents, but several reservations on language and sharp economic disagreement on gaps and sequencing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Bernanke on speculative excess, the dollar and Asia. Duke on banks and CRE.
- Summary: attention comes from bank credit, CRE, asset prices and the carry trade, plus a long segment on liquidity facilities.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 ±6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "I do think we need to maintain the 'extended period' for now." Dudley, "I don't see us tightening in 2010."
- Summary: low-for-long, with conditionality added and a "some time" proposal rejected.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Warsh, "very humble and uncertain about the 2010–11 forecast". Bernanke, "At the last meeting I described my outlook as muddled, and I remain firmly in that position."
- Summary: many cite significant uncertainty, though the worst tail risks have receded.
