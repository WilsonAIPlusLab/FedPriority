# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring (v4.1) - transcript discussion, meeting 2010-01-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It holds four blocks: (a) organizational items and the Desk Q&A, (b) a day-1 go-round on exit strategy (primary credit rate, TAF, sequencing of reserve draining, asset sales, balance-sheet structure, operating framework), (c) the day-2 economic go-round plus the Chair's summary, and (d) the day-2 policy go-round on the draft statement (Alternatives A/B/C, "extended period", "purchases" vs "holdings", TAF wording), then a roll-call read-out. Some replies are missing because staff turns are stripped, so a few questions have no visible answer. No garbled passages that affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Post-crisis recovery, January 2010: unemployment about 10%, fed funds at 0-25 bp, agency MBS purchases tapering to an end in March 2010, "extended period" forward guidance in place. I am scoring what participants say, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish/patient core: Yellen ("economy is clearly operating far below maximum sustainable employment, and inflation is undesirably low"), Dudley ("Slack is abundant. Core inflation is low"), Kohn ("output and inflation likely to remain below our objectives for quite some time"), Tarullo (recovery "still uneven and somewhat fragile"), Evans, Rosengren, Lockhart, Pianalto, Duke, Warsh (on the statement), Bernanke (hold, "move gingerly").
- Hawkish tilt: Plosser (tightening "by late spring or early summer", funds rate near 2% by end-2010), Lacker (about a 30% chance of a rise by year-end; wants reserves cut aggressively), Hoenig (dissent, wants "extended period" dropped, Alternative C), Fisher (broken record against "extended period"; medium-term price worries), Bullard (medium-term inflation risk "high"), Kocherlakota (wants gradual MBS sales, tail inflation risk but supports B).
- The staff-reported Greenbook/Bluebook content mentioned by members is ignored except where members adopt it as their own view.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (below target). Core measures are subdued and decelerating (Kohn: "well below my 2 percent objective"; Pianalto: core PCE about 1 percent this year; Yellen: "undesirably low"). Medium-term risk and rising 5y5y breakevens are noted (Bullard, Plosser, Fisher, Dudley) but these are forecasts and worries, not current pressure. Score about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. "By any measure, the economy is now operating with a substantial output gap" (Kohn); "far below maximum sustainable employment" (Yellen); Bernanke: "Slack clearly seem high". Plosser argues the gap is smaller because the natural rate is higher, but he does not say the economy is above potential. Bright line (below potential means under 50) satisfied. Score about 28.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Far below the midpoint. UE is 10%, "painfully high for years" (Yellen), hiring "very limited", long-duration unemployment high. Score about 16.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below the midpoint. Moderate recovery: consumption "growing modestly", "subdued" (Dudley); business capex "remarkably modest" (Pianalto), "little enthusiasm" (Lockhart). Some strength in manufacturing orders and equipment (Fisher, Evans, Lacker). Score about 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above the midpoint. Several members cite a surge in productivity (Yellen: "a surge in labor productivity", gains possibly permanent; Dudley: "Last year's spurt was a surprise", probably temporary; Bernanke: "tremendous productivity gains"). Score about 68.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (supportive). Bernanke: "Financial conditions remain generally supportive"; Plosser: "conditions in the financial markets continue to support the recovery"; Yellen: conditions easing further with narrower spreads. Offsets: bank lending still contracting, credit tight for small business and consumers (Duke, Tarullo). Net score about 38. Bright line satisfied (supportive means under 50).
- TREASURY_STRESS: The Treasury market is not described as dysfunctional or even discussed as a market-function topic by members (Q&A is about MBS and reserves). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is low. Kocherlakota: "Interbank loan spreads are low relative to historical norms, as are corporate bond spreads"; Evans: spreads "fallen to the point where... risk premiums are being pushed too low"; Warsh: spreads "continue to trend lower". Below normal, so under 50. Score about 32. Bright line satisfied.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only passing references to price moves (Yellen "higher equity prices", Bernanke "stock prices... exactly what they were") with no valuation view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed, improving but not healthy. Duke: delinquencies and charge-offs "have peaked but remain elevated", CRE "will grow but still appear manageable"; Bernanke: losses "still extraordinarily high on a historical basis", CRE and mortgage upside risks, but banks repaying TARP and raising capital; Kohn: banks "rebuild capital". No member calls the system "sound" or "healthy" outright, so no bright-line conflict. Score about 56.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Little and mixed. Warsh: "the dollar sank relative to the euro, dollar stability broadly against foreign currencies is an encouraging sign"; Bernanke: the dollar "moved in an anti-inflationary direction recently" (i.e. firmer). Kocherlakota cites option skew toward a dollar decline. Net roughly stable. Score 50, low confidence.

**6. Inflation sub-components.** Housing: shelter inflation is weak ("rents flat to down", Bernanke; Plosser on the shelter decline pulling core down), score about 30. Goods: raw materials and commodity prices up, finished goods and retail flat (Fisher, Lacker, Warsh, Pianalto), score about 52. Services: not discussed beyond a passing remark that services price trends are stable (Lacker), N/A. Expectations: discussed by many; surveys stable but 5y5y TIPS drifting up, risk premiums and skew a worry (Plosser, Dudley, Kocherlakota, Bullard). Mildly drifting up from anchored, score about 55. Parent INFLATION_PRESSURE at about 32 is consistent: shelter low, goods near stable, expectations mildly elevated, core subdued and headline pulled by earlier energy moves.

**7. Tier 3 in words.**
- Policy lean: Committee holds at 0-25 bp, keeps "extended period", finishes agency MBS purchases (ending March), with a majority resisting "holdings" language. Dovish-to-balanced majority, but a sizeable group (Plosser, Lacker, Hoenig, Fisher, Bullard, Kocherlakota) pushes toward earlier exit preparation, and the day-1 go-round is mostly about how to withdraw accommodation. Hold with a mild-to-balanced lean, not an easing: about 45.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Hoenig, hawkish, wants Alternative C / drop "extended period"). Several reservations: Plosser and Fisher dislike "extended period"; Lacker and Bullard favor C-style inflation language; disagreement on sequencing and sales timing, plus a stated difference over how entrenched the recovery is (Tarullo: "bemused at the certainty"). Tone is collegial. About 38.
- Financial stability emphasis: Moderate. Duke gives an extended banking/credit assessment; Evans, Kocherlakota, Warsh, Dudley, Kohn discuss spreads, EU/Greece sovereign risk, Chinese lending, regulatory effects on credit. Bernanke notes financial conditions were "discussed less than in previous meetings". Day-1 market-functioning talk is mostly about policy tools. About 42.
- Forward guidance: "extended period" retained; the Chair describes it as conditional and says "modal expectation... we are still some meetings away" from changing it, while working "toward a gradual toning-down". Easing-leaning with data dependence, with normalization prep in the background. About 35.
- Uncertainty: Many stress it: Hoenig, Lockhart ("uncertainty remains high"), Pianalto ("uncertainty... remains high"), Dudley ("very uncertain" on labor and productivity), Kohn ("uncertainty greater than usual"), Tarullo, Bernanke ("uncertainty actually is greater than average right now"). Bullard questions whether it is always described as elevated. Elevated but not extreme. About 56.

**8. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 28 (<50, below potential): OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (<50, supportive): OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 32 (<50, low level): OK. BANKING_STRESS 56 (>50, stress persisting; no "sound/healthy" statement): OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 50 (mixed/stable): OK. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**9. Polarity check.** Higher inflation score = more pressure; evidence is subdued, so below 50: matches. Labor 16: higher = tighter, evidence is very loose: matches. Spreads 32: higher = more stress, evidence is low spreads: matches. Hawkish lean 45: majority hold, hawkish minority: just below 50.

**10. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 28 vs LABOR 16: gap of 12, within 15, labor weaker because UE is at 10%. OUTPUT_GAP 28 vs CONSUMPTION 38: gap of 10, fine. FC 38 vs SPREADS 32: co-move, both loose-ish. TREASURY N/A, fine. Inflation parent vs sub-components: consistent.

**11. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not raised by members. EQUITY_VALUATION: only passing mentions of price moves, no valuation commentary. INFLATION_SERVICES: no member discussion beyond one passing remark.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "Both core measures are well below the 2 percent level"; Yellen, "inflation is undesirably low". Center of gravity: core inflation subdued and decelerating, with medium-term worries only. Direction: below neutral because members describe inflation as under objective now.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 28 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "the economy is now operating with a substantial output gap, and that gap is likely to close very gradually"; Dudley, "Slack is abundant". Center of gravity: wide, slowly closing gap. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 +-4 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "unemployment to remain painfully high for years"; Kocherlakota, "The unemployment rate in December was 10 percent". Center of gravity: very loose labor market with minimal hiring. Direction: far below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "Consumption growth is likely to remain subdued"; Pianalto, "capital investment plans... remain remarkably modest". Center of gravity: recovery under way but spending and capex soft versus past recoveries. Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 68 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "the lion's share of the deviation from Okun's law in 2009 reflects a surge in labor productivity"; Dudley, "Last year's spurt was a surprise." Center of gravity: strong, possibly partly permanent gains. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bernanke, "Financial conditions remain generally supportive"; Plosser, "conditions in the financial markets continue to support the recovery". Center of gravity: market conditions easy, bank credit availability still tight. Direction: looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kocherlakota, "Interbank loan spreads are low relative to historical norms, as are corporate bond spreads"; Evans, "some risk premiums are being pushed too low". Center of gravity: spreads below normal levels. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.**

**BANKING_STRESS: 56 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Duke, "Consumer and credit card delinquencies and charge-offs have peaked but remain elevated"; Bernanke, "the rate of losses that banks are experiencing is still extraordinarily high on a historical basis". Center of gravity: stress peaking but still meaningful, CRE a major risk, lending contracting. Direction: above neutral, improving.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 50 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Warsh, "dollar stability broadly against foreign currencies is an encouraging sign"; Bernanke, "the dollar has moved in an anti-inflationary direction recently". Center of gravity: roughly stable with mixed reports. Direction: at neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52.** Evidence: Fisher, "upward pressures on raw materials prices... prices at the retail level have held flat"; Lacker, prices-paid and received up in manufacturing.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 30.** Evidence: Plosser, "the decline in core inflation has been largely a result of the decline in the price of shelter"; Bernanke, "rents flat to down".
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Evidence: Plosser, "TIPS measures of five-year, five-year forwards have been drifting up"; Dudley, "inflation expectations have gradually drifted up"; surveys stable.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 45 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "my preference is to maintain a status quo position... while continuing on our path toward normalization"; Plosser, "we will have to begin tightening policy some time much sooner than forecast". Center of gravity: majority holds with accommodative language, with a hawkish minority pushing exit. Direction: slightly dovish of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 +-7 (High).** Evidence: roll call, "President Hoenig No"; Hoenig, "I would very much prefer alternative C". Center of gravity: one hawkish dissent plus several language reservations, disagreement mostly on timing and tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-10 (Moderate).** Evidence: Duke's extended banking and credit review; Dudley on Greece, "The contagion could be significant". Center of gravity: several members address bank credit, sovereign risk and spreads meaningfully, but it is not the main topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 35 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bernanke, "Right now the modal expectation around the table is that we are still some meetings away from it"; Evans, "any tightening prior to September 2010 is unlikely". Center of gravity: "extended period" kept, conditional, with gradual normalization prep.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 56 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "uncertainty actually is greater than average right now"; Hoenig, "the degree of uncertainty about any of these tools". Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty broadly voiced, with one skeptic of the framing (Bullard).
