# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2008-09-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant turns of a short special-session transcript, about 1,900 words in all. The text is almost entirely procedural. The Chairman introduces three items: enlarged swap lines, the Wachovia/Citigroup deal, and interest on reserves. Members then ask questions, Lacker adds a short account of Wachovia's liquidity position, and Fisher makes one philosophical comment. A roll-call vote read-out follows (10 Yes, no No). There is no economic go-around and no policy-rate go-around. This is an emergency/special meeting, so by the edge-case rule I score only what is discussed, use N/A liberally, and set confidence to Low or Moderate throughout.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2008 financial crisis. I do not let this override the text. The only text-based macro content is financial-market strain.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Bernanke: financial market conditions "remain quite strained". Liquidity measures are being expanded (swap lines roughly doubled, extended to April 30). Wachovia sale used the "systemic risk exception", which "has not been done before". He expects the Treasury rescue/TARP legislation to give a new resolution tool.
- Geithner: the money market fund facilities are "the only area in which there is an immediate, compelling need", with "escalation options" under study.
- Lacker: Wachovia lost about $1bn of deposits a day. Its liquidity would last only until Thursday or Friday under a calm scenario. Counterparties were "pulling away" mid-day Friday. It "didn't think [it] could survive without a lot of support".
- Fisher: more and more concentration, "too big to fail" problems growing. Evans: asks what could go wrong for the balance sheet, and refers to "substantial deleveraging of the financial system". Plosser: questions on the swap-line extension, other tools, and the asset valuation in the Citi deal.
- No participant says anything about inflation, output, labor, spending, productivity, the dollar's level, equity valuations, or credit spreads.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed by any participant. This is truly zero discussion, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as well above the midpoint (tight/stressed). Bernanke says conditions "remain quite strained". Wachovia is facing a deposit run, money fund runs are spoken of, and emergency facilities are being doubled. Strained means >50. The text is brief and gives no mention of credit being literally unavailable, so I score below the Sept 2008 "credit freeze" anchor of 85-95 and place it at 85 with a wide-ish band. Low-to-moderate evidence volume: Moderate confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is mentioned only as an issuer ("support the Treasury ... their auctions") and as the insurer of money funds. Nothing about Treasury-market functioning, so N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread language and no level information. Funding strain and deposit/counterparty flight are covered under FC and banking. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: only Wachovia's share price ($10 to $1) in the context of a bank rescue, not a valuation or market view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see banking stress as well above midpoint. Wachovia was losing about $1bn of deposits a day and "didn't think they could survive without a lot of support". It was rescued with the first-ever systemic risk exception, and Fisher flags "too big to fail". The tone is acute but not "all banks failing", since Fisher also refers to "healthy" super-regionals. Score 85.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: swap lines provide dollar liquidity to foreign central banks but nobody discusses the dollar's exchange value. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says "strained", so a high score matches. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress; evidence says near-failure and rescue, so a high score matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: no rate decision is discussed. The actions are liquidity provision (larger swap lines, a possible escalation of money fund facilities) plus planning for interest on reserves, which is described by Lacker as limiting the tails of the funds-rate distribution. This is a supportive/accommodative liquidity direction, not tightening. The vote is on the swap lines only. The rubric asks for a score, so I place the lean on the dovish side at 38, with a wide band and Low confidence, because the participants are not debating the stance of rate policy.
- Disagreement: unanimous roll call (10 Yes); no one dissents or voices reservations on the actions. Fisher expresses a long-run concern about concentration but explicitly is "not objecting to the move taken". Evans' balance-sheet question is answered and accepted. Near-consensus: 6.
- Financial stability emphasis: the whole meeting is about financial stability: liquidity measures, a failing bank, money funds, and systemic exceptions. It rivals or dominates anything else, since there is no economic discussion at all. But the document is short and mostly procedural Q&A, so I place it at 92 (Sept 2008 anchor 80-95).
- Forward guidance: no rate guidance. The only forward signals are that more liquidity measures are likely ("escalation options" for money funds, "keep you well briefed"). That is mildly easing in liquidity terms but there is no rate signal. I score 42, Low confidence.
- Uncertainty: little explicit uncertainty language ("not-so-pleasant scenario", "what could go wrong", "immediate, compelling need"). The tone is urgent rather than reflective on forecasts. No forecasts are given. I score 55 with a wide band and Low confidence: the situation is clearly fluid and unprecedented ("has not been done before"), but participants do not talk about uncertainty itself.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP N/A (rule not triggered). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS "strained" requires >50: 85 satisfies it. CREDIT_SPREADS N/A. BANKING_STRESS "stressed/troubled" requires >50: 85 satisfies it. DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** Output gap, labor, consumption, and spreads are N/A, so those pairs cannot be checked. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and BANKING_STRESS are both high and consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** Inflation and all sub-components, output gap, labor, consumption/investment, productivity, Treasury stress, credit spreads, equity valuation, and dollar strength are not raised by any participant in the document. Inflation sub-components: not discussed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 85 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "financial market conditions, as you know, remain quite strained". Geithner: money fund facilities are "the only area in which there is an immediate, compelling need".
- Summary: Conditions are described as strained, with emergency liquidity being doubled.
- Direction confirmation: FC is above neutral (tight) because of the strained markets and run dynamics.

**BANKING_STRESS: 85 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker: Wachovia was "losing about $1 billion in deposits a day" and "didn't think they could survive without a lot of support". Bernanke: the Board "invoked the systemic risk exception, which has not been done before".
- Summary: A large bank needed a rescue merger under a first-ever systemic risk exception.
- Direction confirmation: Banking stress is above neutral because of the deposit run and the exceptional rescue.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, inflation sub-components: N/A**
- Not addressed by any participant in the document.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +-12 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "we are proposing to increase the swap lines fairly significantly"; Geithner: "escalation options" for money fund facilities.
- Summary: Participants support expanded liquidity provision; no rate-policy go-around occurs, so the lean is a weak inference.
- Direction confirmation: Lean is below 50 (dovish/accommodative) because every action discussed adds liquidity support and none tightens.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 6 +-4 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Roll call: 10 Yes, none opposed. Fisher: "I'm not objecting to the move taken, obviously".
- Summary: Unanimous vote and no stated opposition to any action.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is near the minimum because the vote is unanimous and the one concern raised is a long-run, non-objecting one.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 92 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "financial market conditions ... remain quite strained"; Lacker's Wachovia liquidity account; Fisher on "too big to fail".
- Summary: The entire discussion is about liquidity measures, a failing bank, and money funds.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is at the top of the scale because there is no other subject in the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Geithner: "likely to give a recommendation to the Chairman sometime later today"; Bernanke: "we'll obviously keep you well briefed".
- Summary: No rate guidance; the only forward signals are further liquidity support.
- Direction confirmation: Tone is slightly below neutral because forward items all involve more support, though no rate signal is given.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-12 [Low]**
- Evidence: Evans: "what could go wrong for our balance sheet in a not-so-pleasant scenario?"; Bernanke: systemic risk exception "has not been done before".
- Summary: The situation is plainly fluid and unprecedented, but participants use little explicit uncertainty language and give no forecasts.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is above the normal range because of the unprecedented actions, but kept moderate because it is inferred from events rather than stated.
