# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2012-03-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds: members' questions to staff, the economic go-around, the Chair's summary, the policy go-round on the statement (alternatives A/B/C), a long afternoon go-round on communications strategy, and a vote read-out. I score participants' views only and ignore procedure and the staff's exhibits.

**Era context.** Early 2012. Policy rate at the zero bound, "late 2014" calendar guidance already in the statement, a maturity extension program in progress, QE-type easing (alternative A, +$500bn MBS) on the table. Unemployment is cited at 8.3%. I take the facts from the text, not from memory.

**Speakers.** Bernanke, Dudley, Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin (Board and NY); Rosengren, Fisher, Lockhart, Evans, Bullard, Pianalto, Plosser, Williams, Lacker, George, Kocherlakota (presidents).

**Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Core PCE is about 1.9-2% (Pianalto, Lacker). Headline is about to rise on gasoline. Several revised forecasts up (Pianalto, George, Lockhart, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Lacker). Williams, Evans, Rosengren and the Chair see inflation at or below 2% over the horizon. The Chair summarizes: "most believe that inflation is likely to return to the 2 percent target." Participants see inflation at the midpoint, with a slight upward tilt near term. Score ~51.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Evans: "the output gap at 5 percent". Williams: "about 4 percent". Tarullo: "the slack is still there". Kocherlakota and George argue the gap is smaller, but still negative (Kocherlakota's -4%). Bright line satisfied (<50). Score ~36, in the 2010-14 recovery anchor band.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment at 8.3% (Rosengren) is well above the ~7% some view as consistent with liftoff. The labor market is clearly improving, but Yellen, Tarullo and the Chair stress "a long way to go". Kocherlakota argues slack is less, citing short-term unemployment. Level reading puts this in the 16-30 band (UE above 7%). Score ~28.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is described as "disappointing", "trend-like" and "moderate". Autos are strong and business investment is turning to growth-oriented projects, but consumption outside autos is weak and real final sales are only 1.7% (Rosengren). Raskin: "households appear to have neither the income nor the confidence". Net slightly below trend. Score ~42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Dudley ("very weak productivity growth") and Yellen ("productivity growth all but stalled"). Plosser notes the staff reduced its productivity forecast. Below trend. Score ~32.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Dudley: "financial conditions have eased, and I view that as significant and supportive". The Chair: "global improvement in financial conditions". Evans: appetite for risk, but "not especially frothy". Offsetting: tight mortgage and small-business credit, and Europe still fragile. Bright line "supportive/eased" means <50. Score ~40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only term-premium effects of LSAPs and a T-bill question were raised. No dysfunction was described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Rosengren: "Bond spreads and CDS spreads have improved." Evans: improved conditions in high-yield bonds and leveraged loans. Duke: spreads on new loans "have come down from their highs, they are still pretty healthy compared with pre-crisis levels". The text gives direction (narrowing) and only weak level information, so I score in the 45-55 band on the side of the move, with Low confidence. Score 48.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Rosengren: S&P up about 8% since the start of the year. Williams: "equity valuations have risen". Evans: "markets do not appear to be especially frothy". These are price moves without a valuation concern. Default band, 47.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Dudley: "the banking system's stability... remains at risk" (Moody's threatened downgrades). The Chair: "U.S. banks are still facing some stress" but "the overall strength of the U.S. banking system, in terms of its capital and resilience, is significantly improved", with asset quality improving. Duke: banks are cautious and not lowering standards. Net healthy with pockets of concern, so <50. Score 42.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Gasoline and oil surge, food price increases and some retail price hikes (Fisher), but other commodity prices are flat and household energy is down (Pianalto, Chair). Net modestly above stable. 55.
- SERVICES: Not specifically discussed (only subdued labor costs). N/A.
- HOUSING: Home prices and inventories were discussed (Duke), but not housing or shelter inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Anchored near 2%. The Chair: "well anchored". Pianalto: market expectations are below a year ago. Lacker and Plosser worry about drift upward. 50.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds policy (alternative B) with date guidance of "at least through late 2014" and the maturity extension program continuing. Evans wants more accommodation, larger than alternative A. Yellen, Raskin, Rosengren and Tarullo lean to continued accommodation, with Yellen and Rosengren ready to ease further if the recovery falters. Lacker, Plosser, George, Kocherlakota and Lockhart lean toward earlier tightening or dislike the date. The Chair and most hold. This is a hold with maximum accommodation, with active two-sided pressure. Score ~37.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Lacker, 9-1), plus open disagreement from the hawkish side (Plosser, George) and the dovish side (Evans). There was a sharp Bullard-Evans exchange, and Bullard apologized for "impolitic comments". The disagreement extends to the output-gap assessment (Evans vs Kocherlakota/George). Distinct camps, but a consensus hold. Score ~46.
- Financial stability emphasis: Europe and the LTRO, financial conditions, bank stress and credit availability were raised by several participants, but the dominant themes were the labor market, the Okun's law puzzle and communications. Moderate-low. Score ~38.
- Forward guidance: The Committee keeps date-based guidance promising exceptionally low rates through late 2014, and rejects moving the date. Thresholds (Evans-rule-like, 7%/2.5%) were discussed, and the 3.4 variant allows for further easing. A minority wants an earlier date. Strong easing commitment. Score ~12.
- Uncertainty: Many participants stress it. Raskin: "swimming in an ocean of uncertainty... profound". Bullard: "uncertainty we face is profound, and it is at the model level". Yellen: "considerable uncertainty". Dudley says forecasts imply precision that is "pretty laughable". The topics are the Okun's law puzzle, the output-gap measure, Europe, oil and the fiscal cliff. Elevated, not extreme. Score ~58.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; evidence says about at target, so ~50 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 means looser; evidence says "eased/supportive", so it matches. BANKING_STRESS 42 means mild stress; evidence says improved with some stress, so it matches. HAWKISH_DOVISH 37 means a dovish lean; evidence says hold at max accommodation with a minority hawkish, so it matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 (<50, below potential): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (<50, supportive): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 48 (direction only, narrowing, 45-55 band): ok. BANKING_STRESS 42 (<50, sound/improved): ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs LABOR 28: an 8-point difference, within 15. Labor slack is more visible than gap slack because some members (Kocherlakota, George) argue the gap is smaller.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 42: within 15.
3. FC 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 48: both near neutral-to-loose and consistent. FC is looser because of equity gains and easing in Europe, while spreads text has only direction and weak level information.
4. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, consistent with normal times.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 51 vs sub-components: goods 55, expectations 50. Consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members did not discuss Treasury market functioning. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no FX discussion. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no price discussion of services or shelter.

**Text quality.** The text is clean. A few staff replies are removed, leaving member questions without answers, which does not affect the scored content. The communications go-round is long, but it is clearly participant discussion of policy tools and guidance, so I used it for the Tier 3 items.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 51 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Pianalto, "the current year-over-year core PCE inflation rate stands at 1.9 percent"; Chair, "most believe that inflation is likely to return to the 2 percent target." Center of gravity: core near target, gasoline pushing headline up, several forecasts revised up, others expecting 1.5-2%. Direction confirmation: inflation pressure is at neutral, with a slight upward tilt.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Evans, "the output gap at 5 percent 33 months after the trough"; Williams, "our estimate of the output gap currently is about 4 percent". Center of gravity: material slack remains despite debate over its size. Direction confirmation: below potential, because even the members who argue it is smaller (Kocherlakota) still put it near -4%.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Rosengren, "At 8.3 percent unemployment, we remain well above the 7 percent"; Yellen, "we still have a long way to go before labor market conditions could be described as normal." Center of gravity: improving but loose, with unemployment above 8%. Direction confirmation: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Rosengren, "real final sales have been growing at only a 1.7 percent rate"; Lacker, car and truck sales "at the highest level in almost four years". Center of gravity: autos and some business investment are firm, but consumption outside autos and income growth are weak. Direction confirmation: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 32 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Dudley, "the rise in hours worked is mainly due to very weak productivity growth"; Yellen, "productivity growth all but stalled". Center of gravity: weak productivity is widely noted as explaining the Okun's law puzzle. Direction confirmation: below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions have eased, and I view that as significant and supportive of economic growth"; Evans, "greater appetite for risk-taking". Center of gravity: conditions eased markedly on Europe, with mortgage and small-business credit still tight. Direction confirmation: looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Rosengren, "Bond spreads and CDS spreads have improved"; Duke, spreads on new loans "have come down from their highs, they are still pretty healthy compared with pre-crisis levels". Center of gravity: spreads narrowing with only limited statements about the level. Direction confirmation: near neutral (direction-only rule, 45-55 band).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-6 (Low).** Evidence: Rosengren, "The S&P 500 has risen roughly 8 percent since the beginning of the year"; Evans, "markets do not appear to be especially frothy". Center of gravity: price gains with no valuation concern. Direction confirmation: roughly fair.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Chair, "overall strength of the U.S. banking system, in terms of its capital and resilience, is significantly improved"; Dudley, "the banking system's stability, I think, remains at risk." Center of gravity: system sound and improving, with pockets of concern (downgrade threats, European deleveraging). Direction confirmation: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 55 (gasoline surge and some retail price increases; Fisher, Pianalto, Chair). SERVICES N/A. HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS 50 (Chair, "well anchored"; Lacker, "challenging to keep inflation expectations anchored near 2 percent").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "I support alternative B"; Evans, "I favor additional asset purchases larger than suggested in alternative A"; Plosser, "it will be appropriate to start policy tightening well before late 2014". Center of gravity: the hold at maximum accommodation is the majority view, with a vocal hawkish minority and a vocal dovish minority. Direction confirmation: below 50, because the Committee is holding exceptionally low rates, with date guidance and the maturity extension program still in force.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 46 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Lacker, "I'm going to dissent on that"; vote 9-1; Bullard, "my impolitic comments to President Evans". Center of gravity: one formal dissent and distinct camps (Evans vs Plosser/George/Lacker), including disagreement over the output gap. Direction confirmation: moderate to notable disagreement, above the broad-agreement band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Dudley on Europe's "real risk that they were going to spiral down" and the Moody's threat; the Chair on stress tests and banks. Center of gravity: several members address it meaningfully, but it is not the main theme. Direction confirmation: this measures attention, which is moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "I still consider our current forward guidance of 'at least through late 2014' appropriate"; Dudley, "I would be very strongly opposed" to moving the date in. Center of gravity: date-based guidance retained and defended, with debate over thresholds and a two-sided 3.4 formulation. Direction confirmation: strongly easing bias, with a minority wanting an earlier date.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Raskin, "swimming in an ocean of uncertainty"; Bullard, "The uncertainty we face is profound, and it is at the model level". Center of gravity: many members stress the Okun's law puzzle, output-gap measurement, Europe and oil, but not at crisis levels. Direction confirmation: elevated.
