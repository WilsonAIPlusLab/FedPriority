# FOMC Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) — 2015-09-17 transcript

Part 1: omitted in this run

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE — 34 ±5 (High)**
- Rosengren: data "consistent with the continued persistent undershooting of our 2 percent inflation goal"; Evans: core PCE "still just 1.2 percent", "news since July has been disappointing"; Brainard: core PCE "stubbornly mired at around 1¼ percent", headline "moved further away from our target"; Chair: "Inflation is way below our target". Hawks (Lacker, Mester, Fischer) call the shortfall transitory but agree current inflation is below target. Below target, below the 38-42 era anchor because the language is "way below" / "stubbornly" / "disappointing".

**OUTPUT_GAP — 46 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Dudley: "growth slightly above trend"; Bullard and Mester: above-trend growth ahead; Mester/Williams: economy "at or nearly at full employment". Against that, Chair: "I would not describe overall growth as robust", "still a ways from full employment"; Dudley: "we still, in my mind, have excess slack"; Brainard: "some slack remains on other margins". Slack-remains language from the Chair and several others puts it just below 50 (bright line).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS — 49 ±5 (High)**
- Unemployment 5.1 percent (Bullard, Mester, Chair: "just a touch above the median of our estimates of its longer-run normal level"); many reports of shortages and wage pressure (Lacker: "Reports of labor shortages ... continue to proliferate"; Lockhart: "hiring challenges persist and have intensified"; Kaplan: "severe labor shortages in various areas"). Offsets: flat wage growth near 2 percent, participation and part-time slack (Chair, Brainard, Dudley). Near NAIRU, balanced.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT — 53 ±5 (Moderate)**
- Lacker: consumption growth "returned to a 3 percent annual rate"; Dudley: consumer spending has "relatively firm underpinnings", housing "a positive"; Brainard: consumer spending "rising at a welcome pace", equipment indicators "turned up"; Chair: consumer spending and residential construction "solid". Offsets: energy capex to "fall as much as 10 to 15 percent" (Kaplan), inventory build unsustainable (Dudley), Fischer: QE effects not seen in investment "very much". Solid, slightly above trend net.

**PRODUCTIVITY — 35 ±5 (High)**
- Lacker: "productivity growth has been slow for several years now"; Williams: "still-subdued pace of productivity growth"; George: "soft productivity readings"; Powell: "productivity increases are quite slow"; Chair: "weak productivity growth". Well below trend, multiple speakers.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS — 56 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Chair: "a material tightening in overall financial conditions"; Dudley: "Financial conditions have already tightened significantly"; Powell: tightened "by well more than 25 basis points"; Brainard: tightening "roughly equivalent to an increase of 50 basis points". Offsets on level: Bullard: stress index "well below the levels reached ... in 2011 and 2012" and was "arguably abnormally low"; Mester: conditions "have tended to stabilize and partially reverse"; Kaplan refers to "artificially accommodative financial conditions". Tightened from easy to somewhat tight; not described as restrictive.

**TREASURY_STRESS — 40 ±10 (Low)**
- Only Q&A remarks: Dudley: "a lot of Treasury supply that's entering the market, presumably from the liquidation of foreign exchange reserves ... causing swap rates to fall relative to Treasury rates"; Lockhart asks about "the thinness of the market" around year-end. No dysfunction described; normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS — 54 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Mester: "somewhat higher credit risk spreads", and "Volatility measures and risk spreads remain elevated but are moving back down" (a stated level, so > 50); Chair: "a modest rise in risk spreads on corporate bonds", "risk spreads higher". Offsets: Bullard: spreads had been "unusually compressed" and the move is "a return to a more normal market operation"; Evans: consumer credit "delinquency rates near all-time lows". Kaplan (availability): "credit situation is in fact worsening for many small- to mid-cap independent producers" (energy only). Just above normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION — 42 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Valuation views stated: Kaplan: "valuations heading into the summer were fulsome by historical standards" and the correction "could potentially be healthy"; Williams: "we already have elevated price-dividend ratios in equity markets"; Bullard: post-decline prices "may be a more appropriate valuation than previously"; Tarullo/Powell: a ~7 percent decline "after a very long period of appreciation" means little. Somewhat elevated after a modest correction; no distress view.

**BANKING_STRESS — 33 ±8 (Low)**
- Williams: "regarding our bank supervision and financial stability, I think we're in a situation that's 10 times better" (than 2005); Evans: banker reports of consumer credit performance "very good"; George: District bankers "monitoring closely their loan portfolios and reviewing collateral values" (ag/energy). Tarullo's banking-risk remarks are about China, not the U.S. Healthy with minor concerns; few speakers.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH — 68 ±5 (High)**
- Brainard: "the dollar now lies 15 percent above its level last summer"; Dudley: "the dollar has appreciated on a broad trade-weighted basis ... the dollar strength has been persistent and does threaten to undermine U.S. net exports"; Mester: "The dollar has appreciated"; Chair: concerned "we could see significant further appreciation of the dollar". Broad appreciation weighing on exports.

**INFLATION_GOODS — 32**
- Evans: "disinflationary headwinds from low energy, commodity, and imported goods prices"; Fischer: dollar appreciation "has direct effects on the prices of many goods"; Kaplan: Texas selling price index "slipped below zero". George notes six-month durables/nondurables pace has moved up. Net goods disinflation.

**INFLATION_SERVICES — 48**
- George only: services component six-month pace moved from "1.6 percent" to "2 percent". Single speaker; near stable.

**INFLATION_HOUSING — 55**
- Weak evidence: Williams: Bay Area "housing crisis is so extreme", lodging rates "astronomical"; house-price-to-rent ratio "about 30 percent above historical norms" (asset prices, not shelter inflation); Kaplan: Texas home values "up 8 percent year over year". Mostly regional and price-level, scored only just above stable.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS — 42**
- Survey anchored: Lacker: "expectations appear to be well anchored"; Chair: "reasonably well anchored". Market-based drifting down: Kocherlakota: "quite concerned about the downward movement"; Chair: breakevens "significantly lower ... certainly disquieting"; Brainard: inflation compensation back to "historically low levels"; Harker: "seeming downward tilt in inflation expectations"; Tarullo: "a little shakiness". Anchored but with downside drift.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN — 55 ±5 (High)**
- Decision to hold (alternative B), but a strong tightening bias: Chair: "considerable support for moving—if not today, then in the near future"; preferences for C from Lacker (dissent), Williams (prefers C, votes B), Bullard, Mester, George; Rosengren: "the time to raise rates as being near"; Fischer: "we should raise the federal funds rate before the end of the year". Dovish wing: Evans (mid-2016 liftoff), Kocherlakota ("drawn to alternative A", negative funds rate in SEP), Brainard ("risks of moving prematurely ... outweigh the risks of waiting"). Hold with a tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY — 48 ±7 (High)**
- Vote read-out: 9-1, "President Lacker No". Beyond that, distinct camps: non-voters Bullard ("I see this as a poor decision today", "Yellen put"), Mester ("seize the day and act"), George favor C; Williams prefers C but supports B; Kocherlakota favors A; Lockhart, Powell, Fischer, Williams call it "a close call". One dissent plus several reservations in both directions and some sharp exchanges.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS — 57 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Market turbulence and financial conditions run through most go-round statements: Rosengren on ETF/circuit-breaker "potential fragilities in the financial market structure"; Bullard: "risk of substantial financial-stability issues ... if we are viewed as an enabler of asset price bubbles"; Williams on "new economic and financial imbalances", house-price-to-rent ratios; Kaplan on "unhealthy imbalances" and asset valuations; Tarullo on Chinese leverage and shadow banking; Fischer: "when the tide goes out, you see who's been swimming naked"; Rosengren on selling assets in a CRE/residential "bubble" scenario. Many participants addressed it meaningfully; it is not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE — 58 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Chair: an initial increase "is a real possibility later this year and what most participants still expect"; Fischer: emphasize "the beginning of normalization to occur in 2015"; Williams, Rosengren, Harker: keep every meeting, "including October", live; Dudley: a "very 'hawky' statement in October that pointed very strongly to December". Tempered by "gradual"/"shallow" path language, Lockhart's view that data dependence gives too little guidance, and doves (Evans, Kocherlakota) preferring a later liftoff. A "begin normalization" signal, low end of the band.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED — 58 ±7 (High)**
- Lockhart: "too early to know whether this episode amounts to a bona fide shock or just a nervous spasm"; Tarullo: "we just don't know" and "more than the normal amount of doubt about the reliability of forecasting"; Fischer: "the genuine uncertainty caused by the Chinese devaluation"; Evans: projection "fraught with uncertainties"; Chair: Chinese growth prospects "highly uncertain"; Rosengren: "not enough time to fully process recent market turbulence". Offsets: Mester: "probably not more uncertainty now than in the past"; Bullard dismisses the China signal. Elevated, with several participants stressing unusual uncertainty.
