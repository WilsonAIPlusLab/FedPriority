# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 — Meeting 1996-03-26 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document type
Member/participant discussion transcript: a short staff-Q&A segment (members' questions only), the regional/national economic go-around, the Chairman's summary, and a policy go-around ending in a unanimous vote read-out (10 Yes, 0 No). The Chairman proposes no change in the funds rate (5-1/4 percent) with a symmetric ("B") directive. Staff turns are absent. Procedural material (minutes approval, ratification of transactions, coffee break, lunch logistics) is ignored.

### 2. Era context
Spring 1996. The text itself describes: a first-quarter disrupted by blizzards, government shutdowns and a GM strike; an inventory correction that is "well along"; a very strong February payroll report; a sharp rise in intermediate and long-term rates since the last meeting (long bond up more than 50 bp, per Melzer); a funds-rate cut at the previous meeting that some now regard as "insurance" that was not needed; core CPI around 3 percent; and high stock prices. I score what participants say, not hindsight.

### 3. Speakers and views
- Parry: growth slightly above 2 percent potential; core CPI to stay ~3 percent; unemployment and capacity utilization near or slightly on the low side of natural rates; inflation risk on the upside; "it might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate."
- Broaddus: much more upbeat data; economy near NAIRU capacity, "not a lot of upside headroom"; nervous about money growth and the "spectacular rise" in long rates; may need to reverse last month's cut, suggests a conference call in April.
- Boehne: resilience; risks balanced; inflation subdued; surge in new housing sales but skeptical on sustainability.
- Hoenig: strong District; labor markets getting tighter; core inflation rise likely to continue; loans readily available.
- McTeer: soft landing; risks balanced (but only because of the last cut); wage/price pressures contained; some heat in construction.
- Minehan: growth near potential; modest upward pressure on wages and prices; risks slightly to the downside on growth but would need "decisive steps" if March payrolls are strong; wants policy kept "restrictive enough."
- Moskow: labor markets tight; GM strike effects; prices show little pressure or declines; output gap estimated about nil before 1995 and some slack built up since (1.4 percent GDP growth in 1995), so slightly more optimistic on inflation.
- Guynn: moderate growth; 2 percent a little below potential; inflation could moderate; more uncertainty than usual.
- Stern: labor markets "very tight"; risks shifted: less risk of stagnation, more risk of inflation.
- Melzer: February jobs a 300,000 upside surprise; last cut was "a speculative bet" that was "likely lost"; ECI worrisome; long bond up 50+ bp; wants a target path for inflation; policy "may not even be neutral," should be somewhat restrictive; will go along with a hold.
- McDonough: risks now "quite well balanced"; supports symmetric directive.
- Kelley: resilience; risks symmetric, moving to the upside; "too neat and too pat."
- Yellen: near-trend growth with stable inflation; concern about a stock market correction ("increasingly difficult to justify in terms of fundamentals") and about the housing/durables effect of higher rates; benefit-cost inflation risk.
- Phillips: fog lifting; moderate growth; credit availability plentiful, banks well positioned; inflation outlook "less favorable"; next move "either up or down."
- Jordan: inflation psychology improved; wants lower inflation projection; yield curve repricing was a move toward less stimulus.
- Lindsey: three unresolved issues (labor/household debt, fiscal entitlements, capital-market prices); risks balanced but growing.
- Greenspan: economy resilient because of wealth effects from stock and bond gains; risks as balanced as in a long while; stock market "probably high by any objective measure"; more volatility ahead; recession will eventually come; supports no change.

### 4. Core dimensions (direction in words first)
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint: core CPI around 3 percent, ECI and benefit costs worrying, "inflation risks are on the up side," but "little upward or downward pressure," "inflation will continue to be subdued," several expect moderation. Above target but stable, with upside risks. About 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Near potential. Parry "slightly on the low side of their natural rates"; Broaddus "somewhere in the neighborhood of nonaccelerating inflation capacity"; Moskow "some slack has built up"; Guynn "2 percent is probably a little less than potential." Net: at or very slightly above potential. 51 (bright-line: near potential = 48-52 satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Tight by nearly all anecdotes ("labor markets remain very tight," "you can't hire people at any wage," UE in New England 5 percent, Missouri 3.4 percent) but wage growth subdued; national UE not quoted precisely, Vice Chairman projects ~6.2 percent next year; the Committee's own natural-rate language is "near." Slightly tight. 57.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Resilient, firm: housing surge, strong retail rebound, capital spending plans at records among small firms, but PC investment seen slowing, consumer debt stretched, interest-sensitive sectors at risk. Modestly above trend. 57.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not discussed as productivity (only a computer-price deflator point by Lindsey and a mention of GDP measurement). N/A.

### 5. Financial dimensions
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Credit is plentiful (Phillips "plenty of credit availability," Hoenig "loans readily available"), equity prices high, wealth effect supportive, cost of capital "still fairly low"; offset by a sharp rise in long rates since the last meeting (Broaddus "spectacular," Melzer 50+ bp) and Jordan's view that the market moved toward "less stimulus." Net: still supportive/accommodative but less so than before. Direction: below midpoint. 43 (bright-line: supportive implies < 50, satisfied).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yield rise discussed only as rates; no dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No statement on spreads. Credit availability is noted but not spread level. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan "probably high by any objective measure"; Yellen "increasingly difficult to justify"; Lindsey on capital-market price level. Elevated valuation concern, stated at length by three speakers. Lower = more overvaluation concern. 24.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only passing: Phillips "banks and markets are still reasonably well positioned"; Hoenig loans readily available; McDonough strong loan demand. Healthy, low stress, thin evidence. 33 (bright-line: healthy implies < 50, satisfied).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.

### 6. Inflation sub-components
- GOODS: Moskow reports little upward pressure or declines for aluminum, copper, paper, steel; Guynn "minimal price increases in both raw materials and finished goods"; offset by higher grain, fertilizer and oil prices (Hoenig, Phillips). Roughly stable, 48.
- SERVICES: Not separately discussed. N/A.
- HOUSING: Not discussed as shelter inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Boehne survey ~3 percent (Parry), "3 percent inflation expectations" (Melzer), market participants "do not expect lower inflation" (Melzer), but Jordan sees psychology improving by about 1/2 point. Anchored near, slightly above, an implied goal, with long-rate worry about inflation premium. 56.

### 7. Tier 3
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Outcome is a hold at 5-1/4 with a symmetric directive, unanimously. Forward lean of members: Parry (might soon raise), Broaddus (may need to reverse the cut, conference call), Melzer (policy should be somewhat restrictive), Minehan (vigilance, restrictive enough, decisive steps if March strong) lean hawkish; Phillips says next move could be either way; others simply agree. No one argues for easing; Parry explicitly says easing would be inappropriate. Hold with a modest tightening bias among a vocal minority. 57.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero dissents, all 10 voters Yes, and most policy-round speakers simply "agree" with the recommendation. Real differences: Melzer's open disagreement with the Chairman on the insurance characterization and his call for an inflation path; Broaddus's early-reversal talk; Moskow/Guynn more optimistic on inflation than Parry/Hoenig. These are reservations on tactics and emphasis, not votes. 20.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Stock market valuation is a notable passage (Greenspan at length, Yellen, Lindsey) and the long-rate backup comes up repeatedly (Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan, Yellen). Still secondary to the real-economy and inflation assessment. Limited-to-moderate attention. 27.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Symmetric directive, "doing nothing today"; Greenspan says the next steps could go either way; Parry and Broaddus signal that a rise could come soon if data stay strong; Phillips says either direction. Neutral to a mild tightening tilt. 54.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Greenspan says the Greenbook outlook is "very unlikely to prevail," expects "more rather than less volatility," and a "most difficult period for monetary policy" ahead; Guynn "more uncertainty than would usually be the case"; Phillips "patches of fog"; Kelley "too neat and too pat"; Lindsey "increasing probability that the expansion will not be able to stay on the middle course." Offsetting: many say risks balanced and the data less foggy than in January. Elevated but not extreme. 55.

### 8. Bright-line check
OUTPUT_GAP 51: "near potential", within 48-52. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 43: supportive/accommodative language, below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS N/A. BANKING_STRESS 33: "reasonably well positioned"/healthy, below 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A (yield rises alone are not dysfunction).

### 9. Polarity verification
- EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more equity stress/concern in the rubric's scale, where "stretched/rich" valuations sit at 16-30. Evidence says stocks are high and hard to justify, so a low score (24) matches the rubric's table, even though it may feel counter-intuitive.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter. Evidence says plentiful credit and high asset prices, but sharply higher long rates. Below 50, closer to neutral than the rest. Match.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. Above 50. Match.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH: higher = more hawkish. Evidence: hold with some tightening bias and no easing advocates. Slightly above 50. Match.

### 10. Cross-dimension coherence
1. OUTPUT_GAP 51 vs LABOR 57: 6 points apart, within 15. Labor is a bit tighter because of widespread tight-market anecdotes, while output gap is held near zero by participants who stress the 1995 slack and the 2 percent potential.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 51 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 57: 6 points apart. Spending is firm and resilient, but growth is seen near potential, not above.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads N/A, no conflict.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A, normal times, no conflict.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 vs sub-components (goods 48, expectations 56): consistent with a parent modestly above neutral given tame goods prices, offset by compensation (ECI) and expectations near 3 percent.

### 11. N/A justification
PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed as such. TREASURY_STRESS: only rate levels and the yield curve are cited, no market functioning issue. CREDIT_SPREADS: no mention of spread levels. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no foreign exchange discussion by members. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no category-specific price remarks by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Parry: "core CPI inflation remaining around 3 percent... more likely [change] on the up side." Boehne: "there really are few signs of an acceleration of inflation at this point."
- Summary: Inflation is seen as steady and somewhat above the goal, with upside risks growing, but not accelerating.
- Direction: Above neutral because 3 percent core inflation, rising compensation costs and increased upside risks outweigh reports of subdued prices.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 51 ±4 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Broaddus: economy "operating for a while now somewhere in the neighborhood of nonaccelerating inflation capacity." Moskow: "some slack has built up in the aggregate economy."
- Summary: Participants see the economy at or very near potential, with slight differences about which side.
- Direction: At neutral because views range from slightly below to slightly above potential.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Stern: "Labor markets remain very tight." Lindsey: the anecdotal comments "universally have referred to tight labor markets."
- Summary: Labor markets are widely described as tight, though wage gains remain moderate and job insecurity persists.
- Direction: Above neutral because of consistently reported shortages in most Districts, tempered by subdued wage growth and a mid-5 percent unemployment context.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Boehne: "surge in new housing sales." Lindsey: "double-digit rate of growth that has occurred in gross private domestic investment."
- Summary: Spending and investment are firm and resilient, with housing and retail rebounding, though business equipment and computer investment is expected to slow.
- Direction: Above neutral because housing, retail and investment are described as strong, offset by the stretched consumer and the expected deceleration.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Phillips: "We seem to have plenty of credit availability. The banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion." Broaddus: "really spectacular rise in both intermediate- and long-term interest rates since the beginning of the year."
- Summary: Credit is available and asset prices are high, but long rates have jumped, so conditions are less supportive than earlier.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because credit is plentiful and equity wealth effects are supportive, partially offset by the rate backup.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 24 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "The market is probably high by any objective measure that we can find." Yellen: "increasingly difficult to justify in terms of fundamentals."
- Summary: Three speakers, including the Chairman, view stock prices as stretched and vulnerable to a correction.
- Direction: Valuations are viewed as elevated (low end of this inverted scale) because of explicit overvaluation concern.

### BANKING_STRESS: 33 ±8 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Phillips: "banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion." Hoenig: "loans are readily available at our banks."
- Summary: Banks appear healthy, lending broadly available; only passing remarks.
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as well positioned and lending freely.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS: 48. Evidence: Moskow: "most reports on prices still seem to point to little upward pressure or declines for a broad range of items"; Hoenig: "Prices of raw materials are showing some increases."
- INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 56. Evidence: Parry: "inflation expectations as measured by Ed Boehne's survey are around 3 percent"; Melzer: "market participants and professional economic forecasters do not expect lower inflation."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 ±5 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Parry: "further easing would be inappropriate... it might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate." Broaddus: "we may need to reverse the move we made last month at some point in the not too distant future."
- Summary: Unanimous hold at 5-1/4 percent with a symmetric directive, and a vocal group leaning toward tightening if data stay strong; no one advocates easing.
- Direction: Slightly hawkish because the stance is held with a moderate tightening bias among several members, balanced by those simply agreeing and Phillips's "either up or down."

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±6 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Vote read-out: all ten voters "Yes." Greenspan: "I disagree with Tom Melzer's characterization of what taking out monetary policy insurance means"; Melzer: "the current policy stance is inappropriate given the 3 percent inflation expectations," yet "I agree with your recommendation."
- Summary: No dissent; modest differences on how restrictive policy should be and on the earlier cut.
- Direction: Low because unanimity on the action and only tactical or emphasis differences.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 27 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "The second and more disturbing issue is the question of the stock market." Yellen: "particularly concerned at this stage about the possibility of a significant stock market correction."
- Summary: The stock market and bond yield backup draw several extended remarks but remain secondary to the macro and inflation discussion.
- Direction: Limited-to-moderate attention.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 54 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: McDonough: "I support the 'B' symmetric proposal... ready to move in the interim." Broaddus: "I would hope that we might have a conference call."
- Summary: Symmetric directive with an explicit signal that policy could move either way, and a modest tilt toward a possible rate rise if March data are strong.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because the hawkish contingencies are articulated more concretely than the easing ones.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Greenspan: the Greenbook outlook "is very unlikely to prevail... we probably are going to be surprised by more rather than less volatility." Guynn: "left us with more uncertainty than would usually be the case."
- Summary: Several members, led by the Chairman, stress that the outlook is fragile and an eventual adjustment is likely, though most call the near-term risk balance more settled than in January.
- Direction: Elevated because multiple participants voice unusual uncertainty, moderated by statements that the fog has lifted.
