# v4.1 Member-Discussion Scoring: Meeting 2016-04-27 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, with staff turns removed. It has three blocks:
- A special session on monetary policy and financial stability (a go-round on a staff memo series, plus a Chair summary).
- A financial-stability (QS) commentary round, a Brexit/Puerto Rico discussion, the economic go-round, and the Chair's economic summary.
- The next-day policy go-round, ending in a vote read-out.

The economic and policy go-rounds are standard, so this is not a special-topic meeting. Staff answers are removed, so some members' questions have no visible answer and some stray "Okay." lines remain. This does not hurt scoring.

**2. Era context.** April 2016 is the second meeting after the December 2015 liftoff. The text shows participants worried about a weak Q1 GDP print, with a strong labor market and core inflation near 1.5-1.7%. Global and financial turbulence of Jan-Feb has eased. A Brexit vote is pending. The text, not hindsight, drives the scores.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chair Yellen, Vice Chair Dudley, and Governors Tarullo, Fischer, Brainard and Powell.
- Presidents Rosengren, Mester, Williams, Harker, Lockhart (non-voter), Evans, Bullard, Lacker, George, Kashkari and Kaplan.

Views:
- **Hawkish camp:**
  - Lacker: "this would have been a fine meeting to raise."
  - George: dissents for a hike now.
  - Mester: "the question should be, 'Is it really appropriate not to take the next step?'"
  - Kaplan and Harker: June hike if data firm.
  - Williams and Rosengren: also lean to raising rates.
- **Cautious/dovish camp:**
  - Brainard: "let the data do the talking."
  - Kashkari: struggles to justify a hike on the data.
  - Evans: risks tilted to the downside.
  - Tarullo: not tilted toward raising.
  - Dudley and Powell: Brexit raises the bar for June.
- **Bullard:** supports B but wants new messaging, saying the normalization program is "grinding to a halt" and that the dot plot should lose its forward guidance on the rate.
- **Yellen:** holds now, with a "strong case" for raising 25bp in June if data confirm and risks are unchanged.

**4. Directional statements, core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as slightly below target and rising only gradually. Evidence: core PCE about 1.5-1.7% (Yellen, "about 1½ percent"; Mester, "Inflation is rising gradually"), with some arguing firming (Lacker) and others still-subdued price pressures (Brainard, Kashkari). Below the midpoint, but not concerning: 42.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as near potential, slightly below. Evidence: Fischer "very close to full employment"; Brainard "some slack remains"; Evans "a little more overall slack"; Yellen "still ¼ percentage point above my estimate of its longer-run level"; Lacker alone says growth will exceed trend. Q1 GDP weakness is read as mostly transitory. Roughly at potential with a slight lean below: 48. This sits at the edge of the bright-line band for "near potential" (48-52), and "slack remains" language pushes it just under 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as near full employment, with unemployment about 5%. Evidence: Lacker "no slack left in labor markets"; Mester "at maximum employment"; Tarullo, Kashkari and Powell question whether slack remains; Yellen "still see some room." UE is about 5% with subdued wages: 52.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as below trend. Evidence: consumption growth "slowed markedly" (Yellen) and business investment "soft" (Mester, Evans, Brainard), with a rebound expected. The Q1 weakness is somewhat discounted: 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Participants see productivity growth as far below trend. Evidence: Fischer "very weak"; Powell "productivity growth remains terrible"; Williams "surprisingly bad for the past five years, averaging about ½ percent." Many members discuss it: 23.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Conditions have eased a lot since Jan-Feb. Dudley says "much easier"; Brainard says "substantial easing." Lingering fragility is noted (Dudley, Brainard). Bright-line: "easing" and "supportive" language puts the score below 50: 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Dudley's valuation comment ("Treasury market being really overvalued") and Brainard's remark on smooth bond-market functioning. This is not dysfunction: 38 with low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Brainard says bond spreads "have come down, although they remain elevated" and "still-elevated bond spreads." Yellen says "risk spreads on corporate bonds narrower"; Evans says Ford's spreads were "the narrowest in about a year." Level rule: "elevated" is a stated high level, so the score must be above 50 even though the direction is narrowing: 56.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Dudley says the forward P/E is elevated but the equity risk premium is not out of whack, which is roughly fair. Others note prices rebounding. No bubble or crash language: 46.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants repeatedly call banks resilient and strongly capitalized (Brainard "relatively resilient"; Fischer, Powell, Evans on post-crisis buffers). Pockets: CRE concentration in small banks, energy loan losses, and Puerto Rico credit unions. Bright-line: below 50: 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Participants see the dollar as having depreciated since March (Yellen "dollar down about 2½ percent"; Fischer "the dollar has weakened"). Kashkari still says "the dollar remains strong" and Brainard sees risk of a higher dollar. Bright-line: recent depreciation is below 50, tempered by the high level and the drag from past appreciation: 40.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Oil and import price declines are fading and stabilizing (Powell, Kaplan). Goods inflation is low but firming: 42.
- SERVICES: Williams notes health-care services prices "resuscitated"; services are otherwise not discussed: 52, thin evidence.
- HOUSING: Not discussed as shelter inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Heavy discussion. Brainard "extremely low"; Bullard 10-year TIPS about 1.35% PCE-equivalent and an "erosion" risk; Yellen sees slippage in survey measures. Lacker, Harker and Mester say anchored. Below target, with some downside worry: 36.

**7. Tier 3.**
- **Policy lean:** The Committee holds at this meeting (alt B). The distribution of stated preferences is:
  - One dissent for an immediate hike (George).
  - A non-voter wanting a hike (Lacker).
  - Several want a June hike (Mester, Kaplan, Harker, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen conditionally).
  - Several are cautious (Brainard, Kashkari, Tarullo, Evans, Dudley, Powell on Brexit).

  Lean hawkish: hold with a tightening bias and June live, but not a majority for tightening now. Score 55.
- **Disagreement:** One formal dissent (George), a non-voter openly arguing for a hike (Lacker), and visible camps on June timing. There is also disagreement on slack and productivity, and a framework dispute (Bullard on the dot plot against Evans in defence). In the special session, framework disagreement is real but cordial. Score about 41. The meeting is a typical one-dissent meeting plus broader reservations.
- **Financial stability emphasis:** The whole first session is the monetary policy and financial stability debate, plus a QS/CRE risk round, Brexit contagion, Puerto Rico bank-run risk, and the effect of financial conditions on policy. This rivals the economic assessment in emphasis: 85.
- **Forward guidance tone:** Gradual normalization; "keep options open for June"; strong case for a June hike if data confirm (Yellen). Many want to signal June live. A mild tightening bias with data dependence: 60.
- **Uncertainty:** Brexit described as "Knightian" (Tarullo); Q1 puzzle, productivity, r*, slack and the participation margin all questioned. Many members emphasize unusual uncertainty, but baselines are fairly stable: 55.

**8. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: 48 satisfies the "near potential" band while reflecting "slack" language. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42, below 50 for easing/supportive. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: 56, above 50 for the stated "elevated" level. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: 33, below 50. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40, below 50 for depreciation. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: 38, no dysfunction. OK.

**9. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR (52): within 15. OK.
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40): within 15. OK.
- FC (42) and CREDIT_SPREADS (56): they diverge slightly. This is explained by the level reading on spreads ("remain elevated") against overall conditions, which have eased broadly through equities, the dollar, rates and oil. The 14-point gap is acceptable.
- FC and TREASURY: no crisis; fine.
- INFLATION (42) and sub-components (goods 42, services 52, expectations 36): consistent.

**10. N/A justification.** HOUSING inflation was never discussed as an inflation component.

**Polarity checks.**
- Higher PRODUCTIVITY means more productivity growth; the evidence is weak growth, so the score is low. Correct.
- Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means more strength; the evidence is depreciation since March, so the score is below 50. Correct.
- Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress; the evidence is resilient banks, so the score is low. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 42 ±5 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "core PCE inflation will come in at about 1½ percent on a four-quarter basis this year"; Mester: "Inflation is rising gradually."
- Summary: Below-target inflation, edging toward target.
- Direction: Below neutral because core is about 1.5-1.7% with subdued price pressures cited by several.

**OUTPUT_GAP 48 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Fischer: "we're very close to full employment"; Brainard: "This suggests some slack remains."
- Summary: Economy near potential with mild residual slack.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral; Lacker's above-trend view is the minority.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 52 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Lacker: "there's no slack left in labor markets"; Yellen: "I still see some room for further improvement."
- Summary: Near full employment, unemployment about 5%, with debate over residual slack.
- Direction: At or just above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40 ±6 (High)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "Consumer spending, in particular, has slowed markedly"; Mester: "Business investment remained weak in the first quarter."
- Summary: Below-trend spending in Q1, with expected rebound.
- Direction: Below neutral because both consumption and capex were soft.

**PRODUCTIVITY 23 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Powell: "productivity growth remains terrible"; Williams: "productivity growth has been surprisingly bad for the past five years, averaging about ½ percent."
- Summary: Productivity growth is far below trend and members see no near-term rebound.
- Direction: Well below the historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 ±6 (High)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "financial conditions are now much easier compared with just a few months ago"; Yellen: "financial conditions have significantly improved."
- Summary: Conditions have eased back to supportive, with some fragility cited.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser), per the bright-line for easing and supportive conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS 38 ±8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "it all comes back to the U.S. Treasury market" being overvalued; Brainard: "relatively smooth functioning of the corporate bond market."
- Summary: Valuation risk raised, but no dysfunction.
- Direction: Below the stress threshold; normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 56 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Brainard: "corporate bond spreads have come down, although they remain elevated"; Yellen: "risk spreads on corporate bonds narrower."
- Summary: Spreads have narrowed but the stated level is still elevated.
- Direction: Above neutral on the level reading; the narrowing is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 46 ±8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "The forward P/E ratio of the equity market looks elevated... If you look at the equity risk premium, it doesn't look particularly out of whack."
- Summary: Roughly fair valuation view, with mild P/E concern.
- Direction: Near neutral, slightly toward elevated valuation.

**BANKING_STRESS 33 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Brainard: "U.S. financial firms and markets came through the elevated volatility... looking relatively resilient"; Powell: "have made the banking system and our overall financial system safer."
- Summary: Healthy and well-capitalized banks, with pockets (small-bank CRE, energy loans, Puerto Rico credit unions).
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "the dollar down about 2½ percent"; Kashkari: "the dollar remains strong."
- Summary: The dollar has depreciated since March, from a high level.
- Direction: Below neutral per the direction-of-movement rule.

**INFLATION_GOODS 42** (value only). Evidence: Powell: "effects of declines in oil and import prices fade over time."

**INFLATION_SERVICES 52** (value only). Evidence: Williams: health-care services prices "resuscitated."

**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 36** (value only). Evidence: Brainard: market-based compensation "remain extremely low"; Bullard: "a possible erosion of FOMC credibility"; against it Mester: "inflation expectations have remained reasonably well anchored."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 55 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Lacker: "this would have been a fine meeting to raise the target"; Yellen: "I would see a strong case for raising the target range... another 25 basis points at our next meeting."
- Summary: Hold today with a tightening bias, June live, one dissent for hiking now, several cautious voices.
- Direction: Above neutral (lean hawkish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 41 ±7 (High)**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President George No"; Lacker: "I believe this would have been a fine meeting to raise."
- Summary: One formal dissent plus visible camps on June timing and messaging.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement, typical one-dissent meeting plus broader reservations.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 85 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "a special session on the relationship between monetary policy and financial stability"; Rosengren on CRE and "a quick price reversal."
- Summary: The first meeting day is largely about financial stability, alongside Brexit and Puerto Rico risk.
- Direction: Dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 60 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "we definitely need to keep our options open for June"; Mester: "operating assumption is that we will likely raise the funds rate in June."
- Summary: Gradual normalization signal with June live, conditional on data.
- Direction: Mildly hawkish forward signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 55 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Tarullo: Brexit "feels to me more like Knightian uncertainty than just risk assessment"; Williams: "recognizing all of the uncertainty."
- Summary: Brexit, Q1 weakness, productivity and r* are all flagged as hard to read.
- Direction: Elevated but with fairly stable baselines.
