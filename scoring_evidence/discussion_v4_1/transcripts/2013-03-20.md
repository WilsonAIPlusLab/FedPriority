# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting of 2013-03-20 (two-day meeting, 19-20 March)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening remarks on a leak investigation (procedural, ignored), questions to staff, a financial-stability go-round, the economic go-round, an efficacy-and-costs-of-asset-purchases go-round, and the policy go-round on draft statement alternatives, ending with a roll-call vote. Staff answers are removed, so some member questions appear without the replies. The last ~25 lines (after "March 19-20, 2013") are scrambled fragments of closing remarks. They are procedural and do not affect the scoring.

**2. Era context.** Early 2013: large-scale asset purchases (LSAPs, open-ended, $85bn/month) are in progress, policy rate at 0-25bp, forward guidance is threshold-based (6.5% unemployment). Fiscal drag from payroll tax increase and sequester is a main theme. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Bernanke, Vice Chairman Dudley, Governors Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin, Stein, Powell, and Presidents Rosengren, Evans, Pianalto, Fisher, Bullard, Plosser, Williams, Lockhart, Kocherlakota, George, Lacker. Camps on policy: one group wants to keep or add accommodation and defend credibility (Rosengren, Evans, Kocherlakota, Yellen, Raskin, Williams, Dudley). A second group wants to taper soon (Plosser, Lacker, George, Fisher, and, more conditionally for a June start, Duke, Stein, Powell, Lockhart, Pianalto). Bullard is mixed.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2% target. Core PCE is 1.3% y/y (Evans, Williams), total 1.2%, "barely above 1 percent" (Bernanke). Dudley sees "no signs of upward pressure." Some see medium-term upside risks tied to the balance sheet (Plosser, Fisher, George, Bullard). The central tendency is below midpoint, in the 26-40 band. Score 33.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output below potential. Rosengren puts unemployment 2.5 points above full employment, Tarullo cites "significant remaining slack," Evans says "resource gaps remain large," and Bernanke says the labor market is "still very weak." Powell hopes the economy "begins to absorb excess capacity." Lacker doubts the gap is far below potential, a minority view. Below 50. Score 35.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 7.7% with payrolls near 200k/month. Slack is large but shrinking. Level rule puts this in 16-30 or low 30s. Score 31.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumers are "taking the payroll tax increase in stride," and housing and autos are strong. Business investment is cautious, lower-income households are squeezed, and sequester worries remain. Net slightly below trend or moderate. Score 45.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched only by Yellen ("employment gains owe more to weak productivity growth") and Fisher (firms "driving for productivity"). Weak productivity is the one directional reading. Score 40, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Bernanke says financial conditions "have eased fairly significantly since last summer and are now more easy than normal." Powell says they are "generally healthy and supportive of growth." Dudley sees "a substantial easing in financial conditions." Bright-line requires below 50. Score 29.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Dudley (citing the Desk) says trading volumes, bid-offer spreads and settlement fails "indicate few difficulties." Yellen says "no evidence thus far of any issues." Duke and George raise only prospective MBS-market worries. Normal functioning. Yield rise (Plosser: 10-year up 40bp) is not dysfunction. Score 32, moderate confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Rosengren says the high-yield spread "is not particularly low," in line with other rates. Dudley notes "narrower credit spreads" (direction) and that high-yield and leveraged loans are "somewhat frothy." Powell says risk-taking has returned to "at least normal levels." Level reading is about normal to slightly below normal. Score 42, moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Rosengren says "stock prices are higher, but so are earnings," and P/E is below the 20-year average. Fisher flags margin debt back at pre-correction levels as "a yellow light." Mixed, roughly fair valued. Score 45, moderate.
- BANKING_STRESS: Fisher says "our banking sector is healthier," with ROA above 1% and noncurrent loans falling. Rosengren and Pianalto cite stress tests showing banks can withstand rate shocks. Cyprus is a European worry only. Below 50. Score 27.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dudley ("firming of the dollar"), Williams ("The dollar has appreciated") and Bernanke ("stronger dollar over time") describe modest appreciation. Above 50. Score 58.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: mixed (gasoline spike with little pass-through, lumber and building materials up about 30%, but commodity prices falling and a stronger dollar), so 50. Services: not discussed, N/A. Housing: Duke warns of pressure through owners' equivalent rent, "rents keep rising and vacancies keep falling," so 62. Expectations: "well anchored" by Pianalto, Dudley, Yellen and Bernanke. George watches 5y5y breakevens at 2.8%, Fisher reports businesses planning for inflation, and Bullard expects higher expectations. Anchored near target with some upside worry, so 52.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says below target, so below 50 matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter; evidence says slack, so low matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress; evidence says healthy, so low matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter; evidence says easy, so low matches. DOLLAR higher = stronger; evidence says appreciated, so above 50 matches.

**6. Tier 3 in words.**
- Policy lean: The policy outcome is continuing $85bn/month of purchases, a "very strong majority for continuing the policy" (Bernanke). Kocherlakota, Evans and Rosengren want to preserve or add accommodation. A substantial group wants tapering started by June, and Plosser, Lacker and George want it sooner. Because the QE debate is over when to slow down, not about removing accommodation, the lean is dovish with a visible hawkish minority. Score 30.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (George). Distinct camps on efficacy and costs, remittance smoothing (Bullard vs Tarullo, Stein, Duke, Lacker), and the timing of tapering. Plosser and Lacker say they would have tapered now yet support B. No heated personal exchange. Score 42.
- Financial stability attention: A dedicated financial-stability go-round, plus an entire afternoon on costs (reaching for yield, agency REITs, ETFs, exit risks, rate-spike risk), plus tapering motivated by stability by Stein and Powell. Dominant topic, rivals the economic assessment. Score 78.
- Forward guidance: Threshold guidance (6.5%) is retained. The statement keeps "will continue" and tapering is discussed as possible in June. Bernanke plans press-conference messaging that tapering would reflect a better economy. Kocherlakota and Powell want a slower exit path to offset tapering. Net signal is strong easing with a growing taper hint. Score 20.
- Uncertainty: Considerable uncertainty about efficacy and costs of LSAPs ("jury is out," Lacker; "uncharted territory," Bullard and Plosser; Dudley "level of uncertainty... is still high" on fiscal drag; Tarullo "we have no idea" on sequester). Offsetting this, Bullard and Lockhart say outlook uncertainty and tail risk have diminished. Elevated but mixed. Score 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 35 (<50, ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 29 (<50, ok). CREDIT_SPREADS 42 (text gives level "not particularly low" and "narrower" as direction only; level reading gives normal to slightly below, so <50, ok). BANKING_STRESS 27 (<50, ok). DOLLAR 58 (>50, ok). TREASURY_STRESS 32 (not >60, ok).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 35 vs LABOR 31: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 35 vs CONSUMPTION 45: gap of 10, fine. FC 29 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 42: both below 50; spreads closer to normal than FC because Rosengren says high-yield spreads are not particularly low, while FC is described as clearly easy by Bernanke and Powell. Acceptable. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: calm, consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 33 vs sub-components (goods 50, housing 62, expectations 52): the parent is lower because headline and core measures are cited far below target, and the sub-components are mixed and mostly anecdotal. The parent is anchored on the direct measures participants quoted.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no participant discusses services prices. All other dimensions have at least some direct participant discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "latest year-over-year core PCE inflation number is down to 1.3 percent... that might be more than 'somewhat.'" Dudley: "I don't see any signs of upward pressure on inflation or compensation trends."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is seen below target, with only a minority seeing upside risks later.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because core PCE is 1.3% against the 2% target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: unemployment is "2½ percent above my estimate of full employment." Evans: "Resource gaps remain large."
- Center of gravity: Most see large remaining slack, with Lacker the exception on gap size.
- Direction confirmation: Output is below potential because of slack language across most speakers.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 31 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "The labor market is still very weak." Tarullo: "significant remaining slack in labor markets."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment at 7.7% is improving but far from full employment.
- Direction confirmation: Labor is looser than neutral because UE is above 7% with slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Pianalto: "consumer spending is growing modestly despite large monthly fluctuations in disposable income." Lockhart: "very uneven consumption growth" with lower-income households economizing.
- Center of gravity: Housing and autos strong, businesses cautious, fiscal drag ahead.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is slightly below trend to moderate, given the hesitancy and fiscal drag.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "recent employment gains owe more to weak productivity growth than to strong output growth."
- Center of gravity: A single clear remark, implying below-trend productivity.
- Direction confirmation: Below neutral because of the "weak productivity growth" reading.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 29 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "financial conditions have eased fairly significantly since last summer and are now more easy than normal." Powell: "Financial conditions remain generally healthy and supportive of growth."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are described as easy and supportive by several speakers.
- Direction confirmation: Conditions are looser than neutral (accommodative and supportive language).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 32 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "trading volumes, bid-offer spreads, failures to deliver securities... all of these things indicate few difficulties." Yellen: "no evidence thus far of any issues."
- Center of gravity: Normal functioning, with only prospective MBS-market concerns.
- Direction confirmation: Stress is below neutral because functioning is normal or improved.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "the spread between high-yield bonds and Treasury securities is not particularly low." Dudley: "narrower credit spreads" and high-yield "somewhat frothy."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are normal to modestly below normal, with some froth in high-yield and leveraged loans.
- Direction confirmation: Below neutral because spreads are seen at or somewhat below normal levels, not elevated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "Stock prices are higher, but so are earnings... below the 20-year average." Fisher: margin debt "back to levels that prevailed before previous serious market corrections."
- Center of gravity: Roughly fair, with scattered caution from Fisher.
- Direction confirmation: Slightly below neutral (mild overvaluation concern from a minority).

**BANKING_STRESS: 27 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "our banking sector is healthier... back above a 1 percent ROA." Rosengren: "most banks subject to our stress-test modeling can weather a rapid rise in triple-B rates."
- Center of gravity: The banking system is seen as healthy and resilient.
- Direction confirmation: Stress is below neutral because banks are described as healthy and well capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "The dollar has appreciated." Dudley: "the firming of the dollar should reinforce that [low inflation]."
- Center of gravity: Modest appreciation noted by three speakers.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because the dollar is strengthening.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 50.** Gas prices rose with limited pass-through (Bernanke), lumber and building materials up about 30% (George), commodities falling (Williams). Net stable.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 62.** Duke: pressure "through owners' equivalent rent," "if rents keep rising and vacancies keep falling."
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Pianalto: "inflation expectations remain well anchored." George watches 5y5y breakeven at 2.8%.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I think there is a very strong majority for continuing the policy." Kocherlakota: "there is a strong argument for actually adding to accommodation." Against: Plosser: "begin tapering asset purchases with an aim to ending them before the end of this year."
- Center of gravity: Continued maximum accommodation with a sizable minority pressing to taper from June.
- Direction confirmation: Dovish because the policy outcome is continued large-scale easing, with a tapering bias in a minority.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: George is the lone "No" in the roll call; she says "Alternative B, however, continues a policy that I believe is too accommodative." Tarullo: "people who were strong supporters... people who were pretty strongly opposed... and people who are ambivalent."
- Center of gravity: One dissent plus distinct camps on LSAP costs and exit, but a strong majority and a cordial tone.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is moderate, above a typical one-dissent meeting because camps differ on assessment of costs and efficacy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 78 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "we have an opportunity to raise issues... on financial stability concerns," followed by a long go-round. Powell: "Communication is the main tool by which... we do manage financial stability."
- Center of gravity: Financial stability and costs occupy a full go-round and recur through the policy discussion.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is high regardless of direction (reaching for yield, REITs, rate-spike risk, exit risks).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 20 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "we could lower the threshold" and "our reaction function is going to be easier than the markets would anticipate." Bernanke on the press conference: "a slowing of purchases is a slowing in the pace at which accommodation is added, as opposed to a tightening."
- Center of gravity: Threshold-based easing guidance is retained and tapering is framed as slower accommodation, though members start preparing markets for a possible June taper.
- Direction confirmation: Easing-biased because the thresholds and open-ended purchases stay, with a mild move toward taper signaling.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "regarding efficacy, I think the jury is out." Bullard: "the consequences are almost wholly unknown." Offsetting: Bullard: "the uncertainty aspect has been greatly reduced"; Lockhart: "tail risk... has diminished."
- Center of gravity: Substantial uncertainty on LSAP effects and fiscal drag, but several see lower outlook uncertainty.
- Direction confirmation: Above the "normal" band but not very high because views are mixed.
