# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1999-08-24

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed: opening procedure, a long Q&A and approval session on Y2K liquidity-facility authorizations (SOMA lending limits, options, special liquidity facility), members' questions to the staff on the outlook, the economic go-around, the policy go-around, the vote read-out (partial), and a closing item on a subcommittee to review the tilt/announcement language. Some words are blanked in the extraction (confidential country names, a few figures); none of these blanks changes the scoring. The read-out lists ten names; McTeer is recorded as "No", the others "Yes".

**2. Era context.** Late 1990s boom: very low unemployment, strong demand, favorable supply shocks (productivity) that may be fading, oil and commodity prices rising, dollar expected to weaken, Y2K liquidity concerns, one earlier tightening (June). I scored what participants said, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: Y2K illiquidity worry, "very strongly in the group that wants to tighten" as insurance, but inflation outlook "still a forecast", price stability "by any measure", productivity strong, gap "less than 1/2 percent", recommends +25bp and symmetric directive.
- McDonough: price stability, but 25bp is "a bit of insurance"; supports "C symmetric".
- Hawkish-leaning presidents/governors: Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Broaddus, Guynn, Kelley, Meyer, Stern, Ferguson, Jordan, Poole (all favor the 25bp move; Broaddus prefers an asymmetric tightening tilt; Jordan would take a 50bp discount rate catch-up).
- Doves/skeptics: McTeer (no rate increase, supply-side and productivity argument, dissents), Gramlich (supports but hopes Greenspan is right), Boehne (risk of raising less than not raising).
- Hoenig, Parry, Broaddus, Jordan raise cautions about the options program.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as roughly at the midpoint to slightly above. Measured core inflation is low and moderate (core CPI 1.7 to 2.1 percent; core PCE 1.4 percent), Greenspan and McDonough call it price stability, McTeer says it is under control. Against that, many see wages turning up, oil and commodity prices rising, import prices rising with the dollar, discounting diminishing, stalled disinflation (Parry), and upside risk to inflation. Net: slightly above 50, about 52.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as above sustainable. Kelley: growth of 3.5 percent or more "would mark a return to a pace above a sustainable level"; Meyer: "at or above trend". Greenspan says the gap is "less than 1/2 percent" and asks whether the economy is overheating (open question). Bright-line: above trend language, so above 50, but modest. 56.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "As all of us have been reporting for a long time, labor markets are very tight" (Moskow); "extremely tight" (Hoenig, McTeer); regional unemployment 3.5 percent, 3.9 percent, below 3 percent in parts of New England; shortages across sectors. UE below 4 percent regionally, so well above 55. 74.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend: record vehicle sales, "robust" consumer spending, brisk construction, strong housing, wealth effects, high confidence; offset by a second-quarter slowdown seen as an aberration, some farm/manufacturing softness. 63.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Heavily discussed. Greenspan sees continued acceleration (about 3.5 percent income-side), McTeer expects 3 percent, Kelley "strong productivity gains", Parry sees the positive shock at risk. Above trend. 72.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Poole: conditions were accommodative until this year, "that situation has changed"; Greenspan cites the substantial rise in real investment-grade yields (BAA real rate up about 1 point) and firming of conditions; Minehan: markets "fragile and volatile", Y2K liquidity fears; Kelley: stimulus from last fall's crisis remaining; the statement text says markets "functioning more normally". Roughly neutral, slightly tightening from loose. 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss Y2K liquidity, SOMA lending, Treasury balances, but not Treasury market dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Minehan: "some widening of spreads is not unhealthy"; Greenspan mentions higher real BBB-type yields. Direction only, no level given. Per v4.1 rule: 52-55, Low confidence. 53.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation concern: Kelley "sky-high stock market", Minehan on PE ratios and the upward trend, Meyer and Broaddus on a possible correction, Ferguson on wealth effects. Stretched valuations. 26.
- BANKING_STRESS: Limited: Hoenig "not seeing major problems surfacing in loan portfolios", nonperforming loans up but far below the prior episode; Boehne farm-area small banks may suffer; McDonough tightening standards, stable delinquencies; Poole "banking system is ready". Sound with pockets. Below 50. 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Members expect and some report dollar weakening: Moskow "weakness of the dollar", Guynn "decline in the value of the dollar", Ferguson "commensurate weakening", Meyer "sharper decline", Minehan "dollar more likely to weaken". Bright-line: weakening so below 50. 35. (Greenspan notes a crisis could produce a flight to the dollar, but that is hypothetical.)

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: oil, commodity and building-material prices rising, some raw materials stable or lower (timber, concrete, memory chips), mixed to modestly firm. 55. Services: medical costs above 4 percent, health care premium increases, wage-driven pressure; moderately firm. 55. Housing: double-digit home price appreciation in parts of NY, faster price increases (Broaddus), Greenspan on owner-equivalent rent possibly rising from 0.1 to 0.3 monthly. 62. Expectations: Parry says survey expectations "flattened out"; bond rates rising (Broaddus); thin evidence, near anchored. 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee raises the funds rate 25bp (second hike). Nearly all favor it; many see another move as likely (Stern, Moskow, Minehan, Parry, Broaddus wants a tightening tilt, Jordan wants more through the discount rate). But Greenspan argues tightening to date is probably adequate, most want a symmetric directive, and several describe the move as insurance (McDonough, Greenspan). Current action moderate hawkish with a modest forward lean: 63.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (McTeer: no increase, supply-side argument, "would prefer no rate increase"). Reservations: Gramlich and Ferguson hope inflation is low, Boehne, Broaddus (asymmetric tilt) and Meyer (market neutral) differ on tilt, Jordan wants discount rate catch-up and would prefer 50bp. Economic views differ (McDonough/McTeer/Greenspan on price stability versus the forecast-based hawks), but a consensus is declared for 25bp symmetric. 1 dissent, moderate differences: 38.
- Financial stability emphasis: Much of the meeting's first half concerns Y2K liquidity risk, liquidity facilities and option authorities, with Greenspan citing illiquidity concerns as a reason to tighten, plus attention to equity market correction risk, dollar and international financial fragility. The go-around itself is mostly about the real economy and inflation. Moderate to prominent: 55.
- Forward guidance: A symmetric directive is adopted; the statement says the risk of rising inflation is markedly diminished; Minehan hopes for "standing pat for a while"; but Poole, Meyer, Stern and Moskow say markets rationally expect more modest increases and the Committee should not preordain. Ahead: mild tightening bias but formally neutral. 54.
- Uncertainty: Sustained discussion of uncertainty over the productivity/new-economy test (Meyer, Ferguson "factual uncertainty"), the poor forecasting record, Y2K ("very low probability... catastrophic"), international political risks, and the statistical discrepancy. Standard hedged central view, no "uncharted" language: moderately elevated. 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 56 (>50, above trend/sustainable language): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 50: no explicit "accommodative now" or "tight" claim for the current state; Poole's accommodative remark refers to the past and says it changed; satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS 53: direction only (widening), within 52-55 and Low confidence: satisfied. BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50, "not seeing major problems"): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 (<50, weakening): satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs LABOR 74 diverges by 18. Explanation: labor tightness is scored by the level of unemployment (very low, shortages) whereas members describe the output gap as small (Greenspan: less than 1/2 percent) with strong productivity and supply growth absorbing demand, so output is only modestly above potential.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 56 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 63: within 15.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 50 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 53: co-move.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress N/A, normal.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 52 vs sub-components (55, 55, 62, 50): mean about 55; parent slightly lower because headline measured inflation is low and Greenspan, McDonough, McTeer emphasize price stability. Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only operational discussion of Treasury balances, collateral and SOMA lending; no market dysfunction described.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Parry: "Since mid-1998 this disinflation has stalled"; McTeer: "I believe that inflation remains under control"; Greenspan: "we have arrived at price stability by any measure".
- Summary: Measured inflation is low, but members see pressure building from wages, oil and the dollar.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because many cite upward wage and cost pressure while others call it price stability.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kelley: "a return to a pace above a sustainable level"; Greenspan: "the gap between potential and real output is in the area of less than 1/2 percent".
- Summary: Economy modestly above sustainable pace and potential.
- Direction: above neutral because above-trend/sustainable language is used.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 74 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Moskow: "labor markets are very tight"; Hoenig: "Our labor markets are extremely tight and our unemployment levels are very low."
- Summary: Pervasive shortages and 3.5-4 percent regional unemployment.
- Direction: well above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "Car sales remain at an exceptionally high level"; Guynn: "robust District retail sales".
- Summary: Spending and construction strong, above trend, despite a second-quarter dip.
- Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I find no evidence yet that the increase in the rate of growth of productivity has slowed"; McTeer: trend "much closer to 3 percent than to 1 percent".
- Summary: Productivity acceleration is a central theme, with only a risk of reversal noted.
- Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole: "financial conditions were quite accommodative... But that situation has changed"; Greenspan: "very significant increase in the real long-term investment grade yield".
- Summary: Conditions have firmed from loose toward neutral, with Y2K liquidity worries.
- Direction: at neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 ±6 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "some widening of spreads is not unhealthy".
- Summary: Direction only (modest widening), no level stated.
- Direction: slightly above neutral per direction-only rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 26 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kelley: "the sky-high stock market"; Minehan: "moderation in the continuing upward trend in stock market indices and PE ratios".
- Summary: Valuations seen as stretched with correction risk.
- Direction: below neutral (elevated valuations) on this scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "we are not seeing major problems surfacing in loan portfolios"; Boehne: farm-area small banks "will suffer".
- Summary: Sound system with agricultural pockets.
- Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Moskow: "the weakness of the dollar"; Ferguson: "a commensurate weakening of the U.S. dollar".
- Summary: Dollar weakening, further decline expected.
- Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS 55, INFLATION_SERVICES 55, INFLATION_HOUSING 62, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 50** (see Part 1 for evidence: oil/commodity/materials; medical costs 4 percent+; Broaddus on faster home price increases and Greenspan on owner-equivalent rent; Parry on flattened survey expectations).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Stern: "mildly surprised if we don't wind up deciding that we need to move the funds rate up a bit further"; Greenspan: "very strongly in the group that wants to tighten up on monetary policy today".
- Summary: Near-consensus for a 25bp hike, modest forward lean, symmetric directive; one member for no move.
- Direction: hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: McTeer: "So I would prefer no rate increase at this meeting" (vote: McTeer No); Jordan: would favor 50bp discount rate; Broaddus prefers asymmetric tilt.
- Summary: One dissent plus tactical differences on tilt and discount rate.
- Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "potential for a very significant adverse event in the financial markets as a consequence of liquidity concerns"; Minehan: "nervous about having no liquidity in the fourth quarter".
- Summary: Substantial attention to year-end liquidity and market fragility, less in the economic go-around.
- Direction: moderate to prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 54 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Meyer: further modest increases are "a very sensible and rational expectation"; Poole: "we shouldn't try to preordain... our policy direction".
- Summary: Symmetric directive, mild expectation of further tightening left open.
- Direction: slightly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "this background of factual uncertainty"; Meyer on "uncertainties about the structure of inflation dynamics and the poor forecasting record".
- Summary: Several members stress uncertainty about productivity, inflation dynamics and Y2K, but views are fairly settled.
- Direction: moderately elevated.
