# FOMC Discussion Scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) — 2003-09-16 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Conclusions (short)

- Document type: full member go-around plus policy round with a roll call (12 Yes, no dissent); not special-topic. The opening minutes-wording item (communication language) is a short procedural section and is ignored for condition dimensions.
- Policy lean: hold the funds rate at 1 percent, keep the "considerable period" language; members want accommodation for a long time (Kohn, Bernanke "vigilant patience", Greenspan "not likely to tighten quickly"), with Parry floating easing as insurance. Exit-strategy talk is about future wording, not tightening. Lean dovish-to-balanced (about 40).
- Dissent: no formal dissents; disagreement is over press-statement wording (several would leave last sentence unchanged, Kohn would add the new clause) and a few differences on disinflation risk. Low (about 18).
- Conditions: growth strong (consumption strong, equipment orders firming, productivity "astounding"); labor market very weak ("jobless recovery"); output gap larger than before; disinflation risk is the main concern (core PCE near 1 percent); financial conditions supportive; spreads back to pre-2000 levels.
- Not scored (N/A): TREASURY_STRESS (long-rate backup mentioned only as a policy-expectations signal, no market-functioning comment) and INFLATION_HOUSING (house prices mentioned, not shelter inflation).
- Special-topic call: No. Policy vote and a stance discussion are both present. Staff answers are missing from the input; scored from member turns only.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±6 Moderate.** Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint (low and still disinflating). Evidence: Parry, "core PCE inflation to average only about 1 percent both this year and next, which is already at the low end of what I consider to be the desirable range"; Kohn, "the risks of further unwelcome disinflation". Hoenig and Moskow see inflation stable and risks balanced, which holds the score off the lowest band. Direction: below neutral because the main concern is inflation becoming undesirably low.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 ±6 Moderate.** Slack, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Evidence: Broaddus, "the output gap is even greater now than it was at the time of the August meeting"; Kohn, "capacity utilization remains stuck at a very low level. So we can be confident that slack isn't declining". Direction: below potential; gap seen as closing only slowly (Pianalto: near potential only by end of 2005).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 High.** Weak labor market, jobless recovery. Evidence: Santomero, "the labor market remains the economy's biggest trouble spot. Payrolls continue to shrink"; Gramlich, "employment growth should have turned up several quarters ago." Some small glimmers (Moskow, Bies) are minor. Direction: well below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±6 Moderate.** Spending seen as above trend with momentum, but business investment still cautious and mostly replacement. Evidence: Ferguson, "the household sector still is the bulwark"; Kohn, "pickup in orders and shipments over a wide range of capital equipment"; Gramlich, "Consumption has continued to be strong". Offsetting: Guynn, "few signs of business willingness to invest except to cut costs." Direction: modestly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 80 ±6 High.** Evidence: Kohn, "The astounding productivity numbers"; Bies, "we are at a sea change in productivity"; Minehan, "a productivity boom." Direction: well above trend (Guynn skeptical that it is true underlying productivity, a minority view).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 ±6 Moderate.** Supportive/accommodative, so below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Evidence: Ferguson, "financial markets are certainly supportive of a continued and more rapid expansion"; Ferguson, "financial conditions actually have been rather accommodative"; Hoenig, "a healthy financial system that can support the credit needs of businesses". Mortgage-rate and long-rate backup is noted but not seen as a limit (Ferguson). Direction: looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Evidence for N/A: long-rate backup and the yield curve are discussed only as policy-expectation signals (Poole, Parry, Ferguson); no participant comments on Treasury market functioning or liquidity.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 Moderate.** A stated level: Kohn, "Yield spreads have retraced all the run-up since 2000. Fortunately, they have not returned to the unsustainably low levels of the middle 1990s." Minehan, "narrowing credit spreads"; Ferguson, "risk spreads ... indicate that risk appetites have increased somewhat." Spreads back to roughly pre-2000 levels, somewhat favorable, below the midpoint. Lax C&I loan demand (Ferguson) is demand, not availability, so not used. Direction: somewhat below normal stress (bright-line satisfied).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 Low.** Kohn gives a valuation view: "the equity premium remains unusually wide, indicating still-cautious pricing" (not overvalued, mildly cheap), while equity prices are rising (Minehan, Parry) and Ferguson notes high-tech valuations signaling more risk appetite. Net near fair value, slight tilt to cautious/cheap pricing. Direction: about neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 ±8 Low.** Only brief remarks. Evidence: Hoenig, "a healthy financial system"; Olson, bankers "fairly far out on the optimistic side". Direction: below neutral (low stress, bright-line satisfied). Low confidence because it is one or two passing comments.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 ±10 Low.** Only passing: Gramlich, "the generally high level of the dollar" raising relative labor costs. No direction-of-move language, so a level reference only; score slightly above 50. Low confidence.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Broaddus: declines in unit labor costs "passed along to households through lower prices ... to a large extent in prices for goods, especially manufactured goods"; offset by Moskow ("firming of output prices ... steel, wallboard, plywood") and Hoenig (raw materials rising).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 40.** Only Broaddus, prices lower for "both goods and services" but mainly goods; thin.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Minehan cites rising repeat-sale house prices, which is not shelter-cost inflation.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Kohn, "Inflation expectations have been falling but by some measures are still higher than a few months ago, providing an anchor"; Hoenig, market economists "expecting somewhat higher inflation."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 ±6 High.** Everyone supports holding at 1 percent. Evidence: Greenspan, "We are not likely to tighten quickly"; Bernanke, "a strategy of vigilant patience"; Parry, "an easing might be appropriate as something of an insurance policy." Against: several want a "carefully crafted exit strategy" soon (Hoenig, Kohn, Santomero), and Poole/Stern see inflation risks nearing balance. Direction: hold with an accommodative, mild easing bias; no one proposes tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 High.** Roll call is unanimous (12 Yes, read to last line). Disagreement is about wording of the last sentence (Gramlich, Bernanke, Moskow, Broaddus, Guynn, Bies, Hoenig prefer no change; Kohn, Ferguson, Minehan support new clause) and about disinflation risk (Broaddus/Parry/Bies vs. Hoenig/Poole/Stern). Greenspan ends by accepting the unchanged-wording view. Tactical, not economic, disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 22 ±7 Moderate.** Financial conditions and market reaction to the statement come up (Ferguson's market review, Kohn on spreads and equity premium, Stern on market turmoil risk, mortgage-rate backup), but as context, not a focus; no financial-stability concerns as such.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 28 ±7 Moderate.** The "considerable period" sentence is retained unchanged; the debate is over how to move later to an economic-condition formulation. Evidence: Greenspan, "we ought to think about an exit strategy"; Stewart, "too early to start an exit strategy." Signal: low-for-long with data-dependent eventual exit; no tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Moskow, "a good deal of uncertainty about how to interpret the strong productivity performance"; Minehan, "we need to be humble ... about our ability to see out to 2004 and 2005"; Pianalto, business contacts "harboring a fair amount of uncertainty". Elevated around the productivity/employment puzzle, but with a broad consensus baseline.
