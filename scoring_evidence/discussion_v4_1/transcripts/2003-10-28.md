# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1), meeting 2003-10-28, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion

## Part 1: Conclusions (short)

- Document is a normal policy meeting (economic go-around, policy recommendation by the Chairman, unanimous 12-0 roll call), plus a long post-lunch communications session that I ignored for condition scoring. Not special-topic.
- Policy lean: hold the funds rate and keep the existing accommodative/"considerable period" wording; a minority (Santomero, Hoenig, Parry, McTeer) already want to loosen the commitment, others (Broaddus, Kohn, Greenspan, Bernanke) see low inflation and downside risk. Net: lean dovish hold (about 42).
- Dissent: zero dissents, all 12 voters Yes; every member concurs with the Chairman's recommendation; differences are emphasis on inflation risk and communication design, so low-moderate intensity.
- Conditions: growth very strong (Q3 about 6%), consumption and investment above trend, productivity extraordinary, but labor market still soft and slack remains; inflation very low (Greenspan: "effectively zero"), risks to inflation seen as downside or near balanced.
- Financial: conditions described as expansionary or highly accommodative (Kohn), spreads sharply compressed (Ferguson), banks healthy (Minehan, Hoenig), dollar falling; equities valued near normal per Ferguson.
- Uncertainty elevated (Kohn "extremely wide confidence interval", Greenspan "uncharted waters", regime-change worries), but a strong shared view that recovery is under way.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the inflation rate for all practical purposes is zero. We have reached price stability." Santomero: "core inflation continues to be subdued"; Bies: "four or five years of inflation numbers that start with a 1 or below."
- Summary: participants see inflation as well below the comfortable level, with disinflation risk fading but still a concern for some.
- Direction: below neutral because inflation is described as near zero and subdued; no member reports upward pressure now.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 37 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Santomero: "a significant amount of economic slack in labor and product markets still remains"; Broaddus: gap "shrunk from around 3 percent ... to below 2 percent," about 1/2 percent by end-2004 in the Greenbook. Moskow: growth "above potential until next year" (growth rate, not level).
- Summary: output is below potential but the gap is closing quickly.
- Direction: below neutral (bright line: "slack remains" requires below 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Minehan: "labor markets remain soft"; Hoenig: "remain soft but are showing some signs of firming"; Stewart: September jobs "a little above zero after a string of numbers slightly below zero."
- Summary: jobless recovery, with early signs of stabilization only.
- Direction: below neutral because of persistent slack, high unemployment and weak hiring.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-7 (High)
- Evidence: Ferguson: third and fourth quarters "extremely robust"; Kohn: "6 percent growth in the third quarter"; Bies: "businesses are reluctant to expand," though investment is "firming up."
- Summary: strong consumer and housing spending and a firming equipment recovery, tempered by cautious capex and the fading tax-cut boost.
- Direction: above trend because of the surge in spending and sales, partly offset by cautious business investment.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 80 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Kohn: "another quarter of an astounding increase in productivity"; McTeer: "very rapid productivity growth"; Guynn: "Productivity is strong."
- Summary: productivity growth is regarded as exceptionally high and a central issue for the labor and inflation outlook.
- Direction: above trend.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Kohn: "Financial conditions have been expansionary for some time. They became a little more so over the intermeeting period"; Kohn: "Policy is already highly accommodative"; Minehan: "Financial markets have strengthened."
- Summary: easy conditions from low rates, rising equities and a falling dollar.
- Direction: below neutral (bright line: accommodative/easy requires below 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Treasury market functioning not discussed by participants (only the rate path and long-term rates in passing).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-9 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the dramatic drop in corporate debt spreads ... certainly factual and correct in the fixed-income markets"; Minehan: "credit spreads have become more accommodative"; Kohn: bond rates "moved down a little for lower-rated credits."
- Summary: spreads are described as dramatically compressed with a lack of fear, though the level is stated in loose terms; moderate confidence.
- Direction: below neutral (bright line: compressed/narrow requires below 50).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-8 (Low)
- Evidence: Ferguson: in equity markets "the risk premium seems to be quite consistent with what it was before the run-up in the '90s"; Kohn: "equity prices rose"; Olson: "shift by consumers into equities."
- Summary: one explicit valuation view (roughly normal risk premium) plus price-rise reports; no concern about overvaluation or distress.
- Direction: at about neutral; Ferguson's view supports fair valuation.

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-7 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Minehan: small and medium-size banks "remain in good shape financially, with good asset quality, strong capital ratios"; Hoenig: "a good strong financial system that can support credit needs"; Pianalto: "Bankers ... are having a profitable year."
- Summary: banks are healthy; weak C&I loan demand is a growth, not a stress, issue.
- Direction: below neutral (bright line: sound/healthy requires below 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 28 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Kohn: "equity prices rose and the dollar fell"; "risks around ... a slight further decline in the dollar are probably tilted toward a steeper falloff"; Parry: "the lower dollar."
- Summary: dollar depreciation is repeatedly noted, welcomed for net exports but a possible inflation risk.
- Direction: below neutral because the dollar is depreciating.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Not discussed by members as price components.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47
- Evidence: Bies: "the only [scenario] ... within hailing distance of 2 percent ... is due only to the fact that people's inflation expectations are higher than actual inflation"; Moskow and Parry note markets and Blue Chip expect higher inflation and funds rates than the Greenbook.
- Summary: expectations are discussed but briefly and as anchored near low inflation; slightly above actual inflation. Low weight.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 42 +-6 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "there's no issue with respect to the appropriate policy. Policy is actually working well"; "what are we preempting from when inflation is effectively at zero?" Santomero: "we will have to begin to take some of the monetary accommodation back ... probably earlier than mid-2005"; Broaddus: "The risks with respect to price stability ... are still decidedly on the downside"; Hoenig: "I'm not convinced that we can define a period within which we can keep rates this low."
- Summary: unanimous hold at a very low rate with the accommodative language kept; hawkish voices exist on timing but the weight is on patience.
- Direction: slightly below 50 (hold with mild easing bias, "lean dovish to balanced").

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 +-5 (High)
- Evidence: Roll call: all 12 voters Yes (Greenspan, Bernanke, Bies, Broaddus, Ferguson, Gramlich, Guynn, Kohn, Moskow, Olson, Parry, Stewart). Every member "concur[s]" with the Chairman's recommendation. Sharper exchanges (Greenspan vs Gramlich on benchmarks; Poole vs Kohn on hints about policy) are about communication, not the action.
- Summary: zero dissents, no reservations on the action, mild disagreement over inflation risk and communication design.
- Direction: low disagreement.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 17 +-6 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Ferguson's question on "the lack of fear" in spreads and financial stability; Kohn on household balance sheets and saving rate; Olson on bank lending and risk appetite.
- Summary: a few passing references and one explicit exchange; the meeting focuses on growth, labor, inflation and communication.
- Direction: limited attention.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 35 +-8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "essentially no change in the press statement"; the accommodative "considerable period" wording is retained; Greenspan: "we're going to have to create a credible exit strategy"; Minehan: "Between now and January we probably will be coming to an appreciation of the risks being more balanced." McTeer: "need for a clearly articulated exit strategy."
- Summary: the easing-leaning commitment stays, with a growing view that it must be loosened soon; data dependence emphasized.
- Direction: below 50, easing lean with data dependence, drifting toward normalization.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 57 +-8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Kohn: "any prediction must have an extremely wide confidence interval"; "vast uncertainty about the supply side"; Greenspan: "we are in uncharted waters"; Santomero: possible "regime change in the Lucas sense"; Broaddus: contacts "more than normally uncertain."
- Summary: several members stress unusual uncertainty about productivity, jobs and model validity, but there is also strong conviction that recovery is under way.
- Direction: elevated.
