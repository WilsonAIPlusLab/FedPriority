## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document: two-day transcript (economic go-around, then policy go-around); members only; staff turns removed. Policy action: hold the funds rate, begin balance-sheet reduction in October (alternative B), unanimous roll call (9 yes).

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-5 (High).** Williams: "core inflation rolled back down the hill"; Kashkari: "core 12-month PCE continues to be low at 1.4 percent... a large shortfall"; Brainard: "inflation continues to run well short of our objective"; Bullard: "core PCE inflation will only be 1.6 percent... well below target". Hurricane gasoline effects seen as temporary (Mester, Dudley). Below target, not deflationary.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +-6 (Moderate).** Williams: "economy will continue to grow above potential, pushing the labor market further beyond full employment"; Mullinix: "economy seems to be operating above sustainable levels"; George: "output gap is estimated to expand to more than 2 percent". Countered by Kashkari: "there's more slack than we appreciate" and Bullard's 2 percent growth regime. Most members: modestly above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 64 +-5 (High).** Mester: "tight labor markets... for the first time in 55 years, his firm had more job orders than it had employees"; Mullinix: "strong--even tight--labor market"; Powell: unemployment "materially below common estimates of the natural rate"; George: District unemployment "3½ percent". National UE 4.4 percent (Bullard, Rosengren). Wage aggregates still subdued (tempers upward).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +-6 (Moderate).** Brainard: "resilient consumers and improved investment"; business equipment and intangibles "at an 8 percent annual rate"; Yellen: "business investment has clearly picked up"; George: "Consumer spending continues at a solid pace"; Evans: "solid underlying momentum". Hurricane drag temporary; Bullard: 2 percent growth.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 30 +-8 (Moderate).** Bostic: "Productivity growth... has been exceptionally weak for most of the recovery"; Powell: "very low productivity growth"; Yellen: "slow productivity growth". Several note it only in passing.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-6 (High).** Dudley: "Financial conditions have become easier, even as we have tightened"; Brainard: "financial conditions have loosened somewhat further"; Yellen: "financial conditions overall are accommodative"; Mester: "financial conditions remain very accommodative". Accommodative, bright-line < 50 met.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 32 +-8 (Low).** Dudley: "lack of response of the U.S. Treasury market... why haven't bond term premiums risen". Orderly market, low term premiums, no dysfunction cited; only Dudley and Brainard (spillovers) touch on it.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 46 +-8 (Low).** Williams: "tightening credit premiums"; Brainard: "tighter risk spreads"; Dudley: no widening of agency MBS option-adjusted spread. Direction only, no stated level: 45-55 on the side of the move per clarification, Low confidence.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Brainard: "asset valuations appear to be elevated"; Williams: "price-to-earnings ratios that look excessive with a 2 percent r* are much more reasonable with r* close to zero"; George: financial stability dashboard "aren't sounding alarms". Mild valuation concern, partly explained away.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-8 (Moderate).** Mester: "a stronger U.S. banking system"; Brainard: "few signs of a dangerous buildup of leverage... important safeguards"; Fischer: Italian bank NPLs "declined from 8 percent to 4 percent". Minor pockets: George "ag credit conditions have weakened", Kaplan on Houston small-business loans. Healthy, bright-line < 50 met.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 26 +-6 (High).** Fischer: "The dollar has depreciated nearly 10 percent since the start of the year"; Brainard: "dollar has depreciated further... about 2 percent"; Yellen: "dollar has depreciated roughly 6 percent". Depreciation, bright-line < 50 met.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 48.** Mixed: hurricane gasoline and dollar-driven import prices pushing up (Mester, Brainard, Kaplan) versus pharmaceutical and apparel drops (Williams, Bostic).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 31.** Williams: "lower cost for health-care services"; telecom and cell-service price declines (Kashkari, Yellen, Williams "airfares... education services").
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** No participant discusses shelter or rent inflation (Kaplan's Houston rental comment is about occupancy).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38.** Bullard: "Inflation expectations are currently too low to be consistent with our inflation target"; Kashkari: breakevens "well below 2 percent"; Evans: "inflation expectations will settle in below 2 percent"; versus Mester: "reasonably stable", George: "stable inflation over the medium term". Mostly slightly below anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-6 (Moderate).** Hold with balance-sheet reduction starting; Williams: "we shouldn't slow down the process of normalization too much"; Rosengren: "another tightening by the end of this year"; Powell: "another rate increase at the December meeting"; versus Kashkari: "hold off on any further increases... until we've reached our inflation goal", Evans: "I don't expect to see enough evidence by December", Yellen: "patience is warranted, but also that it cannot be unlimited". Many favor a December hike, a minority prefer waiting; hold with mild tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-8 (Moderate).** Unanimous roll call, no formal dissents. Kashkari: "I prefer alternative A but can support alternative B"; Bullard (non-voter) pressed to strike the "gradual increases" clause and Yellen replied she would "have concern" over it; divergence on the December hike and the inflation diagnosis (Kashkari/Evans versus Williams/Rosengren/George), but expressed as emphasis rather than votes.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 +-8 (Moderate).** Financial conditions are a recurring argument for tightening: Dudley: "I don't see how one can ignore this"; Rosengren: balance sheet may "provide some desired tightening in financial conditions"; Mester: "risks to financial stability if interest rates remain too low"; Brainard: "primary risk would appear to be that of generating financial imbalances"; George: "increasing risk of financial instability"; Bullard devotes a passage to financial conditions indexes. Several members, in the context of policy, not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-7 (Moderate).** Retained "gradual increases" language after Bullard/Kashkari challenge (Yellen: "the language is consistent with the median path"); Dudley: "important to keep the possibility of a December rate hike alive"; Yellen: "labor market developments may make it appropriate to raise the target range... later this year even if there are still few signs that inflation has started to pick up", but also "avoid being too definitive". Balance sheet reduction to begin in October. Mild tightening bias with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Bostic: "I have not found one argument... to be fully satisfying"; Powell: "not yet a clear or satisfactory explanation"; Brainard: "I remain puzzled by the disconnect"; Evans: "uncertainties are too large and too one sided"; George: "uncertainty we face about our judgments of full employment and low inflation"; Rosengren/Bullard: hurricane noise, "Q4 rebound is very uncertain". Multiple members stress an unresolved inflation puzzle, but the real-activity outlook is described with confidence and risks "balanced" (Williams, Mester, Bostic).
