# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) - meeting 1998-02-04 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Document is a regular two-day meeting transcript with a full economic go-around, a long-run ranges discussion (alternative I kept) and a policy go-around ending in a roll call. It is not special-topic.
- Policy lean: hold the funds rate, "B" symmetric directive. Most members say wait and see on Asia. Broaddus (favors "C"), Parry (asymmetry toward higher rates), Jordan ("our next move will be to raise") and Meyer (next move "more likely to be up") lean hawkish. McTeer ("the E word") and Rivlin and Gramlich (act quickly or forcefully if the downside comes) lean dovish. Net is balanced, slight hawkish tilt.
- Dissent count: zero on the directive and zero on the long-run ranges (both roll calls unanimous). Jordan's early "I do want to dissent" is on an operations or authorization item, not a monetary policy action. Disagreement is about the balance of risks (upside vs Asia downside), not the action.
- Economy: very strong, labor markets "tightest in a generation", GDP "above potential" (Moskow), inflation subdued with upside risk. Productivity is seen as a favorable shock.
- Financial: credit abundant and competitive, low long-term rates, stock market seen as possibly irrational (Rivlin). Credit spreads rest on availability and "pricing for risk" language only (Low confidence). Treasury market not discussed (N/A). Banking is foreign-focused (Asian and Japanese banks) with domestic lenders competing hard, so it is Low confidence.
- Uncertainty is elevated: "unusual degree of uncertainty" (Rivlin), "fatter tails" (Guynn), Asia effect unknown (Greenspan and many others).
- Text issues: a few garbled or cut passages (McTeer "I find it remarkable that in recent days", Jordan's director anecdote). Staff answers are absent, which does not affect member scoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 45 +-6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Parry, "inflation remains subdued"; Kelley, "Inflation... is still showing no signs of rearing its ugly head"; Broaddus, "we are very close to price stability right now"; Greenspan, "failure of pipeline inflation to emerge".
Center of gravity: inflation is low and near or slightly below an acceptable level, with wage and service-cost pressure and upside risks flagged but not yet in prices.
Direction: below neutral because members describe subdued, falling-goods inflation, despite concern about wages and services.

**OUTPUT_GAP 56 +-6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Moskow, "The level of real GDP currently appears to be above potential"; Gramlich, "we are fairly close to target, both on the price side and the output side" with unemployment "below NAIRU".
Center of gravity: the economy runs modestly above potential, with a slowdown expected.
Direction: above neutral (bright-line satisfied, >50).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 76 +-5 (High).**
Evidence: Moskow, "Labor markets are the tightest in a generation"; Greenspan, "as tight as any time I can remember"; Stern, Twin Cities unemployment "below 2 percent".
Center of gravity: near-unanimous report of severe shortages and rising wages.
Direction: well above neutral. Wage pressure is only moderate, which keeps the score below the top of the band.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 66 +-6 (High).**
Evidence: Parry, "economic activity was robust last quarter"; Broaddus, consumer fundamentals "exceptionally favorable" and real disposable income up almost 5 percent; Boehne, "very powerful economy in 1997".
Center of gravity: strong consumption and investment with momentum, expected to cool somewhat.
Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 63 +-7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Stern, "productivity is on a more favorable trend"; Parry, "positive productivity shock"; Boehne, "Wage gains continue to be offset by productivity gains".
Center of gravity: productivity is seen as running above trend, though its durability is questioned (Hoenig, Phillips).
Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 +-7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Broaddus, "if financial conditions remain favorable"; Minehan, "abundant credit"; Jordan, "REITs have a ton of money"; Ferguson, "low long-term interest rates that make for a low cost of capital".
Center of gravity: conditions are loose and supportive. The Asian turmoil lowers long rates and credit is plentiful. McTeer notes real rates and the dollar have tightened policy, but that is policy, not conditions.
Direction: below neutral (bright-line satisfied, supportive means <50).

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**
Evidence: Members mention long-term rates falling on capital reflows (Broaddus) and a flat yield curve (McTeer). No one discusses Treasury market functioning.
Direction: N/A because yield moves alone are not dysfunction and the topic was not raised.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 32 +-10 (Low).**
Evidence: Guynn, "credit still seems to be available and competition is fierce... apt to grant loans at lower rates... pricing for risk"; Jordan, "ample funding for just about any project".
Center of gravity: this rests on availability, with Guynn's pricing-for-risk remark. No quoted spread levels. Asian-affiliated lenders tightening in the 12th District (Parry) is a small offset.
Direction: below neutral (easy, abundant credit).

**EQUITY_VALUATION 38 +-10 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Rivlin, "Yesterday's stock market increase seemed irrational to me"; Gramlich, declines in earnings "could perfectly well be known and already capitalized".
Center of gravity: a mild valuation concern (rich or irrational exuberance), with no one calling it a bubble.
Direction: modestly above fair value.

**BANKING_STRESS 40 +-12 (Low).**
Evidence: Guynn, lenders say standards are not slipping but are granting loans at lower rates; Parry, Asian-owned branches tighten standards "due to concerns about the capital positions of their parents"; McDonough, Japan's "financial and political paralysis".
Center of gravity: domestic lenders are seen as healthy and competitive. Stress sits in Asian and Japanese banks, which affects the US only indirectly. The topic is not discussed directly.
Direction: below neutral for the US system, tempered by foreign bank stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 68 +-7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: McTeer, "the strength in the foreign exchange value of the dollar"; Stern, "the dollar has been appreciating since the spring of 1995"; Hoenig, "the stronger dollar"; Boehne, "the strong dollar".
Center of gravity: a strong dollar is widely cited as an export drag.
Direction: appreciation, above 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS 30.** Evidence: Phillips, "goods inflation appeared to be about nonexistent"; Greenspan, unit nonlabor costs "have continued to decline".
**INFLATION_SERVICES 62.** Evidence: Phillips, services inflation "did persist at about 3 percent"; McDonough, service-sector employment costs "rising rather significantly".
**INFLATION_HOUSING 55.** Evidence: Broaddus, "new home prices are rising"; Boehne, new-home price increases "holding" while existing-home prices stay flat. Thin evidence.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 40.** Evidence: Boehne, "inflationary expectations falling"; Gramlich, "inflation anticipations are turning down"; Ferguson, expectations among factors that "diminish" pressure.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 53 +-6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Greenspan, "stay where we are, namely 'B' symmetric"; Broaddus, "I still favor alternative 'C'"; Parry, "asymmetry in the direction of higher rates"; McTeer, "not too early... to start talking... about whether and when to ease".
Center of gravity: hold with a symmetric bias. A hawkish minority (Broaddus, Parry, Jordan, Meyer) is balanced by easing talk from McTeer, Rivlin and Gramlich.
Direction: at neutral, slightly hawkish tilt.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 28 +-7 (High).**
Evidence: Unanimous roll calls on the long-run ranges and on the directive (12 Yes each). Broaddus (non-voter) states a preference for "C". Jordan's "I do want to dissent" concerns an early operations or authorization item.
Center of gravity: broad agreement on action, with open disagreement about which risk dominates.
Direction: low disagreement, with some difference over the balance of risks.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 30 +-8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Guynn on "excessively speculative commercial construction" and credit standards vs pricing; Rivlin on the "irrational" stock market; extensive Asian financial-crisis discussion.
Center of gravity: financial issues appear as context and risk scenarios, mainly through Asia, with limited domestic focus.
Direction: attention is limited to moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 52 +-6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Greenspan, "important to be sensitive to moving in one direction or the other"; Meyer, "the next move still is more likely to be up"; Ferguson, retain "the balanced approach that we adopted at the last meeting".
Center of gravity: symmetric, wait-and-see, with a possible upward tilt in the Chairman-adjacent commentary offset by readiness to ease.
Direction: neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 62 +-7 (High).**
Evidence: Rivlin, "an unusual degree of uncertainty... more true now"; Guynn, "range of potential outcomes is even wider and more uncertain"; Greenspan, "What we do not know is its eventual order of magnitude."
Center of gravity: many members stress unusual uncertainty about Asia, inventories and the ECI.
Direction: elevated, short of "uncharted territory".
