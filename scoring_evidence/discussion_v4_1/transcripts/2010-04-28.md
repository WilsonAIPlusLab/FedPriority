## Part 2: Evidence and scores

Document read to the last line (vote read-out: 9 Yes, 1 No, Hoenig). Staff turns are absent from the input; only member and participant statements are used.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE = 25 +-6 (High).** Pianalto: median CPI "fallen precipitously, to a record low of 0.6 percent", pricing "unusually tame". Yellen: "very low recent readings on core inflation", inflation "undesirably low"; Kohn: inflation "well below my target"; Rosengren: "continued disinflation". Upside voices (Plosser: prices paid highest since Aug 2008, "upside risk to inflation"; Lacker; Hoenig: raw materials rising) are a minority and mostly about the medium term.

**OUTPUT_GAP = 30 +-5 (High).** Kohn: "output is still below potential" at end-2012; Yellen: "operating far below maximum sustainable employment", "climbing out of a very deep hole"; Evans: "tremendous amount of slack". Recovery picking up, but gap seen as large for years. Below 50 as required.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS = 20 +-5 (High).** Yellen: unemployment about 9 1/4 percent at end-2010, labor markets "remain depressed"; Tarullo: "a lot of slack in virtually every segment of our labor market", "barely begun to have net job growth"; Evans: "huge amount of slack in labor markets". Early signs of improvement (callbacks, temp help) do not change the level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT = 47 +-7 (Moderate).** Yellen: consumer spending and equipment and software investment "rebounded more quickly than I had anticipated", Tech Pulse "blistering 35 percent"; Lacker: pent-up demand; Warsh: consumption "better than okay". Offsetting: Kohn: consumption "harder to see... providing continuing impetus", saving rate low, "Income growth remains quite sluggish"; Lockhart: strength "won't be sustained"; Plosser: nonresidential construction "in the tank"; Tarullo/Bernanke: sustainability doubtful. Net: a moderate rebound from a low base.

**PRODUCTIVITY = 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Pianalto: "high levels of productivity growth we saw in the later part of last year", expects "a notable deceleration"; Tarullo: "unexpectedly high leap in productivity during the depths of the recession"; Lockhart: "a little more productivity growth" than the Greenbook. Current gains above trend, expected to fade.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = 46 +-6 (Moderate).** Bernanke: "Financial markets generally continue to improve, supportive of recovery... Financial conditions overall are close to neutral"; Plosser: "Conditions in financial markets continue to support recovery"; Yellen: conditions "no longer a sizable drag" but "not an impetus for robust growth", "loans remain hard to get and expensive" for bank-dependent firms. Greece stress is a new tightening risk. Near neutral, slightly supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS = N/A.** No participant describes Treasury-market functioning. Kohn and Warsh mention safe-haven demand and fiscal-risk premiums, and the rest is about balance-sheet redemptions, not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS = 53 +-8 (Moderate).** Yellen: corporate spreads "narrowed to levels seen after the much more modest 2001 recession" (a stated level, not tight); bank loan spreads "about 50 basis points above normal"; Bernanke: "bond spreads down", but "risk premiums and spreads are not excessively thin"; Duke: "Spreads are declining" in auto and ABS, "credit is quite scarce in the lower end". Bond spreads near normal and narrowing, bank-loan pricing and availability tight for small firms. Slightly above 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION = 50 +-5 (Moderate).** Yellen: stock market "up more than 40 percent", S&P P/E "only about 20, below its longer-run average"; Kohn: "not clear that asset prices are out of line with fundamentals"; Bernanke: financial conditions "not excessively easy". Valuation view roughly fair.

**BANKING_STRESS = 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Duke: "it is really springtime in the banking business", earnings above expectations; Dudley: US banks "have raised a lot of capital" but rating-downgrade risk to four large firms; Lockhart: "wave of small bank failures will persist through 2010"; Warsh: European banking "very tenuous"; Bernanke: CRE "an important concern for smaller banks". Mixed signals, system seen as sound with pockets of stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH = 52 +-6 (Low).** Dudley: "trade weighted value of the dollar hasn't really actually moved much"; Kohn: "decline in the euro", "enhance the demand for dollars"; Hoenig: "perhaps a decline in the dollar". Broadly stable with mild safe-haven strength.

**INFLATION_GOODS = 52.** Plosser: prices paid "highest level since August 2008"; Hoenig: "raw material prices have continued to rise"; Lacker: furniture suppliers worried about "upward pressure on prices"; Pianalto: producers "hold prices steady, even when commodity prices are picking up". Mild upward goods and commodity pressure, limited pass-through.
**INFLATION_SERVICES = N/A.** Not discussed as a category (rents are scored under housing).
**INFLATION_HOUSING = 30.** Yellen: "weakness in housing costs" accounts for about half a point of the core decline; Bullard: rental markets "distorted"; Bernanke: "distortions in the housing market and rents may be masking inflation trends". Soft shelter inflation.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS = 52.** Yellen: "inflation expectations remain well anchored"; Lockhart: "well anchored for now"; Kohn: "Anchored inflation expectations are all that is keeping inflation from dropping much further"; Kocherlakota: 10-year, 10-year breakeven "close to 3 percent", up 65 bp since September; Bullard: expects expectations to "edge higher". Anchored overall with some upward drift in long-run measures.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN = 42 +-6 (High).** Hold at 0-1/4 with "extended period" retained (alternative B, Treasury redemptions deferred). Dovish-hold majority: Yellen "no case for tightening", Kohn "we won't lose anything by waiting", Dudley "favor doing as little as possible", Bernanke "maintain the low policy rate for now". Hawkish camp: Hoenig (raise toward 1 percent by September), Plosser ("time for taking initial steps to normalize"), Lacker ("raise rates... later this year"), Bullard and Kocherlakota (balance-sheet shrinkage, drop language). Balance-sheet debate on asset sales is exit-oriented. Overall a hold with mild dovish tilt.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY = 40 +-7 (High).** One formal dissent (Hoenig, preferred alternative C and removing "extended period"). Non-voters Plosser and Lacker also argue for earlier tightening, and Bullard proposed many wording changes. Real differences on sequencing and communication of asset sales (Kocherlakota: majority view "not a consensus view"), but all other voters supported B. Moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS = 60 +-8 (Moderate).** Dudley: Greece "keeps getting so much worse", dollar funding, "credit ratings of major U.S. banks"; Warsh: "banking situation in Europe is very tenuous"; Rosengren: need "structured discussion of tails", paths of contagion; Hoenig: LFI briefings, "lender of last resort"; Bernanke: financial stability issues to be incorporated more explicitly; Kohn and Warsh on asset-price distortions. Prominent alongside the balance-sheet topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE = 30 +-7 (Moderate).** Statement keeps "extended period" (Dudley "keeping the 'extended period' language in"; Bernanke "not in favor of dropping"; Yellen "comfortable with the 'extended period' language"). Hawkish tilt only from the minutes-based message that sales are coming (Bernanke: "substantial consensus... sales are needed") and from Bernanke's remark that removing the language "will be de facto a tightening". Easing-lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED = 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Dudley: Greece "still getting worse... very fluid"; Kohn: "a huge amount of uncertainty about what the effect of this announcement would be"; Pianalto: "I am very uncertain about what economic and financial conditions will prevail"; Lockhart: "new territory... a lot is not known"; Warsh: "nonlinear outcomes"; Rosengren: "tails of the distribution"; Yellen: "I am not that confident in this forecast". Elevated, driven by Europe and unprecedented balance-sheet tools, while many express growing confidence in the recovery.
