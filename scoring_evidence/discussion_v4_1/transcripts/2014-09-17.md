# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2014-09-17 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It contains: (a) a joint session with Q&A on financial developments and the overnight RRP testing program, plus the discussion and show-of-hands on the normalization principles (one participant, Lacker, did not support them); (b) the economic go-around; (c) the Chair's summary; (d) the policy round on "alternative B", including the "considerable time" forward-guidance language; (e) the vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Staff-reported content (Tealbook figures, desk survey) is ignored except where members adopt it as their own view.

**2. Era context.** The text is a late-QE, pre-liftoff period: asset purchases are about to end (October), unemployment is 6.1%, PCE inflation about 1.5%, and liftoff is expected sometime in 2015. This backdrop is not used to override what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Yellen, Vice Chair Dudley, Governors Fischer, Tarullo, Powell, Brainard, and Presidents Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Plosser, Fisher, Lacker, Lockhart, Evans, Bullard, Mester, George, Williams.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2% target. Evidence: Evans "core PCE about 1½ percent"; Yellen "shortfall from our 2 percent objective"; Rosengren "persistent undershooting"; Brainard "no imminent threat of a surge". Fisher cites Texas CPI of 2.5% and Powell expects a faster return, but both are regional or forward-looking. The consensus is below target with downside risks diminished. Score is in the 26-40 band.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as below potential. Yellen "significant underutilization" and unemployment of 6.1% against a natural rate near 5.2%; Brainard, Tarullo, Powell and Rosengren say slack remains. Bullard says growth is "considerably in excess of potential" but still reads labour slack as shrinking, and Fisher thinks Texas is "full steam". The gap is narrowing but still negative. Score is below 50 (bright-line satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as below full employment but improving. UE is 6.1% and slack is debated, with Yellen, Rosengren, Brainard, Tarullo and Powell arguing for more slack and Fisher, Bullard and Plosser for less. There are regional shortage reports (Fisher, Lacker). The level puts it in the 31-45 band, upper end.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants describe a moderate, solid pace. Yellen "moderate growth"; Evans "reasonably solid growth of 3 percent"; George forward indicators strong; Lockhart "fairly lackluster consumer activity"; Dudley worries about consumption. Net is at trend, a touch above.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Lacker ("productivity growth ... around 1 percent ... since the recession") and Fischer (MFP at ¾ percent, rate "declined since 2006"). This is well below the historical trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as easy and supportive. Yellen "spread compression that is worrisome"; Fisher "financial market excess", "promiscuous, low credit standards"; Powell "high degree of froth"; Dudley refers to the risk that conditions could "tighten unduly". Rosengren notes credit remains scarce for low-FICO borrowers, a small offset. Score is below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield and term-premium chatter and RRP operational design. No dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Fisher says junk "spreads ... indicate very promiscuous, low credit standards"; Yellen summarises "spread compression that is worrisome". Stated level is compressed, so the score is below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher "Stocks are richly priced", "19 times trailing earnings" versus 15.5 average, "pros are approaching the door"; Yellen "possibly stretched valuations". The valuation concern is clear. This sits in the 16-30 band.
- BANKING_STRESS: Not a theme. Lending and banker reports only: Kocherlakota's contacts saw no irresponsible lending; Plosser says loan growth is organic; Mester finds the Shared National Credits exam "somewhat sobering" on leveraged lending. No impairment is described. Low stress, thin evidence, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dudley "the dollar has strengthened"; Fischer "strengthened sharply in the past year"; Brainard "fairly large, sharp appreciation ... could worsen". Bright-line says above 50, and the language is notable strength.

**5. Polarity check.** INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure, and the evidence says below target, so below 50 is correct. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress, and the evidence says compressed spreads, so below 50 is correct. EQUITY_VALUATION: in the rubric the low end means overvaluation concern, and the evidence is a richly priced market, so the score is low. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciation maps to above 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds policy and keeps "considerable time", ends QE next meeting, and sets out normalization principles. Fifteen of 17 vote or lean for alt B. Hawkish pressure comes from Fisher and Plosser (dissent) and from Lacker, Bullard, Mester and George, who want "considerable time" dropped. Dovish counterweights come from Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Lockhart, Williams, Brainard and Dudley ("go as late as possible"). Net is lean dovish to balanced.
- Disagreement: Two formal dissents (Fisher and Plosser, both hawkish), one participant not supporting the normalization principles (Lacker), and open camps on the forward-guidance language and on slack. Exchanges are cordial.
- Financial stability attention: Fisher's lengthy valuation, M&A and junk-bond passage; Mester and Brainard on leveraged lending and "low for long"; Powell on froth; Bullard on bubbles; Tarullo on RRP size and financial stability. Several participants address it meaningfully, but it is not the dominant topic.
- Forward guidance: "Considerable time" is retained, the SEP shows an earlier and steeper path, and the Chair signals the language will change in October. There is a patient, accommodative signal with normalization on the horizon.
- Uncertainty: Fischer "massive" uncertainty about 2017 rates; Dudley "so much uncertainty about 2016 and 2017"; Tarullo "no one can really say for sure" about slack; Rosengren proposes distributions. Elevated, but largely standard 2014 caveats and not a dominant theme.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential gives < 50 (OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative gives < 50 (OK). CREDIT_SPREADS compressed gives < 50 (OK). BANKING_STRESS healthy-ish gives < 50 (OK). DOLLAR_STRENGTH appreciation gives > 50 (OK). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (41) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): within 15. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52): 11 points, OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (26): co-move. FC vs TREASURY: N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE (33) vs sub-components: goods 36 (falling energy and food prices, subdued import prices), expectations 47 (well anchored near 2%, with some concern about drifting down). Consistent, since the parent is anchored on realized inflation below 2%.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-function discussion beyond RRP mechanics and yields. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-price discussion. INFLATION_HOUSING: house-price and rent inflation not specifically discussed as inflation (housing discussed as activity and prices only in passing).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans "Core PCE inflation is running about 1½ percent"; Brainard "no imminent threat of a surge in inflation"; Rosengren "persistent undershooting of the inflation target".
- Summary: Inflation is clearly below 2%, with only gradual expected firming.
- Direction: Below neutral because members report 1.5% PCE inflation and subdued wages.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 41 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen "I remain comfortable describing underutilization as 'significant'"; Williams "the degree of slack ... remains significant".
- Summary: A negative but narrowing gap; some see growth above potential closing it.
- Direction: Below potential because slack is described as significant and remaining.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen "At 6.1 percent, the unemployment rate is significantly above my own estimate of its longer-run normal level"; Fisher "we're running at full steam" in Texas.
- Summary: Meaningful improvement, but the aggregate market is loose; regional and skill-specific tightness is noted.
- Direction: Below neutral because unemployment is 6.1% against roughly 5.2-5.5% natural rate estimates.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans "reasonably solid growth of 3 percent or so"; Lockhart "fairly lackluster consumer activity"; George "robust growth in business investment" expected.
- Summary: Solid, near-trend spending with investment improving and consumption mixed.
- Direction: At or slightly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 32 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker "productivity growth continuing to average around 1 percent"; Fischer "rate of productivity growth has declined since 2006".
- Summary: Productivity is seen as well below the historical trend.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher "financial market excess"; Powell "a high degree of froth in financial markets".
- Summary: Conditions are easy and supportive of risk-taking, with only pockets of tightness (low-FICO credit).
- Direction: Below neutral (looser), per the accommodative bright-line.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No dysfunction discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 26 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen "spread compression that is worrisome"; Fisher "rates, both nominal rates and spreads, that indicate ... very promiscuous, low credit standards".
- Summary: Spreads are tight and covenant quality is eroding.
- Direction: Below neutral on the stated level (compressed).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 25 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher "Stocks are richly priced ... S&P is trading at 19 times trailing 12-months' earnings"; Yellen "possibly stretched valuations".
- Summary: A clear valuation concern, though not alarm, and Fisher expects the Committee to "not panic" at a correction.
- Direction: Valuations above fair value, hence the low end of the scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota "Banks reported ... relatively few loans as being creditworthy"; Mester "found the report on the 2014 Shared National Credits exam somewhat sobering".
- Summary: No impairment, with minor leveraged-lending concerns.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress), with thin evidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 68 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard "fairly large, sharp appreciation in the dollar exchange rate"; Fischer "dollar, which has strengthened sharply in the past year".
- Summary: Notable appreciation, seen as a headwind to growth and inflation.
- Direction: Above neutral (appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36.** Evidence: Brainard "subdued import prices and a drop in energy prices"; Fisher food prices "at their lowest prices in four years". Falling energy and food prices.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Evidence: Mester "inflation expectations being well anchored at our target"; Williams "inflation expectations are well anchored at 2 percent"; Kocherlakota notes an underlying rate of 1.75% and Williams the euro-area drift lower. Anchored, with a slight downward tilt.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 47 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren "current conditions require a patient monetary policy"; Evans "extraordinarily patient"; Bullard "a rising risk that we will be too late, too slow"; Plosser dissent on forward guidance.
- Summary: A hold with accommodative language and QE ending, a clear patient majority, and a vocal hawkish minority including two dissents.
- Direction: Lean dovish to balanced, consistent with the pre-liftoff anchor.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 55 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher "I will not support alternative B"; Plosser "Absent substantive changes like that, I must dissent"; the vote read-out records two No votes; Lacker "I can't support this version" on the principles.
- Summary: Two formal dissents in one direction, one participant opposing the normalization principles, and open camps on slack and on the forward-guidance language, delivered civilly.
- Direction: Above the broad-agreement zone, in the notable-disagreement band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher "we are pushing the extremes of financial instability"; Brainard "important to keep a very vigilant eye there".
- Summary: Several participants address valuations, leveraged lending and "low for long" incentives meaningfully, but most treat them as a risk to monitor rather than a policy driver.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 36 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams "keeping the language in alternative B"; Yellen "willing to ... leave 'significant'" and "October as a real meeting at which significant changes are possible"; Kocherlakota "communicate a continued clear commitment to being patient".
- Summary: "Considerable time" is retained and patience emphasised, while the principles, the earlier and steeper SEP path, and the pledge to revisit the language in October tilt toward normalization.
- Direction: Easing-lean with data dependence, with a normalization overtone.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fischer "the uncertainty about what that rate will be is massive"; Dudley "I have so much uncertainty about 2016 and 2017"; Tarullo "how much does remain no one can really say for sure".
- Summary: Several participants stress uncertainty about slack and the rate path, but the outlook is otherwise described with reasonable conviction.
- Direction: Somewhat to moderately elevated.
