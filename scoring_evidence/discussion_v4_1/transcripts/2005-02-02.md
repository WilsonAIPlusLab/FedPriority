## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

INFLATION_PRESSURE 47 +-6 Moderate. Ms. Minehan: "core PCE has leveled off at 1½ percent or so"; Ms. Yellen: "CPI and the ECI, have been favorable ... core PCE ... about 1½ percent ... very reasonable"; Mr. Stern: "no detectable acceleration of inflation". Upside risks stressed by Mr. Hoenig ("further increases in core inflation"), Ms. Holcomb ("most of the inflation risks are on the upside"), Ms. Pianalto ("headline inflation was higher than ... acceptable"), but current level is low and contained.
OUTPUT_GAP 46 +-6 Moderate. Mr. Moskow: "expand at a rate somewhat above potential"; Ms. Yellen: "growth only moderately above potential with a very gradual diminution of the remaining labor market slack"; Mr. Kohn: "appreciable slack persisting"; Mr. Lacker: "output gap gradually closes"; Mr. Ferguson: "growth at about potential". Growth rate above potential, level still slightly below.
LABOR_TIGHTNESS 45 +-6 Moderate. Mr. Stern: "labor availability to be ample"; Mr. Poole: "concern about labor availability is not general; it's spotty"; Mr. Hoenig: labor shortages "53 percent in January"; Ms. Minehan: NE unemployment "4.3 percent"; Mr. Guynn: "unemployment rate, at 4.9 percent". Mixed, near-NAIRU with slack said to remain.
CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60 +-6 Moderate. Ms. Yellen: "Consumer spending remains strong"; Mr. Hoenig: "final sales to domestic purchasers ... robust 4.3 percent pace"; Mr. Bernanke: "domestic final demand continued strong"; Mr. Guynn: "solid investment spending to complement sustained consumer spending". Mr. Santomero "moderate pace" tempers it.
PRODUCTIVITY 56 +-8 Moderate. Mr. Bernanke: "productivity growth has surprised repeatedly on the upside for almost a decade", staff projects slowing to 1.7%; Mr. Hoenig: "slowing productivity growth ... is on the horizon". Strong but expected to slow.
FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 25 +-6 High. Ms. Minehan: "Financial markets, as well, are very accommodative ... narrowing credit spreads and abundant liquidity"; Mr. Kohn: "relatively stimulative financial conditions"; Vice Chairman Geithner: "very low risk premia".
TREASURY_STRESS 40 +-8 Low. Mr. Lacker: flattening yield curve, "the 10-year rate barely moved" read as policy credibility; no dysfunction described. Orderly functioning; only the yield/term-structure is discussed.
CREDIT_SPREADS 18 +-6 High. Ms. Minehan: "narrowing credit spreads ... low risk premiums"; Vice Chairman Geithner: "low credit spreads—low risk premia"; Chairman Greenspan: "yield spreads fall to exceptionally low levels"; Ms. Bies: spreads "really come back down again". Level stated low.
EQUITY_VALUATION 48 +-8 Low. Mr. Kohn: "basically sideways movement of equity prices since late last year"; Ms. Minehan: "some bounciness in equity markets". Price moves only, no valuation view.
BANKING_STRESS 32 +-8 Moderate. Ms. Bies: "bankers are very positive about current credit quality" though "sweet spot in that credit cycle"; Mr. Olson: asset quality "has peaked", but data show no risk to forecast. Healthy, with a cyclical caution.
DOLLAR_STRENGTH 27 +-6 High. Ms. Yellen: "weaker dollar continues to benefit District exporters"; Ms. Minehan: "dollar is down 27 percent since the peak in 2002"; Mr. Hoenig: "continued dollar depreciation is strong"; Mr. Santomero: "large depreciation in the dollar".
INFLATION_GOODS 55. Ms. Holcomb: "goods prices have stopped falling and are now on the rise"; Ms. Pianalto: "high input prices ... raw materials".
INFLATION_SERVICES N/A. Not discussed as a category.
INFLATION_HOUSING N/A. House prices discussed only as asset prices / wealth channel, not shelter inflation.
INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 48. Mr. Stern: "inflationary expectations remain essentially very well anchored"; Mr. Lacker: "inflation expectations appear to be contained"; Ms. Pianalto: "without deterioration in the private sector's inflation expectations".
HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 63 +-5 High. Chairman Greenspan: "another 25 basis points on the funds rate"; all vote yes; Mr. Moskow: "we will probably have to raise rates this year to levels that are higher than those expected by futures markets"; Mr. Hoenig: "raising rates more aggressively toward neutral"; Ms. Yellen: "relatively soon we will need to begin to slow this process down"; Mr. Poole wants symmetric risks. Continuing measured tightening, some want faster, a few see a pause approaching.
DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 +-8 Moderate. Vote unanimous (12 yes, no dissent), policy statements nearly unchanged; minor pace emphasis differences (Hoenig, Moskow faster; Yellen slower). Day-one inflation-objective debate had Chairman Greenspan say "literally split 50-50" but it was not a policy vote and remained cordial.
FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 45 +-8 Moderate. Ms. Minehan: "increased risk of financial fragility"; Vice Chairman Geithner: "very low risk premia ... unwelcome shock to asset prices"; Chairman Greenspan: "when you get risk premiums down at very low levels, they can only go in one direction"; Ms. Bies and Mr. Olson on credit quality; asset prices and financial stability also raised on day one.
FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 65 +-6 Moderate. Mr. Guynn: "Markets are expecting further moves"; Ms. Holcomb: "expectations of continued tightening over the year"; Vice Chairman Geithner: signal "neutral to the expectations now prevailing"; Mr. Ferguson: "gradual removal of policy accommodation, but review it ... at each meeting". Measured-pace removal of accommodation continues, statement essentially unchanged.
UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 38 +-8 Moderate. Chairman Greenspan: forecast "has built in to it the seeds of its own imbalances, and we don't know where they are"; Vice Chairman Geithner: forecast "looks implausibly benign"; Mr. Poole: GDP "confidence interval of 2.2 to 5.5 percent". Yet most members are confident ("balanced" risks, "somewhat more confidence").
