# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2011-03-15

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around (Lockhart through Raskin), the Chair's summary and remarks, the policy go-around on statement alternatives A/B/C, a short communications (press conference/blackout) discussion, and a vote read-out (unanimous Yes for alternative B with edits). No staff content is scored.

**2. Era context.** Early 2011, with the second round of asset purchases (the $600 billion program) due to finish in June. The discussion itself carries the backdrop: unemployment 8.9% after a sharp fall, core inflation below 1%, rising oil and commodity prices (Middle East and North Africa unrest), the Japan earthquake and tsunami, the euro-area sovereign debt problem. I scored what members said, not what I know of the period.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish / patient camp: Rosengren, Evans, Williams, Yellen, Raskin, Duke, Dudley, Tarullo, Pianalto (with inflation caveats), Lockhart (B, "stay the course"), Bernanke ("not the time to be shocking markets").
- Hawkish / exit-oriented camp: Plosser (opposes more purchases, wants "extended period" language dropped, exit well before year-end), Hoenig (wants rate increase signalled soon, end purchases early, shrink balance sheet), Fisher (hawkish, but can live with B), Lacker ("irresistible attraction to alternative C", action needed this year), Bullard (prepare to remove accommodation, taper QE2), Kocherlakota (exit planning, slack measures will not justify accommodation by year-end).
- The staff Q&A, the statement wordsmithing and the press-conference discussion are procedural. I ignored them except where members expressed views on conditions or policy.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Members see headline inflation pushed up by energy, food and commodity costs, with firms trying to pass costs through (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Hoenig). Core is described as low and "well below 2 percent" (Rosengren, Evans, Pianalto, Williams, Yellen, Lacker 0.8% y/y core, 1% three-month). Upside risks are growing. Net: somewhat below the 50 midpoint on the core level, with a rising upward tilt, so about 42.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Many members say "significant slack in labor markets" (Rosengren), "substantial slack in labor and most product markets" (Evans), "falling well short on both parts of our dual mandate" (Williams). Growth is above trend (Plosser, Bullard), but the level of output is below potential. Below 50: 34.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 8.9% falling, but payroll gains modest, hiring weak, wage pressure "low except possibly in a few narrow fields" (Bernanke). Hoenig mentions some skilled shortages. Far below full employment: 26.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong auto sales and manufacturing, equipment and software strong, business sentiment improved (Evans, Plosser, Lacker, Yellen). Offsets: gasoline prices drain spending power and confidence (Yellen, Raskin, Williams, Duke), housing a drag, hiring and capex cautious (Lockhart). Net roughly at trend: 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Credit availability improving (autos, student loans, C&I; Duke, Yellen, Bernanke). Fisher sees "cheap money and readily available money", covenant-lite loans, leveraged buyout dividends. Hoenig says low rates are inflating asset values. Housing credit still mired (Duke). Conditions are accommodative/supportive, so below 50: 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No member describes Treasury market dysfunction; the only Treasury-market remarks are about the tapering of purchases. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Hoenig: "high-yield market... where yields are at historical lows". Fisher: covenant-lite issuance exceeding 2006, PIK toggles. Duke: C&I spreads coming down for larger loans, CMBS terms relaxing "not yet alarming", auto ABS back to normal. Levels are below normal, so below 50: 35.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher cites a rally that is "the sixth richest on record" and retail inflows. Yellen notes the stock market has recovered. Kocherlakota's equity premium question (staff Q&A) is not a valuation view. No strong valuation judgment; rich-side tilt, so slightly below 50: 42, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: US bankers report improving credit quality, falling delinquencies and charge-offs (Duke); US institutions have reduced exposure to peripheral Europe (Tarullo). Concerns: European bank stress tests and exposures (Dudley, Tarullo), foreclosure/servicing issues (Bernanke), weak loan demand, residential lending "mired". Healthy with pockets of concern: 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only brief remarks. Bernanke: "the decline in the dollar is not remotely enough to explain" commodity prices. Bullard and Bernanke: less flight-to-quality dollar behavior than expected during MENA. The dollar is described as having declined / not rallied, so below 50: 40, low confidence.

**5. Polarity check.** Higher = more of the named construct on every line. OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS: slack means low. CREDIT_SPREADS: compressed means low. BANKING_STRESS: improving credit quality means below 50. Consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The decision is to hold at 0-0.25%, keep "extended period" language, and complete the $600 billion program; no taper (Bullard alone wants tapering). Seven or so members are firmly patient/dovish, while about six push for earlier exit, language change or hawkish tilt (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker, Fisher, Bullard, Kocherlakota). No new easing is proposed (QE2 is winding down), but Yellen says "prepared to provide additional stimulus should downside risks materialize". Net: hold with accommodative stance and a minority tightening bias, slightly dovish side of balance: 42.
- Dissent: Zero formal dissents; the roll call is unanimous. But there are clearly visible camps (Plosser/Hoenig/Fisher/Lacker/Bullard versus the patient group), Hoenig says "I know I am outside this Committee's boundaries", Lacker feels "an irresistible attraction to alternative C", Fisher "can live with alternative B", Plosser and Hoenig disagree about the assessment of slack and language. Disagreement extends to inflation assessment, not just tactics. Notable differences but no dissent: 36.
- Financial stability attention: Fisher devotes a long section to imprudent risk-taking (covenant-lite, PE dividends, leverage, commodity futures, subprime auto); Hoenig on farmland and asset values and policy's role; Rosengren on farmland supervision; Dudley and Tarullo on euro-area banks and sovereigns; Duke on credit; the Chair's summary lists financial-stability risks. Several members, not dominant: 50.
- Forward guidance: The "extended period" language is retained; the statement's new line says commodity effects are "transitory" and the Committee "will pay close attention" to inflation and expectations. Chair promises clarity on purchases and exit strategy in April. Plosser, Hoenig and Lacker want to move; Bullard wants prep. The signal is still accommodative low-for-long, with exit preparation beginning. Tone: 38.
- Uncertainty: Dudley ("high level of uncertainty and the likelihood that the tails are fat", "very hard to be confident"), Tarullo ("more in the realm of uncertainty than of calculable risk"), Yellen (risks have "increased considerably", labor market "significant mystery"), Kocherlakota (labor force participation), Duke, Japan and the Middle East. Elevated, multiple participants emphasize it: 60.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (below potential: satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (supportive/easy: satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 (low high-yield yields, easing terms: satisfied). BANKING_STRESS < 50 (improving credit quality: satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH < 50 (decline/no appreciation: satisfied). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs LABOR 26: 8 apart, fine. OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47: 13 apart, within 15; spending is at trend growth while the level of activity is below potential. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 35 co-move. Treasury N/A, consistent with normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 42 vs sub-components: goods 63 (commodity pass-through) and expectations 57 sit above the parent, while core services/wages are soft and not scored; the parent weights low core (the stated focus of most members) against the rising headline.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. TREASURY_STRESS: no member discussed Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_SERVICES: no explicit services-price discussion (wages soft, only mentioned as labor cost). INFLATION_HOUSING: no discussion of rents or shelter inflation (house prices and multifamily rents only in the context of balance sheets and credit).

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "core measures remain well below 2 percent and... significant slack." Lacker: "Year-over-year core PCE is up just 0.8 percent... Three-month core inflation... is now 1 percent." Plosser: "I see inflation risks as clearly to the upside."
- Summary: Core is low but firming, headline is rising, risks are tilting up.
- Direction: Below neutral because most members describe underlying inflation as well below 2 percent, offset by rising headline and pass-through attempts.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "there still is substantial slack in labor and most product markets." Williams: "We're still falling well short on both parts of our dual mandate."
- Summary: Recovery strengthening but the level of activity remains well below potential.
- Direction: Below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 26 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "there is an awfully long road to travel to attain our maximum employment objective." Evans: "I don't think we're looking yet at a vibrant labor market with robust hiring." Hoenig: "in some of the high-skilled areas, we're seeing some indications of shortages... a lot of slack there as well."
- Summary: Unemployment still near 9%, hiring weak, wages restrained.
- Direction: Below neutral because of high unemployment and slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "Household spending continues to show... decent momentum... equipment and software continues to show strength." Yellen: "Consumers are hardly ebullient," with gasoline prices a threat to spending.
- Summary: Solid business investment and autos offset cautious consumers and the housing drag.
- Direction: Roughly at trend; slightly below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "the intoxicating ambrosia of cheap money and readily available money." Duke: "access to financing... improved" (autos), though "quite uneven across product types and markets."
- Summary: Conditions are supportive and loosening, with residential and small-business credit still tight.
- Direction: Below neutral (accommodative) per bright-line.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "the high-yield market... where yields are at historical lows." Duke: "Interest rate spreads on C&I loans are coming down for larger loans... relaxation of terms" on CMBS.
- Summary: Spread levels are below normal for corporate credit, coming in further.
- Direction: Below neutral because levels are compressed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "The current rally... is the sixth richest on record, going back to 1932." Yellen: "The stock market has recovered."
- Summary: Rally noted with froth worries from one member; no broad valuation view.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (richer rather than depressed).

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Duke: "continued gradual improvement in credit quality... delinquency... declining in typical cyclical fashion." Dudley on Europe: "the euro sovereign debt/banking crisis isn't over by a long shot."
- Summary: US banks improving, weak loan demand and European and housing-related pockets of concern.
- Direction: Below neutral because US banking credit quality is improving.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the decline in the dollar is not remotely enough to explain the movement in commodity prices." Bullard: "We did not see the flight-to-safety effect as strongly as I would have expected."
- Summary: Dollar is described as lower/not strengthening, only passing remarks.
- Direction: Below neutral per bright-line (depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 63 [value only]** Evidence: Fisher on processed food, apparel (cotton), steel up 14%; Lacker on textiles and aluminum price increases; Plosser on 57% of manufacturers having raised prices. Rising goods/commodity prices with pass-through under way.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57 [value only]** Evidence: Dudley: Michigan "highest level since August 2008"; Lockhart: TIPS "at the top end of the range we've been depicting as stable"; Bernanke: "not much evidence of pass-through... well within historical ranges." Anchored but drifting up.

**INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 42 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "continued accommodative monetary policy is necessary to support this modest recovery." Plosser: "I believe we will need to tighten policy considerably sooner than in the Tealbook." Yellen: remain "prepared to provide additional stimulus should downside risks... materialize."
- Summary: Majority holds steady and completes QE2; a sizable minority pushes for earlier exit.
- Direction: Below the midpoint, dovish-leaning balance, since no one proposes tightening now and the majority wants patience.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "I know I am outside this Committee's boundaries." Lacker: "I feel an irresistible attraction to alternative C, but I can support the status quo." Vote read-out: all ten voters Yes.
- Summary: No dissents, but distinct hawkish camp and open disagreement on inflation risk and slack.
- Direction: Moderate differences, no formal dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "increasingly imprudent risk-taking... covenant-lite loans." Hoenig: "we are seeing new leverage being introduced almost as we speak" and farmland values.
- Summary: Several members (Fisher, Hoenig, Rosengren, Dudley, Tarullo, Duke) address risk-taking, farmland, European banks; not the dominant topic.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Statement keeps "exceptionally low levels... for an extended period" (Lockhart quoting paragraph 4; Bullard: "I would not change the extended period language"). Bernanke: "by the April meeting, we should be providing fairly definitive guidance... about what we're going to do with our purchase program."
- Summary: Low-for-long guidance retained with exit planning beginning and a hawkish minority pressing to change it.
- Direction: Below neutral (easing lean with data dependence), tilted upward by exit preparation.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "the high level of uncertainty and the likelihood that the tails are fat." Tarullo: "We are more in the realm of uncertainty than of calculable risk."
- Summary: Oil, Japan, Europe, labor-force interpretation and fiscal paths all flagged as hard to bound, though baseline is described with cautious confidence.
- Direction: Elevated.
