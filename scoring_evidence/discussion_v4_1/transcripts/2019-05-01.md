# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript discussion, meeting 2019-05-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). The text covers a long-run SOMA-composition go-around (non-decisional), questions during the Desk and staff briefings, a financial-stability go-around, the economic go-round, a short discussion of a historical episode (1994-95), the policy go-round (all support alternative B), and a vote read-out (all ten voters Yes). Much of the first part is about balance-sheet design, which is largely not a statement of economic conditions; scoring rests mainly on the financial-stability go-around, the economic go-round, and the policy go-round.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2019. Participants describe Q1 GDP at 3.2 percent (flattered by inventories and net exports), unemployment 3.8 percent, core PCE inflation 1.6 percent, recovered financial markets after the Q4 sell-off, and a "patient" policy stance. Scored from what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Powell, Vice Chair Williams, Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles, Bowman, and Presidents Rosengren, Daly, Mester, Bostic, Harker, Barkin, Evans, Bullard, Kaplan, George, Kashkari. Groupings: Evans, Bullard, Daly, Harker, Kashkari, Brainard, Clarida focus on soft inflation and expectations (Bullard and Evans float eventual easing; Mester and Brainard float "opportunistic" inflation/reflation). Quarles, Rosengren, Bostic, Kaplan, Barkin lean toward no easing, with Quarles still expecting a hike "at some point". Kashkari argues there is still labor slack.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (below target). Core PCE 1.6 percent, "disappointing", underlying 1.8 percent, expectations soft. Offsets: many see the softness as idiosyncratic/transitory, trimmed mean at 2 percent, wages up (AHE 3.2 percent). Anchor 38-42 for 2013-19 below-target periods. Score 37.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see activity as modestly above potential. Clarida: "an output gap that is modestly positive"; Brainard: "rising somewhat above potential"; Rosengren: growth "above my estimate of potential"; Williams/George/Mester see growth moving to or near trend. Kashkari/Daly argue labor slack or lower u*, which cuts the other way. Bright line: above-potential language, so > 50. Score 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above the midpoint. Unemployment 3.8 percent, "labor markets remain very tight" (Rosengren), "well below natural rate" (Bowman), "extremely well" (Bowman), claims at 50-year low (Barkin). Dissenting view: Kashkari "still slack"; Daly/Bostic say u* is lower so gap is larger, which if anything supports tightness. UE < 4 percent so score should clearly exceed 55. Score 66.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Near trend, with a soft Q1 and an expected rebound. Consumption growth 1.2 percent in Q1, equipment flat (Quarles), business investment slowing; offset by robust March retail sales, strong sentiment, and expected PCE pickup. Net about 51.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Barely discussed (Clarida: Q1 productivity "will likely show a sharp pickup"; Quarles: productivity story depends on investment; Williams: low business formation tied to lower productivity growth). Mixed, no clear level. 50, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (looser). Brainard: "relatively accommodative conditions"; Quarles: "relatively easy financial conditions"; Powell: "more accommodative financial conditions"; Clarida: "rebound in financial conditions". Bright line: accommodative/easy means < 50. Score 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Treasury market functioning is not raised as a stress topic (SOMA composition and reserve-scarcity talk concern term premia and money-market rates, not dysfunction).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Below the midpoint on level. Quarles: leveraged-loan/high-yield spreads "ground down to post-crisis lows before widening dramatically last fall ... since then contracted again but remain solidly in the middle of their post-crisis ranges"; Evans: "competitive pressure on rates and terms in CRE and C&I", "good environment for borrowers"; Bowman: borrowing conditions "generally favorable"; Mester/Brainard: terms eroding, covenant-lite share. The widening and re-narrowing is direction only; the level is favorable/not stressed. Score 38 with moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed. Brainard: valuations "again in the elevated range", "overall valuation of the stock market has regained its previous peak", "asset valuations are already stretched"; Quarles: stock market "hitting record highs"; Williams pushes back: equity premium "not particularly low relative to history", "no real sign ... of elevated prices". Net modestly elevated. Score 38, moderate confidence, wide band.
- BANKING_STRESS: Below midpoint. Quarles: the largest banks "are in a position to weather" a business downturn given annual stress tests; George: "bank capital is strong, at least relative to pre-crisis levels"; Brainard: CLO risk but banks sound. Concerns: Rosengren on CLO exposure and European banks "not showing great resilience", smaller banks' margins. US system described as sound. Score 35.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A (a grep of the text found no mention of the dollar or exchange rate).

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter; evidence says looser; score 36 matches. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress; evidence says favorable/mid post-crisis range; 38 matches. BANKING_STRESS higher = more stress; evidence says sound; 35 matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: scale reads low for overvaluation concern; participants split between elevated (Brainard) and fair (Williams); 38 matches modestly elevated.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: all hold at alternative B. Overall, the center is a patient hold. Dovish pulls (Bullard: "now may be a good time to think about a slightly lower setting", may move to alternative A; Evans; Harker; Kashkari "no need to raise"; opportunistic inflation from Mester and Brainard) outweigh hawkish pulls (Quarles "likely that we will want to hike rates a bit further at some point"; Rosengren "important that we not encourage expectations that we are likely to ease"; Bostic; Kaplan; Barkin wanting no forward guidance; Brainard noting a case for "modest further increases" if risks stay low). Net slightly dovish-leaning hold, 46.
- Disagreement: zero dissents, unanimous vote, Chair: "good consensus centered on our policy". Real differences exist on whether low inflation justifies easing, on u*/labor slack (Kashkari vs Evans exchange), and on SOMA composition (short vs proportional portfolio), but these are about tactics and framing rather than the vote. Broad agreement with notable emphasis differences, 22.
- Financial stability: a dedicated financial-stability go-around with long statements from Mester, Rosengren, Brainard, Barkin, Quarles, George, Williams (CLOs, leveraged loans, business debt, CCyB, valuations), plus reach-for-yield remarks in the policy round (Rosengren, Kaplan, Brainard) and a balance-sheet discussion that touches on financial stability. Several participants at length, not the dominant theme: 58.
- Forward guidance: "patient" retained; no direction, with several wanting to drop or modify the sentence in June (Barkin, Williams), Bullard flagging a possible move toward alternative A, Quarles expecting eventual hikes. Powell: "no strong case for moving in either direction". Neutral to slightly easing-leaning, 46.
- Uncertainty: Standard caveats plus specific uncertainty (u*, r*, Phillips curve "inflation puzzle", term-premium estimates "highly uncertain", Williams: "the outlook does remain cloudy", "uncharted waters" on QE tools). Most participants sound fairly confident about the baseline. Somewhat elevated, 36.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 (> 50, satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 (< 50, satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (level favorable, < 50, satisfied; widening in Q4 was direction only and reversed). BANKING_STRESS 35 (< 50, satisfied). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 66: 11 points apart, within 15 (labor tightness exceeds output gap because several see growth moving back toward trend and Kashkari/Daly stress remaining slack). OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 51: consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move. TREASURY_STRESS N/A, no conflict. INFLATION_PRESSURE 37 vs sub-components (goods 40, services 42, expectations 38): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market dysfunction discussed. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not mentioned. INFLATION_HOUSING: housing inflation/rents not discussed as price pressure (only house prices as an asset valuation matter). Goods and services sub-components are scored on thin but real evidence (apparel and commodity prices; portfolio-management, health-care and airfare prices).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Mester: "core PCE inflation has also softened to 1.6 percent in March"; Powell: "first-quarter inflation readings have disappointed, slipping below our 2 percent target."
- Center of gravity: Below-target, softer-than-expected inflation, with many calling it partly transitory.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is below neutral because core PCE is 1.6 percent and underlying inflation is 1.8 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "we face an inflation gap that is modestly negative and an output gap that is modestly positive"; Brainard: "Economic activity is rising somewhat above potential."
- Center of gravity: Modestly above potential, with growth expected to slow toward trend.
- Direction: Output gap is above neutral because members say growth and activity are above potential/trend.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 66 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "labor markets remain very tight"; Bowman: "the unemployment rate well below what the staff notes as the natural rate."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment 3.8 percent, below natural rate estimates, strong hiring; Kashkari's slack view is a minority.
- Direction: Labor tightness is above neutral because UE is below 4 percent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 51 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bowman: "Consumption growth dropped to 1.2 percent in the first quarter"; Brainard: "Retail sales posted a robust gain in March, and motor vehicle sales moved up sharply."
- Center of gravity: Soft Q1 spending and investment (equipment flat) offset by a strong March and expected rebound.
- Direction: Roughly at trend, neither above nor below, on a net basis.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "productivity growth in Q1, when it's released, will likely show a sharp pickup from the fourth quarter"; Quarles notes the productivity story depends on investment, which was weak.
- Center of gravity: Mentioned only in passing with mixed signals.
- Direction: At neutral because evidence is mixed and thin.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "financial conditions have rebounded, returning to the relatively accommodative conditions we saw before the fourth-quarter selloff"; Quarles: "relatively easy financial conditions, including a stock market that's hitting record highs."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are easy/supportive after the Q4 tightening reversed.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because members describe them as accommodative/easy.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "spreads have contracted again but remain solidly in the middle of their post-crisis ranges"; Evans: "banks in the District report ongoing competitive pressure on rates and terms in CRE and C&I. This is a good environment for borrowers."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are re-narrowed to a favorable, not stressed level, with eroding terms noted.
- Direction: Below neutral on level; the Q4 widening is direction only and has largely reversed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "The overall valuation of the stock market has regained its previous peak, and investors' appetite for risk is again quite high"; Williams: "there's no real sign, at least on the equity price premium, of elevated prices."
- Center of gravity: Modestly elevated valuations, with one prominent dissent from the stretched view.
- Direction: Below neutral on the scale (valuation concern) because Brainard and others see elevated valuations, partly offset by Williams.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "the core of the financial system, the largest banks and their broker-dealer subsidiaries, are in a position to weather the consequences should we see a business-sector downturn"; George: "assurances that bank capital is strong, at least relative to pre-crisis levels."
- Center of gravity: Banks are sound; concerns are about CLO and leveraged-loan exposures and foreign banks.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress) because the U.S. system is described as resilient.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A**

**Inflation sub-components.** INFLATION_GOODS 40 (apparel price declines, commodity prices "stabilized or turned down" per Evans, softer nonlabor costs per Mester, partly offset by tariffs). INFLATION_SERVICES 42 (declines in portfolio management, health care, airfares, partly offset by wage pressure). INFLATION_HOUSING N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 38 (Clarida: Michigan survey "tied for the lowest ever recorded"; Bullard: expectations "on the low side of where we'd like to see them"; Daly insists they remain anchored at 2 percent).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "now may be a good time to think about a slightly lower setting for the policy rate ... it's premature at this meeting"; Quarles: "it's likely that we will want to hike rates a bit further at some point".
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold with a patient stance, with dovish inflation concerns somewhat outweighing hawkish tilt.
- Direction: Slightly dovish-leaning balanced hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "there's a good consensus centered on our policy"; vote read-out shows every voter Yes. Kashkari to Evans exchange on whether cutting rates could lift expectations.
- Center of gravity: No dissents, with debate on emphasis and economics (u*, expectations) rather than the decision.
- Direction: Low disagreement, broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "I'd like to add some exclamation marks to the box ... on elevated business debt ... more than $2 trillion"; Brainard: "business debt ... is close to the highest level experienced over the past 20 years".
- Center of gravity: A dedicated go-around plus reach-for-yield comments in the policy round, but the meeting is not dominated by it.
- Direction: Moderate-to-prominent attention, regardless of direction.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 46 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "the incoming data strongly support our patient policy stance ... I see no strong case for moving in either direction"; Williams: wording could change in June, but "we want to keep ... the stance of policy where it is today."
- Center of gravity: Patient, data-dependent, no directional commitment, some lean toward accommodation.
- Direction: Neutral to slightly easing-leaning.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 36 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "the outlook does remain cloudy"; Daly: u* and r* estimates "are highly uncertain and hard to pin down ex ante."
- Center of gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty about inflation, u*/r*, and trade, but with fairly confident baselines.
- Direction: Above normal-caveat level, below genuinely high uncertainty.
