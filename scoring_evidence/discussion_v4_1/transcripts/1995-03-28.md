# v4.1 Member-Discussion Scoring: 1995-03-28 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** This is a member/participant transcript with staff turns removed. It opens with procedural business (minutes, FOIA delegation, security program, foreign currency authorizations and a Mexico/warehousing discussion). It then has members' questions to the foreign-desk, Greenbook and policy staff, an economic go-around, a short policy go-around on alternative B and symmetry/asymmetry, and a vote read-out (11 Yes). The staff answers are missing, so some members' questions are left without answers (for example, Blinder's "Okay. Fine."). I score only what members say.

**2. Era context.** Spring 1995, after a year of tightening that ended with a 50bp move in February (funds rate 6 percent, as members mention). Members are looking for a soft landing. The dollar has been falling and the Mexican crisis is under way. I let the text set the scores, not that backdrop.

**3. Speakers.**
- Slowdown acknowledged by nearly everyone, with a split over whether it is lasting. Downside-leaning: Blinder (downside risks "slightly higher", forecasts understate swings), Yellen (risks balanced, "not too hot, not too cold"), Parry (spending equations point to downside real-side risks, yet the inflation path is "unacceptable"), Stern (confident in the slowdown, expects an inventory correction).
- Upside-risk and hawkish voices: Kelley ("agnostic", risks on the up side), Broaddus (upside risk skew), Hoenig, Moskow, Forrestal (stronger growth, higher inflation than the Greenbook), Melzer (inflation worrisome, expectations at 4 percent), Minehan (wants to tighten "sooner rather than later"), McDonough (euphoria in markets; wants asymmetry as a signal), Jordan (risks tilt to more inflation, but he worries about output slowing too fast and cites weak money growth).
- Lindsey is a "scared agnostic" (fiscal drag, Japanese banks, German currency, debt ceiling).
- Greenspan: B (no change), mildly asymmetric toward tightening, with a worry about bubbles and euphoria.

**4. Directional signals (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. Core CPI has accelerated (Melzer: core about 4.2 percent in Jan-Feb versus 2.8 percent in 1994). Greenbook-type forecasts of 3.2-3.7 percent are cited approvingly by Moskow, Forrestal, McDonough, Hoenig and Parry, and the inflation path is called unacceptable (Parry). Offsets: wages and compensation are subdued (Boehne "nonproblem", Yellen, Philadelphia and Atlanta contacts) and goods price pass-through is limited (Forrestal). Net is clearly above target but not broad-based, so about 66.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint, modestly. Parry says the economy "will remain above levels of labor and capacity utilization that are consistent with steady inflation". Phillips says slack is "significantly diminished or perhaps the economy is operating over capacity". Jordan says "right at the ceiling". Growth is slowing toward trend and capacity utilization is cited at 83-85 percent. Bright line (above potential means >50) is satisfied: 57.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Tight, though the unemployment rate (5.4 percent, "below 5-1/2") is not extreme. Moskow: "labor markets remain very tight", "difficulties finding workers"; Stern: labor "in quite short supply"; Forrestal: labor shortages and signing bonuses; Melzer: 4.5 percent unemployment in parts of the District; Yellen and Phillips argue there may be more slack than the 5.4 percent rate suggests. A 5.4 percent rate anchors at about 50, and the strong shortage language pulls it up: 58.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net near trend, slightly below the midpoint once the slowdown in housing, autos and retail is weighed against strong business fixed investment and capacity additions. Housing, autos and retail sales are weakening (Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Yellen, McDonough), while business investment, backlogs and orders remain strong (Broaddus, Forrestal, Phillips, Greenspan). Net 50. The slowing is mostly from a very strong Q4, so it is not below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned only in passing (Phillips on cost control and capital spending; Jordan on productivity as an issue). No participant assesses productivity growth, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Looser than in the recent past but not described as "accommodative". Bond yields are down, stocks are strong, and the markets are "euphoric" (McDonough). Kelley: "I don't think we can call the real rates high by any reasonable standard". Yellen, Stern and the Philadelphia and Atlanta contacts point to higher rates having bitten the interest-sensitive sectors. Mixed, leaning somewhat easy: 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries are touched only via the Q&A on demand and via the large swing in the 2-to-10 year curve (Greenspan). That is yield movement, not dysfunction. I give a low-confidence normal-functioning score of 42 and flag the thin evidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not raised by any participant (only C&I loan flattening, bank loan growth). N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: McDonough ("stock market is very strong indeed", "euphoric on the top side"), Greenspan (downside risk from "significant increases in stock and bond prices", wealth effects "unsustainable and potentially creating bubbles"), Lindsey (the 1993 bond bubble). This is a valuation-concern view on the elevated side, so below 50 under this scale: 36.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only Lindsey (the foreign financial sector "in much more precarious shape ... than it is here", Japanese banks marking to market) and local merger remarks. US banks are implied sound and the stress is abroad: 38, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar is weak and falling, and this is discussed by many (Forrestal, Yellen, Blinder, Hoenig, Kelley, Melzer, McTeer, Phillips, Stern). Bright line (depreciation means <50) is satisfied: 22.
- Inflation sub-components. GOODS: raw-material/intermediate price pressure in paper, plastics, leather, steel (Minehan, Hoenig, Phillips); pass-through is limited (Forrestal). SERVICES and HOUSING: not discussed, N/A. EXPECTATIONS: mixed. Melzer cites a Michigan 5-10 year figure just over 4 percent and says few believe the Committee's commitment. Blinder and Broaddus cite surveys with no deterioration and bond yields lower. Net slightly elevated: 58.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher means more of each construct. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is low because the dollar is described as weak (depreciation, so <50). EQUITY_VALUATION is low because the concern is overvaluation, as the scale defines. BANKING_STRESS is low because US banks are not stressed (<50).

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: all 11 voters back no change (B). Ten of the 11 who spoke on the directive say they favor or accept asymmetry toward tightening or can live with it, and the Chairman leans "marginally, tentatively, unsurely" toward asymmetry. A hawkish minority wanted to tighten (Minehan prefers to tighten sooner, Parry's first preference is a small increase, Blinder notes that the implication of some views would be a large hike). No member argues for easing. Hold with a tightening bias gives 59.
- Disagreement: unanimous vote, no formal dissents. Differences are over symmetric versus asymmetric (Stern, Forrestal, Yellen, Blinder, Lindsey for symmetric; most others for asymmetric, several with only mild preferences) and over whether the slowdown is real (Blinder and Yellen versus Kelley and Broaddus). Lindsey remarks that "a few dissents" would be a better signal. Tactical differences in a unanimous vote: 27.
- Financial stability emphasis: The early foreign-currency/warehousing/Mexico discussion is procedural and I do not weight it much. In the go-around, bubbles, euphoria in stock and bond markets, the Japanese banks, a possible German currency problem, and the debt ceiling and the dollar are raised by McDonough, Greenspan, Lindsey, Melzer and a few others. That is limited-to-moderate attention, above a typical 1993-96 meeting, because markets and the dollar are an explicit policy consideration: 30.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric toward tightening, "remain open to further increases", with "wait and see" now. Mild tightening bias: 65.
- Uncertainty: Many say "agnostic" (Kelley, Lindsey, Melzer), call for "humility" (Boehne), say "very, very wide" confidence bands (Stern), and say they are uncertain where the economy is going (McDonough). Greenspan: "the variance is rising". Others are fairly confident (Yellen, Jordan, Forrestal). Elevated but not extreme: 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 57 (>50 for above-potential language) satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 (no "tight" language; the only "easy/accommodative"-type language is about real rates not being high) acceptable. CREDIT_SPREADS N/A. BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50) satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 22 (<50 for depreciation) satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS 42 (not >60) satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 57 versus LABOR_TIGHTNESS 58: close. OUTPUT_GAP 57 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50: a 7-point gap, within 15, reflecting the slowdown in spending alongside a still-high level of activity. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus CREDIT_SPREADS: credit spreads are N/A, so this check does not apply. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, so Treasury stress stays in the normal range. INFLATION_PRESSURE 66 versus sub-components (goods 58, expectations 58, others N/A): a modest gap, because the core CPI acceleration and the forecast of rising inflation are discussed at the aggregate level, not by component.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment by members. CREDIT_SPREADS: no member discusses spreads or credit availability. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as categories.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 66 +-6 (Moderate-High confidence reported as High)**
- Evidence: Melzer: "In January and February core inflation averaged about 4.2 percent, up substantially from the 2.8 percent rate we saw in 1994." Parry: "I find the path for inflation in the baseline forecast unacceptable."
- Summary: Core inflation has picked up and forecasts are for higher inflation in 1995-96, offset by subdued wages.
- Direction: above neutral, because members expect rising inflation and call the trajectory a concern.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 57 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Parry: "the economy most likely will remain above levels of labor and capacity utilization that are consistent with steady inflation." Phillips: "the economic slack currently is either significantly diminished or perhaps the economy is operating over capacity."
- Summary: Slowing toward trend but still modestly above potential.
- Direction: above neutral, per the bright-line rule.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "labor markets remain very tight ... difficulties finding workers." Stern: "labor remains in quite short supply in most of the District."
- Summary: Shortages are widely reported, but the 5.4 percent unemployment rate and subdued wages keep this moderate.
- Direction: above neutral, because the tightness language is broad.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "retail activity clearly has weakened"; same speaker: "continued strength of business fixed investment". Yellen: housing and autos "leading the way" down.
- Summary: Weakening consumer and housing spending balanced by strong business investment and capacity additions.
- Direction: at neutral, because the weakness and strength roughly offset.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not assessed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kelley: "Nominal rates all along the yield curve are well off their highs ... I don't think we can call the real rates high." McDonough: stock and bond markets "euphoric on the top side".
- Summary: Markets are buoyant and long rates have come down, though no member calls conditions accommodative.
- Direction: somewhat below neutral (looser than normal), based on the market commentary.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the largest swing in 2-year to 10-year maturities that I can recall in a long time ... the disappearance from the markets of an expectation of significant further tightening."
- Summary: Yield moves reflect policy expectations; no dysfunction described.
- Direction: at the normal-functioning level, below 50. Low confidence because only yield behavior is discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** Not raised.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 36 +-10 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McDonough: "The stock market is very strong indeed." Greenspan: wealth effects "unsustainable and potentially creating bubbles"; "the real danger is that things may get too good."
- Summary: Several members voice concern about stretched or bubble-prone valuations.
- Direction: below neutral on this scale (overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-12 (Low)**
- Evidence: Lindsey: "the financial sector is in much more precarious shape around the world than it is here ... problems with the Japanese banking industry."
- Summary: Banking concern is placed abroad, with the US sector implied sound.
- Direction: below neutral for the US system.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 22 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Melzer: "the constraints put on policy by the weak dollar in foreign exchange markets." Hoenig: "if the dollar continues to weaken."
- Summary: A broad dollar decline is discussed repeatedly and treated as a policy consideration.
- Direction: below neutral, because the dollar is weakening.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58.** Minehan: price increases in "paper, plastics, and leather"; Hoenig: materials hikes "being passed through" more; Forrestal: increases "generally have not been passed through".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 58.** Melzer: Michigan survey "just over 4 percent for 5 to 10 years"; Blinder: surveys "have shown no deterioration".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 59 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Minehan: "My own bias would be to tighten sooner rather than later." Greenspan: "I lean marginally, tentatively, unsurely toward asymmetry."
- Summary: Hold at B with a majority favoring a tightening bias; nobody argues for easing.
- Direction: hawkish side, in the 51-60 band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Roll call: all 11 Yes. Lindsey: "a few dissents would be a much more appropriate way of sending that signal"; Stern, Forrestal, Yellen and Blinder prefer symmetry while others prefer asymmetry.
- Summary: Unanimous vote with tactical disagreement on directive wording and a few differences on outlook.
- Direction: low, broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McDonough: markets "euphoric"; Lindsey: Japanese banks, German currency, the debt ceiling.
- Summary: Limited to moderate attention to market euphoria, the dollar and foreign financial risks; the economy dominates.
- Direction: attention is above the typical mid-90s level but still modest.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "we clearly should remain open to further increases." Greenspan: "mildly asymmetric" toward tightening.
- Summary: Hold now with a tightening bias and data dependence.
- Direction: hawkish side of neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Boehne: "we need more than the usual amount of humility about our assessment of the outlook." Stern: "the confidence bands around these forecasts ... are very, very wide."
- Summary: Many members call themselves agnostic and say risks are unusually hard to judge, while others are fairly confident.
- Direction: elevated.
