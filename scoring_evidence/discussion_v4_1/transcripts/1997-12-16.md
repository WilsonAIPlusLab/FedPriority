# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) - meeting 1997-12-16 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (all member and participant turns; staff turns removed, so staff answers are missing). Read to the last line, including the vote read-out: 11 Yes, 1 No (Broaddus). It has an economic go-around, a Chairman's closing economic commentary, a policy go-around (alternative B, symmetric vs asymmetric directive) and a roll call. It is NOT a special-topic document under V4.1.1 rule 1 (it has both a vote and a stance go-around).

**Era context.** Late 1997, a strong expansion with very low unemployment, very low measured inflation, and an Asian financial crisis (Thailand, Korea, Indonesia, contagion worries for Hong Kong, China, Brazil) that arrived since the prior meeting. The text, not the era, drives the scores below.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (productivity acceleration, CPI surprisingly low, would "probably come out in favor of moving" absent Asia but argues B symmetric); McDonough (international "ridge" analogy, B symmetric); Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Minehan, Jordan, Stern (upside risks on growth/inflation, tight labor); Boehne, Guynn, McTeer, Hoenig, Kelley, Gramlich, Phillips, Ferguson, Rivlin, Meyer (mixed, mostly wait-and-see); First VP Rives (Dallas/St. Louis acting: asymmetric B).

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the level that would worry them, low and drifting down: "inflation continues subdued" (Parry), "Inflation, of course, remains dormant" (Kelley), Greenspan on "reams of ever lower CPI readings". Offsetting: wage pressures building, upside risk skewed. Below 50, in the "contained / modest" band, not deflationary (members divided on the deflation talk; Rivlin dismisses it). Score ~36.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy above potential: Meyer "already operating beyond its point of sustainable capacity", growth "well above trend", Rives "above most estimates of potential growth". Partial offset: Gramlich says capacity utilization is now close to its historical average. Above 50 (bright line satisfied), modest: 58.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Extremely tight, repeated across nearly every District: Broaddus "at least as tight as they have been... record-breaking job vacancy backlogs", McTeer "extremely tight", Meyer 4.6% unemployment, Parry "well below the natural rate", Moskow unemployment at or below 4% in the District. Score 70.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong, above trend: GDP "near 4 percent" (Meyer), Greenspan "powerful surge", Guynn consumer "a little stronger", business investment "maintained at strong levels longer"; some soft retail anecdotes (Moskow "sluggish", mixed holiday sales) and a slowdown expected from Asia. Net above trend: 62.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed substantively: Greenspan "productivity has been accelerating over the past several quarters"; Ferguson "increase in productivity above the anemic 1 percent trend"; Phillips "productivity picture ... getting a bit clearer". Above trend: 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Not tight: Ferguson "credit conditions are not overly tight" and loosening lending standards; Minehan "Monetary policy is not overly tight... capital inflows... reducing interest rates"; Broaddus says capital reflow "has some of the effects of an easing". Hoenig notes real funds rate relatively high. Mildly loose side (<50 per bright line): 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Bond rates falling is mentioned only as a yield move (Broaddus); no market-functioning discussion.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spread/yield-premium wording. Availability/lending-standard language only: Ferguson "some loosening of lending standards", Greenspan "Is bank lending pulling back here? I do not see any evidence of that whatever". Per V4.1.1 rule 3, score on availability: easy availability, below 50 (38), CONF Low, UNC +-10.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation view stated: Greenspan "At any realistic earnings forecast, stock prices will be substantially overvalued" and equity premium "at the low end of normal ranges"; Rivlin/Phillips hope for orderly drift down. Elevated valuations, concern direction on the table's "elevated/stretched" side: 30.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks seen as sound, Asia exposure small (Greenspan "our financial institutions have not lent that much"), with minor concerns about loosening standards and CRE lending (Minehan, Ferguson). "Healthy with minor concerns": 36 (bright line <50 satisfied), CONF Low-Moderate.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciation: Parry "the dollar's appreciation over the last year" and "continued appreciation"; Hoenig, Rivlin, Gramlich echo. Above 50 (bright line): 62.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS: Phillips "price stability or deflation in the commodity and goods sectors"; Greenspan on falling underlying/commodity indices: 22. SERVICES: Phillips "Services inflation, largely affected by wages"; firm but not surging: 55. HOUSING: not discussed as inflation: N/A. EXPECTATIONS: "inflation expectations remain subdued and are declining" (Hoenig), Parry on the sizable drop in long-term expectations: 37.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: Hold at B (no rate change); directive moved from tightening bias to symmetric by a "modest majority". Several (Parry, Minehan, Moskow, Guynn, Rives) prefer to keep asymmetry; Broaddus wants +25bp; Greenspan and Meyer say the next move is still likely up but not soon; Kelley and others stress downside Asia risk. Stance = balanced, hawkish drift in underlying views offset by the dovish-ish removal of the bias. Score 50.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Broaddus, hawkish). Several expressed preference for asymmetry but all accepted B symmetric (Rives said "We would support B asymmetric"; he is not a voter in the roll call). No sharp exchanges; differences about risk balance and directive wording. Score 33.
- Financial stability emphasis: The Asian financial crisis, contagion, bank weakness abroad (McDonough), IMF, Greenspan's stock-overvaluation/implosion tail, Minehan/Ferguson on CRE and lending standards. Prominent but framed as international/real-economy risk; domestic financial-stability content is moderate. Score 50.
- Forward guidance: No explicit commitment; symmetric directive "to respond flexibly", yet Greenspan: "we will have to move rates up at some point... most probable next move"; Meyer: next move still likely tightening. Neutral with latent hawkish tilt: 52.
- Uncertainty: Heavily emphasized: Rivlin "widened our confidence intervals quite a lot", Stern "more than enough uncertainty to go around", Greenspan "tails... getting more extended", Gramlich "hard for me to know where the predominant risks are", Kelley "difficult to judge". Many participants, not Knightian. Score 68.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.** OUTPUT_GAP 58 (>50, "beyond sustainable capacity"); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (<50, "not overly tight"/loosening); CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (<50, availability easy); BANKING_STRESS 36 (<50, sound); DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 (>50, appreciation); TREASURY_STRESS N/A. Higher score = more of the named construct in each case.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 58 vs LABOR 70 differ by 12 (<15), explained by Gramlich's capacity-utilization argument holding the gap down while labor evidence is very tight. OUTPUT_GAP 58 vs CONSUMPTION 62: fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move. TREASURY N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 vs sub-components (goods 22, services 55, expectations 37): consistent with a weighted average in the high 30s.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant discusses Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: no participant discusses shelter/rent inflation (housing appears only as activity).

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Kelley, "Inflation, of course, remains dormant at this time"; Boehne, "inflation remains subdued despite some upward pressures on compensation"; Greenspan, "reams of ever lower CPI readings". Center of gravity: low and falling measured inflation with wage-pressure risk to the upside. Direction: below neutral because members describe inflation as subdued/dormant.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Meyer, "an economy already operating beyond its point of sustainable capacity"; Rives, "Real GDP growth... is above most estimates of potential growth"; Gramlich, utilization "close to its historical average". Center of gravity: modestly above potential, contested on capacity side. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +-5 [High].** Evidence: Broaddus, "at least as tight as they have been... record-breaking job vacancy backlogs"; McTeer, "Labor markets remain extremely tight". Center of gravity: near-universal report of shortages and wage pressure at 4.6% unemployment. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Meyer, "growing at a pace near 4 percent"; Guynn, "Business investment spending has been maintained at strong levels". Center of gravity: above-trend spending with Asia-related slowing expected. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "productivity has been accelerating over the past several quarters"; Ferguson, "increase in productivity above the anemic 1 percent trend". Center of gravity: several see acceleration, hedged by data weakness. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Ferguson, "credit conditions are not overly tight... some loosening of lending standards"; Minehan, "Monetary policy is not overly tight. We have inflows of capital to the United States that are reducing interest rates." Center of gravity: supportive, easing via capital reflow. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-10 [Low].** Evidence (availability only, no spread wording): Greenspan, "Is bank lending pulling back here? I do not see any evidence of that whatever"; Ferguson, "loosening of lending standards". Center of gravity: credit freely available. Direction: below neutral (less stress).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "stock prices will be substantially overvalued" at realistic earnings; "the equity premium at the low end of normal ranges". Center of gravity: elevated valuation concern from the Chairman, hopeful orderly decline from others. Direction: valuation seen as rich (low end of scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 +-9 [Low].** Evidence: Greenspan, "our financial institutions have not lent that much to borrowers in that part of the world"; Minehan, "Commercial real estate lending... raising regulatory as well as economic concerns". Center of gravity: sound system with lending-standard watch items. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry, "the dollar's appreciation over the last year" and "continued appreciation of the dollar will help to hold down prices". Center of gravity: appreciation cited as restraint on exports and prices. Direction: above neutral (strengthening).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 22.** Phillips, "price stability or deflation in the commodity and goods sectors". **INFLATION_SERVICES: 55.** Phillips, "Services inflation, largely affected by wages". **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 37.** Hoenig, "inflation expectations remain subdued and are declining".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 50 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan, "I would be marginally in favor of alternative B symmetric"; Broaddus, "a modest 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today would be a prudent step". Center of gravity: unanimous hold except one hawkish dissent, directive made symmetric but next move expected to be up. Direction: balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-6 [High].** Evidence: roll call "President Broaddus No"; Moskow, "I would personally favor retaining the asymmetric directive... although I could live with" B symmetric. Center of gravity: one formal dissent, several mild reservations on wording. Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: McDonough, "implosion of the financial systems in some of the Asian countries"; Greenspan, "significant implosion here. I believe the odds are small but not zero". Center of gravity: Asian financial crisis is a prominent topic, domestic financial-stability content is moderate. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point"; Meyer, "without changing our view that the next move is still likely to be in a policy tightening direction". Center of gravity: neutral directive with latent tightening view. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Rivlin, "widened our confidence intervals quite a lot"; Stern, "there is more than enough uncertainty to go around". Center of gravity: many participants stress unusually high uncertainty from Asia. Direction: high.
