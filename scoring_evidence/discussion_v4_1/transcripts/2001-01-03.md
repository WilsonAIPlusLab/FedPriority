# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2001-01-03

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It is a short intermeeting-style policy session, not a standard go-around. The first part is procedural (election of Chairman and Vice Chairman, selection of the Reserve Bank for open market operations). Then the Chairman gives an assessment and proposes a 50bp cut plus a discount-rate package. Members then comment on the proposal, and the discussion turns to how to frame the move publicly and how to handle the press. A vote read-out at the end shows 10 Yes votes and no dissent. Staff turns are removed. Several short turns by one speaker (Stern, Parry) are partly missing, as is the roll call itself. This does not affect scoring.

**Era context.** Read from the text: policy rate at 6-1/2 percent, a 50bp increase "last May", a December meeting that moved the balance-of-risks statement, sharply falling equity earnings estimates, weak autos, an energy price shock easing, and a market-driven "psychological" deterioration. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.** Greenspan describes sharply weakening growth. He cites initial claims rising, S&P earnings revisions turning negative, poor orders, a very weak Christmas season and a dramatic decline in autos. He has cut his Q1 real GDP forecast to 1.7 percent and says growth is moving closer to zero. He also cites stabilizing elements (homebuilding, nonresidential construction, falling oil prices, refinancing cash-out). Inflation expectations and pricing power are declining, so the real funds rate is rising, and rates are "higher than we actually need." McDonough sees markets and firm leaders "at or near a point of psychological crisis." Hoenig says the funds rate has been above the natural rate and that risks are to the downside. Stern points to an inventory correction and the reversed wealth effect from equities. Broaddus cites a negative turn in sentiment in his District. Minehan says the economy is slowing from 5.5-6 percent to about 2-2.5 percent, and that the "tone" is what is terrible. Jordan says 6.5 percent is tighter than before given financial markets. Moskow notes weak autos and that bankers may be overreacting. Poole, Guynn, Santomero, McTeer and Kelley support the cut. Ferguson cites spreads and tightness in financial markets that could take growth far below potential. Meyer says the economy has slowed dramatically and warns about instrument instability and overshooting. Gramlich says to take the big moves early.

**Direction statements and magnitude.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see pressure as below target or contained. The statement says "inflation pressures remain contained," the Chair says "pricing power continues to decline," and inflation expectations are falling. Energy prices are high but easing. Score is below 50, about 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Participants see the economy as decelerating sharply toward a growth rate that Ferguson says could be "far below potential". Kelley says the economy is moving from an unsustainable growth rate to a sustainable one, which implies it was above potential and is now falling through it. The Chair says growth is near zero in Q1. Below potential, about 40. Bright-line: Ferguson's "far below potential" supports a score under 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Labor is barely discussed. The only evidence is a "significant increase in initial claims and insured unemployment." No level or tightness language is given. I score slightly above neutral, reflecting a still-tight market that is loosening at the margin, with low confidence.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Participants see spending and investment as below trend and weakening fast. Evidence: a "rather poor" Christmas season, autos "dramatic decline," production cutbacks and weakening orders. Offsets: housing stabilized, refinancing is up, nonresidential construction is reasonably good, and consumer confidence is "historically high" in absolute terms. Score is about 31.
- PRODUCTIVITY. The Chair, Moskow and Hoenig say there is little evidence of deterioration in the long-term gains in technology and productivity. Participants see the productivity trend as still strong but give no new data. Score is modestly above 50, about 62, with moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Ferguson: "spreads and other indicators of tightness in financial markets." Statement: "tight conditions in some segments of financial markets." Jordan: "financial markets... 6-1/2 percent... is in effect tighter." Equity declines are reversing the wealth effect. Tighter than neutral, about 62. Bright-line (tight means above 50) is satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS. N/A. The yield curve and TIPS comparisons are discussed only as signals for inflation expectations and for the policy rate. No functioning or dysfunction commentary.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Ferguson is the only member to mention spreads. He cites them as an indicator of tightness. No level is given, but "tightness" indicates levels above normal. Bright-line: a high level means above 50, and I score about 60 with low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. The Chair and Stern discuss falling earnings estimates and the equity market decline reversing the wealth effect. There is no valuation commentary (neither bubble nor cheap). By the rule that price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55, but there is a concern component. About 55.
- BANKING_STRESS. Only Moskow, who mentions "anecdotal evidence... on the banking side" and "bankers may be overreacting." This points to some tightening of bank lending behavior but no capital or solvency concern. About 54, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. N/A. The "strong dollar mantra" remark is an analogy about communications discipline, not a view of the dollar.
- Inflation sub-components. GOODS, SERVICES and HOUSING are N/A. EXPECTATIONS: the Chair says inflation expectations "are continuing to decline" (TIPS spread down appreciably) and he doubts the absolute level. Scored about 40.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; evidence says contained or falling, so below 50. Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential; evidence says below, so below 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; evidence says tight in segments, so above 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress; evidence says tightness, so above 50. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN means tighter policy advocated; evidence is an aggressive cut, so very low.

**Tier 3 check.**

- Policy lean: all participants support an immediate 50bp cut, intermeeting in effect, with the downside risk statement retained and an extra discount-rate cut. This is large, immediate easing short of emergency or QE. Hawkish/dovish lean sits in the strong-dovish band, about 14. The mild cautions (Broaddus, Meyer, Hoenig) concern pace and messaging, not the cut itself.
- Dissent: 10-0 vote with no dissent. Non-voters Broaddus and others also support it. Disagreement is limited to framing (taking back the May increase versus real rate framing versus insurance), plus Hoenig's unease about claiming long-term strength and Meyer's caution on future aggressiveness. Roughly 12.
- Financial-stability attention: moderate. McDonough "psychological crisis," Stern's wealth effect, Ferguson on spreads, Moskow on bankers, and the Chair on earnings revisions. This is a theme in a minority of turns, not dominant. About 38.
- Forward guidance: the downward-weighted risk statement is retained; the Chair says he has "no clue" about the next meeting and that the cut does not presuppose the next move; Poole stresses a willingness to "move decisively" if needed; Broaddus worries markets will expect more. Easing bias with data dependence, about 27.
- Uncertainty: Chair says "free fall" has not yet occurred but "a free fall doesn't look like a free fall until you really start falling," that data are likely to be revised down, and that it is "not obvious" growth is 2-1/2 percent. Hoenig is unsure about long-term strength. Several members stress the speed and unusual nature of the sentiment shift. Elevated to high, about 58.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**

1. OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (55) diverge by 15. Labor receives only one data point, rising claims, and no tightness language, so I do not push it below neutral. I keep confidence low. The divergence sits at the threshold; the explanation is the thin labor evidence.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (40) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (31) differ by 9. Consistent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) and CREDIT_SPREADS (60) co-move. Consistent.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS. Treasury is N/A, which suits a non-crisis tightening of conditions.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) and EXPECTATIONS (40) are consistent.

**Anchor sanity.** A 2001 slowdown with contained inflation, a growth scare and an aggressive cut fits low inflation pressure (high 30s), below-potential output, and strong dovish lean. Nothing needs changing.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants as conditions.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "pricing power continues to decline"; policy statement read by Greenspan: "inflation pressures remain contained." Center of gravity: inflation is contained and drifting lower, with energy a partial offset. Inflation pressure is below neutral because pricing power and expectations are declining.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson: growth could slow "to a pace that probably would be far below potential"; Kelley: moving "from an unsustainable growth rate to a sustainable one." Center of gravity: the economy is decelerating fast from above-trend growth to near-zero Q1 growth. Output gap is below neutral because growth is falling below potential and the Chair sees it moving toward zero.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Greenspan: "significant increase in initial claims and insured unemployment." Center of gravity: little explicit labor commentary; claims rising from a tight base. Labor tightness is slightly above neutral because there is no slack language, but this is inferred from indirect evidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 31 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "extraordinary weakening... of the motor vehicles industry," "Christmas season... rather poor." Center of gravity: sharp weakness in autos, orders and production, offset only partly by housing and refinancing. Spending and investment are below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-10, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "little evidence to date of any deterioration in the long-term advances in technology and the related expansion in productivity"; Moskow agrees. Center of gravity: the productivity trend is viewed as intact and strong. Above neutral because members describe the trend gains as continuing.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson: "spreads and other indicators of tightness in financial markets"; Greenspan reading the statement: "tight conditions in some segments of financial markets." Center of gravity: tightness in segments of markets plus an equity decline. Conditions are tighter than neutral, not crisis-level.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Ferguson: "I look at spreads and other indicators of tightness." Center of gravity: a single member cites spreads as showing tightness, with no level given. Above neutral because tightness implies spreads above normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: S&P earnings estimates "revised down at a fairly aggressive pace"; Stern: "what has happened to equity values... clearly has reversed the wealth effect." Center of gravity: concern about the equity decline, but no valuation judgment. Slightly above neutral reflecting the decline and its wealth effect, per the default for price moves.

**BANKING_STRESS: 54 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Moskow: "anecdotal evidence... on the banking side" and "bankers may be overreacting to the situation." Center of gravity: some caution in bank lending, no capital issues. Banking stress is slightly above neutral because of emerging caution only.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Evidence: Greenspan: "inflation expectations are continuing to decline." Expectations are below neutral because the decline is substantial, tempered by the Chair's doubt about the absolute level.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 14 +-5, High.** Evidence: Greenspan: "we have to move the funds rate lower... down 50 basis points"; Moskow: "If we don't do it, we risk falling behind the curve." Center of gravity: unanimous support for an aggressive cut. The lean is strongly dovish; Meyer's and Broaddus's cautions about future pace are minor offsets.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 +-5, High.** Evidence: vote read-out shows 10 Yes; Hoenig: "I'm a bit uneasy with what you've suggested" (messaging only). Center of gravity: unanimous, with differences confined to how to explain the move. Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-10, Moderate.** Evidence: McDonough: markets "at or near a point of psychological crisis"; Ferguson on spreads and tightness. Center of gravity: a recurring but not dominant theme around sentiment, equities and credit. Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 27 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: retain "the balance of risks statement to the down side"; "I don't think we should presuppose that whatever we do today suggests anything very specific about what we will do at the next meeting." Center of gravity: downside-weighted risks and a possible discount-rate follow-up, but explicit data dependence. Easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "a free fall doesn't look like a free fall until you really start falling"; Hoenig: "I'm not sure we know that because events have come on rather rapidly." Center of gravity: the speed of deterioration, data revisions and the sentiment shift lead several members to stress unusual uncertainty.
