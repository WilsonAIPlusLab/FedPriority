# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript discussion, meeting 2020-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It contains: brief member questions in the Desk Q&A; a financial-stability go-around (Rosengren, Mester, Barkin, Brainard, George, Quarles, Kashkari, Williams); the economic go-around (Clarida through Williams) and the Chair's summary; the policy go-around (Clarida through Williams, with Powell closing); procedural motions; and a roll-call read-out in which all ten voters say Yes. There is no dissent. The text reads cleanly; I found no garbled passage that affects scoring.

**2. Era context (not used to override the text).** The speakers describe a pandemic shutdown recession, with lockdowns, 25-26 million initial claims, unemployment heading to about 20 percent, an oil price collapse, emergency Fed liquidity facilities and a funds rate at the lower bound. I score what they say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Clarida, Brainard, Williams, Powell, Bowman and Quarles describe a historic collapse, a disinflationary shock and an output gap of at least 4 percent persisting into 2022. They say easing in financial conditions reflects Fed actions and may not last.
- Rosengren, Kashkari, George, Daly, Mester and Barkin worry about bank capital, dividends and the length of the recovery.
- Bullard is more optimistic and sceptical of the shutdown, and argues the Fed should not call its actions "stimulus".
- Harker, Kaplan, Bostic and Evans report District evidence of collapsing activity.
- In the policy round, all support holding the funds rate at the lower bound and the statement as written. Brainard, Kashkari, Daly, Clarida and Mester discuss stronger forward guidance, yield curve control and QE for later. Quarles and Barkin discuss exit and moral hazard.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Clarida: the shock is "disinflationary, not inflationary" and core PCE will run below 2 percent. Kashkari: "a lot of factors are pushing inflation further below target." Brainard: "disinflationary effects of lower energy prices, increased slack, and a stronger dollar". George expects persistently low inflation. Kaplan raises "disinflation and even deflationary risks in the short run". The one offset is Mester reporting that households expect higher inflation, which she calls at odds with the Phillips curve. Score about 22.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Far below potential. Clarida: "an output gap of at least 4 percent of GDP persisting into 2022" in the best case. The language is "steepest decline since World War II", with Q2 GDP at minus 30 to minus 38 percent annualized. This matches the COVID Q2 2020 anchor of 15-22. Score 15.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Catastrophically slack. Over 25 million claims and unemployment heading to 18-20 percent or higher ("levels last seen during the Great Depression"). The anchor is 15-22 for 2020 Q2 and the descriptor is "massive layoffs". Score 12.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Collapsing. Brainard: spending "down nearly 100 percent" at restaurants and theaters, with retail sales down 8.7 percent in March. Harker: card charges down 30 percent, travel down 90 percent. Mester, George and Barkin report capex frozen. Small pockets of strength (home improvement, groceries) do not change the net. Score 13.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Barely discussed as current growth. Barkin: "I also worry about productivity... health-distancing protocols... will reduce throughput." Evans: safety investments "will reduce productivity". Kaplan lists improving productivity as a structural need. The signal is modestly below trend and prospective only. Score 37, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Mixed. Clarida: "financial conditions have eased, and considerably so in many markets," due to Fed actions, but this "may not prove to be durable". Williams: funding and credit conditions "improved markedly". Daly: "easing financial market conditions". Against this, Quarles cites the "widespread tightening of lending conditions" in the SLOOS and Mester says risk spreads have not returned to prepandemic levels. Brainard says HY spreads remain elevated and the leveraged loan market shows fragility. The level is eased from the March panic but still somewhat tight and stressed, and no one calls conditions "accommodative". Score 55, Moderate.
- TREASURY_STRESS. Discussed. Barkin: "the world's premier market for safe assets shouldn't be misfiring... now that, I hope, the dysfunction in these markets has passed." Brainard: hedge-fund unwinds "contributed to the massive distortions in Treasury securities markets," but "smooth market functioning" has been restored. Kashkari asks about bid-ask spreads. Past dysfunction is recognized but the current state is described as recovered and orderly. Score 50, Moderate.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. The stated level is elevated although improved. Brainard: "In the high-yield corporate bond market, spreads remain elevated" along with a "reduction in spreads more generally". Kaplan: HY energy spreads are "about 1,600 basis points", double the HY index. Mester: "risk spreads have not returned to prepandemic levels". Daly: "corporate default risk remains elevated". A stated high level means the score must be above 50. Score 66, Moderate.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. A single passing remark from Williams ("equity markets have rallied") and Brainard's note that "declines in asset valuations" could weigh on banks, with no valuation view. Default to the neutral range. Score 48, Low.
- BANKING_STRESS. Participants say banks are healthy now and expect stress ahead. Brainard: banks "came into the current crisis with strong capital and liquidity buffers". Quarles: "U.S. banks remain financially healthy" and "banks are well capitalized". Against that, Mester says CECL provisioning "will potentially eat significantly into bank capital cushions". Kashkari says Blue Chip scenario losses would cut BHC capital "in half". George and Daly argue banks should suspend dividends. Williams says illiquidity is "becoming a solvency problem... ultimately for banks". "Healthy and sound" requires below 50, and the prospective concern keeps it from going low. Score 45, Moderate.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Only two passing mentions, both of a strong dollar. Brainard: "a stronger dollar" among disinflationary factors. Kashkari: "the strong dollar". Direction is appreciation, so the score is above 50. Score 60, Low.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more inflation pressure; the evidence is disinflation, so the score is low. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR: higher means more above potential or tighter. The evidence is deep slack, so low. Matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. The evidence is spreads still elevated, so above 50. Matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. Banks are "healthy" now, but losses are feared, so slightly below 50. Matches.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: a strong dollar maps to above 50. Matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean. This is a hold at the lower bound with unanimous support. The lean for the future is toward more accommodation: Rosengren wants broader asset-purchase authority; Brainard, Kashkari and Daly want strong forward guidance, YCC and QE; Clarida discusses "rates on hold until we actually achieve our 2 percent". Dovish, at maximum accommodation. Countervailing but weak: Bullard says the Fed is not stimulating and the shutdown should end, and Quarles and Barkin raise exit and moral hazard. Score about 15.
- Disagreement. The vote was 10-0 and the Chair called it "a strong consensus supporting the statement as written". There are no formal dissents. Differences are in emphasis: Bullard's optimism and critique of the shutdown, Rosengren's and others' push on bank dividends, George's preference for forward guidance over a larger balance sheet, and Kashkari's request to skip the June SEP. Score about 18.
- Financial stability. An entire go-around is devoted to it (banks, dividends, nonbank vulnerabilities, stress tests, CRE and leveraged-loan fragility), and it recurs in the economic and policy rounds. Prominent but not overwhelming relative to the macro collapse. Score about 72.
- Forward guidance. The existing guidance is unchanged, but the signal in the round is toward stronger and more explicit easing commitments (outcome-based guidance, yield curve control, average inflation targeting). Williams says markets expect rates near zero through 2022. Score about 10.
- Uncertainty. Pervasive and extreme. Powell: "tremendous uncertainty remains". Brainard: "the biggest factor in my forecast is uncertainty". Daly: "profoundly uncertain". Mester: "standard models are not equipped". Multiple scenarios (V, U, L, "Second Waves") are debated. Score about 90.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 15: "far below potential", so below 50. Satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 55: no participant uses "accommodative/supportive/easy" for conditions, and spreads are still elevated, so a value above 50 is allowed. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 66: a stated high level ("elevated", 1,600 bp), so above 50. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 45: "well capitalized/healthy", so below 50. Satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60: strengthening, so above 50. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS 50: no current dysfunction described, only past dysfunction. Satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (15) and LABOR (12) are within 15 points.
- OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION (13) are within 15 points.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) and CREDIT_SPREADS (66) co-move in the same direction (both somewhat tight); credit is judged a bit more stressed than overall conditions because markets eased on Fed backstops.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: the crisis-level dysfunction has passed, so Treasury stress sits near 50 and slightly below conditions. Consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) is consistent with the sub-components (goods 30 from falling energy, expectations 35 with market measures falling).

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not addressed by participants. Services price dynamics were not raised, and shelter was mentioned only as mortgage forbearance, not as price inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "the COVID-19 contagion shock is disinflationary, not inflationary... the projected core PCE inflation path in the Tealbook may be too optimistic." Kashkari: "A lot of factors are pushing inflation further below target."
- Center of gravity: near-consensus that inflation is below target and headed lower.
- Direction: below neutral, because members expect persistent sub-2 percent inflation and disinflationary forces dominate.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 15 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "an output gap of at least 4 percent of GDP persisting into 2022"; "the steepest decline in U.S. real economic activity since World War II."
- Center of gravity: a deep, historic contraction.
- Direction: far below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 12 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "more than 25 million Americans have filed for unemployment benefits... double-digit levels last seen during the Great Depression." Bullard: "Most estimates are in the range of 15 to 20 percent".
- Center of gravity: mass job loss, unemployment near 20 percent.
- Direction: far below full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 13 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "Spending is down nearly 100 percent over the previous year at restaurants and movie theaters"; "Total retail... sales plunged 8.7 percent in March." Mester: "More than half of District contacts reported they have delayed or canceled planned capital projects."
- Center of gravity: a collapse in spending and capex, with only pockets of strength.
- Direction: far below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 37 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Barkin: "A new set of health-distancing protocols... will reduce throughput." Evans: safety investments "will reduce productivity."
- Center of gravity: a prospective, modest drag on productivity, discussed only briefly.
- Direction: modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "financial conditions have eased, and considerably so in many markets... may not prove to be durable." Quarles: "widespread tightening of lending conditions that we saw in the April [SLOOS]."
- Center of gravity: conditions improved sharply from the March panic but remain somewhat tight and fragile.
- Direction: slightly above neutral (tighter), because spreads and lending standards have not normalized.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 50 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Barkin: "now that, I hope, the dysfunction in these markets has passed." Brainard: "These forceful early interventions have been effective at restoring smooth market functioning."
- Center of gravity: past dysfunction acknowledged, current functioning recovering.
- Direction: at neutral, because the described dysfunction is behind the market.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 66 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "In the high-yield corporate bond market, spreads remain elevated." Kaplan: "The average credit spread for high-yield energy companies now is about double the overall high-yield index, about 1,600 basis points."
- Center of gravity: spreads are off the peaks but still wide.
- Direction: above neutral, on the stated elevated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Williams: "equity markets have rallied". Brainard: "declines in asset valuations... could weigh on bank balance sheets."
- Center of gravity: price moves noted without a valuation view.
- Direction: at neutral by default.

**BANKING_STRESS: 45 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "Thus far, U.S. banks remain financially healthy." Mester: loan losses "will potentially eat significantly into bank capital cushions."
- Center of gravity: sound now, with considerable concern about coming losses.
- Direction: just below neutral, because the current assessment is healthy and the stress is prospective.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "disinflationary effects of lower energy prices, increased slack, and a stronger dollar". Kashkari: "the strong dollar".
- Center of gravity: a strong dollar noted in passing as a disinflationary factor.
- Direction: above neutral, because of appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30**
- Evidence: Kashkari: "Fuel prices are down, freight rates are down... large declines in livestock and dairy prices"; Brainard: "lower energy prices".
- Center of gravity: falling energy and commodity prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** (not discussed).

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** (not discussed).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 35**
- Evidence: Kashkari: "The five-year, five-year-forward rate is down about 50 basis points." Clarida: "inflation expectations could move uncomfortably lower."
- Center of gravity: drifting below target, with a small offset from Mester's household survey, where households expect higher inflation.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 15 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "we should be asking for broader authority to directly purchase a wider set of assets now." Daly: "we will likely need to rely on aggressive forward guidance as well as QE and other tools, like yield curve control."
- Center of gravity: a hold at the lower bound with maximum accommodation, and a lean toward further easing tools.
- Direction: strongly dovish. Bullard (the shutdown is not "stimulus"), Quarles and Barkin (exit and moral hazard) are weak counterweights.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "obviously, a strong consensus supporting the statement as written." The roll call shows ten Yes votes.
- Center of gravity: unanimous vote with differences of emphasis only (Bullard's outlook and shutdown views, bank dividends, balance sheet versus forward guidance).
- Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: A full financial-stability go-around (Brainard on nonbank and CRE vulnerabilities; Rosengren, Kashkari, George and Daly on bank dividends; Quarles on stress tests). Williams: "this success should not breed a sense of complacency."
- Center of gravity: a prominent topic across all three rounds, though the macro collapse is still the main subject.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 10 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "committing to strong outcome-based forward guidance that conditions liftoff on sustained achievement of full employment and 2 percent average inflation." Kashkari: "I do think we should soon be adopting a firmer commitment not to raise rates".
- Center of gravity: no change today, but the forward signal is toward stronger and longer easing commitments.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 90 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "tremendous uncertainty remains about what lies ahead." Brainard: "the biggest factor in my forecast is uncertainty... an unusually wide range seems plausible."
- Center of gravity: most speakers stress deep, unusual uncertainty and multiple scenarios, with models questioned.
