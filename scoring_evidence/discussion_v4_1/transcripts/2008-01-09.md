# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting date 2008-01-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** A member/participant discussion transcript. The file holds all non-staff turns of a short unscheduled Committee videoconference. The Chair opens with a long statement of his own views and then calls on each participant in turn. Staff turns are removed, so only members' questions to staff remain (Fisher on the five-year/five-year-forward measure and on the inflation revisions, Lacker on nonlinearity). There is no vote and no policy action: the Chair says at the outset that he is not proposing action and wants a consultation before his speech and testimony. The text is clean, with no garbled passages. It is about 77k characters and covers all participants.

**2. Era context.** The text itself places the discussion after 100 bp of cuts since September and a weak employment report. Participants talk about credit and housing problems, bank markdowns and capital raising, and energy-driven headline inflation. I score only what they say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Bernanke: downside risks "quite significant"; recession risk "at least 50 percent"; banks fragile; policy "not gotten ahead of the curve"; inflation "would probably take care of itself" in a recession.
- Rosengren: wants 50 bp now. Yellen: 25 bp today preferred, 50 bp in the near term. Evans: 50 bp today. Kohn: policy "still restrictive," would have supported a move today, will wait. Geithner: "more risk to us now in gradualism than in force." Mishkin: at least 50 bp, possibly more, at the next meeting, but not today.
- Lacker, Stern, Fisher, Plosser, Poole, Lockhart, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin: against an intermeeting move on tactics and credibility grounds. Most of them favor a substantial cut at the end-of-month meeting (Plosser "open"; Lockhart "very likely" 50 bp; Poole "could support 50"; Kroszner "fairly bold move").
- Inflation-wary: Hoenig (risks "as dangerously" elevated for inflation as for slowdown, wants to be cautious), Fisher (dual mandate, demand-pull from emerging economies), Lacker (worried about inflation strategy), Pianalto (can support 50 bp only if it merely offsets the lower equilibrium real rate), Plosser and Poole (worry about taking accommodation back).

**4. Directional signals (words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Headline has run "significantly above core," core came in "a little higher," Pianalto notes 60 percent of the November CPI basket rose 3 percent or more, and energy prices are high. Offsetting this, expectations are "contained" and "anchored," wage growth has slowed, and slack is expected to restrain prices. Score about 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as below potential. Evidence: Yellen says "we already have slack in the labor market and more seems likely to develop." Kohn has unemployment rising to 5¼, half a point over the NAIRU. Mishkin expects "increased economic slack." Growth is forecast at about 1 percent. The gap is described as emerging rather than deep, so about 42.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as softening to below full employment. Evidence: the employment report was weak, "the unemployment rate was a lot higher," "stagnant or contracting labor market," Bernanke's cited 0.6 stall-speed rise. The level (about 5 percent, moving toward 5¼) is near NAIRU. Slack is said to be developing and wage growth has been low. Score about 42.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending has been "amazingly robust so far" (Yellen), but members expect it to soften: Evans says a "crack" in the supporting foundation, Rosengren cites wealth effects, Kroszner sees sharp deterioration in credit-card behavior. Residential construction is falling, housing is weak, credit is tightening, and there is a "sharp deterioration in consumer behavior." Net, below trend and worsening. Score about 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as tight. Evidence: Bernanke says "lending is going to be quite tight," banks are "raising their internal capital targets," credit contracting in mortgages and HELOCs, and tighter conditions are expected in credit cards, CRE and non-investment-grade corporate lending. Yellen: equity prices down about 6 percent, spreads up, borrowing rates higher. Counterweight: market functioning, especially money markets, has "improved" (Yellen, Warsh). Score about 68.
- TREASURY_STRESS: only a passing note that Treasury yields declined (Yellen). No dysfunction is discussed. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Bernanke lists "rising credit spreads" as a regime-switch indicator, and Yellen says "credit spreads are up." No spread level is stated. There is availability information: credit is tight, banks are reluctant to lend, a "credit crunch" is feared. Under the v4.1 rule, direction alone gives only 52-55, but the availability language puts it somewhat higher. I take 60 with Low confidence and a wide band.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity prices fell "very substantially" (Yellen, about 6 percent), and falling stock prices are a recession indicator and a wealth-effect worry (Rosengren, Bernanke). There is no valuation commentary (no "overvalued" or "distressed"). Per the rule, this is price-move commentary only, so I stay in the 55-60 band at Low-Moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see significant stress. Evidence: Bernanke's "$75 billion in extraordinary markdowns," "quite a fragility," low loan-loss reserves, "no Plan B." Warsh: "the problems among large financial institutions are very serious" and the "state of these institutions has not improved." Kroszner: capital constraints. Score about 73.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: no discussion framed as goods, services or housing prices. Energy and commodity prices are mentioned only in passing as a source of headline inflation. N/A for goods, services and housing. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is discussed by nearly everyone: "contained" (Evans), "anchored" (Kohn), "well behaved" (Yellen), "moved down since December" (Geithner), "very solidly grounded" (Mishkin). Lacker notes CPI-based expectations are around 2½ percent and bounce above 2, and Hoenig and Pianalto worry about creep. Net: anchored with a minor upward tilt. Score 50.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure: members say upside risks exist but expectations are anchored, so modestly above 50 (58). Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter: members say softening and slack, so below 50 (42). Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter: members say tight lending and bank capital constraints, so above 50 (68). Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: members say fragile, so above 50 (73). Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress or wider: spreads are "up" and credit is tight, so above 50 (60). All checks consistent.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the Chair and a clear majority favor substantially lower rates, with the end-of-January meeting as the target (50 bp or more, and Mishkin and Evans suggest more). Several wanted action now (Rosengren, Yellen, Evans, Kohn would have supported it, Lockhart in a week or so). The hawkish counterweights are Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Pianalto, Plosser and Poole on inflation and reversal risk. No action is taken today, but the lean is clearly dovish: moderate-to-strong, about 26.
- Disagreement: no vote and no formal dissents. There are two camps on timing (act now versus wait for the January 29-30 meeting, with the larger group preferring to wait) and a smaller camp on whether the easing should be this large (Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Pianalto). Assessments of the economy are broadly shared, and Geithner and the Chair see "a pretty solid consensus." So: moderate differences on tactics and inflation risk, about 33.
- Financial stability attention: very high. Bernanke spends a long section on bank balance sheets, capital and credit. Warsh, Kroszner, Kohn, Stern, Yellen (credit crunch) and Mishkin (financial disruptions, nonlinearity) all develop it. Prominent, close to dominant, together with the growth-risk discussion. About 70.
- Forward guidance: the explicit purpose is to signal an easing bias in the Chair's speech and testimony ("we are likely to move against the emerging economic weakness," Kohn; Geithner wants substantial further action). There is no explicit commitment and several members insist on data dependence. Strong easing bias, about 14.
- Uncertainty: very high. The Chair says it is "extremely hard to tell" whether the stall speed has been reached and notes "a very wide range of possible outcomes" for house prices. Others mention nonlinearity, regime-switching, tail risks and "no Plan B." Many emphasize difficulty, but most have a central view (the economy weakens, easing is warranted). About 62.

CREDIT_SPREADS 60 (spreads "up," availability tight; no stated "narrow" or "compressed" level: pass). BANKING_STRESS 73 (stress language: pass). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A (no dysfunction described).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 42 / LABOR 42 / CONSUMPTION 38: all within about 4 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 68 / CREDIT_SPREADS 60: co-move. TREASURY N/A, so that pair does not apply. ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Pianalto, "In November, 60 percent of the CPI market basket prices increased at rates of 3 percent or greater." Evans, "Headline inflation has been significantly above core for long enough to make people wonder about underlying inflation, but inflation expectations have remained contained."
- Center of gravity: modest upside pressure from energy and recent CPI, moderated by anchored expectations and expected slack.
- Direction: above neutral because members acknowledge rising realized inflation and upside risks, though most expect it to ease.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 42 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Yellen, "we already have slack in the labor market and more seems likely to develop." Mishkin, "there will be increased economic slack in the economy."
- Center of gravity: the economy is seen weakening to below potential, with slack emerging rather than deep.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe slack and a rising unemployment rate over the NAIRU (Kohn).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the unemployment rate rising to 5¼ percent, half a point over the NAIRU." Yellen, "we have entered, at best, a period of slow employment growth."
- Center of gravity: a sharply weakening labor market moving into modest slack.
- Direction: below neutral because unemployment is rising toward or above the NAIRU and job growth has stalled.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Yellen, "consumer spending has been amazingly robust so far, but I find it unimaginable that it can continue." Kroszner, "a very sharp deterioration in consumer behavior."
- Center of gravity: spending was robust but is expected to slow, with housing and credit tightening dragging.
- Direction: below neutral because members see weakening consumption, falling residential construction and a squeeze from credit.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 68 +-7 [High].**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "The implications of this... are that lending is going to be quite tight. Banks are reluctant to take loans onto their balance sheets." Yellen, "equity prices have fallen very substantially... Credit spreads are up, and borrowing rates for many borrowers are higher."
- Center of gravity: credit supply tightening and capital constraints dominate, with money-market functioning only somewhat better.
- Direction: above neutral (tight) because lending and funding conditions are restrictive and tightening further.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No dysfunction discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 +-10 [Low].**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Credit spreads are up." Bernanke, listing "rising credit spreads" among regime-switch indicators.
- Center of gravity: spreads are widening and credit availability is shrinking, but no level is stated.
- Direction: above neutral because widening plus tight availability implies less favorable credit. The score is held modest because no explicit level of spreads is given.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 +-8 [Low].**
- Evidence: Yellen, "equity prices have fallen very substantially—I guess around 6 percent since our last meeting." Rosengren, "continued declines in housing prices and stock prices."
- Center of gravity: price declines are treated as a wealth-effect and recession-signal concern, with no valuation argument.
- Direction: above neutral (modestly depressed, "declined but orderly") because members see falling equity prices as a source of stress, not as reflecting an overvaluation worry.

**BANKING_STRESS: 73 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "$75 billion in extraordinary markdowns... I think, though, on net that there is really actually quite a fragility here." Warsh, "The problems among large financial institutions are very serious."
- Center of gravity: the Chair and several governors judge large banks to be capital-constrained and impaired as credit intermediaries.
- Direction: above neutral because members describe fragility, low reserves and write-down and capital-raising still "far, far from being complete."

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** No category-level discussion.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Inflation expectations do remain anchored." Lacker, "they are around 2½ percent on the CPI. It is not clear that what they reflect isn't that occasionally we get it down to 2 but most of the time it bounces around above that."
- Center of gravity: expectations are broadly anchored, with a minor concern about the 2½ percent CPI level.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 26 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "I don't think that our policy thus far has gotten ahead of the curve, so to speak, in terms of taking out insurance." Geithner, "I think there is more risk to us now in gradualism than in force." Counterweight, Hoenig: "I want to be very cautious about coming down with our interest rates."
- Center of gravity: a clear majority favors substantial easing, with differences mainly over timing and a minority cautious on inflation.
- Direction: below 50 (dovish) because the Chair and most speakers endorse 50 bp or more and no one argues for tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Lacker, Fisher and Stern oppose acting today, while Rosengren says "if it were up to me, I would support doing it right now" and Evans prefers 50 bp today. Geithner, "I hear a lot of agreement around the room both on your diagnosis."
- Center of gravity: no formal vote or dissent, a shared diagnosis, but real splits over timing and over the inflation risk.
- Direction: above the near-consensus range because there are notable differences on tactics and on the inflation weight, but below the distinct-camps range because the assessment of the economy is largely shared.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 70 +-7 [High].**
- Evidence: Bernanke devotes a sustained section to the 21 large banks, capital, off-balance-sheet exposures and credit tightening. Warsh, "these top twenty or so institutions are still largely ill-equipped to facilitate credit or to be shock absorbers."
- Center of gravity: financial and credit channels are the main stated reason a recession could become worse, discussed at length by the Chair and many members.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Kohn, "you should signal something in your speech tomorrow that we are likely to move against the emerging economic weakness." Chair, "there is at least a reasonable amount of support for the idea that some insurance is worthwhile."
- Center of gravity: the purpose of the meeting is to authorize an easing signal, tempered by "no pre-commitment" and data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "ex ante it's extremely hard to tell" and "a very wide range of possible outcomes" for house prices. Mishkin, "a lot of potential for nonlinearity."
- Center of gravity: many stress tail risks and regime-change uncertainty, though most hold a central view that the economy is weakening.
