## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 15 +-10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Corrigan: "if we have the usual partisan street fight in the Congress... [the decline in long-term rates] could turn around in a flash and with a vengeance." Greenspan: that reversal "would bring the stock market down with it."
- Center of gravity: financial-market reaction to the fiscal package is a recurring theme, but it is framed as fiscal credibility and communication, with no stress, banking, or stability discussion.
- Direction confirmation: attention to financial stability is low because financial markets appear only as the market verdict on the deficit plan.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 54 +-8, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Melzer: "if long rates came down and stayed down, then people could reasonably expect us to lower short rates. And I'm not sure that we want to be in that position." Corrigan: the "adjusting policy" sentence sounds "like dangling something out there here and now"; Greenspan: "one we've got to stay very fuzzy on."
- Center of gravity: members push to avoid any easing implication and to keep the signal noncommittal, with Angell and Jordan stressing price stability as the objective; no tightening signal is given.
- Direction confirmation: the forward signal is at or slightly above neutral because members resist an easing implication but add no tightening commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-12, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Greenspan's draft: "The effects of changes in taxes and spending on the economy are complex and difficult to predict." Greenspan on the long-rate decline: "There is absolutely no way we can know."
- Center of gravity: several members stress that the fiscal effects and the cause of the bond rally are unknowable and could reverse, but this is not unusual uncertainty about the economic outlook itself.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is above the "normal" band and below "elevated/high" because the doubt is explicit and recurring but narrow in scope.
