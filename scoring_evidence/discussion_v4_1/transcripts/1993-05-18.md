# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, 1993-05-18 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript with staff turns removed. It opens with procedure (minutes approval, a Broaddus report on a System operating-procedures study, foreign-exchange and domestic desk operations with members' questions). The Chairman then gives a long opening on the price puzzle, followed by the economic go-around and then the policy go-around. It ends with a roll-call vote on an asymmetric directive (alternative B). Some staff answers are missing, so a few member questions appear without replies. This causes no scoring problem. Staff-reported content is ignored.

**2. Era context.** The meeting is in the early 1990s recovery, with the funds rate at 3 percent. Members discuss a recent upturn in measured inflation, soft first-quarter real growth, and fiscal and health-care policy uncertainty. I score what they say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: inflation is not explained by money, credit or slack, so he reads it as inflation expectations and "political pessimism". He says a 3 percent funds rate is "too low" and the question is "when do we move". He recommends no move today, an asymmetric directive toward tightening, and a phone call before any action.
- Hawks: Angell, Mullins, Broaddus, Jordan and Lindsey want to act now. Melzer would "probably move rates up a little right now". Stern, McTeer and Parry lean toward asymmetry or are concerned.
- Doves and cautious members: Forrestal, Boehne, Keehn, Hoenig, Syron, LaWare, Kelley and Phillips stress weak growth, fiscal drag and the risk of looking "trigger happy". Most of them accept asymmetry.
- Corrigan: not persuaded "the genie is out of the bottle", but comfortable with B asymmetric.
- Mullins and Kelley on growth: Mullins sees moderate growth (employment gains, UE down from 7.7 to 7 percent). Kelley sees "slack in the economy".

**4. Core dimensions, direction stated first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see inflation as above the midpoint and above their goal. Broaddus cites core CPI at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate and says it "really bothers me". Mullins says "I think we have 4 percent inflation". Angell says inflation will not reach the 2-1/2 to 2 percent he thought doable. The price data are called "disturbing" and "discouragingly" fast. Offsets: Forrestal, Keehn and Boehne report no pass-through, and Kelley and Corrigan doubt a new trend. The Chairman says the data may be temporary, aside from expectations. This is clearly above target, with some doubt about breadth: about 66.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Slack is below the midpoint. Kelley cites "the fundamentals of the slack in the economy". Greenspan says "labor market slack" and "weak demand". Unemployment is 7 percent, Forrestal says "high level of unemployment", and Kelley says "no excesses". Score about 38. Bright-line: below potential gives under 50, satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Labor is below the midpoint. Unemployment is 7 percent, firms are doing more with fewer people, Phillips says growth is "hardly enough to accommodate even the population growth", and Forrestal sees "no wage pressures at all". Mullins offers upside points (payrolls, record overtime). Score about 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Spending is just below trend. First-quarter growth was disappointing and fiscal and tax uncertainty is freezing investment ("every CEO absolutely frozen"). Autos, housing, trucks and farm equipment are strong. Net, spending is slightly below trend: about 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Briefly mentioned. Kelley says competitiveness is improving "through productivity", Keehn says steel talks focus on productivity, and Phillips lists "improved productivity" as an offset to prices. It is modestly positive and thin evidence: about 57, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Conditions are somewhat loose, below the midpoint. Banks are "more aggressive" and "shaving rates" (Keehn), Kelley says the credit crunch is easing, Forrestal says credit is improving, and real short rates are negative (Broaddus, Mullins). Mullins calls the capital market environment the one "stimulative" component. Offsets are sluggish loan demand (Boehne) and long bond yields up 25bp. Score about 39. Bright-line: easing and supportive give under 50, satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS. The long bond is discussed as an inflation and credibility gauge (the 25bp ratchet). There is no dysfunction anywhere. Yield moves alone are normal, so the score is 40-ish with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. No direct credit-spread discussion. The reported items are credit easing, banks cutting rates, strong inflows into junk bond funds (Syron), and a wide prime-to-cost-of-funds spread reflecting bank margins (Parry, not a risk spread). The level is below normal and favorable: about 38, Low confidence. Bright-line satisfied.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Only a passing remark on funds "going into the stock market" (Mullins) and mutual fund flows (Syron). There is no valuation view, so N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS. Banks are healthy. Hoenig reports record bank earnings and ROA above 2 percent in Oklahoma, and banks are seeking loans. Boehne's weak loan demand is not stress. Score about 26. Bright-line: healthy gives under 50, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. The Foreign Desk discussion and the Chair's account describe intervention against the yen to arrest dollar weakness ("forestalled an erosion"). Kelley mentions a "somewhat softer dollar", Boehne a "weaker dollar", and Angell asks "if the dollar were to weaken". The tenor is weaker or unsteady dollar: about 37, Moderate-Low confidence. Bright-line: weakening gives under 50, satisfied.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- Higher inflation means higher score: matches.
- Slack means a lower gap and lower labor tightness: matches.
- Easy credit and healthy banks mean low scores: matches.
- Softer dollar means a score under 50: matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean. The outcome is no rate change, but the directive is asymmetric toward tightening. The Chair says 3 percent is too low and a move is likely, and the vote is 10-2. Several (Angell, Broaddus, Jordan, Lindsey, Mullins, Melzer) want action now. A sizable minority (Forrestal, Boehne, Hoenig, Keehn, Syron, LaWare) prefer waiting or symmetry. This is hold with a tightening bias and a strong push beyond it: about 64.
- Dissent. There were two formal "No" votes, Angell and Boehne. They come from opposite sides: Angell wants tighter and sooner, Boehne wants no bias. There was also a vote reversal and an open argument over symmetry and procedure, and explicit reservations from LaWare and Phillips. The disagreement extends to the assessment, though the Chair works for unity: about 50.
- Financial stability. Attention is limited to bank lending conditions, capital-market and long-rate discussion (Mullins at length), bond fund flows (Syron), and gold and exchange-rate gauges. It is not a focus: about 20.
- Forward guidance. The asymmetric directive plus the phone-call commitment gives a mild-to-moderate tightening signal: about 68.
- Uncertainty. Many participants stress it: "mystery", "perplexed", "testing the limits of our theoretical knowledge", "widest spectrum of fundamentally conflicting possibilities", "unstable environment". This is high and genuine: about 77.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 (below potential, under 50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 39 (supportive, under 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (favorable, under 50). BANKING_STRESS 26 (healthy, under 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 37 (weakening, under 50). TREASURY_STRESS 40 (no dysfunction, not over 60). All satisfied.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs LABOR 33 vs CONSUMPTION 44: all within 15 points.
- FC 39 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move.
- FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: both low or normal.
- INFLATION 66 vs sub-components goods 60, housing 55, expectations 74: consistent (services N/A). The mix of goods and housing near 58 and expectations well above makes the parent in the mid 60s.

**9. N/A.** EQUITY_VALUATION: no valuation view, only passing fund-flow remarks. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-price commentary by members, so N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 66 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus: "core CPI ... has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate ... that really bothers me." Mullins: "Since I think we have 4 percent inflation." Summary: members see inflation clearly above the goal and no longer falling, with some doubt about how lasting it is. Direction: above neutral because the upturn is called disturbing by most speakers.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley: "the fundamentals of the slack in the economy." Forrestal: "7 percent is not a desirable number." Summary: slack remains, though the economy is growing moderately. Direction: below neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Forrestal: "no wage pressures at all that I can tell." Phillips: growth "hardly enough ... to accommodate even the population growth." Summary: unemployment near 7 percent and cautious hiring mean a loose labor market. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Syron: "every CEO he knows is absolutely frozen." Keehn: auto and truck sales improving, but "at best ... a pretty modest recovery." Summary: investment is held back by uncertainty, offset by autos and housing. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Kelley: competitiveness improving "through productivity." Phillips lists "improved productivity" among countervailing price pressures. Summary: a thin, mildly positive mention. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 39 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Keehn: banks "have begun to shave rates ... have not begun to ease their credit standards." Kelley: "the credit crunch is easing." Summary: credit availability is improving and real short rates are negative. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Mullins and Angell discuss a 25bp rise in long yields as an inflation-credibility signal; nobody describes malfunction. Summary: yield moves with orderly functioning. Direction: normal.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-9 (Low).** Evidence: Syron: "very, very strong inflows into bond funds, junk bond funds." Keehn: banks "shave rates." Summary: favorable credit with no stress signs. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** No valuation view.

**BANKING_STRESS: 26 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Hoenig: "Bank earnings are at record levels ... returns on assets of higher than 2 percent." Summary: banks are healthy and profitable. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 37 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan on the intervention: it "may have forestalled an erosion of some of the gains." Kelley: "a somewhat softer dollar." Summary: the dollar has been soft against the yen, with intervention to steady it. Direction: below neutral.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 60 (steel up about 12 percent for non-contract buyers, durables, lumber and cattle, offset by aluminum and truck prices). SERVICES N/A. HOUSING 55 (Greenspan: owner-equivalent rent "popped up", but the sample is "dubious"). EXPECTATIONS 74 (Greenspan: "accelerating psychological expectations"; Jordan: "I think we have inflation psychology").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan: "a 3 percent funds rate is too low ... The issue is: When do we move?" Broaddus: "a good move today would be to raise the funds rate 1/4 point." Summary: no change today, but a majority backs a tightening bias and several want an immediate move. Direction: above neutral (hawkish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: the vote was 10-2, with Angell and Boehne voting No. Boehne: "The cost of an asymmetric directive ... is not insignificant." Lindsey: "deal psychology a death blow ... now." Summary: two formal dissents from opposite directions, plus open debate on timing and symmetry. Direction: notable disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 20 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Syron on bond fund investors; Mullins at length on long rates and the capital market environment. Summary: brief and contextual, not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan: "go to an asymmetric directive ... a telephone conference" before any action. Summary: an explicit tightening bias plus a commitment to consult, signaling a likely move. Direction: tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 77 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Kelley: "about the widest spectrum of fundamentally conflicting possibilities that I can remember." Greenspan: "we are pretty much testing the limits of our theoretical knowledge" and "an unstable environment." Summary: most speakers say they do not understand the inflation puzzle. Direction: high uncertainty.
