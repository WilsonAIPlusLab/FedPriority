# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2016-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A questions (staff answers removed, so some exchanges are fragmentary), the economic go-round (day 1), the Chair's closing summary, a short monetary policy briefing Q&A, the policy go-round (day 2), a drafting exchange on the statement, and a roll-call read-out. Staff content is ignored; only participants' views are scored.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Late 2016, immediately after the presidential election. Participants describe a rapid post-election rise in Treasury yields, the dollar and equities, and a prospective fiscal expansion. The Committee is deciding on a 25 bp funds rate increase (alternative B).

**Speakers.** Chair Yellen, Vice Chairman Dudley, Governors Fischer, Tarullo, Brainard, Powell, and Presidents Rosengren, Williams, Lockhart, George, Evans, Bullard, Kaplan, Harker, Mester, Lacker, Kashkari.

**Dimension by dimension (direction in words first).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as still slightly below the 2 percent target but rising and closing in. Yellen: core PCE 1 3/4, headline close to 1 1/2. Brainard: core 1.7, "below our target today." Risks are tilting up (Tarullo, Lockhart, Kaplan, Williams "inflation has picked up"). Below 50 but not concerning: 45.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Many say the labor market is at or beyond full employment. Powell: "output is expanding moderately and above potential." Evans: "real GDP growth will run moderately above its potential rate." Yellen sees utilization "more or less back to normal." Brainard and Kashkari still see residual slack. Net modestly above 50 (bright line satisfied by the "above potential" language): 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 4.6 percent, "a bit below most estimates of the natural rate" (Powell). Harker: "essentially, no slack." Lockhart: "as tight as it was before the recession." Williams: "hot labor market." Wage growth is described as still subdued (Yellen, Evans, Brainard). The level (4.6) points to roughly 46-52 on the anchors, but the explicit below-natural-rate and shortage language pushes it up: 57.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption is solid and growth has picked up to a moderate pace. Business investment is weak ("nonresidential investment has not increased in two years," Powell) but is expected to rebound on post-election optimism (Lacker, Lockhart, Dudley). Net at trend: 52.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed as poor. Powell: "poor productivity outcomes." Harker: prolonged low productivity growth from demographics. Bullard: "low productivity growth" regime. Kashkari: "welcome evidence of some pickup." Net below trend: 35.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Rates and the dollar are up, equities are high, and Dudley calls it "a tightening of financial market conditions" but says it reflects optimism, not risk aversion. Evans: "financial conditions and the dollar have already tightened." Mester refers to "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions" and "accommodative financial conditions we have had for quite some time." Kashkari: "doesn't feel like tighter conditions." Net still on the loose side of neutral, and Mester's explicit "accommodative" wording keeps it below 50: 46.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss only yield and term-premium moves (10-year up 60-70 bp), described as policy and inflation-expectation driven. No dysfunction is described. Normal functioning: 42, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed by members in terms of spreads or credit availability. Lacker mentions a flurry of bank borrowing requests, and Mester raises a CMBS refinancing worry, but neither addresses spreads or credit pricing. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities at new highs. Lacker: "exuberance... take with a few grains of salt"; a director calls the euphoria "irrational." Bullard: the rally reflects a corporate tax revaluation, not fundamentals. Mild valuation concern, modestly above fair value: 40.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banks are not described as stressed. Brainard cites low leverage and constrained risk-taking. European bank weakness (Italian NPLs, low capital at some large European banks, "a large bank in Northern Europe") is flagged as a risk by Brainard, Kashkari and Tarullo. Sound domestic system with foreign pockets of stress: 42, Low confidence (bright line satisfied: below 50 for healthy/sound system).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar has appreciated more than 3 percent since October and is expected to strengthen further. Kashkari: the trade-weighted index is up more than 25 percent since mid-2014. Several note it holds down inflation and weighs on trade. Appreciation, so above 50 (bright line satisfied): 66.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods, services and housing are not discussed in category terms: N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: inflation compensation has risen ("welcome") but is "still low by historical standards" (Kashkari, Brainard); Powell says the move "supports confidence that inflation expectations are well anchored." Slightly below target, anchored: 44.

**Tier 3 check in words.**

- Policy lean: unanimous support for a 25 bp hike (alternative B as written). Forward lean is gradual, with a hawkish tilt: Lacker, Rosengren, Mester and Harker want to move away from "gradual" or pick up the pace; Williams, Powell and Kaplan see a faster path as plausible; Evans, Tarullo, Brainard and Dudley stress caution and the lower bound; Bullard says policy is only slightly behind and favors a low path but also floats balance sheet runoff. A moderate hawkish lean around the liftoff anchor: 64.
- Dissent: zero dissents, a unanimous roll call, and "considerable support for alternative B as written." There are emphasis differences about the pace and the "gradual" language (Lacker and Mester vs Evans and Tarullo), but no reservations about today's action (Tarullo: "not going along reluctantly"). Broad agreement: 24.
- Financial stability emphasis: post-election market moves (yields, dollar, equities), Chinese capital outflows, Italian and European banks, leverage and CMBS get meaningful but not dominant attention. Yellen refers to financial stability only in passing. Limited to moderate: 38.
- Forward guidance tone: the statement keeps "only gradual." Several participants want to back away from it at coming meetings, and the Chair signals gradual adjustment, with Fischer expecting no move in January. Mild tightening bias with caution: 62.
- Uncertainty: very high on fiscal and other policy. Fischer: "more uncertainty... than there has been since the beginning of the Great Recession." Dudley: "the uncertainty... is just enormous." Many others stress it, though some (Williams, Bullard) say market gauges do not show elevated uncertainty and the near term is clear. High: 70.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION means more pressure: evidence is slightly below target, so below 50, which matches. DOLLAR_STRENGTH higher means stronger dollar: appreciation, so 66, which matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher means tighter: mixed, with "accommodative" language, so below 50, which matches. BANKING_STRESS higher means more stress: domestic sound, so below 50, which matches. HAWKISH_DOVISH higher means more hawkish: a hike with a faster-path lean, so above 50, which matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 with "above potential" language: pass. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 with "accommodative" language: pass. BANKING_STRESS 42 with sound system: pass. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 66 with appreciation: pass. TREASURY_STRESS 42 with no dysfunction: pass. CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 57: aligned (3 apart). OUTPUT_GAP 54 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 52: aligned. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 with CREDIT_SPREADS N/A: nothing to contradict. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: normal-times behavior (TREASURY_STRESS about 40-50). INFLATION_PRESSURE 45 and INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 44: consistent; the other sub-components are N/A.

**N/A justification.** CREDIT_SPREADS, INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants in the relevant terms.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 +-5 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Brainard: "inflation has been persistently below our target for eight years, and it remains below our target today." Yellen: "core PCE inflation is now running at 1 3/4 percent."
- Summary: Slightly below target and rising, with upside risks noted.
- Direction: Below neutral because core is 1.7-1.75 and headline about 1.5 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Powell: "output is expanding moderately and above potential." Evans: "real GDP growth will run moderately above its potential rate."
- Summary: Economy at or slightly above potential, with a few still seeing residual slack.
- Direction: Above neutral because of the explicit above-potential statements and unemployment below the natural rate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Powell: "unemployment rate at 4.6 percent is now a bit below most estimates of the natural rate." Lockhart: "The labor market looks about as tight as it was before the recession."
- Summary: Labor market at or past full employment, though wage growth is still described as moderate.
- Direction: Above neutral because participants place unemployment below the natural rate with reported worker shortages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Powell: "Consumption accounts for the lion's share of the domestic demand growth and will likely post moderate gains... pace of business investment remains a concern." Kashkari: "solid consumption growth... investment remains worryingly weak."
- Summary: Solid consumers offset by weak business investment, with an expected pickup.
- Direction: At or just above trend (about neutral).

**PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Powell: weak investment "is undoubtedly contributing to the poor productivity outcomes." Harker: "we may be in for a period of prolonged low productivity growth."
- Summary: Productivity is seen as weak, with only a tentative pickup noted by Kashkari.
- Direction: Below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 46 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "if you view it in terms of a financial condition index, it looks to be a tightening of financial market conditions. But I'm not really concerned by this." Mester: "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions."
- Summary: Post-election tightening in rates and the dollar is offset by high equities and optimism, leaving conditions near or slightly easier than neutral.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (mildly supportive) because several members still describe conditions as accommodative and the tightening is read as optimism, not risk aversion.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "The 10-year Treasury yield has increased more than 60 basis points." Lacker: the 10-year "term premium move from negative territory to about zero."
- Summary: Yield and term-premium moves only, with no dysfunction described.
- Direction: Normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** Spreads and credit availability were not discussed by participants.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Lacker: "worth taking the recent outbreak of exuberance with a few grains of salt." Bullard: the rally reflects "lower corporate tax rates, which... would lead to revaluation of the U.S. corporate sector."
- Summary: Equities at new highs with mild skepticism about the justification, but no bubble language.
- Direction: Modestly rich (concern slightly above neutral on the 1-100 scale's low-number side).

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Brainard: market participants "highlighted a low level of leverage... regulatory changes... have constrained... undue leverage in the financial sector." Brainard on Italy: "Nonperforming loans are quite high."
- Summary: US system sound, with foreign bank weakness flagged as a risk.
- Direction: Below neutral because the domestic system is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 66 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar has increased more than 3 percent since October." Kashkari: "The trade-weighted index is up more than 25 percent since mid-2014."
- Summary: Notable dollar appreciation with further strengthening expected and cited as an inflation and trade drag.
- Direction: Above neutral because of appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed by category.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44 (Moderate; value only)**
- Evidence: Powell: "the move up in inflation compensation supports confidence that inflation expectations are well anchored." Kashkari: still "low by historical standards."
- Summary: Rising from low levels, still a bit below target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Lacker: "we may soon need increases in our policy rate that do not qualify as gradual." Mester: "We need to be open to 50 basis point increases if necessary and to ending reinvestments sooner."
- Summary: Unanimous 25 bp hike with gradualism endorsed but a sizeable group leaning toward a faster path, balanced by cautious voices (Evans, Tarullo, Brainard).
- Direction: Above neutral because the Committee is tightening and the forward lean is hawkish-to-neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: Chair: "I did hear considerable support for alternative B as written"; unanimous roll call. Tarullo: "I'm not today going along reluctantly."
- Summary: No dissents and no reluctant supporters; the differences are over the pace and the "gradual" language.
- Direction: Low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-10 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley: the post-election financial-conditions tightening reflects optimism, not risk aversion. Brainard on China, Italian banks and leverage.
- Summary: Market moves and foreign financial risks are addressed by several members as context, not the focus.
- Direction: Limited-to-moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Williams: "we may need to modify the 'only gradual' phrase." Mester: "we do need to find a way to step back from the 'gradual path' language."
- Summary: The statement keeps "only gradual" but many want to move away from it, signaling a tilt toward faster hikes if fiscal stimulus materializes.
- Direction: Above neutral (mild tightening bias).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 70 +-8 (High)**
- Evidence: Fischer: "There must be more uncertainty about economic developments over the next four to five years... than there has been since the beginning of the Great Recession." Dudley: uncertainty about fiscal policy "is just enormous in terms of magnitude, timing, and composition."
- Summary: Pervasive policy uncertainty emphasized by most speakers, tempered by near-term clarity and some saying market gauges are calm.
- Direction: High uncertainty.
