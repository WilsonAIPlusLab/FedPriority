# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 — Meeting 2019-01-30 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two sessions. Day 1 opens with organizational items, a long Q&A and go-round on the long-run balance-sheet operating framework (abundant reserves, "Harker plan" stopping date, MBS cap, wording of a "monetary policy implementation" statement). It then has a financial stability round (Rosengren, Brainard, Quarles) and the economic go-round. Day 2 has the policy go-round on "alternative B" and a short vote read-out. The balance-sheet framework debate is largely technical; I score the economic assessments, financial comments and the policy go-round, and use the framework debate only where it bears on financial conditions, market reaction, or policy lean. This is not a special-topic meeting.

**2. Era context.** Early 2019 per the text: unemployment 3.9%, solid job growth, core PCE about 1.9%, fed funds range 2.25-2.5%, a December rate hike followed by a market sell-off, a government shutdown just ended, trade tensions, Brexit and slowing China/Europe. I score what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Powell, Vice Chair Williams, Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles, Bowman, and Presidents Bullard, Rosengren, Harker, Daly, Mester, Kaplan, Barkin, Evans, George, Bostic, Kashkari. Staff turns are removed.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as at or slightly below the 2% target (below midpoint). Evidence: Clarida, "not yet clear that PCE inflation has moved back to 2 percent on a sustainable basis"; Evans, "inflationary pressures are AWOL"; Powell, "no signs of an unwelcome rise in inflation"; Bullard, expectations imply a miss on the low side. Wages near 3% are said to be in line with productivity, and Mester notes elevated district price pressures. Net slightly below 50, about 42.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is expected to be modestly above potential (Williams, "above my 1.8 percent estimate for potential growth, but by just a smidgen"; Powell, "even if growth remains modestly above potential"; Daly, "slightly above its long-run potential"; Evans, "resource pressures continuing to build"). Brainard says the risks of an overheated economy have fallen. Bright-line: above potential means above 50. About 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the market as tight (above midpoint). Unemployment is 3.9%, the lowest in about 50 years, below the natural rate (Mester, Bowman), claims at a 50-year low, record job openings and shortages (Quarles, Evans). Kashkari and Brainard say "near full employment but not clearly beyond it" and that slack may remain, so this is held below the 66+ band. About 63.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong consumer spending and jobs, but sentiment is falling, housing is declining, manufacturing is softening, and investment plans are cautious (Kaplan, Barkin, Evans). Roughly at or slightly above trend, "solid" (the Chair's word). About 54.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned by a few. Brainard says productivity rose almost 2%; Quarles sees a pickup as important to his outlook; Clarida says wages are in line with productivity; George says productivity is key to real wages. Near trend, slightly positive. About 52, Moderate-Low.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tightened materially since September (Powell, Williams, Daly, Evans), eased somewhat since December (Clarida). Brainard says indexes moved from about 1 SD below average to about 0.5 SD below average, so still looser than average. Williams says levels "still appear to be broadly accommodative". The level is therefore near neutral and slightly loose, and the bright-line rule forbids going above 50 on the "accommodative" language. About 48.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members mention a decline in Treasury market depth in December and lower two-way liquidity (Quarles, Kaplan). These are concerns, not dysfunction. Yields falling is not dysfunction. About 46, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Brainard, "bond spreads are now near the center of their historical range, compared with being well below it previously". Williams, "corporate spreads are below their 2016 levels". Quarles and Kashkari say spreads remain well above last year's lows, and Quarles says high-yield and loan issuance was almost nonexistent in December. The level reading is near normal. Widening is direction only. About 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Williams, "price-to-earnings ratios remain elevated"; Quarles, investor appetite peaked and risk premium rose, then reversed part of Q4 losses; Powell, risk appetite has reverted to normal; Kaplan, the market does not yet fully reflect likely earnings downgrades. Roughly modestly rich to fair, with concern about the Q4 sell-off. About 45.
- BANKING_STRESS: Quarles, "large U.S. banks remain well capitalized"; Brainard has concerns about leveraged loan exposures and wants a countercyclical buffer; Rosengren warns against complacency about European banks; Bowman notes slightly rising ag loan delinquencies. Mostly sound with pockets of concern. Bright-line: below 50. About 36.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Clarida, "lagged effects of the appreciation of the dollar over the past couple of years"; George, "last year's dollar appreciation"; Daly expects further strengthening; Williams sees increased risk of appreciation. Direction is appreciation, so above 50. About 60.
- Sub-components: GOODS: tariffs largely absorbed in margins (Quarles, Evans), steel and input costs easing, oil down, so mildly soft at about 44. SERVICES: N/A (only passing health-care remark). HOUSING: N/A (housing activity discussed, not shelter inflation). EXPECTATIONS: heavily discussed; Clarida, "well anchored but ... somewhat below 2 percent"; Bullard says market measures are uncomfortably low; Williams, "anchored somewhat lower than we'd like"; Mester, stable. About 42.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more. INFLATION: evidence of below-target pressure, so below 50: ok. OUTPUT_GAP: above potential, so above 50: ok. LABOR: tight, so above 50: ok. FIN_COND: slightly loose level though tightened, so just under 50: ok. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; near-normal level, so about 50: ok. DOLLAR: appreciation, so above 50: ok.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds (alt B), drops "further gradual increases", and adopts "patient". Most favor pause and data dependence. Bullard calls policy "mildly restrictive" and wants "if any". Kashkari calls the move a "substantial dovish shift". Brainard talks about taking out insurance. A few keep an upward bias (Quarles) or expect hikes later in the year (Rosengren, Mester). Balanced to lean dovish, about 42.
- Dissent: unanimous 10-0 vote and unanimous support for alt B. Disagreements are about tactics: the word "abundant", the June, September or December stopping date, the MBS cap, the second-bullet wording, whether to scrap the dot plot (Bullard, Quarles, Kashkari against; Evans, Williams, Brainard for), the "if any" clause, and "patient" as forward guidance. Economic views largely shared. About 22.
- Financial stability emphasis: a dedicated financial stability round (leveraged loans, CLOs, European banks, CCyB, run risk in funds, market liquidity), plus extended discussion of the December market turmoil, financial conditions and market reaction to the balance sheet. Prominent, about 62.
- Forward guidance: removal of the tightening bias and the "patient" language is a pause signal. Members describe it as no bias either way, though several note hikes could resume later in the year and that "patient" may be short-lived. Easing lean with data dependence, about 40.
- Uncertainty: heavy. Rosengren, "driving the economy partially blind"; Harker, "ambiguity ... dense fog"; Kaplan, "highly uncertain"; Mester, "considerable uncertainty"; Daly, policy uncertainty near a 15-year high; Brainard, "very elevated". Baseline views are still favorable and the language is partly rhetorical. About 66.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 consistent with "broadly accommodative" levels ok. CREDIT_SPREADS about 50, no "compressed" or "wide" level claim ok. BANKING_STRESS below 50 ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 ok. TREASURY_STRESS about 46, below 60 ok.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs LABOR 63: gap of 9, within 15 (labor is tighter than output gap given UE below natural rate while growth is only modestly above potential). OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION 54 vs 54: ok. FIN_COND 48 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 50: co-move. FIN_COND vs TREASURY 48 vs 46: ok (normal times). INFLATION 42 vs sub-components (goods 44, expectations 42): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no sustained member discussion of services or shelter price inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±5 [High].** Evidence: Clarida, "it's not yet clear, to me, that PCE inflation has moved back to 2 percent on a sustainable basis"; Evans, "inflationary pressures currently are AWOL." Center of gravity: inflation at or just under target with downside concerns. Direction: below neutral because members see core PCE slightly below 2% and no upside breakout.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±4 [High].** Evidence: Williams, "still above my 1.8 percent estimate for potential growth, but by just a smidgen"; Powell, "Even if growth remains modestly above potential." Center: modestly above potential, narrowing. Direction: above neutral (above-potential language).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 ±5 [High].** Evidence: Quarles, "we're benefiting from one of the strongest job markets in decades"; Mester, unemployment "at 3.9 percent, is near its 50-year low". Center: tight, with a minority (Kashkari, Brainard) seeing room left. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bowman, "consumer spending data remain strong but have slowed slightly"; Barkin, businesses "not yet scaling back, are also reluctant to scale up." Center: solid but moderating spending, cautious investment, weak housing. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 52 ±7 [Low].** Evidence: Brainard, "labor productivity rose almost 2 percent last year"; Quarles, "A pickup in productivity growth is an important factor in my own outlook." Center: about trend, mild positives. Direction: at neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 48 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Powell, "financial conditions that have tightened materially since September"; Williams, levels "still appear to be broadly accommodative". Center: tightened sharply from loose, now near neutral to slightly loose. Direction: just below neutral per level language.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 46 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Quarles, "the depth of the Treasury securities market declined in December"; Kaplan, "two-way liquidity ... is substantially lower". Center: thinner liquidity noted, no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 50 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Brainard, "bond spreads are now near the center of their historical range"; Quarles, "spreads on both bonds and loans remain much higher than their lows last year." Center: near-normal level after widening from compressed. Direction: at neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Williams, "price-to-earnings ratios remain elevated"; Kaplan, the market "doesn't yet fully reflect" possible earnings downgrades. Center: valuations somewhat rich but rebalanced after the Q4 sell-off. Direction: slightly below neutral (modestly above fair value).

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Quarles, "large U.S. banks remain well capitalized against their direct and indirect exposures to corporate debt"; Brainard, share of leveraged-loan exposures "nonnegligible". Center: sound system with watch items. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Clarida, "lagged effects of the appreciation of the dollar over the past couple of years"; Daly, "I expect the dollar to strengthen". Center: appreciation, with more expected. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 44.** Evidence: Quarles, firms "absorbing [tariffs] in their margins"; Evans, "significant easing in material cost increases". **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Clarida, "well anchored but appear to be well anchored somewhat below 2 percent"; Williams, "anchored somewhat lower than we'd like."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 42 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Powell, "the case that the economy demands further rate increases has weakened"; Quarles, "I myself retain more of an upward bias to rates." Center: pause, no tightening bias, a few holdouts. Direction: slightly dovish of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: unanimous support for alt B and a 10-0 vote; disagreements on the dot plot (Bullard, Quarles, Kashkari vs Evans, Williams, Brainard) and balance-sheet details. Center: broad agreement on policy with tactical differences. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Rosengren, "Two fault lines ... freeze-up of transactions that occurred in the highly levered lending and high-yield bond markets"; Brainard, "Forced sales could lead to liquidity dislocations." Center: a dedicated round plus pervasive attention to tightened conditions. Direction: prominent topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Clarida, "no particular bias toward raising or lowering rates"; Brainard, removal of forward guidance but "doesn't preclude a return to a funds rate path along the lines of the December SEP later in the year." Center: patient, data-dependent pause. Direction: easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Harker, "Ambiguity is like being in a dense fog"; Kaplan, "I continue to be highly uncertain about the outlook." Center: many stress unusual uncertainty, with a favorable baseline. Direction: high.
