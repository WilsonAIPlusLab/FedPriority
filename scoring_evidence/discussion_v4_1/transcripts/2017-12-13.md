# Part 2: Evidence and scores (v4.1 rubric + V4.1.1 clarifications; participants only)

## Economic conditions
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 35 +-6, High. Kashkari: core PCE "remains low at 1.45 percent... a large shortfall relative to our target"; Yellen: 12-month core "remains disquietingly low-well below our 2 percent objective"; Brainard: "stubbornly soft inflation"; Mester: core PCE "has been 1.4 percent in three of the past four months". Offsetting: Rosengren "3-month ... 1.9 percent", Powell "turning up, albeit just very, very gradually". Below target, expected to firm.
- OUTPUT_GAP 57 +-6, Moderate. Williams: growth "above the economy's potential growth rate", "well past our full employment goal"; Powell: "above-trend pace", unemployment "below all of our individual assessments of the natural rate"; Mester: "above-trend real GDP growth". Offset: Dudley notes manufacturing capacity utilization 76.4 percent, "plenty of manufacturing capacity"; Kashkari "likely below our maximum-employment objective". Mostly modestly above.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 62 +-6, High. Rosengren: unemployment "4.1 percent", will fall "1 full percentage point below" sustainable; Williams: "hot labor market"; Yellen: unemployment "almost 1/2 percentage point below my assessment of its longer-run sustainable level"; Kaplan: Texas "3.9 percent", "majority of businesses report challenges hiring workers"; Mullinix: "We can't find workers." Dissenting view: Kashkari "no evidence of wage pressures building", wages about 2.5 percent. Tight but wages subdued.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60 +-6, High. Evans: Caterpillar "blowout", Discover "best in 10 years"; Brainard: equipment and intangibles "8.5 percent annual rate", "Consumer sentiment remains buoyant"; Quarles: "Investment has been fairly robust this year"; Mester: "holiday sales have been stronger than anticipated". Bostic/Harker/Evans note business caution on tax effects. Above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY 40 +-8, Moderate. Mester: "low productivity growth we have seen over the expansion"; Bullard: "probability is still in the 90 to 95 percent range that we're in the low productivity growth state", recent "improvements... only from very low levels"; Kashkari: "uptick in productivity, 1 1/2 percent". Modestly below trend.

## Financial conditions
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 27 +-6, High. Brainard: "financial conditions that remain very accommodative by historical standards"; Powell: "Accommodative financial conditions have supported demand"; Dudley: "financial market conditions today are even easier than when we began to remove monetary policy accommodation in 2015"; Rosengren: "financial conditions have eased further". Below 50 (bright line met).
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 +-8, Low. Yield curve flattening discussed at length (Bullard, Kashkari, Dudley "yield-curve flattening is not particularly pronounced... 40th percentile"; Quarles, George) but as a recession-signal and term-premium question; no dysfunction, liquidity or auction problem described. Orderly, normal functioning.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 22 +-6, Moderate. Brainard: "Corporate bond spreads remain below longer-run averages, especially in the case of high-yield bonds, for which spreads are near historical lows"; Powell: "credit spreads narrowed"; Rosengren: "credit spreads have narrowed". Stated level below average, so below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION 30 +-8, Moderate. Rosengren: "already-high P/E ratios"; Mester: "equity prices that appear high in relation to earnings", "high stock market valuations"; Kaplan: "elevated levels of today's real asset and financial markets versus historical measures". Valuation concern stated, tempered by Kashkari "stock market remains strong". Elevated.
- BANKING_STRESS 30 +-8, Moderate. Mester: "There doesn't seem to be excessive leverage, and banks have relatively high levels of capital and liquid assets"; Quarles: prolonged inversion "would have very negative effects on bank profitability" (a conditional concern). Healthy, below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 36 +-8, Moderate. Brainard: "the dollar's depreciated about 1/2 percent since our previous meeting and about 6 percent so far this year", "further weakening of the dollar"; Powell: "the dollar has moved down over the course of the year". Weakening, below 50 (bright line met).

## Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS 45. Dudley: manufactured goods prices "do not appear to be sensitive to wage costs", "significant excess capacity in U.S. manufacturing"; George: "nonpetroleum import prices... have started to rise"; Evans: steelmakers "unable to make the higher prices stick". Mixed, slightly soft.
- INFLATION_SERVICES 52. Dudley: "higher wages are associated with higher services prices"; George: core PCE supported by housing services. Roughly stable to modestly firming.
- INFLATION_HOUSING 56. George: "housing services inflation... has trended higher to an annualized rate of about 3 1/2 percent over the past six months". Single speaker.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 38. Evans: "inflation expectations are too low to be consistent with our symmetric objective"; Brainard: "Michigan consumer surveys continue to show inflation expectations at subdued levels"; Kashkari: "Measures of inflation expectations remain low"; against: Dudley "broadly stable", George "remained anchored", Mester "reasonably well-anchored". Anchored, but some below target.

## Discussion-specific
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 64 +-6, High. 25bp hike to 1.25-1.5 percent supported by 7 of 9 voters, with the median path at three hikes in 2018: Yellen "perhaps three hikes in 2018 might be appropriate"; Rosengren "five 1/4 point change increases"; George "four rate increases for next year"; Dudley "risk... on the side of a more rapid pace". Moderating voices: Harker "two rate hikes in 2018", Bullard "too 'hawkish' for 2018", Evans "pause until mid-2018", Kashkari "alternative A".
- DISSENT_INTENSITY 52 +-6, High. Two formal dissents in the roll call (Evans No, Kashkari No) in a 7-2 vote, opposing the hike on grounds that inflation and expectations are too low; Bullard "we're too 'hawkish' for 2018" and Harker "somewhat disappointed" with statement language are reservations; disagreement extends to the assessment of slack (Kashkari "likely underestimated labor market slack" vs Williams "well past our full employment goal"), but exchanges were courteous.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 48 +-8, Moderate. Rosengren: leveraged-loan covenant arbitrage, "financial stability risks posed by maintaining low interest rates"; Mester: "lofty" commercial real estate, "financial stability risks may be building"; Kaplan: "excesses and imbalances... likely to build", "heightened vigilance"; Quarles on bank profitability and transmission; yield curve and financial conditions discussed repeatedly. Several address it meaningfully but it is not dominant.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66 +-7, Moderate. Yellen: "perhaps three hikes in 2018", "a somewhat steeper path of the federal funds rate over the next few years may be called for", "if inflation remains soft... fewer rate increases might be called for"; Bullard and Brainard want to remove "likely to remain, for some time, below levels... in the longer run" language; Mester "gradual removal of accommodation". Gradual tightening signal, data dependent.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 42 +-8, Moderate. Yellen: "considerable uncertainty attends any assessment of the medium-term macroeconomic effects of the changes to fiscal policy"; Quarles: "very high level of uncertainty" on pass-through rate change; Powell: "inherently uncertain"; Bostic "article of faith"; inflation puzzle and r* debated; but growth outlook described with fairly high confidence and risks as roughly balanced (Williams).
