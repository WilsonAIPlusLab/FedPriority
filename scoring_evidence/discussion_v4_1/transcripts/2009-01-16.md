## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document read to the last line ("END OF MEETING"). No vote read-out and no policy go-around are present. Content: Bank of America ring-fence Q&A, go-around on an explicit inflation objective and communication, and discussion of meeting structure and balance-sheet oversight.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 +-8 Moderate.** Yellen: "most of us anticipate a period that may be quite extended in which inflation will be below the mandate-consistent rate"; Bernanke: "we do face, if not deflation, certainly some disinflation"; Kohn: "inflation and output [are] weak over an extended period"; Bullard: five-year TIPS "at minus 35 basis points... making me nervous", "Japanese-style deflation". Below target with meaningful deflation risk. Some members also voice medium-term inflation worry from the balance sheet (Yellen, Plosser, Bernanke), which keeps the score off the floor.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 22 +-10 Low.** Yellen: "huge adverse shock", "enormous negative shocks to the economy over the past year"; Cumming: "steep global downturn"; Evans: "economy in the doldrums". Large slack is implied, but the output level is not discussed directly, so confidence is Low.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Labor markets not discussed (only long-run unemployment projections).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: N/A.** Spending and investment not assessed by participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Only a passing generic reference (Yellen, "low productivity growth" in a wage-rigidity argument), not an assessment.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 80 +-8 Moderate.** Bernanke: "fight this incredible crisis", "prevent a financial meltdown", "the preferred capital approach is reaching its limits. The markets don't view preferred capital injections as being a perfect substitute for common equity"; Fisher: "We have a financial crisis upon us. I do not believe that has been alleviated, and I think we are just at the beginning of the game." Tight and crisis-level conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Treasury market not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** No participant mentions spreads, yields, premiums, lending standards or credit availability (TIPS breakevens are an inflation measure, not credit). Staff and Desk text is absent.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Equity markets not discussed as a market (Lacker's "equity investors scared off" is about bank recapitalization).

**BANKING_STRESS: 82 +-7 High.** Bernanke: "very sick firms that require emergency assistance", Citi and BAC "obviously under serious stress"; Lacker: "debt-overhang problem", BofA "tangible common equity ratio", Merrill losses of "$10 billion or $15 billion in just a couple of weeks"; Hoenig: "resources that are themselves unsound". Severe stress in large banks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed (Yellen's remark on falling consumer prices and energy and food costs is aggregate, not goods-specific).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36.** Kohn: "Expectations are already well anchored in the United States" but "a threat of a bigger problem" from "declining inflation expectations"; Yellen: "dispersion... now very large", "a period... below the mandate-consistent rate"; Bullard: "expectations are extremely fluid", TIPS at minus 35 bp. Anchored but at risk of unanchoring to the downside, with some upside worry. Value only.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: N/A.** No policy-stance go-around and no policy vote. Remarks about policy (Yellen "pulling out all the stops", Rosengren "remove the accommodation quickly enough") are in the context of communication design, not a stance discussion.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: N/A.** No vote. The disagreement was about the timing of adopting an inflation target (not a policy decision): most favor in principle; Warsh, Duke, Fisher, Evans and Hoenig (conditional) say the timing is poor; no formal dissents. Recorded in the note rather than scored.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 +-10 Moderate.** The opening Q&A on the Bank of America deal and "too big to fail" is a full segment (Fisher, Rosengren, Lacker, Hoenig, Warsh, Bullard); Fisher: "financial crisis upon us"; Warsh: "the very nature, role, and responsibilities of the Fed are central and are being asked about"; Duke: "almost impossible to articulate the role of capital in the financial system". The main agenda item is the inflation objective, so the emphasis is substantial but not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-12 Low.** Yellen: wants "guidance on the path of future short-term interest rates", noting "the market... has priced in a fairly steep upward trajectory to the path of the funds rate, at least relative to the Greenbook"; Bernanke: "communicate that we will be effective and timely in removing that stimulus"; Rosengren: "we will remove the accommodation quickly enough". Mild easing-leaning guidance interest with an exit-credibility counterweight, with no explicit rate signal; Low confidence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-10 Moderate.** Duke: "a fairly high level of uncertainty and a low level of confidence in our forecasts, and we are responding... with untested tools"; Bernanke: "relationships between our tools and our objectives are much less clear even to us than normal"; Yellen: "inflationary psychology... doesn't correspond well to our theoretical models"; Bullard: "expectations are extremely fluid"; Fisher: "just at the beginning of the game".
