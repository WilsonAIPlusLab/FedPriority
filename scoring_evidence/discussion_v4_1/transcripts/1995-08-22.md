# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1995-08-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Transcript of participants' turns only (staff turns removed). It holds opening remarks, members' questions to staff, the economic roundtable, a Chairman closing statement, a go-around of "I support your proposal" statements, and a vote read-out (11 Yes, no dissent). Policy per the Chairman: "B" and symmetrical, i.e. no change. Minor extraction gaps: staff answers are missing, so some questions are one-sided. One passage in McTeer's remarks is garbled ("sales of nationwide have been weaker than expected"). It does not affect scoring.

**Era context.** Mid-1995, after the 1994-early 1995 tightening and a recent 25bp easing, with a second-quarter growth pause and an inventory correction. I scored from what participants say.

**Speakers.** Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Boehne, Forrestal, McTeer, Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan, Stern, Lindsey, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, Yellen, Greenspan. Questions came from Minehan, Parry, Stern, Blinder, Moskow, Hoenig, Forrestal, Lindsey, Broaddus and Melzer.

**Dimension directions (stated before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above the price-stability goal but receding. Hoenig expects core "not much below 3 percent". McDonough puts core at about 3 percent. Phillips says the outlook is "in the vicinity of 3 percent... some distance to go". Melzer says inflation is "not price stability". On the other side, many say pressures are subsiding and wages are contained. The net is a little above midpoint, so 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Near potential. Hoenig says "at or above capacity". Phillips says growth "at potential". Blinder says GDP is below potential by a hair (0.4 percent) at the horizon, with a minute overshoot earlier. Greenspan says real pressure on the upside is "a good distance away". I put it at 49, which is inside the 48-52 band.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor is somewhat tight. UE is 5.7 percent, below the Greenbook NAIRU of 5.9 that McDonough cites. Phillips calls it "historically low". Stern says labor is in "relatively scarce supply". There are scattered shortages (Moskow, Melzer, Jordan, Broaddus), but wages are not accelerating and shortages have diminished (Forrestal). Parry and Phillips doubt that 5.7 percent understates slack. The 5.7 percent level pulls toward the low 40s on the generic table, but participants frame it as at or just below the natural rate, so 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is at trend and resuming after the pause. Yellen cites "continued strength of consumption and investment". Phillips says "both consumer and business spending have resumed". Autos and housing are mixed, and Phillips and Kelley flag consumer debt. I score 51.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Greenspan argues measured productivity is understated and a real acceleration is under way. Jordan says the numbers are "consistently coming in so much stronger than people expected". Phillips notes a commitment to productivity improvement. Kelley and Parry express caution. I score 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Somewhat loose to neutral. Phillips cites a low cost of capital, a strong stock market, a steeper yield curve and unwidened spreads. Credit demand is reasonable, and Forrestal reports very strong bank competition on terms and price. Offsets are the 35bp backup in long rates (Broaddus, Yellen) and tighter apartment-construction standards in Dallas (McTeer). I score 45.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The bond backup is a yield movement driven by expectations. No dysfunction is described, so N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Phillips says "Yield spreads have not widened". Forrestal reports easy credit terms. There is no level language and no direction of widening, so I score 42 at low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: The stock market is "robust" (McDonough) and "paused but didn't tank" (Phillips). Greenspan notes market-to-book ratios. There is no valuation concern, so 47.
- BANKING_STRESS: Loans are growing healthily (Hoenig, Melzer 15 percent). Competition is strong. There is minor caution in apartment lending (McTeer). Concern about Japanese banks is foreign. So 35 at low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen cites "significant appreciation of the dollar". Minehan and McDonough discuss the intervention and the stronger dollar. McDonough thinks the current level is more rational and still attractive. The bright line puts the score above 50, so 58.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods, services and housing are touched only in anecdotes, so N/A. Expectations: Melzer says they "continue to exceed current inflation" at 3-4 percent. Broaddus reads the bond backup as an inflation-expectations change. Jordan sees growing credibility. So 60 (mildly elevated).

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: The vote is unanimous for no change with a symmetrical directive. Hawkish voices are Melzer (credibility, against alternative A easing), Hoenig and Broaddus. Blinder, Yellen, Greenspan and Lindsey stress that the real funds rate is above neutral and should eventually fall, especially after the fiscal contraction. Balanced to slightly dovish, so 47.
- Dissent: 0 dissents. Melzer's pushback on real-rate analysis is a tactical reservation. Differences are about emphasis only. I score 15.
- Financial stability: Only brief to moderate mentions. These are the bond-market backup, possible market disruption from the fiscal showdown (Blinder, Greenspan), and Japan's banking sector. 1993-96 typical is 1-10, so 12.
- Forward guidance: "B and symmetrical" with contingent decisions and possible October conference calls. Eventual real-rate declines are noted, but no direction is committed. 46.
- Uncertainty: Fiscal "train wreck", "no historical precedent", "who knows what the distribution looks like" (Greenspan). Yet most say risks are balanced and the outlook is good. Moderate, so 52.

**Polarity and bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP 49 is in 48-52, consistent with near-potential language. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 45 is under 50, consistent with low cost of capital and ample credit. CREDIT_SPREADS 42 is under 50, consistent with unwidened spreads. BANKING_STRESS 35 is under 50, consistent with healthy lending. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 is over 50, consistent with appreciation. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 49 and LABOR 54 differ by 5. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION 51 differ by 2. FC 45 and spreads 42 co-move. INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 and expectations 60 are consistent.

**N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction or functioning commentary. INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: only scattered anecdotes with no overall assessment.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig, "I do not expect core inflation to move much below 3 percent". Phillips, "the outlook for inflation still seems to be in the vicinity of 3 percent". Summary: inflation sits modestly above goal, though many see it receding. Above neutral because the level is about 3 percent with a stated distance to go.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 49 ±5 Moderate.** Evidence: Blinder, "GDP is below potential by just a hair". Phillips, "growth outlook reasonably close to potential". Summary: the economy is close to potential. At neutral.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 54 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Stern, "labor does remain in relatively scarce supply". Phillips, "the unemployment rate is historically low". Summary: labor is somewhat tight without wage acceleration. Slightly above neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 51 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "continued strength of consumption and investment spending". Phillips, "Both consumer and business spending have resumed". Summary: spending is at trend after a pause. At neutral.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 62 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "an acceleration of productivity". Jordan, "consistently coming in so much stronger than people expected". Summary: several see stronger than expected productivity. Above neutral.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 45 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Phillips, "a fairly low cost of capital". Forrestal, "very, very strong competition" among banks. Summary: conditions are somewhat loose; the backup in long rates is a partial offset. Below neutral.
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not discussed as a functioning issue.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 42 ±8 Low.** Evidence: Phillips, "Yield spreads have not widened". Summary: spreads are at favorable levels with no stress. Below neutral.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 47 ±6 Low.** Evidence: McDonough, "the stock market is quite robust". Phillips, "paused but it didn't tank". Summary: no valuation concern is expressed. Near neutral.
- **BANKING_STRESS 35 ±8 Low.** Evidence: Hoenig, "loans at our banks have resumed growing at a healthy pace". McTeer, banks "tighten standards for apartment construction loans". Summary: the system looks healthy with minor pockets of caution. Below neutral.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "a significant appreciation of the dollar". McDonough, current exchange rate levels are "more rational". Summary: the dollar has strengthened since July. Above neutral.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 60.** Evidence: Melzer, "expectations for future inflation continue to exceed current inflation". Summary: moderately elevated expectations.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 47 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "'B' and symmetrical seems to me the most sensible approach". Melzer, "incomprehensible to me that we somehow could cut short rates again". Summary: hold, balanced, with an eventual-easing tilt from some.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 15 ±5 High.** Evidence: all 11 voters vote Yes; every member says "I support your proposal". Summary: near-consensus, with only tactical reservations from Melzer.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 12 ±5 Moderate.** Evidence: Blinder, financial markets "could truly be rattled" by the fiscal event. Broaddus, the long-rate backup. Summary: only brief mentions, so low attention.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 46 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "several telephone conference calls in the month of October". Greenspan, the real funds rate is "somewhat higher than we are likely to want it to be... down the track". Summary: the signal is neutral, with no commitment.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 52 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "who knows what the distribution looks like". Greenspan, "I don't recall any historical precedent". Summary: elevated on fiscal outcomes, though risks are called balanced.
