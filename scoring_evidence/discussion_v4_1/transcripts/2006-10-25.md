## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document type: transcript discussion (economic go-around, policy go-around with vote, then a long communications/price-stability session). Not special-topic: it has a policy vote (hold at 5 1/4, Lacker dissenting) and a stance go-around. The communications session was ignored for condition dimensions. Staff turns are removed, so some member questions have no visible answers.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 64 +-6 High
- Plosser: core CPI "edged up to 2.9 percent, a rate that I consider well above price stability"; Yellen: "measures of consumer price inflation remain uncomfortably high"; Lacker: "I'm deeply concerned about inflation"; Bies: "I am very worried about inflation".
- Bernanke: "the general view, which I think essentially everyone shares, was that the upside risks to inflation exceed the downside risks to growth"; also "the direction is actually very good" (core PCE 3-month 2.20). Clearly above target but easing.

OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-6 Moderate
- Yellen: "we're in the vicinity of full employment" and "the moderate amount of excess demand that appears to be in the economy"; Bies: "housing is running below trend but the rest of the economy is running at or above potential".
- Kohn: "running modestly below the rate of growth of its potential", but "how far the economy is running below potential in an underlying sense is uncertain"; Minehan: "our sense of any gap in resource usage remains about the same". Growth is below potential; the level is near or slightly above potential.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-5 High
- Fisher: "we're behaving as though we were a full-employment economy" (welder wages $19 to $25); Hoenig: "Unemployment rates are low... shortages of skilled labor"; Bernanke: "Most people noted that the labor market is quite healthy, with widespread shortages"; "we have very tight labor markets". Yellen: unemployment 4.6 percent, directors "almost uniformly" say labor markets are tight; Warsh: "the labor market may actually be tightening".

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-6 Moderate
- Lacker: "Consumer spending is holding up well... business investment spending continues to grow"; Bies: "Consumer spending is good, and business fixed investment is very sound"; Yellen: "Consumption spending seems on track for healthy growth".
- Offsets: Plosser: other sectors "growing somewhat below trend"; Warsh: Greenbook has "the general slowdown in business fixed investment... about right"; housing and autos weak. Net about trend.

PRODUCTIVITY: 47 +-8 Low
- Minehan: "Corporate-driven productivity growth, though we haven't seen it escalate recently, could come to the rescue... hard to bet on that"; Pianalto: contacts "think productivity gains will keep costs in check"; Bernanke: "the behavior of productivity [is an] important unknown". Thin evidence, roughly trend.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6 High
- Minehan: "the very accommodative nature of financial markets"; "Financial markets remain quite accommodative"; Kohn: "growth supported by favorable financial conditions"; Geithner: "exceptional factors supporting what the markets call liquidity"; Warsh: companies have "plenty of ammunition in terms of their access to capital markets".

TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Members mention the inverted yield curve and TIPS spreads only as policy and expectations signals (Poole, Warsh); nothing on Treasury market functioning or dysfunction.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-10 Low
- Geithner: "Equity prices, credit spreads, and market interest rates all reflect somewhat less concern about both recession and inflation risks"; Yellen: "risk spreads, suggest some optimism" (direction only, no level).
- Availability side: Minehan "financial markets remain quite accommodative"; Geithner on abundant liquidity. The score rests on direction plus availability language, not on a stated spread level (V4.1.1 rules 3b and 3c).

EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-7 Moderate
- Warsh: "the growth in the equity markets is all about earnings and not about multiples"; Barron: "equity markets not only are at record highs"; Kohn: "equity prices are vulnerable to disappointing earnings as labor costs rise"; Kroszner: tension "if the markets start to come down and profits aren't quite as robust". A valuation view exists but is mildly cautious, not stretched.

BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 Low
- Bies (supervisor): "overall mortgage credit quality is still very, very strong", "predatory lending only in pockets"; but 2005-vintage early delinquencies are higher, "implies a loosening of underwriting standards". Poole: small builders "have perhaps six months" before bankruptcy. Banks themselves are not discussed as stressed.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- No participant discusses the dollar's direction (only passing mention of dollar-asset demand in Geithner's liquidity list and exchange rate in a staff question).

INFLATION_GOODS: 38
- Bernanke: "costs of raw materials and energy are rising more slowly or are declining"; Pianalto: contacts "not indicating much of an impetus for higher final goods prices"; Fisher: Chinese steel dumped at prices 40 percent lower.

INFLATION_SERVICES: 62
- Barron: "Core inflation, especially in the service price component, continues to drift upward"; labor-cost and wage-premium pressure (Fisher, Warsh, Bernanke "wage growth probably presents the biggest upside risk").

INFLATION_HOUSING: 64
- Fisher: OER "high threes, upward"; Pianalto: "rent part of the picture will [not] quickly fade"; Kohn: "rent-of-shelter component has been increasing less rapidly"; Kroszner: "temporary elevation in owners' equivalent rent... seems to have come off a bit". Elevated but decelerating.

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 56
- Mishkin: "inflation expectations are solidly grounded"; Plosser: "long-run expectations have been stable"; Lacker: market expectations "clustered around 2 1/4 percent", above his goal; Warsh: relevant expectations "may have deteriorated much more substantially than the TIPS markets... suggest". Anchored but a bit above target, with concern.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +-5 High
- Action: hold at 5 1/4 (10 yes, Lacker no, favoring a hike: "a further tightening is needed"; "I favor alternative C"). Forward: keep "additional firming that may be needed" language; Poole alone argues for a symmetrical outlook ("my policy outlook going forward is symmetrical around 5 1/4"). Geithner: "the signal needs to be asymmetric"; Hoenig: "would support additional tightening should inflation reverse course". Hold with a tightening bias.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-6 High
- One formal dissent (Lacker, hawkish: "I'm going to be outvoted again"); Kohn rebuts Poole's symmetry argument ("I don't agree with his conclusion"); otherwise wording-level differences (B vs B+, "moderate"). Economic assessments are largely shared.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 +-8 Moderate
- Financial-market conditions and housing-credit risk come up from several participants (Warsh on debt markets, Geithner on liquidity and spreads, Bies on mortgage quality, Poole and Minehan on builder defaults), but as context for growth and inflation, not as a focus. Bies and Mishkin mention financial stability in the communications session.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-6 Moderate
- Statement language kept: "The extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed"; Bernanke: "we can continue with our verbal tightening"; Yellen leans B+ "upward bias for future rate changes"; Geithner: plausible policy "lie above what's now priced in the market". Poole's symmetrical outlook is the dissenting view on guidance.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +-7 Moderate
- Yellen: "there's uncertainty in all directions", "bimodal economy", "I don't have confidence in the scenario underlying" inflation decline; Kroszner: "a lot of uncertainty with respect to both the economy and inflation", "I don't understand the path or the dynamics"; Geithner: "too soon to be confident"; Barron: "uncertainty with regard to the degree of the slowdown". Many caveats, but the tone is soft-landing and the outlook is described as "pretty good", so only moderately elevated.
