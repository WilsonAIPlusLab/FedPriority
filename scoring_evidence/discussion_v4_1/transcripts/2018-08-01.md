# Part 2: Evidence and Scores (v4.1 rubric + V4.1.1 clarifications; SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

Document type note: not special-topic. It has a monetary policy go-round and a vote (8-0 roll call at the end of the file). The opening toolkit/effective-lower-bound session was ignored for condition dimensions (treated as a framework session at the start of a normal meeting), except where members spoke about current conditions.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +-5 High
- Brainard: core PCE "very close to our target", Dallas trimmed mean "somewhat below the target at 1.8 percent". Gould: "effectively reached our inflation target", "growing resource constraints are likely to push it up further". Kashkari: "very close to our inflation target", core PCE 1.9. Quarles: "Inflation pressures remain subdued." Mester: inflation "near 2 percent" but "stubbornly below target" earlier, with "upside inflation risk". Harker: "highest readings on prices paid and prices received". Bostic/Barkin/George: tariff and input-cost pass-through.
- At target, modest upside tilt.

OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +-6 High
- Gould: "operating beyond potential and expected to stride further past it". George: "output moving further above potential". Powell: GDP growth "well above potential". Brainard: "above-trend growth will continue"; Williams: growth "far above my estimate of 1 3/4 percent for potential". Dissenting-tone views (Kashkari: "still slack in the labor market"; Powell: "at least not overheated") are minority.
- Above potential, bright line satisfied (>50).

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 64 +-5 High
- Rosengren: "tight labor markets", NH/VT/ME unemployment "below 3 percent". Mester: "moving the economy further beyond maximum employment". Evans: Manpower "conditions are as tight as they have ever seen". Williams: unemployment to "a touch below 3 1/2 percent... strongest labor market we will have seen in 50 years". Brainard: job openings ratio "highest we have seen since 2000". Powell: unemployment 4 percent, "at or beyond maximum sustainable employment" but "no meaningful or sustained acceleration in wages". Kashkari/Quarles: slack remains (minority).
- Level of tightness (UE about 4 percent, many say beyond full employment), tempered by modest wages.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 High
- Brainard: "real GDP rose at a robust 4 percent annual rate", "private domestic final purchases... increased 4 percent". Williams: consumer spending "off to the races", business investment "on a strong upward trajectory". Mester: "both consumption and business investment were strong". Bostic: "demand remained robust", investment "moderate gain". Trade uncertainty a drag on investment plans (Barkin, Evans), Harker: residential weak.
- Above trend on net.

PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +-8 Moderate
- Mester: "low productivity growth is likely to constrain" wage increases. Bullard: "slow productivity growth, which is not expected to pick up meaningfully". Kaplan: "sluggish" workforce growth and "lagging education and skill levels... affecting productivity". Brainard: productivity gains "moving back toward pre-crisis levels" (mildly offsetting).
- Modestly below historical trend.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-6 High
- Brainard: "overall financial conditions remain quite supportive of real GDP growth". Mester: "Monetary policy and financial conditions remain accommodative". Quarles: "favorable financial conditions". Powell: tightening financial conditions only abroad (emerging markets).
- Accommodative/supportive, bright line satisfied (<50).

TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 Low
- Only yield-curve/term-premium discussion: Bullard "longer-term yields are not rising in tandem with the policy rate", Barkin "term premium... currently negative", Brainard "10-year Treasury yield was unchanged". No member describes market dysfunction; Mester lists Treasury valuations "elevated" only as a valuation remark.
- Normal, orderly functioning; scored with Low confidence because functioning itself was not discussed.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-8 Moderate
- Brainard: "spreads on leveraged loans and the securitized products backed by those loans are very low"; "asset valuations and risk appetite now exceed the 90th percentile". Quarles: "weakening in lending standards"; "7 is the new 6"; "strong demand for leveraged loans". Mester: "banks are beginning to lower lending standards". George: "underwriting standards are easing".
- Stated low level of spreads plus ample credit; below normal, bright line satisfied (<50). Spread wording present (Brainard), supplemented by availability language.

EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-8 Moderate
- Mester: "Asset valuations in equity, corporate bond, and Treasury securities markets are elevated above historical norms"; "Valuations are high". Quarles: "Asset valuations are elevated across a broad range of assets, but no major asset class is caught in a wild speculative bubble". George: "asset prices appear elevated". Brainard: equity prices "up about 3/4 percent since June".
- Elevated but not bubble-like valuation view.

BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-7 Moderate
- Brainard: "our supervised banking institutions remain well capitalized"; but capital ratios "flatlined", "some banks may well need capital buffers that are somewhat thicker". George: "large bank capital levels and capital quality have improved" but payouts "run counter to financial stability aims". Quarles: "financial-sector leverage is very low", "the financial sector appears ready to absorb, rather than unusually amplify, that shock". Barkin: possible agricultural-lending spillover.
- Well capitalized with minor concerns; bright line satisfied (<50).

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-5 Moderate
- Brainard: "the dollar has appreciated by about 1 percent since our June meeting and 4 percent so far this year"; "6 percent appreciation... against the Chinese renminbi". Kaplan: "increase in the value of the trade-weighted dollar... some effect on export growth". Evans: "dollar appreciation... still downside factors".
- Modest appreciation, bright line satisfied (>50).

INFLATION_GOODS: 62
- Bostic: contacts "able... to pass along input cost increases", tariffs on steel and aluminum. Harker: prices received highest "since June 2008", prices paid highest "since February 1989". George: "ISM input price index... highest in seven years". Barkin: steel/aluminum prices up but offsetting oversupply abroad ("don't see much net effect on inflation").

INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A (no member discussion of services prices as a category)

INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A (house prices discussed only as asset valuation/financial stability, not rent or shelter inflation)

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42
- Brainard: longer-run expectations "remain somewhat below our 2 percent objective", "moving in a positive direction"; staff-model clustering is staff content, ignored. Kashkari: "stable, but... somewhat low by historical standards". Bullard: "TIPS-based inflation expectations remain subdued". Evans: "low inflation expectations are still downside factors". Mester: long-run expectations "broadly stable".

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 +-6 Moderate
- Action: unanimous hold ("I support alternative B"), but Williams: "a rate increase will be appropriate at our next meeting... another rate hike... in December". Powell: "continued gradual increases"; "I would likely see it as appropriate to raise the target range... at that meeting [September]". Mester: "continue on its path of gradually raising the federal funds rate". Rosengren: "continue to tighten gradually". Counterweights: Bullard "no need to be preemptive", "overkill"; Kashkari "I don't see any evidence... that we should be moving to a contractionary policy stance"; Kaplan "cautious as we remove accommodation".
- Hold now, majority favors further gradual tightening; a minority urges pausing near neutral.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +-6 Moderate
- Vote 8-0 (roll call at the end, all Yes). Disagreements: Bullard ("the market view is about right", asks for a "stopping rule"), Kashkari ("outlier" on dropping "accommodative" now; "still slack"), versus Rosengren/Mester wanting continued tightening; split on timing of removing the "accommodative" sentence (Williams/Brainard soon; Rosengren/Kaplan/Harker later). Differences on assessment of slack but no dissent.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +-8 High
- Dedicated financial-stability round: Rosengren (money-market fund reform), Mester ("increased attention to financial stability risk is warranted"), George, Quarles ("Our greatest risk is complacency"), Brainard (leveraged loans, countercyclical buffer). Also Mester and Rosengren in policy round: "financial stability risks will build", "more quickly unraveling financial stability". Most other members did not comment.
- Moderate, several participants at length.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 +-6 Moderate
- Williams: "assuming the economy performs about as expected, I anticipate that another rate hike will be appropriate in December". Powell: "consistent with continued gradual increases"; September hike likely; "something that we ought to do later this year" (drop "accommodative"). Statement keeps "further gradual increases". Offsetting: removal of "accommodative" and debate over stopping near neutral (Kaplan, Bullard, Kashkari), George wanting "greater data dependence and less certainty regarding the future rate path".
- Clear but gradual tightening path signalled.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +-8 Moderate
- Trade: Bostic "continuing uncertainty about trade policy"; Powell "outcomes... remain so uncertain"; Powell "the outlook is perhaps a bit more uncertain". Yield curve: Barkin "a great deal we don't understand". Neutral rate: Kashkari "huge uncertainty associated with the models"; Williams "great deal of uncertainty about the level of the neutral rate". Balanced against: Powell "risks roughly in balance", Mester "risks... broadly balanced", Williams "risks... balanced". Toolkit-session uncertainty about ELB tools not counted.
- Somewhat elevated, mostly trade and neutral-rate uncertainty.
