# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2015-10-28 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It contains: (a) a long session on equilibrium real interest rates (r*) with member questions and remarks, (b) member questions in the financial developments / debt-limit / financial-stability session, (c) the full economic go-round (two days of the meeting; Chair's summary at the end of day one), (d) the policy go-round on statement wording (alternatives B and C), and (e) the roll-call read-out. Several staff answers are removed, so some exchanges are one-sided fragments. I scored what members said about conditions and policy and ignored pure procedure (wording votes on "slightly", "at its next meeting", "based on incoming data").

**2. Era context.** Fall 2015: policy rate at the zero lower bound since 2008, liftoff delayed in September because of global/China turmoil. Backdrop in the text: strong dollar, low oil, soft manufacturing, unemployment 5.1%, core PCE inflation about 1.3-1.4%. I scored from the text.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish / "liftoff now or soon": Lacker (supports C, the one No vote), Williams (prefers C, will go with B), Harker, George, Lockhart, Mester, Bullard (dovish liftoff in December, but also worries about getting stuck at ZIRP), Kaplan, Rosengren, Powell (leans to December if data hold).
- Cautious / dovish: Brainard (wait another meeting or two), Evans (December premature, wants a shallow path of about three hikes through end-2016), Tarullo (show-me, would have preferred no language change), Dudley (supports B, wants a Plan B easing menu), Fischer (supports B but says consider expansionary measures if no December move).
- Outlier dovish: Kocherlakota (final meeting) recommends a new round of asset purchases and announcing intent to explore negative rates. Chair Yellen: December is "live" but undecided; wants above-trend growth and labor-market improvement; unemployment gap possibly more than half a point; gradual path.

**4. Directional statements (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as below the midpoint (target). Core PCE is "well below our target" (Yellen, Brainard 1.4%), "Inflation is well below target" (Kocherlakota). Most expect a return to 2% as transitory energy/dollar effects fade, but "transitory" does not lower the score. Some firming in core/trimmed measures (Mester, Kaplan). Below target, not deflationary: low 30s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: slightly below the midpoint. Growth "near or only slightly above trend" (Yellen), "slack remains" in some measures (Rosengren, Brainard, Yellen), but Lacker says slack is "all but gone" and Williams expects overshooting next year. Net: slightly below potential. Bright line (slack remains means below 50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: near the midpoint. Unemployment 5.1%, claims at 1973 lows (George), elevated openings, worker shortages in pockets (Lacker, Kaplan, Mester), but subdued wage growth and elevated U-6 and involuntary part-time (Rosengren, Brainard, Yellen). Roughly at NAIRU, a hair below the midpoint.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: modestly above the midpoint. Private domestic final purchases about 3-3.9% ("robust" - Rosengren, Lockhart; "strong" - Lacker, George, Powell), vehicle sales at record, housing improving, offset by weak net exports, energy investment and manufacturing.
- PRODUCTIVITY: below the midpoint; repeatedly described as weak or disappointing (Fischer, Powell, George, Rosengren).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: roughly neutral. Several note stress receded since September (equities retraced, VIX down, "more favorable readings in a variety of broad indexes" - Williams), while Evans and Rosengren report tighter credit and wider spreads, and Tarullo/Brainard warn that liftoff may tighten conditions more than 25bp suggests. No member calls conditions "accommodative" or "tight" as a general verdict. Policy stance is excluded.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Rosengren says spreads moved from "unusually low levels" to "multiyear highs last seen several years ago," high-yield up 267bp since June 2014, though "significantly below the peaks seen during the financial crisis." Evans: wider bond spreads in health-care and tech. A stated high level relative to recent years, below crisis. Somewhat wide: above 50 (bright line satisfied). Powell notes EME corporate spreads declined; minor.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciated markedly over the past year (about 10%; Brainard, Yellen) and held down net exports; recently stabilized or partly reversed (Lacker, Mester). Above 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: only Kaplan gives valuation commentary (market "quite overvalued", 19x declining earnings, "full"); others note the August selloff and the partial recovery without valuation views.
- BANKING_STRESS: lightly discussed: QS shows financial-sector vulnerabilities as "moderate" with "almost nothing that looks dangerous" except consumer credit and CRE (Fischer); large capital increases; bank lending "growing rapidly" (Harker, Evans). Low stress.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no market dysfunction discussed (Treasury yields appear only in a hypothetical, and inflation-breakeven discussion is about expectations). N/A.
- INFLATION sub-components: goods: price declines from energy/dollar/import prices (Lockhart, Kaplan "price declines for the ninth consecutive month"). Services: mixed (Kaplan service selling prices up 3-3.5%; Williams on weak health-services prices). Housing/shelter inflation: not discussed as inflation (house-price valuation is a stability topic, not shelter inflation): N/A. Expectations: survey measures stable and "anchored" (Mester, Yellen) but long-run market breakevens "near or at historical lows" (Kocherlakota), "troubling" (Dudley), Bullard worried; net below-target-leaning but anchored.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more of the construct. INFLATION_PRESSURE: evidence "below target" means less pressure, so score below 50: matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: evidence "multiyear highs" means more stress, above 50: matches. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciation means above 50: matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher means more stress/undervaluation; Kaplan's overvaluation concern puts it below 50: matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: policy held at zero but the Committee adds an explicit December reference ("at its next meeting"), a clear tightening bias; one hawkish dissent (Lacker wanted C), a dovish tail (Kocherlakota QE/negative rates, Fischer/Dudley contingency easing). Hold with tightening bias: lean hawkish, mid-50s.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (hawkish). Visible camps (early-liftoff group vs show-me group) and a far-dovish outlier, plus a long wording fight, but broad agreement on alternative B and a gradual path. Moderate: about 40.
- Financial stability emphasis: dedicated QS/financial-stability session with about eight speakers (Williams on CRE/house prices, Dudley on Puerto Rico, Rosengren on credit spreads, Kaplan on activism/carry, Fischer, Kocherlakota, Tarullo); Mester and Powell cite building imbalances; the August turmoil features in the go-round. Moderate to fairly prominent: about 58.
- Forward guidance: statement signals December is live; consensus on a gradual or shallow path; Yellen says it "very well may be appropriate to move." Mild tightening bias, tempered by shallow-path emphasis: about 62.
- Uncertainty: r* highly uncertain ("great uncertainty" - Fischer; "highly uncertain" - Yellen), mixed labor data, global risks, inflation-model dispute; but many also express reasonable confidence. Elevated: about 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 45 (<50 with "slack remains": ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 52 (no explicit accommodative/tight verdict; ok). CREDIT_SPREADS 58 (stated multiyear-high level: >50 ok). BANKING_STRESS 35 (<50 for sound/moderate: ok). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 63 (appreciation: >50 ok). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (45) vs LABOR (48): within 15. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION (56): an 11-point gap, explained by strong final demand coexisting with weak net exports and the view that slack is only gradually closing. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (52) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (58): co-move roughly; spreads are the tighter element, offset by equity recovery and lower volatility. FC vs Treasury: Treasury N/A, no crisis. INFLATION_PRESSURE (33) vs sub-components (goods 28, services 42, expectations 34): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member describes Treasury market functioning problems. INFLATION_HOUSING: shelter/rent inflation not discussed as a price category (house-price valuation talk is a financial-stability topic).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "Inflation is well below target"; Brainard: "measures of underlying inflation trends remain below our 2 percent target ... between 1 1/4 and perhaps 1 3/4 percent."
- Summary: Core inflation of 1.3-1.5% is well below target; expected return to 2% is not scored.
- Direction: below neutral because nearly all participants describe inflation below objective, though not deflationary.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 45 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: growth "near or only slightly above trend" and "we still have a ways to go" to full employment; Lacker: "slack is still gone."
- Summary: Slack is diminishing but most still see some remaining; Lacker and Williams are the above-potential voices.
- Direction: below neutral because most say some slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: George: "initial claims ... levels last reached in 1973" and economy "likely close to full employment"; Rosengren: "U-6 ... still needs to decline further."
- Summary: Unemployment 5.1%, near NAIRU, with elevated openings and pockets of shortage but muted wages and elevated U-6.
- Direction: at or marginally below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: private domestic final purchases "grew at a robust 3.9 percent rate"; Dudley: "domestic final sales still seem to be rising relatively briskly."
- Summary: Strong consumer and housing demand net of weak energy investment and exports.
- Direction: modestly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 33 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fischer: "structural productivity growth having been so weak in recent years"; Rosengren: "relatively sluggish productivity growth holding potential down."
- Summary: Productivity growth viewed as weak, though a staff pickup is projected.
- Direction: below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "more favorable readings in a variety of broad indexes of financial conditions"; Evans: "tighter credit conditions in the United States ... wider bond spreads."
- Summary: Stress receded since September but credit pockets tightened; roughly neutral.
- Direction: at neutral, with offsetting views.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no dysfunction discussed).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "credit spreads reaching multiyear highs last seen several years ago," though "significantly below the peaks seen during the financial crisis"; Evans: "wider bond spreads for domestic health-care and technology firms."
- Summary: Spreads moved from unusually low to the highest in several years, below crisis levels.
- Direction: above neutral because the stated level is multiyear-high; far from crisis.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kaplan: "The U.S. stock market leads the list ... trading at about 19 times 2015 earnings ... This multiple really strikes me as full."
- Summary: Only one clear valuation view (rich); other mentions concern the selloff and recovery.
- Direction: below neutral because of overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fischer: QS "overall assessment being that risks and vulnerabilities are moderate" and "very large increases in capital"; Harker: "bank lending has been growing rapidly."
- Summary: Banking system seen as sound with isolated CRE/consumer-credit flags.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 63 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "marked appreciation of the dollar over the past year"; Lacker: "Dollar appreciation has reversed course."
- Summary: Strong appreciation over the year, partly stabilized since September.
- Direction: above neutral because the dollar has appreciated.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 28** - Lockhart: subtarget inflation "mostly due to a stronger dollar and falling global commodity prices"; Kaplan: manufacturing "price declines for the ninth consecutive month."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 42** - Kaplan: service "selling price index still went up 3 percent in September and 3 1/2 percent in October" versus Williams: weak health-services prices pulling down core.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** - shelter inflation not discussed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 34** - Mester: "survey-based measures have been broadly stable"; Kocherlakota: "inflation breakevens are near or at historical lows."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "it very well may be appropriate to move at our next meeting"; Kocherlakota: "I would recommend that the FOMC initiate a new round of asset purchases."
- Summary: Hold at zero with an explicit December tightening bias; many want to go now, a few want more accommodation, one extreme dove.
- Direction: above neutral because the majority leans toward tightening soon.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Roll call: Lacker "No" (supports alternative C); Kocherlakota urging QE and negative-rate exploration versus Williams/Lacker "the case to raise rates is still strong."
- Summary: One formal hawkish dissent, a wide spread of prescriptions, but broad agreement on B and a gradual path.
- Direction: moderate disagreement, above the near-consensus range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kaplan: activist capital and financial engineering "getting to be dangerous"; Powell: "concerned with the buildup of financial and economic imbalances ... at the zero lower bound for an extended period."
- Summary: A dedicated financial-stability session with many speakers, plus the financial-stress discussion in the go-round, though the policy debate centers on macro data.
- Direction: attention (not direction) moderate to fairly prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "December is a 'live' possibility, but also ... no decision has been made"; Williams/Rosengren/Kaplan: signal a "shallow path" after liftoff.
- Summary: Begin-normalization signal, tempered by emphasis on a very gradual path.
- Direction: above neutral because the signal points toward tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fischer: "there is great uncertainty about future r*"; Yellen: "the outlook is highly uncertain."
- Summary: Elevated uncertainty over r*, mixed labor data and the global outlook, with many members still expressing reasonable confidence in the baseline.
- Direction: elevated, not extreme.
