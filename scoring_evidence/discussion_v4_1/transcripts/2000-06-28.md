# FOMC member discussion scoring v4.1 + V4.1.1 clarifications — meeting 2000-06-28 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript: a staff Q&A section (member questions only), the economic go-around, a morning session on monetary-aggregate ranges and inflation targeting, a policy go-around, and a vote read-out at the end of the file. The read-out lists ten "Yes" votes and no "No" or dissent. The text has a policy go-around and a vote, so it is NOT special-topic under V4.1.1 rule 1. The range and inflation-targeting segment is a side item of a normal meeting and is ignored for the condition dimensions.

**Era context (kept separate from the scoring).** The text describes a tight labor market (about 4 percent unemployment), a recent run of strong growth, a rise in the funds rate (members refer to "50 basis points ... at our May meeting" and "100 basis points higher" this year), high energy prices, and some early signs of a slowdown.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), McDonough, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Guynn, Stone, Jordan, Broaddus, Stern, Poole, Hoenig, McTeer, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson. Staff turns are absent. I used only member statements, and ignored the Greenbook and Bluebook figures except where members adopt or contest them.

**Dimension-by-dimension direction (stated before the numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core inflation is rising and expected to rise further (Parry: core PCE 2.1 and 2.2 percent "well above last year's 1.5 percent"; Meyer: "inflation is rising ... overall ... and ... core"; Broaddus: "emerging trend of higher core inflation"). Several dissenting tones lower this: Jordan is "not as convinced that inflation is accelerating," McTeer says the trend "does not seem alarming," and Hoenig expects core PCE under 2 percent this year. The energy effect is flagged as part of the increase. I place it in the 51-60 band, near the upper end: 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint. Meyer: "at least as convinced as the staff that output is beyond potential"; Broaddus: "economy is currently operating above its potential." Gramlich doubts the economy is far beyond NAIRU, and growth is slowing toward trend (Hoenig: "only slightly below estimates of trend growth"). Bright line (above potential means > 50) is met: 58.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Far above the midpoint. "Labor markets are still very tight" (Moskow, Stone, Broaddus, Hoenig); Minehan: hard to find workers, 10 to 20 percent increases; Parry: California 5 percent, "near a 30-year low." Some easing is noted (Jordan, Guynn: construction trades, summer help). With unemployment about 4 percent: 72.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is slowing from a very strong pace (autos, housing, big-ticket items) while equipment investment is described as very strong (Greenspan: "backlogs continue to build"; Guynn: "likely to remain strong"). Net slightly above trend: 58.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Productivity gains are clearly discussed as strong. Moskow: "definite acceleration in productivity"; Greenspan: "multifactor productivity has been running at a fairly strong pace"; McTeer: "continued productivity gains." Above trend: 68.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Tightening is described (Parry: "tightening financial conditions"; Guynn: "less accommodative financial conditions"; Hoenig: higher real rates, flat yield curve), while Ferguson notes conditions have become "a little more accommodative" since May and Meyer notes "recent rally in bond and equity markets" unwound some restraint. Net slightly tight: 55. No bright-line phrase ("accommodative" as the level, "restrictive") is applied at the level; Ferguson's phrase describes a direction since the last meeting.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed by members as a market-functioning issue. The Treasury operations Q&A is a question about auction bids and staff content is not evidence; Gramlich's mention of the nominal/real spread is an expectations point. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spreads discussed, but availability language exists: Guynn reports "tightening credit standards and more cautious and less accommodative lending," a "pullback in lending to marginal companies"; Jordan's banker sees slower loan demand; Ferguson notes "net corporate borrowings, driven by bond issuance, seem to have picked up." Under V4.1.1 rule 3 this rests on availability only: 54, wide band, Low confidence. Bright line is not triggered (no stated spread level).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members mention equities as a feature of the outlook: "equity values have leveled off and in some cases declined" (Stern); Stone's contact "the days of irrational exuberance are over and now we just have exuberance"; Jordan "People have learned that stock prices can go down"; Greenspan notes tech stock prices "come down so markedly." This is a mild valuation-concern view with an orderly decline, roughly offsetting: 47, Low.
- BANKING_STRESS: Guynn: lead bank examiners are told "their best days in terms of credit quality may be behind them," lending is cautious; Stone's bankers see borrowers falling short of revenue targets. System described as sound with mild concern: 42, Low (brief and anecdotal). Bright line (sound means < 50) is met.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar is described as strong relative to what the model assumes. McDonough: "we could still maintain a considerably stronger dollar than in the model"; Greenspan: "the strength of the dollar is an indirect piece of evidence ..." Strength implies > 50 (bright line met), but there is no discussion of recent appreciation or an export drag: 60, Moderate.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS: energy and gasoline prices up, some steel softening and "no subsiding of competitive pressures" (McTeer): 57. SERVICES: health care costs "escalating at a faster pace" (Guynn, Stone), wage pressure anecdotes: 58. HOUSING: Minehan reports shelter costs rising in Boston, but housing price appreciation is slowing: 55. EXPECTATIONS: "remarkably stable" (Gramlich), "haven't moved up very much" (Ferguson), Stone "stable," offset by Broaddus's concern about falling behind the curve: 51.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: All ten voters support no change (hold) now. Greenspan wants the upside-risk statement kept: "probability that we are through tightening ... has to be less than 50-50." Hawkish voices: Kelley ("may indeed need to tighten more--perhaps quite soon"), Broaddus ("a solid case could be made for a further move today"), Meyer ("data are likely to show the wisdom of moving further perhaps as early as the next meeting"), Minehan, Guynn ("better than even chance that we'll need to tighten at least a bit more"). Dovish-leaning about the next move: McTeer, Hoenig, Gramlich ("it is possible that the Fed has already done enough"), Jordan. Hold with a tightening bias: 56.
- Dissent: Zero dissents, a unanimous roll call. There are differing views on the NAIRU framework, on the weight of recent data (Stern and Poole against Meyer and Gramlich), on whether to tighten now (Broaddus, Kelley) and on the inflation target (Poole, Meyer). Mostly differences of emphasis and model, not policy action; the Chairman characterizes the Committee as closer together than any earlier FOMC: 22.
- Financial stability: Only passing mentions (credit standards, equity values leveling off, sentiment shift, Greenspan's tech stock remark, Jordan on risk repricing): 18.
- Forward guidance: Retain the asymmetric statement that risks are toward heightened inflation; Greenspan: be careful not to suggest the last move was the last in the cycle. Kelley, Guynn, Ferguson, Stone: "do not signal that we've concluded tightening." But the immediate action is a pause: 66.
- Uncertainty: A great deal of the first day is about NAIRU and productivity uncertainty. Greenspan: "wider range of uncertainty about the specification"; McDonough: "humble about the reliability of the forecast"; Kelley: "maybe, maybe, maybe"; Meyer: "high order uncertainty"; Greenspan: "I know of no evidence that conclusively would suggest which of those two scenarios is true." Many speakers make this point but the language is largely about model structure rather than a Knightian outlook: 62.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says rising inflation, so above 50. Higher OUTPUT_GAP = more above potential; evidence says beyond potential, so above 50. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence is tightening standards offset by strong bond issuance, so near 50 on the stress side. DOLLAR: strong = above 50.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 58 vs LABOR 72 differ by 14 (<15; labor is at record tightness while growth is slowing toward trend and Gramlich/Hoenig doubt the economy is far above potential). OUTPUT_GAP 58 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 58 agree. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 55 and CREDIT_SPREADS 54 co-move. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 is consistent with the sub-components (57, 58, 55, 51), the parent being slightly higher because members weight core price trend and energy.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member discussion of Treasury market functioning.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 ±8 (Moderate-High).** Evidence: Parry, "We expect the core PCE price index to increase 2.1 and 2.2 percent this year and next, well above last year's 1.5 percent rate"; Meyer, "the data on prices are clear and that inflation is rising." Center of gravity: rising, modestly above comfort, with a few members less worried. Direction: above neutral because most see an upward trend.

**OUTPUT_GAP 58 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer, "I am at least as convinced as the staff that output is beyond potential"; Broaddus, "economy is currently operating above its potential." Center: modestly above potential while growth moderates. Above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 72 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "our labor markets are still very tight"; Minehan, "it remains difficult to find workers of almost any type ... 10 to 20 percent increases." Center: unusually tight with a few signs of easing. Above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 58 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "demand for motor vehicles clearly is easing ... the orders coming into the capital goods companies ... have remained very strong"; Guynn, "business investment spending ... is likely to remain strong." Center: slowing consumer and housing against strong investment. Above neutral, slightly.

**PRODUCTIVITY 68 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "definite acceleration in productivity in their industries over the past three years"; Greenspan, "Multifactor productivity has been running at a fairly strong pace." Center: strong productivity is assumed by many, but doubted for the forecast (Broaddus). Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 55 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "deceleration is due mainly to tightening financial conditions"; Ferguson, "financial market conditions ... have become, if anything, a little more accommodative." Center: tightened over the year, partly reversed since May. Slightly above neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS 54 ±10 (Low).** Evidence (availability only, no spread wording): Guynn, "more reports of tightening credit standards and more cautious and less accommodative lending"; Ferguson, "Net corporate borrowings, driven by bond issuance, seem to have picked up again." Center: modest tightening of bank credit while bond market is open. Slightly above neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 47 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Stone relaying a contact, "the days of irrational exuberance are over and now we just have exuberance"; Stern, "equity values have leveled off and in some cases declined." Center: froth fading, no sharp correction or distress. Near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS 42 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Guynn, "their best days in terms of credit quality may be behind them"; Stone, "business borrowers are more frequently falling short of their revenue targets." Center: sound system, minor concerns. Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "we could still maintain a considerably stronger dollar than in the model"; Greenspan, "the strength of the dollar is an indirect piece of evidence." Center: strong dollar assumed to persist. Above neutral.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 57 (energy and gasoline up; steel prices turned back down per Greenspan and Jordan). SERVICES 58 (health care costs escalating, Guynn; Stone). HOUSING 55 (Minehan: Boston shelter and fuel costs; Parry and Guynn: housing slowing). EXPECTATIONS 51 (Gramlich: "remarkably stable"; Ferguson: "haven't moved up very much").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 56 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the wise move at this stage is basically to do nothing today ... risks remain unbalanced toward ... heightened inflation"; Kelley, "We may indeed need to tighten more--perhaps quite soon." Center: unanimous hold with a clear tightening bias and a few voices wanting more. Hold with tightening bias lean.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 22 ±6 (High).** Evidence: roll call of ten Yes; Greenspan, "we are closer together than any earlier FOMC I have been associated with"; Broaddus, "a solid case could be made for a further move today. I can certainly accept your proposal." Center: broad agreement on the action, model and target differences aired.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 18 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn on credit standards; Jordan, "I think we have delivered a very effective and important wake-up call" on risk-taking. Center: brief, scattered mentions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 66 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the probability that we are through tightening in this cyclical expansion has to be less than 50-50"; Ferguson, "we should be careful not to signal that we're done." Center: retain an upside-inflation-risk bias; no immediate move.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 62 ±8 (Moderate-High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "wider range of uncertainty about the specification"; Meyer, "The new data at the very least raise the degree of uncertainty about the forecast." Center: pervasive uncertainty about NAIRU, productivity and the slowdown, expressed by many but with decision-making continuing.
