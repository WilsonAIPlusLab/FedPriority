# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2014-01-29 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two sessions. Day 1 opens with organizational items (officer elections, authorizations, the longer-run goals statement, a vote on an overnight reverse-repo facility resolution), then members' questions to staff, then the economic go-round. Day 2 has the Chairman's summary, a policy go-round on "alternative B", a short wording discussion, and a roll-call read-out (10 Yes, no dissents). Staff turns are removed, so some members' replies sit without the questions. I score only what members say. The only garbled bit is "at itg" in Yellen's remarks, which does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Early 2014: the Committee is in the middle of the asset-purchase taper and has 6.5 percent unemployment threshold guidance. The text itself shows unemployment at 6.7 percent, PCE inflation near 1 percent, a strong second half of 2013, a weak December payroll report, and emerging-market turmoil. This is Bernanke's last meeting. I score from what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Doves or caution: Evans, Rosengren, Yellen, Kocherlakota (wants stronger guidance), Pianalto, Tarullo, Lockhart on labor. Hawkish or normalization voices: Plosser, George, Bullard (partly), Lacker, Fisher. Stein and Powell raise financial stability and term-premium or asset-price risks while supporting the baseline policy.

**4. Directional signals by dimension.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as well below the 2 percent target and low (about 1 percent), with little pricing power and low wage and cost pressure, so below the midpoint. Evans: "actual inflation has been stuck near 1 percent." Yellen: "inflation has been disconcertingly low." Expected to drift back up, but this is not the present reading. Score in the 26-40 band, toward the lower middle.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy below potential. Williams: "considerable slack remains." Yellen: "the absolute level of slack in the economy is probably still quite large." Some (Plosser, Powell, George) argue slack may be smaller than the Tealbook says, but the dominant view is slack. Below 50, 31-40 band.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 6.7 percent, participation falling, long-term unemployed and part-time-for-economic-reasons high, wages muted. A few Texas and construction shortage reports (Fisher "severe labor shortages", Lockhart skilled-worker difficulties). Level reading: UE 5.5-7 percent maps to 31-45. Score about 36.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Second-half 2013 growth near 3.75 percent, private domestic final purchases about 3.5 percent, business optimism, capex plans up; offset by inventory and trade being one-off, cautious consumers (Bernanke), weak retail (Fisher), fiscal drag. Net: at to modestly above trend, about 55.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not assessed by members in any substantive way (Dudley says only that productivity growth in 2014 is unknown). N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Dudley: "financial conditions really haven't changed much and still remain broadly supportive of economic activity." Williams: financial conditions for entrepreneurs "more favorable than they have been in years." Powell: high-yield spreads near 15-year lows. Selloff in EM and equities is called small. Below 50 (supportive/easy), about 35.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members mention the 10-year yield falling (Rosengren, Fisher) and term-premium risk (Stein), but nothing about market functioning or dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Powell: spreads for single-B and triple-C bonds "approached 15-year lows, which is to say extremely low, lower even than they were in the pre-financial-crisis bubble." George: "froth" in high-yield issuance and leveraged lending. Stated low level means below 50, bright line satisfied. Score about 25.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher: S&P "two and a half times its March 9, 2009 low," "enormously robust, bullish market," a 10-plus percent correction "would not be at all surprising." Powell on asset prices possibly too supported. Valuation concern is modest but present (stretched, not bubble). Score about 32.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only light mentions: Bernanke's summary that banks "see credit metrics improving"; Lacker's bankers "credit metrics are improving"; Fisher on weak loan growth and large reserves; Puerto Rico monoline contagion flagged by Dudley but expected modest. Healthy with minor concerns, below 50, about 35, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a passing mention of "stronger dollar" in Powell, who is relaying staff factors. Members do not discuss the dollar. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods: Williams "import price inflation has been very low", George "import prices stabilizing", low goods inflation, about 35. Services: medical care CPI smallest December-to-December rise since 1949 (Williams), low services pressure, about 33. Housing: not discussed as inflation, N/A. Expectations: repeatedly "well anchored" (Williams, Plosser, Dudley, Pianalto) but Evans, Rosengren, Bullard doubt how binding anchoring is; Bernanke "somewhat worrisome". About 47.

**5. Polarity verification.** For each: higher = more of the named construct. Inflation 28: members say inflation is low, so less inflation pressure, below 50, matches. Labor 36: slack remains means looser, lower score, matches. Credit spreads 25: spreads extremely low means low stress, lower score, matches. Equity 32: elevated valuation concern is toward the low end by the rubric, matches. Banking 35: healthy means low stress, matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Alternative B, a further $10 billion taper step, with highly accommodative stance retained and purchases continuing. Everyone supports it (Rosengren would have paused, Plosser and George would accelerate in strong data, Bullard "leisurely tapering baseline"). Still easing via purchases, with normalization starting. Moderately dovish, about 38.
- Dissent: zero dissents on the policy vote (10-0 roll call). Two voters opposed the RRP resolution (7 vs 2, with proposed cap amendments losing 6 to 9, and a 4 vs 11 straw show for a different amendment), a procedural split. Differences on the labor-slack interpretation, quantitative vs qualitative guidance (Kocherlakota vs Lockhart/Stein/Williams), taper acceleration, and Tarullo's abstention on the goals statement. Broad agreement with some reservations: about 27.
- Financial stability attention: Dudley (Puerto Rico, positioning), Tarullo (extended EM-contagion analysis), Powell (asset bubble, spreads, wants it addressed in guidance), Stein (term premium, financial conditions conditionality), George (froth), Kocherlakota (financial-stability knockout clause), Bernanke (macroprudential tools). Moderate to prominent, about 50.
- Forward guidance: Existing "well past 6.5 percent", rates near zero into mid-2015, considerable time after purchases end. Discussion is mostly about how to keep it, with proposals to add inflation emphasis (Dudley), lower threshold (Kocherlakota), keep patient (Yellen). A few note rules call for liftoff earlier. Strong easing bias: about 15.
- Uncertainty: Many participants stress uncertainty about trend versus cycle (Rosengren), slack and natural rate (Dudley, Powell), inflation process (Williams, Bullard, Evans, Plosser "wild card"), EM risks (Tarullo), and the labor-market debate. Pianalto says uncertainty is "normalizing from elevated levels". Elevated but not extreme: about 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential: score < 50 (38). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS "supportive": < 50 (35). CREDIT_SPREADS stated extremely low level: < 50 (25). BANKING_STRESS healthy: < 50 (35). DOLLAR and TREASURY: N/A, no violation.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs LABOR 36: aligned.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs CONSUMPTION 55: 17 points apart. Explained: consumption and investment are scored as spending growth relative to trend (recent strong, at or above trend), while the gap is a level measure after a long recovery; both can hold at once.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 25: both loose, consistent.
4. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A, no conflict.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 28 vs sub-components 35, 33, 47: parent is a bit lower than the sub-average because members stress the persistently low realized headline and core inflation (about 1 percent), while expectations are near anchored at target. Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no member assessment. TREASURY_STRESS: yield moves only, no functioning commentary. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: a single relayed staff mention only. INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as an inflation component.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans, "actual inflation has been stuck near 1 percent for some time." Yellen, "inflation has been disconcertingly low over the past year."
- Center of gravity: Realized inflation is far below the 2 percent target with little pricing power or wage pressure.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe a persistent downside miss and low wage and cost pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Williams, "considerable slack remains." Yellen, "the absolute level of slack in the economy is probably still quite large by historical standards."
- Center of gravity: Growth is accelerating but the economy is seen as still below potential, with some dissent that slack may be smaller.
- Direction: Output gap is below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 36 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the fraction of the labor force working part time for economic reasons still stands at 5 percent... long-term unemployment still stands far above its pre-recession level." Fisher, "a growing number of businesses reporting... severe labor shortages" (Texas).
- Center of gravity: Unemployment 6.7 percent with low participation and muted wages, only isolated regional tightness.
- Direction: Labor market is below neutral because unemployment is elevated and wages are flat.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans, "very good fourth-quarter numbers for consumption and fixed investment." Bernanke, "private domestic final purchases... grew at about a 3½ percent rate in real terms in the second half."
- Center of gravity: Spending is solid and firming but partly one-off and consumers remain cautious.
- Direction: Slightly above trend because second-half strength and capex plans outweigh caution.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not assessed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions really haven't changed much and still remain broadly supportive of economic activity." Williams, "financial conditions for entrepreneurs are more favorable than they have been in years."
- Center of gravity: Supportive conditions despite a small EM and equity selloff.
- Direction: Conditions are below the tight midpoint (loose) because members call them supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only yield moves discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell, "spreads for single-B and triple-C bonds have recently approached 15-year lows, which is to say extremely low." George, "froth in various pockets... high-yield bond issuance and leveraged lending."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are seen as very compressed, a stability worry rather than stress.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is extremely low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 32 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher, "enormously robust, bullish market... not at all surprising to see a not insignificant correction in equities—on the order of 10-plus percent." Powell, accommodative policy "could produce a damaging asset bubble."
- Center of gravity: Valuations are viewed as stretched by a few, without bubble language.
- Direction: Below neutral on this scale because concern is about elevated prices.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Lacker, "Several bankers have said that credit metrics are improving and loan pipelines are growing." Bernanke summary, "Banks do generally see credit metrics improving."
- Center of gravity: Banking is mentioned briefly as improving, with only Puerto Rico monoline contagion flagged and expected to be modest.
- Direction: Below neutral because the system is described as improving and sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed by members.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 35.** Williams, "import price inflation has been very low." George, "import prices stabilizing."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 33.** Williams, medical care CPI "smallest December-to-December increase since 1949."
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Williams, "inflation expectations remaining well anchored"; Rosengren, "the longer we miss significantly... the less confidence we should have"; Bernanke, heavy reliance on stability "somewhat worrisome."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "keep policy highly accommodative for quite some time and... be patient in removing accommodation." Plosser, "we are likely going to have to start pulling back on our policy accommodation sooner than we might have once thought... inclined to encourage speeding up the pace of tapering."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous support for another $10 billion taper step while asset purchases continue and accommodation is retained, with a hawkish minority on pace.
- Direction: Moderately dovish because purchases and near-zero rates continue.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll call 10 Yes, no dissents; Rosengren, "though I personally would have preferred to wait, I can support alternative B." Reverse-repo resolution: 7 for, 2 against among voters.
- Center of gravity: Policy agreement, with procedural split on the RRP cap and debate over labor slack and guidance design.
- Direction: Low-to-moderate disagreement because no policy dissents, some reservations.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell, "the danger of financial-stability risks comes now as the recovery strengthens." Tarullo, "I think the downside risk is actually somewhat greater than has been generally recognized" on EM contagion.
- Center of gravity: Several members give meaningful time to EM, Puerto Rico, term premiums and asset prices, alongside macro.
- Direction: Moderate discussion across several participants.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 15 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, guidance "well past" 6.5 percent implies "somewhere in the middle of 2015." Kocherlakota, "keep the fed funds rate extraordinarily low at least until the unemployment rate falls below some threshold lower than 6.5 percent."
- Center of gravity: Strong low-for-long signal, with discussion focused on keeping or strengthening it.
- Direction: Easing bias, with a few voices noting rules point to earlier liftoff.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "pretty uncertain about the ultimate timing of liftoff for short-term rates." Powell, "there is real uncertainty about how close the economy is to potential output."
- Center of gravity: Several members stress uncertainty about slack, inflation process and EM risks, while Pianalto says it is normalizing.
- Direction: Elevated, multiple participants.
