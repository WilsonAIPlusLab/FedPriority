# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2007-08-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed, so Dudley's market update and Madigan's remarks are absent; only members' questions to them remain). It is a short, special-circumstances meeting about financial-market disruption and a proposed change to the discount window plus a proposed FOMC statement. There is no standard economic go-around and no policy-rate decision. The vote read-out is 10 Yes, 0 No. The text is clean apart from missing staff answers.

**Era context (not used to override text).** Mid-2007, financial markets are under acute stress. The members' own words set the scores below.

**Speakers and views.**
- Bernanke: says markets show "panic," "markets seizing up, with good credits not being able to be financed." The economy was strong in Q2 and is at a "moderate pace" in Q3, but the balance of risks has shifted toward softness. He prefers to resist a rate cut for now and to use the discount window.
- Kohn: describes funding uncertainty at banks, a "considerable shortening of the tenor of funding," and "rising spreads" for term bank funding. He supports the discount-window package.
- Yellen: "almost complete illiquidity in the asset-backed commercial paper market"; "we've developed a credit crunch"; "I can easily imagine that we will be cutting the federal funds rate in the not-too-distant future."
- Fisher: banks "are not too sick to lend. They're too scared to lend." He prefers the "surgical" discount-window tool to the "blunt" rate cut.
- Lacker: strong skeptic of the credit-policy action ("sham," "bailout," "not monetary policy"). He says the statement omits inflation, that "downside risks to growth have appreciated considerably," and that he would rather have a rate cut than the discount-window change.
- Mishkin and Moskow: want 75bp rather than 50bp on the discount rate.
- Stern, Hoenig, Rosengren, Plosser, Warsh, Kroszner, Geithner, Lockhart: mostly supportive. Plosser calls it "a bit of wishing and hoping." Stern and Kohn say not to wait for "definitive evidence."

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants barely discuss inflation. The only reference is Lacker's complaint that the statement drops inflation and his wish to show "we're still paying attention to inflation." No level is given. Direction: roughly at the midpoint, with a slight watchfulness. Score about 52, wide band, Low confidence.
- OUTPUT_GAP: No potential or trend language. Only "strong in the second quarter... moderate pace in the third" (Bernanke). Direction: at the midpoint, 50, Low confidence.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: No discussion at all by participants. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: "Moderate pace" (Bernanke, the statement text), with prospective drag from wealth and confidence effects and the mortgage and consumer-credit spillover. No hard evidence of weakness yet. Direction: at trend, slightly leaning down on prospects. Score 48, Low-Moderate confidence.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed, N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as clearly tight: "panic... markets seizing up" (Bernanke), "tighter credit conditions" in the statement, "credit crunch" (Yellen), banks shortening funding tenor (Kohn). Dampening notes: Kohn says "We're not in a panic mode," and Fisher says banks are "scared." This is not a 2008-style freeze. Direction: above the midpoint, tight, with stress emerging. Score 78.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury markets are not discussed (the discussion covers fed funds, ABCP and mortgages). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kohn cites "rising spreads for one-month to three-month or anything past overnight funds for the banks." Yellen says ABCP and asset-backed markets are in "almost complete illiquidity." Bernanke says "good credits not being able to be financed." Lacker says markets "lock up at zero quantities." Rosengren says foreign names pay 25-50bp more in the fed funds market. Level reading: the text describes markets that have seized, which means very wide or unavailable credit, so the level is above normal, not only widening from a compressed base. Direction: well above 50, in the "reduced access / near rationing in ABCP" range. Score 76.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity markets are not discussed (only "wealth effects"). N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Funding-tenor and liquidity uncertainty at banks, and Countrywide tightening standards (Yellen), versus Fisher's "not too sick to lend," Lacker's "they view themselves as well positioned in terms of liquidity," and Kohn's "pretty conservative" collateral margins. Banks are described as hesitant, not insolvent. Direction: slightly above neutral, emerging liquidity-funding stress in a sound system. Score 56, Moderate confidence. Bright line: no "well-capitalized" language that forces <50, and no "stressed or troubled" language that forces >60, so 56 is consistent.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed, N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: not discussed, N/A.

**Tier 3, in words.**
- Policy lean: The funds rate is held unchanged (Bernanke wants to "resist a rate cut until it is really very clear"), but there is an easing tilt. The discount rate is cut 50bp, the statement says downside risks "increased appreciably," and the Committee is "prepared to act as needed." Yellen foresees funds rate cuts soon, and Lacker says "we're going to have to think about a rate cut." Several want an even bigger discount-rate cut (Mishkin, Moskow, Yellen would accept more, Hoenig would have accepted 75). Nobody argues for tightening. This is hold with a mild easing bias plus liquidity easing (credit policy, not monetary policy). Score 38.
- Dissent: The vote is 10-0, with no formal dissent. Differences:
  - 50 vs 75bp on the discount rate (Mishkin, Moskow, and mildly Yellen and Hoenig against Bernanke, Geithner, Stern, Kohn).
  - Lacker's fundamental objection to the credit-policy framework ("sham," "bailout," would prefer a rate cut). He does not vote this year, and Bernanke notes "I hear President Lacker's principal objection."
  - Fisher and Lockhart's wording disputes on "appreciably" (Lockhart wanting "somewhat").
  These are notable differences on tactics, with one substantive framework disagreement but no dissent. Score 34.
- Financial stability emphasis: The entire meeting is about market functioning, the discount window, ABCP, mortgages and bank liquidity. It is dominant, close to "financial stability IS the meeting." Score 93.
- Forward guidance: The statement signals "downside risks to growth have increased appreciably" and "prepared to act as needed," and Bernanke says it "raises the probability of action." Yellen expects cuts. It is an easing-leaning, data-dependent signal. Score 27.
- Uncertainty: Many members stress it. Examples: "we don't know whether or not this will be effective" (Kroszner), "I don't know what probability to assign" (Stern), "no guarantee whatsoever" (Kohn), Plosser's "crap shoot" and "wishing and hoping," Geithner on "the risks and the uncertainty," and "increased uncertainty" in the statement. This is high but not yet Knightian. Score 66.

**Polarity checks.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter. Evidence says "tighter credit conditions," "credit crunch," so it is above 50. Matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more credit stress. Evidence says seizing markets and rising spreads, so it is above 50. Matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. Evidence of funding anxiety offset by "not too sick," so slightly above 50. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH: higher means more hawkish. Evidence is hold with an easing tilt, so it is below 50. Matches.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: no above/below language, so 50 is consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "tighter," "credit crunch," so >50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level stated as seized and locked-up markets with rising spreads, so >50. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: no forcing language, as discussed.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (N/A): no divergence to explain.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (50) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (48): within 15 points.

4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS (N/A): fine.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS is N/A because there is truly zero discussion. INFLATION_PRESSURE was touched on only by Lacker's remark, so it is scored with Low confidence and a wide band.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 ±10 Low.**
- Evidence: Lacker: "it makes no mention of inflation... some observation just so that people know we're still paying attention to inflation."
- Center of gravity: inflation is raised only as a concern the statement should not drop, with no level or trend given.
- Direction: at or marginally above neutral because the only reference is a watchfulness signal, with no stated level.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 ±8 Low.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the economy appears to have been strong in the second quarter. It seems to be running at a moderate pace in the third quarter."
- Center of gravity: activity is described as moderate, with no potential-relative language.
- Direction: at neutral because members call growth moderate and give no gap view.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 ±8 Low.**
- Evidence: the statement: "recent data suggest that the economy has continued to expand at a moderate pace." Bernanke: "significant wealth effects and confidence effects... some spillover into other kinds of consumer credit."
- Center of gravity: spending is at a moderate pace, with prospective downward pressure.
- Direction: at or slightly below trend because the current pace is moderate and members foresee drag.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 78 ±7 High.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "a certain amount of panic; a certain amount of markets seizing up, with good credits not being able to be financed." Yellen: "We've developed a credit crunch."
- Center of gravity: conditions are sharply tight and deteriorating, but members distinguish this from a full panic.
- Direction: above neutral (tighter) because members report a credit crunch and seizing markets.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 76 ±8 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Kohn: "rising spreads for one-month to three-month or anything past overnight funds for the banks." Yellen: "almost complete illiquidity in the asset-backed commercial paper market and in asset-backed markets more broadly."
- Center of gravity: spreads and availability are described as severely impaired in ABCP and mortgage-related markets; the level reading puts this well above normal.
- Direction: above neutral because the text describes seized markets and high term funding spreads, not merely a widening from a tight base.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.**

**BANKING_STRESS: 56 ±10 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Fisher: "banks are not too sick to lend. They're too scared to lend." Kohn: "funding uncertainty in the banks... the banks' drawing back and being potentially concerned about their sources of liquidity."
- Center of gravity: bank liquidity-funding anxiety is clear, but banks are described as sound and hesitant, not impaired.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because funding stress is emerging in a basically sound system.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 ±6 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "we should try to resist a rate cut until it is really very clear from economic data... that it is needed." Yellen: "I can easily imagine that we will be cutting the federal funds rate in the not-too-distant future."
- Center of gravity: rates are held, but the statement, the discount-rate cut and the stated readiness to act tilt toward easing; no one argues for tightening.
- Direction: below neutral (dovish) because the lean is hold with an easing bias, plus liquidity easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 34 ±8 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Mishkin: "I would actually argue that we should move the discount rate down 75 basis points rather than 50." Lacker: "To some extent this looks to me like sort of a sham... I think this will look like a bailout." Vote: 10 Yes, none against.
- Center of gravity: the vote is unanimous, with tactical disagreement on 50 vs 75bp and one non-voter's strong objection to the framework.
- Direction: moderate, not near-consensus, because of several camps on tactics and one fundamental critic, but no formal dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 93 ±4 High.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "financial markets have come under even more stress... we would like in this meeting to discuss possible responses to that situation." The whole agenda is the discount window and market functioning.
- Center of gravity: market functioning and liquidity are the entire subject of the meeting.
- Direction: very high attention because essentially every substantive remark concerns it.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 27 ±7 High.**
- Evidence: the statement: "the downside risks to growth have increased appreciably. The Committee is monitoring the situation and is prepared to act as needed." Bernanke: "it raises the probability of action—no question."
- Center of gravity: the proposed statement signals a higher chance of easing, with a data-dependent framing.
- Direction: below neutral (easing) because the guidance raises the probability of a cut.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±8 High.**
- Evidence: Kroszner: "we are in an uncertain area where we don't know whether or not this will be effective." Stern: "I don't know what probability to assign to it."
- Center of gravity: many members stress that the effectiveness of the action, the market valuation of assets and the economic fallout are unknown.
- Direction: elevated to high because multiple participants stress difficulty, but outcomes are not described as unboundable.
