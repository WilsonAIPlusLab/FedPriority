# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2019-09-18 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) a monetary-policy framework-review session (staff-Q&A by members plus a framework go-round), (b) a short Q&A on financial developments and repo/reserve operations, (c) the economic go-round (a two-day meeting; the economic go-round is on the first day), and (d) the second-day policy go-round, the vote and a secretary's roll call. The framework-review and repo material is not an economic assessment, but the economic go-round and policy go-round are standard, so I score the economic and policy content and do not treat the meeting as special-topic.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2019. The text itself describes: unemployment 3.7 percent, core PCE inflation around 1.6-1.8 percent, an escalating trade war, weak manufacturing and business investment, strong consumer spending, an inverted yield curve, an attack on Saudi oil facilities, and a Committee about to cut 25 bp for the second time in this cycle. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Powell, Williams, Clarida, Brainard, Bowman, Quarles (governors/Board and NY), and presidents Rosengren, Bullard, Daly, Mester, Harker, Evans, Barkin, Kaplan, Bostic, George, Kashkari. Views in brief:
- Economy "in a good place" but slowing toward trend (Powell, Clarida, Williams, Brainard, Bowman). Consumer strong, business investment and manufacturing weak (nearly everyone).
- Inflation below 2 percent, expectations measures low (Clarida, Daly, Kashkari, Brainard, Williams, Evans, Bullard). A minority (Mester, Bostic, Barkin, Harker, Rosengren) point to firming core measures (core CPI 2.4, trimmed means near 2, 3-5 month core PCE above 2).
- Policy: Alt B (-25 bp) for Clarida, Evans, Kaplan, Bowman, Quarles, Daly, Brainard, Williams, Powell, with Barkin reluctantly accepting a cut and wanting a flatter trajectory; Alt A (-50 bp) for Bullard and Kashkari; Alt C (hold) for Rosengren, George, Mester, Harker, Bostic.
- Vote: 7 yes, 3 no (Bullard for -50; George and Rosengren for hold).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (below target) because core PCE is 1.6-1.8 percent ("inflation remains stubbornly below target", Powell; "core inflation remains below target, with 12-month core PCE inflation at 1.6 percent", Kashkari), with the Tealbook never getting to 2 percent. A minority see firming, which lifts the score toward the high 30s rather than the low 30s. "Below target, disinflation" band is 26-40. Score 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as slightly above potential: unemployment below the natural rate (Evans: about 1/2 point below; Harker: "unemployment rate will remain below its natural rate"; Clarida: staff baseline "generates excess demand"), growth at or near trend (Powell: "growing at about its trend rate"; Brainard: "decelerating gradually from its above-trend pace"; George: growing "at an above-trend rate in recent years"). Net: modestly above, decelerating to trend. Bright line requires above 50 given "above trend / below natural rate" language. Score 53.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 3.7 percent, "near a 50-year low" (Mester), "tight by nearly all measures" (Barkin), widespread worker shortages. Offsets: payroll growth revised down, "no longer tightening" (Powell), wages moderate, Kashkari sees "treading water". Level governs; UE below 4 percent. Score 66.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong consumer ("the consumer is the engine of growth", Clarida; PCE about 3 percent, Brainard) against contracting business investment and manufacturing and falling exports. Weak investment gets heavy discussion emphasis, but consumers are two-thirds of GDP and growth is about trend. Net at trend. Score 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mixed. Powell and Clarida cite upward revisions to structural productivity and the supply side surprising to the upside; Daly (g* symposium) lists headwinds and says productivity is "beating your trend" at the same time. Net roughly at trend, thin evidence. Score 50, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as accommodative: "financial conditions are accommodative... easier than for most of the 2015 to 2017 period" (Mester); "highly accommodative levels in terms of aggregate indexes" (Brainard); Harker and Kaplan agree. Bright line: below 50. Score 28.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yield moves (long-end rally, inversion) are explicitly described as fundamentals, flight to safety and technical demand, i.e. not dysfunction. The one real strain is the repo/funds market spike (funds rate trading high in the range, Fed temporary repo operations, "turmoil in this market", "liquidity scrambles"), which is money-market/collateral funding stress rather than Treasury cash-market failure. Score modestly above normal, 54, low confidence. Not above 60 because no auction/settlement dysfunction is described.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Mester: "corporate credit spreads in the United States have increased relatively little and remain at historically narrow levels". Brainard: speculative-grade yields down about 50 bp. Rosengren: corporate bond market "on fire". Level is compressed, so below 50. Score 30.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: "equity prices are near record highs" (Mester), stocks at "near all-time highs" and CRE-related stocks elevated (Rosengren), "some asset valuations remain elevated" (Daly), "elevated risky corporate debt and the associated asset valuations" (Brainard). Valuation concern is moderate, not bubble language. Score 38 (modestly above fair value on the rubric's scale).
- BANKING_STRESS: System seen as sound: "strong capital positions of our major financial institutions" (Daly), "low leverage, low funding risk, and low maturity transformation" (Powell). Concerns are limited to falling capital buffers (Brainard, George) and farm-credit stress (Bowman). Bright line: below 50. Score 34.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar described as strong/appreciating and a drag: "strong dollar" (Brainard), "dollar strengthening somewhat" (Brainard), "dollar appreciation" affecting investment (Daly), goods prices falling "as the dollar has appreciated" (George), stronger dollar an import-price drag (Clarida). Appreciation, so above 50. Score 62.
- Sub-components: INFLATION_GOODS - goods prices "continued to fall as the dollar has appreciated" (George), lower steel/copper/shipping costs (Mester), offset by tariff upside risk; below stable, 42. INFLATION_SERVICES - not addressed as a category: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING - shelter inflation not discussed (only residential investment): N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS - market and survey measures "exceptionally low" (Bullard), "declining" (Kashkari), risk of unanchoring to the downside (Williams, Brainard, Daly), long-run SPF steady at 2 percent: 33.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more credit stress; evidence says spreads are historically narrow, so below 50. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress; evidence says banks are well-capitalized, so below 50. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says accommodative, so below 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher = stronger dollar; evidence says dollar has appreciated, so above 50. All match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: A majority (10 of 17 voting and non-voting participants, including the Chair) favors a 25 bp cut; two want 50; five want to hold, with Rosengren, George, Mester, Harker, Bostic arguing against insurance cuts and Rosengren/Mester/Bostic wanting at most an easing bias. No one argues for tightening. Moderate dovish: 26-40 band. The hold camp pulls the average up; the 50 bp camp and the Chair's "adjustment to a more accommodative stance" pull it down. Score 37.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Three formal dissents (7-3) in opposite directions with different reasoning (Bullard wants 50 bp; George and Rosengren want to hold), plus three non-voters (Mester, Harker, Bostic) who would have held and one (Kashkari) who would have cut 50. Disagreement is mostly about policy tactics, communication and risk management, with broadly shared economic assessments; tone is civil and the Chair acknowledges "the diversity of perspectives". Framework-review debate shows differences on makeup strategies. Multiple dissents with different reasoning, but not heated and not an economic-framework split: lower end of "significant division". Score 64.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Rosengren, George, Mester, Brainard, Barkin, Kaplan and the Chair discuss leverage, corporate debt, reach-for-yield, low-for-long risks and bank capital; Daly rebuts; plus extended discussion of repo/funding-market turmoil and the yield curve. Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the dominant topic. Score 58.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Chair: "another rate cut by the end of the year - although I don't have a strong conviction on that at all", flat path afterwards, "not on a preset course", would respond aggressively to a sharp downturn. Many want optionality (Quarles, Barkin, Mester, Rosengren want an easing bias at most). Easing lean with data dependence. Score 38.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Elevated: trade-policy uncertainty ("fundamental uncertainty about the rules governing international transactions", Brainard), oil shock, Brexit, r* uncertainty (Powell, Williams, Kashkari), "increasingly fragile outlook" (Quarles), recession probabilities of 45-60 percent cited. Offset: many describe the baseline as stable and the economy as healthy. Score 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (>50, language "above trend/below natural rate"): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 (<50, "accommodative"): satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS 30 (<50, "historically narrow"): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS 34 (<50, "strong capital"): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 (>50, appreciation): satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS 54 (below 60; yield moves are not dysfunction): satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 66: 13 points, within 15; labor level is very tight while growth has slowed to trend and staff/members say u* has fallen, so the gap is smaller than unemployment alone suggests. OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50: coherent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 30: co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28 vs TREASURY_STRESS 54: normal times, Treasury stress near 50 and driven only by the repo strain. INFLATION_PRESSURE 38 vs sub-components (goods 42, expectations 33): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no participant discusses services or shelter price inflation as such. Every other dimension is discussed by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +-5 [High]**
- "inflation remains stubbornly below target" (Powell); "core inflation remains below target, with 12-month core PCE inflation at 1.6 percent" (Kashkari).
- Center of gravity: core PCE 1.6-1.8 percent and projected never to reach 2; a minority sees firming.
- Direction: inflation pressure is below neutral because participants repeatedly cite persistent undershooting.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +-5 [Moderate]**
- "I continue to anticipate that the unemployment rate will remain below its natural rate" (Harker); "decelerating gradually from its above-trend pace" (Brainard).
- Center of gravity: economy at or slightly above potential, moderating toward trend.
- Direction: the gap is slightly above neutral because members describe unemployment below the natural rate and growth at/above trend.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 66 +-6 [High]**
- "The labor market remains tight by nearly all measures" (Barkin); "The unemployment rate continues to run at 3.7 percent, near a 50-year low" (Mester).
- Center of gravity: very low unemployment and shortages, tempered by slower payrolls and "no longer tightening" (Powell).
- Direction: above neutral because of the level of unemployment (3.7 percent).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-6 [High]**
- "continued strong consumer spending" against "business investment has been weak and on track for an outright contraction" (both Brainard).
- Center of gravity: strong consumer offsets contracting investment and manufacturing; growth at trend.
- Direction: at neutral because the two forces net out.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- "The supply side of the economy continues to surprise on the upside" (Clarida); "a variety of headwinds to productivity growth" (Daly).
- Center of gravity: mixed signals, near trend.
- Direction: at neutral because favorable data revisions and structural headwinds offset.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-6 [High]**
- "on net, financial conditions are accommodative" (Mester); "financial conditions uniformly have remained at highly accommodative levels" (Brainard).
- Center of gravity: easy conditions from low yields, narrow spreads and high equity prices.
- Direction: below neutral (loose) because multiple members call conditions accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 54 +-9 [Low]**
- "technical factors were prominently cited" for long-end Treasury moves (Brainard, not dysfunction); funds/repo spikes "turmoil in this market" (Brainard) and "liquidity scrambles" (Bullard) prompting Fed operations.
- Center of gravity: cash Treasury market functioning, with money-market/repo strain lifting the score slightly.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because of repo-market volatility, but no auction or settlement dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-6 [Moderate]**
- "corporate credit spreads in the United States have increased relatively little and remain at historically narrow levels" (Mester).
- Center of gravity: spreads compressed, issuance strong ("corporate bond market has also been on fire", Rosengren).
- Direction: below neutral because the stated level is historically narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-8 [Moderate]**
- "equity prices are near record highs" (Mester); "some asset valuations remain elevated" (Daly).
- Center of gravity: valuations viewed as elevated but without bubble language.
- Direction: below the neutral point (more overvaluation concern) because members repeatedly point to elevated valuations.

**BANKING_STRESS: 34 +-8 [Moderate]**
- "the strong capital positions of our major financial institutions" (Daly); "low leverage, low funding risk, and low maturity transformation in the financial system" (Powell).
- Center of gravity: system sound, with concerns on buffers (Brainard, George) and farm credit (Bowman).
- Direction: below neutral because members characterize banks as well-capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-6 [Moderate]**
- "the dollar strengthening somewhat" and "a strong dollar" (Brainard); "dollar appreciation have directly affected their business investment" (Daly).
- Center of gravity: dollar appreciated and is a drag on exports and import prices.
- Direction: above neutral because members describe appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42 [value only]** - "goods prices and the PCE price index have continued to fall as the dollar has appreciated" (George), offset by tariff upside risk.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** - not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** - not discussed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 33 [value only]** - "Inflation expectations... are exceptionally low" (Bullard); "inflation expectations are too low and declining" (Kashkari).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 +-6 [High]**
- "I support alternative B as written" (Clarida, Evans, Kaplan, Bowman, Quarles, Daly, Williams); "I support alternative A" (Bullard, Kashkari); "I support alternative C" (Rosengren, Harker, Bostic, George).
- Center of gravity: majority for a 25 bp cut with a minority wanting a hold and a few wanting 50 bp.
- Direction: dovish because the plurality-to-majority favors easing and no one argues for tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 64 +-7 [High]**
- Roll call: Bullard No, George No, Rosengren No (7-3); Mester: "it's difficult to see how this ends well."
- Center of gravity: three dissents from opposite sides plus additional non-voting holds, but a civil debate on tactics rather than the economic assessment.
- Direction: well above consensus because of multiple dissents with different reasoning.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-8 [Moderate]**
- "Persistently lower rates could increase financial stability vulnerabilities" (Powell); "Reducing rates at the peak of the cycle has more undesirable financial stability features because it encourages more risk-taking" (Rosengren).
- Center of gravity: leverage, corporate debt and funding-market turmoil are raised by many, but alongside the economic and framework agenda.
- Direction: moderate to prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 +-7 [Moderate]**
- "I have written down another rate cut by the end of the year - although I don't have a strong conviction on that at all" (Powell); "we are not on a preset course" (Powell).
- Center of gravity: modest easing lean with data dependence and a desire for optionality.
- Direction: below neutral (easing) because the forward signal is a possible further cut and a flat path.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 [Moderate]**
- "creating what is now looking like fundamental uncertainty about the rules governing international transactions" (Brainard); "an increasingly fragile outlook" (Quarles).
- Center of gravity: elevated trade, geopolitical and r* uncertainty, tempered by confidence that the baseline is stable.
- Direction: elevated, above the "normal caveats" range.
