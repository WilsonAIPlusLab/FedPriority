# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1994-02-04 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript, two sessions (afternoon go-around, then the next morning's policy go-around and a short ranges discussion). Staff turns are removed; members' questions in the staff Q&A are kept. Much of the first day is procedure (officer elections, warehousing authority, disclosure and announcement mechanics), which I ignored except where it shows policy views. Early-era (1994) structure: a regional/national roundtable on day one, an announcement debate, then a policy roundtable on day two.

**Era context.** Early 1994 expansion after a strong second half of 1993; the funds rate has been unchanged for roughly 17 months, and the Committee is considering its first tightening in about five years. I score only what members say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Keehn, Parry, McTeer, Syron, Boehne, Forrestal, Hoenig, Broaddus, Stern, Jordan, Melzer, Kelley, LaWare, Phillips and McDonough describe strong, broadening growth, labor shortages in pockets, and the first signs of price pressure.
- Lindsey raises household income and debt constraints.
- Kelley and LaWare flag a euphoric stock market and consumer debt.
- Parry notes California is still weak.
- Kelley sees "some meaningful soft spots", while Greenspan says recent growth of 4-5% is unsustainable and will come down.
- On policy, all favor tightening. Melzer, Jordan, Broaddus, Lindsey, Hoenig, Stern, Parry, Phillips, LaWare (and McTeer, conditionally) prefer 50bp. McDonough, Boehne, Forrestal, Syron, Kelley and Keehn prefer 25bp. Greenspan insists on 25bp and symmetry, citing the risk of "cracking the markets". The vote is unanimous at 25bp.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core CPI ran 3.4% in Q4 (Melzer, Kelley). Contacts report prices "edging up", commodities are drifting up, and the wage ECI is stuck at 3.5% (Parry). Jordan finds the "no further progress" pattern "very disturbing". Phillips says recent PPI and deflator numbers "look good", and Syron says prices are "well behaved". Level is about 3%, above a sub-2-3% goal and rising at the margin. Score 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as near but still slightly below potential. Parry: the gap has narrowed. McDonough: the gap is "quite small" and will be used up in 1994. Kelley: "I just don't see the economy as overheated... approaching full employment but still with some meaningful soft spots". Bright line: "gap remains" is below 50, so the score is under 50. Score 46.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is near the NAIRU (McDonough: NAIRU "low 6 percent area"). There are widespread shortages of skilled labor, trucking and construction trades, and some districts are under 4% (Jordan). Hiring is still reluctant. Stern says there are "no discernible wage pressures yet". The level of unemployment (about 6.5%) argues for the upper 40s, and the shortage reports lift it a little. Score 48.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as above trend. Greenspan: growth "well over 4 percent... may be running over 5 percent". Kelley: "emerging boom". Keehn: auto schedules 15% higher and retail "surprisingly strong". Phillips: people are dissaving. Lindsey and Kelley note debt constraints, but those are forward caveats. Score 67.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only a passing mention by Phillips and a comment on potential by Syron. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants describe policy as "excessive accommodation" (McDonough), the real funds rate as "quite low" (Broaddus), a "euphoric" stock market (Kelley), strong credit demand and no credit crunch (Jordan), and "strong financial markets continuing to provide financing" (Phillips). Bright line: accommodative gives a score below 50. Score 33.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only a throwaway "long end is now down 3/32". N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are never mentioned. Only credit availability is discussed (strong loan demand, no crunch, lenders flush). Under the level reading this implies availability is ample, so a modest below-50 score at Low confidence. Score 38.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: LaWare says the euphoria has "bloated stock and bond prices beyond rational values". Kelley says "euphoric stock market" and fears a "bust". Greenspan says the market is "at an elevated level... by any measure". Participants see valuations as stretched (the concern is overvaluation, so this is below 50). Score 22.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips says "banks are better positioned to contribute", and Melzer and LaWare report increased loan activity. These are thin but healthy-leaning signals. Score 32 at Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a one-line question "What about the dollar?" by Forrestal. N/A.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: commodity indexes "persistent upward drift" (Kelley), CRB up 13% (Melzer), steel, lumber and concrete up. Score 60.
- Services: not discussed. N/A.
- Housing: rental and apartment rates "edge up" (Melzer, Forrestal), house prices rising, construction material prices up, but the evidence is mostly about construction costs. Score 56.
- Expectations: concern about credibility and "scotching inflationary expectations", with some worry that progress on inflation has stalled. Nobody says expectations are unanchored. Score 55.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Unanimous agreement to tighten for the first time in five years. Ten members preferred 50bp, six or so 25bp, and Greenspan expects more moves. This is a moderate-to-strong hawkish lean. Score 69.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero dissents, with a unanimous vote. But there is a real, extended split on magnitude (about 10 of 18 favoring 50bp), several "can live with 25", and Greenspan asked explicitly for unanimity and said "I rarely ask this". The disagreement is over tactics and market risk, not the economics or direction ("I don't think there is a philosophical difference anywhere around this room"). Per the rubric, tactics dissent scores below economics dissent, and a meeting with reluctant supporters can reach 40-50. Score 27.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: The stock market, speculation, debt and income ratios (Lindsey, Kelley), fund-manager "bandwagon" into emerging markets (Hoenig), and fear of cracking markets with 50bp feature noticeably in the policy debate, and Greenspan wants anticipations "to subdue speculation in the stock market". But financial stability is secondary to growth and inflation. Score 32.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The directive stays symmetric, but Greenspan says he would "move again" at the next meeting or the one after, wants a "Sword of Damocles" over the market, would go asymmetric if the economy runs ahead, and agrees to a telephone call. Many members expect or want further moves. The announcement says "move toward a less accommodative stance". Mild-to-clear tightening signal. Score 70.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Mostly confident (Boehne: "as good as it gets in terms of convincing evidence"). Some hedging from Forrestal and Keehn about debt ratios, trading partners and fiscal policy, and about M2. Roughly normal caveats. Score 27.

**Polarity checks.**
- Higher inflation pressure means more inflation; the evidence is modestly elevated, which gives 58. OK.
- Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; the evidence is loose and accommodative, so it is below 50. OK.
- Higher EQUITY_VALUATION means more concern, but the rubric's anchor for bubble/overvaluation is low (1-30). Participants fear overvaluation, so the score is low, 22. OK.
- Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress; the evidence is healthy, so below 50. OK.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 46 (below 50, "gap remains/small gap"), satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 (accommodative), satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (ample availability, no level quoted; Low confidence), satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 32 (healthy), satisfied.
- DOLLAR and TREASURY are N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 46 and LABOR 48: close. Fine.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 46 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 67: 21 points apart. Spending is above trend but the economy is still just below potential after a long recovery from slack, and participants say the gap is closing this year. Acceptable.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 and CREDIT_SPREADS 38: both loose. Fine.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY: Treasury is N/A, which is normal for non-crisis times.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 versus sub-components (60, 56, 55): consistent.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH and INFLATION_SERVICES were not substantively discussed by members.

**Text problems.** Day-one staff turns are removed, so a few member remarks appear as bare questions and some answers are missing. Two copies of the vote read-out sit at the end (duplicate roll calls, ten voters, all Yes, plus a separate vote on the ranges). Page markers are present. None of this affected scoring. The 1994 meeting includes extensive disclosure and announcement debate, which I treated as procedure.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Melzer, "the CPI rose at a 3.4 percent rate during the last three months of 1993"; Parry, "we would not be able to expect any further progress in bringing inflation down in 1994"; Phillips, "the recent numbers certainly look good, particularly the PPI and the deflator". Summary: Inflation is about 3% and not yet falling, with emerging pressures. Direction: above neutral because several members see no further progress and edging-up prices, partly offset by good recent PPI.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 46 +-5 (High).** Evidence: McDonough, "the gap between actual and potential GDP is now quite small, and... that which remains will be used up in the course of 1994"; Kelley, "I just don't see the economy as overheated now... approaching full employment but still with some meaningful soft spots". Summary: Slightly below potential and closing. Direction: below neutral because members describe a remaining, small gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, unemployment "coming down to... the NAIRU--in the low 6 percent area"; Stern, "growing reports of labor shortages... does not seem to have translated into discernible wage pressures yet". Summary: Near full employment with shortages in pockets. Direction: roughly at neutral, since the level is about 6.5% but shortage reports are widespread.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 67 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "well over 4 percent and, adjusting for weather effects, may be running over 5 percent"; Kelley, "an emerging 'boom'"; Keehn, retail sales "surprisingly strong". Summary: Spending and investment are well above trend, though members call it unsustainable. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Only passing remarks (Phillips on productivity opportunities, Syron on potential).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "excessive accommodation"; Broaddus, "the real funds rate would still be quite low"; Jordan, "I haven't heard any reference to the notion of a credit crunch in some time now". Summary: Conditions are accommodative with strong equity markets and credit demand. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Jordan, "very strong credit demands--commercial, consumer, and residential"; Phillips, "strong financial markets are continuing to provide financing". Summary: Credit is readily available; spreads are never quoted. Direction: below neutral because availability is ample, but this is inferred.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 22 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: LaWare, "has bloated stock and bond prices beyond rational values"; Kelley, "a rather euphoric stock market". Summary: Valuations are seen as stretched. Direction: below 50 on this scale, since overvaluation concern is the low end.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Phillips, "banks are better positioned to contribute"; Melzer, "significant increase in all major categories of loans". Summary: Banks look healthy; discussion is brief. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60.** Kelley, "all of the commodity price indexes... a rather persistent upward drift"; Keehn, steel and purchased-product prices edging up.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 56.** Melzer, "apartment occupancy rates and rental rates edge up"; McTeer, "upward pressure on construction prices".

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** LaWare, "inflationary expectations in terms of anticipatory price increases"; Broaddus, the System's stance has "done a great deal to increase our credibility".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 69 +-6 (High).** Evidence: McDonough, "the question is not so much whether we should tighten but by how much"; Melzer, "signal this change with a 50 basis point increase". Summary: Unanimous support for the first tightening in five years, with a majority preferring 50bp over the 25bp adopted. Direction: hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I don't think there is a philosophical difference anywhere around this room... I would request that, if we can, we act unanimously"; Parry, "25 basis points and symmetry just doesn't represent what most of us think". Summary: Unanimous vote, with a real debate on size and tactics but agreement on direction and economics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the stock market is at an elevated level... by any measure"; Lindsey and Kelley on household debt ratios. Summary: Several members raise equity froth and debt, but as context for the policy move.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I would not mind moving again at the next meeting or the meeting after that"; "Sword of Damocles over the market". Summary: Directive stays symmetric, but the signal is that more tightening is likely. Direction: tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 27 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Boehne, "this is as good as it gets in terms of convincing evidence to move"; Forrestal, "the uncertainty in the economy... income/debt ratios... trading partners". Summary: Standard caveats, conviction on the move.
