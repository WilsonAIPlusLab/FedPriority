# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2018-06-13 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff Q&A (members' questions only), economic go-round, a policy Q&A on a yield-curve box, the policy go-round on alternatives A/B/C, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. The text reads cleanly; I noticed no garbled passages. Several staff-Q&A turns appear with no question text, which does not affect scoring.

**Era context (not used to override text).** A hiking cycle with strong growth, a very low unemployment rate, inflation reaching the target, fiscal stimulus and rising trade-policy worries. I scored only what participants said.

**Speakers and views.**
- Williams: "robust economic expansion", UE 3.75%, natural rate 4.6%, tight labor market, modest overshoot of 2% by 2020, four hikes in 2018.
- Rosengren: "Strong but Precarious"; UE heading to 3.4%; policy path "somewhat too accommodative"; financial fragility (Deutsche Bank, Italy).
- Harker, Barkin, George, Kaplan, Quarles, Evans, Mester, Brainard, Dudley, Powell: strong growth, tightening labor market, inflation near 2%, gradual hikes. Mester wants a steeper path.
- Bullard: economy "sunny" but the Committee risks being "too hawkish"; supports alternative A; flat Phillips curve; low market inflation expectations.
- Kashkari, Bostic, Kaplan, Evans: lean toward a slower pace or a pause at neutral. Kashkari sees remaining labor slack. Quarles sees possible participation slack.
- Heavy discussion of yield-curve inversion and r*.

**Direction statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as essentially at target, with building pressure. Core PCE is 1.8-1.9%, headline about 2.2% on energy, and "within a whisker of our target" (Williams). Contacts report cost pass-through, but Bullard cites a trimmed mean of 1.7%. Slightly above the midpoint. 53.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above midpoint. Williams has growth "well above the pace of potential", Mester has the economy "beyond maximum employment", Brainard has "above the trend rate" and Rosengren has "running hot". Quarles and Evans see a smaller gap. Bright-line rule requires > 50. 63.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE is 3.8%, heading to 3.5%. Williams says "exceptionally tight", the unemployment rate is the lowest since 1969 or 2000, and there are shortages. Kashkari, Quarles and Brainard note remaining slack at the margin (participation), and wage growth is moderate. UE below 4% means above 55. 68.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend. Consumer spending bounced back, investment is "robust" (6%), and manufacturing is at records. Trade uncertainty is delaying capex (Bostic, Evans, Kaplan, Mester). Net is modestly above trend. 62.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Kaplan, Quarles, Barkin, Williams and Dudley. Productivity growth is described as low ("recent torpidity", "low productivity growth is likely to temper..."), with some optimism about the future. Below trend at present. 40.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mostly "accommodative" (Powell, Mester, George "accommodative financial conditions", Williams "favorable"). Brainard says some net tightening has occurred over the year but the intermeeting net effect is "a wash". Below 50 as required. 43.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields and curve slope are discussed at length, but as a recession signal, not market dysfunction. No liquidity or functioning problems. Normal functioning. 40.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Participants do not discuss US credit spreads. Mester mentions them only as a model input, and Dudley mentions peripheral European sovereign spreads, which is not a US credit measure. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Mester sees "signs that asset valuations are elevated" and Rosengren mentions asset prices. Brainard says equities are up 5%, "a positive sign". Few participants discuss valuation. Modest concern, 42, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Rosengren flags Deutsche Bank (FDIC problem list, downgrade, CDS). Bullard and Rosengren discuss bank stocks in the Q&A. US banks are not described as stressed or sound. Mixed. 50, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has appreciated (Brainard "the dollar has appreciated", "continued strengthening"; Mester "recent dollar appreciation"; Powell "stronger dollar"). Bright line > 50. 60.
- Inflation sub-components. Goods: input costs, steel and aluminum, tariffs, price pass-through (Harker, Mester, Kashkari, George), so modestly rising: 58. Services and housing: not discussed, N/A. Expectations: market and survey measures "a little bit low" (Evans, Kashkari, Bullard), though "well anchored" (Rosengren) and "stable" (Mester): 45.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: A 25bp hike was broadly supported (alternative B). The SEP median moved to four hikes in 2018. The path is gradual. Some members (Rosengren, Mester) want steeper, while Bullard (alternative A), Kaplan, Bostic, Kashkari and Evans lean slower or want a pause at neutral. Hawkish overall, tempered by "gradual" and "patient". 65.
- Dissent: unanimous vote of the 8 voters, no formal dissent. Bullard, a non-voter, preferred A. Meaningful but mild differences on pace, the yield curve, and r*. Broad agreement. 27.
- Financial stability emphasis: yield-curve and financial-conditions discussion is extensive. Italy, EME stress, Deutsche Bank, and Dudley's lender-of-last-resort remarks add more. It is about financial conditions and risk, but not dominant. Moderate. 45.
- Forward guidance: the statement removes the "below longer-run levels" language, which members say reflects progress and no change in the path. It keeps "gradual increases". Members signal a pause near neutral. Mild tightening bias. 62.
- Uncertainty: r* and the neutral rate, trade policy, Italy, the yield-curve signal and the tight labor market ("how tight is too tight") are all flagged. Risks are mostly "balanced". Dudley raised inflation uncertainty to "above average". Somewhat elevated, but not extreme. 40.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 63 (> 50, OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 43 (< 50, OK). CREDIT_SPREADS N/A. BANKING_STRESS 50 (no sound or stressed label applied to US banks). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 (> 50, OK). TREASURY_STRESS 40 (no dysfunction, OK).

**Polarity verification.** LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter, evidence says tight, 68 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter, evidence says accommodative, 43 matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more stress or depressed prices, evidence says modestly elevated valuation, 42 matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 63 vs LABOR 68: 5 apart, fine.
- OUTPUT_GAP 63 vs CONSUMPTION 62: aligned.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads are N/A, so no conflict.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 43 vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: consistent for normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 53 vs sub-components (goods 58, expectations 45): consistent.

**N/A justification.** CREDIT_SPREADS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not addressed by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 53 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Williams, "closing in on our 2 percent goal ... modest overshooting"; Brainard, "core PCE prices rose 1.9 percent"; Bullard, trimmed mean "1.7 percent".
- Summary: Inflation is at or just under target with building cost pressures.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because headline is 2.2%, pressures are building and an overshoot is projected, though core is just under 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP 63 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Williams, "well above the pace of potential"; Mester, "operating beyond maximum employment".
- Summary: Most participants see the economy above potential, with some saying the gap is smaller than the staff's.
- Direction: above neutral because of "above trend" and "beyond maximum employment" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 68 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Williams, "labor markets are quite tight" with UE 3.75%; Mester, "lowest level in 18 years".
- Summary: Participants describe a historically tight labor market, with some noting slack in participation.
- Direction: above neutral because UE is below 4% and shortages are widespread.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Brainard, business fixed investment "robust pace of 6 percent"; Powell, "strong job growth, fiscal stimulus".
- Summary: Spending is above trend, with trade uncertainty restraining new capex commitments.
- Direction: above neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Quarles, "recent torpidity"; Mester, "low productivity growth is likely to temper the pace of inflation".
- Summary: Current productivity growth is viewed as low, with optimism about the future.
- Direction: below neutral because current growth is low.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 43 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Powell, "still-accommodative financial conditions"; Brainard, "some net tightening in conditions has occurred since the beginning of the year".
- Summary: Conditions are accommodative overall, with modest tightening from higher yields and the dollar.
- Direction: below neutral because of "accommodative" language.

**TREASURY_STRESS 40 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Brainard, "10-year Treasury yield is up about 50 basis points"; the yield-curve discussion by Williams, Bullard and others concerns the recession signal, not functioning.
- Summary: The Treasury market is discussed only as a yield and slope signal, with no dysfunction.
- Direction: normal functioning, below the 50 midpoint.

**CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.** US credit spreads are not discussed. European sovereign spreads and a model input mention do not give a level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 42 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Mester, "we do see signs that asset valuations are elevated"; Brainard, equities "up about 5 percent".
- Summary: A modest valuation concern was raised by one participant.
- Direction: slightly toward overvalued, below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS 50 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "Deutsche Bank is on the FDIC's list of problem banks"; Brainard and Kaplan do not describe US banks as stressed.
- Summary: Foreign-bank stress is flagged and the US system is not characterized.
- Direction: at neutral, with a stressed foreign bank offsetting the unspoken US soundness.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Brainard, "the dollar has appreciated"; Mester, "recent dollar appreciation".
- Summary: Moderate appreciation, with expectations of further strengthening.
- Direction: above neutral because the dollar is strengthening.

**INFLATION_GOODS 58; INFLATION_SERVICES N/A; INFLATION_HOUSING N/A; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 45.**
- Evidence goods: Harker, "successfully passing through increased prices for raw materials"; Mester, "little resistance on the part of their customers to these higher prices".
- Evidence expectations: Evans, "still-low levels of inflation expectations in household surveys and TIPS pricing"; Rosengren, "well-anchored expectations".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 65 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Powell, "continue on our gradual path by raising the target range ... by 25 basis points"; Bullard, "I support alternative A ... in some danger of coming off as too hawkish".
- Summary: Broad support for a hike and a gradual continuing path, with a few members preferring slower and a few preferring faster.
- Direction: hawkish side because of a hike and four-hike median, tempered by gradualism.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 27 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: the vote read-out shows all voters "Yes"; Bullard (non-voter) prefers alternative A; Kaplan "three rate increases in 2018, not four".
- Summary: Unanimous voting and differences concentrated on pace and communication.
- Direction: low, with some disagreement about the path and the yield curve.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 45 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "financial fragility"; Dudley, floor system and "lender-of-last-resort backstops"; the extended yield-curve and financial-conditions debate.
- Summary: Several participants address it meaningfully, but it is not the main topic.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 62 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Williams, forward-guidance sentence "has grown stale ... in no way suggest a shift in our policy approach"; Evans, "statement continues to say that the Committee expects future rate increases to be gradual"; Kashkari, pause once at neutral.
- Summary: Further gradual hikes are signaled, with pause talk near neutral and less rigid guidance.
- Direction: mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Quarles, "considerable uncertainty regarding the value of the neutral rate"; Dudley, "we don't really know how tight is too tight".
- Summary: Standard to somewhat elevated, concentrated on r*, trade, and the yield-curve signal; risks mostly described as balanced.
- Direction: above the "normal" band, below "elevated".
