# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2002-09-24 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: staff-Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around (all Reserve Bank presidents and governors in turn), the Chairman's policy summary, a short policy round ("I support your recommendation"), a discussion of the press statement, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed, so several Greenspan lines are orphaned replies to staff. Whole file read; no garbled passages that affect scoring (one `SPEAKER (?)` "Lunch!" line, irrelevant).

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2002, a weak, "jobless" recovery after the 2001 recession. Funds rate 1.75 percent, described as accommodative. Backdrop in the text: corporate governance scandals (WorldCom), equity prices down 6-8 percent over the intermeeting period, rising oil prices and possible war with Iraq, strong autos and housing, weak business investment, low and falling inflation, zero-bound/deflation worries. I score what participants say, not the backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish/easing-now camp: Gramlich ("we ought to cut rates"), McTeer ("time has come to go ahead and ease"; at least 50bp), Bernanke ("prima facie case for easing", but accepts waiting), Bies ("very close to the time when we must consider further rate cuts"), McDonough ("increasingly likely that we will have to ease policy and do so robustly"), Kohn (case for easing, wants more evidence), Olson ("code yellow to code orange"), Minehan (preference for stronger growth, but patient).
- Patient/hold: Parry, Stern, Santomero, Moskow, Hoenig, Poole, Ferguson, Broaddus, Jordan.
- Most hawkish: Guynn - no cut, low rates may have contributed to excesses and a "policy trap"; says he is in the minority. No one advocates tightening.
- Chairman: stay put, keep the weakness risk statement, add geopolitical wording as "insurance", and signal that a move before the next meeting is quite possible.
- Vote: 10 yes (hold), 2 no (Gramlich, McTeer), both preferring a cut.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as well below the midpoint, low and still falling (Greenbook core PCE about 1.3 percent; Kohn "inflation is already about as low as it ought to go"; Bies "inflation hovering around 1 percent"; Bernanke on inflation "as low as projected" being a serious risk; Greenspan "no pricing power"). Deflation risk is discussed but as a tail. Magnitude: "below target, disinflation" band 26-40, with deflation-risk talk pushing the low end. Score 26.
- OUTPUT_GAP: participants see output below potential and the gap persisting (Gramlich "prolonged output gap"; Kohn "output gaps persist"; Ferguson, in confusingly signed language, "growth that continues to be under potential... a positive output gap that lingers", meaning a shortfall; Minehan "below potential"). Bright-line satisfied (below potential means below 50). Score 32.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: participants see labor as slack and weak (unemployment above 5.5 percent and projected 6+ percent, Gramlich; "tepid employment growth", McTeer; "jobless recovery", Santomero; Greenspan acknowledges employment figures "clearly" not positive). Score 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumer spending, autos and housing strong (equity extraction, Greenspan; Hoenig "fairly robust"), business investment and capex weak and delayed (Kohn, Moskow, Bernanke "consumer and business sectors can't diverge"). Net slightly below trend. Score 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: very strong, repeatedly cited (Greenspan: 4.3 percent in Q2, 6.4 percent for nonfinancial corporations, "quite startling"; Bernanke "remarkable growth of productivity"; Kohn "huge increases in efficiency"; Moskow "extraordinary growth"). Score 77.

**5. Polarity checks.** INFLATION: higher = more pressure; evidence says low, so below 50: matches. LABOR: higher = tighter; evidence says slack, so below 50: matches. PRODUCTIVITY: higher = more growth; evidence says very strong, so above 50: matches.

**6. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: mixed. Offsetting evidence: equity prices down sharply, credit spreads "greatly widened" over the spring/summer, syndicated credit hard to arrange (Olson); versus credit markets stabilised and bond issuance resumed (Kohn), low long rates and mortgage rates, banks competing for small-business loans (Minehan), very high corporate liquidity. Several describe policy and rates as "accommodative" and low rates "supportive". Bright-line "accommodative/supportive" implies below 50. Net slightly looser than neutral. Score 46.
- TREASURY_STRESS: only yield-level discussion (Treasury yields declining, Fannie Mae hedging, JGB oversubscription questions); no dysfunction described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kohn says an unusual heightening of risk perceptions "greatly widened credit spreads" and expects "risk premiums narrow" later; credit markets have since stabilised. Direction information with only implicit level (risk premiums still above where they should be). Per the level-governs rule: direction only, so 52-55, Low confidence. Score 55.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: equity prices fell 6-8 percent in the intermeeting period, with "further substantial declines" (Kohn), implied volatility high (Ferguson), concern about negative wealth effects; Poole cautions against "bailing out the stock market"; Kohn notes the earlier overvaluation unwinding. Participants treat the declines as concerning but orderly. Score 58.
- BANKING_STRESS: Olson, "banking industry is in a strong capital position, a strong liquid position, and has good asset quality"; Guynn, consumer credit quality holding or improving; some trade-credit stretching. Healthy means below 50. Score 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only incidental remarks (Canadian dollar, peso down 15 percent, yen), no assessment of the U.S. dollar by participants. N/A.

**7. Inflation sub-components.** GOODS: price deflation in goods and durables (Greenspan, McTeer "deflation in durable goods", Jordan "goods sector" deflation from productivity): 24. SERVICES: not discussed, N/A. HOUSING: house prices rising rapidly and high relative to rents (Parry, Santomero, Guynn on speculative deals), while commercial rents are falling; home price appreciation is not exactly shelter inflation, so scored cautiously at 58. EXPECTATIONS: "inflation and inflationary expectations are dropping" (Gramlich), deflation concern (Bernanke): drifting below target, 36.

**8. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: hold at the meeting, but a clear easing bias. A large share (Gramlich, McTeer, Bernanke, Bies, Kohn, McDonough, Olson, Minehan) is leaning toward a cut, the Chairman signals possible intermeeting action, two vote for a cut now, nobody for tightening, one (Guynn) is firmly against easing. Lean dovish: 32.
- Dissent: two formal dissents (both for a cut); disagreement mostly on timing (now vs. wait for data), size (50bp vs 25bp, Broaddus/McTeer), and one explicit opponent of easing (Guynn). Assessments of the economy are broadly similar. Notable but not sharp: 44.
- Financial stability emphasis: credit spreads, equity declines and wealth effects, corporate governance scandals, housing bubble/speculation (Guynn, Poole, Bies), policy-induced excess (Guynn), banking health (Olson), corporate liquidity, zero-bound contingency planning (Broaddus). Several participants address it meaningfully, but the economic assessment dominates: 40.
- Forward guidance tone: retain the "weakness" balance-of-risks language, add geopolitical wording framed as insurance, and prepare the market for a possible move before November 6 (Greenspan, McDonough, Kohn). Mild easing bias: 24.
- Uncertainty: "Knightian uncertainty" (Hoenig), "uncharted territory" (Kohn), "extraordinary uncertainty" (Greenspan), "incredible uncertainty" (Gramlich), "substantially raised uncertainty" (Moskow), models cannot capture the Iraq risk. High, though partly the war-specific and event-driven sort: 72.

**9. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 (<50, below potential) OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 (<50 on "accommodative/supportive" low rates, partly offset by equity and spread tightening) OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 55 (direction-only, in 45-55 band, Low) OK. BANKING_STRESS 30 (<50, "strong capital") OK. DOLLAR and TREASURY N/A.

**10. Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR 33: aligned.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44: 12 points apart; consumption is strong but investment weak, so spending is only modestly below trend while the gap judgment is about slack overall. Acceptable (within 15).
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 55: slight divergence, 9 points; credit spreads and equities were a headwind but low rates and stabilised credit markets kept conditions close to neutral. Acceptable.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A, normal times.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 26 vs sub-components: goods 24, expectations 36, housing 58 (house prices, discounted since it is not shelter inflation); weighted average consistent with about 26-30 given heavy emphasis on the overall low core PCE.

**11. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning remarks, only yield-level questions to staff. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member assessment of the dollar. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-specific price comments.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 26 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Inflation is already about as low as it ought to go"; Bies, "inflation hovering around 1 percent"; Bernanke, "inflation as low as projected is potentially a serious risk".
- Summary: Inflation is low and falling, with deflation a discussed tail risk.
- Direction: below neutral because core inflation is projected near 1.3 percent and seen as too low.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Gramlich, "chronic problem of a prolonged output gap"; Kohn, "output gaps persist"; Minehan, growth "a bit below our estimate of potential".
- Summary: Output is below potential and not expected to close the gap soon.
- Direction: below neutral because participants repeatedly describe underutilized resources and sub-potential growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Gramlich, "unemployment is high now and does not seem to be getting lower"; McTeer, "tepid employment growth... underscores the continued weakness in the labor market".
- Summary: Labor markets are slack with weak hiring and unemployment near or above 6 percent.
- Direction: below neutral because of rising projected unemployment and the "jobless recovery" framing.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "continued strong spending by consumers" versus "reluctance of firms to hire or invest"; Greenspan, equity extraction giving "a strong impetus on household spending".
- Summary: Strong households (autos, housing) are offset by weak business capex.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because investment weakness and softening retail sales net against solid consumers.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 77 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "Nonfarm productivity was up 4.3 percent at an annual rate in the second quarter... quite startling"; Kohn, "huge increases in efficiency".
- Summary: Productivity growth is exceptionally strong and widely noted.
- Direction: above neutral because of repeated "extraordinary/remarkable" language and cited rates well above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 46 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "credit markets have stabilized, allowing bond issuance to resume" alongside "greatly widened credit spreads and decreased equity prices"; Hoenig, low interest rates for housing and autos "have led consumers to continue buying".
- Summary: Low rates and stabilised credit offset equity declines and wider risk premiums, leaving conditions near neutral, slightly easy.
- Direction: slightly below neutral (looser) because policy and rates are described as accommodative and supportive, tempered by equity weakness.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** - Treasury market functioning not discussed; only questions about yield levels.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "greatly widened credit spreads"; Kohn, "risk premiums narrow" expected as the economy expands; Olson, syndicated credits "particularly tough to arrange".
- Summary: Spreads widened in the spring/summer and have partly stabilised, with level mostly implicit.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because only the widening direction and a still-elevated risk premium are described (direction-only rule, 52-55).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 58 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson, equity prices "move down by somewhere between 6 and 8 percent" in the intermeeting period; Kohn, "further substantial declines in equity prices".
- Summary: Equities fell notably and raise wealth-effect worries, but the decline is seen as orderly rather than a rout.
- Direction: above neutral on the stress/concern scale because declines and volatility are a prominent worry, not extreme.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Olson, "the banking industry is in a strong capital position, a strong liquid position, and has good asset quality"; Guynn, consumer credit quality "holding or improving".
- Summary: Banks are described as healthy, with pockets of weak business lending demand.
- Direction: below neutral because of well-capitalized/healthy language.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** - participants did not assess the U.S. dollar; only passing remarks on the yen, Canadian dollar and peso.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 24** - Greenspan, price deflation in the corporate sector; McTeer, "deflation in durable goods"; Jordan, goods-sector deflation from productivity.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** - not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58** - Parry, home prices "somewhat high relative to rental rates"; Santomero, "price appreciation continued at a rapid rate". House prices rather than rents, so scored cautiously.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36** - Gramlich, "Inflation and inflationary expectations are dropping".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "prima facie case for easing policy at this point"; Greenspan, "we may well move before the next meeting"; Guynn, "I don't think we're dealing with the need for an interest rate cut".
- Summary: A hold today with a strong easing bias, two votes to cut now and no voice for tightening.
- Direction: dovish because most participants favor eventual easing, several immediately.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 44 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, Gramlich and McTeer voting No; McTeer, "time has come to go ahead and ease"; Guynn, "I'm probably still in the minority today".
- Summary: Two formal dissents for a cut plus an opposing hawkish voice, but broadly shared economic assessment and mainly timing/size differences.
- Direction: moderate-to-notable disagreement above the one-dissent anchor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn, risk perceptions "greatly widened credit spreads and decreased equity prices"; Guynn, "purely speculative housing and property deals"; Broaddus, the zero-bound contingency planning.
- Summary: Several participants address credit, equity and housing-risk issues meaningfully, but the economic assessment dominates.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 24 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "stay with the balance of risks toward weakness, but to keep in mind the distinct possibility that we may want to move before the next meeting"; Moskow, describe any easing as "a form of insurance".
- Summary: The signal retains the weakness risk statement and primes the market for possible easing.
- Direction: easing bias, below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "Knightian uncertainty"; Kohn, "we are in uncharted territory"; Greenspan, "extraordinary uncertainty about what is happening".
- Summary: Many participants emphasize unusual uncertainty (Iraq, corporate governance, investment cycle), and models are questioned.
- Direction: well above neutral, high but event-centered rather than total.
