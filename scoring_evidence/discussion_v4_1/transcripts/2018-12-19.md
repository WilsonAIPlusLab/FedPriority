# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 — Transcript Discussion, Meeting 2018-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds (a) a long-run framework/balance-sheet session (reserves, ceiling facilities, composition), which is not an economic assessment and was ignored for scoring; (b) members' questions in staff Q&A; (c) the economic outlook go-round (first day); (d) the Chair's opening on outlook and policy, a Q&A on the statement, and the policy go-round (second day); (e) a vote read-out. A few garbled tokens from extraction (e.g. "ma81ut8181ngactual", "85ut85", "88ut") sit inside otherwise readable sentences and did not affect scoring.

**Era context.** Tightening cycle late in a long expansion, with a recent bout of financial-market turbulence. Used only as background; scores come from what the participants say.

**Speakers.** Chair Powell; Vice Chair Williams; Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles, Bowman; Presidents Bostic, Rosengren, Harker, Barkin, Bullard, Mester, Evans, Daly, Kaplan, George, Kashkari. The common thread: "data good, mood bad" (Daly, Powell). Strong real data (growth about 3 percent, UE 3.7 percent, strong consumers) sits against tighter financial conditions, global slowdown, trade worries, and downside risks. Inflation is near but slightly under 2 percent and softening.

**Dimension-by-dimension direction statements.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as at or slightly below the midpoint. Core PCE is 1.8 percent y/y (Bullard, Kashkari), 1.5 percent annualized over six months (Clarida), "softened but near 2" and "muted". Upside anecdotes (tariff pass-through, input costs: Evans, Mester, Bostic) are real but the net view is muted. Below 50, modestly.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint. "Growth well above potential" (Williams), "output and employment growing above trend" (Mester), "economy continues to grow above trend... beyond full employment" (Daly), "meaningfully above-potential real GDP growth" (Powell). Bright line satisfied (>50). Modest rather than far above, since growth is expected to move toward trend.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well above the midpoint. UE 3.7 percent, the lowest in nearly 50 years (Mester, Brainard); labor "at or beyond u*" (Clarida); hiring difficulty, rising wages. Kashkari alone sees remaining slack. UE < 3.8 percent puts this in the 66-80 band.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Modestly above. Consumer spending robust (Brainard, Quarles, Barkin, Williams); business investment slowed, Q3 weak, trade uncertainty, firms shelving projects (Daly, Kaplan), housing weak. Net slightly above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed with mixed views. Productivity surprised to the upside this year (Powell), "2.25 percent over two quarters, fastest in some time" (Quarles), but Williams's models put trend near 1.3 percent. Near trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants stress sharp tightening (Powell: "deteriorated quite sharply"; Williams: "sharp tightening in financial conditions"; Brainard: from "exceptionally accommodative" to "a neutral factor"). The level now is roughly neutral to somewhat tight against the 2017-19 neutral anchor. Above 50, modestly.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields and the curve are discussed (yields down, curve flat, inversion risk: Bullard, Harker, Kashkari), but there is no functioning problem. Yield moves alone are not dysfunction, so score in the normal band, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction is clearly widening ("corporate credit spreads have risen": Mester; "no high-yield issuance... beginning of a credit crunch": Kaplan). Level: Brainard says spreads were in the lower part of their distribution earlier and are now "close to the middle". A stated mid-distribution level means about 50, nudged up by Kaplan's access concerns and the widening. Bright line (a stated level, not direction) satisfied: no "elevated/wide" level stated, so no forced >50, and no "compressed" level now, so no forced <50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities fell "significantly" (Rosengren), but the valuation view is mixed. Quarles: the fall was not unexpected "in light of ... market valuations", Powell mentions a possible return to "stretched valuations and frothy markets", Brainard sees "a repricing of risk after a long period of very elevated risk appetite". Falling prices are not undervaluation. Near fair value with mild concern: just above 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are not discussed as a system. Bowman mentions agricultural loan delinquencies "slightly increased", and Mester and Brainard cite leveraged-lending risk, but this is corporate and non-bank credit rather than a statement about bank health. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Discussed and clearly strong. Clarida: the real broad dollar is "up 12 percent... a 16-year high"; Bostic cites "relative strength of the dollar"; Mester cites a possible stronger appreciation; George notes the dollar rising with oil falling. Above 50.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS: tariff and input-cost pass-through discussed (Bostic, Evans, Mester, Kaplan, Daly: modest effect), mildly above stable. SERVICES: not discussed, N/A. HOUSING: only housing-market activity and house-price-to-rent ratios (Williams), not rents or shelter inflation, N/A. EXPECTATIONS: market-based measures fell (Bullard, Clarida: "at best barely in the range"), survey-based measures "well anchored" (Quarles, Mester, Powell), Michigan low (Clarida). Slightly below anchored-at-target.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: A 25 bp hike was approved, but with a strong patience theme. Three participants favored no hike (Bullard, Harker, Kashkari, all non-voters by the roll call), others were reluctant (Kaplan, George, Bostic, Bowman, Daly), and the Chair stressed patience. Rosengren, Barkin, Quarles, Williams and Brainard want continued gradual increases. Current action is hawkish, forward lean is much softer (median two hikes in 2019, pause talk): lean hawkish, in the 55-62 range.
- Dissent: The vote read-out shows all ten voters "Yes", so zero formal dissents. But three participants preferred alternative A, several (Kaplan, Bostic, Daly, Bullard, Harker) pressed for statement changes, and Bullard said he would not support B unrevised. This is expressed reservation among many, with distinct camps on pace but not on the economic assessment. Moderate, below a meeting with formal dissents.
- Financial stability emphasis: The financial-market disconnect is a central theme of the go-round (volatility, tightened conditions, credit spreads, leveraged lending, corporate debt, mutual fund liquidity mismatch: Brainard, Mester), plus the extended funding-market/ceiling-facility discussion of the framework session. Prominent, most participants address it.
- Forward guidance: The statement drops the "further gradual increases" language in favor of data dependence plus a monitoring clause. Powell: "some further gradual increases" only if downside risks do not materialize, "no preset course", "can afford to be patient". Near neutral, a shade hawkish because further hikes are still the base case.
- Uncertainty: Elevated and widely stated (Brainard: uncertainty "increased notably"; Kaplan: "high level of uncertainty"; Mester: "considerable uncertainty" about the path; Powell: dark-room analogy; Bostic and Harker similar). Offset by firm conviction in the real data and by Williams's call not to overstate it.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says slightly below target, so below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter; UE at 3.7 percent, so well above 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter; evidence says tightened to about neutral-to-somewhat-tight, so above 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress; widened to mid-distribution, so about 50 or a touch above. Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH = stronger dollar; evidence says strong, so above 50. Higher HAWKISH = tighter policy; evidence says hike plus patience, so mildly above 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP >50 (met). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: members describe tightening, not "accommodative", level neutral-to-tight, >50 (met). CREDIT_SPREADS: level stated as near the middle; no forced side, scored 53 (met). BANKING_STRESS: N/A. DOLLAR_STRENGTH >50 (met). TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, 36-50 band (met).

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (58) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): diverge by 14, within 15; labor is tighter than the gap because the UE level is very low while participants call growth only modestly above potential and expect it to converge to trend. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (57): consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (53): co-move, both mildly above 50. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS (43): normal-times pattern, fine. INFLATION_PRESSURE (44) vs sub-components (goods 55, expectations 43): the parent sits below the goods sub-score because goods pass-through is anecdotal while the aggregate readings are muted; consistent with the expectations score and the headline core PCE numbers.

**N/A justification.** BANKING_STRESS (bank system not discussed as such), INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING (no services-price or rent/shelter inflation commentary by members).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "core PCE... 1.8 percent year-over-year—still below our target"; Clarida: "core PCE inflation has been running at an annual rate of 1.5 percent" over six months. Powell: inflation "just a bit more muted than expected".
- Center of gravity: Inflation is near but slightly under target and softening, with scattered tariff and input-cost pass-through anecdotes.
- Direction confirmation: Below neutral because most members report core inflation under 2 percent and muted.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Williams: "Economic activity continues to grow well above potential"; Daly: "The economy continues to grow above trend. Labor markets are pushing further beyond full employment."
- Center of gravity: Economy modestly-to-clearly above potential, with growth expected to ease toward trend.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because members say growth is above trend/potential and labor is beyond full employment.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Mester: "unemployment rate has remained at 3.7 percent, its lowest level in nearly 50 years"; Clarida: "labor market is operating at or beyond our estimate of u*".
- Center of gravity: Very tight labor market with hiring difficulties and firming wages; only Kashkari argues for remaining slack.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because UE is below 3.8 percent and members cite shortages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "latest data on consumer spending for the fourth quarter are robust"; Daly: large companies "tabling projects that were right on the margin".
- Center of gravity: Strong consumers offset by slowing business investment and weak housing, netting modestly above trend.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because consumption is called robust and investment still positive, though softening.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 52 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Quarles: productivity "grew 2.25 percent, on average, over the past two quarters... fastest pace in some time"; Williams: models put "over 90 percent probability" of a low-growth phase around 1.3 percent.
- Center of gravity: Recent upside surprise but a split on whether trend has shifted.
- Direction confirmation: About neutral because evidence pulls in both directions.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Powell: "Financial conditions have deteriorated quite sharply, and that process may not have run its course"; Brainard: conditions "now a neutral factor... compared with a strong tailwind midyear".
- Center of gravity: Sharp tightening from very easy conditions to roughly neutral, with risk of more.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because the dominant message is tightening, with the level around or somewhat past neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 43 ±6 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "10-year rate is well within a trading range"; Kashkari: "the yield curve continues to flatten, with the spread... around 15 or 16 basis points".
- Center of gravity: Yield declines and flattening are discussed as signals, not as market malfunction.
- Direction confirmation: Normal functioning band because no liquidity or auction problems are cited.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Brainard: spreads "had been in the lower part of their historical distribution earlier in the year, but they're now close to the middle"; Kaplan: "credit spreads on corporate debt are widening... we may be at the beginning of a credit crunch."
- Center of gravity: Spreads have widened to mid-distribution, with a credit-access worry from one member.
- Direction confirmation: At or slightly above neutral because the stated level is the middle of the distribution and the move is wider.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Quarles: the fall in equity prices "I don't think any of us should find it completely unexpected, in light of what our assessment of market valuations were"; Brainard: "repricing of risk after a long period of very elevated risk appetite".
- Center of gravity: A correction from somewhat rich valuations, viewed as orderly and not yet a real-economy problem.
- Direction confirmation: Near fair value because members treat the decline as a repricing, with no distress language.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
- Banks as a system were not assessed by participants; agricultural-loan and leveraged-lending remarks concern borrowers and non-bank holders.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 68 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "broad trade-weighted dollar in real terms, up 12 percent... at a 16-year high"; Kashkari: rate increases "strengthen the dollar".
- Center of gravity: A sizable appreciation this year with lagged drag still to come.
- Direction confirmation: Above neutral because members describe a strong, appreciating dollar.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55** — Tariff and input-cost pass-through reported by contacts (Evans: "expecting to pass all of these increases on to customers"), tempered by Daly's modest 0.1 pp consumer-price estimate.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43** — Clarida: expectations "at best, just barely in the range consistent with our goal"; Bullard: market-based compensation fell sharply; Quarles/Powell: survey measures "well anchored".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "appropriate to raise rates 25 basis points"; Bullard: "first-best for today... is alternative A"; Powell: "we can afford to be patient about further policy firming".
- Center of gravity: A hike delivered, with a cautious forward lean and a sizeable minority preferring a pause.
- Direction confirmation: Mildly hawkish because the action tightens but the forward stance is patient.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out shows ten "Yes" votes (no formal dissent); Bullard: "If you don't want to make those changes to the statement, then I wouldn't support the statement"; Kashkari and Harker favor alternative A.
- Center of gravity: No formal dissents but several non-voters prefer a hold and many voters are reluctant.
- Direction confirmation: Moderate disagreement (camps on pace/risk, little disagreement on the economic assessment).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 64 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "financial market volatility and uncertainty have increased materially" and detailed corporate leverage/leveraged-lending vulnerabilities; Mester: "Risks to financial stability appear to be elevated."
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions and market turbulence were a headline topic of the go-round and policy discussion.
- Direction confirmation: Prominent attention (direction-neutral measure).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Powell: "neither the pace nor the endpoint of any further rate increases is known. There is no preset course"; Williams: the revision is "an appropriate step in moving away from the strong explicit guidance toward a more data-dependent posture".
- Center of gravity: Guidance is being softened toward data dependence while some further increases remain the base case.
- Direction confirmation: Near neutral with a slight tightening tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the uncertainty surrounding that modal outlook has increased notably"; Kaplan: "there is a high level of uncertainty, at least for me, in regard to the outlook."
- Center of gravity: Many members emphasize elevated uncertainty and downside risks, though the baseline is firmly stated.
- Direction confirmation: Elevated, not extreme, because conviction in the real data persists.
