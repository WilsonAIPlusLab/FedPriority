# FOMC member-discussion scoring (v4.1), meeting 2012-09-13, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion with staff turns removed. Two sessions: day 1 (member questions to staff, financial stability remarks, the economic go-round, the Chairman's summary and remarks) and day 2 (policy go-round on alternative B and paragraph 5 versus 5', then the consensus-forecast trial run and a roll-call vote). The text is clean. There are occasional bare question or answer fragments where staff replies were removed, but all member views are legible. No passage was too garbled to score.

**Era context.** September 2012. The funds rate is at the lower bound. The Committee is deciding on an open-ended agency MBS purchase program (alternative B, $40bn or $30bn per month), an extension of date guidance to mid-2015, and the choice between date guidance (paragraph 5) and unemployment-threshold guidance (paragraph 5'). I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.**
- Dovish and advocating action: Bernanke, Dudley, Yellen, Williams, Rosengren, Evans, Kocherlakota, Raskin, Tarullo, Stein.
- Supports B but with reluctance or smaller scale: Powell, Duke, Pianalto, Lockhart.
- Opposed to more asset purchases: Lacker (dissent), Plosser, Fisher (accepts B only as the least bad option), George, Bullard (sympathetic only to a limited version).
- Most members prefer paragraph 5 to 5' for now.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see inflation as slightly below the 2 percent objective. Examples: Williams, "PCE inflation to remain below 2 percent for the next few years"; Powell, "bouncing around just below 2 percent"; Evans, "high inflation is not a concern"; Pianalto expects more core disinflation. Bullard says "subdued but generally close to target". Lacker, Plosser and Kocherlakota worry about future inflation, but nobody says it is high now. Below 50, around 40.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Most see slack. Evans: "resource slack quite large". Yellen: a "large margin of labor market slack". Powell: "substantial amount of slack". Bernanke: "at or below trend" and "well below potential" as the alternative view. A minority (Lacker, Bullard, Plosser) argue the policy-relevant gap is small, and Yellen also says "at or near its potential" for current growth. The center of gravity is below potential, around 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Unemployment is 8.1 percent. Payroll gains are weak, participation is depressed, long-term unemployment is high, and employment-to-population is at a post-recession low. This is clear slack, around 24. Tarullo and others judge that structural factors are a small share of it.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Growth is about 2 percent and described as "sluggish". Business investment is weak, with capital goods orders below shipments, plans on hold and uncertainty paralyzing capex. Consumer confidence is soft. Retail and auto sales and housing are improving. Net is somewhat below trend, around 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Not discussed as a topic, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Mostly supportive. Long rates are "exceptionally low" (Bullard), equities are at four-year highs, financial stress indexes are "not particularly worrisome", and Bernanke's summary says "financial froth has not been reported". Credit is tight in mortgage and small-borrower pockets (Duke, Williams). Plosser says the problem is not "excessively tight financial conditions". The net is accommodative, below 50, around 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS. Only Bullard's remarks on very low long yields and flight to safety bear on it. No dysfunction is described, so the score is in the normal-functioning range with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Fisher: high-yield spreads show "things obviously are going our way". Powell: leveraged finance terms "reminiscent of the bubble days". The mortgage primary-secondary spread is "unusually wide" (Duke), a partial offset. Level is below normal, around 33.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Equities at four-year highs, "no froth" (Evans's contacts, Bernanke's summary). There is no valuation-concern language either way, so roughly fair, around 47, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS. Fisher: banks show a "brighter picture", the best since 2006 by the Dallas rating measure. Duke: "balance sheet strength, debt service capacity, and credit supply". Bernanke's summary: "credit supply is available when loan demand strengthens". European bank stress is mentioned, but the domestic system is sound, so the score is below 50, around 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Bullard notes "the euro has been stronger in recent weeks", which implies a weaker dollar against the euro. It is a minor mention, so the score is mildly below 50 with Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components. GOODS: oil, gasoline and grain price rises (Dudley, George, Powell on commodity effects), set against Lockhart's contacts reporting no supplier price requests, so slightly above neutral. SERVICES: not discussed, N/A. HOUSING: house prices are discussed, but not shelter inflation, so N/A. EXPECTATIONS: "stable", "well anchored" (Bernanke, Bullard, Evans), with Fisher noting rising breakevens and Bullard noting 10-year breakevens above 250bp, so about neutral and a touch above.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean. The outcome is open-ended MBS purchases at $40bn per month "through the end of the year", an extension of the date to mid-2015, and a strong dovish majority. Dudley and Yellen say "act forcefully", Kocherlakota and Williams want threshold guidance, and Duke is concerned about preserving "fire power". Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, George and Bullard oppose or are reluctant. The lean is strongly dovish, around 14.
- Dissent. One formal dissent (Lacker, the roll call shows 11 to 1). Five non-voting or voting participants (Plosser, Fisher, George, Bullard, Lacker) argued against more purchases in the economics round or the policy round. Powell supports B "with a certain lack of enthusiasm", Lockhart and Pianalto have reservations, and Pianalto, Lockhart, Stein, Powell, Duke, Tarullo, Bullard, Lacker and Fisher oppose or defer 5'. The split covers the efficacy of purchases, the slack concept and the guidance design, but the tone is collegial and the vote is nearly unanimous. This is notable disagreement with distinct camps and one dissent, around 52.
- Financial stability attention. A dedicated financial-stability agenda item (credit-to-GDP, money market funds, imbalances), plus the ECB and Europe, plus cost-of-LSAP risks (market functioning, losses, exit, froth). It is a substantive but not dominant topic, around 45.
- Forward guidance. The statement extends the date to mid-2015, adds open-ended purchases, and members strongly favor moving to state-contingent threshold guidance (6.5 percent unemployment, 2.5 percent inflation ceiling) within the next few meetings. Strong easing commitment, around 7.
- Uncertainty. Heavy and widespread. Bernanke: "incredibly uncertain", the decision is "a shot in the proverbial dark", and "nobody really knows" what is holding back the economy. Plosser: "uncharted waters". Others cite the fiscal cliff, Europe, wide confidence intervals around the estimates of the effects of asset purchases, and slack measurement. Many emphasize uncertainty, but the language is not Knightian or crisis-level, around 62.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: below potential language, score below 50. Satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: supportive and low rates, score below 50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level read as favorable or tight, score below 50. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: sound and healthy, score below 50. Satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: euro stronger implies a weaker dollar, score below 50. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, score in the 36-50 range. Satisfied.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 36 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 24 differ by 12, within 15. The labor market is more depressed than overall output, as members say.
- OUTPUT_GAP 36 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38 are consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 33 co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is near-normal, consistent with calm markets.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 40 is consistent with goods near neutral (55), expectations about neutral (51), and no discussion of services or housing.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed by participants as topics.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "I continue to expect overall PCE inflation to remain below 2 percent for the next few years"; Powell, "inflation that is bouncing around just below 2 percent... but close to target". Center of gravity: modestly below target. Direction: below neutral because most members describe inflation as below or near 2 percent and not a threat.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 ±8 (High).** Evidence: Evans, "This pace of growth leaves resource slack quite large"; Yellen, "a large margin of labor market slack". Counter-view: Lacker argues the policy-relevant gap is small. Center of gravity: below potential. Direction: below neutral because the majority see slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Pianalto, "the economy had made very little progress in labor markets over the summer"; Yellen, "we will look back on a lost decade". Unemployment 8.1 percent. Direction: below neutral because there is large slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Lockhart, "contacts have resigned themselves to a 2 percent growth world"; Dudley, "capital goods orders have clearly been weak". Housing and auto sales are better. Direction: below trend, sluggish.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bullard, "Long-term interest rates are exceptionally low" and "financial stress... relatively low"; Duke, "mortgage lending conditions are still extremely tight" (a counterweight). Direction: below neutral because conditions are supportive overall, with pockets of tight credit.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 30 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Bullard, "Ten-year Treasury trading... around 170 basis points. That's extremely low". Low yields are not dysfunction, and no impairment is described. Direction: normal or calm functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Fisher, "high-yield spread on panel 6, things obviously are going our way"; Powell, leveraged finance "reminiscent of the bubble days". Duke notes mortgage primary-secondary spreads remain "unusually wide". Direction: below normal, so score below 50 by the level rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Bullard, "equity markets are at recent highs"; Evans, financial contacts "do not say that they are seeing excessive risk-taking or anything approximating financial froth". Direction: about fair, with price rises only and no valuation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Fisher, "a brighter picture for the average bank"; Duke, credit supply "will be fully available". Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Bullard, "the euro has been stronger in recent weeks". Direction: below neutral because the dollar is mildly weaker against the euro. Only a brief mention.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55.** Higher oil, gasoline and grain prices (Dudley, George), offset by Lockhart's contacts reporting no supplier price requests. **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 51.** Evidence: Bernanke's summary, "Inflation expectations are stable"; Fisher noting breakevens "on the rise". About anchored at target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 14 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "I conclude that we should use them more forcefully"; Yellen, "we intend to provide additional accommodation through new asset purchases until we observe substantial and sustained improvement". The Committee adopts open-ended MBS purchases. Hawkish voices (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, George, Bullard) are a minority. Direction: strongly dovish, in the QE-era range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 52 ±8 (High).** Evidence: the vote is 11 to 1 with Lacker dissenting ("I cannot support a new asset purchase program"). Plosser: "costs of further action greatly exceed expected benefits". Powell supports B "with a certain lack of enthusiasm". Disagreement extends to the slack concept, the efficacy of purchases and paragraph 5 versus 5'. Distinct camps, but collegial, with a single formal dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 (Moderate).** A dedicated financial-stability item (Dudley, Bullard, George, Rosengren on credit-to-GDP, money market funds), extended Europe and ECB discussion, and recurring cost-of-LSAP concerns (George on "risks to financial stability due to the extended low rate environment"). Several participants engage meaningfully, but it is not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 7 ±4 (High).** Evidence: the date is extended to mid-2015; Kocherlakota and Williams push thresholds that promise low rates until unemployment falls to 6.5 percent; Bernanke, "communicating low rates for longer... is an approach to dealing with the zero lower bound". Strong easing bias and commitment, comparable to threshold-based guidance. The threshold version was not adopted but is expected soon.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "we're incredibly uncertain, and we have to make decisions under uncertainty... a shot in the proverbial dark"; Plosser, "We are and will be in uncharted waters". Many members stress the fiscal cliff, Europe, the efficacy of asset purchases and the measurement of slack.
