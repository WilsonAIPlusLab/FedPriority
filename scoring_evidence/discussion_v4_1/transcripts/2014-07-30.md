# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1: meeting 2014-07-30 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion, transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript with staff turns removed. It has six blocks:
- Day 1: a short Desk Q&A.
- Day 1: a long monetary-policy-normalization discussion (overnight RRP facility, IOER, caps, reinvestment, delegation to the Chair, principles).
- Day 1: staff Q&A and a financial-stability round.
- Day 1: the economic go-around.
- Day 2: the rest of the economic go-around and the Chair's summary.
- Day 2: the policy go-around on statement alternative B, followed by a roll-call read-out.

This is not a special-topic meeting. It has a full economic go-around and a full policy go-around, so every dimension is scored on the usual basis. The normalization block is procedural and operational, so I used it only where members speak about financial stability or the policy outlook.

**2. Era context.** July 2014: the recovery is in its fifth year, the asset-purchase taper is nearing its end, and the funds rate is at the zero bound. The text says the following. Q1 GDP fell about 3% (annualized) and is widely discounted as anomalous. The unemployment rate is 6.1%. Payrolls are strong. PCE inflation is about 1.6-1.8% and rising toward 2%. The question is how soon forward guidance and liftoff should move.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Yellen: labor slack is "significant" but shrinking faster than expected. Inflation is below 2% but the downside risk has diminished. March liftoff is "very much on the table". She would keep "significant" in the statement and move in steps.
- Hawkish voices:
  - Plosser wants the forward guidance dropped, liftoff sooner and a smaller balance sheet. He votes No.
  - Fisher wants earlier or faster removal of accommodation, but supports B with reluctance.
  - George says "we may already be behind the curve".
  - Mester and Bullard want "significant" dropped and the guidance changed soon.
  - Lacker is cautious about shifting away from U-3.
  - Fischer and Dudley expect an earlier liftoff, to be signaled in steps.
- Dovish or patient voices:
  - Kocherlakota sees "no reason" to raise rates while the inflation outlook is below 2%.
  - Evans, Rosengren, Williams and Brainard want more evidence first.
  - Tarullo and Powell see slack remaining, though Powell is prepared to revisit if the data hold.
- Policy action: no change in guidance. The pace of asset purchases is cut by another $10 billion (taper). The statement is alternative B, which says "significant underutilization" and "for a considerable time".

**4. Directional signals, in words, before the numbers.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see it as slightly below the midpoint. Inflation is firming toward target but is still "below our 2 percent objective" (Yellen). Several (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren) see PCE around 1.6%. Powell: the threat of persistently low inflation has diminished, and no breakout is in sight. Higher means more pressure, and the evidence says slightly less than target, so a score just under 50 is right.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see it as below potential and closing. Slack "significant" (Yellen), "substantial slack" (Rosengren) and "significant slack remains" (Powell). Fischer expects the natural rate to be reached in about a year. Bright line: below potential, so the score must be under 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see it as below full employment but improving fast. The unemployment rate is 6.1%, which falls in the 5.5-7% band (31-45). Some report shortages (Lockhart, George). Wages are "subdued" (Yellen), and many cite part-time-for-economic-reasons and participation problems.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see it near trend to slightly below. Consumer spending is soft (Lacker, Evans). Housing is "disappointing". Business investment is "bumping along" (Brainard). Rebounding capital spending, autos and Texas strength offset these. Net: slightly below 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Several members mention low productivity, so it is discussed. Plosser asks about total factor productivity after output stalled while jobs grew. Dudley refers to the "dismal first-half performance". Powell says "productivity has been low". Fischer says growth may have declined. Below trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see them as accommodative. Credit is "readily available" (Lockhart). Financing is easier from banks (Mester). Brainard cites a "considerably brighter picture on credit provision from the SLOOS". Powell cites the "extremely low cost of financing". Fischer: zero interest rates. Bright line: accommodative, so the score must be under 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning is not discussed. Members talk about the money-market and ON RRP take-up and quarter-end window dressing, which is not Treasury dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are not discussed directly. Availability is ample, and the references are to leveraged lending (Rosengren, Mester) and to "credit market turbulence" in a staff report (Kocherlakota). Level information is indirect (ample availability), so below normal, with Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuations are "substantially stretched" in small biotech and small media (Dudley). Fischer pushes back that the stretch is natural at zero rates. Powell notes very high M&A valuations. Pockets of stretch, not market-wide concern.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are broadly healthy, with organic loan growth (Lacker) and stronger loan demand (Evans). Concerns are narrow: legal risk at large banks (Kocherlakota), leveraged-lending guidance (Rosengren, Mester), and Puerto Rico (Dudley). Brainard: risks are "reasonably well contained". Below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components:
  - Goods: food commodity prices have retraced (Fisher, George), while shippers are taking price (Fisher). Evans reports that competition is compressing retail margins.
  - Services: only thin evidence (airlines and express shippers up 2.5-3%, and Lockhart on pricing power).
  - Housing: rising home prices with weak volumes (Lacker) and rising rents in Texas (Fisher).
  - Expectations: long-run measures are "well anchored" (Powell, Mester, George, Bullard), with Kocherlakota arguing they are drifting below target.

**5. Polarity verification.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: higher means more above potential. The evidence says slack, so below 50. Match.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter. The evidence says easy credit, so below 50. Match.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. The evidence says ample credit, so below 50. Match.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. The evidence says banks are sound, so below 50. Match.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: the scale rates valuation concern, with higher meaning depressed or stressed and lower meaning stretched. Dudley's "stretched" pockets map to below 50. Match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: hold steady with a tightening bias. The Committee unanimously backs B (a hold with taper). Hawkish dissent and hawkish statements are present, but dovish voices (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren) argue for patience. The Chair signals earlier liftoff if the data continue. Lean hawkish, mildly.
- Disagreement: the roll call lists one formal No, Plosser, on the forward guidance. Fisher supports B only "with reluctance". Camps differ on economics (Is U-3 a sufficient statistic? Will inflation rise or fall?), on the word "significant" and on the timing of guidance changes. Normalization views are "passionate and mixed" (Yellen) on the ON RRP facility, delegation and reinvestment. Moderate-to-notable.
- Financial stability: a dedicated round (Rosengren, Kocherlakota, George, Fisher, Dudley, Fischer, Lockhart) plus extended ON RRP stability concerns and Powell's M&A item. Moderate-to-prominent.
- Forward guidance: "considerable time" is retained, but nearly everyone expects to move it soon. Yellen says March "is very much on the table". Mild tightening bias against a still-accommodative text.
- Uncertainty: the Q1 GDP puzzle, the natural rate, the wage-price link and the "uncharted territory" for normalization (Brainard) are all cited. Brainard: "considerable uncertainty". Elevated but not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50, FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50, CREDIT_SPREADS < 50, BANKING_STRESS < 50. The other two (TREASURY_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH) are N/A. All pass.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40) are within 2 points.
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (47) are within 5 points.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) and CREDIT_SPREADS (35) co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A. In calm times that is fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (44) is consistent with the sub-components (goods 45, services 52, housing 54, expectations 46). The mean is about 49, and the parent sits a little lower because members emphasize that headline inflation is still below 2%.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member discusses Treasury-market functioning. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member discusses the dollar or the exchange rate (the only mention is a joke about a misprinted banknote).

**Text-quality notes.** The extracted text has some staff answers removed, so a few member replies read as fragments. This does not affect scoring. The roll-call read-out lists Fisher as "Yes" and Plosser as "No". Fisher's own remarks (support for B "with reluctance") are consistent with that. I scored the text as given: one formal dissent plus reluctant and wording-based reservations.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### Economic conditions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: inflation "has moved higher but is still running below our 2 percent objective". Powell: "the threat of inflation to remain persistently low has clearly diminished" while "no threat of a breakout to the upside".
- Center of gravity: inflation is firming toward but still under target, with no concern about overshoot.
- Direction: below neutral because core and headline PCE are about 1.6-1.8%, below 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "substantial slack"; Powell: "significant slack remains"; Fischer expects the natural rate to be reached in about a year.
- Center of gravity: below potential, with slack disappearing faster than expected.
- Direction: below neutral because most members still see appreciable underutilization.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: the preponderance of evidence "still points to significant underutilization of labor resources"; Lockhart and George note emerging shortages in some segments and wage pressure for certain jobs.
- Center of gravity: unemployment is 6.1%, with a wide array of improving indicators but still-subdued wages.
- Direction: below neutral because the level of unemployment and part-time-for-economic-reasons remain above normal; the pace of improvement does not lift the level score.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker is "skeptical that the long-awaited acceleration in consumer spending is upon us"; Brainard says business investment is "bumping along"; Lockhart reports auto purchases "quite strong" and capital investment strengthening.
- Center of gravity: a Q2 rebound, but housing and consumers lag, so roughly trend-minus.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the soft elements (housing, consumer income) are cited more than the strong ones.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 36 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "dismal first-half performance"; Powell: "productivity has been low".
- Center of gravity: several members see weak productivity, though some treat it as a measurement puzzle.
- Direction: below the historical trend because the discussion is of weak or declining productivity growth.

### Financial conditions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lockhart: "most medium and larger companies indicated that credit is readily available"; Powell: the "extremely low cost of financing for investment-grade corporates".
- Center of gravity: credit is easy and funding costs are very low.
- Direction: below neutral (accommodative) because the cited conditions are loose.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Treasury market functioning is not discussed by participants.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Brainard: a "considerably brighter picture on credit provision from the SLOOS"; Mester: it is "easier to obtain financing from banks".
- Center of gravity: credit availability is ample, but spread levels are not described. Leveraged lending is flagged as something to monitor.
- Direction: below neutral because availability is good and no one calls spreads wide.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: valuations "substantially stretched" for small biotech and media stocks and "pretty extreme" in those two areas; Fischer: dividing "by zero, you get a large number".
- Center of gravity: stretched in pockets, contested as to cause, and not seen as systemic.
- Direction: below neutral on this scale (stretched, not depressed).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: legal risk at very large banks could "shock the financial system"; Brainard: risks are "reasonably well contained".
- Center of gravity: banks are healthy with growing loan demand; concerns are narrow (legal risk, leveraged lending, Puerto Rico).
- Direction: below neutral because the system is described as sound with a few pockets of concern.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A**
- The dollar and exchange rates are not discussed.

### Inflation sub-components

- **INFLATION_GOODS: 45.** Fisher: food commodity prices have retraced ("wheat from $8 to $5"); Evans: competition is "putting downward pressure on retail prices". Offsetting: shippers are taking price. Slightly soft.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES: 52.** Fisher: airlines and shippers are "beginning to take price", about 2.5-3%; Lockhart sees firms succeeding with price increases. Thin evidence.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING: 54.** Lacker: "rising prices and disappointing volumes"; Fisher: "rents are rising" in Texas. Thin evidence.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46.** Mester: the Cleveland 10-year measure is "essentially stable near 2 percent"; Powell: "stability of long-run inflation expectations"; Kocherlakota argues they are drifting down.

### Discussion-specific

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 54 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: if the data continue "we're likely going to be moving up sooner than we previously anticipated" and "March is very much on the table"; Plosser: forward guidance "is far too constraining".
- Center of gravity: hold plus continued taper is the action. The lean is toward earlier tightening, but a sizable minority (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren) argues for patience.
- Direction: above neutral (lean hawkish) because the forward lean is toward tightening, though the current action is a hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 45 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "I can no longer support the language in alternative B or C", and the roll call shows him as the one "No". Fisher: "I support alternative B, but with reluctance."
- Center of gravity: one formal dissent, a reluctant supporter and several wording objections, with real disagreement over economics (U-3, inflation outlook) and over normalization design.
- Direction: moderate-to-notable disagreement; no sharp personal exchanges that dominate (a brief heated exchange on inflation expectations).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: The Chair opened a dedicated financial-stability round (Rosengren, Kocherlakota, George, Fisher, Dudley, Lockhart). Powell and Tarullo flag stability concerns about the ON RRP facility at length.
- Center of gravity: a full section plus a recurring theme in the normalization debate. It is not the center of the meeting, which is the economy and the statement.
- Direction: attention is above the typical 20-35 band; this is an attention measure, not a direction.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "for a considerable time" is retained in B, but Mester: "pretty soon, it's going to be both necessary and desirable for us to change that forward guidance"; Dudley lays out a stepwise sequence to a March liftoff.
- Center of gravity: current text is still accommodative, but the signal is that the language will tighten in coming meetings.
- Direction: below 50 because the text is still an easing commitment, but tilted toward change.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "considerable uncertainty" about the first-quarter GDP and GDI puzzle; Yellen: "we cannot know with certainty how well any specific approach to normalization will work".
- Center of gravity: multiple participants stress puzzles (Q1 GDP, the natural rate, the wage-price link) and an unprecedented exit, though most sound fairly confident on the trajectory.
- Direction: elevated, short of "very high".
