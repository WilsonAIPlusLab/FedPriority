# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 scoring: 2003-01-29 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. The file opens with organizational business (election of officers, authorizations, rules changes) and a Day 1 research seminar on interest-rate smoothing/policy inertia and optimal rules, with a short Q&A on markets. Day 2 holds the economic roundtable (all 12 Reserve Bank/Board participants), the Chairman's summary and recommendation, the policy go-around, and the roll-call read-out. Staff answers are removed, so some members' questions appear without answers.

**2. Era context.** Early 2003: a sluggish recovery after the 2001 recession, fed funds at 1.25% after the November 2002 cut, an imminent Iraq war, Venezuelan oil disruption, falling inflation and a nearby zero bound. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.** Parry sees excess capacity, low inflation, and says further easing may be appropriate. Broaddus finds the Greenbook plausible, with an exceedingly wide but symmetric band, and says policy may need to move substantially and promptly either way. Hoenig and Guynn find activity sluggish. Moskow and Stern are a bit better. Minehan sees mostly downside risks but is attracted to inertia. Kohn sees weak present and strong future, with inflation low and falling and the zero bound a worry. Gramlich is the bear but says to wait. McDonough and Ferguson favor doing nothing under enormous uncertainty. McTeer sees downside risk and doubts the "neutral bias". Poole stresses that the war is a large unknown. Pianalto and Santomero are skeptical on investment. Olson reports on bank credit quality and fiscal politics. Bernanke would ease aggressively if the economy stalls. Bies sees a very wide output gap and low core inflation. Greenspan proposes holding at 1.25% with balanced risks.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see it as below the midpoint. Bies: core consumer inflation "running well below 2 percent". Parry: "slightly on the low side", below 1 percent ex-bias. Bernanke: core PCE "may slip to 1 percent or lower". Kohn: inflation "already low and poised to decline". Hoenig: "largely subdued". Heavy discounting everywhere. Not deflationary spiral. Score about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below midpoint. Parry: "excess capacity", Bies: "output gap very wide", Bernanke: "ongoing slack in utilization of labor and capital", Santomero: low capacity utilization. Score 33 (bright line < 50 satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: below midpoint. Broaddus: unemployment rates rising in all District states, layoffs. Guynn: job growth "stubbornly absent". Moskow: employment components weak. Stern: stabilized. Pianalto: jobless-recovery pattern. Score 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: modestly below trend net. Housing and autos strong (Bies: housing starts at sixteen-year highs; Guynn: residential strong), but holiday sales weak/flat (Hoenig, Guynn, Parry), business investment "continues to disappoint" (Kohn), "flat" (Minehan), state and local retrenchment. Score 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: above trend. Broaddus: "strong productivity growth" supporting recovery. Kohn: "estimates of underlying productivity growth have been revised up". Stern: "favorable productivity outlook". Some doubts (Guynn, Stern, Bernanke on the technology lull) but the weight is positive. Score 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: slightly loose. Hoenig: "financial market conditions...relatively strong". Kohn: real rates for businesses fell, "reduced perceptions of risks on the part of lenders". Offsets: equities weak/low points (Minehan, Gramlich), corporate debt "very expensive to issue" (Bies), banks tightening standards (Olson, McDonough), market skittishness. Members call monetary policy accommodative, but that is policy. Net 44 (bright line: no "tight" language, so < 50 is fine).
- TREASURY_STRESS: not discussed as dysfunction. N/A. The steep curve (Kohn) and a question on TIPS (Greenspan) are not dysfunction.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kohn: "a decline in risk spreads"; Ferguson asks whether the corporate spread is still elevated by historical standards (no answer in the text); Bies: debt issuance down and costly. Direction is narrowing; level not stated. Per the rule: direction only, so 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Score 47.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: members note weak equities (Minehan: "equity markets hitting low points"; Gramlich: "stock market is weak") without valuation commentary, as a source of unease. Default range, nudged to modestly depressed side. Score 55, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Stern: bankers comfortable with household credit, less so with business credit. Olson: consumer portfolio quality strong, uptick in residential mortgage NPLs, tightened standards. McDonough: tighter commercial standards, consumer delinquencies retreating. Kohn: credit problems "beginning to top out". Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50. Score 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Gramlich: "the dollar is falling"; Broaddus: dollar not acting as a safe haven and "could decline even more substantially". Depreciation, so < 50. Score 30.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher inflation score = more pressure; evidence is low inflation, so low score matches. Output gap: slack, below 50. Labor: loose, below 50. Credit spreads and banking stress: higher = more stress; evidence is mild easing, so below 50. Dollar: falling, below 50. Equity: higher = more depressed/concerned; mild.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold at 1.25% with balanced risks. The discussion leans toward easing: Parry (further easing may be appropriate), Bernanke (ease aggressively if stall), McTeer, Minehan (downside), Kohn (zero-bound lesson), and Bies (vigilance). Broaddus is the one who says the stance would be too accommodative if the war is resolved quickly. Hold with a mild easing bias, score about 41.
- Disagreement: no dissent on the policy vote (unanimous roll call; the only "No" in the file is Broaddus's long-standing objection on the foreign currency authorization, which is not the policy decision). Differences are in forecast optimism and in contingent preferences. Score about 15.
- Financial stability attention: Day 1 discussed financial fragility as a reason for gradualism and risk aversion, plus market spreads, debt issuance, bank credit quality; the roundtable mostly focuses on the real economy and Iraq. Moderate-limited, score about 32.
- Forward guidance: stay put, keep balanced risks, wait-and-see; Greenspan says action could be larger in either direction later. Slight easing bias in member remarks, but the formal signal is neutral. Score 45.
- Uncertainty: "enormous uncertainty" (McDonough), "maximum uncertainty" (Gramlich), "exceedingly wide" band (Broaddus), McTeer's uncertainty "as great as they've ever been", Poole's avalanche metaphor, Greenspan: military uncertainty "so overwhelming". Most participants emphasize it. Score 80.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 < 50 ok (slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 < 50 ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 47: level not stated, direction narrowing, so 45-55 on the side of the move. BANKING_STRESS 40 < 50 ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 < 50 ok (depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs LABOR 34: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40: gap 7, ok (housing/autos strong). FC 44 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 47: co-move, fine. FC vs TREASURY: N/A. Inflation parent 30 vs sub-components (goods 42, services 36, expectations 44) are slightly higher than the parent: the parent reflects explicit "well below 2 percent" core statements; sub-component evidence is anecdotal and mixed (oil/commodity rises offset retail discounting).

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction raised. INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed shelter/rent inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 ±6 (High).** Bies: "Core consumer price inflation is running well below 2 percent." Parry: inflation next year "below 1 percent after taking out the estimated bias...slightly on the low side." Center: inflation low and falling, with no deflation spiral. Below neutral because members describe inflation under the objective and drifting lower.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 33 ±6 (High).** Bies: "That leaves the output gap very wide." Parry: "excess capacity in the economy brings consumer inflation down." Center: considerable slack. Below neutral because excess capacity persists through 2004.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 34 ±6 (High).** Broaddus: "Unemployment rates are rising in all of our District states." Guynn: "Job growth in our region has been stubbornly absent." Center: weak labor market. Below neutral because layoffs and no hiring.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40 ±6 (High).** Kohn: "business investment continues to disappoint." Bies: "Households continue to show optimism, and businesses continue to find ways not to expand." Center: strong housing/autos offset weak holiday sales and investment. Below trend on net.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 62 ±8 (Moderate).** Kohn: "Estimates of underlying productivity growth have been revised up." Broaddus: "an accommodative monetary policy and strong productivity growth". Center: above-trend productivity is a pillar of the outlook, with some doubts (Guynn, Stern). Above neutral.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 ±8 (Moderate).** Hoenig: "financial market conditions...are relatively strong." Kohn: "Real interest rates for businesses have declined further." Counter: Minehan on equities at lows, Bies on costly debt issuance. Slightly loose overall, so below neutral.
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not raised as dysfunction.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 47 ±8 (Low).** Kohn: "we have seen a decline in risk spreads." Ferguson asks whether the spread is "still elevated by historical standards" (no level stated in the text). Direction narrowing, level unknown, so 45-55 on the narrowing side.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 55 ±8 (Low).** Minehan: "equity markets hitting low points." Gramlich: "the stock market is weak." No valuation commentary; modestly depressed and a source of unease.
- **BANKING_STRESS 40 ±7 (Moderate).** Olson: asset quality of consumer portfolios "is strong" but a "noticeable uptick" in nonperforming first mortgages and raised lending standards. Stern: bankers less comfortable with business credit. Healthy with minor concerns, so below neutral.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 ±8 (Moderate).** Gramlich: "the dollar is falling." Broaddus: "the dollar could decline even more substantially." Depreciation, so below neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 42.** Parry/McDonough/Moskow: retail discounting, "race to the bottom"; Poole and Greenspan note rising commodity/steel scrap prices. Mixed, slightly below stable.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES 36.** Parry: pressure to lower prices on service contracts and IT support. Below stable.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.**
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 44.** Kohn: expectations "likely to be at least a little lower"; stable otherwise. Slightly below target-anchored.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 41 ±5 (Moderate).** Greenspan: "stay with a 1¼ percent federal funds rate and leave the risks balanced." Parry: "a further easing of policy might be appropriate before too much more time passes." Center: unanimous hold with an easing tilt in the member remarks, partly offset by Broaddus's "too accommodative" scenario. Lean dovish to balanced.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 15 ±5 (High).** All 12 voting members "support your recommendation"; the roll call is unanimous. Differences are only about forecast optimism and contingent responses. Near-consensus.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 32 ±8 (Moderate).** Greenspan on Day 1: "we do not know, and cannot know, the degree of financial fragility in the system"; Ferguson/Kohn on fragility and gradualism; credit quality and spreads questions. Context rather than the focus of the economic roundtable.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 45 ±5 (Moderate).** Greenspan: "the less we do, even in how we phrase our post-meeting statement, the better off we are," keeping balanced risks. Bernanke would "ease aggressively earlier rather than later" if the economy stalls; McTeer questions the "continued credibility of our neutral bias." Neutral formal signal with a mild easing lean.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 80 ±6 (High).** McDonough: "an enormous amount of uncertainty." Broaddus: "confidence interval...symmetrical but exceedingly wide." McTeer: uncertainty "as great as they've ever been." Most participants emphasize deep, mostly geopolitical, uncertainty (war timing and outcome).
