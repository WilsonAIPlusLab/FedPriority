# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2009-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of participants' turns only (staff turns removed). It covers an afternoon session (balance sheet, lending programs, Treasury-Fed accord, legacy-asset plans, asset-purchase views, FX swap lines) and a morning session (economic go-around, policy go-round, statement drafting), then a vote read-out. Many turns are one-sided questions to staff; I ignore those and score participants' own views.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2009, fed funds rate at the zero bound, deep recession, banking system under strain, Committee debating a large expansion of asset purchases. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Bernanke (Chair), Dudley, Kohn, Tarullo, Warsh, Duke (governors), and presidents Bullard, Fisher, Lockhart, Rosengren, Hoenig, Plosser, Yellen, Pianalto, Stern, Lacker, Evans. Broad consensus that the economy is falling further and faster than expected. Disagreement is about tools: Treasuries vs MBS, legacy TALF, Fed bills vs SFP, monetary base targeting, and whether to pause balance-sheet growth (Hoenig).

**4. Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation far below target with deflation risk. Bullard: core PCE 1% in 2009 and below 1% thereafter, "deflation trap". Yellen: "the probability of outright deflation is significant". Bernanke's summary: most expect inflation "very low for a time". Plosser says deflation risk "somewhat abated", and Bullard notes a medium-term inflation risk, which pull slightly up. Score about 15.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Far below potential. Evans: "enormous resource gaps through 2010", an 8-point gap, "worst economic downturn since the 1930s". Kohn: "very high output gap". Score about 14.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very slack. Unemployment headed to 9.5-10% (Evans, Pianalto), "shedding jobs rapidly" (Bernanke). Score about 16.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Weak. Capex being cut everywhere (Fisher), investment "very weak" (Kohn), household deleveraging (Yellen). There are tentative signs consumption has stopped free-falling (Plosser, Lacker, Stern, Bernanke), which lifts it slightly off the floor. Score about 20.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched only by Warsh (risk of "less trend productivity, less growth, and a higher NAIRU") and Tarullo (structural adjustment). Brief, forward-looking, so Low confidence. Score about 35.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tight. Kohn: "generalized tightening of financial conditions" over the intermeeting period (dollar up, equities down, risk-free and corporate rates up). Dudley: "financial conditions are tighter". Bernanke: "banks are under exceptional stress". Yellen: credit conditions only "a modest degree of accommodation" by her metric, with non-price terms still tightening. Some stabilization in Fed-supported markets keeps it below crisis-freeze levels. Score about 75.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Mixed, but no dysfunction. Fisher: "no liquidity problem in the Treasury market". Warsh: "functioning reasonably well; highly liquid". Dudley doubts it is "all that liquid". Funding-capacity worries (Warsh) are about credibility, not functioning. Score about 38.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (level reading): Spreads are described as high in level. Fisher: private-Treasury spreads show markets "not functioning normally". Yellen: MBS "spreads are still high, although they've come down". Hoenig: a bank asked LIBOR plus 500. Duke: trust preferred at 11-12% vs LIBOR plus 375 last year, "high loan spreads are here to stay". Kohn: CDS spreads up everywhere. ABS markets "pretty much shut down" (Bernanke). A few improvements (Warsh's pharma deal, Lockhart's investment-grade funding) keep it short of a freeze. Score about 78.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: No valuation commentary. Participants note falling equity prices (Kohn, Rosengren: life-insurer shares under one-fifth of six months ago; worry about further setbacks). This is price-move and stress commentary only, so score modestly above neutral with Low confidence. Score about 58.
- BANKING_STRESS: Severe. Stress tests, banks needing "substantially more capital" (Rosengren), CDS widening, "failure of several large institutions is a nontrivial risk" (Kohn), "banks under exceptional stress" (Bernanke), rising problem banks (Hoenig). Duke finds deposits high and liquidity plentiful, so it is not a total freeze. Score about 80.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has appreciated and hurts exports. Yellen: "recent dollar appreciation"; Kohn: "the dollar has increased", "stronger dollar"; Bernanke's summary: "stronger dollar". Modest to notable strength. Score about 62.

**5. Polarity checks.** Inflation: evidence says low, so low score is right. Credit spreads: higher = more stress, evidence says wide, so high. Banking stress: evidence says stressed, so high (>50). Dollar: appreciation, so >50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the Committee is launching a major QE expansion ($750bn more MBS, $100bn agency debt, $300bn Treasuries, about $1.15 trillion), with Bullard calling it "very aggressive" and Rosengren saying "be very aggressive". No one argues for tightening. Hoenig would prefer a pause and Plosser/Warsh/Fisher voice reservations about particular instruments, but all vote yes. Emergency dovish, near the March 2009 anchor.
- Dissent: the final vote is unanimous (10 yes, no dissent). Genuine disagreement exists on instruments (Treasuries, legacy TALF, Fed bills, monetary-base language), Hoenig preferred no more balance-sheet growth, and Warsh/Fisher/Lockhart gave reluctant support on pieces. Economic assessment is shared. Moderate tactical disagreement without formal dissent.
- Financial stability emphasis: day 1 is almost entirely about lending programs, TALF/legacy assets, the Treasury accord, swaps and bank rescue, and the go-around has long bank and financial-market passages. Near dominant.
- Forward guidance: statement moves from "for some time" to "for an extended period" (Yellen, agreed by Bernanke because liftoff is now expected later), Evans says no hike before 2013, Kohn says the program underlines expectations of low rates. Strong easing bias, but not a threshold or date.
- Uncertainty: Kohn "Uncertainty is extremely high"; Lacker "the magnitude of the uncertainty is quite significant"; Tarullo "navigating in waters in which there's not a particularly good map"; Yellen invokes the Brainard principle; Evans cannot evaluate effectiveness for a few quarters. Very high, not quite Knightian.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (below potential): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 (tightening): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS stated high level, above 50: ok. BANKING_STRESS stressed, above 50: ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH appreciation, above 50: ok. TREASURY_STRESS no dysfunction described, kept 36-50: ok.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 14 / LABOR 16 / CONSUMPTION 20: all within 15 of each other, consistent. FC 75 / CREDIT_SPREADS 78: co-move. FC / TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market judged functioning, FC tight because of credit and banking, not Treasuries; explained. INFLATION_PRESSURE 15 vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 33: expectations seen as at risk of drifting down but not collapsed; consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: not meaningfully discussed by members (a couple of retail anecdotes on gasoline and apparel prices only; house-price declines are asset-price, not shelter inflation). INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is discussed (Bullard, Evans, Kohn, Plosser, Bernanke) and scored.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 15 ±6, High.** Evidence: Bullard, "core PCE inflation at 1 percent in 2009, and below 1 percent" later; Yellen, "the probability of outright deflation is significant". Summary: participants see well-below-target inflation with real deflation risk. Direction: below neutral because disinflation and possible deflation dominate.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 14 ±5, High.** Evidence: Evans, "enormous resource gaps through 2010"; Kohn, "a very high output gap". Summary: deep recession, slack of historic size. Direction: below neutral (below potential).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 ±5, High.** Evidence: Pianalto, unemployment peaking "at 10 percent"; Evans, "unemployment rate headed to 9½ percent". Summary: severe slack, rapid job losses. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 20 ±7, High.** Evidence: Fisher, "very little cap-ex taking place"; Plosser, "the free fall in consumption may have come to an end". Summary: very weak spending and investment with tentative leveling in consumption. Direction: below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 35 ±12, Low.** Evidence: Warsh, "less trend productivity, less growth, and a higher NAIRU". Summary: one governor flags weaker trend productivity as a medium-term risk. Direction: below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 75 ±7, High.** Evidence: Kohn, "generalized tightening of financial conditions at a time when the economy was weak"; Dudley, "financial conditions are tighter". Summary: tight, with credit and bank stress, partly offset by stabilization in Fed-backed markets. Direction: above neutral (tight).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Fisher, "no liquidity problem in the Treasury market"; Warsh, "functioning reasonably well". Summary: market judged orderly, with minor dissent from Dudley on liquidity. Direction: below neutral, no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 78 ±7, High.** Evidence: Fisher, spreads show "markets are not functioning normally"; Duke, "those high loan spreads are here to stay". Summary: spreads at a high level with credit rationing at the margins, slightly eased in supported markets. Direction: above neutral on level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 58 ±10, Low.** Evidence: Rosengren, worry that "stock prices and credit markets may suffer additional setbacks"; Kohn, "equity markets have fallen". Summary: price declines and stress concerns, no explicit valuation view. Direction: modestly above neutral, held near the default band.

**BANKING_STRESS: 80 ±6, High.** Evidence: Kohn, "failure of several large institutions is a nontrivial risk"; Bernanke, "banks are under exceptional stress". Summary: systemic bank stress, capital needs, political and stress-test uncertainty. Direction: above neutral (stressed).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "recent dollar appreciation have driven down our exports"; Kohn, "the dollar has increased". Summary: dollar up over the intermeeting period, a headwind for exports. Direction: above neutral (appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed as price-pressure topics.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 33.** Evidence: Bullard, "Deflationary expectations could become entrenched during 2009"; Evans, long-term expectations will stay anchored with a well-designed program. Summary: risk of downward drift, still broadly anchored. Direction: below neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 5 ±3, High.** Evidence: Rosengren, "this is the time to be very aggressive"; Bernanke, "a pretty strong consensus for the combined package, the total of $1.15 trillion". Summary: QE expansion with near-universal support, only instrument-level reservations. Direction: strongly dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig, "I think we should pause" on balance-sheet growth; Warsh, "quite uncomfortable" with long-term Treasury purchases; final roll call all yes. Summary: no formal dissent but distinct camps on instruments and several reluctant supporters. Direction: moderate disagreement, tactical rather than about the economy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 80 ±6, High.** Evidence: Hoenig, "we cannot get the economy going until we get the banking and intermediation process fixed"; Duke's long banking review. Summary: banking, credit programs and bank rescue occupy most of the discussion. Direction: attention very high.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 9 ±4, High.** Evidence: Yellen, switch to "an extended period"; Bernanke, "our expected liftoff time is probably longer". Summary: low rates signaled for longer, with further purchases ready. Direction: strongly easing, but not threshold or date based.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 80 ±7, High.** Evidence: Kohn, "Uncertainty is extremely high"; Tarullo, "waters in which there's not a particularly good map". Summary: pervasive, deep uncertainty about outlook and policy effects, short of Knightian. Direction: very high.
