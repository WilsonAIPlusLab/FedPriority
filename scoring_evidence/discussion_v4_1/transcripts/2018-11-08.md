# v4.1 Member-Discussion Scoring: meeting dated 2018-11-08 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Participant-only transcript of a two-day FOMC meeting. It has four blocks:
1. An opening special-topic session on the long-run operating framework. This covers floor versus corridor, abundant versus scarce reserves, and which policy rate to target (fed funds, OBFR, SOFR, GLOSTR).
2. A Desk-report and IOER technical-adjustment item.
3. A financial-stability go-round, followed by the economic go-round and the policy go-round on "alternative B" versus "alternative A".
4. A communications-subcommittee update, then a vote read-out. All nine voters named in the read-out vote Yes.

Staff turns are removed, so some participant questions to staff appear without the answers. The operating-framework block is a special topic with no economic assessment. I score only the economic, financial-stability and policy content (blocks 3 and 4) and treat block 1 as procedure. It contributes only to the forward-guidance and uncertainty reading, where it is silent.

**Era context.** Late 2018, in a hiking cycle with the unemployment rate at 3.7 percent. I let the text drive the scores, not the backdrop.

**Speakers.** Powell (Chair), Williams, Clarida, Quarles, Brainard, Mester, Barkin, Harker, Rosengren, Bullard, Kaplan, Evans, George, Bostic, Daly, Kashkari.

**Directional signals, core dimensions.**
- **INFLATION_PRESSURE.** Participants see inflation as at target.
  - Powell: "Inflation is right at our target."
  - Williams and Brainard: core PCE is at 2 percent, a "perfect alignment".
  - Mester: price pressures in her District "remain elevated".
  - Rosengren expects higher inflation than the Tealbook. Bullard says "inflation pressure seems to be quite low".
  - There are many anecdotes of tariff and input-cost pressure that are "not showing up in actual inflation data" (Powell).
  - The net view is at or marginally above target, so slightly above 50.
- **OUTPUT_GAP.** Participants see the economy as above potential.
  - Daly: "well above its potential and has pushed past full employment".
  - Brainard: growth "above its trend rate".
  - Rosengren: "the output gap continues to expand".
  - Powell expects 18 more months of above-trend growth. Barkin says the economy is "closer to overheating than to needing stimulus".
  - Kashkari dissents: "slack remains". Quarles and Clarida stress rising supply.
  - Net is modestly above potential. This satisfies the bright line (greater than 50).
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** The labor market is very tight.
  - The unemployment rate is 3.7 percent, "the lowest in almost 50 years".
  - The quits rate is the highest since 2001, and the job openings rate is above the unemployment rate (Brainard).
  - Many participants cite shortages and rising wages.
  - Evans and Kashkari qualify this (no "breakout" in wages, participation room). Per the rubric, a rate below 3.8 percent means 66-80.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.** Consumption is strong. Business fixed investment was "surprisingly weak" in Q3, and housing and residential investment are soft. GDP growth is about 3 percent. The net is modestly above trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY.** Discussed briefly and mixed.
  - Clarida cites 2 percent productivity growth in 2018, above trend.
  - Kashkari cites 1.3 percent.
  - Quarles and Mester discuss capital deepening and hopes for a pickup.
  - The net is near to slightly above trend.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.** Conditions have tightened in the intermeeting period (dollar up, 10-year yield up, spreads wider, equities down). Even so, participants call them "relatively accommodative overall when judged against historical norms" (Brainard) and "accommodative" (Mester). Clarida says indexes are "still in the easy zone". The bright line requires a score below 50, and the tightening pushes it toward the upper 40s.
- **TREASURY_STRESS.** There is talk of term-premium and yield rises and of Treasury issuance. No one describes dysfunction, and market functioning is not specifically discussed. I mark it N/A. The framework discussion about repo and the money market concerns instrument design, not stress.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS.** The level is low.
  - Brainard: spreads widened by 20 basis points (BBB) and about 50 basis points (HY), but they "remain at the very low end of their historical range".
  - Harker: "remain at low levels".
  - Mester: "credit spreads have changed relatively little".
  - Kaplan expects a future gap-out but says spreads "did not gap out" in this selloff.
  - Level governs under v4.1, so the score is below 50.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION.**
  - Mester: "elevated valuations in the stock market".
  - Clarida and Brainard: valuation pressures and "elevated valuations".
  - Kaplan is less concerned about overvaluation than about debt.
  - Quarles calls it a "daily dive". Most see the decline as modest and orderly.
  - Valuations are somewhat stretched and the correction is mild, so slightly below midpoint.
- **BANKING_STRESS.** Banks are sound.
  - Brainard: "the banking sector has much more high-quality capital".
  - Quarles: risk management at the largest banks is "much improved".
  - Clarida: banking changes are "all positive".
  - There are pockets of concern: Rosengren on CRE exposure at regional banks, Bostic on small-bank card delinquencies, and Brainard on flat capital ratios.
  - The bright line requires a score below 50.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** The dollar is stronger.
  - Brainard: the dollar "has appreciated about 1½ percent".
  - Kashkari: the "broad dollar is up 7 percent since the start of the year".
  - George and Kaplan cite export headwinds.
  - Clarida expects continued support. The bright line requires a score above 50, and this is modest to notable strength.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: tariff and input-cost increases, with some B2B pass-through (Bostic), against Williams noting softness in core goods in Q3.
- Services: Williams says core services "has actually been quite stable".
- Housing: housing activity and prices are discussed, but shelter inflation is not. N/A.
- Expectations: described as anchored but on the soft side. Clarida, Bullard and Brainard say below 2 percent. Rosengren and Mester say anchored at 2 percent, with upside risk from tariffs.

**Tier 3 check.**
- **Policy lean.**
  - The action is to hold, alternative B, keeping "further gradual increases" language. The Chair says the Committee is "on track to raise the federal funds rate in December" and nearly at neutral.
  - Most voters support B and a likely December hike (Clarida, Brainard, Mester, Barkin, George, Bostic, Daly, Williams, Evans, Rosengren).
  - Barkin wants to err high on neutral. Brainard sees upside risks that "might argue for a somewhat less gradual path".
  - Against that, Bullard and Kashkari prefer alternative A (pause). Quarles is open to waiting until January or March. Harker is undecided on December. Kaplan says the "easier moves are behind us".
  - Net is hold with a tightening bias and a likely December move: lean hawkish, around 60.
- **Disagreement.**
  - There are zero formal dissents. All nine named voters vote Yes. Bullard and Kashkari, who prefer A, are not in the roll call, so they are evidently non-voters this year.
  - Expressed reservations: Bullard and Kashkari (explicit alternative A), Quarles (timing), Harker (undecided).
  - Economic assessments are similar. The disagreement is mainly about tactics and pace, with some economic disagreement over slack (Kashkari versus Daly and Rosengren).
  - The exchanges are collegial. This is broad agreement with a few reservations, so low-to-moderate.
- **Financial stability.** A dedicated session is devoted to it, with about 8 speakers on leveraged lending, CLOs, CRE and the CCyB. It recurs in the economic and policy rounds (Mester, Kaplan, Williams, Bostic, Bullard's comment on raising rates for financial stability). It is prominent but shares the meeting with other topics.
- **Forward guidance.** The current "further gradual increases" language stays, and a December hike is signaled as likely. Many want to move toward data dependence and remove the forward guidance soon, and the Chair says the language "will need to evolve". Net is a mild tightening bias with a softening of the guidance, about 60-62.
- **Uncertainty.** Repeated emphasis on r* uncertainty and Phillips-curve uncertainty. The Chair says "high uncertainty about the reaction of inflation to sustained unemployment in the mid or even low 3s" and calls the circumstances "unprecedented or nearly so". Daly says "more uncertain... than a year ago", and Kaplan and Harker say the same. Others show high confidence in the baseline ("great baseline outlook"). The net is moderately elevated, around 48.

**Polarity checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: "above potential" language implies greater than 50. Done.
- LABOR: low unemployment implies more tightness, so high.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" implies below 50. Done.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: low level implies below 50. Done.
- BANKING_STRESS: sound implies below 50. Done.
- DOLLAR: appreciation implies above 50. Done.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: in this rubric, elevated valuation concern sits at the low end and a sharp correction at the high end. Elevated valuations with a mild decline put it slightly below 50.

**Coherence checks.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (58) against LABOR (74) differ by 16, slightly above the 15 limit. Labor is anchored on the 3.7 percent unemployment level, which the rubric ties to 66-80. Output-gap language is "modestly/well above" from some and "slack remains" from others (Kashkari, Evans, Quarles), so it is pulled toward 50.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (58) against CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (57) are consistent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (44) and CREDIT_SPREADS (28) are both below 50 and co-move. The FC score is higher because of the recent tightening.
4. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, so there is no conflict.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (52) against sub-components (goods 55, services 50, expectations 46) is consistent.

**N/A justifications.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market dysfunction was discussed by participants. INFLATION_HOUSING: shelter prices are not discussed, only housing activity.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Powell: "Inflation is right at our target." Williams: "core inflation remains right at our 2 percent objective". Rosengren: "My own forecast is for a higher inflation rate than in the Tealbook."
- Center of gravity: inflation is at target, with modest anecdotal and tariff upside pressure not yet in the data.
- Direction: inflation is slightly above neutral because Mester, Rosengren and Kaplan see building pressure while Bullard, Kashkari and Harker see little.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 58 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Daly: "The economy continues to grow well above its potential and has pushed past full employment." Brainard: "real GDP growth remaining above its trend rate into next year."
- Center of gravity: most see above-trend growth and a modestly positive gap. Kashkari argues slack remains.
- Direction: the output gap is above neutral because of the above-potential and above-trend language from the majority.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 74 ±5 [High]
- Evidence: Brainard: "The unemployment rate remains at 3.7 percent, the lowest level in almost 50 years." Williams: "The quits rate is the highest since 2001."
- Center of gravity: the market is very tight at an unemployment rate of 3.7 percent. Evans and Kashkari qualify the extent.
- Direction: labor is above neutral because the unemployment rate is below 3.8 percent and shortages are widely reported.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Kashkari: "strong growth continues, mostly driven by strong consumer spending. Investment has weakened notably." Barkin: "Consumer spending remains healthy."
- Center of gravity: strong consumers and about 3 percent GDP, offset by soft business and residential investment.
- Direction: spending is above trend on balance.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 54 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Clarida: "productivity growth in the business sector... is averaging 2 percent". Kashkari: "With 1.3 percent labor productivity growth, unit labor costs are rising at 1.5 percent."
- Center of gravity: productivity is near to slightly above the estimated trend of about 1.4 percent, and its future path is uncertain.
- Direction: productivity is slightly above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 ±6 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Brainard: financial conditions "remain relatively accommodative overall when judged against historical norms." Mester: "Overall financial conditions are accommodative."
- Center of gravity: conditions tightened in the intermeeting period but remain accommodative by historical standards.
- Direction: conditions are below neutral, meaning looser, because of the "accommodative" language. The recent tightening keeps the score from falling lower.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Brainard: "corporate bond spreads are also near the low end of their historical range." Harker: "some risk spreads have increased, although they do remain at low levels."
- Center of gravity: spreads widened this period but stay near the low end of their historical range.
- Direction: spreads are below neutral because of the stated low level. The widening alone does not move the score above 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Mester: "In light of the elevated valuations in the stock market, the decline in equity prices and increased volatility might have been expected." Brainard: "the elevated level of valuations".
- Center of gravity: valuations are seen as somewhat stretched, and the selloff as modest.
- Direction: valuation concern is below neutral on this scale because of the elevated-valuation framing.

### BANKING_STRESS: 33 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Brainard: "the banking sector has much more high-quality capital to absorb potential credit losses." Quarles: risk management at the largest banks is "generally quite a bit better than it was a decade ago."
- Center of gravity: banks are sound, with small pockets of concern (CRE exposure at regional banks, small-bank card delinquencies).
- Direction: stress is below neutral because the banking system is described as well capitalized.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar has appreciated about 1½ percent." Kashkari: "The broad dollar is up 7 percent since the start of the year."
- Center of gravity: the dollar is stronger, with export and emerging-market headwinds cited.
- Direction: dollar strength is above neutral because of appreciation.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS: 55. Tariff and input-cost pass-through (Bostic, Evans, Williams) against Q3 softness in core goods (Williams).
- INFLATION_SERVICES: 50. Williams: core services "actually been quite stable".
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. Housing activity is discussed, but not shelter inflation.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46. Anchored but on the soft side (Clarida, Bullard, Brainard).

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Powell: "this probably leaves us on track to raise the federal funds rate in December." Bullard: "I support alternative A for today. I don't think we should be planning further policy rate increases."
- Center of gravity: the Committee holds this meeting with most expecting a December hike. A minority (Bullard, Kashkari) prefers a pause, and Quarles and Harker are open to delaying.
- Direction: the lean is hawkish, with a tightening bias, because the majority backs gradual increases and most signal a likely December hike.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: the vote read-out records all nine voters as Yes. Kashkari: "pausing right now seems the prudent policy... I would, therefore, prefer alternative A." Quarles: "open to not moving in December".
- Center of gravity: no dissents, but two participants prefer a different alternative and a few others voice reservations on timing. Economic assessments are broadly similar.
- Direction: disagreement is moderate-low because of the stated reservations, with no formal dissent.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 66 ±7 [High]
- Evidence: Mester: "increasing risks with respect to leveraged lending and business credit that concern me." Brainard: "now is the time for banks to be fortifying their capital buffers."
- Center of gravity: a full session and repeated returns to leveraged lending, CLOs, CRE and the CCyB across the go-rounds. The meeting also covers other topics.
- Direction: this is a prominent topic, with most participants discussing it at length.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Powell: "the statement language regarding further gradual increases will need to evolve, and now is the right time to begin laying that groundwork." Clarida: "monetary policy... is not on a preset course."
- Center of gravity: the "further gradual increases" language stays and a December hike is signaled as likely. The Committee is moving toward a more data-dependent statement.
- Direction: the signal is a mild tightening bias, softened by the move toward data dependence.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Powell: "there is high uncertainty about the reaction of inflation to sustained unemployment in the mid or even low 3s." Daly: "I'm more uncertain looking forward than I was perhaps a year ago."
- Center of gravity: many cite uncertainty about r*, the Phillips curve, trade, fiscal fade and the cycle. The baseline outlook is held with confidence.
- Direction: uncertainty is elevated, but several speakers show high conviction.
