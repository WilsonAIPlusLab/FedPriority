# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion), meeting dated 2008-06-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion. The file holds two sessions: the afternoon economic go-round and the long-term-projections discussion (first day), then the morning policy go-round, the vote, and a second special-topic discussion on investment-bank supervision and the emergency liquidity facilities (PDCF/TSLF). It also holds many members' questions to staff (staff answers removed) and a vote read-out at the end (Fisher "No", all other voters "Yes" on alternative B, funds rate held at about 2 percent). Some staff-Q&A passages are question-only fragments. This is not a pure special-topic meeting, so it is scored in full.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2008: the FOMC has cut aggressively since January (rate at 2 percent), Bear Stearns was rescued in March, and incoming data have surprised on the upside. Oil and commodity prices have spiked. I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish camp:
  - Hoenig (wants +25bp now and says the stance is "much more accommodative than necessary").
  - Fisher ("inflation is upon us"; votes for alternative C, the lone dissent).
  - Lacker (tighten soon, willing to stand pat today).
  - Plosser ("take back ... insurance", expectations "fragile").
  - Bullard, Evans (prefers about 2.5 percent), Warsh, Lockhart (later in the year).
- Cautious or balanced camp:
  - Geithner ("soon but not yet", financial fragility acute).
  - Kohn (not extraordinarily accommodative, "we can afford to be a little patient").
  - Rosengren (on hold; excess capacity, core PCE falling).
  - Yellen, Mishkin, Pianalto, Kroszner (next move is up, but wait).
  - Stern, Lockhart (hold now, firming later).
- Chairman Bernanke: hold ("watchful waiting"), tail risks on growth and financial stability still "quite substantial", but "lean, not lurch" toward concern about inflation. He expects to "begin normalizing interest rates relatively soon" and wants to "begin to prepare the markets".

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as clearly above the midpoint. Headline is very high (Lacker: CPI at "8 percent" annualized in May; Plosser: prices-paid index highest "since 1980"; Fisher's CEO testimonials; Bernanke: "very ugly headline inflation numbers"). Core is moderate (Rosengren: core PCE 2.1 percent; Kohn: core "a little to the soft side"). Expectations are a central worry, with a split between Fisher/Plosser and Yellen/Mishkin/Stern, who see them as still anchored. Clearly above target but with core contained, so about 68.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Geithner: growth "significantly below potential"; Rosengren: "growing below potential", "excess capacity"; Evans: "modest resource gaps" persisting; Kohn and Evans see growth near potential only in 2009-10. Data are better than feared, so the gap is modest rather than deep: about 38.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Unemployment is about 5.5 percent and rising toward a 5.5-6 percent peak (Bernanke's summary; Rosengren, Kohn). Slack is cited as restraining wages (Kohn, Geithner). Hoenig's and Fisher's districts are tight locally, but the national read is softening. Score 5.5-7 percent band, upper end: about 38.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend on net. Q2 consumer spending beat expectations but is attributed to the fiscal stimulus and may be borrowed from H2. Fundamentals (wealth, income, credit, energy prices, sentiment) are weak, housing is in "deep slump", motor vehicles are worsening, and Warsh reports a "miserable" June. Business investment and exports are okay: about 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned only briefly. Warsh: "Productivity growth looks, frankly, impressive". Geithner: "a little higher than we thought". Modestly above trend: about 60, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Above the midpoint (tighter). Kohn: "financial conditions are tighter", spreads "unusually wide", bank terms "tightened enough". Geithner: "somewhat tighter than when we last met". Banks are deleveraging and tightening standards (Yellen), securitization is impaired, and LIBOR-OIS is near 70-80bp. Partial offset: improved from the March trough. Distinct from the policy-stance debate, which is about the funds rate. Score about 66.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as market functioning (only the Treasury curve as a vehicle for policy expectations). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is high. Kohn: "almost all spreads remain unusually wide". Rosengren: LIBOR-OIS "quite high by historical standards". Kroszner: "extremely elevated" (about 80bp versus a normal of about 10bp). Offset: Warsh and Plosser say spreads are "well off their March highs" and have "fallen". Level governs: score above 50, about 70.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss price declines (bank, broker and retailer stocks, Rosengren, Kohn, Warsh) with a financials-specific earnings story but no broad valuation view. Default band, slightly elevated for the financial-sector concern: about 56, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Above the midpoint. Yellen: "Bank asset quality continues to deteriorate"; Rosengren: "more, significant difficulties ahead", Lehman, regional banks; Kroszner: monolines, write-downs, high funding costs, regional banks and Federal Home Loan Banks "tapped out"; Bernanke: "systemic risk ... in abeyance". Emerging to significant stress, but no failures yet: about 72.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Little discussion. Bernanke says that in the intermeeting period "the dollar strengthened"; Warsh worries about a weakening dollar if policies diverge; Geithner's forecast assumes the dollar "only modestly weaker". Recent appreciation, mixed forward view: about 54, low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components:
  - Goods: very high (energy, food, steel, import prices from China, cost pass-through anecdotes), about 76.
  - Services: modest pressure (some service components "drifting higher", Pianalto; labor costs subdued), about 54.
  - Housing: only a passing mention of rising rents in Dallas/Houston (Fisher) while home prices fall, so N/A.
  - Expectations: debated, with short-term and survey measures up, long-term "up a bit", and members split; net above anchored, about 62.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure: participants say high, so above 50 matches. OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS below 50 match slack language. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress: "unusually wide" gives above 50, matches. BANKING_STRESS higher = more stress: deterioration language gives above 50, matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter: "tighter" gives above 50, matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds at 2 percent, but the forward lean is clearly toward tightening. Hawks are Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Plosser, Bullard, Evans and Warsh; the Chair and most others say the next move is up. Doves and cautious members are Rosengren, Geithner, Kohn and Mishkin, who want to wait. A hold with a strong tightening bias, short of a majority wanting a hike now: about 60.
- Disagreement: one formal dissent (Fisher, wanting +25bp). Hoenig, a non-voter, also advocated an immediate hike, and Lacker and Plosser argued tightening is needed soon. There is a real debate over how accommodative policy is (Kohn versus Plosser, Lacker, Kroszner exchanges) and over the inflation-expectations read. The tone is collegial and the economic assessments are largely shared. Moderate: about 40.
- Financial stability attention: very high. The economic go-round is heavy on bank balance sheets, Lehman, regional banks and systemic risk, and the second half of the meeting is entirely about supervision of investment banks and the PDCF/TSLF. This rivals the economic assessment: about 80.
- Forward guidance: Bernanke wants to "prepare the market for the normalization", "maintain market expectations toward tightening", and notes the statement is "a little more hawkish". Several members want that signalled. But "lean, not lurch" and a caution about financial fragility: mild to clear tightening bias, about 70.
- Uncertainty: high. Kohn: "defining characteristics ... are uncertainty and risk", shocks "unprecedented in size and persistence"; Geithner: uncertainty "substantial", "fat" tail risks; Bernanke: "Uncertainties about the growth prospects are great", possible "nonlinear, discontinuous change". Some members (Fisher, Hoenig) are confident on inflation. High but not extreme: about 68.

BANKING_STRESS stressed, so over 50 (72): pass. DOLLAR_STRENGTH recent appreciation, so over 50 (54): pass. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "CPI came in at an annual rate of 8 percent in May"; Plosser: prices-paid index "highest level it has been since 1980"; Bernanke: "very ugly headline inflation numbers"; Rosengren: core PCE 2.1 percent.
- Center of gravity: Headline is far above target and cost pass-through is spreading, while core remains moderate.
- Direction: above neutral because most participants see inflation risk as now to the upside and headline as very high.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner: growth "significantly below potential"; Rosengren: "growing below potential ... excess capacity"; Evans: "modest resource gaps".
- Center of gravity: Modest to moderate slack, narrowed by upside data surprises.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe slack and sub-potential growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke's summary: unemployment peak "between 5½ percent and 6 percent"; Rosengren: "further weakening in labor markets".
- Center of gravity: Unemployment is around 5.5 percent and rising, softening nationally despite pockets of tightness (Hoenig, Fisher).
- Direction: below neutral because of the unemployment level and its upward trajectory.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: strength "largely borrowing from the second half"; Warsh: "pretty miserable" June read from card companies; Bernanke: "plunge in sentiment".
- Center of gravity: Q2 beat expectations on stimulus, but the underlying consumer and housing are weak.
- Direction: below trend because fundamentals are weak and the strength is viewed as transitory.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "Productivity growth looks, frankly, impressive"; Geithner: "Productivity growth is a little higher than we thought".
- Center of gravity: Two brief favorable mentions.
- Direction: above neutral because both mentions are favorable.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 66 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "financial conditions are tighter with higher bond rates and lower equity prices"; Geithner: "Overall financial conditions are probably somewhat tighter than when we last met".
- Center of gravity: Credit tight and bank lending standards stricter, partly offset by improvement since March.
- Direction: above neutral (tighter) because members describe tightening credit and impaired securitization.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Not discussed by participants.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 70 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "almost all spreads remain unusually wide"; Kroszner: LIBOR-OIS "still at extremely elevated levels".
- Center of gravity: Spreads are wide in level, with some narrowing from March peaks (Warsh, Plosser).
- Direction: above neutral because the stated level is high; the narrowing from March is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: investment banks "trading substantially below" April levels; Kohn: "sharp declines in equity prices on financials".
- Center of gravity: Price declines in financials discussed without a broad valuation view.
- Direction: slightly above neutral reflecting declines in financials and concern, near the default band.

**BANKING_STRESS: 72 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "Bank asset quality continues to deteriorate. Banks continue to deleverage"; Rosengren: "more, significant difficulties ahead for many financial institutions".
- Center of gravity: Significant stress in regional and investment banks, with systemic risk contained but not gone.
- Direction: above neutral because deterioration, funding strain and capital needs are stressed repeatedly.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the dollar strengthened. The two-year rate rose 50 or 60 basis points"; Warsh: "effect of a weakening dollar".
- Center of gravity: A recent appreciation noted, with mixed forward concern.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because the one factual statement is of strengthening.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 76 [N/A for UNC/CONF per format]**
- Evidence: Fisher: Frito-Lay, Kimberly-Clark and Dow Chemical price increases; Hoenig: steel costs doubled.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 54**
- Evidence: Pianalto: "prices for a number of general service components have been drifting higher"; Lockhart: wage increases "could be a significant factor, particularly in service price inflation".

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62**
- Evidence: Fisher: "inflation expectations are [not] presently well anchored"; Yellen: "reasonably well anchored"; Bernanke: longer-term measures "up a bit on net".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "increasing the fed funds rate target by 25 basis points at this meeting"; Bernanke: "we will begin normalizing interest rates relatively soon".
- Center of gravity: Hold now with a widespread view that the next move is up, and a hawkish minority wanting action sooner.
- Direction: above neutral because the forward lean is toward tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "I am going to vote for alternative C"; Hoenig also argued for an immediate hike; the roll call has one "No".
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent and a visible disagreement on accommodation and expectations, in a collegial tone.
- Direction: moderate because there is a single dissent and shared economic assessments.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 80 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I do not yet rule out the possibility of a systemic event"; a full session on investment-bank supervision and PDCF/TSLF extension.
- Center of gravity: A dominant topic across both days.
- Direction: high attention (direction-agnostic).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "we need to begin to prepare the market for the normalization"; Bernanke: alternative B inflation paragraph is "a little more hawkish".
- Center of gravity: A mild-to-clear tightening signal, tempered by "lean, not lurch".
- Direction: hawkish because the signal is toward future increases.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "defining characteristics of the current situation are uncertainty and risk"; Bernanke: "Uncertainties about the growth prospects are great".
- Center of gravity: Many members emphasize unusually high uncertainty and fat tails on both sides.
- Direction: high because multiple participants stress deep uncertainty and unprecedented shocks.
