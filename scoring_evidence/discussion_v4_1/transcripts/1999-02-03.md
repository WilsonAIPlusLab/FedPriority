# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 1999-02-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). The input holds the Feb 2 afternoon session (organizational items, Q&A on the chart show, Humphrey-Hawkins forecast go-around) and the Feb 3 morning session (M2/M3/debt ranges, Humphrey-Hawkins reauthorization chatter, Q&A on the Bluebook, the Chairman's policy view, the policy go-around, and the vote read-out). Staff turns are removed, so many members' questions appear without answers. Organizational and procedural material is ignored.

**2. Era context.** Early 1999. Text-based backdrop: very strong growth (Q4 1998 GDP reported at 5.6 percent), a 4.4 percent national unemployment rate, very low measured inflation, record stock prices, the aftermath of the fall 1998 financial turmoil and 75 bp of easing, and fresh Brazilian currency problems. Scores below rest on what participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chairman Greenspan: strong growth is unsustainable, yet inflation is "dead in the water". He recommends no change and a symmetric directive, but a strong tightening signal in the testimony if data turn. He argues a productivity and global-capacity "missing variable" has replaced NAIRU.
- Hawkish voices: Broaddus (would undo easing now, prefers asymmetry), Minehan (worried; wants asymmetry and tighter policy), Jordan (wants the November cut reversed, but not now), Meyer (incremental asymmetric Taylor rule), Hoenig (unwind soon), Guynn, Poole (tighten starting in spring), Moskow (policy possibly too loose), Parry (case for asymmetry), Kelley (upside risks decidedly).
- Cautious or dovish voices: Rivlin ("any action ... likely to do more harm than good"), McDonough ("watching and waiting"), Gramlich (sit tight), Boehne (watch and wait), Stern, McTeer, Ferguson (support hold, ready to move).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation as low and below any worrying level (Greenspan: "inflation is actually dead in the water"; McDonough: "dead in the water"; Moskow: no evidence in recent years; Boehne: "inflation low"). Some hawks fear future upward drift (Broaddus: 2.5 percent CPI "still worries me"; Minehan: CPI just below 3 percent by year-end). Per the "score what members report NOW" gotcha, the level is below the midpoint: 36.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Greenspan: "substantial, and what many analysts regard as unsustainable, strength"; Meyer: "already operating beyond the point of sustainability in labor markets"; Broaddus: "domestic demand is growing at an unsustainable pace"; McTeer: three years with growth above potential and unemployment below NAIRU. Bright-line rule needs above 50: 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Unemployment 4.4 percent nationally, 1.5 percent in the Twin Cities, "exceptionally tight" (McTeer), "generation low" (Ferguson), and job seekers falling about a million a year (Greenspan). Level is high: 72.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Well above trend. Q4 growth of 5.6 percent, "booming", "surge", "consumer sentiment and spending remain very strong". The expected slowdown is a forecast, not current: 68.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length. Greenspan: productivity growth of 3.8 percent over the year and 4.8 percent in Q4, a "dramatic pickup" in multifactor productivity; also Parry, Gramlich, Stern. Significantly above trend: 78.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Accommodative or stimulative. Poole: "Credit conditions and monetary growth are both very clearly on the stimulative side"; Ferguson: "accommodative credit conditions"; Minehan: markets "financing just about anything"; stocks at record levels. Some residual spread widening remains. Bright-line rule needs below 50: 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A (see item 9).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The level is stated by Greenspan: junk yields "significantly above Treasuries" and A/AA spreads "that we haven't seen for a very long time". Minehan says spreads are "wider than at earlier points last year" but not shutting out borrowers. Level reading: somewhat wide, so above 50, but only mildly because access is good: 56.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants view stocks as stretched. Ferguson: "levitating above ... reasonable levels"; Minehan: "bursting of the asset bubble, if there is one"; Jordan on buying equities "with not a thought as to what the earnings potential". Gramlich and Parry offer a counter view (E/P consistent with lower real rates). Elevated-valuation concern: 27.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy, with concern about lender aggressiveness. Minehan: "lowest rate of delinquencies in at least a decade", "very good conditions for their own profitability"; Stern: bankers "chasing deals very aggressively". Healthy means below 50: 33 (thin evidence).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only passing mentions. Meyer cites "the appreciation of the dollar" as a past special factor; Poole says the exchange rate "has behaved so well". Mild past strength, not a focus: 57, Low confidence.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more credit stress; Greenspan's "spreads we haven't seen for a very long time" implies more stress than normal, so a score above 50 matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher means more concern/stress. The overvaluation view sits in the low-score range of the rubric (stretched, bubble), so 27 matches. BANKING_STRESS: healthy lenders give a score below 50, which matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the outcome is no change with a symmetric directive ("B"), and the vote was unanimous among voters. The discussion leans toward tightening: Greenspan says consensus is "a shade in the direction of asymmetry", and several would unwind easing soon. Doves hold firm against any move or signal (Rivlin, McDonough). Lean hawkish, hold with a tightening bias: 57.
- Disagreement: there are zero formal dissents in this vote (both roll calls were unanimous). Differences are on the asymmetry/tactics: Broaddus, Minehan, Parry and Meyer wanted asymmetry; Rivlin cautioned against signaling; Jordan notes his November dissent but accepts holding. Minehan says "I do not have a vote so I can't dissent, but I am really concerned." This is reservations on tactics, with economic assessments broadly shared: 27.
- Financial stability attention: moderate. Brazil contagion, asset-price and bubble worries (Minehan, Jordan, Stern), spreads, the fall freeze (Greenspan). Economic assessment dominates: 40.
- Forward guidance: a clear lean toward signaling possible tightening in the Humphrey-Hawkins testimony (Greenspan: "highly desirable" if data tighten; Boehne, Hoenig, Guynn, Broaddus, Poole "starting sometime this spring ... tightening"). Directive symmetric. Mild tightening bias: 64.
- Uncertainty: high. NAIRU "in hiding", models "missing a crucial variable", forecast "too good to be true", the Brazil outcome unknown, multiple participants questioning models (Greenspan, Moskow, Boehne, Rivlin, Broaddus). Some is analytical humility rather than alarm: 62.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 62 > 50: OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 < 50 (accommodative/stimulative language): OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 56 > 50 (stated high level, not just direction): OK.
- BANKING_STRESS 33 < 50 (healthy): OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 > 50 (appreciation language): OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, no dysfunction discussed.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs LABOR 72: 10 apart, within tolerance (labor tightness is more extreme than the output measures).
- OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs CONSUMPTION 68: 6 apart: OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 56: they diverge by 20. Participants call overall conditions easy (low policy rate, stimulative credit and money, record equities) while noting that corporate spreads are still above their pre-turmoil level. This is a real mixed picture in the text, not an inconsistency. The spread score is kept near the midpoint to reflect that.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY: Treasury is N/A.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 vs sub-components (goods 30, services 42, expectations 42): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not discussed by members. The only market-structure remarks are procedural (securities lending, euro bid-ask).
- INFLATION_HOUSING: no discussion of rents or shelter costs as an inflation component (home price and office-rent remarks are regional anecdotes, not shelter inflation).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-8 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "If inflation were not actually dead in the water at this stage..."; Broaddus: 2.5 percent CPI "still worries me"; Moskow: "we have not seen any evidence [of inflation] in recent years."
- Center of gravity: current inflation is low and subdued, with worry confined to the future.
- Direction: below neutral because members see actual price pressure as minimal now.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 62 +-7 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "substantial, and what many analysts regard as unsustainable, strength"; Meyer: "we are already operating beyond the point of sustainability in labor markets."
- Center of gravity: demand and activity run clearly above sustainable potential.
- Direction: above neutral because of repeated "unsustainable" and "above trend" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: McTeer: "Labor markets remain exceptionally tight"; Greenspan: national unemployment "4.4 percent"; Stern: Twin Cities unemployment "about 1½ percent".
- Center of gravity: very tight labor markets nearly everywhere.
- Direction: above neutral because unemployment is at a generation low.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Rivlin: Q4 GDP "5.6 percent with the possibility of an upward revision"; Broaddus: "consumer sentiment and spending remain very strong essentially across the board."
- Center of gravity: well above trend, with deceleration expected but not yet seen.
- Direction: above neutral because current spending and investment are surging.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 78 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "productivity growth of 3.8 percent ... The productivity growth figure for the fourth quarter is 4.8 percent"; Gramlich: growth "may be stepping up by a fairly noticeable amount."
- Center of gravity: productivity growth is seen as exceptionally strong and explains benign inflation.
- Direction: above neutral because of accelerating productivity.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Poole: "Credit conditions and monetary growth are both very clearly on the stimulative side"; Ferguson: "accommodative credit conditions".
- Center of gravity: easy overall, with residual spread widening after the fall turmoil.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because credit is available, money growth is strong, equities at records.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "yields on junk bonds remaining significantly above Treasuries and even obligations rated A and AA still running spreads ... that we haven't seen for a very long time"; Minehan: spreads "wider than at earlier points last year, do not seem to be shutting out borrowers."
- Center of gravity: spreads somewhat above normal but borrowers still have access.
- Direction: above neutral because a high level, not merely a move, was stated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 27 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the stock market does seem to be levitating above what one might think of as reasonable levels"; Minehan: "a bursting of the asset bubble, if there is one".
- Center of gravity: stocks viewed as richly valued, with a minority arguing falling real rates justify them.
- Direction: valuation concern is elevated (low score on this scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Minehan: insurer reported "the lowest rate of delinquencies in at least a decade"; Stern: "bankers still seem to be chasing deals very aggressively."
- Center of gravity: banks and lenders seen as healthy; the concern is aggressiveness, not impairment.
- Direction: below neutral because lenders are described as healthy and profitable.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: Meyer: special factors including "the appreciation of the dollar"; Poole: exchange rate "has behaved so well because of the sustained low rate of inflation."
- Center of gravity: passing references to past appreciation, no active concern.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because appreciation, not weakness, is the only direction mentioned.

**Inflation sub-components**
- INFLATION_GOODS 30: Greenspan: "tradable goods prices are being significantly held down by excess world capacity"; high-tech prices falling 17 to 18 percent a year.
- INFLATION_SERVICES 42: Greenspan: service price inflation "has been restrained appreciably"; offset by medical-care price increases (Minehan, Guynn).
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 42: Poole: "the expectation of continuing low inflation is the factor dominating this outcome"; Minehan fears expectations could turn negative if policy stays easy.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-5 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the consensus is probably a shade in the direction of asymmetry"; Poole: "starting sometime this spring, I think it is going to be appropriate to be tightening up on the funds rate."
- Center of gravity: hold now, with a clear tightening bias among many members and a dovish minority that wants no signal.
- Direction: slightly hawkish because the discussion leans toward unwinding easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: both roll calls unanimous; Minehan: "I do not have a vote so I can't dissent, but I am really concerned"; Broaddus: "I am probably the only person in the room who feels this way [undo easing now]."
- Center of gravity: no dissents, with reservations on asymmetry and timing among a few members.
- Direction: low-moderate disagreement because differences are tactical.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: the fall action "was directed at countering a freezing-up of financial markets"; Jordan: expectations that prices only go up "ultimately become destabilizing".
- Center of gravity: several members address asset prices, Brazil, and spreads meaningfully, but the economy and inflation dominate.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "a fairly strong signal in the Humphrey-Hawkins testimony that we might tighten would be wholly appropriate"; Guynn: "put people on notice that we are in fact prepared to tighten".
- Center of gravity: conditional tightening signal planned for the testimony, while the directive stays symmetric.
- Direction: hawkish because the forward message points to possible tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 (High)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "Something has to give, but we don't know when"; Moskow: "there is a lot we don't know."
- Center of gravity: many members question models, NAIRU, and the sustainability of the good outcome.
- Direction: elevated to high.
