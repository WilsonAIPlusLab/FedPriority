# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2013-12-18

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, v2 format (staff turns removed). It covers a two-day meeting: the Dec 17 session (staff Q&A, a financial-stability comment round, economic go-round) and the Dec 18 policy go-round, followed by a roll-call read-out. Many Q&A turns appear without the staff answers, so some questions are only partly intelligible; none of the scored content is garbled.

**Era context.** QE3 is running at $85bn a month, the funds rate is at the lower bound with 6.5 percent unemployment threshold guidance, and the first taper is the decision on the table. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.** Almost all members speak. In the economic round: Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Evans, Lockhart, Fisher, Williams, Pianalto, Bullard, Plosser, George, Lacker, Dudley, Yellen, Tarullo, Stein, Powell, then the Chairman's summary. In the policy round: Lockhart, Rosengren, Plosser, Evans, Bullard, Williams, Fisher, George, Pianalto, Lacker, Kocherlakota, Yellen, Tarullo, Stein, Powell, Dudley. Roll call: 9 Yes, 1 No (Rosengren).

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. They cite PCE at 0.7 percent and core at 1.1 percent (Evans, Rosengren), call it "unwelcome", "troubling" and "disturbingly" lower (Kocherlakota), and say it is below target for years. Most expect a gradual rise because expectations are anchored. Dudley and Powell are less worried and call the low readings special factors. Not deflationary: Rosengren notes deflation risk only after a large shock, and Fisher says the deflation risk is mitigated. Score about 28 (the 11-25 "very low" band is too low, the 26-40 band fits).
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Growth of about 2 percent is expected to pick up to about 3 percent. Evans cites "diminishing resource slack"; Dudley says "the amount of slack in the economy is gradually diminishing"; Rosengren wants evidence of growth "above potential" that is not yet there. Score 37.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Unemployment is 7.0 percent, the employment-to-population ratio is flat (Kocherlakota), and wages are not accelerating. There are only spot shortages (Lockhart, Fisher, Bernanke's summary). Williams, Bullard and Plosser argue the unemployment rate is a good slack measure. Level-based score 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumer spending is strengthening (retail sales, autos, wealth effects; Dudley, Yellen, Bernanke's summary) but business fixed investment is "inexplicably weak" (Dudley), housing is softening (Yellen, Dudley, Stein), and Lacker is skeptical. The net is roughly at or just below trend. Score 46.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed in passing and negatively. Yellen and Powell worry about a "decline in structural productivity growth" or supply-side damage, and Fisher notes firms extracting productivity gains while GDP is weak. Below trend, thin evidence. Score 40, Low.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (accommodative). Tarullo cites "relatively accommodative financial conditions" supporting equity and housing wealth, and Fisher cites "sustained low interest rates, abundant credit". Many worry that the taper might tighten conditions, which is a forward risk, not the current state. Score 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members talk about yields, term premia and the summer 2013 sell-off (Bernanke, Dudley, Evans), which are rate moves, not dysfunction. Bright-line rule keeps this in the normal band. Brief and indirect. Score 40, Low.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No explicit spread levels are given. Fisher reports "abundant credit", banks "rushing in to provide credit", pricing and tenor pressure, and deteriorating leveraged-loan quality (second liens). Dudley discusses loosening covenants. That points to easy credit, so below normal. Score 35, Low.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Mixed valuation concern. Bullard says the S&P forward P/E "doesn't look too out of line" while small caps look rich. Fisher notes margin debt up 25 percent and earnings driven by buybacks. Tarullo and Dudley see equity gains as supporting spending. Modest valuation concern, so slightly below the midpoint on this construct. Score 42.
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A. The only remarks are Fisher's on competitive lending by banks with excess balance-sheet capacity; no assessment of bank health or stress.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. The dollar is not discussed.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods: Plosser "goods prices have been falling" and the Chairman's summary says commodity prices are driving the decline (goods 30). Services: "prices of services have been stable" (Plosser), about 2 percent (Chairman's summary), with medical services soft (Yellen) (services 47). Housing: only house-price level comments (Fisher), not shelter inflation, so N/A. Expectations: "well anchored" or "stable" from nearly everyone, with a slight Livingston decline noted by Plosser (47).

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The outcome is a small first taper ($10bn) paired with strengthened low-rate guidance and stronger inflation language. The Chairman and others stress the aim is to keep accommodation about where it is ("tapering is not a tightening"). Hawkish pole: Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Lockhart and George want a larger or more fixed-path taper and less rate pushback. Dovish pole: Rosengren (no taper, lower threshold), Kocherlakota (no taper), and Evans (prefers no taper, supports B as third best). Center remains accommodative with QE still running at $75bn a month. Lean about 38.
- Disagreement: 1 formal dissent (Rosengren, No). Kocherlakota (not a voter this year) argues strongly against tapering. Evans and Tarullo preferred waiting but support B. Lockhart, Plosser, Fisher, Lacker and George voice reservations, and Plosser and Bullard exchange sharply over state-contingency ("Chinese water torture" versus "optimal policy"). Economic assessments are largely shared; the split is mostly about tactics and communication, with some difference over how worrying low inflation is. Moderate, about 45.
- Financial-stability attention: A dedicated financial-stability item with staff presentation and survey on costs of asset purchases is part of the meeting. Kocherlakota gives a long structured argument, Stein builds a framework, Fisher raises margin debt, leveraged loans and buybacks, George notes imbalances, and the Chairman calls financial stability the cost he takes most seriously. Many speakers also worry about tightening of financial conditions from the taper. Moderate to prominent, about 58.
- Forward guidance: Rate guidance is strongly easing ("well past" 6.5 percent, inflation floor language, SEP median path). The asset-purchase guidance signals further measured reductions. Net strong easing bias on rates; score 16.
- Uncertainty: Many say "far from certain" (Yellen), "can't say I know that growth has stepped up" (Evans), "be humble about our forecast" (Rosengren), asterisks on forecasts (Lockhart, Lacker, Dudley), "puzzle" over inflation (Plosser, Dudley, Chairman), and "provisional" assessments of labor market damage (Tarullo). But Bullard, Williams and Powell are fairly confident on growth. Somewhat elevated, 42.

**Polarity checks.** OUTPUT_GAP: higher = more above potential; evidence says below, score 37 (<50, consistent). LABOR_TIGHTNESS: evidence says slack, 33. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence "accommodative", 36 (<50). CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence says easy credit, 35 (<50). BANKING and DOLLAR: N/A.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 satisfied; FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 satisfied; CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 satisfied (level-based, with Low confidence); TREASURY_STRESS held at 40 (no dysfunction described).

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 37 vs LABOR 33: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 37 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 46: gap 9, within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 35 co-move. FC vs TREASURY 40: normal times, near 40-50. INFLATION_PRESSURE 28 vs sub-components (goods 30, services 47, expectations 47): the parent is lower than the average of the sub-components because participants anchor on headline PCE at 0.7 percent and "well below target", with medical, import and banking-services components dragging; sub-component scores describe direction and stability of each category, so a modest divergence is accepted.

**N/A justification.** BANKING_STRESS: bank health not assessed. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not mentioned. INFLATION_HOUSING: only house-price levels were mentioned, not shelter inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 28 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Evans, "Total PCE inflation moved down to 0.7 percent... core PCE inflation remains at 1.1 percent... well below" the 2 percent objective; Kocherlakota, the Tealbook forecasts "inflation will be below target for seven consecutive years." Center of gravity: inflation is far below target, expected to rise only gradually. Direction: below neutral because nearly every participant calls it well below objective.

**OUTPUT_GAP 37 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "the amount of slack in the economy is gradually diminishing"; Rosengren, wants evidence of growth "above potential" before tapering. Center of gravity: slack remains but is diminishing. Direction: below neutral because the economy is still below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 33 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kocherlakota, the employment-to-population ratio at 58.6 percent is "basically the same as it was two years ago"; Rosengren, unemployment of 7 percent "remains above the peak unemployment rate of the previous recession." Center of gravity: improving but still slack, spot shortages only. Direction: below neutral since 7 percent unemployment with weak participation.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 46 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "Consumer spending appears to be on a firmer trajectory" but "Business fixed investment still seems inexplicably weak"; Lacker, "modest fluctuations around a modest average growth rate." Center of gravity: consumers pick up, capital spending and housing lag. Direction: just below neutral, net trend-like.

**PRODUCTIVITY 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Yellen, a "more persistent decline in structural productivity growth"; Powell, "the case is for still more supply-side adjustments." Center of gravity: modest sub-trend productivity, discussed briefly. Direction: below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Tarullo, "relatively accommodative financial conditions" supporting wealth effects; Fisher, "Sustained low interest rates, abundant credit." Center of gravity: conditions are supportive, with worry that the taper could tighten them. Direction: below neutral (loose/accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Dudley, "the yield curve is already very steep... five-year, five-year forward... about 4¼ percent"; Bernanke, term premiums fell "to a very negative level" then "market instability" over the summer. Center of gravity: rate and term-premium moves, no dysfunction. Direction: near the normal band, not stressed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 35 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Fisher, banks "rushing in to provide credit" with "loan tenor, structure, and pricing pressures"; Fisher, leveraged-loan quality "deteriorating." Center of gravity: easy credit with loosening terms. Direction: below neutral (easy availability, level inferred).

**EQUITY_VALUATION 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bullard, forward P/E "doesn't look too out of line compared with historical valuations"; Fisher, NYSE margin debt "up 25 percent year over year... sending to me something of a signal." Center of gravity: some valuation and leverage concern, not bubble talk. Direction: slightly toward elevated valuation, below 50.

**BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH:** N/A.

**INFLATION_GOODS 30; INFLATION_SERVICES 47; INFLATION_HOUSING N/A; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47.** Evidence: Plosser, "The prices of services have been stable, while goods prices have been falling"; Williams, "inflation expectations remain well anchored."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 38 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "the first modest reduction in the pace of purchases" with "a highly accommodative stance"; Bernanke, "keep accommodation reasonably close to where it is now." Center of gravity: a small taper that most call non-tightening, with hawks wanting more and doves wanting none. Direction: below neutral because accommodation is intended to persist and QE continues.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 45 +-6 (High).** Evidence: roll call, Rosengren "No"; Kocherlakota, "I would recommend against tapering at this time"; Plosser versus Bullard, "Chinese water torture" versus "optimal policy." Center of gravity: one dissent plus many stated reservations on tactics. Direction: moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kocherlakota, "financial stability considerations should not influence the current stance of monetary policy" but "will loom larger"; Bernanke, "the most convincing cost was financial stability." Center of gravity: a dedicated item plus recurring concern about financial conditions tightening. Direction: moderate-to-prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 16 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bullard, the "well past" language shows the "strengthened commitment to keeping rates lower for longer"; Williams, guidance that rates stay low "well below 6½ percent." Center of gravity: strong easing guidance on rates alongside gradual taper path. Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "I am still far from certain"; Evans, "I can't say I know that growth has stepped up to a higher level yet." Center of gravity: many hedge on growth, inflation and market reaction, but several are fairly confident. Direction: somewhat elevated.
