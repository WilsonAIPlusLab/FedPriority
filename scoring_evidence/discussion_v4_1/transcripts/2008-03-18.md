# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) - 2008-03-18

Part 1: omitted in this run

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 64 +-6 (High)**
- Lockhart: "the inflation picture has become quite troubling. Headline inflation ... has been elevated since late summer, as have measures of core inflation, though less so."
- Plosser: "little moderation in price pressures"; "future-prices-paid index is at its highest level since the early 1980s"; Lacker: "overall CPI is still up over 4 percent year over year"; Hoenig: core PCE "considerably above" the Committee's projections. Bernanke: "I'm very concerned about it."
- Counterweight: Yellen and Rosengren expect excess capacity to reduce inflation; Yellen notes compensation "quite moderately" up, unit labor costs up "less than 1 percent".
- Clearly above target and described as troubling by most; doves call the risk manageable. Above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +-7 (High)**
- Rosengren: "a very weak economy and that, in all likelihood, we have entered a recession"; "substantial excess capacity"; Kohn: "despite the greater output gap"; Lacker: "compelling evidence that a recession is in train."
- Most participants say recession has begun or is likely; slack building but not yet described as deep (Greenbook "mild recession" seen as reasonable by Mishkin). Below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 45 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Rosengren: "significantly weaker labor markets"; "three months of declining private payroll employment"; but "the housing market has been quite weak, despite relatively low unemployment rates."
- Kroszner: "We had a very low unemployment rate and a very low rate of wage inflation"; unemployment "below 5 percent" with forecasts of 5.5-6 percent.
- Level still near the natural rate but deteriorating fast (payrolls falling, Manpower CEO reports clients trimming staff). Slightly below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 30 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: housing, finance and commodity shocks "have now spread to most corners of the economy, including the major segments of consumption and business fixed investment"; "Exports represent about the only source of strength"; retail sales declines not seen "for almost fifteen years."
- Lacker: "flat-to-sluggish consumption", nonresidential construction decline "only the beginning"; Warsh: business cap-ex "as threatened as has been represented today."
- Counterweight: Evans and Kroszner, "spending is not collapsing", "no collapse but certainly a continued downtrend." Contracting / sharp pullback. Below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-8 (Low)**
- Yellen (only mention): "productivity growth has been fairly robust."
- One participant, brief. Slightly above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 82 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: "financial conditions have unambiguously tightened"; Kohn: "financial conditions have tightened for everybody but the government"; "illiquid markets, extreme volatility, deleveraging, margin calls, forced sales."
- Mishkin: "we are in the worst financial crisis that we've experienced in the post-World War II era"; Warsh: a credit intermediation system that is "broken"; Geithner: "exceptionally tight financial conditions relative to the given target fed funds rate."
- Crisis-level tightness described by nearly every participant. Bright line: "tight/strained" → >50, satisfied.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +-10 (Low)**
- Kohn: "a flight to safety and liquidity"; "business bond yields have risen as well, even with Treasury rates going down."
- Fisher: "When I was nine years old, three-month Treasury bills were trading where they are today"; question whether bunds trade through Treasuries.
- Only flight-to-quality yield declines; no participant describes Treasury market dysfunction. Bright line: yield moves alone are not dysfunction, so within normal range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 80 +-6 (High)**
- Rosengren: "Credit spreads have widened significantly over the past six weeks, with many spreads more than 50 basis points higher than at the last meeting."
- Warsh: GSE and even Ginnie Mae spreads "have widened substantially"; "maybe a third of the credit availability of the real economy has been taken out"; Kroszner: "Obviously the markets are closed"; Evans: CMBS "dried up" and other sources "had dried up, too"; Pianalto: "some credit channels are closing down."
- Level stated through crisis language ("worst financial crisis", markets "closed", availability sharply cut), not direction only. Wide to severely wide. Bright line satisfied (>50 on stated high level).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 +-8 (Low)**
- Yellen: net changes "negative across the board in both the equity and the credit markets"; Kohn: "equity prices falling"; Warsh: broker-dealers with "falling share prices"; Bernanke: low rates help "in keeping bank share prices from entirely collapsing."
- Price declines reported with no valuation view; per V4.1.1 rule 2, 45-55 on the side of the move, CONF Low.

**BANKING_STRESS: 72 +-7 (High)**
- Warsh: "this broad class is systematically undercapitalized"; Pianalto: "banks and other financial institutions are getting squeezed from both sides of their balance sheets"; Rosengren: CDS rise for "Washington Mutual and Lehman Brothers indicates increased concerns for the solvency of other large financial institutions"; "the potential ... for runs on additional financial firms is significant."
- Counterweight: Geithner, "very hard to make the judgment now that ... the banking system as a whole is undercapitalized" (though "short of capital relative to what would be ideal"); Plosser and Hoenig: District/ag banks relatively unscathed and healthy; Stern: depositories in better shape than 1990.
- Significant stress (Bear Stearns, solvency concerns), moderated by "system sound on average" views. Above 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 18 +-6 (High)**
- Plosser: "the dollar has fallen sharply"; Warsh: "the accelerated depreciation of the dollar is troubling ... risks of a disorderly move on the dollar ... are hard to discount"; Lockhart: "growing mention of the possibility of a dollar currency crisis."
- Sharp, accelerating depreciation. Bright line: depreciation → <50, satisfied.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 70**
- Fisher: food producers "intending to pass through price increases of somewhere between 5 and 15 percent"; Wal-Mart budgeting "a little over 5 percent" on food items; Evans: first wallboard price increase in 20 months; Plosser: "significant increases in prices, particularly of imported inputs."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** - participants do not discuss services prices beyond a passing freight-surcharge remark.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** - house-price declines are discussed as an asset/wealth issue; no participant discusses rents or shelter costs.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 63**
- Lacker: "I believe inflation expectations no longer qualify as well anchored"; Plosser: "I am concerned that we are seeing those warning signs"; Hoenig: expectations "may be moving higher."
- Counterweight: Mishkin, "not a big increase but a slight increase ... on the order of about 10 basis points", "reasonably well contained"; Kroszner: "doesn't suggest that our inflation credibility has been lost"; Yellen: index "still within the range of variation."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 +-7 (High)**
- Bernanke: "I would recommend the 75 basis point reduction"; Yellen: "I don't believe in gradualism ... I'd like to see the funds rate moved down to where it needs to go sooner rather than later"; Mishkin: "from an insurance perspective, we should actually be doing more than 75 basis points"; Rosengren: "I strongly prefer alternative A" and expects "further easing will be necessary."
- Hawkish minority: Hoenig "I would much prefer to hold"; Plosser prefers "a pause today"; Fisher "75 basis points ... is way too much"; Lacker "ease much less aggressively"; Sapenaro and Lockhart reluctantly accept 75 having preferred less; inflation language ratcheted up.
- Large majority endorses an aggressive 75bp cut to 2 1/4 percent; hawkish resistance and reluctant supporters pull slightly toward the upper part of strong dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 64 +-7 (High)**
- Roll call: Fisher No, Plosser No (two formal dissents out of ten voters).
- Non-voting opposition: Hoenig ("I think this is a mistake"), Lacker ("my threshold has been crossed"), Sapenaro (prefers alternative D, accepts 75 "with some trepidation"), Lockhart (prefers 50). Sharp exchange Mishkin-Fisher on policy efficacy ("I think it is just plain wrong"; Fisher: "a very poor analogy"); Geithner vs. credibility hawks; Geithner-Hoenig on public dispersion.
- Clear camps with different prescriptions, multiple dissents, disagreement on the inflation-expectations assessment itself.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 82 +-6 (High)**
- Lockhart: "In the current circumstances, financial stability must be priority one"; Kroszner: "macro stability is probably the primary thing"; Mishkin: "worst financial crisis"; extended discussion of PDCF, tri-party repo, Bear Stearns, capital, solvency by Geithner, Lacker, Warsh, Kroszner, Rosengren.
- Dominant topic rivalling the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Fisher on alternative A: "It really says that we're not done"; Rosengren: "even with this move, further easing will be necessary"; Evans: "I expected that we would have to go beyond that"; Kohn: "reasonable to await more evidence about whether at some point we need to go further."
- Counterweight: added inflation language "ratchet up at least our verbal attention to inflation" (Bernanke); Plosser, Lacker, Warsh: markets "are going to need to be disappointed."
- Easing bias retained, partly offset by stronger inflation language.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 74 +-7 (High)**
- Kohn: "the extraordinary uncertainty we are dealing with"; Kroszner: "uncharted territory in terms of real and nominal price declines"; Evans: "substantial uncertainty over the length and breadth of this process"; Rosengren: housing effects "difficult to capture in statistical models"; Mishkin: downside risk "just plain scary"; Stern: "I don't know"; Bernanke: "I just don't have much confidence about the timing."
- Many participants emphasize deep, model-breaking uncertainty.
