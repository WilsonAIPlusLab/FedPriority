# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1994-04-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). Staff turns are removed, so the file holds the Chairman's opening remarks, members' questions to staff, and a one-pass round of member comments on a single proposal: a 25 bp funds rate increase today, with the discount rate left unchanged. It reads like a short meeting held between regular meetings. The Chairman refers to "the May 17 FOMC meeting", "move between meetings" and "another call". About 2,800 words, but the substantive content is the Chairman's statement plus roughly 14 short member comments. There is no formal economic go-around.

**2. Era context.** The text itself places this in the middle of a tightening sequence: "as we move for the third time" (Keehn), "March 22 meeting", funds rate at 3.75 percent after the move, a prior sharp decline in stock and bond prices. I score only what is said.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (proposal, financial-market and inventory reasoning); Broaddus (prefers a 1/2 point discount rate move, rising prices); McDonough (economy "clearly a good deal stronger", markets healthier, 25 bp better than more); Boehne (growth solid, 25 bp leaves us behind the curve, wants the discount rate raised); Keehn (agrees with Boehne); Parry (agrees with Greenspan, expects a bigger move next time); Stern (backup in long rates means slower growth later, wealth destroyed); Forrestal (growth faster than expected, would have done 50 bp, wants to quell inflation expectations); Melzer (favors a stronger move to show commitment to price stability); Hoenig (agrees); Minehan (favors 25 bp, discount rate later). Laware, Boehne and Minehan also ask questions in the staff Q&A.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Broaddus reports "more frequent comments of rising prices and expected future increases in prices, especially for basic materials". Forrestal wants to "put to rest this inflationary expectation". Greenspan's "cyclical pressures" from lead times and inventories is a risk, not yet a realized price problem. No one says inflation is currently high. Score 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: The economy is described as "clearly a good deal stronger than we thought" (McDonough), "moving much faster than most of us would have expected" (Forrestal), and policy "still leaves us behind the curve" (Boehne, Greenspan). Nobody says "above potential" or "below potential" explicitly. Strength relative to expectations and being behind the curve imply modestly above-trend operation. Score 56, with moderate uncertainty. The bright-line rule is not triggered by explicit wording.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor markets are not discussed (only "growth is widespread" and loan demand). N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: "tremendous strength in motor vehicle sales" (McDonough, though likely not to continue), "growth is solid" (Boehne), "quite good growth" (Forrestal), inventory build-up. Above trend. Score 63.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Conditions have tightened. Stock and bond prices fell sharply, long rates backed up, "wealth has been destroyed" (Stern), markets "still precarious" (Greenspan) but "healthier" (McDonough). Not stressed or frozen. Score 58, which is above 50, consistent with the bright-line rules (no "accommodative" language).
- TREASURY_STRESS: The bond market is discussed (the sharp bond price decline, "selling begetting selling" as a low-probability danger, portfolios moving "from weak to stronger hands"). This is yield movement and portfolio reshuffling with a feared but not realized dislocation, not dysfunction. Score 50, low-moderate confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: not discussed. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: The decline "defused a significant part of the bubble", but "we have an awful lot left in there". Participants still see valuation as stretched, only partly corrected. Lower = more overvaluation concern. Score 38.
- BANKING_STRESS: only "increases in loan demand". No view on the banking system. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58: higher = more pressure, evidence says modestly rising prices and expectations concern, so 58 matches.
- EQUITY_VALUATION 38: higher = more stress/undervaluation concern. Evidence says a bubble partly deflated but still overvalued, so below 50 matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58: higher = tighter. Evidence says tightened after a sharp market decline. Matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: unanimous support for a 25 bp increase done between meetings, the third increase. Broaddus, Forrestal (would have done 50) and Melzer prefer more. Boehne says 25 "on economic grounds still leaves us behind the curve". The Chairman and Parry expect further moves, perhaps larger, with the funds rate "somewhat above 4 percent". Only McDonough and Minehan lean toward restraint on the discount rate (tactical). Stern notes slower later growth, a mild counterpoint. Majority favors tightening, with a hawkish tail. Score 70.
- Disagreement: no formal dissents and no vote read-out in the text; the Chairman asks for objections and Minehan says "no objection". The differences are tactical: size (25 vs 50) and whether to raise the discount rate now (Boehne, Keehn want it; Greenspan, McDonough, Forrestal, Parry, Minehan do not). Two or three clear reservations on tactics, none on the economy. Score 24, low end of "moderate differences".
- Financial stability attention: Large share of the Chairman's statement concerns the bubble, portfolio restructuring, "surface tension", 1987 comparison, and risk to financial markets. McDonough, Boehne and Stern also address market conditions. Substantial but secondary to the rate decision. Score 55.
- Forward guidance: Funds rate to "in excess of 4 percent" eventually; probable discount rate move at the next meeting, possibly 50 bp; "I don't know how much above 4 percent we're going to have to be". Clear signal of further tightening, tempered by the market-risk caution. Score 76.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan "I don't know how much above 4 percent"; markets "precarious"; "it's much too soon to conclude" about inventories; "low probability ... consequences ... very large". Mostly confident on direction; uncertainty is on size and market risk. Score 40.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP: no explicit above/below potential wording, score 56 is >50 and consistent with the "behind the curve" language. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 (>50, no accommodative language). CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH are N/A. TREASURY_STRESS 50, below the >60 threshold since no dysfunction was described.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (56) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (63): diverge by 7, within tolerance. LABOR N/A, so that pair is not checked. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads N/A. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 vs TREASURY_STRESS 50: consistent, since a normal-functioning but sharply repricing market. INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 vs sub-components (goods 60, expectations 62): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** LABOR_TIGHTNESS, PRODUCTIVITY, CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants. Building materials are goods, not housing services.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Broaddus: "we are hearing more frequent comments of rising prices and expected future increases in prices, especially for basic materials like paper, building materials, and textiles." Forrestal: "it's time that we tried to put to rest this inflationary expectation that is in the market."
Summary: Price pressures are emerging but described as reports and expectations, not an acceleration. Direction: above neutral because participants cite rising materials prices and a need to anchor expectations.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: McDonough: "the economy is clearly a good deal stronger than we thought it was at the last meeting." Boehne: a 25 bp move "almost surely leaves us behind the curve."
Summary: Strong growth and being behind the curve imply modestly above-trend activity, without explicit potential language. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: McDonough: "tremendous strength in motor vehicle sales"; Forrestal: "This economy is moving much faster than most of us would have expected."
Summary: Spending is solid to strong, with an inventory build also noted. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "the sharp declines in both stock and bond prices since our last meeting"; Stern: "wealth has been destroyed, at least a bit."
Summary: Markets repriced and long rates backed up, with conditions tighter but orderly. Direction: above neutral (tighter).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 50 +-10 Low.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "selling begetting selling. That is potentially quite dangerous. I think it has a low probability at all times."
Summary: Bond market is repricing with a feared but unrealized dislocation. Direction: at neutral because no dysfunction is described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.**

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-10 Low.**
Evidence: Greenspan: the declines "have defused a significant part of the bubble"; "we have an awful lot left in there."
Summary: A valuation bubble is viewed as partly, not fully, corrected. Direction: below neutral (residual overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A.** **DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60.** Basis: Broaddus on paper, building materials, textiles rising.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62.** Basis: Forrestal "this inflationary expectation that is in the market"; McDonough "not appearing to know more than the market does about inflation expectations"; Melzer on whether the Fed "really is committed to price stability."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we should adjust the funds rate by 25 basis points today"; Forrestal: "I would have been prepared this morning to move even 50 basis points."
Summary: A third consecutive tightening move between meetings with several members wanting more. Direction: hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 Moderate.**
Evidence: Minehan: "we have no objection to the 25 basis points"; disagreement is over the discount rate (Boehne, Keehn: raise it now; Greenspan, McDonough, Forrestal, Minehan: not yet).
Summary: Unanimous on the funds rate move, with tactical differences on size and the discount rate only. No vote read-out appears in the text, so the lack of formal dissents is inferred from the exchange.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we have to be careful about breaking this so-called surface tension of the market"; Boehne: "You have ably laid out ... the financial market risks."
Summary: Market fragility shapes the choice of a 25 bp rather than a larger move, and several members address it.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 76 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we've got to move the funds rate up somewhat above 4 percent"; "we may choose in the next meeting to move the discount rate 50 basis points."
Summary: Further tightening is signaled explicitly, with the pace conditional on market calm.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "I don't know how much above 4 percent we're going to have to be"; "much too soon to conclude that that process is irreversibly under way."
Summary: Somewhat elevated uncertainty about the extent of tightening and market dynamics, but conviction on direction.
