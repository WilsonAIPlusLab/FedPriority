## Part 2: Evidence and Scores (participants only; staff turns absent from the input)

Document type: two-day meeting transcript (reinvestment policy, SEP changes, financial stability comments, economic go-around, policy go-around, vote). Not special-topic: contains a go-around on the policy stance and a roll-call vote on alternative B.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-6 (Moderate-High)**
- Brainard: "Inflation remains noticeably and persistently below our 2 percent target"; Yellen: headline PCE "likely to remain close to zero until early 2016", core "close to 1 1/4 percent"; Powell: "Current readings, especially headline, are well below that objective"; Mester: "Inflation continues to run below our 2 percent target, but recent readings show signs of firming."
- Offsetting: Lacker core CPI "2.4 percent" over four months; Holcomb CPI "2.9 percent", PCE "2.1 percent" since January. Most expect a return to 2% only gradually. Score: below target, firming.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +-5 (Moderate-High)**
- Evans: "resource gaps essentially closing by late 2016"; Holcomb: "Slack continues to be eliminated"; Williams: "growth above trend... continued progress in closing output and unemployment gaps"; Brainard: "some slack still remains"; Tarullo: "nontrivial amount of additional slack". Below potential but closing; bright-line (<50) satisfied.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 46 +-5 (High)**
- Williams: "closing in on full employment"; Mester: "at or nearly at the Fed's mandated... maximum employment"; Holcomb (Texas): "Texas, at least, is at full employment"; Lacker/Lockhart: tightening anecdotes, "Hiring challenges continued to intensify"; Powell: unemployment 5.3%, "still some amount of slack"; Brainard: "some slack still remains, even with overall unemployment at 5.3 percent"; Yellen: "we still have a ways to go". Near but slightly short of full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-5 (Moderate-High)**
- Brainard: "Consumption remains moderate"; "fairly tepid increases in investment spending"; Mester: "business equipment spending remains subdued", consumer spending "pickup"; Fischer: "consumer spending has been disappointing"; Dudley: household spending "well supported". Moderate, net slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 34 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Brainard: "strikingly weak measured productivity growth"; Mester: "weakness in labor productivity growth may persist" but "too early to conclude much from the recent data". Below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-10 (Low)**
- Kocherlakota: "term premiums remarkably low"; "Risk premiums themselves don't seem that high"; Tarullo: "we don't have generalized asset levels above historical norms"; Powell/Fischer: Greece and China risks diminished; Mester: market participants report "liquidity problems in the fixed-income market." Mildly supportive overall; indirect evidence. Bright-line (<50 for supportive) satisfied.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low)**
- Mester: "market participants are telling us that there are liquidity problems in the fixed-income market, and yet our measures really don't seem to be picking that up"; Williams: "markets do seem to be sufficiently deep"; Evans/Dudley year-end liquidity concerns are tied to the liftoff calendar. Orderly, only anecdotal concern.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-10 (Low; rests on availability and risk-premium wording)**
- Kocherlakota: "Risk premiums themselves don't seem that high"; Dudley: "easing of lending standards for residential mortgages"; Mester: "steady loan growth"; Rosengren: rapid growth of highly leveraged lending; George: "less tightening of energy financing than had been expected." No explicit spread level; ample credit availability, somewhat below normal stress.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-10 (Low)**
- Bullard: "financial wealth-to-income ratio is higher today than it was in 1999 or 2007"; Kocherlakota: staff-described valuation pressures "notable" but he attributes them to low term premiums; Dudley: stock market "at a multiple of book value" while big banks stay depressed; Fischer: Chinese stock-market correction. Mildly elevated valuation concern, little direct discussion.

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Williams: financial-sector vulnerability is "essentially none on the quantitative side"; Tarullo: "Very little of this stuff is held on the balance sheet of regulated institutions"; Dudley: "some very large banks... still selling at a discount [to] book value"; Mester: "banks... leveraged loan guidance." Sound system, residual concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 70 +-5 (High)**
- Williams: "dollar appreciation, which has been sizable over the past year and may well continue"; Brainard: "exchange rate is up an additional 3 percent since" June; Fischer: "appreciation of the dollar that has significantly reduced aggregate demand"; Powell: risk of the dollar strengthening "more than forecast." Notable appreciation; bright-line (>50) satisfied.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 28 (value only)**
- Powell: "core inflation is also being held down about 40 basis points by the indirect effects of energy and import prices"; Evans: "downward price pressures" in commodity markets; Brainard: "dollar appreciation and falling commodity prices."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 50 (value only)**
- Dudley: "some scope for higher inflation from a firmer trajectory for shelter and health-care costs"; Kocherlakota: services inflation in Twin Cities "essentially the same... as nationally", "little signs of inflationary pressures, even in nontradables." Mixed, stable.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 55 (value only)**
- Dudley: "firmer trajectory for shelter"; George: "House prices continue to rise"; Mester: "higher levels of sales and prices in the residential housing sector." Modest firming.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44 (value only)**
- Mester: "Longer-term inflation expectations have remained stable"; Yellen: decline in market-based compensation "calls into question the premise" but is "mostly... risk premiums"; Brainard: "hints of some movement downward"; Dudley: "not that large." Anchored, slight downside worry.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: "liftoff at our next meeting, in September, is a distinct possibility"; Lacker: "I would oppose any further delay"; Bullard: "emergency policy settings for a long time"; Fischer: "criteria for beginning normalization have very nearly been met." Opposed or cautious: Kocherlakota "I recommend alternative A"; Tarullo: "my preferred policy option" is not September; Brainard: "maintain optionality around December." Hold today (alternative B) with a tightening bias; several want September, a few want later.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 (High)**
- Vote 10-0 for alternative B. But expressed disagreement over timing: Kocherlakota urges alternative A and delaying liftoff to "latter portion of next year"; Lacker "less and less comfortable with further delay"; Tarullo and Brainard prefer later than September; Evans "still skeptical." No formal dissents; real differences in emphasis and on liftoff timing, so above the unanimous floor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 47 +-8 (Moderate)**
- A dedicated financial-stability segment: Williams on the radar chart ("things that aren't on the chart today that are really the biggest dangers"); Rosengren on the tabletop exercise; Kocherlakota proposing a financial-stability sentence in the statement; Dudley on Puerto Rico; Tarullo: "ample reason to be paying attention to leverage and funding vulnerabilities." Several participants engage; not the dominant topic of the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: "refrain from speculating about the time of liftoff... even after the initial increase, policy will remain accommodative", path likely "somewhat more slowly"; Lockhart: "some" is "a slight foreshadowing of a possible move in September"; Mester: alternative B "keeps September alive"; Fischer: "timely and gradual." Mild tightening bias with a gradual, data-dependent path.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: "there's a lot we don't understand about the inflation process", "uncertainty that we face"; Tarullo: "considerable uncertainty about the relationship between wage acceleration... and price inflation"; "I do not pretend to understand what is going on in China"; Williams: "high degree of uncertainty associated with the measures of full employment"; Mester: uncertainty "in line with historical norms." Somewhat elevated, standard caveats, China the main concern.

Coherence checks: OUTPUT_GAP 44 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 46 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47 are within 15 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 and CREDIT_SPREADS 40 co-move. Treasury and credit scores rest on thin, indirect evidence (Low confidence). Inflation sub-components are consistent with the parent (goods low, services and housing near or slightly above neutral, expectations near target).
