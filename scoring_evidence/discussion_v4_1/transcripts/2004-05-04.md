# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) — 2004-05-04 transcript discussion

## Part 1: Thinking Process (conclusions only)

- Document: full transcript member discussion (economic go-around, statement-language debate, policy go-around, roll call at end). Not special-topic: it contains a stance discussion and a 12-0 roll-call vote on the policy action.
- Policy lean: unanimous hold at a 1 percent funds rate, but with an explicit tightening bias. The Chairman says "we have completed our period of accommodation" and positions the Committee for a possible 25bp move in June. Guynn would like to move now, and Minehan, Gramlich and Hoenig favor moving soon. Kohn, Bernanke and Ferguson want a gradual pace. Lean-hawkish hold: 60.
- Dissent: 0 formal dissents (all 12 voters Yes). Several members had reservations about the new "measured" language: Moskow, Guynn, Hoenig, Bies and McTeer preferred dropping it, Santomero was uneasy, Poole was concerned and Geithner called it only "better than the known alternatives." McTeer and Poole also say the risk assessment understates upside inflation risk. The disagreement is mostly tactical (wording), with some differences on the economics (inflation persistence, inferring the gap from inflation). Score: 38.
- Conditions: participants see strong, broad-based growth in spending and investment, while slack remains in output and labor (the statement reads "resource use slack"). Inflation pressure is rising from a low level, with widespread pass-through of goods prices. Financial conditions are still accommodative even though yields rose. Banks are described as "bulletproof", and the dollar has strengthened recently.
- Bright lines satisfied: OUTPUT_GAP < 50 ("slack", "output gap still sizable"); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 ("accommodative"); BANKING_STRESS < 50 ("excellent shape"); DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50 (recent appreciation); TREASURY_STRESS 45 (yield rise only, no dysfunction); CREDIT_SPREADS < 50, resting on availability/issuance wording only (CONF Low, UNC +-10).
- Coherence: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (62) is more than 15 points above OUTPUT_GAP (42) because spending growth is above trend while output is still below potential (the gap is closing, not closed). The INFLATION_PRESSURE parent (51) sits slightly below the sub-component average (about 56): the subs capture the direction of goods, housing and expectations pressure, while members repeatedly call the level "low".
- Forward guidance: the Committee adopts "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured", i.e. language to begin normalization at a gradual pace (65). Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, mainly about the output gap, the inflation process and model structure, while confidence in growth is high (40).
- EQUITY_VALUATION is scored only from Bernanke's price report that stocks were "flat" (50, Low). The text is clean apart from merged words at line breaks; no garbling affected the reading.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 51 ±8 [High]
- Evidence: McTeer: "cost pressures are now evident across the board, and more and more producers are passing on higher costs." Ferguson: "we are starting at a relatively low level of inflation... not close to reaching the kind of inflation that... would be considered inconsistent with price stability." Kohn: "inflation will remain low and stable, albeit at a higher level than I thought a few months ago."
- Center of gravity: inflation has stopped falling and pricing power is returning broadly, but members still describe the level as low. Most see risks as balanced; McTeer, Poole and Hoenig see them as tilted up.
- Direction: at or slightly above neutral, because the rising pressure is offset by the low starting level.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High]
- Evidence: Broaddus: "the employment gap and the output gap are still sizable." Minehan: "a slowly declining output gap". The adopted statement: "with inflation low and resource use slack."
- Center of gravity: slack remains but is being absorbed, and several members (Moskow, Poole, McTeer, Santomero) suspect the gap is smaller than estimated.
- Direction: below neutral because participants say slack remains (bright line satisfied).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 39 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the labor market remains in an early stage of recovery, with essentially no increase in the employment-to-population ratio." Ferguson: "Labor markets clearly have not completely recovered... only one good data report." Counterpoint: Santomero and Greenspan report skilled-labor shortages; Poole reports that driver supply is "extremely tight".
- Center of gravity: after one strong payroll month, the labor market is still slack overall, with shortages only in skilled niches.
- Direction: below neutral because of persisting labor slack and weak hiring.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Moskow: "Household spending and business spending have been robust." Minehan: "rising capital goods orders and double-digit growth in investment spending." Stern: "the economy does appear to be firing on virtually all cylinders." Olson's bank contacts are less optimistic.
- Center of gravity: both household and business spending are described as strong and broad-based.
- Direction: above neutral because of robust consumption and accelerating capex.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±7 [High]
- Evidence: Kohn: productivity growth "slowed... to an average of merely 3 percent from the 4 to 5 percent range"; "productivity growth remains elevated." Ferguson: "the structural productivity story is still in place." Greenspan: "the productivity numbers are still sufficiently strong to suppress the overall rise."
- Center of gravity: productivity growth is slowing from a boom pace but remains above historical trend.
- Direction: above neutral because of elevated structural productivity growth.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Minehan: "reasonably accommodative financial markets"; "Market risk-taking in an environment of very accommodative policy." McTeer: "a big swing toward easier C&I lending standards and terms." Bernanke (direction): "monetary and financial conditions... have tightened considerably since our last meeting."
- Center of gravity: conditions are tightening from a very easy level but remain accommodative.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because of "accommodative" language (bright line satisfied).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 45 ±8 [Low]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the ten-year Treasury rate and the high-grade corporate bond rate have risen about 75 basis points." Greenspan asks about mortgage-hedging vulnerabilities "in the event... that we get another 50 basis point rise in the ten-year note."
- Center of gravity: yields rose on policy expectations; there is concern about possible bond-market overreaction but no dysfunction.
- Direction: normal functioning (below 50), because only yield movements are discussed.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 37 ±10 [Low]
- Evidence (availability and issuance only, no spread-level wording): McTeer: "easier C&I lending standards and terms." Olson: "IPO issuance and bond issuance are strong, supply may exceed demand," with "an emerging pickiness" among high-yield investors. Guynn: "significant improvement in C&I lending."
- Center of gravity: credit availability is ample and issuance strong; Olson notes slight investor pickiness.
- Direction: below neutral, based on availability and issuance language (V4.1.1 rule 3).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 ±8 [Low]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Stock prices have been flat despite strong earnings news."
- Center of gravity: a price report only (flat), with no valuation view.
- Direction: at neutral (V4.1.1 rule 2 default).

### BANKING_STRESS: 22 ±6 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Olson: "asset quality across the board is bulletproof. Banks are in excellent shape, and loan quality is in excellent shape."
- Center of gravity: one emphatic report of a very healthy US banking system (Broaddus's banking remarks concern China, not the US).
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy (bright line satisfied).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 ±7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the dollar has appreciated about 2 percent since March and more than 4 percent since the beginning of the year." Kohn: "the dollar has strengthened." Minehan: "the increasing value of the dollar." Hoenig and Moskow refer to the effects of the past depreciation.
- Center of gravity: the dollar has appreciated modestly in recent months after an earlier decline.
- Direction: above neutral because of the recent appreciation (bright line satisfied).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 63
- Evidence: Bies: "prices on the goods side generally have stopped their decline... from used cars to apparel." Broaddus: "the end of deflation in durable goods prices." Pass-through of steel, metals and plastics prices is reported across Districts.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 48
- Evidence: Bies: "the rate of inflation for services... has continued to come down... other services is continuing to moderate." Against this, airline and trucking surcharges and rising health-care costs are reported (Moskow, Poole, Minehan).

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 58
- Evidence: Geithner: "Much of the rise in prices is due to housing." Moore: "double-digit price appreciation and record sales." Santomero: a homebuilder is raising prices "about 1 to 3 percent per week."

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57
- Evidence: Hoenig: "signs that inflation expectations are increasing." Guynn: "we have already had some deterioration." Moore: "longer-term inflation expectations remain at relatively high levels." Against these, Kohn: "long-term inflation expectations do appear to be stable," and Broaddus sees no "dramatic recent change in inflation compensation."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 [High]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "we have completed our period of accommodation... the point of waiting until June is to be sure that we are positioned to move." Guynn: "I wish we were in a position to move today." Kohn: "we should await confirmation... the path of tightening... is likely to be gradual."
- Center of gravity: a unanimous hold with a clear bias toward beginning to tighten soon.
- Direction: lean hawkish, because the Committee is preparing to remove accommodation.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±8 [High]
- Evidence: the vote read-out shows 12 Yes and no dissents. Moskow: "I'd prefer to drop the word 'patience' and not substitute the word 'measured.'" Hoenig: "we would be better served not to have this last sentence." McTeer: "I don't think any of us... really believes that the risks... have moved into balance."
- Center of gravity: there are no dissents, but six or seven members have tactical reservations about the language, and there are modest differences on the inflation outlook.
- Direction: moderate differences, mainly about tactics.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 ±8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Bernanke and Kohn discuss the tightening of financial conditions and the risk of bond-market overreaction. Minehan cites "market risk-taking". Greenspan asks about mortgage-hedging vulnerabilities. Santomero notes that households "may be fully extended."
- Center of gravity: financial conditions serve as context for the policy and communication decision; they are not the focus.
- Direction: limited attention.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 ±7 [High]
- Evidence: the adopted sentence: "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured." Minehan: "sentence 8 does say positively that... we're going to take the policy accommodation away."
- Center of gravity: the guidance signals that normalization is about to begin, at a gradual pace.
- Direction: mild tightening bias.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 [High]
- Evidence: Gramlich: "There are too many uncertainties to be resolved." Kohn: "I am less certain of this than I was a few months ago." Geithner: "less confident in that forecast." Greenspan is "uncomfortable with the very narrow ranges" of the simulations.
- Center of gravity: uncertainty about the gap, the inflation process and model structure is somewhat elevated, while growth is viewed confidently.
- Direction: somewhat above normal.
