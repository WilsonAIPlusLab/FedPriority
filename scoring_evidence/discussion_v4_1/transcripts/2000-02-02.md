# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2000-02-02

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript for a two-day meeting (afternoon go-around, then morning session). It contains: staff Q&A (members' questions only), the economic go-around, a long debate on whether to raise the monetary aggregate ranges, members' questions on the Bluebook policy simulations, the Chairman's policy recommendation, the policy go-around, and a vote read-out. Staff answers have been removed, so some turns are blank or fragments. Nothing is garbled enough to block scoring.

**Era context.** Early 2000. The text itself describes a booming, productivity-driven expansion, record stock prices, tight labor markets and a series of prior funds-rate increases. The question for the meeting is how much to tighten. I scored what members said.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish voices, mostly on inflation risk and excess demand: Guynn ("whites of inflation's eyes"), Parry, Broaddus, Moskow, Hoenig, Meyer, Gramlich, Kelley, Stern, Boehne.
- Greenspan: productivity is still accelerating, the wealth effect has created excess demand, and the equity market is highly valued. He argues for 25bp now, an asymmetric directive, and tightening at each meeting.
- Doves or caution: Ferguson, McDonough and Minehan lean toward gradualism and uncertainty. McTeer and Poole see little labor or wage pressure in their districts, though Poole says inflation risks are to the upside.
- Parry, Broaddus and (initially) Guynn preferred 50bp but accepted 25bp. Meyer voted No on the aggregate-ranges vote (he proposed a wider alternative III).

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see actual inflation as modestly above the midpoint and building. The evidence runs both ways. Core is "just under 2 percent" (Parry) and "inflation has not moved an iota" (Greenspan), and firms still lack pricing power (McDonough, Moskow). Against that, members report rising input and health costs and early signs of pricing flexibility (Broaddus). Many say the risks are "significantly tilted toward higher inflation" (Meyer) and that Friday's data was "a red flag" (Parry). Level is slightly above target with rising pressure, so about 56.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Meyer: "output has moved beyond potential". Ferguson: "expand at a faster pace than its potential". Hoenig: "excess demand". Boehne: "demand is pressing too much on supply". The bright-line rule requires >50, and I score 64.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Moskow: "difficult, if not impossible, to find any signs of easing". Minehan: "very tight labor supply". Gramlich: unemployment "well below" NAIRU. Texas unemployment is 4.4% and NY-area rates are at 10-year lows, but wage gains are still moderate. Score 73.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Well above trend. Minehan: "consumption is booming". Guynn: the holiday season was "a blockbuster". Autos are very strong, and investment is high (Kelley, Hoenig). Housing and construction are cooling slightly in some districts. Score 72.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Strongly above trend. Greenspan says it is "still accelerating", and Kelley says gains are "very strong". Parry and Moskow's advisers are skeptical about sustainability, and others see a much higher trend. Score 76.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: The wording is mixed, and no one uses "accommodative" or "tight". Members stress record stock prices, buoyant credit demand, and a wealth effect that is boosting spending. Against this, real long rates have risen "quite significantly" (Greenspan), mortgage and corporate yields are about 8%, bankers are tightening standards (Stern), and Meyer says policy has been "somewhat slow to tighten". Net: still on the easy side but moving toward neutral, so 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries come up only as yields, TIPS and the on-the-run/off-the-run adjustment, with a passing mention of the year-end flight to quality and the buyback program. Greenspan treats the TIPS illiquidity as a measurement issue. There is no dysfunction, so 40 with low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed as spreads. Mentions are only about credit demand (Jordan), tightening standards (Stern), and corporate debt growth (Minehan). N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Heavy concern about overvaluation. Greenspan: the equity premium is "downright scary" and the market discount rate is "virtually zero". Ferguson: "significant downside corrections". Broaddus: a "disorderly plunge". Score 15.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mentioned lightly. Jordan reports squeezed margins, deposit runoff and bank-stock selling. Stern reports tightening credit standards and examiner pressure. Nobody calls banks troubled, so the level is below 50: 40 with low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Greenspan says the dollar is "essentially unchanged", and Kelley says it rose earlier. McDonough and Jordan raise fears of a future reversal tied to the current account deficit. Stable now, so 50.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS 54, SERVICES 56, EXPECTATIONS 54 (Greenspan sees flat expectations, Gramlich a rising premium that he later conceded was overstated, Stern a survey deterioration). HOUSING is N/A: residential and office comments are regional and mostly about prices and vacancy, not shelter inflation.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous for a 25bp increase and a balance-of-risks-toward-inflation statement. Several wanted 50bp. Greenspan expects "a sequence of 25bp increases at each meeting" and says "significant" funds-rate increases are coming. Strong hawkish, tempered by the size of the move: 72.
- Dissent: the policy vote was unanimous (the roll shows Meyer "No" on the first roll, which was the aggregate-ranges vote, not policy). Reservations were visible, with Parry, Broaddus and Guynn preferring 50bp, and the ranges debate split the room. Moderate: 33.
- Financial stability emphasis: Greenspan's argument for gradualism rests on equity valuations and the wealth effect. Ferguson, Broaddus, Minehan (balance sheets, asset values) and McDonough (current account) add to it. Several participants engage meaningfully: 52.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric statement, "full expectation that we will be moving again in March", and a "significant amount of tightening" (Boehne). Clear tightening signal: 78.
- Uncertainty: many stress uncertainty about productivity, NAIRU and the models (Stern, Meyer, Minehan, Greenspan "never experienced before"). Yet all are confident about the direction of policy. Elevated to high: 62.

**Bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 64 (>50, above-potential language).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44: no explicit accommodative or tight wording; the score follows the net description above.
- CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS 40 (<50, sound but margin pressure).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 50 (unchanged).
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 (no dysfunction).

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 64 vs LABOR 73 (gap 9) and CONSUMPTION 72 (gap 8): consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: both normal.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 56 vs sub-components 54-56: consistent.

**N/A justification.** CREDIT_SPREADS and INFLATION_HOUSING were not addressed in the relevant sense by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "inflation has not moved an iota"; Meyer, "risks have now become significantly tilted toward higher inflation"; Guynn, "beginning to see the whites of inflation's eyes".
- Summary: Current inflation is only modestly above comfortable levels but is rising, and members' concern is forward-looking.
- Direction: above neutral because most speakers see price pressure building, though the level is still low.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 64 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Meyer, "output has moved beyond potential"; Ferguson, "faster pace than its potential".
- Summary: Demand exceeds supply, which is the stated reason for tightening.
- Direction: above neutral because members explicitly describe output as beyond potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 73 +-5, High.**
- Evidence: Moskow, "difficult, if not impossible, to find any signs of easing in our very tight labor markets"; Gramlich, unemployment "well below the structural estimate of NAIRU".
- Summary: Nearly every district reports shortages, with only moderate wage acceleration so far.
- Direction: above neutral because of widespread shortages and unemployment near 4%.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 72 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Minehan, "consumption is booming"; Guynn, "holiday selling season was a blockbuster".
- Summary: Consumer and business spending run far above trend, with only modest housing cooling.
- Direction: above neutral because of boom language.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 76 +-7, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "productivity probably is still accelerating"; Kelley, "Productivity gains continue to be very strong".
- Summary: Most members accept a much higher productivity trend, though some doubt its durability.
- Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, the long-term real rate "has been going up quite significantly"; Jordan, corporate and mortgage yields "at 8 percent"; Stern, bankers "tightening credit standards".
- Summary: Conditions are still easy given record equity prices and ample credit, although rising rates are beginning to tighten them.
- Direction: below neutral because asset values and credit availability remain supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8, Low.**
- Evidence: Gramlich on TIPS yields and the "flight to quality around the turn of the year"; Greenspan on the on-the-run/off-the-run adjustment.
- Summary: Treasury markets are discussed only as yield and liquidity-premium measures, with no dysfunction.
- Direction: normal functioning, below neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** Spreads are not discussed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 15 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, the equity premium is "downright scary"; Broaddus, risk of "a disorderly plunge in the stock market".
- Summary: Valuation concern is extreme and central to the case for gradualism.
- Direction: high overvaluation concern, in the low-score range under this rubric's polarity.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 +-8, Low.**
- Evidence: Jordan, "interest margins are under pressure"; Stern, bankers "are tightening credit standards".
- Summary: Margin pressure and stock runoffs are noted, but nobody describes banks as troubled.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is described as functioning.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 50 +-7, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "held the dollar essentially unchanged"; McDonough on a possible foreign flight from dollar assets.
- Summary: Currently stable, with a tail risk of reversal.
- Direction: at neutral.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 54 (input costs up, little pass-through, Internet pricing pressure; Moskow, Guynn). SERVICES 56 (health care and pharma "double-digit increases", benefit costs; Guynn, Minehan). HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS 54 (Greenspan "absolutely flat" breakevens; Stern's survey deterioration).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 72 +-5, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "a sequence of 25 basis point increases"; Broaddus and Parry preferred 50bp.
- Summary: Unanimous tightening with a bias toward more, restrained by the size of the move.
- Direction: hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Meyer recorded as "No" on the aggregates roll; Parry, "I would prefer a 50 basis point increase".
- Summary: Policy vote unanimous, with differences on pace and on the aggregate ranges.
- Direction: moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we could crack the market"; Ferguson, "a placid surface may disguise some underlying turmoil".
- Summary: Equity and balance-sheet risks shape the size of the move but are not the meeting's main topic.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 78 +-6, High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "full expectation that we will be moving again in March"; Boehne, "more tightening ahead of us".
- Summary: Asymmetric statement and an expected series of increases.
- Direction: hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8, Moderate.**
- Evidence: Stern, uncertain about productivity, NAIRU and effects of rate rises; Greenspan, "very unusual period that we have never experienced before".
- Summary: Much explicit uncertainty about the structure of the economy, but conviction about the policy direction.
- Direction: high.
