# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2019-12-11 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It has (a) a long framework-review round (Fed Listens, distributional/HANK memo, communications about below-target inflation), (b) members' questions in the staff Q&A (repo/T-bill operations, international, r*), (c) the economic go-around, (d) a debate on the draft statement (alternative B, paragraph 2 wording), (e) the policy go-around and the vote read-out. Some staff-answer fragments are missing (single-speaker runs such as Daly "Yes, so you go through that"), which is expected from staff removal and does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Late 2019, year 11 of the expansion, after three rate cuts (target range 1.5 to 1.75). The text itself says: unemployment 3.5 percent (50-year low), core PCE about 1.6 percent, manufacturing weak, trade uncertainty, consumer strong, housing rebounding. Scored from what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Essentially all 17 participants. Economic go-around: Rosengren, Daly, Clarida, Evans, Kaplan, Mester, Harker, Brainard, Bullard, Barkin, Bowman, Bostic, George, Quarles, Kashkari, Williams, Powell. Policy go-around: all support holding the range unchanged (alternative B).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target ("stubbornly below target", core PCE 1.6; Clarida, Brainard, Williams, Kashkari, Powell; "muted"), with a minority (Rosengren, Mester) pointing to firming wage/margin pressure and stronger median/trimmed measures. Below the midpoint, clearly but not deeply: about 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP: "Growth slightly above potential/trend" (Rosengren, Daly, Bowman, Powell "a bit above trend", George "at or above potential", Brainard "growth running above potential"), unemployment well below natural rate estimates. Clarida/Williams/Mester say about trend. Bright line: "above potential" language means > 50. Modestly above: 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 3.5 percent, "very tight labor market" (Rosengren), "historically tight" (Kaplan), "50-year low" (Powell), prime-age EPOP at pre-recession peak. Offsetting: wage growth moderate, Williams prefers "strong" to "tight", Powell says not as tight as late 1990s, Kashkari sees slack in wages. UE < 3.8 guidance, so above 55; tempered by the wage caveats: 66.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumer "robust" (Brainard 3 percent Q3, Quarles "robust", Williams "healthy"), housing rebounding; business investment "moribund/anemic", manufacturing "still in recession" (Powell, Evans). Net: about trend, 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only Clarida (1.5 percent over three years, "not great, but up about half a point") and Kashkari (real wages in line with productivity); Daly's potential-growth risk. Modestly below historical trend but improving: 44, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Brainard "very accommodative... every FCI near the low end of its historical range"; Williams "favorable"; Rosengren "supportive"; Kaplan markets "running away". Bright line: accommodative/supportive means < 50. Loose: 33.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Discussed via repo/T-bill operations, year-end funding and G-SIB window-dressing worries; September repo disruption described as past and being handled ("right now things are going very well", Williams; "market volatility caught all of us by surprise", Brainard). No current dysfunction, only year-end and T-bill liquidity contingencies: about 45, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kaplan "credit spreads for more highly-rated debt are very tight; triple-C gapped out"; Brainard "investment credit spreads tightening a little further", triple-B concerns, "elevated risky corporate debt". Stated level is tight/compressed for IG, so bright line < 50: 32.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Kaplan "P/E expansion", "markets running away from us"; Brainard "valuations of risk assets" elevated; Bostic "elevated stock valuations"; Rosengren rising stock prices. Valuation concern modest to moderate: 33.
- BANKING_STRESS: Evans (bankers: credit quality good, delinquencies very low) and Bowman (ag-sector debt restructuring, some nonaccrual ag loans) - system sound, localized farm-credit stress. Bright line sound means < 50: 40, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar mentioned only as a factor in export-demand multipliers (Clarida) and a tariff justification about weaker Latin American currencies (Kaplan). No participant states a direction or level. N/A.
- Sub-components: Goods: tariffs lifting core goods prices / retailers raising some prices (George, Mester): 55. Services: rent and health-care services keeping core CPI wedge (George): 54 (thin). Housing: rent mention only by George, and public concern about housing costs (Powell): 54 (thin). Expectations: "somewhat below target" / "low end of range" / "CIE at all-time low" (Clarida, Brainard, Williams, Kashkari, Powell) versus "well anchored" (Bostic): 36.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says below target, so below 50, matches. LABOR_TIGHTNESS higher = tighter; 3.5 percent UE means tight, above 50, matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter; evidence says very accommodative, so below 50, matches. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress; evidence says tight, so below 50, matches. EQUITY_VALUATION higher = more concern/stress by rubric; elevated valuations is on the low side (16-45), matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold after three cuts; Rosengren, Bostic, George, Bullard, Mester, Quarles, Harker want to pause (some wary of easing-bias language, Quarles notes accommodation will eventually be removed); Evans, Kashkari, Daly, Clarida want accommodation to persist until inflation reaches and overshoots 2 percent, Kashkari sees a "decent chance" of further cuts; the framework round leans toward run-hot / makeup. Net: holding with mild easing bias, slightly dovish: 44.
- Disagreement: zero dissents, unanimous vote. Differences: how to word paragraph 2 (Powell: "splits the Committee right down the middle" on the sentence), a few expressed preferences on "near" and "global developments", framework differences (Rosengren, Kaplan, Barkin, George vs Daly, Evans, Kashkari), Rosengren's worry about the easing tilt. These are tactical/wording and framework differences, not an assessment split: 22.
- Financial stability: Rosengren, Kaplan, Barkin, George, Mester, Bostic, Daly (framework round), Brainard (imbalances, macroprudential), and Kaplan on market froth; the Committee scheduled a financial-stability memo for January. Several address it meaningfully but it is not dominant: 50.
- Forward guidance: stance "appropriate" and "likely to remain so as long as incoming information remains broadly consistent... not on a preset course"; "muted inflation" and "global developments" kept (Powell calls it possibly dovish); Clarida wants policy to persist until inflation is somewhat above 2; Kashkari wants a no-hike commitment. Patient, data-dependent, easing-leaning: 40.
- Uncertainty: trade/geopolitics "new normal" (Williams, Bullard), u* "highly uncertain", "case for humility" (Powell), models "not ready" (Mester, Bullard), risks tilted down but outlook "in a good place". Normal to somewhat elevated: 38.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 (above potential language, > 50, OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 (< 50, OK). CREDIT_SPREADS 32 (tight, < 50, OK). BANKING_STRESS 40 (sound, < 50, OK). TREASURY_STRESS 45 (no dysfunction, not > 60, OK). DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs LABOR 66: 12-point divergence, within 15; explained by the labor market being tight in level terms while participants cite weak wage pressure, flat inflation and growth only slightly above trend. OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50: within 15. FC 33 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 32: co-move. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS 45: normal-times level, consistent. INFLATION parent 38 vs sub-components (goods 55, services 54, housing 54, expectations 36): parent reflects measured core running at 1.6 percent plus subdued expectations; the three price-level sub-scores rest on thin tariff/rent anecdotes while headline measured inflation is below target, so the parent is weighted toward the measured and expectations picture. Noted as a mild tension, not an error.

**9. N/A justification.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no participant states a direction or level of the dollar.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +-6 [High]**
- Brainard: "Inflation, by contrast, remains stubbornly below target... Core inflation of 1.6 percent is not okay." Williams: "inflation continues to be stubbornly below our target."
- Center of gravity: most participants see core inflation below 2 percent with only a few citing firming pressure (Rosengren, Mester).
- Direction: below neutral because core PCE is 1.6 percent and "muted".

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Rosengren: "growth slightly above that of potential and tight labor markets." Daly: "slightly above my estimate of longer-run potential growth."
- Center of gravity: growth modestly above trend with unemployment below natural rate estimates, though Clarida/Williams/Mester see about trend.
- Direction: above neutral because of "above potential/trend" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 66 +-6 [High]**
- Rosengren: "continued difficulty hiring workers in a very tight labor market"; Powell: "With the unemployment rate at 3½ percent... the labor market is strong... not as tight as it was in the late 1990s."
- Center of gravity: 50-year-low unemployment and high prime-age EPOP, tempered by moderate wage growth and Williams's "strong rather than tight".
- Direction: above neutral because unemployment is far below natural rate estimates (3.5 vs 4 to 4.4).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-6 [High]**
- Brainard: "Consumer spending increased at a robust 3 percent in the third quarter"; Evans: capital spending by manufacturers "remains moribund."
- Center of gravity: strong consumer and housing offset by weak business investment and manufacturing.
- Direction: at neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 44 +-8 [Low]**
- Clarida: "productivity, which itself has picked up to a 1.5 percent pace over the past three years—not great, but up about ½ percent."
- Center of gravity: modestly below the roughly 2 percent historical trend but improving; thin evidence.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Brainard: "financial conditions appear very accommodative. Every financial conditions index (FCI) I consult is near the low end of its historical range." Williams: "favorable financial conditions."
- Center of gravity: loose conditions with risk assets rallying and spreads tight.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because of accommodative/supportive language.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +-8 [Low]**
- Williams: "right now things are going very well" in T-bills; Brainard: year-end "funding conditions... remain a key focus," September volatility "caught all of us by surprise."
- Center of gravity: past repo disruption now managed; year-end and T-bill liquidity are contingency worries, not current dysfunction.
- Direction: at normal-functioning with a small stress-risk tilt.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Kaplan: "credit spreads for more highly-rated debt are very tight"; Brainard: "investment credit spreads tightening a little further."
- Center of gravity: IG spreads compressed, with concern about triple-B size and triple-C widening.
- Direction: below neutral because the stated level is tight.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Kaplan: "a pretty healthy price-earnings ratio (P/E) expansion... markets running away from us"; Brainard: "signs... in current elevated risky corporate debt and the valuations of risk assets."
- Center of gravity: moderate valuation concern from a few members.
- Direction: valuations above fair value (low-score side of the scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 +-8 [Low]**
- Evans: "Bankers noted that credit quality is good... delinquencies... still very low"; Bowman: "some banks reporting shifting troubled agricultural loans to nonaccrual status."
- Center of gravity: sound system with localized farm-credit stress; thinly discussed.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed with a direction or level.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55.** George: "tariffs are temporarily lifting CPI core goods prices"; Mester: retailers "increasing prices on some of their products."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 54.** George: "factors affecting rent and health-care services are likely to keep the wedge... from fully normalizing" (thin).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 54.** George (rent) and Powell ("rising costs of medical care, housing, and college") (thin).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36.** Clarida: expectations "anchored at a somewhat lower level today", CIE "at an all-time low"; Powell: "we do not want to see our inflation expectations sliding down." Contrary: Bostic "inflation expectations are well anchored."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 44 +-5 [High]**
- Bostic: "I see no need for further stimulus at this time"; Kashkari: "there's a decent chance we will need to make further cuts"; Quarles: "we'll have to begin gradually removing this accommodation" eventually.
- Center of gravity: unanimous hold after three cuts, with a pause-and-assess majority and a dovish-leaning minority.
- Direction: slightly below neutral (hold with mild easing bias).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +-6 [High]**
- Vote read-out: ten yes votes, no dissents. Powell: "it splits the Committee right down the middle" on paragraph 2 wording.
- Center of gravity: unanimous with wording and framework differences only.
- Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Brainard: "I am increasingly concerned about financial imbalances in this late-cycle environment"; Rosengren: "inflated asset prices... rising imbalances can increase the likelihood of... high-unemployment periods."
- Center of gravity: raised meaningfully by several members across the framework and policy rounds, but not the main subject.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Powell: stance "appropriate... as long as incoming information... remains broadly consistent... Policy is not on a preset course"; Clarida: policy "will remain accommodative until inflation moves up to—and... somewhat above—our 2 percent objective."
- Center of gravity: patient, data-dependent, easing-leaning with "muted inflation" language retained.
- Direction: below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Powell: "The case for humility about our ability to identify the precise level of maximum employment in real time is overpowering"; Williams: trade/geopolitics now "the new normal."
- Center of gravity: standard-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty (trade, u*, models) within a favorable baseline.
- Direction: somewhat elevated.
