# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting dated 2009-08-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds the staff Q&A (agency ARM purchase proposal, reserve-management tools, TALF), two economic go-rounds (afternoon and morning), the policy go-round on alternatives B / B (revised) / C, and a unanimous roll-call read-out. Several member questions have their staff answers removed, so some turns are orphaned fragments. I treated these as procedural and scored only member views. No passage was so garbled that meaning was lost.

**Era context.** The text is from mid-2009, after the deep recession. Members describe a bottoming economy, a financial system that has stabilized, a funds rate at 0-1/4, and large-scale asset purchases (Treasuries, agency MBS, agency debt) in progress. I scored from what members say.

**Speakers.** The Chair, Vice Chair Dudley, Governors Kohn, Warsh, Duke and Tarullo, and Presidents Evans, Fisher, Rosengren, Plosser, Lockhart, Yellen, Stern, Bullard, Hoenig, Pianalto and Lacker all speak.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Core PCE is about 1.5-1.8% (Evans, Lacker), core is falling (Kohn, Dudley), and large slack and falling compensation are disinflationary (Yellen, Pianalto, Kohn). A hawkish minority (Plosser, Lacker, Bullard, Fisher) stresses future risk, but current pressure is low. Expectations are described as anchored, with public alarm about the longer run. Not as low as the 15-20 "deflation fears" anchor, because there is no outright deflation talk and "risks balanced" is the Chair's summary. Score about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy well below potential. Evidence: "enormous slack" (Yellen), "large output gap that I see closing only slowly" (Pianalto), "utilization stands at its lowest level of the past 60 years" (Yellen), and Dudley's "large output gap that is going to take a long time to close". Several skeptics doubt the gap's use as an inflation predictor, but none deny slack. Bright line (below potential means under 50) satisfied. Score about 22.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as very slack. Unemployment is 9.4% (Dudley), "jobless recovery" (Yellen), and payroll declines are only slowing. Score about 17.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as below trend but stabilizing. Consumer spending is weak with a high-saving risk and tight credit. Investment is weak (nonresidential construction bleak, capex subdued). Cash-for-clunkers gives an auto bounce, and housing is firming. Net score about 33.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Fisher, Yellen ("healthy productivity growth") and the Chair ("productivity has risen") all cite strong productivity from cost-cutting. Cyclical, so above trend. Score about 62, Moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Improvement is large ("cumulative improvement ... quite significant", the Chair; Warsh on markets "self-satisfied"). Kohn and Duke stress that conditions are "still quite tight in a number of dimensions". Credit is tight for households and small business, and bank lending is falling (Tarullo: an 18% decline). Net is somewhat tight but easing. Bright line (tight means over 50) satisfied. Score about 57.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as market functioning. Treasury purchases are discussed as policy, and Evans mentions futures depth back at pre-Lehman levels. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The level is still somewhat wide but narrowing. The Chair says "private credit spreads continue to come in". Evans cites "more-normal spreads". Kohn says BBB yields are "not far from where they were" in Aug 07 and Sep 08. Agency ARM spreads are "outsized" and Lacker notes "some spreads are elevated". Level is above normal, so over 50. Score about 57, Moderate.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Rally discussed (Warsh, Kohn, Chair). Warsh's caution ("so self-satisfied since ... 2007") hints at richness, while Kohn notes stocks are still down 30% from Aug 2007. No clear valuation view. Score about 47, Low-Moderate.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see significant stress. Commercial real estate losses threaten small and medium-sized banks (Yellen: "many community and midsized banks to fail"; Chair; Hoenig), capital impairment (Tarullo), and large credit losses still to come. Hoenig is the mitigating voice (FDIC resolution). Score about 66.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Briefly discussed. Kohn mentions "the weaker dollar" helping net exports, but the broad index is "unchanged relative to August '07 and higher relative to September '08". The Chair says "perhaps a weaker dollar". Recent weakening, so below 50 (bright line). Score about 38, Low.
- Sub-components: Goods is scored from Dudley (core goods inflation expected to moderate; core goods above core services) and Fisher on pricing pressure (airfares down 14%). Services is scored from Dudley (core services unusually low). Housing was not discussed (rents/shelter), so N/A. Expectations: Plosser cites surveys near 3% and TIPS up. The Chair says they are "anchored" but have risen. Kohn says "very firmly anchored". Dudley and Warsh say "well anchored". Score about 52.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Alternative B (revised) is adopted unanimously. It keeps 0-1/4 with "extended period", lets the Treasury purchase program expire (with a taper signal), defers the MBS decision, and leaves the door open to resumption. Most want to "stay the course" (Chair, Kohn, Stern, Dudley). The dovish tail is Yellen ("strong case exists for further monetary stimulus") and Rosengren ("would favor more-aggressive measures"). The hawkish tail is Plosser (taper MBS immediately, drop "extended", alternative C), Hoenig (stop purchases), Lacker (merit in C), and Bullard and Fisher (hawk-leaning but supporting B). The ending of Treasury buying is a mild hawkish element, but easing remains in place. No tightening is proposed. Lean about 38, between "majority favors some easing" and "hold with mild easing bias".
- Dissent: The vote is unanimous (10 of 10 yes), with no formal dissent. But there are distinct camps: Plosser's taper-now and no-"extended" prescription, Hoenig, Lacker's discomfort with MBS, and Yellen/Rosengren wanting more. Gap and inflation framework disagreements are also visible (Plosser, Lacker, Bullard and Stern against Kohn, Dudley and Yellen). The agency ARM question was "pretty evenly divided". Score about 35.
- Financial stability emphasis: Large attention to financial markets, credit availability, CRE and banks (Duke, Kohn, Warsh, Rosengren, Hoenig), TALF, securitization and exit tools. It rivals but does not exceed the economic assessment. Score about 55.
- Forward guidance: "Extended period" is retained (Kohn, Dudley, Chair). Plosser wants it dropped, and Bullard and Plosser reject date-based language. Exit-tool and communication work is emphasized, but there is no near-term tightening signal. Mild easing bias, "low for long". Score about 25.
- Uncertainty: Kohn says "huge uncertainty ... nontrivial odds" of a bad scenario. Tarullo says "somewhere north of 75 percent" for tepid-to-bad 2010. The Chair says "an awful lot of uncertainty". Evans says "uncharted territory" and "most difficult and confusing period". Duke and Lockhart cite scenarios. Elevated to high. Score about 62.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50, satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 ("still quite tight"), satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS above 50 on a stated elevated level, with narrowing direction not setting the side, satisfied. BANKING_STRESS above 50 (stressed), satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 (weaker dollar), satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 22 and LABOR 17 are within 15 (5 apart). OUTPUT_GAP 22 and CONSUMPTION 33 are within 15 (11 apart). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 and CREDIT_SPREADS 57 co-move. Treasury stress is N/A, which is fine for a non-crisis period. INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 is consistent with goods 40, services 35 and expectations 52 (expectations pull up, realized core pulls down, and the parent weighs current realized inflation).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member describes Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: no shelter/rent inflation commentary.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-7 (High).**
- Evidence: "core PCE inflation to remain near its current 1½ percent pace" (Evans); "the next move in underlying inflation is more likely to be down than up" (Kohn); "no pricing power for a number of years" (Kohn).
- Summary: Low realized inflation and disinflationary slack, partly offset by hawkish concern about expectations.
- Direction: Below neutral because core is well under 2% and falling.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 22 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: "large output gap that I see closing only slowly" (Pianalto); "enormous slack in the economy" (Yellen).
- Summary: Deep slack, with a slow recovery.
- Direction: Below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: "unemployment rate is currently 9.4 percent and likely to rise higher" (Dudley); "yet another jobless recovery" (Yellen).
- Summary: Very high unemployment and weak rehiring prospects.
- Direction: Far below full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-7 (High).**
- Evidence: "consumer spending growth remains weak ... most saw signs of stabilization" (Chair); "I don't think that we can look to consumer spending as a strong engine of recovery" (Yellen).
- Summary: Weak but stabilizing spending, subdued capex, offset by autos and inventories.
- Direction: Below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-10 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "With such healthy productivity growth" (Yellen); "their workforce is much more productive" (Fisher).
- Summary: Cyclical productivity gains from cost-cutting.
- Direction: Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "financial conditions have improved, they are still quite tight in a number of dimensions" (Kohn); "cumulative improvement in financial markets since the spring is quite significant" (Chair).
- Summary: Big improvement in markets but credit availability to households and small business is tight.
- Direction: Somewhat above neutral (tight).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** The Treasury market is not discussed as to its functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 57 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "Private credit spreads continue to come in" (Chair); "the rate on ARMs ... on the order of 150 basis points higher than you would expect" (Yellen); BBB yields "not far from where they were in August '07 or September '08" (Kohn).
- Summary: Spreads narrowing but still above normal in many segments.
- Direction: Above neutral on level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: "markets haven't seemed so good or seemed so self-satisfied since ... the spring of 2007" (Warsh); "Stock prices are down 30 percent from August '07" (Kohn).
- Summary: A rally with mild caution, no clear valuation judgment.
- Direction: Near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 66 +-8 (High).**
- Evidence: "loan losses at many regional and community banks could become truly debilitating" (Tarullo); "banks face significant credit losses yet to come" (Chair).
- Summary: CRE-driven stress in small and medium banks, with a shrinking lending book.
- Direction: Above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 +-10 (Low).**
- Evidence: "with the weaker dollar, net exports will be less of a drag" (Kohn).
- Summary: Brief mentions of a weaker dollar.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 40.** "more likely that core goods inflation will moderate" (Dudley).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 35.** "core services inflation has been lower than core goods inflation" (Dudley).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** "they still may be characterized as anchored" while "risen" (Chair); "survey measures ... now at about 3 percent" (Plosser).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "stay the course on our policy" (Chair); "strong case exists for further monetary stimulus" (Yellen); "begin tapering ... immediately" (Plosser).
- Summary: Accommodation kept in place with the Treasury program ending; a hawkish minority and a dovish minority.
- Direction: Dovish-leaning.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Unanimous vote on B (revised); "we did not find a consensus in either direction ... pretty evenly divided" on ARMs (Chair); Plosser, Hoenig and Lacker versus Yellen and Rosengren.
- Summary: No formal dissent, but visible camps on pace and on the output-gap framework.
- Direction: Moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +-10 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Duke's long credit-conditions report; Kohn's financial-conditions section; Warsh's markets assessment.
- Summary: Several participants discuss markets, credit and banks at length.
- Direction: Moderate-to-prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 25 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "keeping the pre-commitment of an extended period in place" (Dudley); "Just drop 'extended'" (Plosser).
- Summary: "Extended period" retained, with exit-tool work in the background.
- Direction: Easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: "There is huge uncertainty" (Kohn); "we are in uncharted territory" (Evans); "an awful lot of uncertainty about that" (Chair).
- Summary: Many emphasize uncertainty about the strength of recovery and inflation.
- Direction: High.
