# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting date 2012-04-25 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of member/participant discussion: an opening agenda item on a staff scenarios exercise and communications, members' questions during staff Q&A (financial developments, economic situation, financial stability), the economic go-round (April 24 session), the Chair's summary, the policy go-round (April 25 session), a straw poll on statement language, and a roll-call vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. There is no garbled passage that changes meaning (one stray run-together, "policyTaylor", is immaterial).

**2. Era context.** Federal funds rate near zero, large asset holdings, calendar-based forward guidance ("late 2014"), Europe and fiscal-cliff risks, unemployment 8.2%. I score what members say, not the backdrop.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Bernanke; Vice Chair Dudley; Governors Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin; Presidents Rosengren, Fisher, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Pianalto, Williams, Bullard, Evans, George, Lacker, Lockhart.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- Inflation pressure: Participants see inflation as close to the 2 percent objective, with headline slightly above, core "close to target" and "well anchored" (Bernanke), "at or below" 2 percent in coming years (Yellen), "well contained" (Evans), below 2 percent (Williams). A hawkish minority (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher partly, George as medium-term risk) calls it "stubborn". Net: just below to at the midpoint, mid-to-high 40s.
- Output gap: Below the midpoint. "Resource slack remains unacceptably high", staff gap 4 3/4 percent (Evans); "substantial resource utilization gap still to be filled" (Tarullo). Kocherlakota, Bullard and Lacker argue the gap is smaller, but nobody says the economy is at or above potential. Bright line (below potential => <50) satisfied.
- Labor tightness: Well below the midpoint. Unemployment 8.2% "very elevated" (Plosser), "dismal employment situation" (Rosengren), "far from full employment" (Yellen). Skill-specific shortages and the participation debate are noted, but there is "absolutely no sign of wage pressures" (Tarullo). UE above 7% anchors this in the 16-30 band.
- Consumption/investment: Slightly below to at trend. Consumer spending "continues to expand" with strong autos and retail (Fisher, Duke, Bernanke summary), but income growth is weak, "there doesn't seem to be the fuel needed to accelerate" (Bernanke), capital spending is aimed at cost savings rather than expansion (Lockhart), and fiscal drag is large (Raskin). Net: moderate, slightly sub-par.
- Productivity: Mentioned only incidentally (potential output markdowns, firms' productivity-driven capex). No assessment of productivity growth: N/A.
- Financial conditions: Somewhat loose. "Easing of financial conditions" (Tarullo), "credit conditions continue to improve" (Williams), "funding for loans is abundant", banks "aggressively competing" (Duke), SLOOS shows easing (Yellen). Offsets: European stress and jitters, mortgage credit and CRE weak, small firms find credit hard (Kocherlakota). Net: below 50. Bright line (accommodative/easing => <50) satisfied.
- Treasury stress: Treasury-market functioning is not discussed as such (only yield effects of MEP/LSAP). N/A.
- Credit spreads: Direction only: "further easing of spreads on C&I loans" (Yellen), "spreads on C&I loans have come in" (Bernanke); Duke describes plentiful credit across the credit spectrum and low delinquencies, which hints at favorable (below-normal) levels. European sovereign spreads (Italy/Spain +90bp) are a separate, non-US topic. Level not stated outright; score on the favorable side with moderate confidence. Bright line satisfied (<50).
- Equity valuation: Thin. Fisher: S&P "one standard deviation above the trend" pre-1968, "right on trend" since 1990, and he is "worried" about reaching for returns; Plosser notes a "pickup in equity wealth"; Bernanke says the S&P is unchanged. Valuation view is a mild above-fair-value concern from one speaker: low-40s, Low confidence.
- Banking stress: US banks are described as sound (credit quality improving, charge-offs "at historic lows", Duke; "lots of capital", "very large liquidity buffers", Dudley). Offsets: broker-dealer funding fragility and the Moody's downgrade spiral risk (Dudley, Rosengren), European bank stress, Spain's banking obligations (Bernanke). Net: healthy with concerns => below 50, around 40. Bright line satisfied.
- Dollar: Brief. Europe "keep[s] the dollar stronger than it otherwise would be" (Tarullo); "a stronger dollar and weaker non-fuel import prices" (Bernanke). Modest strength, above 50, thin evidence.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more inflation / more above potential / tighter labor / stronger spending / tighter financial conditions / more credit stress / more banking stress / stronger dollar. Evidence (large slack, high unemployment, easing credit, healthy banks) maps to low gap, low labor tightness, below-50 financial conditions, below-50 spreads and banking stress. Consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The outcome is a hold (alternative B) with "late 2014" retained and downside risks judged larger than upside (Bernanke, Yellen, Dudley, Rosengren, Raskin, Evans). Dovish voices want more: Evans and Rosengren want stronger commitment, Yellen/Dudley/Duke/Williams/Tarullo want additional balance sheet action ready if the outlook weakens (Dudley prefers another MEP, Duke "additional balance sheet measures", Williams "alternative A ... if downside risks materialize"). Hawkish voices: Lacker (liftoff mid-2013, dissents), Plosser (reduce accommodation "late this year or early 2013"), Fisher, George (lay groundwork for exit), Bullard (inflation-genie risk, but supports B), Kocherlakota (alternative C, a slightly less accommodative option; Taylor-rule discussion). Net: hold with a mild easing bias at the center, with a vocal hawkish minority. Not QE-debate territory of how much more to buy; the contingency is secondary. Score in the high 30s.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Lacker, pre-announced). Reservations: Lockhart ("increasingly untenable"), Williams ("end of its shelf life"), Pianalto, Plosser and George want the date moved or dropped; Kocherlakota prefers C; Fisher opposed the 2014 date previously; Raskin worries the hold is contractionary. Straw poll of 3-4 for reverting statement language and 8 for dropping the "supported by highly accommodative monetary policy" clause. Disagreements are mostly about communications and reaction functions, civil in tone, plus some economics (output gap size, Taylor rule). One dissent plus several reservations: lower 40s.
- Financial stability attention: A dedicated financial-stability segment (broker-dealer funding and Moody's downgrades, pension funds, farmland prices, asset valuations, reach for yield) with comments from Rosengren, Dudley, Tarullo, Fisher, Lacker, Plosser, George, Lockhart. Plus Europe/bank stress in the go-round and Fisher and George raising financial stability in the policy round. Moderate-to-substantial, mid-50s.
- Forward guidance tone: Calendar guidance "at least through late 2014" retained; a strong date-based easing commitment, though many argue it is becoming stale and want state-contingent language and some want an earlier date. Strong easing bias, low teens.
- Uncertainty: "A great deal of uncertainty about how this recovery is going to proceed" (Bernanke); Okun's law puzzle, weather distortions, participation, Europe, fiscal cliff; Evans "awash in downside risks"; Pianalto notes GDP uncertainty back near normal but unemployment and inflation uncertainty higher. Elevated but not extreme: mid-50s.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (below potential/slack) ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (easing/accommodative) ok. CREDIT_SPREADS < 50: direction (narrowing) plus Duke's plentiful-credit level description; ok. BANKING_STRESS < 50 (sound, well-capitalized) ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50 (stronger dollar) ok. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR 25: 7 apart, consistent (both well below 50). OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 46: 14 apart, within 15; spending is described as moderate while slack stays large, consistent with a slow recovery. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 42: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A, no crisis. INFLATION_PRESSURE 47 vs sub-components: goods ~50 (energy-driven rise expected to fade, commodities flat) and expectations ~50 (anchored); services and housing not discussed. Consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment of productivity growth by members. TREASURY_STRESS: no discussion of Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_SERVICES: not discussed (only wages, which are about labor). INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices discussed, but no shelter/rent inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 47 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: inflation "is very close to 2 percent, and it does seem to be well anchored overall... mostly a gasoline story." Yellen: inflation "will remain at or below the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." Counter-view, Plosser: "Inflation remains above our target and has been more stubborn than many anticipated."
- Center of gravity: At/near target with a modest upside lean from headline and a few hawks, offset by slack-driven below-target views.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because the center sees core near 2 and expects containment, with several seeing below-2 outcomes.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "Resource slack remains unacceptably high... the latest Tealbook assessment... has the output gap at 4 3/4 percent." Tarullo: "by any reasonable measure, a substantial resource utilization gap still to be filled."
- Center of gravity: Large slack acknowledged, with some (Kocherlakota, Bullard, Lacker) believing it smaller.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe sizeable remaining slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "plodding improvements in a dismal employment situation." Plosser: unemployment "though still very elevated at 8.2 percent." Tarullo: "absolutely no sign of wage pressures."
- Center of gravity: High unemployment, little wage pressure; scattered skilled-worker shortages.
- Direction: Well below neutral because unemployment is far above its normal level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: consumer spending "continues to expand, notwithstanding high gas prices," but "there doesn't seem to be the fuel needed to accelerate." Lockhart: investment "aimed at productivity gains and cost savings" with "actual business expansion... considerably more subdued."
- Center of gravity: Moderate growth, solid autos and retail, but limited momentum from income, fiscal drag and cautious businesses.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because spending is moderate and constrained by headwinds.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A** (not assessed by members).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "easing of financial conditions." Duke: "Funding for loans is abundant... banks are aggressively competing." Counter: Kocherlakota: "small and young businesses are finding credit hard to obtain"; Bernanke: "markets have once again become jittery."
- Center of gravity: Credit conditions improving and supportive in the US, with European stress and weak mortgage/CRE credit as offsets.
- Direction: Below neutral because conditions are described as easing and supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (Treasury market functioning not discussed).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "further easing of spreads on C&I loans." Duke: "credit is plentiful enough across the full spectrum of credit quality to meet whatever demand materializes."
- Center of gravity: Spreads narrowing and credit available; levels not quantified.
- Direction: Below neutral because narrowing spreads and plentiful credit imply favorable, below-normal stress.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "if you go back pre-1968, current S&P market values are one standard deviation above the trend; if you go back to 1990, they're right on trend." Plosser: "pickup in equity wealth."
- Center of gravity: One member's mild valuation concern; others only note stable or higher wealth.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (modestly above fair value) because the only valuation view is mildly elevated.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "the banks do have lots of capital, they have very large liquidity buffers." Duke: "charge-offs and delinquencies are at historic lows." Counter: Rosengren on broker-dealers' funding model "under duress, could be potentially troubling."
- Center of gravity: US banks sound, with a pocket of concern in broker-dealer funding and exposure to Europe.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe healthy banks with specific, contained vulnerabilities.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Tarullo: Europe "keep[s] the dollar stronger than it otherwise would be." Bernanke: "a stronger dollar and weaker non-fuel import prices."
- Center of gravity: Brief references to a moderately strong dollar.
- Direction: Above neutral because the dollar is described as stronger.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52** (Bernanke: "mostly a gasoline story", non-energy commodities flat and 16 percent below peak, Dudley: apparel pressure expected to moderate; energy-driven rise expected to fade).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** (not discussed).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** (shelter inflation not discussed).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50** (Pianalto: measures "have remained anchored"; Yellen: "longer-run inflation expectations firmly anchored"; Lockhart notes some heightened longer-run worry in survey work).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "broad support for alternative B... downside risks, which I think are greater than the upside risks." Yellen: "I would see a very strong case for initiating another round of the maturity extension program" if the outlook weakens. Counter: Lacker: "I'll dissent if the statement isn't close to [mid-2013]"; Plosser: "begin reducing the level of accommodation as soon as either late this year or early 2013."
- Center of gravity: Hold at highly accommodative settings with contingency for more easing at the center and a sizable hawkish minority urging earlier exit.
- Direction: Below neutral (dovish) because the center prefers holding with downside-risk asymmetry and readiness to add accommodation.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Roll call: "President Lacker No" (one dissent). Lockhart: "I would like to register some reservations... 'late 2014' language as increasingly untenable." Straw poll to drop the "highly accommodative" clause: "one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight."
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus several reservations about the date, the SEP and policy rules, in a civil tone.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement; mostly over communications and reaction functions, with some disagreement on the output gap and risk asymmetry.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dedicated segment on the staff financial stability report: Rosengren on broker-dealer funding and farmland prices, Dudley on the "vicious circle" from Moody's downgrades, Fisher on asset valuations and "reaching for returns". Plosser: signaling risks "misallocations of capital and credit".
- Center of gravity: A full discussion segment and repeated references across the go-rounds, though economic assessment and communications dominated.
- Direction: Attention is moderately high, several participants engaging meaningfully.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "maintaining the 'late 2014' language is still appropriate"; Rosengren seeks language "that conveys a greater commitment to keeping rates at exceptionally low levels through 2014". Counter: Plosser/Lockhart/Williams want to move away from the calendar date; Lacker wants earlier liftoff.
- Center of gravity: A date-based commitment to near-zero rates through late 2014 is retained with a contingent easing option, despite growing calls for state-contingent or earlier-date language.
- Direction: Easing-biased guidance, well below neutral; not as extreme as an explicit thresholds/2015 commitment because the date is under pressure from many speakers.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "a great deal of uncertainty about how this recovery is going to proceed." Evans: "a world that is awash in downside risks." Pianalto: uncertainty about GDP is back near historical norms, but unemployment and inflation remain more uncertain than normal.
- Center of gravity: Multiple participants stress unusually elevated uncertainty (Europe, fiscal cliff, Okun's law puzzle, weather-distorted data), though not extreme.
- Direction: Elevated uncertainty, above the "normal" band.
