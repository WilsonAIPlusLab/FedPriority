# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 2001-05-15 (transcript, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: the Chairman's opening business (minutes, swaps, questions to staff), the economic go-around (all Reserve Bank presidents and governors, with the Vice Chairman), the Chairman's policy recommendation, the policy go-around, a short recess, then a go-around on the draft statement and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. I ignore staff-reported content and procedure.

**2. Era context.** A slowdown with an investment/high-tech bust, falling manufacturing, rising unemployment from low levels, a very aggressive easing sequence (funds rate cut 200 bp this year, five 50 bp moves including two intermeeting; the proposal today is another 50 bp to 4 percent), and energy price pressure on core inflation. Scored from the text only.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: Greenbook is the best point estimate, but risks are asymmetric to the downside, "risks are really different from any I've ever seen." Proposes a 50 bp cut and a weakness-biased risk statement; thinks 4 percent may be the end; wants to hint at an end of easing in June, not today. Is not very worried about inflation (CPI "flawed", pricing power limited).
- Hawkish-leaning or caution voices: Hoenig (core inflation creeping, policy "accommodative", wants 25 bp, votes No), Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Guynn, Jordan, Santomero (all want to slow or signal an end), Meyer (risks more balanced, end easing sooner), Poole (potential growth risk, upside risks, equal odds next move is up or down).
- Dovish voices: McTeer, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, McDonough, Parry, Moskow (downside risks dominate; "lean harder against recessionary winds"; Ferguson wants a "slightly stimulative stance").
- Economy: very weak manufacturing, investment "largely sidelined", payroll employment fell sharply in April, unemployment rising (about 4.5 percent), consumption and housing holding up, inventory correction advanced outside high tech.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core measures have "crept up" (Gramlich, Santomero, Guynn, Hoenig, Meyer), energy and benefit costs are pushing, CPI about 3 percent (Broaddus), core PCE just over 2 percent (Parry). Offsetting: pricing power limited (Greenspan), "price inflation remains subdued" regionally (Guynn, McDonough), "more reports of price declines" (McTeer), "series of blips" (Gramlich). Net: 55.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output below potential. Ferguson: economy "growing far below its potential"; Meyer: output "falling below potential, but not very far below"; capacity utilization at 10-year lows (Gramlich); unemployment rising toward NAIRU only in 2002. The slowdown began from above potential, but the current gap is negative and modest. Net: 41. Bright line (<50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor markets loosening ("easing", help-wanted signs diminished, layoffs swamping hiring, sharp April payroll drop) but unemployment still about 4.5 percent (Broaddus), 3.8-4 percent in parts of the region, regional shortages of nurses (Jordan), and Poole argues 4.5 percent may not be above the natural rate. Level near 4.5 percent is neutral-to-slightly tight, direction is easing. Net: 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Weak on net. Business investment "largely sidelined", equipment spending down two quarters, capex plans cut, high-tech overhang; consumption "holding up reasonably well", housing and construction strong, April retail sales better. Weighted toward investment collapse and consumer fragility. Net: 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Meyer (structural productivity growth stabilized in 2000 and is projected to decline), Gramlich ("productivity is down", unit labor costs rising), Greenspan ("virtual stall in productivity growth"), Santomero ("strong upward trend" earlier). Participants see recent productivity as below trend/stalling after strong gains. Net: 42, moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Easing, "decline in risk spreads" and "double-digit M2 growth" (Hoenig), bond issuance and equities up 6-9 percent since March (Ferguson), steeper yield curve; against that, banks tightening standards modestly, wealth destruction, bear market in stocks. Hoenig calls policy accommodative; Ferguson calls real rate "about neutral" moving to slightly stimulative. Somewhat loose to neutral. Net: 46. Bright line (<50 for accommodative) satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market discussed only as yield movements (long rates up about 1/2 point, steeper curve, TIPS spread, loss of scarcity premium). No dysfunction. Normal functioning. Net: 40, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only direction given: Hoenig cites "the decline in credit risk spreads"; bond issuance strong; banks tightening standards. No level stated. Per the v4.1 rule, direction-only goes within 45-55 on the side of the move (narrowing), low confidence. Net: 46.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan: stock prices "still probably higher than what our models say would be consistent with their fundamental determinants" (modestly rich); also bear market, wealth destruction, but equities up 6-9 percent since March (Ferguson). Valuation view is modestly above fair value with decline risk. Net: 42, low-moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Broaddus: "lending institutions now are well capitalized"; Guynn: no new consumer credit concerns, some worry about syndicated credits; Poole and McDonough: standards tightening modestly; Jordan: bank profits "very good". Foreign (Japan) bank problems noted but out of scope for the US system. Healthy with minor concerns. Net: 35. Bright line (<50) satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only Ferguson: "The dollar also has remained strong." Level strong, no stated appreciation. Net: 54, low confidence.
- Sub-components: goods N/A (not addressed as such). Services: health care costs, nurses' salaries, benefits rising (Guynn, McDonough, Jordan), Greenspan says service inflation is exaggerated in the indexes: 56, a modest signal. Housing: only Minehan's regional CPI remark; N/A. Expectations: Michigan one-year about 3 percent (Broaddus), TIPS spread rose, steeper curve may reflect inflation expectations (Santomero, Minehan, Hoenig), but Gramlich says surveys are stable and credibility is strong (McDonough, Broaddus): 56.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more inflation; evidence is modest upcreep, so slightly above 50: matches. OUTPUT_GAP below 50 with "below potential": matches. BANKING_STRESS 35 with "well capitalized": matches (low stress). CREDIT_SPREADS: narrowing with no level gives slightly below 50: matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: A 50 bp cut, backed by a seeming majority, with an asymmetric weakness-biased risk statement: dovish. Large minority wants 25 bp or a signal that easing is nearly done (Hoenig, Guynn, Jordan, Santomero, Minehan, Broaddus, Stern); Poole sees equal odds next move is up. Moderate dovish with a hawkish-leaning tilt in the signal debate. Score 35.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Hoenig, preferring 25 bp; his economic and risk view otherwise matches). Many "I could have supported 25" reservations (Minehan, Broaddus, Meyer, Santomero, Guynn, Stern, Jordan). Sharp-ish but polite disagreement between Ferguson and the Chairman over speeches, and a 6-4 split on when to add the "limit of easing" sentence. Disagreement is mostly tactical (size, signaling). Score 40.
- Financial stability emphasis: Several mention markets vs economists, equities, credit spreads, bank standards; Minehan raises "financial market excesses", one-way bets; Greenspan on stock valuations and wealth effect. It is context, not the focus. Score 30.
- Forward guidance: The statement keeps the weakness bias and drops the intermeeting-move language; Greenspan plans to hint that 250 bp may be enough, in June; Poole wants "equal probability next change up as down"; others (Ferguson, Kelley, McDonough) want no extra signal. The signal is a mild move away from easing but is explicitly not given today. Score 40.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "extremely uncertain. So am I"; Moskow "highly uncertain"; Minehan "extremely uncertain"; Greenspan: risks "different from any I've ever seen", models do not work; Poole "very uncertain situation"; Ferguson "future ... not foreseeable". Many emphasize it, some rhetorical. Score 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 41 (<50 for below potential): OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 (<50 given accommodative language): OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 46 (<50, direction only narrowing, 45-55 band): OK. BANKING_STRESS 35 (<50): OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 54 (>50 for "remained strong"): OK. TREASURY_STRESS 40 (<55): OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 41 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 50: gap 9, fine (unemployment level still low, flagged by Poole). OUTPUT_GAP 41 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: close. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 46: consistent. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: normal. INFLATION_PRESSURE 55 vs sub-components (services 56, expectations 56): consistent.

**9. N/A.** INFLATION_GOODS: not discussed as a category. INFLATION_HOUSING: only a one-line regional CPI remark by one participant; not enough to score.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 55 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Hoenig, "The gradual increase in core inflation over the past year is a concern to me"; Gramlich, "various measures of core inflation have risen slightly ... a series of blips largely related to energy prices." Center of gravity: modest upcreep in core, offset by limited pricing power. Inflation pressure is above neutral because core measures and energy costs are rising, though most call it contained.

**OUTPUT_GAP 41 ±6 [High]**. Evidence: Ferguson, "an economy growing ... far below its potential"; Meyer, output falls "below potential, but not very far below." Below neutral because growth is far below potential and capacity utilization is at 10-year lows.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 50 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Stern, "Labor market conditions appear to be easing ... help-wanted signs has clearly diminished"; Poole, "4-1/2 percent unemployment" may not be above the natural rate; Broaddus, 4-1/2 percent "still at a relatively low level." At neutral: low-but-rising unemployment, loosening direction.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38 ±6 [High]**. Evidence: Guynn, investment "remains largely sidelined"; Poole/McTeer, consumer spending "holding up reasonably well." Below neutral because investment is collapsing while consumption and housing hold, with consumers seen as fragile.

**PRODUCTIVITY 42 ±8 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Meyer, "structural productivity growth stabilized in 2000" and is projected to decline; Greenspan, "the virtual stall in productivity growth." Below neutral because of the stall/decline.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Hoenig, "the decline in short-term rates, the decline in risk spreads, and double-digit rates of growth in M2"; Poole, bankers say "lending standards are being tightened modestly." Slightly below neutral (somewhat loose to neutral) because easing and falling spreads outweigh modest bank tightening.

**TREASURY_STRESS 40 ±8 [Low]**. Evidence: Broaddus, "Long-term Treasury rates have moved up by nearly 1/2 point"; Greenspan on the scarcity premium. Normal functioning; yield moves are not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 46 ±6 [Low]**. Evidence: Hoenig, "the decline in credit risk spreads"; Ferguson, "businesses are issuing bonds." Direction-only narrowing, no stated level, so slightly below 50 under the v4.1 rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 42 ±8 [Low]**. Evidence: Greenspan, "stock market prices are still probably higher than what our models say would be consistent with their fundamental determinants"; Ferguson, equities up "6 to 9 percent." Modestly rich valuations with a decline risk, below 50 on this polarity scale.

**BANKING_STRESS 35 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Broaddus, "Lending institutions now are well capitalized"; Guynn, bankers "not reporting any new concerns about consumer credit quality." Below neutral: healthy with minor concerns (tightening standards, syndicated credits).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 54 ±6 [Low]**. Evidence: Ferguson, "The dollar also has remained strong." Slightly above neutral: strong level, no stated move.

**INFLATION_GOODS N/A. INFLATION_SERVICES 56. INFLATION_HOUSING N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 56.** Services: Guynn on nurses' salaries and health costs; Greenspan that service inflation is overstated in the indexes. Expectations: Broaddus, Michigan survey one-year expectations about 3 percent and indexed bond rates suggest increase; Gramlich, "inflation expectations in the Michigan and Philadelphia surveys are stable."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 35 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Greenspan, "a further 50 basis point reduction ... and a balance of risks statement biased toward economic weakness"; Hoenig/Guynn/Jordan/Santomero ask for 25 bp. Moderate dovish: a majority backs a 50 bp cut, with a sizable minority arguing for a smaller step and an end-of-easing signal.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 40 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Hoenig votes No ("I think we should pull back on the throttle today"); Minehan, "I could have supported 25 basis points"; vote of 6-4 on adding the signal sentence in May versus June. One formal dissent plus many reservations, mostly over tactics and signaling.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 30 ±8 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Minehan, "financial market excesses should be [a concern]"; Ferguson on the disconnect between markets and economists. A few discuss markets, spreads, and equity valuations as context.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 40 ±8 [Moderate]**. Evidence: Greenspan, "we probably can stop our easing moves here"; Poole, "more or less an equal probability that the next rate change is going to be up as down." The weakness bias is retained, and a hint of an end to easing is deferred to June.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 62 ±7 [Moderate]**. Evidence: McDonough, "the main message the market is conveying is that it is extremely uncertain. So am I"; Greenspan, "the risks are really different from any I've ever seen." Many emphasize difficulty and downside tail risk, with models said not to work.
