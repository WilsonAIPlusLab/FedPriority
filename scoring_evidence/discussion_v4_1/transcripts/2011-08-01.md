- TREASURY_STRESS: 54 (+-12, Low). Evidence: Dudley, "pretty strong market consequences" last week, and now "people are getting into cash and therefore that is putting upward pressure on repo rates"; Evans, action 6 would "alleviate negative T-bill or repo rates"; Yellen, concern "about the liquidity of T-bills." Most of the stress language is hypothetical or contingent (Dudley: bill rates "would probably go up significantly" if the vote fails), and Lacker argues that price moves are not dysfunction. No auction failure or settlement problem is reported. Elevated concern, not dysfunction, so it stays below 60.

- INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. Not discussed.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. Not discussed.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A. Bullard and Kocherlakota discuss only a hypothetical un-anchoring if the Fed were seen to monetize debt, not a current reading.

Discussion-specific
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: N/A. Special-topic document; no policy stance discussed. Evans notes that actions 7 and 9 "might confuse with QE3" and that action 6 might look like "the beginning of an exit strategy," but he is warning against misreading, not advocating a stance.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: N/A. No vote; no dissent. Members differ on options 6-10 (Plosser "deeply worried" about item 8, Fisher "firmly against" 9 and 10, Dudley wants a bill-purchase backstop) but Bernanke summarizes "a lot of support for 1 through 5" and "the majority" for a very high bar on 9 and 10.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 85 (+-7, High). Evidence: the whole meeting is on market function, money market funds and Treasury auction risk; Dudley, "the Federal Reserve is prepared to intervene to ensure orderly money market function"; Yellen, "potential ramifications for financial stability"; Bernanke, "financial stability concerns were sufficiently severe." Nearly every participant speaks to it.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: N/A. No rate-path or policy-path signal; communication discussed is about lender-of-last-resort actions (Kocherlakota, Williams, Evans), not guidance on policy rates.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 (+-12, Low). Evidence: Dudley, "there is still some risk that there will be no deal, so we're not out of the woods yet"; Tarullo, "maybe an 80 or 85 percent chance that we're going to get by this"; Bullard, "I could imagine little or no market for defaulted securities"; Yellen, a default "might well have had catastrophic market consequences." The uncertainty is about a binary political event and market reactions, not the economic outlook, and is stated with specific probabilities, so moderately elevated.
