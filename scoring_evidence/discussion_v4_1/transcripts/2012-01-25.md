# v4.1 Scoring: member-discussion transcript, meeting 2012-01-25 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript for a two-day meeting (Day 1: special topic on household leverage and lending, organizational items, adoption of a longer-run goals statement, financial developments, financial-stability remarks, economic go-round; Day 2: policy questions, policy go-round, wording of the statement, vote read-out). Staff turns are removed, so many member questions appear without answers. No garbled passages that block scoring.

**2. Era context.** Early 2012: unemployment around 8.5%, a stop-and-go recovery, short-term rate at the zero bound, asset purchases and calendar-date guidance in use, European sovereign/bank crisis with ECB three-year LTRO relief. I score what participants say, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Optimists on the data: Plosser, Lockhart, Bullard, Fisher, George, Duke. They see firmer footing, more business optimism and falling unemployment.
- Cautious or pessimistic: Williams, Yellen, Tarullo, Raskin, Evans, Rosengren, Pianalto, Dudley (cautiously better). They see large slack, weak income and a "false dawn" risk.
- Policy camps. Doves: Evans (wants roughly $600bn of purchases and thresholds), Rosengren, Williams, Yellen, Dudley, with Tarullo, Raskin and Duke supporting an extended date and open to MBS later. Hawks: Lacker (dissents), Plosser (wants tightening to begin this year), Bullard, Fisher, George (no more accommodation, drop date or thresholds). Middle: Pianalto, Lockhart, Kocherlakota (non-voter, wary of exhausting tools). Chairman backs B(3') with the late-2014 date.

**4. Directional statements (core dimensions).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. They report recent deceleration, inflation near or below 2% (Williams 1.5% forecast, Yellen persistently below, Evans "lack of inflation risk"), and the Chairman calls inflation risks "relatively low". A few see risks (Plosser says inflation is above goal, Pianalto flags OER, Lockhart is a bit higher than the Tealbook). Below 50, about 40.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Substantial slack is described ("resource slack so large", "a lot of slack remaining", Tarullo "considerable amount of slack"). Plosser sees growth "slightly above trend" but does not claim the economy is above potential. Clearly below 50, about 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment about 8.5%, U-6 15%, four job seekers per opening, 6 million jobs short. Some improvement but level is far from tight. Low band, 25.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption "moderate", income "nearly flat", sentiment up but low, investment mixed, strong in autos, energy and equipment. Net slightly below trend, 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing remarks on cost-containment and unit labor costs, no assessment of productivity growth. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Dudley says conditions "have eased considerably recently" (dollar excepted); the Chairman says they are "somewhat better"; Bullard calls the real-rate stance "particularly easy"; Dudley says market QE expectations keep conditions "more accommodative". Offsets: Europe still fragile, mortgage credit tight (Duke), broker-dealer funding fragility. Accommodative on balance, below 50: 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members discuss Treasury yields (flight to quality, term premium) and hypothetical capacity limits for purchases, but nobody describes dysfunction or stress in the Treasury market. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No statement of the level of US corporate spreads. Evidence is mixed: Dudley sees credit availability improving; Duke sees credit "actively being offered" outside mortgages but "tight credit conditions" in mortgages; European sovereign spreads fell with the LTRO but remain high (Portugal around 14% 10-year); broker-dealer CDS "as high as they are" (Rosengren). Level not stated, so near normal: 52, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Price moves noted (equities up, volatility down) as a signal about the economy; no valuation commentary. Default 45-55: 48. Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Dudley: US firms have more capital, bigger liquidity buffers, cleaner balance sheets; Lacker: banks hold abundant liquidity. But Dudley and Rosengren stress run vulnerability of broker-dealers with no lender of last resort, and European banks are still stressed though relieved by the LTRO. System described as sound with pockets of stress: 45.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dudley's phrase "with the notable exception of the dollar" implies a dollar that has appreciated and tightened conditions; Plosser cites flight to quality into the dollar. Only passing mentions: 57, low confidence.
- Sub-components: goods fell back (Chairman: durables and autos "come back down", apparel stable, commodity prices down about 10% since July): 36. Services: no clear services-price statement (wages "limited" is labor, not prices): N/A. Housing/shelter: OER "steadily picked up" (Pianalto), rents rising, though the Chairman sees OER stabilizing near 2%: 57. Expectations: stable, "low end of historical ranges", Kocherlakota worries about downward drift: 40.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more inflation, more slack-free economy, tighter labor, stronger spending, tighter financial conditions. My evidence points to low inflation, big slack, loose labor market, modest spending, eased conditions, so scores sit below 50 on all of these. CREDIT_SPREADS and EQUITY_VALUATION are near 50 because the text gives no level or valuation view.

**6. Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee extends the date from mid-2013 to late 2014 (B(3')), no new purchases today; a large group wants more accommodation (Evans $600bn; Rosengren, Williams, Yellen, Dudley say MBS purchases are warranted soon if the outlook does not improve; Chairman says a long period of below-target inflation would be suboptimal). A hawkish minority (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, George, Bullard) opposes or wants guidance removed, and Plosser/Lacker see tightening in 2013. Moderately dovish majority with a hawkish minority: 31.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Lacker, vote read-out). Day 1 consensus statement: 16 in favor, one abstention (Tarullo said he could not support it). Sharp disagreement on thresholds, calendar date, SEP as substitute for guidance, and on whether more accommodation is warranted (Bullard vs Evans on optimal policy); disagreement is in both directions and extends to economics (natural rate, slack). Distinct camps with one dissent: 50.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Dedicated financial-stability agenda item (Rosengren, Dudley, Lacker on broker-dealers, runs, money funds), Europe discussed by most participants, leverage special topic, financial developments briefing. Prominent but not dominant over the economic assessment: 58.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Extends date-based near-zero guidance by about 18 months to late 2014, with further balance sheet action kept open (Dudley, Chairman). Strongly easing, though the threshold version (7% rule) was dropped and the Chairman stresses "not a commitment": 11.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Many stress forecast error bands (Plosser RMSE, Bullard "huge"), Europe tail risks (Yellen: risks "exceptionally large"), Rosengren "higher-than-normal level of uncertainty", natural rate estimates "all over the map" (Lacker), Chairman "we really don't know why the saving rate has fallen". Elevated but not extreme: 58.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 (<50, slack language) OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 (<50, "eased"/"accommodative") OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 52: no high-level or low-level wording, within the 45-55 direction-only band, OK. BANKING_STRESS 45 (<50, "more capital", sound) OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 (>50, appreciation implied) OK. TREASURY_STRESS N/A (no dysfunction described).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs LABOR 25: gap of 8, fine. OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 43: gap of 10, fine (spending near trend-ish while level of activity is below potential). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 52: modest divergence; conditions eased on equities, rates and Europe relief, while credit-spread evidence is mixed and stated only indirectly. Accepted with low confidence on spreads. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 40 vs sub-components (goods 36, housing 57, expectations 40): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment by members. TREASURY_STRESS: only yields/capacity hypotheticals, no market-function description. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-price statement apart from shelter, which is scored under housing.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±6 (Moderate-High, scored High)**
- Evidence: Williams, "Inflationary pressures have clearly diminished over the past six months"; Bernanke, "inflation risks seem relatively low at this stage."
- Summary: Inflation seen as running at or somewhat under 2% and easing, with a minority noting upside risks (OER, Plosser).
- Direction: below neutral because most project inflation at or below the 2% goal.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Evans, "With resource slack so large and inflation projections well contained"; Williams, "All indicators point to a lot of slack remaining in labor markets."
- Summary: Broad agreement on substantial slack; Plosser's slightly-above-trend growth is not an above-potential claim.
- Direction: below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Pianalto, "Today, there are about four job seekers for every job opening"; Tarullo, "U-6 ... is still at 15 percent."
- Summary: Unemployment about 8.5%, improving but far from full employment.
- Direction: below neutral because the level of unemployment is high.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 ±6 (High)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "what is most likely is a moderate path of expansion in consumption"; Raskin, real DPI "has been nearly flat."
- Summary: Moderate spending with weak income and mixed investment, strong pockets (autos, energy).
- Direction: slightly below trend/neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions, with the notable exception of the dollar, have eased considerably recently"; Bullard, "a particularly easy stance of monetary policy."
- Summary: Conditions viewed as eased and accommodative, with European and mortgage-market caveats.
- Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 52 ±8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "Credit availability continues to improve"; Duke, "Tight credit conditions are still a problem" (mortgage market).
- Summary: No stated spread level; mixed signals around the middle.
- Direction: at neutral; no level language, so kept in the direction-only band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±6 (Low)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "equity prices rose considerably and volatility declined"; Dudley, depressed market values of broker-dealers tied to "questions about the business model."
- Summary: Price moves noted as signals, no valuation view.
- Direction: at neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 45 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "firms have more capital ... bigger liquidity buffers"; Rosengren, "Is the broker-dealer model sustainable if we have a European crisis?"
- Summary: US banks sound, with run-vulnerability in broker-dealers and European bank stress eased but unresolved.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the system is described as better capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±6 (Low)**
- Evidence: Dudley, conditions eased "with the notable exception of the dollar"; Plosser, "flight to quality, to the U.S. dollar."
- Summary: Only indirect references, pointing to dollar appreciation.
- Direction: above neutral because the dollar is described as having strengthened.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36**: Bernanke, "Prices of autos and other durables have come back down."
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 57**: Pianalto, OER "has steadily picked up", could add about half a point to core CPI; Bernanke sees OER "stabilizing lately close to 2 percent."
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40**: Bernanke, "Inflation expectations remain at the low end of historical ranges"; Lacker, "pretty stable."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 31 ±6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the outlook plainly justifies additional policy accommodation"; Plosser, "the Committee will need to commence policy tightening this year."
- Summary: Majority backs an extended low-rate date and keeps MBS purchases in reserve, against a hawkish minority.
- Direction: below neutral (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 ±7 (High)**
- Evidence: Lacker, "I can't support alternative B as written" (the one "No" in the vote read-out); Evans, "I favor more accommodation today," against Bullard's argument that high unemployment and low inflation do not show more should be done.
- Summary: One formal dissent plus camps pulling in opposite directions on thresholds, dates and the need for more easing; Tarullo's abstention on the consensus statement.
- Direction: above the broad-agreement range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 ±8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Dudley, "there is no really good lender-of-last-resort backstop for these firms"; Williams, "vulnerabilities to runs, vulnerabilities to a liquidity stress."
- Summary: Dedicated agenda item, widespread discussion of Europe and leverage, but the economic outlook and communication dominate.
- Direction: moderately prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 11 ±5 (High)**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "I would strongly support and strongly urge us to accept the date"; Dudley, we should "make it clear that we're prepared to do more if economic conditions warrant."
- Summary: Date pushed out to late 2014 with additional purchases held in reserve; threshold language set aside for later.
- Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 ±7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the risks to the forecast remain exceptionally large"; Bullard, "The amount of uncertainty is huge."
- Summary: Several members stress forecast error bands and Europe tail risks; not described as unprecedented.
- Direction: elevated.
