## Part 2: Evidence and Scores (v4.1 + V4.1.1; participants' views only)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE = 36 +-6 High.** Kashkari: "core 12-month PCE inflation is very low at 1.3 percent... a large shortfall relative to our target". Brainard: "inflation, which has been below target for five years and has moved down rather than up this year". Evans: "underlying inflation is mired below 2 percent". Offsetting minority: Mester "price pressures... may be changing" (Cleveland output-price index up), Harker "signs of price pressures". Most expect a return to 2 percent only gradually.

**OUTPUT_GAP = 56 +-5 High.** Williams: "This pace of growth is substantially above the economy's potential"; "The economy continues to grow above potential". Mullinix: "With real GDP running above potential, the labor market continues to tighten". Yellen: "growth will run somewhat above potential"; "I don't yet consider it overheated". Level is modestly above (bright line > 50 satisfied).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS = 60 +-6 High.** Rosengren: unemployment "4.2 percent... well below" the natural rate; claims "at lows last seen in the 1970s"; "unusually tight". George: "little evidence of slack remaining". Powell: "near full employment" though "some remaining slack" in participation; Kashkari: "there is still slack in the labor market"; Bostic: "near full employment". UE 4.2 percent with muted wages gives a mid-60s ceiling, pulled down by the slack camp.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT = 58 +-6 High.** Mullinix: "healthy growth in consumer spending and business equipment investment". Powell: "continued strong business investment"; Brainard: "Business investment is up over last year, and consumers remain upbeat". Williams: "solid gains in consumer and business spending". Harker: residential "lackluster". Net: above-trend, solid.

**PRODUCTIVITY = 38 +-8 Moderate.** Kaplan: productivity "surprisingly muted". George: "annual labor productivity growth of 0.7 percent" over five years. Mester: "Unless the pace of productivity growth picks up". Bullard/Powell hold out only a possible pickup from investment. Below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = 30 +-6 High.** Brainard: "Financial conditions remain accommodative". Dudley: "financial conditions are easy"; "still at a very accommodative setting". George: "uneasy about the current accommodative stance of financial conditions"; VIX "near historical lows". Yellen: "I judge them to have tightened a bit" (dollar), but still easy. Bright line < 50 satisfied.

**TREASURY_STRESS = 38 +-10 Low.** Dudley: balance-sheet effect on "Treasury security and agency MBS markets has been very hard to discern"; ten-year yields up about 20bp due to stronger economy and tax-cut hopes. Kaplan names a "term rate shock" only as a possible future catalyst. No dysfunction cited; orderly functioning, thin evidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS = 22 +-6 High.** Quarles: "Junk bond spreads are at the 15th percentile of their historical distribution"; CRE cap-rate spread "at post-crisis lows". Rosengren: "Very narrow interest rate spreads". Brainard: "corporate bond spreads have fallen further, especially for high-yield bonds". Level stated as compressed (< 50).

**EQUITY_VALUATION = 20 +-6 High.** Kaplan: US market cap "approximately 140 percent of U.S. GDP... highest... since the 1999-2000 peak"; Quarles: "asset values are high relative to fundamentals". Brainard: "increasingly stretched asset valuations". George: equity prices "flashing red". Yellen: "rising valuation pressures... disquieting". Explicit valuation concern, tempered by "limited leverage".

**BANKING_STRESS = 32 +-7 Moderate.** Quarles: "banks with much higher capital throughout the system"; "the system is much more resilient". Brainard: "subdued financial-sector leverage"; "resilience of our financial system". Rosengren's dissenting concern about small-bank CRE exposure keeps it from the lowest range. Healthy (< 50).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH = 60 +-6 High.** Brainard: "the dollar strengthened about 3 percentage points in trade-weighted terms"; "good reason to expect further appreciation". Yellen: "the dollar has also appreciated". Appreciation (> 50).

**INFLATION_GOODS = 50.** Harker: "significant increases in the Prices Paid Index"; Mester: Cleveland output-price index up; against Williams's "big drops in pharmaceuticals". Mixed, near stable.

**INFLATION_SERVICES = 38.** Williams: "big drops in pharmaceuticals, air fares, cellular communications, and other services", plus legislative cuts in health-care payments; Powell: "sharp decrease in mobile phone service prices". Soft services prices.

**INFLATION_HOUSING = N/A.** Only Rosengren's sectoral-Phillips-curve finding on rents/OER (responsiveness, not a level or direction) and Williams's local post-fire house-price comment; no national housing-inflation view.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS = 40.** Evans: "inflation expectations have slipped well below 2 percent"; Kashkari: "inflation expectations are too low"; Bostic/Brainard worry about drift below 2 percent. Against: Mester, Dudley, George, Mullinix see expectations "stable", "albeit at low levels". Anchored but low, with downside-drift worry.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN = 58 +-6 High.** Hold at this meeting (alt B, unanimous), with "gradual removal of accommodation" endorsed by most. Hawkish side: Williams "moving monetary policy back to neutral... not with timidity"; Powell "an increase in December is likely to be appropriate"; Dudley "I would favor tightening"; George, Mester, Rosengren, Mullinix, Quarles comfortable with December. Dovish side: Evans "delaying further rate increases... until the middle of next year"; Kashkari "don't think we should raise the federal funds rate until core PCE inflation actually hits 2 percent"; Bullard "my preference is for no December rate move"; Brainard "wait-and-see". Yellen: December confidence "slightly overstates". Lean hawkish: hold with tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY = 36 +-7 High.** Vote record: all nine voters "Yes", no dissent. But sharp policy and analytic divisions about December and the Phillips curve (Bullard "closer to dead" vs Williams/Dudley/Quarles; Kashkari "ghost story"; Evans, Kashkari, Bullard, Brainard against Dec hike vs Williams, Powell, Dudley). Disagreement about economics and path, none at this vote.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS = 62 +-7 High.** A dedicated financial-stability round precedes the go-round: Rosengren's CRE simulation, Kaplan's valuation/low-volatility remarks ("a little ominous"), Brainard, Quarles; again in the policy round (Bullard on equity valuations, Kashkari "countercyclical capital buffer", George, Kaplan "imbalances"). Prominent but not dominant next to inflation/labor debate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE = 62 +-7 Moderate.** Yellen: "gradually raising the federal funds rate back to neutral over the next few quarters"; market's high December probability: "do not think it would be advisable to try to adjust the market's expectations". Mester: "I'll support an increase in the funds rate in December". Statement keeps "gradual increases" signal; Yellen "keep an open mind". Mild tightening bias, data-dependent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED = 45 +-8 Moderate.** Yellen: "my confidence in this story slips a bit further"; Mester "we can't forecast inflation with much precision"; Bostic "an article of faith"; Powell "wide confidence intervals surrounding estimates of the natural rate"; hurricane-distorted data; fiscal outcome "still quite uncertain" (Dudley, Powell). Mostly confined to inflation, natural rate and hurricane noise; growth outlook confident.
