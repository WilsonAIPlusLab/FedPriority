# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2000-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). Structure: (a) an opening block on the System Open Market Account (SOMA) authorization, agency-securities collateral and a proposed FOMC study/subcommittee on the future of open market operations (procedural and institutional, not conditions); (b) questions to staff on the forecast; (c) the economic go-around (District reports plus national views); (d) the Chairman's policy recommendation and policy go-around; (e) a post-recess exchange on the press statement; (f) a vote read-out (10 yes, no dissents).

**2. Era context.** Early 2000, the late-1990s boom: very strong output growth, productivity acceleration, record-low unemployment, a rising-rate cycle, rising energy prices and a diverging NASDAQ/Dow. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), McDonough (Vice Chair), Reserve Bank presidents Parry, Jordan, Broaddus, Boehne, Guynn, Minehan, McTeer, Moskow, Stern, Hoenig, Poole; governors Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Measured core inflation is "well contained" (Meyer, Kelley, Broaddus "no broad signs of an acceleration in prices"), and Greenspan sees unit labor costs flat. But there are many reports of rising input prices, energy, freight and health costs, and a widespread view that core inflation will rise (Parry, Moskow, Meyer, Hoenig, Guynn "gradual upward creep"). Net: somewhat elevated and rising, not clearly above target.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. "Very strong growth in demand pressing on supply" (Boehne), "demand side ... simply too strong" (McDonough), "extremely strong demand driving the economy toward overheating" (Kelley), "unsustainable ... given the current levels of resource utilization" (Parry). Greenspan alone disputes "overheating" in the inflation sense, but concedes growth rates are very high. Clearly above, bright line > 50 satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. "Labor markets are as tight or even tighter ... than ever" (Guynn), unemployment 2.8% in New England (Minehan), 4.4% Texas "lowest in 20 years" (McTeer), Meyer cites national unemployment near 4% heading to 3.6%, Poole "the people just aren't there to hire". Wage pressure is mixed (Hoenig, Parry, Boehne say moderate). UE below ~4% anchors this high.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Well above trend. "Breathtaking" household spending, demand "insatiable", light vehicle sales "unbelievable" (Moskow), Q4 GDP at 7% (Broaddus), capital spending strong also outside tech (Ferguson).
- PRODUCTIVITY: Significantly above trend and accelerating. 3.7% in 1999, staff structural trend raised to 3.2% (Broaddus), Greenspan "second derivative is still positive", 4-3/4% for nonfinancial corporations.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Somewhat loose. Ferguson: financial markets "indeed somewhat accommodative to strong growth"; Stern: higher rates have had no effect on spending; Broaddus: long-term rates are not supplying the needed restraint; Parry's own forecast assumes "significant further tightening of financial conditions" is yet to come. Accommodative language means < 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market shrinkage (buybacks, projected debt paydown) was discussed in the SOMA item and by Gramlich (buybacks affecting nominal yields) and Minehan (liquidity/safe-haven role). No dysfunction described. Orderly, normal functioning; scored low with Low confidence. A 40ish score is consistent with the guidance (yield and supply effects are not dysfunction).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No participant gives spread levels. Indirect: Guynn "unsolicited knocks on the door from people wanting to push money at them", loan demand and availability strong, Moskow "loans are readily available", Broaddus BBB rates flat. Credit is ample, so below normal; Low confidence because there is no explicit spread statement.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation concern is on the elevated side. Minehan: "stock market ebullience" resembling the inflationary excess of the late 70s/early 80s; McDonough: "most ridiculously overpriced stocks are those of new technology companies that have never made any profits"; Poole: NASDAQ/S&P negative correlation "unprecedented". Offsetting: the NASDAQ was falling and old-economy stocks sagging despite earnings (Jordan, McTeer). Stocks viewed as rich in tech, so < 50 (the 2000 anchor is 15-25; the mixed picture tempers it).
- BANKING_STRESS: Briefly discussed. US credit healthy: Guynn "not picking up any signs of broad credit problems", bankruptcies 12% below year ago; Jordan banks report all loan categories strong; bank stock funds saw redemptions (market, not stress). Poole's concern is the Japanese financial system, which is foreign and a risk, not US banks. Healthy, so < 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Few mentions. McTeer: "The dollar remains strong"; Poole argues for a stable-to-stronger dollar given rising rates. Modest strength, so > 50.

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says accommodative/not yet restraining, so below 50. OUTPUT_GAP: evidence of excess demand implies above 50, matches. BANKING_STRESS: healthy implies below 50. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher = more stress/undervaluation concern; the participants' concern is overvaluation, so below 50. DOLLAR: strong = above 50. All consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: unanimous 25 bp hike with an upside-inflation-risk tilt retained. Several wanted a larger or more forceful approach (Minehan "probably could have gone with 50"; Gramlich "dalliances with 50"; Meyer a "40 percent case"; Broaddus "asymmetry with a vengeance"; Ferguson "a 50 bp move may be appropriate later"). A smaller group favoured a gentler path (McTeer "one step at a time", Hoenig gradual and not a committed path, Poole "very, very opposed" to 50). Overall consensus for continued tightening in gradual steps: strong hawkish but gradual, about 70.
- Disagreement: no formal dissents; all ten voted yes. Differences are about pace (25 vs 50) and how firm the forward commitment should be (Hoenig vs Broaddus), Broaddus's objection to the "essentially the same" sentence in the statement, and Greenspan's rejection of the overheating narrative; also earlier discomfort over agency collateral resolved into a consensus (Jordan, Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole). Broad agreement with notable emphasis differences.
- Financial stability: The stock market, wealth effects, NASDAQ/Dow divergence, financial-condition restraint and Japan financial fragility get meaningful but secondary attention from several participants; no discussion of systemic stress. The SOMA/GSE opening gets institutional rather than stability attention.
- Forward guidance: balance-of-risks sentence toward inflation retained; Greenspan "continue on a tightening path until there are problems with that path", Poole "25 basis point increases until the data convince us ...". Hoenig insists it is not a sure thing; Moskow notes re-examination at each meeting. Tightening signal, mildly qualified.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan "something very unusual is going on"; McDonough "a lot about this economy that is very difficult to understand"; Gramlich on S-curve productivity and the NAIRU: "This uncertainty more than anything else"; Hoenig "level of uncertainty"; Parry "we are uncertain" on energy pass-through, stock prices and productivity. Elevated, but participants are fairly confident on the near-term policy action.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP > 50 (ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 on accommodative language (ok). CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 because credit is ample; no stated high level (ok). BANKING_STRESS < 50 (ok). DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50 on a "strong" dollar (ok). TREASURY_STRESS ~40, no dysfunction (ok).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (66) vs LABOR (77): diff 11, within 15. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION (77): diff 11, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (36): co-move, both loose. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal, ~40. INFLATION_PRESSURE (55) vs sub-components (goods 58, services 58, housing 55, expectations 56): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** None of the four inflation sub-components was a main focus, but each had scattered participant comment (energy/materials for goods, health care for services, shelter in Boston for housing, expectations from Ferguson, Hoenig, Gramlich), so they are scored with the lowest confidence implied by the value-only format. No dimension is wholly N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Meyer: "core inflation remains well contained ... it will soon begin to rise"; Parry: "very high risk that core inflation will rise at a faster pace this year and next." Center of gravity: contained now, widely expected to rise. Direction: modestly above neutral because of rising input/energy/health costs and forecasts of acceleration, tempered by flat unit labor costs.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 66 ±6, Moderate/High (scored High).** Evidence: Boehne: "very strong growth in demand pressing on supply"; Kelley: "extremely strong demand driving the economy toward overheating." Center of gravity: demand outstripping supply with tight resource use. Above neutral because most participants describe excess demand; Greenspan alone disputes overheating.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 77 ±5, High.** Evidence: Guynn: "Labor markets are as tight or even tighter--if that's possible--than ever"; Minehan: "dropped to 2.8 percent in January." Center of gravity: unemployment near 4% and below, shortages everywhere. Above neutral by a wide margin.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 77 ±6, High.** Evidence: Moskow: "current growth in household spending is breathtaking"; Broaddus: real GDP "grew at a 7 percent rate in the fourth quarter." Center of gravity: demand surge, called unsustainable. Above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 80 ±6, High.** Evidence: Broaddus: "productivity growth came in at 3.7 percent in 1999 ... trend ... 3.2 percent"; Greenspan: "productivity is still accelerating. The second derivative is still positive." Center of gravity: structural acceleration widely accepted. Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson: financial markets "somewhat accommodative to strong growth"; Stern: directors say rates have had "no, not to this point" effect on spending. Center of gravity: conditions supportive and not yet restrictive. Below neutral (looser), though stock weakness and higher rates temper the looseness.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8, Low.** Evidence: Poole: Treasury debt "is not going to run out"; Gramlich: nominal rates "influenced by the Treasury's buyback program." Center of gravity: shrinking supply is a structural concern, not market dysfunction. Normal functioning, within the 36-50 band.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 ±8, Low.** Evidence: Guynn: "people wanting to push money at them"; Moskow: "Loans are readily available to businesses with reasonable prospects." Center of gravity: credit ample, no spread level stated. Below neutral on availability only; confidence Low as there are no explicit spread statements.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: McDonough: "most ridiculously overpriced stocks are those of new technology companies that have never made any profits"; Minehan: "today's period of stock market ebullience." Center of gravity: richly valued tech, odd divergences, a wealth effect feeding demand. Below neutral (overvaluation concern), moderated by falling NASDAQ and old-economy weakness.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±8, Low.** Evidence: Guynn: "not picking up any signs of broad credit problems ... bankruptcies ... 12 percent below"; Jordan: "all loan categories ... are reported strong." Center of gravity: US banks healthy; the Japan concern is foreign. Below neutral. Confidence Low because the system is touched only in passing.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±8, Low.** Evidence: McTeer: "The dollar remains strong"; Poole: conditions suggest "continued maintenance of the dollar at its current level or, if anything, a stronger dollar." Center of gravity: dollar strong and perhaps firming. Above neutral.

**Inflation sub-components (value only).** Goods 58: Jordan reports asphalt, roofing, paper and packaging prices up sharply, freight "skyrocketing", but Guynn: competition limits pass-through and Hoenig: increases not sticking. Services 58: health care costs in double digits (Jordan, Minehan, Poole), temp-sector wage gains, tempered by Boehne/Hoenig on moderate wages. Housing 55: Minehan cites "housing" and shelter cost increases in New England, Austin supply strain (McTeer); limited and regional. Expectations 56: Ferguson "inflation expectations have made a bit of a worrisome move in the wrong direction"; Gramlich notes a Michigan rise but a declining breakeven spread; Hoenig "if we do see higher inflation expectations begin to emerge."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 ±5, High.** Evidence: Greenspan: "moving another 25 basis points today ... continue on a tightening path"; Broaddus: "asymmetry with a vengeance"; Minehan: "I think it is going to take more than this." Center of gravity: unanimous tightening with a hawkish tilt and several voices preferring faster action, balanced by gradualists (McTeer, Hoenig, Poole). Hawkish because the entire Committee supports a hike plus the tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±6, High.** Evidence: Vote read-out, ten yes votes; Gramlich: "private mental dalliances with 50 basis points, but I'll get over it"; Broaddus asks to delete a statement sentence and Greenspan overrules. Center of gravity: unanimous, with differences about pace and wording only. Broad agreement, low-to-moderate band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Poole on the unprecedented NASDAQ/S&P divergence and Japanese financial fragility; McDonough on stock prices and monetary policy. Center of gravity: stock market and financial conditions are a recurring but secondary theme for about six to eight participants. Moderate discussion, low end of the 40-60 band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 74 ±6, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: balance of risks "remains tilted toward inflation" and a "tightening path"; Poole: "25 basis point increases until the data convince us that a further increase is no longer necessary"; Hoenig: "it is not a sure thing." Center of gravity: explicit upward bias with an expectation of further increases, kept data-dependent. Mild-to-clear tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "something very unusual is going on in the economy"; McDonough: "a lot about this economy that is very difficult to understand." Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty over productivity, NAIRU and the stock market, while near-term policy was agreed with confidence. Elevated but not extreme.
