# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1), meeting 2002-06-26, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Document type: full two-day transcript with a go-around, a policy discussion and a roll call. It is not special-topic. The morning session on inflation and productivity research is ignored for condition scoring, except where members state views on conditions.
- Policy lean: the Committee holds the funds rate at 1 3/4 percent with a balanced-risk statement. Many members (Moskow, Minehan, Santomero, Hoenig, Jordan, Barron) say a move toward neutral is coming and the markets should be prepared. McTeer, Poole, Bies, McDonough, Olson and Ferguson want steady policy and no signalling. Greenspan: "a lot" needed to cut, not "a huge amount" to raise. Net lean is about 52.
- Dissent: the vote read-out shows 10 Yes votes and no No votes (Greenspan, McDonough, Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Jordan, McTeer, Olson, Santomero, Stern). The read-out lists only those ten names (the Board and Reserve Bank members who are currently voting), but the spoken support in the go-around covers every other participant. Reservations: Hoenig on the balanced-risk wording, and a sharp exchange over "preparing the market" (McDonough against Hoenig, Santomero and Moskow). DISSENT_INTENSITY is about 26.
- Conditions: moderate, uneven recovery with growth slightly below or near potential. Unemployment is just under 6 percent and labor markets are soft. Consumption and housing are solid and business investment is weak. Productivity is very strong. Inflation is low and well contained (core PCE about 1.5 percent), with a few price increases (steel, packaging, health premiums).
- Financial: equity weakness, accounting scandals, a weaker dollar and widening risk spreads, offset by low rates, falling long rates and a healthy banking sector with pockets of stress (Ohio bank losses, CAMELS downgrades). Credit spreads rest on direction-only wording, so Low confidence. Treasury stress is N/A.
- Tier 3: financial-stability attention is moderate to prominent (scandals, Brazil and Argentina, dollar). Forward guidance is slightly hawkish. Uncertainty is high ("uncertainties more important than risks", "uncomfortably balanced", "schizophrenic", "don't have a clue").
- Special-topic call: No. There is a policy vote and a policy go-around.
- Text caveat: staff answers are absent, and some member questions in the morning session are fragmentary. No passage was garbled enough to alter a score.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 35 +-6 (High).** Participants see inflation as below the midpoint because they describe it as low and contained. Evidence: Parry, "core PCE price index averaging about 1½ percent both this year and next"; Bies, "Inflation remains at extremely low levels, and CEOs continue to lament their limited ability to increase prices." A few signs of firming (Moskow on steel and packaging; Santomero's prices-paid indexes positive since March; the lower dollar) keep it from falling further. Direction: below neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP 42 +-6 (Moderate).** Direction: slightly below potential, with a recovery that is closing the gap. Evidence: Barron, "growth will be slightly below potential, at least through much of the rest of this year"; Santomero, "continued slack in the economy"; Moskow, growth "somewhat above potential in the second half". Bright line satisfied (slack language gives under 50).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 36 +-6 (High).** Direction: below full employment, soft labor markets. Evidence: McTeer, "Texas unemployment rate has inched up to just over 6 percent... may waffle between 5½ and 6 percent"; Ferguson, "initial claims... remain historically high". Directly consistent with the unemployment anchor.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44 +-6 (High).** Direction: slightly below trend on a net basis. Consumption and housing hold up, business investment is weak. Evidence: Gramlich, "Consumption and housing remain strong"; Bies, "Business fixed investment still does not show signs of sustained recovery."

**PRODUCTIVITY 76 +-6 (High).** Direction: well above trend. Evidence: McTeer, "the stunning productivity gains of recent quarters"; Bies, "continue to drive productivity to strong levels"; Greenspan on the 7 percent annual rates.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Direction: roughly neutral, with offsetting forces. Evidence: Minehan, "Credit markets have been buoyed by a healthy banking sector and a vibrant bond market, but risk spreads have widened"; Parry, accounting concerns "may be raising borrowing costs for some firms"; Poole, "significant decline in longer-term interest rates". Equity weakness tightens, low rates and a healthy banking sector loosen. Bright line: no member calls conditions restrictive or accommodative as such, so the score stays just under 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Members mention long-rate declines but no Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 53 +-10 (Low).** Direction only. Evidence: Minehan, "risk spreads have widened"; Gramlich (quoting a bank newsletter), "the spread on emerging-market debt is widening". No level is stated. V4.1.1 rule: 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Minehan's "healthy banking sector and vibrant bond market" keeps it from the top of the band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 56 +-8 (Moderate).** Direction: modestly depressed and under stress, with a valuation view of roughly fair after correction. Evidence: Gramlich, "The stock market may not be too far out of equilibrium now. Forward earnings-price ratios have returned to a decent margin over real interest rates"; McDonough, markets "pounded by... an expanding scandal".

**BANKING_STRESS 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Direction: healthy overall, with pockets of stress. Evidence: Minehan, "healthy banking sector" but "increase in CAMELS downgrades... doesn't bode well"; Jordan, "one bank whose survival is very much in doubt and two others whose capital has been severely impaired"; Stern, "consumer credit quality is in good shape". Bright line satisfied (below 50).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 +-6 (High).** Direction: depreciation. Evidence: Bies, "sustained lower value of the dollar"; Gramlich, "The dollar... is miles away from its equilibrium value". Bright line satisfied (below 50).

**INFLATION_GOODS 40.** Value only. McDonough, "finished goods inflation has remained stable and low"; Moskow, "very little pricing power" with steel up.
**INFLATION_SERVICES 55.** Value only. Poole, health insurance premiums rising "about 20 percent a year" and university tuition increases.
**INFLATION_HOUSING 56.** Value only. McDonough, "aside from shelter costs, finished goods inflation has remained stable and low" (shelter costs singled out as firmer).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47.** Value only. Poole, "entrenched low inflation going forward"; Broaddus, "we don't want it to turn back up".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 52 +-6 (Moderate).** Unanimous hold, balanced risks, but a sizable share want to start preparing for tightening, set against a firm no-signalling camp. Evidence: Hoenig, "we are overly accommodative, and we should begin to consider a longer-term strategy to move ... toward a more neutral position"; McTeer, "Tightening at this juncture would... be premature." Direction: just above balance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 26 +-8 (Moderate).** No dissent. Differences concern signalling and timing. Evidence: McDonough, "we would be ill advised now to be preparing the market"; Hoenig, "I do want to make one comment... we're not quite sure the risks are as balanced as the statement is going to say."

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Direction: moderate to prominent attention. Evidence: McDonough, "The probability of an international financial crisis would be difficult to exaggerate"; Gramlich's five concerns (accounting irregularities, equity and dollar overshoot).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 55 +-7 (Moderate).** The signal is hold, with an eventual move up, and no commitment. Evidence: Greenspan, "It won't take a huge amount to make us move in the other direction"; "we will have to move"; the balanced risk statement is retained.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 68 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Jordan, "the uncertainties are more important than the various risks that we can identify"; Ferguson, "uncomfortably balanced... These are indeed uncertain times"; Greenspan, "We don't know how to evaluate them."

Coherence: OUTPUT_GAP (42), LABOR (36) and CONSUMPTION (44) are within 15 points of each other. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (48) and CREDIT_SPREADS (53) are close, with the spread score reflecting only direction. Inflation sub-components (about 40-56) average near the parent of 35, with the parent held lower by the explicit "contained" language.
