# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion, transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion), meeting 2005-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Participant-only transcript of a two-day FOMC meeting. It opens with the previous afternoon's special-topic session on housing valuations and monetary policy. Only the members' questions and comments survive from that session, and the staff answers are gone, so many turns are one-sided fragments. It then has the morning Q&A after the chart show, the economic go-around, the Chairman's summary and policy proposal, the policy go-around, and a vote read-out (11 Yes in the listed roll call, no No). The ordinary scoring surface is the go-around plus the policy round.

**Era context.** The text shows a committee that has been raising the funds rate in 25bp steps for a year ("measured pace"). Growth is around 3.5 percent. Core PCE is about 2 percent. Oil is near $60. Long-term yields are low (the "conundrum"). Housing is hot. This is background only. I score from what members say.

**Speakers.**
- Greenspan: GDP growth of 3 percent plus is comfortable. Unit labor cost pressure and margin squeeze are a concern. Risks to output and inflation are "clearly balanced," but the loss function favors not stopping too soon. He proposes +25bp with minimal language change.
- Moskow: growth near trend. Slack has narrowed, and we "may already have closed most meaningful resource gaps." He wants policy removal to continue and possibly more than the futures market prices.
- Santomero: economy near potential and close to full employment. More tightening is needed. He projects rising core inflation.
- Guynn: housing bubble or near bubble in parts of Florida. Inflation risks are on the upside. Continue the recent policy path.
- Hoenig: growth is slightly over potential. Policy must keep moving out of its accommodative stance toward a neutral zone of 3.5 to 4.5 percent.
- Minehan: the better risk is to err on the tighter side. Spreads are narrow and there is reaching for risk.
- Fisher: no pricing power anywhere, goods deflation from imports, and a shift in concern toward slower growth.
- Pianalto: discouraged by inflation drift, has "second thoughts about pausing too soon."
- Stern: growth is solid and inflation is getting locked in at a somewhat higher rate.
- Yellen: the Greenbook path is appropriate, but be careful not to overdo tightening, and pausing is near.
- Poole: labor supply of hourly workers is plentiful.
- Lacker: 2 percent is the upper limit of his comfort zone. He worries about an inflation scare.
- Geithner: the real funds rate should go up. Better to end at the high end of the terminal-rate range than to stop early.
- Ferguson: "full steam ahead," with market signals and financial stability discussed.
- Kohn: the output gap is about disappearing. He is careful about the pace and open to deviating in August.
- Bies and Olson: mortgage product risk, layering, and securitization discipline.
- Gramlich: raise rates, go somewhat north of neutral, don't prick bubbles.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Members see inflation as modestly above their comfort level. Core PCE is about 2 percent, the Greenbook revision upward is "striking" (Lacker), and "2 percent is the upper limit of my own comfort zone." Several prefer 1.5 percent. Offsetting this, Fisher sees no pricing power and Yellen notes core PCE at 1.7 percent over 12 months. The score is slightly above 50, around 56.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Mixed but net slightly above. Hoenig says growth is "slightly over our potential rate." Kohn says growth is "perhaps a bit above." Lacker says "slightly faster than potential." Yellen and Kohn also note slack remains in some measures. The bright line is satisfied at 52 given the above-potential language dominates, while the "slack remains" comments pull it toward 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Near full employment, with mixed views. Santomero says "close to full employment" and reports crisis-level shortages in southern New Jersey. Poole says hourly labor supply is plentiful. Yellen says unemployment is near NAIRU but other slack measures remain. No national unemployment rate is stated, so the score sits near 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Solid to a bit above trend. Hoenig says consumer spending is solid. Stern lists broad positives. Yellen says growth is "respectable and not stellar" and rests on a few rate-sensitive sectors. The net is about 56.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by several members. Yellen cites "still rather robust structural productivity growth." Geithner expects strong productivity. Greenspan says momentum has been lost in recent months. Hoenig worries about productivity coming down. Net is slightly above trend, with mixed views, so about 52 with a wide band.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose. Members describe accommodative policy, low long yields, narrow spreads, abundant liquidity, and "reaching for risk" (Minehan). Yellen quotes a director on lending for deals that are "stupid." Geithner mentions "a significant tightening of financial conditions" as a headwind, a countervailing note. The score is below 50 at about 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields are discussed at length (the long-rate conundrum, term premia, a fear of a bond-market inflation scare). That is yield behavior, not dysfunction, and no one describes a liquidity problem. Scored in the normal-functioning range with low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is stated as low. Bies: "skinny spreads," "back to skinny spreads now." Guynn: "narrowing of spreads." Minehan: "relatively narrow credit spreads." Kohn: "still fairly narrow risk premiums in credit markets." The score is well below 50 at about 20.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only Ferguson discusses equity pricing in terms of valuation (low implied volatility, a relatively wide equity risk premium driven by the fixed-income side). Kohn mentions rising equity prices. No valuation concern is voiced, so the score is near neutral with low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are described as sound. Moskow says institutions are in good shape and capital is high. Bies says "record profits and capital in banks, I think there's a huge cushion." Concerns exist for local developers and construction lending (Guynn), and about layered risk in MBS (Olson), but these are not system stress. The score is below 50, about 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen cites "an appreciation in the dollar." Kohn cites "the strength of the dollar." Santomero anticipates some depreciation ahead, and Gramlich and Geithner worry about the current account. Current direction is modest strength. The score is slightly above 50, about 57, with moderate confidence.
- Inflation sub-components. INFLATION_GOODS: Fisher reports ongoing goods deflation from imports, while Moskow and Santomero report energy and input cost pass-through, so the net is slightly below stable at about 45. INFLATION_SERVICES: not discussed, so N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices were discussed at length, but shelter and rent inflation is only raised hypothetically (Kohn), so N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: contained and falling (Lacker, Kohn, Yellen, Moskow), with the Geithner caveat that they sit above 1.5 percent. About 47.

**Tier 3, in words.**
- Policy lean: A 25bp hike is proposed and supported by all. Some want a steeper path (Moskow, Hoenig, Santomero, Geithner, Pianalto). Yellen and Kohn stress that a pause is near. The Chairman's loss-function argument is for continuing. The majority favors tightening with a gradual pace, so moderate hawkish.
- Disagreement: Zero formal dissents, 11 Yes in the roll call. Minor reservations on wording (Fisher and Yellen on "elevated") and on the forward path. Broad agreement.
- Financial stability attention: The first-day housing session is in the document, and today Bies, Olson, Guynn, Minehan, Yellen, Ferguson and Lacker spend real time on mortgage products, bubbles, spreads and market signals. Prominent but not dominant.
- Forward guidance: Statement language held largely unchanged (alternative B, "measured pace"), with a signal that a pause is approaching (Greenspan, Kohn "should not be read as implying anything beyond August"). Mild tightening bias with data dependence.
- Uncertainty: Standard caveats and a few explicit statements of not knowing (the neutral rate, the terminal rate, bubble status). The Chairman says risks are balanced. Normal to somewhat elevated.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: above-potential language dominates and the score is above 50 (52). OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" and "easy" language, so the score is below 50. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "skinny," "narrow," so the score is below 50. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: "sound," "huge cushion," so the score is below 50. OK.
- DOLLAR: appreciation, so the score is above 50. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction, so the score is not above 55. OK.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51): consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (56): within 15 points.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) and CREDIT_SPREADS (20): both loose and co-moving. Consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, Treasury stress is in the normal range. Consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (56) versus the sub-components: goods (45) and expectations (47) are below the parent. The parent is anchored to participants' repeated "upper end of comfort zone" core PCE language and the upward forecast drift. Explained.

**N/A justification.** Services and housing inflation: members did not address services or shelter price inflation as such. Every other financial dimension had at least some participant discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Lacker: "2 percent is the upper limit of my own comfort zone for core PCE inflation." Stern: "inflation, if not tilted to the upside, is at least getting locked in at a somewhat higher rate." Fisher: "no evidence of the price pressures and pricing power."
- Center of gravity: Core PCE about 2 percent with an upward-revised path and repeated unease, offset by a few members seeing contained pricing.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is slightly above neutral because members place it at or just above the upper end of comfort zones and describe the drift as upward.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-5 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Hoenig: "GDP growth running about 3½ percent, slightly over our potential rate." Kohn: "with the output gap about disappearing." Yellen: "several other measures ... do suggest that slack remains."
- Center of gravity: Growth slightly above potential and slack nearly closed, with a minority noting residual slack.
- Direction confirmation: The output gap is slightly above neutral because the above-potential language outweighs the residual slack remarks, and the bright line requires above 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 +-5 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Santomero: "growing near its potential and is close to full employment." Poole: "labor supply of hourly workers is plentiful," citing 10,000 applicants for 400 Wal-Mart jobs.
- Center of gravity: Near full employment, with tightness in skilled labor and plentiful low-skill labor.
- Direction confirmation: The labor market is at neutral because members describe full employment with balanced evidence.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +-6 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Hoenig: "Consumer spending continues to grow solidly." Stern: "continuing increases in consumer spending; and a positive situation with regard to capital spending." Yellen: "respectable and not stellar growth."
- Center of gravity: Spending and investment solid to modestly above trend.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is above neutral because the broad reports are positive, tempered by Yellen's note that growth rests on a few rate-sensitive sectors.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 52 +-8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Yellen: "still rather robust structural productivity growth." Greenspan: "there doesn't appear to have been much in the way of gains in output per hour from December through April."
- Center of gravity: Trend-like to slightly strong structural growth, with near-term momentum doubts.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is slightly above neutral because the structural view is strong, offset by recent softness.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-7 [High]
- Evidence: Minehan: low interest rates "contributed to what most market practitioners view as a sense of reaching for risk." Yellen: "liquidity is abundant and ... lending is taking place for deals that ... [are] 'stupid.'"
- Center of gravity: Accommodative policy, low long yields, narrow spreads and plentiful liquidity.
- Direction confirmation: Conditions are below neutral (loose) because members describe ease and abundance; Geithner's reference to a past tightening is a minor counterweight.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +-8 [Low]
- Evidence: Minehan and Ferguson discuss the 10-year yield "conundrum"; Lacker raises a possible "inflation scare in the bond market." No one reports liquidity or functioning problems.
- Center of gravity: Yield behavior is puzzling but markets are orderly.
- Direction confirmation: Treasury stress is below neutral because there is no dysfunction, only a debate over low yields.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 20 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Bies: "We're back to skinny spreads now." Kohn: "still fairly narrow risk premiums in credit markets." Guynn: C&I lending is highly competitive "with a resulting narrowing of spreads."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are stated to be narrow by several members, with concern that risk is underpriced.
- Direction confirmation: Spreads are below neutral because the stated level is narrow or skinny.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Low]
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the implied share price volatilities of both Nasdaq and the S&P 500 are really quite low, and yet the risk equity premium is relatively wide." Kohn: "the rise in equity prices."
- Center of gravity: No valuation concern is voiced; the signals are described as contradictory but benign.
- Direction confirmation: Equity concern is at neutral because members offer no valuation alarm either way.

### BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Bies: "Especially with the record profits and capital in banks, I think there's a huge cushion." Moskow: "Capital levels of the financial institutions are relatively high."
- Center of gravity: Banks are sound, with localized lending-risk concerns (Florida construction, layered mortgage products).
- Direction confirmation: Banking stress is below neutral because members describe a healthy, well-capitalized system with pockets to watch.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Yellen: "an appreciation in the dollar." Kohn: "owing to the strength of the dollar." Santomero: "We also anticipate some depreciation of the dollar."
- Center of gravity: Recent modest strengthening, with an expected later reversal.
- Direction confirmation: The dollar is above neutral because current-move references are to appreciation and strength.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS 45. Fisher: Wal-Mart "deflating prices in electronics, clothing, and almost all retail sectors," offset by energy and input costs (Moskow, Santomero).
- INFLATION_SERVICES N/A. Not discussed.
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A. House-price levels discussed, shelter inflation only hypothetically (Kohn).
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47. Lacker: "inflation expectations have fallen somewhat." Geithner: still "some margin above 1.5 percent."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 65 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I would propose that we make another 25 basis point move on the funds rate today." Moskow: "we may need to increase rates more than currently expected by the futures markets." Yellen: "I think we are nearing the point when we will need to start pausing."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous 25bp hike, several advocating a firmer path, a minority emphasizing a coming pause.
- Direction confirmation: The lean is hawkish because the entire Committee supports tightening and the loss-function argument favors continuing.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 +-5 [High]
- Evidence: All speakers support the proposal; Fisher: "I don't think this is the time to argue." Yellen on "elevated": "it may well perturb expectations upward. But I support the recommendation."
- Center of gravity: No dissents; mild wording and path-length differences.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is low because the vote is unanimous and the reservations are about language.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Bies: "when I see a product growing 30 percent a year for three years, I tend to get a little nervous about the concentration risk." Olson: "It's the layering that really causes the risk." Guynn: "an accident waiting to happen in our area."
- Center of gravity: Housing, mortgage-product risk, spreads and market signals take a large share of attention, including a full prior-day session, but not the whole meeting.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is above the moderate band because many members devote substantial remarks to it.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "change the language as little as possible, recognizing that there is going to come a time ... when we ought to start to flatten out the funds rate path." Kohn: "our statement today should not be read as implying anything about our expectations beyond August."
- Center of gravity: Continued "measured pace" language with an acknowledged approaching pause and data dependence.
- Direction confirmation: The signal is mildly hawkish because further increases remain the base case while the stopping point is flagged.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +-8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Kohn: "the underlying truth is that we really don't know how much we need to tighten." Pianalto: "I'm not feeling any more confident or certain about the outlook."
- Center of gravity: Ordinary caveats plus several admissions of not knowing the terminal rate and bubble status.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated relative to normal but not unusual, since the Chairman calls risks balanced.
