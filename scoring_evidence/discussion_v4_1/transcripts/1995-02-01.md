# FOMC member-discussion scoring v4.1 — transcript discussion, meeting 1995-02-01

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript for the 31 Jan - 1 Feb 1995 meeting; staff turns are removed, so many staff-Q&A passages appear only as the members' questions. The file contains a lot of non-economic material: officer elections, disclosure policy (announcements, tapes, concurring statements, blackout), an inflation-targeting debate (Broaddus pro, Yellen con, then a go-around), a Mexico financing discussion (two sessions), a conventional economic go-around, the policy go-around, and a vote read-out. Participants' views on conditions and policy come mainly from the economic go-around and the policy round on 1 Feb.

**Era context.** Early 1995, after a year of tightening (funds rate 5.5% going in). Strong 1994 growth (about 4%), unemployment 5.4%, and a Mexican peso crisis. Members' own language is used below, not hindsight.

**Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: data "unbelievable set of one-sided data" with only "tentative signs" of "slight cracks". He sees strong capital goods, low inventories and easy credit terms. He recommends +50bp on the funds and discount rates and a symmetric directive, citing dollar vulnerability ("just barely investment grade").
- Hawks and near-hawks: Parry (urgency, wants asymmetric language), Moskow (growth above potential, 3.5% CPI by H2), Melzer, Hoenig ("well above potential"), Stern, Boehne, Broaddus, Forrestal ("insurance policy"), Jordan, McDonough.
- Wait-and-see: Blinder (prefers waiting two weeks, risk 1 in 3 of "setting the thermostat too low") and Yellen (prefers waiting, uncertainty "increased enormously"). Both vote yes. Lindsey would wait on domestic grounds but supports 50bp as insurance against the dollar.
- Cautious on the slowdown: Lindsey (consumer overextended, wealth destruction), Minehan and Forrestal (how to know when to stop).

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. Raw-material price increases are "pervasive", firms report price increases becoming more feasible, and utilization is "inconsistent with steady inflation" (Parry). Realized consumer inflation has not accelerated, and there was a "good inflation surprise" in Q4 (Blinder, Yellen). So pressure is rising but not yet realized: modestly-to-clearly above, about 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Moskow: "grow faster than its potential". Hoenig: growth "well above potential". Phillips: "at or above capacity". Greenspan and Blinder agree "we have overshot". Bright line requires > 50. Score about 61.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Members call labor markets tight. Melzer: "tightest they have seen in years". Moskow: Detroit 3.5%, clerical wages up more than 10%, shortages. Unemployment is 5.4%, which is below the staff NAIRU of 6%. Wage acceleration is not widespread (Stern, Jordan, Yellen citing the ECI). There is a tension with the objective anchor: 5.4% sits in the table's 5.5-7% band (31-45), while participant language says below the natural rate and shortages. I score the participants' assessment but pull it down: 58.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend but moderating. Capital goods and profits are very strong, consumer confidence is high, and holiday sales were up. Offsetting signs are weak Nov-Dec retail sales, auto rebates, housing slowing, and Lindsey's overextended-consumer worry. Net is clearly above trend, about 62.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched only in passing (Boehne: "potential for further productivity gains"; Jordan and Phillips: investment in productivity). There is no assessment of productivity growth relative to trend, so the score is near neutral with Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Credit conditions are easy: Greenspan notes "easing in credit terms" and says "bankers have not been told about our monetary policy tightening", C&I and consumer loans are "extraordinary", and the cost of capital is "quite low". Boehne and Forrestal report slipping underwriting standards. Against that, Kelley and Yellen say real policy rates are not low, and Phillips says financial markets are "not as supportive of direct financing". Conditions, as distinct from the policy rate, are somewhat accommodative: below 50, about 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Only bond yields and rallies are mentioned (long bond about 7-7/8, "bond market was up 25/32"), with no functioning commentary.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level below normal. Greenspan: "quality spreads ... forward risk premiums ... quite modest". Boehne: "loan pricing does not adequately compensate for risk". Below 50 required, score 36.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only Greenspan discusses it: "stock market is not low ... We have taken a lot of the bubble out"; Lindsey raises wealth-destruction risk generally. That points to roughly fair to slightly rich, with reduced concern. Score 46, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Phillips: "banking system is quite strong". Lindsey: banks "not capital-constrained", FDIC premium cut. Minehan: First District banks cautious after portfolio losses. Boehne: slipping underwriting. The system is healthy with minor concerns, so below 50: score 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar is described as weak and vulnerable. Kelley: "We have a weak dollar". Greenspan: "exchange market for the dollar ... not all that impressive", "the dollar would undoubtedly fall". McDonough: dollar "extremely vulnerable". Bright line requires < 50. Score 32.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods are scored from the widespread raw-material and input price reports, with pass-through "becoming more feasible" but retail discounting in apparel. Services and housing are not discussed as price categories (housing is mentioned only as activity and as falling New York prices), so they are N/A. Expectations are scored from Melzer ("longer-term inflation expectations substantially above current inflation") and Jordan (uncertainty about objectives).

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: +50bp funds and discount, the largest move in the cycle. All vote yes. The broad rationale is insurance against inflation and the dollar. Two members (Blinder, Yellen) would rather wait. Forward stance is symmetric and several say this may be the last move. The action is strongly hawkish but the forward lean is balanced, so I score moderately hawkish, about 66.
- Dissent: There is no dissent on the monetary decision (the discount-rate vote was "7 to zip"). Reservations: Blinder and Yellen openly prefer waiting; Lindsey says domestically he would wait; Parry prefers asymmetric. The Committee called itself "split down the middle" on inflation targeting. On the Mexico swap and warehousing items, two formal No votes were cast (Lindsey, Melzer); Jordan said he would oppose; Broaddus and Hoenig were uneasy. Disclosure policy produced mild disagreement (Lindsey on tapes, LaWare on announcing a policy). Mexico votes are not monetary policy, but they are formal disagreement within the discussion. Score about 46.
- Financial stability emphasis: Mexico and the international financial system take a large share of the transcript (Truman: "serious threat to the international financial system"), plus brief mentions of Orange County, Bankers Trust, consumer debt, wealth destruction. Melzer notes no US systemic crisis case. Much of the Mexico talk is about central bank exposure and politics rather than stability analysis. Moderate: 38.
- Forward guidance: Symmetric directive. Greenspan: "by no means evident" there will be another tightening. Parry wants asymmetric. Yellen sees no further 150bp. Minehan hopes to "back off" in time. Release language: "views these actions as necessary to keep inflation contained". Neutral to slightly dovish tilt: 47.
- Uncertainty: Yellen says uncertainty is "extremely high" and "increased enormously". Blinder gives wide error bands. Lindsey, Phillips (testing the NAIRU and capacity confidence distributions), and Greenspan ("murky") add to this. Others are confident (Parry, Moskow). Elevated but not extreme: 52.

**Polarity checks.** Output gap above 50 matches "above potential". Financial conditions below 50 matches easy credit terms. Credit spreads below 50 matches "modest risk premiums". Banking stress below 50 matches "quite strong". Dollar below 50 matches weak and vulnerable. Equity score near 46 reflects Greenspan's "not low, bubble partly out", so no strong overvaluation concern. All pass.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 61 (> 50), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 (< 50, accommodative credit), CREDIT_SPREADS 36 (< 50, level reading), BANKING_STRESS 30 (< 50), DOLLAR_STRENGTH 32 (< 50, weakening), TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 61, LABOR 58, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62: within 15, consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 and CREDIT_SPREADS 36 both below 50: co-move. Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 consistent with goods 58 and expectations 60.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS (no functioning commentary), INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING (no category-specific price discussion).

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation"; Moskow, "pickup in inflation is considered likely ... CPI inflation to rise ... 3-1/2 percent". Center of gravity: inflation not yet accelerating but pressures are building and broadly expected to rise. Direction: above neutral because most members expect a cyclical rise, tempered by the benign Q4 inflation report.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 61 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "economy continues to grow faster than its potential"; Hoenig, "leaves overall growth well above potential". Center of gravity: broad agreement the economy is above capacity. Direction: above neutral because of explicit above-potential language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 ±9 (Moderate).** Evidence: Melzer, "labor markets are the tightest they have seen in years"; Moskow, "labor markets remain quite tight ... 3-1/2 percent in the metropolitan Detroit area". Center of gravity: tight in members' eyes though aggregate unemployment (5.4%) is below the 6% NAIRU only modestly and wage pressure is not widespread. Direction: above neutral because members cite shortages and below-NAIRU unemployment. The overall level anchor (5.4%) argues for somewhat lower, hence the wide band.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "capital goods markets, the data are uniformly very strong"; Kelley, "booming consumer confidence ... Business investment will continue to be strong". Center of gravity: strong spending with early signs of moderation. Direction: above trend, tempered by weaker Nov-Dec retail, autos and housing.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 54 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Boehne, "much potential for further productivity gains"; Jordan, "plans to spend a lot more ... improving productivity". Center of gravity: passing reference to productivity investment with no trend assessment. Direction: slightly above neutral on the mild positive references.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we are still getting some easing in credit terms"; Greenspan, "the cost of capital continues to be quite low". Center of gravity: credit availability and terms remain easy despite the funds rate increases. Direction: below neutral because credit is described as easy and loan growth is strong (Yellen and Kelley's "real rates not low" refer to policy, not conditions).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "quality spreads within the financial markets ... forward risk premiums ... are quite modest"; Boehne, "loan pricing does not adequately compensate for risk". Center of gravity: spreads thin, with underwriting loosening. Direction: below neutral by the level reading.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan, "while the stock market is not low, it clearly is not anywhere close to being as elevated as it was a year or so ago ... We have taken a lot of the bubble out". Center of gravity: valuation concern has receded to roughly fair. Direction: slightly below neutral (slight remaining richness), a single speaker.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "The banking system is quite strong and can certainly support expansion"; Lindsey, "banks are not capital-constrained". Center of gravity: healthy banks, minor concerns about underwriting and portfolio losses. Direction: below neutral because members call the system strong.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 32 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley, "We have a weak dollar"; McDonough, "the dollar is extremely vulnerable and likely to fall". Center of gravity: dollar weak and at risk of falling further. Direction: below neutral because the dollar is described as weak.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58.** Evidence: Moskow, "raw materials price increases are pervasive"; Forrestal, "Reports of rising prices of raw materials remain very widespread". Offset: Minehan, "selling prices have not yet ticked up generally".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 60.** Evidence: Melzer, "longer-term inflation expectations substantially above current inflation"; Jordan, "uncertainty about our policy objectives".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 66 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "raising both the funds rate and the discount rate by 50 basis points makes the most sense"; Parry, "there is urgency in the need to tighten policy". Center of gravity: unanimous large hike for insurance and dollar reasons, with a symmetric forward stance and two preferring to wait. Direction: hawkish because a 50bp increase is the action; the score is held below the very-strong range by the symmetric directive and Blinder and Yellen's reluctance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 46 ±9 (Moderate).** Evidence: Blinder, "this would be a great time to wait for a few weeks ... I am willing to go along"; Greenspan, "we are as split down the middle as we could possibly get" (inflation targeting). Also Lindsey and Melzer cast No votes on the Mexico swap and warehousing votes, and Jordan said he "would oppose the package". Center of gravity: unanimous on the rate action but with open reservations and a deeper split on framework and Mexico. Direction: moderate disagreement; the formal No votes are on non-monetary items.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 ±10 (Moderate).** Evidence: Truman, "serious threat to the international financial system"; Lindsey, "the consumer is simply overextended". Center of gravity: Mexico takes sizeable time but is framed mainly around central bank exposure and politics; domestic financial-stability remarks are scattered. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 47 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we will be truly symmetric if we raise the federal funds rate to 6 percent. It is by no means evident ... there will be another tightening"; Parry, "I would prefer asymmetric language, but I certainly could live with symmetry". Center of gravity: balanced forward stance, no commitment to more tightening. Direction: roughly neutral, a slight lean to the end of the cycle.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "my level of uncertainty has increased enormously, and ... the potential forecast error at this point is extremely high"; Blinder, "plenty of error I want to emphasize". Center of gravity: several prominent voices emphasize two scenarios and NAIRU doubt, while others are confident. Direction: elevated but not pervasive.
