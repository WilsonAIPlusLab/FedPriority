# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting date 2008-01-21 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript of a short, unscheduled (intermeeting, holiday) meeting. Contents: Chair's case for a 75 bp cut, a round of comments by about 17 participants, Hoenig/Poole follow-ups, the Chair's reply, the vote read-out. About 54k characters, so not a "very short" document. Staff (Dudley) remarks are removed; I ignore the Chair's reports of Greenbook numbers as staff content, except where he uses them as his own argument.

**2. Era context.** Early 2008, housing contraction, monoline insurer downgrades, global equity selloff. I scored only what the text says.

**3. Speakers.** Bernanke (proposes 75 bp, "behind the curve," recession and credit-crisis risk). Strong supporters: Evans, Stern, Yellen, Rosengren, Geithner, Lockhart, Pianalto, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner. Reluctant or conditional: Hoenig (troubled, inflation above 4%, will vote yes), Lacker (reservations about timing, supports), Plosser (torn, "gut says no," nonvoting), Fisher (not convinced of the economic case, nonvoting). Dissent: Poole (votes No).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as somewhat above the midpoint. Stern says core "a little higher than I thought"; Kroszner cites unwelcome data in headline and core; Hoenig and Fisher say total inflation is above 4% y/y. The Chair and Kohn expect moderation from a soft economy and lower oil, and the statement says inflation is expected to moderate. "Expected to moderate" is a forecast, so I score the current reading: modestly to clearly above target, about 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. "Deep and protracted recession" belief in markets, "edge of a more serious downturn," Kohn: "declining resource utilization, a soft economy," Kroszner: growth "certainly not close to potential." This is a forward-looking weakening, and current slack is not yet deep. About 34.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Little direct discussion; the statement notes "some softening in labor markets." The text gives no unemployment level. Mildly below the midpoint, 43, Low confidence.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below midpoint. "Deepening of the housing contraction," Kohn: "consumption data were a little weaker than we expected," consumer debt problems, Fisher sees slowing (credit card delinquencies). About 33.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed, N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Above midpoint (tight). "Credit has tightened further for some businesses and households," Chair: "withdrawal from normal credit extension," Pianalto: policy "inadvertently restrictive." Offsets: short-term funding strains eased (Poole on LIBOR, Warsh on TAF). About 72.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market not discussed by participants, N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Evidence is thin on level. Kroszner: "credit default swap spreads on a number of the financial institutions have been going up" (direction); credit tightening and withdrawal from risk (availability falling). No level stated. The dimension also covers availability, and availability is described as deteriorating. Strictly the rule for direction-only text caps at 55; availability language lifts it modestly, so 60 with Low confidence and a wide band.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Global stocks falling sharply (S&P futures down ~5%, 16.5% cumulative, German market down 8%). Concern is explicit and strong (Warsh: "panic," "self-reinforcing" losses; Kohn: equity declines "destroy wealth"). There is no valuation commentary as such, but the concern about market stress is the construct. 66, Moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Above midpoint. Monoline downgrades force banks to mark down billions; Kroszner: "astonishingly large losses," "a lot of stress on bank balance sheets," but banks have been "nimble in bringing in a lot of capital"; Pianalto: problems spreading beyond mortgages. About 68.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Chair: "The dollar rose today"; Warsh: "interest in the safest currencies"; Kohn mentions dollar risk only conditional on inflation expectations. Slight appreciation, thin. 56, Low.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services, housing not discussed, N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: raised as something to watch (Chair; Kohn: a build would be "very serious and destructive"), with no claim that they are rising. Near anchored but watched: 53.

**5. Polarity verification.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (weak growth, slack): consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 (tighter): "tightened further," "restrictive": consistent. BANKING_STRESS above 50: stress language, not "healthy": consistent. DOLLAR above 50 for the day's rise: consistent with the bright line.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: 75 bp intermeeting cut proposed by the Chair and backed by a large majority, with explicit expectations of more cuts (Geithner: "move significantly further"; Yellen: "move somewhat further"; Hoenig: "another move"). Strongly dovish, not QE/emergency-hold-the-line type. 14. Hawkish-leaning participants (Hoenig, Fisher, Poole, Plosser) argue timing or size, not for tightening.
- Dissent: 1 formal dissent (Poole), 4 explicit reservations (Hoenig, Lacker, Plosser, Fisher), mostly about timing and precedent, partly about economics (Fisher: no widespread recession; inflation). Moderate to notable, 42.
- Financial stability emphasis: dominant topic. Monolines, credit contagion, funding markets, fragility, "financial stability" explicitly named as the focus (Kohn, Chair). 86.
- Forward guidance: statement "act in a timely manner as needed" and downside risks to growth; participants expect further cuts. Strong easing bias; the inflation-wording tweak softens slightly. 12.
- Uncertainty: "no one can know," "very hard to judge the probability," Kroszner "we are still uncertain," regime-switch language, tail-risk emphasis. High, 68.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 72 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 60: both on the stress side, co-move. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 vs expectations 53: consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "even core inflation has been running a little higher than I thought it would"; Hoenig, "total inflation is above ... 4 percent." Summary: modestly above target, with members split on whether it will moderate. Direction: above neutral because several members cite elevated headline and core readings.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Evans, "we are on the edge of a more serious downturn"; Kohn, "declining resource utilization, a soft economy." Summary: weakening growth and recession risk imply slack. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: statement text read by the Chair, "some softening in labor markets." Summary: only a passing mention of softening. Direction: below neutral, slightly.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Chair, "deepening of the housing contraction"; Kohn, "consumption data were a little weaker than we expected." Summary: housing and consumer spending weak, consumer credit deteriorating. Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 72 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Chair, "withdrawal from normal credit extension"; Pianalto, "not stay inadvertently restrictive." Summary: credit tightening and market selloff, partly offset by easing of short-term funding strains. Direction: tighter than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Kroszner, "credit default swap spreads on a number of the financial institutions have been going up"; Chair, "dynamic of withdrawal from risk." Summary: direction and availability point to stress, level not stated. Direction: above neutral, modestly.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 66 +-10 (Moderate).** Evidence: Warsh, "Panic appears to be begetting further pullbacks"; Chair, S&P 500 cumulative decline of 16½ percent. Summary: strong participant concern about sharp equity declines, no valuation judgment per se. Direction: stressed/depressed side.

**BANKING_STRESS: 68 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kroszner, "astonishingly large losses" and "a lot of stress on bank balance sheets"; Chair, monoline downgrade forces banks "to mark down billions more." Summary: significant and spreading losses, though capital raising continues. Direction: above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Chair, "The dollar rose today"; Warsh, "continued interest in the safest currencies." Summary: slight appreciation on the day. Direction: above neutral per the bright line.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 53.** Evidence: Kohn, "we cannot take our eyes off inflation, particularly inflation expectations." Summary: watched, no sign of de-anchoring asserted. Direction: roughly anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 14 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Chair, "I am proposing a cut of 75 basis points"; Geithner, "we are likely to have to move significantly further." Summary: large majority backs an aggressive intermeeting cut with more expected; dovish. Direction: strongly easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Poole, "I do not see a convincing argument for acting today"; formal vote 6-1 read-out (Poole No); Hoenig, "I am troubled by this," Plosser, "My gut instinct tells me 'no'." Summary: one dissent and four reservations, mostly on timing. Direction: moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 86 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "Our focus right now ... must be on financial stability"; Warsh, "The losses appear to be self-reinforcing." Summary: the meeting is driven by market fragility and credit contagion. Direction: very high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "the statement creates that expectation" of further moves; statement, "will act in a timely manner as needed." Summary: clear easing bias, with only a small inflation-wording hedge. Direction: easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Geithner, "It is very hard to judge what the probability of that risk is"; Kroszner, "we were uncertain before—we are still uncertain." Summary: many stress tail risks and unknowable market reaction. Direction: high.
