# FOMC member-discussion scoring v4.1: meeting 2020-03-02 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, with staff turns removed. There is no formal economic go-around. The text is the Chair's opening policy proposal, short comments from six participants (Bullard, Kashkari, Mester, Daly, Evans, Brainard), a messaging exchange, and the vote read-out (9 Yes among those called; the Chair says he votes the next day). About 2,800 words of content, which is above the 500-word "very short" threshold, but narrow in scope. Treat as an unscheduled/emergency-format meeting centred on a single proposal.

**Era context.** Early in the coronavirus outbreak, as described by the participants. The backdrop is stated only by participants: a strong outlook a month earlier, rapid global spread of the virus, and a proposed 50 bp cut. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Powell: the virus is a "material risk to the economic outlook," previously "a strong one"; wants to "counter a tightening of financial conditions"; proposes a 50 bp cut, "in a forceful manner"; wants to "maintain as much policy flexibility."
- Bullard: supports the cut as "insurance"; baseline of only modest first-half GDP effects but "meaningful downside risk"; worries the move will be read as a response to the equity selloff; inflation target missed "to the low side" for years; wants a plan for the sequence.
- Kashkari: "insurance" given "how much uncertainty"; fears a return to the lower bound; wants work on tools.
- Mester: supports "somewhat reluctantly"; would have preferred to wait for evidence of a demand shock.
- Daly: CEOs report uncertainty "already weighing down on their demand," not yet in hard data; supports the cut.
- Evans: risk management; asks about messaging.
- Brainard: "sharp declines in asset valuations and long-term yields reflect a notable increase in tail risk"; supports "decisive action."

**Core dimensions (direction first, then number).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Only Bullard speaks to it. Participants see inflation as below the midpoint because the Committee "has missed its stated inflation target to the low side ... for many years" and the shock "is likely to push inflation still lower." Single speaker, so 38, wide band, Low confidence.
- OUTPUT_GAP: No explicit gap language. Powell calls the outlook "a strong one as recently as a month ago," and Bullard's baseline is modest effects on first-half GDP. The economy is described as near trend with downside risk. Neutral to slightly below (the bright-line wording is not triggered). 48, wide band, Low confidence.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: No participant discusses the labor market. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Brainard says consumer confidence and activity "have been the key to our resilience over the past year." Daly reports demand softness not yet in the hard data. Bullard cites forecasts of Q1 growth at 2.1 percent and 2020 at 1.9 percent. Near trend with downside risk, 47, Low confidence.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as somewhat tighter than normal. Powell speaks of a "tightening of financial conditions"; Brainard cites "sharp declines in asset valuations and long-term yields" and the need to "ease financial stress among indebted businesses and cash flow constrained households"; Bullard cites last week's equity selloff. The Chair says the Friday statement and foreign central-bank statements gave "at least a temporary improvement in markets." Not described as "restrictive" or in crisis. 60, Moderate confidence. Polarity: higher means tighter; the evidence says tightening, which matches. The bright-line rule is not violated (no "accommodative" language).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only mentions of yield levels (Brainard: long-term yields declined; Kashkari: a hypothetical zero curve). Yield moves are not dysfunction, and no participant describes market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kashkari mentions "credit spreads are wide" only inside a hypothetical future scenario, not as a current condition. No current level or direction is given. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Bullard says equity pricing is "notoriously volatile," "some corrections are warranted," and the selloff may be "consistent with a move toward more rational pricing." Brainard refers to "sharp declines in asset valuations." The valuation view is that the selloff is a partly warranted correction with no sign of distress. Per the guide, a price decline is not undervaluation, so I put it modestly above the midpoint. 54, Moderate-Low confidence; Bullard is the main source, so Low.
- BANKING_STRESS: not discussed. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services and housing are not discussed. Expectations are not discussed (Bullard speaks only of realized inflation relative to target). All N/A.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Chair proposes a 50 bp cut to be announced the next morning and "forceful" action; every participant who speaks supports it, some "reluctantly" (Mester) or with concerns about signaling (Bullard). The move is large and immediate, with coordinated central bank messaging. Strongly dovish; I place it near the emergency-easing band but not at the extreme because there is no QE or explicit promise of more. 12, narrow-moderate band.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: No dissent; the roll call is all Yes. Reservations: Mester "somewhat reluctantly," Bullard's concern about interpretation and the lack of a plan. No disagreement about the assessment. Near-consensus with a couple of reservations. 17.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Financial conditions and asset prices are raised by Powell, Bullard, Brainard and Kashkari, but the discussion centres on the virus and the real-economy outlook. Moderate-to-limited. 38.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The Chair tells participants not to signal either way about March ("not before us today"), and wants to "maintain as much policy flexibility." Kashkari and Mester want to plan for the lower bound and next steps. Intended signal is neutral-to-easing; no commitment. 30.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Kashkari ("how much uncertainty ... is it going to be much worse than we expect or not"), Bullard ("the situation remains quite fluid"; "meaningful downside risk"), Daly, Brainard's "tail risk," Powell's "material risk." Worst-case pandemic scenarios are discussed. High but with a baseline view that the effects are modest. 72.

**Polarity verification.** Inflation: higher means more pressure; Bullard says below target and pushed lower, so below 50: matches. Financial conditions: higher means tighter; evidence of tightening: matches. Equity: higher means more concern/depressed; the view is a modest decline that is partly warranted: slightly above 50, matches. Hawkish/dovish: higher is hawkish; evidence is a large cut, so low: matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP: no above/below potential language; 48 is acceptable. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: no "accommodative/supportive" wording, tightening language, so above 50: ok. CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, so no violations.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** Output gap (48) vs consumption (47): consistent. Output gap vs labor: labor N/A. Financial conditions vs credit spreads: spreads N/A (the only mention is hypothetical). Financial conditions vs Treasury stress: N/A. Inflation parent vs sub-components: sub-components N/A.

**N/A justification.** Labor, productivity, Treasury stress, credit spreads, banking, dollar and the four inflation sub-components are not raised by participants as current conditions.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 ±10 Low.** Evidence: Bullard, "the Committee has missed its stated inflation target to the low side ... for many years, and this shock is likely to push inflation still lower." Summary: one speaker, inflation below target. Direction: below neutral because inflation is below target and the shock is seen as disinflationary.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±10 Low.** Evidence: Powell, "an outlook that was a strong one as recently as a month ago"; Bullard, "real GDP growth in the first half of 2020 is likely to be only modestly affected." Summary: no explicit gap language; near potential with downside risk. Direction: marginally below neutral because the risks are to the downside, though the outlook is described as strong.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±8 Low.** Evidence: Brainard, consumer "confidence and activity, which have been the key to our resilience over the past year"; Daly, uncertainty "weighing down on their demand ... we don't see it yet in the hard data." Summary: solid recent spending with an expected pullback. Direction: slightly below neutral on the emerging demand risk.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Powell, "counter a tightening of financial conditions"; Brainard, "sharp declines in asset valuations and long-term yields ... ease financial stress among indebted businesses." Summary: conditions have tightened somewhat, with partial stabilization after the Friday statement. Direction: above neutral because participants describe a tightening.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** **CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A** (the Kashkari mention is a hypothetical scenario, not a current observation).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 54 ±8 Low.** Evidence: Bullard, "some corrections are warranted, as pricing sometimes moves well away from ... market fundamentals"; Brainard, "sharp declines in asset valuations." Summary: a selloff seen as partly a warranted correction, with no distress language. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** **Inflation sub-components: N/A.**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 12 ±5 Moderate.** Evidence: Powell, "a 50 basis point cut ... take such action today in a forceful manner"; Brainard, "I do support taking the decisive action today." Summary: unanimous support for an outsized, immediate cut. Direction: strongly dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: roll call all Yes; Mester, "I support the move somewhat reluctantly"; Bullard, "I am also moderately concerned about how the Committee intends to play this sequence." Summary: no dissent, minor reservations about timing and signaling. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 ±10 Moderate.** Evidence: Bullard on the equity selloff and the risk the cut is seen as a response; Brainard on asset valuations and financial stress. Summary: financial conditions are a notable but secondary theme beside the virus outlook.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Powell, "Don't say, 'we're not going to do it.' Don't say, 'we're going to do it.'"; "maintain as much policy flexibility going forward as we possibly can." Summary: deliberately no explicit signal, with an easing-leaning posture and scenario planning for the lower bound. Direction: below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Kashkari, "how much uncertainty there is about what's going to happen with the coronavirus"; Bullard, "the situation remains quite fluid ... meaningful downside risk." Summary: many participants stress uncertainty about the virus path, though baselines are given. Direction: high.
