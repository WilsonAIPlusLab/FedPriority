# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion (transcript), meeting 1999-12-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion: questions in the staff Q&A, the full economic go-around, a short policy/directive go-around (symmetric vs asymmetric wording), a long Working Group discussion of directive and disclosure policy (balance-of-risks language, Chairman's intermeeting authority), and a roll-call read-out. One passage in Mr. Poole's Y2K remarks is garbled (blank gaps); it concerns Y2K industry operations only and does not affect any score.

**Era context.** Late 1999: very strong expansion, three funds-rate increases already made in 1999, Y2K rollover days away. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.** Nearly every Reserve Bank president and governor reports strong activity and very tight labor markets (Parry, Hoenig, Jordan, Broaddus, Guynn, Boehne, Minehan, McTeer, Moskow, Stern, McDonough, Gramlich, Poole, Ferguson, Kelley, Meyer). Core inflation is described as benign or subdued so far, with most expecting a gradual rise (core 2.1 to about 2.3-2.8 by 2001). Productivity is described as surprisingly strong. Policy: nobody wants to raise rates now because of Y2K; nearly all expect tightening in February. Hawkish voices on the economics: Guynn (would argue for a snugging move except for Y2K), Broaddus ("may be at least a little behind the curve"), Meyer ("unsustainably rapid pace of growth"), Minehan (75bp or more needed), Boehne ("Some further increases ... highly likely"). Less hawkish: Hoenig (risks balanced), Stern (skeptical of the NAIRU relationship). The Chairman argues the economy is not classically "heating up" and the heat comes from a wealth effect that cannot continue.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation as roughly moderate to slightly below the level that worries them: "few signs of rising inflation" (Hoenig), "overall inflation remains subdued at the consumer level" (McDonough), pricing power limited. But there are emerging pressures (steel, oil, advertising prices, owners' equivalent rent, import-price benefit ending) and an expected rise. Score what is reported now: near the middle, a bit above. About 52.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Meyer: "unsustainably rapid pace of growth on top of an already unsustainably high labor utilization rate"; Guynn: economy "more resource constrained"; McDonough: "demand already exceeds even the increased capacity of the supply side." The Chairman counters that it is not classic overheating, but still cites "very evident imbalances in demand over supply." Bright line requires > 50. About 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. District unemployment about 3.25% (Hoenig), "pool of additional workers has dried up" (Guynn), "Labor markets are very tight" (Boehne, Stern, Broaddus), fast-food restaurants on weekends-only schedules (Jordan). Wage gains still modest. Level governs: about 76.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong, above trend. "Consumer spending remains strong" (Broaddus), "raw strength of the continued expansion" (Guynn), Q4 growth near 5% (Minehan), "no significant restraint from any quarter" (Poole). Housing and some interest-sensitive sectors slightly soft. About 68.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length and seen as well above trend: "gangbuster gains in productivity for the fourth quarter" (Greenspan), "outsized productivity gains" (Boehne), "virtuous cycle" (McTeer). About 74.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Minehan says conditions "seem quite accommodative, notwithstanding our recent tightening"; stock market surprising on the upside and "frothy." Bright line requires < 50. Greenspan sees discount factors beginning to rise, and some note higher rates slowing housing, so not extremely loose. About 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members discuss Y2K liquidity and settlement worries in general terms, but no participant addresses Treasury market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only a direction statement by the Chair: BBB yields "have risen more than yields on U.S. Treasuries, as evidenced by widening spreads." No level stated. Per the v4.1 rule, direction only gives 52-55, Low confidence. 53.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Strong valuation concern. Moskow "concerned about high valuation levels for the stock market, particularly for the Internet stocks"; Gramlich "seemingly overvalued"; Greenspan "only a question of how much of a bubble"; Minehan "frothy." About 20.
- BANKING_STRESS: Briefly touched. Guynn: "more evidence of credit quality problems in health care lending," loan loss provisions; elsewhere bankers are upbeat and loan growth is strong. Healthy with minor concerns, below 50. About 38, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not about a move up. McDonough expects "either a stable or somewhat weaker dollar"; Gramlich says the dollar is seemingly overvalued but "not yet falling." Stable to expected weaker, so slightly below 50. About 44, Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods (steel and commodity increases, import-price cushion ending, but pricing power limited), services (health care, sharp wages in some service industries, but modest overall), housing (McDonough notes housing price increases; Greenspan expects owners' equivalent rent to accelerate), expectations (concern to keep them anchored: Guynn, Ferguson; no sign of unanchoring). All near the middle, slightly above.

**Tier 3 check in words.**
- Policy lean: Action today is hold, by common agreement ("I assume that nobody here wants to raise rates at this time"), but the tightening bias is broad and explicit; the Chair says both drafts say "we are very likely to move rates at the next meeting." Hold with strong tightening bias: about 60.
- Dissent: Unanimous vote, 10 of 10 voting members yes. Economic views largely aligned on direction (differences on how much heat vs. supply-side). The symmetric vs asymmetric split was acknowledged as a "close call" of minor importance. In the directive/disclosure discussion, four preferred the old language, two opposed the intermeeting-authority text, and Broaddus and Guynn expressed reservations, but this is tactical and procedural and resolved by consensus (Meyer: "I would certainly consider myself bound"). Low-to-modest: about 22.
- Financial stability attention: Y2K market calm/liquidity is the organizing concern of the policy decision, plus extended equity valuation / wealth-effect / bubble commentary. Moderate: about 50.
- Forward guidance: Symmetric directive adopted, but the stated message is a tightening bias retained and a likely move in February ("the most hawkish symmetric directive in history"). About 70.
- Uncertainty: Standard to somewhat elevated; many cite uncertainty about productivity, the NAIRU, stock market and wealth effects, oil, and Y2K, but conviction on the strength of the expansion is high. About 45.

**Polarity and bright-line verification.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 > 50 matches "above" language. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 < 50 matches "accommodative." CREDIT_SPREADS 53 is direction-only, in the 52-55 band. BANKING_STRESS 38 < 50 matches healthy-with-minor-concerns. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 44 < 50 matches expected stable-to-weaker (no appreciation described). TREASURY_STRESS N/A. EQUITY_VALUATION low = high overvaluation concern, matches.

**Coherence checks.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs LABOR 76: gap 14, within 15; labor is a level-driven anchor (UE well under 4%), while participants note productivity expands supply, which damps the gap. OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs CONSUMPTION 68: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 53: mild tension; spreads is direction-only low-confidence and the 53 is the rule-mandated band, not a level claim. TREASURY_STRESS N/A in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 52 consistent with sub-components 52-55.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market not discussed by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Hoenig: "few signs right now of rising inflation"; Moskow: "the deceleration in our price measures has ended."
Summary: Current inflation is reported as benign but with emerging upward pressures.
Direction: At or just above neutral because pressures are building while measured inflation is subdued.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 62 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Meyer: "unsustainably rapid pace of growth on top of an already unsustainably high labor utilization rate"; McDonough: demand "already exceeds even the increased capacity of the supply side."
Summary: Most see demand outrunning supply, though the Chair resists "heating up."
Direction: Above neutral (above potential).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 76 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: Guynn: "the pool of additional workers has dried up"; Hoenig: district unemployment "about 3¼ percent."
Summary: Nearly everyone reports very tight labor markets, with wage pressures modest so far.
Direction: Well above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: Guynn: "amazed at the raw strength of the continued expansion"; Poole: "Everything we're looking at is solid, strong."
Summary: Spending and investment are well above trend, with only housing softening at the edges.
Direction: Above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 74 ±8 [High]**
Evidence: Greenspan: "gangbuster gains in productivity for the fourth quarter"; Boehne: "outsized productivity gains."
Summary: Productivity growth is seen as exceptionally strong, a recurring theme.
Direction: Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Minehan: "financial market conditions in general seem quite accommodative, notwithstanding our recent tightening"; Parry: stock market strength boosting spending.
Summary: Conditions are described as accommodative, with a strong equity market, offset by some noted rate effects in housing.
Direction: Below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 ±8 [Low]**
Evidence: Greenspan: BBB yields "have risen more than yields on U.S. Treasuries, as evidenced by widening spreads."
Summary: Only a direction statement, no level, so score is just above neutral per the rule.
Direction: Slightly above neutral on the side of the move; level unknown.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 20 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Moskow: "concerned about high valuation levels for the stock market, particularly for the Internet stocks"; Greenspan: "only a question of how much of a bubble there is."
Summary: Several participants voice overvaluation/bubble concern.
Direction: Below neutral (overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low]**
Evidence: Guynn: "more evidence of credit quality problems in health care lending"; Boehne: bankers believe Y2K "will be a non-event."
Summary: System seen as healthy with minor pockets of credit concern.
Direction: Below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 44 ±8 [Low]**
Evidence: McDonough: "either a stable or somewhat weaker dollar"; Gramlich: dollar "seemingly overvalued ... But it is not yet falling."
Summary: Dollar seen as stable, with expectations of some weakening; no appreciation described.
Direction: Slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52; INFLATION_SERVICES: 54; INFLATION_HOUSING: 55; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.**
Evidence: Moskow: steel contract prices up about 1 percent after down 5 percent; Broaddus: "quite sharp wage increases in some service industries" but manufacturing "fairly moderate"; Greenspan: "owners' equivalent rent is going to start to accelerate"; Guynn: policy aimed at avoiding "a deterioration in inflation expectations."
Summary: Each category is stable to modestly firming, with no sign of surge.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±5 [High]**
Evidence: Greenspan: "I assume that nobody here wants to raise rates at this time"; both drafts say "we are very likely to move rates at the next meeting"; Boehne: "Some further increases in interest rates ... are highly likely."
Summary: Hold now for Y2K, with a broad, explicit tightening bias.
Direction: Above 50, lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±7 [High]**
Evidence: Unanimous roll call; Broaddus: "I can live with either of these statements"; Meyer: "I would certainly consider myself bound by this consensus."
Summary: Differences are tactical (wording, disclosure policy) and economic nuances, none sharp, no dissent.
Direction: Low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Boehne: "get into the new year with financial markets as settled as we can make them"; Greenspan on the wealth effect and "how much of a bubble."
Summary: Market stability around Y2K shapes the decision and equity-valuation worry is recurrent, but the bulk of discussion is on the economy and directive wording.
Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we don't want to remove the general view in the market that we retain an upward bias"; McDonough: "the most hawkish symmetric directive in history."
Summary: Formally symmetric but the message is that tightening is likely in February.
Direction: Above neutral, tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Meyer: "many elements of uncertainty surrounding the outlook, especially about inflation dynamics, NAIRU, and productivity"; Moskow: "we don't really know whether productivity growth will slow, remain high, or continue to accelerate."
Summary: Several name uncertainty over productivity, the NAIRU, wealth effects and Y2K, but conviction in the growth outlook is high.
Direction: Somewhat elevated.
