# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting of 1996-01-31 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It opens with organizational business (election of Chairman and Vice Chairman, staff officers, authorizations), then the members' questions to staff, the economic go-around (day 1), the monetary aggregate ranges round (day 2), a long discussion of an explicit inflation objective and the Mack Bill, the policy go-around, and a vote read-out with two roll calls. Staff answers are removed, so some questions appear without replies. Procedure is ignored for scoring. No passage was garbled enough to prevent scoring.

**2. Era context.** The text is from early 1996 (Humphrey-Hawkins meeting). The members describe a mature, slowing expansion: weak January (weather, a government shutdown, auto cuts), inventory adjustment, soft retail, CPI inflation near 3 percent, and unemployment of 5.6 percent. The Committee had eased in July and December. I score what the members say, not the recalled history.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan (Chair): downside risks "significantly larger"; real rates "higher than I feel comfortable with"; proposes a 25 bp cut with a symmetric directive as "insurance".
- Cut supporters: McDonough, Boehne, Jordan, Kelley, Lindsey, McTeer, Phillips, Stern, Yellen, Guynn. Several (Lindsey, McTeer) say a larger cut could be justified.
- Prefer to wait: Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Minehan, Moskow. Melzer opposes strongly and also objects to the discount rate cut.
- Fed Governors Lindsey and Yellen are the 2 "No" votes on the ranges roll call (they preferred Alternative II or III). Everyone else chose Alternative I.
- Inflation-objective debate: Broaddus, Melzer and Jordan push an explicit objective. McDonough, Boehne and Parry favor waiting for the Mack Bill or acting through deeds. Yellen, Phillips and Moskow want more study.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above a price-stability objective but not pressing. CPI is about 2.7 to 3 percent and is "too high" (Melzer, Jordan, Yellen). Many report "little upward pressure on prices" (Moskow) and "clear lack of any significant inflationary pressure" (Minehan). Others see wage firming (Broaddus, Phillips, Melzer's PPI remark). Slightly above the midpoint: 54.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is "at or a little below potential" (Hoenig), "near its potential" (Moskow, Phillips). Parry and Yellen say unemployment is "a tad below" the natural rate, Hoenig mentions a "mild overshooting". The first quarter is soft. At or just below potential: 48.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 5.6 percent, "around the NAIRU without a lot of head room" (Broaddus), "labor markets are tight by any traditional definition" (Minehan), "labor market tightness is pushing up wages" (McTeer), with shortages of skilled labor. McDonough and Phillips doubt the 5.6 percent figure signals tightness, and job insecurity is widely cited. Near NAIRU: 52.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Retail and holiday sales were weak, and autos and heavy trucks soft, with inventory adjustment (Stern, Greenspan). Offsetting this are housing "good", light trucks and equipment investment "healthy" (Stern), and "slow but solid growth" (Minehan). Net slightly below trend: 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned only in passing: a "favorable productivity shock" in 1995 (Jordan), investment in machines "to improve productivity" (Phillips), and "unless productivity also increases" (Kelley). It is thin, so I give it a mildly above-trend score at Low confidence: 54.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Difficult to argue that monetary policy is restrictive": long yields low, stock market at records, "credit remains readily available" (Melzer). Phillips says "no bottlenecks in the capital markets" and "credit is still available". Greenspan and Yellen argue real policy rates are still somewhat restrictive, which is policy, not market conditions. Conditions are supportive, so below 50 (bright-line): 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Long-bond yield levels are discussed (Lindsey) but only as a yield signal, with no functioning issues. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spread levels are given. Credit is described as "plentiful" or "available" (Hoenig, Phillips, Melzer), though some retail lending caution (Moskow) and "credit growth has slowed". The side is below normal, with Low confidence: 38. Bright-line check: no widened or elevated-spread language, so below 50 is consistent.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stocks are at records, "supported by earnings" (Phillips), "ebullience of financial markets" (Minehan). Lindsey notes households are net sellers. No participant voices valuation concern. Roughly fair, with slight rich-valuation tilt: 44.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only brief remarks: "credit quality generally remains quite good" and delinquencies moderating (Moskow), "lenders report healthy loan demand" (Melzer). Healthy, so below 50: 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "The dollar has appreciated about 3 percent on a trade-weighted basis since our last meeting" (Yellen). Stern mentions a "stronger dollar" and Phillips a "strong dollar". Appreciation, so above 50: 58.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence says ample credit and no widening, so a score below 50 is consistent. BANKING_STRESS: healthy means low stress, so below 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: appreciation means above 50. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: supportive means below 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: a 25 bp cut with a symmetric directive, supported by 11 participants (all 10 voters plus Guynn), described as "insurance". Six members (Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Minehan, Moskow, Melzer) preferred to wait, and none argued for a larger cut. This is a moderate dovish lean, tempered by the small size and the "insurance" framing.
- Disagreement: the final policy vote is unanimous with no formal policy dissent (the six who wanted to wait did not vote). However, the ranges vote had 2 "No" votes (Lindsey and Yellen) on a technical matter. Six participants disagree on the cut, one of them "strongly" (Melzer). There is also substantive debate over an inflation objective and the opportunistic strategy. Formal dissent is low, but the disagreement is real, so I place it between "moderate differences" and "notable disagreement".
- Financial stability attention: low. There are brief mentions of household balance sheets (Lindsey), credit availability, the stock market and lending conditions. This is not a focus.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive; "if the insurance turns out to be inappropriate ... it will get reversed" (Greenspan); the press statement says "slight easing". It is slightly easing-leaning but mostly neutral and data-dependent.
- Uncertainty: "more fog than usual in Washington and in the outlook" (Phillips), data unreliable due to weather and shutdown, large confidence interval for the natural rate (Yellen), "a lot of uncertainty" (Moskow). Elevated but ordinary.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 48 (at or near potential, within 48-52). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (<50, supportive). CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (<50). BANKING_STRESS 32 (<50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 (>50). TREASURY_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52): gap of 4, fine. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (44): gap of 4, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) and CREDIT_SPREADS (38): co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, which is fine for a normal period. INFLATION_PRESSURE (54) vs sub-components: goods are about neutral to soft (steel, paper, commodity prices weak, food up) and expectations are anchored at about 3 percent. Services and housing are not discussed. Consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only yield levels mentioned, no functioning commentary. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as inflation categories by participants (a few rental-rate remarks are regional and not about price pressure).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 +-7, Moderate.**
Evidence: "I can't find it acceptable [3 percent inflation]" (Jordan); "clear lack of any significant inflationary pressure" (Minehan).
Summary: Inflation is near 3 percent and above a price-stability goal, but few see an acceleration.
Direction: Slightly above neutral because 3 percent is "too high" yet pressures are subdued.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 +-5, Moderate.**
Evidence: "real GDP likely to run at or perhaps a little below potential" (Hoenig); "economy expanding near its potential growth rate" (Moskow).
Summary: Most see growth near potential with a soft first quarter.
Direction: At or slightly below neutral because growth is described as at or a bit under potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 +-6, Moderate.**
Evidence: "operating in the neighborhood of the NAIRU without a lot of head room" (Broaddus); "Labor markets are tight by any traditional definition" (Minehan).
Summary: Unemployment of 5.6 percent is near the estimated natural rate with regional shortages.
Direction: Marginally above neutral because the natural rate is seen as slightly above actual unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 +-6, Moderate.**
Evidence: "Christmas sales were very disappointing" (McTeer); "slow but solid growth in ... housing, overall employment, personal income" (Minehan).
Summary: Retail and autos are soft, with housing and equipment spending holding up.
Direction: Below neutral because weak consumer demand and an inventory adjustment outweigh solid housing and capital spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 54 +-10, Low.**
Evidence: "we had a favorable productivity shock" (Jordan); "increased investment in machines and equipment to ... improve productivity" (Phillips).
Summary: Productivity is mentioned only in passing, with a mild positive tilt.
Direction: Slightly above neutral because the references to it are favorable.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7, Moderate.**
Evidence: "stock market ... continues to set new records ... and credit remains readily available" (Melzer); "no bottlenecks in the capital markets" (Phillips).
Summary: Markets and credit are supportive even though some members regard policy rates as still somewhat restrictive.
Direction: Below neutral (looser) because credit is available, equities are strong, and long rates are low.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only yield levels discussed; no functioning commentary.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-10, Low.**
Evidence: "Capital and loans are still available and even plentiful in some cases" (Hoenig); "Credit growth has slowed but credit is still available" (Phillips).
Summary: Credit is generally available, though spread levels are never stated.
Direction: Below neutral because availability is described as ample and no widening or elevated spreads are mentioned.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +-8, Moderate.**
Evidence: "The stock markets have been strong; they appear to be supported by earnings" (Phillips); "ebullience of financial markets" (Minehan).
Summary: Strong markets, seen as earnings-supported, with no explicit overvaluation concern.
Direction: Slightly below neutral (richer side) because of the record highs without stress.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8, Low.**
Evidence: "Credit quality generally remains quite good on the commercial side" (Moskow); "Lenders report generally healthy loan demand" (Melzer).
Summary: Banking is healthy, with minor retail-lending caution.
Direction: Below neutral because banks and credit quality are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-7, Moderate.**
Evidence: "The dollar has appreciated about 3 percent on a trade-weighted basis since our last meeting" (Yellen); "a stronger dollar than we now anticipate" (Stern).
Summary: Appreciation is the main reference, treated as equivalent to some tightening.
Direction: Above neutral because the dollar is rising.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 45.** Steel prices "down significantly" (Moskow), "commodity prices are weakening" (Greenspan), offset by expected food price increases (Melzer).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** "inflationary expectations down as low as they have been since the late 1960s" (Boehne) versus "3 percent inflation is embedded in long-term expectations, and ... too high" (Melzer).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 36 +-6, Moderate.**
Evidence: "it would be wise in my judgment for us to move rates down 25 basis points" (Greenspan); "I would not favor reducing the federal funds rate" (Parry).
Summary: A modest 25 bp insurance cut is favored by 11 participants, with six preferring to wait and none urging a larger cut.
Direction: Dovish because a majority favors some easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-8, Moderate.**
Evidence: "I would favor alternative B. I would favor it strongly" (Melzer); "I am afraid I am the odd one out here" (Yellen, on ranges).
Summary: The policy vote is unanimous, but 6 non-voting participants wanted to wait, and there were 2 "No" votes on the ranges.
Direction: Moderate differences about pace and risk, with disagreement on the inflation strategy but no formal policy dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 10 +-5, Moderate.**
Evidence: Lindsey's household balance-sheet discussion ("financial assets greater than non-mortgage debt"); credit availability remarks.
Summary: Brief mentions only; the meeting is about growth, inflation, and the policy framework.
Direction: Low attention, consistent with the era's anchor of 1 to 10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 44 +-8, Moderate.**
Evidence: "a symmetrical directive probably would be wise" (Greenspan); "if the insurance turns out to be inappropriate ... it will get reversed" (Greenspan).
Summary: A symmetric directive with a reversible insurance cut signals only a mild easing lean.
Direction: Slightly below neutral because there is no tightening bias, with some dovish tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-8, Moderate.**
Evidence: "There is more fog than usual in Washington and in the outlook" (Phillips); "A lot of questions are being raised about the short-term outlook" (Moskow).
Summary: Data noise from weather and the shutdown, and uncertainty about the natural rate, are widely noted but without deep alarm.
Direction: Elevated on short-term data and policy uncertainty.
