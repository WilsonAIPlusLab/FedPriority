# v4.1 scoring: transcript discussion, meeting 2001-10-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). It holds the opening procedural turns, members' questions during the Desk (Dino) and staff Q&A, the district go-around, the policy go-around, the closing roll of "I support your recommendation", and a short vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Procedure, the minutes approval and the discount-rate recess are ignored.

**Era context.** Roughly three weeks after the September 11 attacks. The funds rate target has been cut repeatedly this year, and members refer to cuts "from 6-1/2 to 3 percent". The Chairman proposes another 50 bp cut and retaining the "risks weighted toward economic weakness" language. This is not an emergency meeting, but it is shock-dominated. I score only what members say.

**Speakers.** Every voting member speaks in the go-around: Greenspan, McDonough, Moskow, Parry, McTeer, Poole, Stern, Hoenig, Minehan, Guynn, Santomero, Broaddus, Jordan, Ferguson, Kelley, Meyer, Gramlich. All say "I support your recommendation" in the policy go-around.

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Moskow: "One positive note is the lack of inflationary pressures"; Parry has core PCE of "about 1-1/2 percent in 2002"; Stern says "inflation risks have diminished"; Kelley says inflation is "going to trend downward"; Ferguson says "inflation and inflation expectations are well contained". Guynn says price increases are "generally limited to the health-care sector and liability insurance". There is no deflation language, so this is moderately low rather than very low, about 30. A few voices (Minehan's contacts, Guynn, Meyer) raise longer-run inflation risk from stimulus, which keeps it from going lower.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Parry: growth "will likely remain well below the potential rate for some time" and "greater expected slack in labor and product markets". McTeer: "pushed it over that brink" into recession. Minehan: "two or three quarters of mildly negative growth". Meyer expects unemployment in the 6 percent range. The recession is expected to be mild, so about 30. This is not the 16-30 "deep contraction" band.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint and loosening. Ferguson: initial claims "jumped", with unemployment near 6 percent "not out of the realm of possibility". Layoffs are cited at Boeing, airlines, hotels and trucking. Minehan notes "a very welcome softening in labor markets". The level of unemployment is still around 5 percent (not stated directly), which keeps this from being very low. I score about 40.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Moskow: "Consumer spending has definitely slowed"; business investment "outlook... continues to be poor". Parry, Hoenig, Ferguson and Santomero agree. Offsets are a quick retail rebound (Stern, Poole's Wal-Mart contact, Broaddus) and housing "remains sound" (Stern). The net is about 31.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only Minehan's hypothetical question about staff structural-productivity estimates. This is staff content and a conditional, not a member assessment. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mildly tight relative to the easing. Minehan: "with the decline in the stock market and the widening of risk spreads, financial conditions are not as accommodative as our degree of policy easing might imply". Ferguson: "fixed income markets have not become more accommodative"; junk-bond yields "stubbornly high"; banks "watching closely". Meyer: policy must "counter at least the adverse effects on overall financial conditions". Against that, Minehan says commercial paper "more or less returned to normal". No member calls conditions tight or restrictive outright, so about 56.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury discussion is about the yield curve and whether the long end reflects supply or an inflation premium (Minehan, Greenspan, Ferguson: "driven by concerns about the budget outlook"). No dysfunction is described, so this is normal functioning, about 45, with low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only, no level. Minehan: "widening of risk spreads"; Gramlich: "risk spreads on lesser-rated bonds"; Ferguson: below-investment-grade yields "remain stubbornly high". Under the v4.1 rule (direction without level, clear widening) the score is 52-55, with low confidence. I use 54.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Prices have fallen sharply ("over $1 trillion lost", indices down 10 to 20 percent). Greenspan gives an explicit valuation view: "the equity market is still slightly above where it ought to be", with a risk of over-adjustment downward. That is a slightly-above-fair-value view with ongoing downside concern, so I put it just below fair at 44 (31-45 band, near the edge). Per the gotcha, the price decline itself is not undervaluation.
- BANKING_STRESS: Poole: "strong bank capital, unlike... 1990-91. Banks can make loans". Against that, Guynn reports "some tightening of credit standards, and some deterioration of credit quality", Ferguson says banks see default risk rising, and Stern mentions a brokerage firm lost. The system is described as sound, so it must be below 50. I score 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Minehan notes "the strong dollar" hurting manufacturers and forest products (pre-attack). Ferguson notes "safe haven flows from the dollar... into the Swiss franc". These are mixed and only incidental, so I score a near-neutral 52, with low confidence. The bright-line rule is satisfied because the net is not a depreciation statement.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION = more pressure: evidence says little, so low. Higher OUTPUT_GAP = more above potential: evidence says below, so low. Higher LABOR = tighter: evidence says loosening, so below 50. Higher CONSUMPTION = stronger: evidence says weaker, so below 50. Higher FIN_COND = tighter: members say "not as accommodative", so slightly above 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress: members say widening, so slightly above 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: banks sound, so below 50. All match.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous support for a 50 bp cut with a weakness-tilted risk statement. Hoenig and Parry say monetary policy "should do more". McTeer thinks "something less would be sufficient" but supports the cut to avoid disappointing markets. Guynn calls the cut temporary and worries about longer-run inflation. Several (Broaddus, Kelley, Meyer, Gramlich, Hoenig) foresee a reversal later, but none argue for tightening now. Strong dovish to moderately dovish, about 24.
- Dissent: zero dissents, all 17 voting-member go-around turns end in support. Small differences: McTeer (smaller cut preferred), Guynn (temporary, a "bit more optimistic"), Jordan (monetary-base proviso and Desk operations), and a few on signalling. Broad agreement, about 17.
- Financial stability emphasis: Desk operations and liquidity (Dino Q&A, Jordan, McDonough), fragility of markets (McTeer, Hoenig), commercial paper, risk spreads, bank behaviour, equity losses (Ferguson, Gramlich, Greenspan's closing segment), and "confidence" as a theme. Substantial but the economic assessment still dominates, so about 55.
- Forward guidance tone: weakness-risk language retained and no tightening signal. McDonough: "very ill-advised to... put everyone on notice that we may tighten"; Hoenig: "better if our statement is shorter". Members want to avoid signalling reversals now, even though Kelley, Meyer, Guynn and Broaddus anticipate it later. Mild easing bias, about 21.
- Uncertainty: pervasive. McTeer: "That knowledge... is now obsolete"; Meyer: "an order of magnitude above normal"; Greenspan: "extraordinarily uncertain"; Hoenig: "exceedingly difficult" to forecast; Stern: "the key word... is uncertainty". Very high, about 85.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP (30, below potential: OK). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (56, no "accommodative/supportive" self-description as current state; only "not as accommodative": OK). CREDIT_SPREADS (54, direction only, within 45-55 rule on side of the move, low confidence: OK). BANKING_STRESS (38, "strong bank capital": OK). DOLLAR_STRENGTH (52, mixed: OK). TREASURY_STRESS (45, no dysfunction: OK).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs LABOR 40: 10 apart, within 15 (labor level still near 5 percent, gap reflects expected slack). OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs CONSUMPTION 31: aligned. FIN_COND 56 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 54: co-move. FIN_COND vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times for Treasuries, so about 45 is fine. INFLATION 30 vs EXPECTATIONS 33: consistent.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: only a staff-estimate hypothetical. INFLATION_GOODS: only oil/commodity prices mentioned in passing, no goods-price assessment. INFLATION_SERVICES: only a regional health-care/insurance remark, not a services assessment. INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed (housing activity only).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "One positive note is the lack of inflationary pressures in today's economy"; Ferguson, "both inflation and inflation expectations are well contained". Center of gravity: inflation low and falling, no deflation talk. Direction: below neutral because members see inflation risks as diminished.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Parry, growth "will likely remain well below the potential rate for some time"; McTeer, the economy was "teetering on the brink of recession and this event has surely pushed it over that brink". Center of gravity: recession with growing slack, expected to be mild. Direction: below neutral because of falling output and rising unemployment.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Ferguson, initial claims "jumped" and unemployment near 6 percent "does not seem out of the realm of possibility"; Minehan, "welcome softening in labor markets". Center of gravity: labor market loosening from a still low unemployment level. Direction: below neutral because of layoffs and rising claims.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 31 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "Consumer spending has definitely slowed" and "firms are becoming even more cautious about capital spending"; Stern, retail "bounced back quite quickly" as an offset. Center of gravity: weak spending and investment partially offset by retail rebound. Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 56 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "financial conditions are not as accommodative as our degree of policy easing might imply"; Ferguson, "fixed income markets have not become more accommodative". Center of gravity: easing offset by equity declines and wider risk spreads. Direction: slightly above neutral (tighter than the policy easing implies).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 45 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan and Minehan on the curve being supply or inflation premium; Ferguson, bond market "driven by concerns about the budget outlook". Center of gravity: yield-curve discussion with no dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 ±6 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "widening of risk spreads"; Gramlich, "risk spreads on lesser-rated bonds". Center of gravity: widening, level not stated. Direction: slightly above neutral per the direction-only rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the equity market is still slightly above where it ought to be", with a history of over-adjustment; Ferguson, "over $1 trillion of equity wealth has been lost". Center of gravity: slightly rich valuation view with downside concern. Direction: just below neutral (modestly above fair value).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Poole, "strong bank capital, unlike the situation in 1990-91"; Guynn, "some tightening of credit standards, and some deterioration of credit quality". Center of gravity: sound system with early credit-quality worries. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "The strong dollar and slowing demand" hurt manufacturers; Ferguson, "safe haven flows from the dollar... into the Swiss franc". Center of gravity: incidental and mixed. Direction: near neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 33.** Evidence: Poole, "very low expected inflation"; Broaddus, "inflation expectations have been declining so far this year". Direction: anchored and slightly low. Goods, services, housing: N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "ease by 50 basis points and retain the statement indicating that the risks are tilted toward economic weakness"; Minehan, "monetary policy should do more as well". Center of gravity: unanimous easing, with only talk of later reversal. Direction: dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 ±5 (High).** Evidence: every member states "I support your recommendation"; McTeer, "I tend to think something less would be sufficient". Center of gravity: unanimous with minor emphasis differences. Direction: broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, losses "of more than a trillion dollars in the value of stock market holdings" and over-adjustment risk; Jordan, concern about contraction of "central bank liquidity"; McTeer, "psychology of the financial markets and their fragility". Center of gravity: a recurring theme but not the main focus. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 21 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, retain "risks are tilted toward economic weakness"; McDonough, "very ill-advised to test our credibility by... putting everyone on notice that we may tighten". Center of gravity: weakness bias retained, no tightening signal, though some foresee later reversal. Direction: easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 85 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Meyer, uncertainty "an order of magnitude above normal"; McTeer, "That knowledge... is now obsolete"; Greenspan, "extraordinarily uncertain". Center of gravity: nearly every speaker stresses unprecedented uncertainty. Direction: very high.
