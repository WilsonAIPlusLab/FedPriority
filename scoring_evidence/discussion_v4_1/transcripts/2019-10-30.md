# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1: meeting of 2019-10-30 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant turns only, staff turns removed. The text spans two days. Day one (Oct 29) holds three blocks: a framework/toolkit round (forward guidance, balance sheet, negative rates, yield curve control), a round on repo operations and a standing repo facility, and then the financial stability comment round and the economic go-round. Day two (Oct 30) holds the policy go-round and the vote read-out. Much of the early text is about tools and money-market plumbing, not an assessment of conditions. I scored the economic go-round, the financial stability comments, the policy go-round and the vote. I used the toolkit and repo rounds only where members spoke about conditions, such as the September repo dislocation, or about policy-space attitudes.

**Era context.** The backdrop is a mature expansion with a global manufacturing slowdown and trade tensions. The text itself says the policy rate is being cut by 25bp for the third time this year, after the July and September cuts.

**Speakers.** Many of the views come from the same group. Chair Powell, Vice Chair Williams and Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles and Bowman spoke. Presidents Mester, Rosengren, Harker, Bostic, Barkin, Evans, Bullard, Kaplan, Kashkari, Daly and George also spoke.

**Core economic dimensions, direction first.**
- **Inflation.** Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. They cite core PCE of about 1.7 to 1.8 percent (Quarles, Powell, Kashkari), "inflation remains below target" (Brainard, Williams) and "too low for eight years" (Kashkari). Mester notes some firming measures. George's "core inflation currently at 2.2 percent" is an outlier. Inflation is below target but not deflationary, so I score about 36.
- **Output gap.** Participants see the economy as near potential. Powell says growth is "about trend, or 2 percent." Brainard says "stabilizing at a near-trend pace" and "near-potential." Williams says "slightly below potential." Bostic and George say "at or above potential." The mix averages to the midpoint, so I score 50.
- **Labor.** The unemployment rate is 3.5 percent, a 50-year low. Powell calls the labor market "in a strong position" and Mester says "hard to find and retain workers." Counterweights are slower payrolls, moderating wages and job openings, and Kashkari's "still slack." Level governs. The unemployment rate is below 3.8 percent, so the score sits above 55, which I temper to 66 because of the wage and job-growth moderation.
- **Consumption and investment.** Consumers are solid or "the engine" while business investment, manufacturing and exports are weak (nearly every speaker). The net is slightly below trend, about 45.
- **Productivity.** There is only passing mention: Bullard on improvement, Kashkari on 1.8 percent labor productivity, and automation comments. I score near trend with low confidence.

**Financial dimensions.**
- **Financial conditions.** Participants describe policy as "accommodative" (Quarles: "very accommodative"; Barkin: "simple message of accommodation"). Mester says firms "have ample access to credit" and Rosengren and Mester describe "reach for yield" and "very easy credit terms." The bright-line rule requires below 50. Offsetting this, September repo stress is a funding-side tightening, but members treat it as being resolved. I score 40.
- **Treasury stress.** Members discuss the September repo and funds-rate dislocation at length ("panic broke out" per Williams, "stress in repo markets" per Brainard). Mester also says Treasury market liquidity "has deteriorated." This is actual liquidity impairment but is largely in funding markets and now contained. It is not a yield-movement story. I score 57 with wide uncertainty.
- **Credit spreads.** The levels described are low: Brainard cites "very low credit spreads" and spreads "out of line with fundamentals," and Quarles says triple-C spreads are wider "in recent months." Kaplan expects future widening, which is direction and not the current level. The level rule requires below 50, so I score 32.
- **Equity valuation.** Mester says "equity prices relative to earnings... high by historical standards" and Rosengren says "extremely rich" pricing. There is valuation concern on the rich side, so I score modestly below 50 on this scale (higher means more depressed or stressed), at 38.
- **Banking stress.** Quarles says "banks are well capitalized," Mester says "banking system is well capitalized," and Brainard and George worry about falling buffers. Bright-line rule requires below 50. I score 28.
- **Dollar.** Clarida cites "a stronger dollar" as a force holding down inflation, and George cites "a strong dollar" weighing on durable goods orders. This is modest strength, discussed only in passing, so I score 58 with low confidence.
- **Inflation sub-components.** Inflation goods, services and housing are not discussed in a way that supports a score (Mester's apparel and footwear tariff remark is one retailer). Inflation expectations are discussed by many speakers as low, record-low in surveys and drifting down, so I score 30.

**Tier 3 check.**
- **Policy lean.** The Committee cuts 25bp. Eleven participants support B (cut) and six prefer C (hold): Rosengren, Harker, Mester, Bostic, Barkin and George. Most of the supporters of the cut say it is the last for a while. Quarles says that accommodation may need to be removed "gradually" at a practicable date. The lean is dovish-to-balanced, about 40.
- **Dissent.** There are two formal dissents (George and Rosengren, both for holding). Roughly six participants prefer C and several others (Kaplan, Quarles, Evans) call the decision close. Both camps share the same economic read and differ on risk-management tactics, not on the economy. Score about 45.
- **Financial stability emphasis.** A full financial stability comment round (nearly everyone), a long repo and money-market session, Brainard's and Daly's climate and stablecoin remarks, and financial-stability worries inside policy turns (Mester, George, Brainard) give a prominent topic, about 70.
- **Forward guidance tone.** The messaging is a pause with "not on a preset course": "continue to monitor" language replaces "act as appropriate." Several speakers say no further cuts are expected. Neutral with a slight easing hold, about 44.
- **Uncertainty.** Participants talk about downside risks, trade uncertainty and "talking ourselves into a recession," but risks are said to have diminished and the modal outlook is confidently stated. Elevated but not extreme, about 50.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- Output gap, 50: no "above" or "below" language dominates, so the 48 to 52 band applies.
- Financial conditions, 40: "accommodative" and "easy" language, so below 50 holds.
- Credit spreads, 32: low stated level, so below 50 holds.
- Banking stress, 28: "well capitalized," so below 50 holds.
- Dollar, 58: strengthening described, so above 50 holds.
- Treasury stress, 57: no auction failures, so under 60 holds.

**Coherence checks.**
- Output gap (50) and labor tightness (66) differ by 16. The reason is that members say unemployment is far below the natural-rate estimate while growth is only at trend and the unemployment rate is low because of the labor-supply side. This is a modest divergence.
- Output gap (50) and consumption and investment (45) differ by 5, so they are consistent.
- Financial conditions (40) and credit spreads (32) co-move.
- Treasury stress (57) is high against financial conditions (40). This reflects a distinct September funding-market episode, not a general tightening.
- The inflation parent (36) and expectations (30) are consistent.

**N/A justification.** Inflation goods, services and housing are N/A because no member discusses them in substance.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Kashkari, "inflation remains below target. Reaching 2 percent seems as elusive as ever"; Quarles, "core PCE inflation is now running at 1.7 percent." Center of gravity: below 2 percent for years with softening expectations. Inflation is below neutral because core PCE is under target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 ±5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell, growth "about trend, or 2 percent"; Brainard, "stabilizing at a near-trend pace." Center of gravity: near potential with mixed language. Output gap is at neutral because the descriptions straddle potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 66 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "unemployment rate at 3½ percent... labor market is in a strong position"; Mester, "hard to find and retain workers." Center of gravity: very low unemployment tempered by cooling wages and openings. Labor market is above neutral because unemployment is below 3.8 percent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "Household spending continues at a solid pace... Business investment, manufacturing, and exports remain weak"; Kaplan, "not getting any support from business investment or exports or manufacturing." Center of gravity: solid consumer offset by contracting business investment. Spending is slightly below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 52 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Bullard, "productivity has been improving and will continue to improve"; Kashkari, "labor productivity growth of around 1.8 percent." Center of gravity: near trend, thin discussion. Productivity is at neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mester, "Firms already have ample access to credit"; Quarles, "I view the stance of policy as being very accommodative." Center of gravity: easy credit and low rates with a transient September funding squeeze. Conditions are looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 57 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Williams, "a panic broke out, especially on Tuesday morning"; Mester, "Liquidity in the U.S. Treasury securities market and the equity futures market has deteriorated." Center of gravity: real but contained funding-market dysfunction. Stress is modestly above neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Brainard, "very low credit spreads and high levels of indebtedness"; Brainard, "low spreads and strong risk appetite appear to be out of line with fundamentals." Center of gravity: stated level is low, with only marginal credits widening. Spreads are below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mester, "Equity prices relative to earnings and corporate bond prices are high by historical standards"; Rosengren, pricing "extremely 'rich'" in private equity. Center of gravity: valuations stretched but not described as a bubble. Concern is on the rich side of neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Quarles, "banks are well capitalized and can weather a significant downturn"; Mester, "currently the banking system is well capitalized, but several large banks are planning to reduce their voluntary buffers." Center of gravity: sound system with buffer-erosion worries. Stress is below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Clarida, a "stronger dollar" offsets cyclical inflation pressure; George, "negative effects due to a strong dollar." Center of gravity: passing remarks on a strong dollar. Dollar is above neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 30.** Evidence: Kashkari, "survey measures of inflation expectations have declined further. Both the Michigan and the New York Fed surveys are now at record lows"; Clarida, the common factor "is at an all-time low level." Center of gravity: anchored below target and drifting lower. Expectations are below neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Clarida, "I support alternative B... lower the funds rate target range by 25 basis points"; Harker, "keep our powder dry, and I'm not in favor of the cut." Center of gravity: majority cut with a pause signal, a hawkish minority of six. Lean is dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 45 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Vote read-out, "President George No / President Rosengren No"; Rosengren, "I support alternative C at this meeting." Center of gravity: two formal dissents and six preferring a hold, with a shared economic read. Disagreement is moderate to notable.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 70 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Brainard, "high levels of indebtedness among risky, nonfinancial corporates as the area of greatest vulnerability"; Mester, "financial stability risks remain moderate, but... risks are rising." Center of gravity: a full dedicated round plus a repo session and policy-turn worries. Attention is prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 44 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mester, "this is the last rate cut for a while"; Williams, "no presumption of further cuts, while we remain vigilant and data dependent." Center of gravity: pause with an easing option retained. Tone is slightly dovish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Barkin, "uncertainties expand the range of inaction"; Rosengren, downside risks "have diminished somewhat since September." Center of gravity: elevated policy and trade uncertainty with a stated modal view. Uncertainty is elevated.
