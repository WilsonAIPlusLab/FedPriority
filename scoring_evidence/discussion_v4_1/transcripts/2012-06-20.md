# v4.1 Discussion Scoring — Meeting 2012-06-20 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It opens with administrative items (SOMA manager appointment, staff communications policy) and a long Committee debate on a consensus forecast / quarterly monetary policy report. That communications content is not about conditions or policy stance and is ignored for scoring except as background. After that come brief member questions to staff (Europe, fiscal cliff, financial stability, Morgan Stanley and broker-dealer risk, Brian Sack's balance sheet report), the full economic go-round, the Chairman's opening policy remarks, the policy go-round on Alternatives A/B/B', a statement-wording debate, and a roll-call. Staff answers are removed, so some questions appear without answers. No passage is garbled enough to affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2012, funds rate at zero, forward guidance "late 2014", unemployment 8.2%, European crisis (Greece, Spain), US fiscal cliff, falling oil and commodity prices. The decision under discussion is whether to extend the maturity extension program (MEP, "Twist") through year-end (B), do more (A), or do nothing. I score what speakers say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chair Bernanke: persistent headwinds, "agonizing" situation, "if the federal funds rate were currently 3 percent... quite a straightforward call for us to ease further". Notes balance-sheet limits and costs.
- Doves wanting more: Evans (wants A or more, "slack huge"), Rosengren (more MBS purchases), Yellen and Dudley (B only as a temporizing step, would favor more), Tarullo, Stein, Raskin (all supportive of B with easing bias).
- Cautious supporters of B: Lockhart, Pianalto ("close call"), Williams, Powell, Duke ("not proud of it").
- Opponents of any action: Lacker (the dissent), Plosser, Bullard, George, Fisher (non-voter, strongest rhetoric).
- Kocherlakota: sees a small output gap and inflation above 2% under the planned path, favors less accommodation in his own modal forecast, but in the policy round presumes the MEP extension and argues for Alternative A language (more accommodative signaling).

**4. Dimension directions (words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as slightly below target to near target. Headline is falling on energy and commodity prices and the stronger dollar. Most expect close to 2% or a bit below (Williams 1.3% this year, Dudley "likely to fall short", Fisher "price pressures... don't exist", Bernanke "moderate inflation"). Kocherlakota alone sees above 2% by 2014, and Plosser and Lacker see 2%. Below 50, not deflationary. 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Evans "slack... huge", Powell "substantial output gap", Yellen/Tarullo/Bernanke endorse slack. Kocherlakota and Bullard contest the size ("less negative") but not the sign. Bright line satisfied (<50). 32.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE 8.2%, long-term unemployed are real slack, weak payrolls, U-6 high, "inundated with qualified applicants". Far from full employment (16-30). 24.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Momentum slowed. Retail sales ex autos flat, investment held back by Europe and fiscal-cliff uncertainty, hiring on hold. Offsets are resilient consumption, good auto sales, housing bottoming and lower gasoline. Below trend. 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Barely discussed. George lowered trend growth partly for productivity, Dudley uses a 1.5% trend, Powell says productivity has improved (manufacturing). Mixed and thin. 43, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Yellen: "significant tightening of domestic financial conditions, including lower equity prices, higher risk spreads, and a stronger dollar". Bernanke: "financial conditions... tighter", overall impulse "negative". Offsetting: very low Treasury yields, policy accommodative, and Lockhart doubts that conditions are tighter. Somewhat tight overall. 57.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Discussed as safe-haven flows driving yields and term premia very low, and as a future market-functioning limit on Fed purchases (Bernanke, Stein, Fisher). No present dysfunction. Normal functioning. 40.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Williams "high-yield bond spreads have soared"; Yellen "higher risk spreads"; Fisher says the junk spread is outside the historical norm in width and issuance dried up; Rosengren refers to particularly wide asset spreads. Against this, Kocherlakota and Bullard note little imprint of European stress on US markets (St. Louis stress index only slightly up). Level reads somewhat wide. Bright line (>50 for stated high level) satisfied. 58.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price moves noted ("stock market has slid", "lower equity prices", VIX unchanged per Kocherlakota). No valuation commentary. Default near neutral. 52, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banks sound (Duke: credit quality normal or better, capital high, ample liquidity; Stein "banks are stronger"; Bernanke summary: credit supply not the problem). Offsets: Morgan Stanley CDS and downgrade risk and broker-dealer funding fragility (Rosengren, Dudley), and European bank/ELA dynamics (Bernanke, Dudley). Healthy with identified pockets of stress. Bright line (<50) satisfied. 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciated recently on safe-haven flows (Williams, Yellen, Lacker, Dudley, Stein, Bernanke). Stein expects partial mean reversion. Bright line (>50) satisfied. 62.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods are discussed (commodity prices, energy, steel, Walmart rollbacks, falling), giving 28. Services have no explicit discussion (only generic wage comments), so N/A. Housing inflation (rents or shelter) is not discussed, so N/A (house prices are discussed as activity, not inflation). Expectations are stable to slightly lower (TIPS 185 bp, Cleveland model 1.2%, Pianalto "developing risk", Bernanke "stable to slightly lower"), giving 43.

**5. Polarity checks.** LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter, evidence = slack, so low. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter, evidence = "tighter", so above 50. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress, evidence = widened and described as wide, so above 50. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress, evidence = sound, so below 50. DOLLAR: appreciation so above 50. All consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: majority favors some easing (MEP extension; 11 of 12 voters). Several participants want more (Evans, Rosengren, Yellen, Dudley, Tarullo, Stein would ease if away from the ZLB). A bloc wants no action (Lacker, Plosser, Bullard, George, Fisher). Kocherlakota is mixed. Dovish, with a modest action and QE3 left open. About 27.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Lacker). Non-voter Fisher is sharply opposed, with a heated exchange with Dudley and Tarullo over leaks and "road to perdition". Distinct camps on both sides (some wanting more, some wanting nothing), plus an economics disagreement on slack (Kocherlakota, Bullard vs. Yellen, Tarullo, Evans, Powell). Around 50.
- Financial stability: a staff financial-stability presentation plus a dedicated discussion item; extended Dudley and Rosengren treatment of Europe contagion, Morgan Stanley and dealer funding, 13(3) facilities; Bernanke and others on term-premium snapback and market functioning. Prominent but not dominant over the economic assessment. About 62.
- Forward guidance: late-2014 guidance retained. Many projected later liftoff (Williams end-2014, Yellen late 2015), discussion of moving the date, open-ended LSAP, and A(3) language; Bernanke says merely extending guidance would not be very effective. Statement signals readiness for more action. Strong easing bias. About 14.
- Uncertainty: "extraordinarily high" (Pianalto), "enormous uncertainty" (Stein), Raskin "so high... equally horrific", Powell "exceptionally broad" range, Evans "truly frightening", Dudley "cannot overemphasize how dangerous". Lacker explicitly says it is not Knightian. High. About 74.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (below potential) OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS > 50 (stated "tighter") OK. CREDIT_SPREADS > 50 (stated historically wide/soared) OK. BANKING_STRESS < 50 (sound/healthy) OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50 (appreciated) OK. TREASURY_STRESS 40, no dysfunction OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR 24: an 8-point gap, fine (labor slack is more vivid in the talk than the gap, given disputes over potential). OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 and CREDIT_SPREADS 58 co-move. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress stays at 40 because tightness comes from spreads, equities and the dollar, not Treasury malfunction. Inflation 38 with goods 28 and expectations 43 is consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not raised as price-pressure topics by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Williams, "I now expect overall PCE inflation to come in at 1.3 percent this year"; Fisher, "with regard to price pressures, they don't exist". Center of gravity: inflation near to somewhat below 2%, falling headline, few upside voices (Kocherlakota, Plosser's steeper-path comment). Direction: below neutral because most participants see inflation at or under target and projections undershooting.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Evans, "the current amount of slack in the economy seems huge to me"; Powell, "I believe that the chances are that there's a substantial output gap now". Center of gravity: substantial slack, with disagreement only on size. Direction: below potential; Kocherlakota's "less negative" still has a negative gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 ±5 [High].** Evidence: Williams, "I expect no progress on closing the unemployment gap until 2014"; Rosengren, "firms are describing being inundated with qualified applicants". Center of gravity: UE 8.2%, weak payrolls, long-term unemployed treated as real slack. Direction: well below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±7 [High].** Evidence: Lacker, "retail sales ex autos and gas have been flat for two months"; Dudley, "forward momentum of the economy has slowed". Offsets: Chair, "those who commented on consumers actually were a little bit upbeat". Center of gravity: slowed, below trend, with resilient households and housing bottoming. Direction: below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 43 ±10 [Low].** Evidence: Dudley, "if you put in a 1½ percent productivity trend, unit labor costs are roughly running about ½ percent"; George lowered longer-run growth for "lower... labor productivity". Powell's "our productivity has improved" is a manufacturing remark. Center of gravity: modestly below the historical trend, thin evidence. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "significant tightening of domestic financial conditions, including lower equity prices, higher risk spreads, and a stronger dollar"; Bernanke, "financial conditions, which have recently gotten more tight". Dissent: Lockhart, "can we really... [say] tighter conditions?" Center of gravity: moderately tighter, offset by very low Treasury yields. Direction: above neutral (tighter) because most speakers who address it say tightening.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bernanke, "safe-haven flows have lowered Treasury yields"; Fisher/Bernanke on future market-functioning limits and Fed holdings dominating the market. Center of gravity: orderly, extremely low yields from flight to safety, concern is prospective and not current. Direction: at or below neutral; no dysfunction described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Williams, "high-yield bond spreads have soared"; Fisher, "I think we're outside of the historical norm in terms of the width of that spread". Counterweight: Bullard, stress "not particularly high compared with previous values". Center of gravity: spreads wide in high yield with issuance dried up, investment-grade funding cheap. Direction: above neutral (level described as wide).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Williams, "the stock market has slid"; Kocherlakota notes the VIX is basically unchanged since January. Center of gravity: price moves noted with no valuation view. Direction: near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Duke, "by every measure, capital levels are quite high"; Rosengren, "a run on Morgan Stanley whose credit default swaps are quite elevated". Center of gravity: US banks sound and liquid, with a specific broker-dealer and European-bank tail risk. Direction: below neutral because the system is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±6 [High].** Evidence: Williams, "the dollar has appreciated"; Yellen, "a stronger dollar". Center of gravity: recent safe-haven appreciation raising concern about exports. Direction: above neutral (appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 28 [value only].** Evidence: Fisher, "no pricing power whatsoever in most areas... price rollbacks"; Dudley, "many commodity prices have fallen recently". Falling goods prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed separately.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Shelter or rent inflation not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 [value only].** Evidence: Chair, "inflation expectations remain stable to slightly lower"; Pianalto, "a shift in inflation expectations looks to be a developing risk". Anchored, drifting slightly low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 27 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bernanke, "it would be quite a straightforward call for us to ease further at this point"; Evans, "I support any form of additional accommodation". Against: Lacker, "I do not believe that the current situation calls for any further monetary policy action". Center of gravity: majority extends the MEP with open readiness for QE3, a hawkish minority wants no action. Direction: dovish; the action is modest, so not at the extreme.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 50 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: roll call 11-1 with Lacker no; Fisher, "this would be 'the road to perdition'"; Tarullo/Dudley sharp exchange over Fisher's leak remark; Kocherlakota and Bullard vs. Yellen/Tarullo on slack. Center of gravity: one dissent but camps on both flanks, reservations among several supporters ("not proud of it"), and a substantive economics dispute. Direction: moderately high.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Dudley on Morgan Stanley downgrade and European "bad dynamics" as a "real risk from a financial stability perspective"; Bernanke lists financial stability risks of balance sheet policy. Center of gravity: prominent theme through Europe contagion, dealer funding, facilities and term-premium snapback, though the economic outlook still dominates.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "I'm open to a further LSAP, including a program that's open ended, or an extension of our forward guidance"; Dudley, would "strongly consider altering our forward guidance". Center of gravity: late-2014 guidance retained, strong easing bias and readiness to do more. Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 74 ±7 [High].** Evidence: Pianalto, "the level of uncertainty is extraordinarily high right now"; Raskin, "my level of uncertainty is so high that... I found all of the alternatives equally horrific". Center of gravity: nearly everyone stresses unusual uncertainty from Europe and the fiscal cliff, with some rhetoric moderated (Lacker "I don't interpret this as Knightian").
