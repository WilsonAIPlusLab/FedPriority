## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document treated as NOT special-topic: it contains a full economic go-around, a policy go-around, and a roll-call vote on a target-range increase (alternative B). A long reinvestment/balance-sheet session on day one was used only for the Tier 3 dimensions and the few condition remarks members made in it.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 46
- Mester: "PCE inflation rose to 1.9 percent... core PCE inflation remains stable at about 1.7 percent"; Powell: "inflation moving gradually up and ever nearer to 2 percent"; Yellen: "Core inflation has been running close to 1¾ percent... somewhat below our longer-run 2 percent objective"; Kashkari: "Core PCE inflation basically hasn't moved"; Lacker: "headline inflation is now virtually at our 2 percent target".
- Slightly below target, converging; score 46 +-5, Moderate-to-High (High).

OUTPUT_GAP: 53
- Dudley: "economic expansion will continue at a slightly above-trend pace"; Rosengren: "growth next year being 50 basis points above potential"; Bullard: "Hours growing at 1.2 percent... suggests an economy growing at trend"; Yellen: labor market "more or less back to normal but not overheating".
- Near potential, mild above-trend language from several; 53 +-5, Moderate.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55
- Gooding: "labor market that appears to be at or very near full employment"; Kaplan: "intensifying wage pressures and reports of labor shortages"; Powell: "near full employment, and labor markets are still tightening"; Kashkari: "job market may still have some slack left in it"; Brainard: unemployment "near 4.7 percent"; Williams: "at, or even maybe a little beyond, full employment".
- Full employment, shortage anecdotes widespread, a few members see residual slack (Kashkari, Powell, Brainard); 55 +-5, High.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50
- Powell: "real PCE fell 0.3 percent in January" but "I assume that the January softness will prove fleeting"; Lacker: "robust consumer spending in the year ahead"; Yellen: "we have yet to see much evidence that this exuberance is actually translating into stronger spending"; Brainard: "signs of renewed growth in business investment"; Gooding: optimism "has not turned into much action".
- Solid fundamentals, soft Q1 hard data; net at trend; 50 +-6, Moderate.

PRODUCTIVITY: 34
- Lacker: "The reconciliation... comes from low productivity growth, and I'm expecting that to continue"; Powell: "lackluster productivity gains"; Williams: "TFP growth has slowed substantially".
- Below trend, but only brief remarks; 34 +-8, Moderate.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 34
- Dudley: "easing of financial conditions. I find this quite noteworthy"; Powell: "Overall, financial conditions are actually looser than they were a year ago"; Mester: "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions"; Brainard: "easier financial conditions".
- Bright line (accommodative/easier) satisfied (<50); 34 +-6, High.

TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- No participant describes current Treasury market functioning; Treasury-market disruption mentions are hypothetical runoff-design risks and a past debt-ceiling remark (Brainard); yield moves only (Dudley "rise in long-term Treasury yields").

CREDIT_SPREADS: 30
- Rosengren: "high-yield corporate bond spreads to 10-year Treasury securities have come down significantly. Several research studies have highlighted the fact that narrow credit spreads can reflect ebullient financial markets"; Brainard: "spreads on high-yield corporate bonds have moved down further"; Dudley: "the narrowing of credit spreads"; Kaplan: "a widening of credit spreads... healthy adjustment".
- Stated level narrow (Rosengren) plus direction down; below normal; 30 +-8, Moderate.

EQUITY_VALUATION: 25
- Rosengren: "A number of asset prices are also showing signs of froth... stock P/E ratios have risen well above their five-year average"; Brainard: "valuations in some asset markets, such as that for equities, now appear somewhat stretched"; Yellen: "valuations have become quite 'rich.' We could well see a market correction"; Kashkari: "prices do seem high relative to standard valuation measures"; Powell: "clear risk of a correction in equity markets".
- Several members state a stretched/rich view; most "somewhat stretched" rather than bubble; 25 +-6, High.

BANKING_STRESS: 33
- Harker: "Our region's banks are also doing well... classified assets at an all-time low"; Brainard: stresses faced by German and Italian banks "navigated without significant damage"; Dudley: "glad to see that Deutsche Bank is planning to raise $8 billion of equity capital"; Powell: "you don't see high credit growth or high leverage appearing".
- Healthy system, scattered remarks, minor European concerns; 33 +-8, Moderate.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52
- Brainard: "The dollar has declined slightly"; Dudley: "relatively flat trajectory of the U.S. dollar"; Kashkari: "no change in the value of the dollar"; Yellen: "Weak foreign activity and an elevated dollar are continuing to hold down net exports"; Mester: "further appreciation of the dollar" anticipated; George: "the strength of the dollar".
- Direction roughly flat; level elevated and a mild drag; 52 +-6, Moderate.

INFLATION_GOODS: 55
- Evans: "reports of higher prices across a variety of manufacturing sectors"; Mester: "rising prices for raw materials"; Yellen: energy and import price drag "dropped out"; Kaplan: globalization and technology "limit pricing power".
- Mild goods pressure from energy and inputs, partly offset; 55 (value only).

INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
- No participant discusses services prices separately.

INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Housing is discussed as activity and regional house prices (Harker), not shelter inflation.

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42
- Mester: expectations "have been broadly stable"; Brainard: expectations "still remain somewhat low relative to historical experience, there has been movement in the right direction"; Dudley: NY Fed survey "gradual updrift... three-year reached 3.0 percent"; Williams: staff forecast has "long-run inflation expectations... below 2 percent"; Kashkari: "expectations remain low by historical standards".
- Anchored but slightly low, edging up; 42 (value only).

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +-5 (Moderate-High)
- Vote: 25 bp increase, 9 Yes and 1 No (Kashkari, favored alternative A, dovish). Mester: "we need to focus on the risks of moving rates up too slowly"; Rosengren: "persistent but gradual increase in rates should be presumed over the year"; Bullard: "leaning toward alternative A... could be persuaded to support alternative B"; Evans: "I don't see any great urgency for moving today"; Dudley: "grounds for gradually removing monetary policy accommodation are very much in place".
- Consensus to tighten, gradual path, some urging faster (Rosengren, Mester, Powell) and some slower (Bullard, Kashkari, Evans, Gooding); balance-sheet runoff expected later this year. Confidence High.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 +-7 (High)
- One formal dissent, Kashkari ("I support alternative A today. It was a close call for me"); Bullard leaned to A but supported B and objected to wording ("too 'hawkish' a trajectory"); Lacker asked to delete the "carefully monitor" sentence and was overruled; open disagreement on reinvestment timing (Powell: "middle of 2018"; Williams/Fischer/Rosengren: this year; Lacker: asset sales) and on whether to drop "only" before "gradual".
- One dissent plus several reservations, differing camps on pace and balance sheet, not on the economic assessment as such.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-7 (Moderate)
- Rosengren: "A number of asset prices are also showing signs of froth... reaching-for-yield... enhanced financial stability concerns"; Powell: "past two cycles have ended... because of overheating financial conditions"; Fischer: "maintenance of financial stability appears to be especially difficult"; Tarullo: ON RRP "financial stability concern"; Brainard: stretched valuations and macroprudential "first line of defense"; Dudley: easing of financial conditions "important".
- Several members address it meaningfully, but it is not the focus of the meeting.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-6 (Moderate-High)
- Statement keeps "gradual increases" and drops "only" (Yellen: "the Committee expects gradual increases"); Yellen: "likely be appropriate to continue gradually removing accommodation and at a pace similar to what I anticipated"; Dudley: balance-sheet strategy to be "ready to go as soon as, potentially, the third quarter"; Mester: "the base case... removing accommodation".
- Mild tightening bias with data dependence, runoff to begin later this year; no stronger signal.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 +-7 (Moderate)
- Evans: "Uncertainty remains high"; Gooding: "As one director put it, uncertainty will rule the year"; Kashkari: "it is very uncertain how much labor slack remains"; Tarullo: "a somewhat uneasy position from which to be conducting monetary policy"; Harker: "the risk I worry about the most is model uncertainty"; Yellen: "estimates of the sustainable rate of unemployment... are highly imprecise" but risks "roughly balanced".
- Fiscal/trade policy, slack and r* uncertainty emphasized by several, yet risks viewed as balanced and forecasts largely unchanged.
