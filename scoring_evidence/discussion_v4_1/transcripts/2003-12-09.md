# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) - 2003-12-09 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Not special-topic: the document has a full economic go-around, a policy go-around and a 12-member roll call (all "Yes") at the end; the closing communications working-group session is ignored for condition dimensions.
- Policy lean: unanimous hold at a 1 percent funds rate; members broadly see inflation risks moving to balance and many want to begin preparing markets for eventual tightening (Moskow, McTeer, Hoenig, Poole, Gramlich, Geithner, Kohn), while Bernanke, Broaddus and Ferguson still stress downside inflation risk. Net: hold with a mild tightening tilt (52).
- Dissent: zero formal dissents; several stated reservations about the statement (Hoenig "strong preference" to drop "considerable period"; Poole and McTeer preferred dropping it; Minehan "willing to go along"; Broaddus and Ferguson would have changed less; Bies uncomfortable with commitment). Disagreement is about communication tactics, not the rate (33).
- Conditions: inflation very low (~1 percent core, "bottom of the range") with deflation risk largely gone; output and labor slack described as sizable/considerable despite rapid growth; spending and investment strong; productivity growth very strong; financial conditions described as accommodative/stimulative; dollar falling; banks healthy with strong asset quality.
- Forward guidance: "considerable period" retained but conditioned on low inflation and slack, overall balance-of-risks sentence dropped; an easing-lean signal that is being dialed back (32).
- Uncertainty: most members say uncertainty has fallen; a few (Minehan, Geithner, Greenspan on output-gap measurement) stress forecast uncertainty (32).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the odds of inflation rising significantly any time soon from its current very low level seem small." Broaddus: "we are now at the lower bound of ... our inflation tolerance range." Gramlich: "inflation is now near the bottom of the target range."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is very low/below members' preferred ranges, though the risk of further disinflation has diminished and risks are seen as near balance.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe inflation as very low and below their target ranges.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-5 [High]
- Evidence: Santomero: "significant slack remains in both product and labor markets." Kohn: "output gaps remain sizable"; Greenspan: "the output gap, however one measures it, is quite sizable."
- Center of gravity: Growth is rapid but the economy is still clearly below potential; the gap is expected to narrow only gradually.
- Direction: below neutral ("slack remains" bright line → < 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Bernanke: the employment-to-population decline "is comparable to what occurred during the deep 1981-82 recession"; household employment "some 2.9 million jobs below normal." Santomero: national unemployment rate "5.9 percent."
- Center of gravity: Hiring has begun but labor markets retain substantial slack; firms remain cautious about hiring.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe a slack labor market with improvement only beginning.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 61 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: McTeer: "a robust expansion is clearly under way." Moskow: "the orders data point to further robust gains in expenditures for equipment and software"; Ferguson: "household consumption and residential investment have remained robust."
- Center of gravity: Strong household spending, booming housing and a capital-spending rebound, tempered by some reports of discount-driven retail and consumer spending "ebbing a bit" (Minehan).
- Direction: above neutral because spending and investment are described as robust/strong.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 70 +-7 [High]
- Evidence: Bies: "the pace of productivity growth may well continue to run above historically high levels." Broaddus: weak wage growth and strong productivity "combined to deflate unit labor costs at an annual rate of about 6 percent."
- Center of gravity: Members describe a productivity surge and expect growth to stay strong, possibly stronger than the staff assumes.
- Direction: above neutral because members cite exceptional productivity gains.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 27 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Kohn: "financial conditions are becoming more accommodative even as the faster economic expansion takes hold." Hoenig: "accommodative monetary policy, stimulative financial conditions."
- Center of gravity: Several members (Kohn, Hoenig, Minehan "continuing favorable financial market conditions") describe easy, supportive conditions.
- Direction: below neutral ("accommodative"/"stimulative" bright line → < 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 33 +-8 [Low]
- Evidence: Kohn: "stable Treasury bond rates." Greenspan: "The domestic credit markets have been rather tranquil."
- Center of gravity: Treasury markets are described as calm; Geithner's "sharper rise in interest rates" is a prospective risk, not dysfunction.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe quiet, orderly markets; no dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-10 [Low]
- Evidence: Kohn: "the persistent erosion of risk premiums, especially on lower-rated issuers." Geithner: "The extent of the decline in credit spreads and the magnitude of opportunities for the development of leveraged positions ... mean that we have to be attentive to the risks."
- Center of gravity: Spreads have narrowed and Geithner treats their low level as a source of leverage risk; Olson adds ample bank lending capacity ("a significant amount of firepower"), while McTeer reports subprime auto buyers having trouble getting credit. Level is implied rather than stated, so confidence is Low.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe narrowing spreads to a level that invites leverage concerns, with ample credit availability.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 54 +-8 [Low]
- Evidence: Ferguson: the equity risk premium "seems to be relatively wide, even somewhat wider than historically normal." Kohn: "rising equity prices."
- Center of gravity: The only valuation view (a wider-than-normal risk premium) implies equities are not richly valued; prices are rising. Slightly above 50.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because the one valuation view points to modest, not stretched, valuations.

### BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Olson: "we are seeing very strong asset quality" and "there was no need for loan-loss provisioning." Guynn: credit card lenders "remain profitable," though "chargeoff ratios are now approaching 7 percent."
- Center of gravity: Banks are profitable with strong asset quality and lending capacity; minor pockets of consumer-credit concern.
- Direction: below neutral ("strong asset quality"/healthy → < 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 +-5 [High]
- Evidence: Parry: "the dollar obviously has been falling recently." Guynn: "a drop in the value of the dollar"; Geithner: "potential cliffs for the dollar."
- Center of gravity: Widespread references to a declining dollar and concern about further depreciation.
- Direction: below neutral (depreciation bright line → < 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 33
- Evidence: Pianalto: "CPI goods prices are still falling"; Poole: consumer electronics prices "15 to 20 percent lower than last year." Offset slightly by rising raw-materials prices (Hoenig, Santomero).

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 55
- Evidence: Minehan: "Health costs are clearly escalating"; Guynn: prices muted "except for health care." McTeer expects services inflation to converge down toward goods over time.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- No member discusses U.S. rents or shelter costs (Greenspan's house-price remark concerns foreign central banks; office rents are commercial real estate).

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52
- Evidence: Moskow: the five-to-ten-year TIPS period "has shown a quite dramatic increase in inflation expectations"; Kohn: "stable inflation expectations"; Greenspan: "very little evidence that the markets are pricing in a buildup in long-term inflation expectations."

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 52 +-5 [High]
- Evidence: Moskow: "we need to start preparing markets for inevitable rate increases." Gramlich: "It is way too soon to start raising rates, but it may not be too soon to change some of our rhetoric." Bernanke: "we should continue to remain patient and not choke off growth unnecessarily."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold with a shift toward neutral risk language and preparation for eventual tightening; a dovish minority stresses downside inflation risk.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because the hold comes with a tightening tilt in members' forward views.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-6 [High]
- Evidence: Hoenig: "I don't agree with the change in the statement ... that would be my strong preference." Minehan: "I am willing to go along with your proposal at this time." Roll call: 12 Yes, no dissents.
- Center of gravity: No formal dissents but four to six members registered reservations about the statement wording; differences in inflation-risk assessment (downside vs balanced) are moderate.
- Direction: below neutral because disagreement is limited to tactics and wording with a unanimous vote.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 +-8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Geithner: "the magnitude of opportunities for the development of leveraged positions combined with the large scale of the mortgage-backed market." Guynn: consumers "have increased their debt burdens"; "Delinquency rates on subprime mortgages far exceed those of other mortgages."
- Center of gravity: A few members (Geithner, Guynn, Olson, Poole on BoJ capital) address financial-stability themes as context, not the focus.
- Direction: below the midpoint because attention is limited.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 32 +-7 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Proposed statement (Greenspan): "with inflation quite low and resource use slack, the Committee believes that policy accommodation can be maintained for a considerable period." Geithner: "it's appropriate to dial back a bit the forward-looking signal we're sending."
- Center of gravity: A low-for-long signal is retained but made conditional and is clearly being prepared for removal.
- Direction: below neutral because the forward signal is still accommodation for a considerable period.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 32 +-8 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Moskow: "Much of the uncertainty that was pervasive earlier in the year has dissipated." Minehan: "I have to admit to a great deal of uncertainty about any particular forecast"; Geithner: "we face a fair degree of uncertainty over many of the key elements of this recovery."
- Center of gravity: Most members report reduced uncertainty, while some stress output-gap measurement and forecast uncertainty (Greenspan, Bies).
- Direction: slightly above normal because a few members emphasize uncertainty even though the majority say it has fallen.
