- Document type: a short conference-call session (about 150 lines) on extending the Federal Reserve's swap/credit arrangement with Mexico. It is not a monetary policy meeting.
- No member discusses the policy stance, the funds rate, inflation, output, labor or spending; the vote is on a Mexico facility, not a target rate, directive or policy statement. Treated as special-topic under V4.1.1 rule 1 (operational/facility session). This is a borderline call because a roll call exists; the roll call is a swap-facility vote, not a monetary policy action.
- Policy lean and dissent intensity are therefore N/A. For the record, the roll call is 8-1 on the Mexico proposal (Broaddus No; he objected that it becomes a permanent enlargement of the $700 million swap line and risks harm to the System under congressional scrutiny).
- All eight other speakers support it (Boehne, Forrestal, McDonough, Hoenig, Minehan, Stern, Parry, Keehn; Kelley moves it, Lindsey seconds). Tone is near-consensus on substance, with one principled objection.
- Financial stability is touched explicitly but briefly: McDonough says Mexico "could have a destabilizing effect on our financial market" and that events had an "adverse effect on both the stock and the bond markets" that day; Stern cites "disorderly markets" risk. Scored as limited discussion.

- Forward guidance and uncertainty: no member comments on the policy outlook or expresses uncertainty about it, so both N/A.
