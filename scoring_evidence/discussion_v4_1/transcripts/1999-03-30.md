# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1999-03-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It holds the staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, the Chairman's closing assessment and policy recommendation, the policy go-around, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Pure procedure (minutes approval, domestic operations vote) is ignored.

**Era context.** Spring 1999. Participants describe a very strong expansion, extremely tight labor markets, very low measured inflation, a soaring stock market, and an easing of the international crisis (Asia, Brazil). The Committee eased 75bp last fall as "insurance". The debate is whether to unwind some of it or adopt a tightening-bias (asymmetric) directive. Kosovo has just escalated. I scored what the text says, not what I recall about the period.

**Speakers.** Greenspan, McDonough, Poole, Broaddus, Parry, Jordan, Minehan, McTeer, Hoenig, Moskow, Guynn, Boehne, Stern, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, Rivlin, Gramlich. All 18 speak. Voting members appear in the roll call: Greenspan, McDonough, Boehne, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, McTeer, Meyer, Moskow, Rivlin, Stern (11 yes votes, no dissents). Non-voters Poole, Broaddus, Parry, Guynn, Minehan, Jordan and Hoenig also speak.

**Dimension directions (stated before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation as below target/very low. Greenspan says "inflation effectively at zero", Ferguson says it has "disappeared", and McDonough says there is a "complete absence of inflation". They worry about 2000 (Moskow, Meyer, Guynn). The Gotcha says to score the current state, so it sits below the midpoint, at the low end of "contained, running below target".
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Guynn says the economy "very likely is operating beyond sustainable levels". Broaddus says "already operating well above potential GDP". Meyer says "we have allowed the economy to move beyond its sustainable capacity". Offsets: McTeer notes low capacity utilization, Boehne sees no boom, and Rivlin doubts the rate is unsustainable. Above 50, modestly to clearly.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Hoenig's district unemployment is 3.3%, Parry cites below 3% in Phoenix, Minehan projects 3.9% nationally, and Rivlin cites "continued extreme tightness". Greenspan says "labor markets are very taut. Initial claims are unbelievable". Well above 50. The national rate is not stated, but the 1999 anchors for this dimension are 65-75.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Far above trend. Broaddus says Greenbook domestic final demand is "close to 7 percent". Greenspan says private domestic final purchases are "truly awesome". Ferguson says consumption and investment are "quite robust". Manufacturing and agriculture are soft, but they are not the weight of the discussion.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Strong acceleration is a central theme. Greenspan and Gramlich cite a speed-up since 1995, Broaddus the "apparent increase in trend productivity growth", and Moskow "very heartening productivity growth". Above trend, 70s.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose. Minehan says "credit is plentiful" and "the cost of capital is too low". Ferguson cites "relative ease of access to credit". Poole says markets are "mostly back to normal" after the fall disturbances. Bright line: accommodative means < 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The only mentions are Poole's and Greenspan's questions to the Desk manager about on-the-run/off-the-run relationships and bid/ask spreads. The answers are staff content and are removed, and no member gives an assessment. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan says "credit market conditions continue to be regarded as favorable, with spreads wider than a year ago but funding readily available". "Wider than a year ago" is direction only. The stated level is favorable and ample, and Poole says disturbances have dissipated. So the score is below 50. Evidence is thin, so confidence is Low.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants see stretched, frothy equity valuations. Minehan quotes "frothy, bordering on excessive" and "The stock market remains exuberant". Rivlin cites the "apparently unwarranted continued upward price march" and "bubbly heights". Counterweight: Minehan says P/E ratios "may be appropriate" given productivity, and Greenspan discusses earnings expectations as rationale. Score in the 15-30 band.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mentioned only in passing. Minehan contrasts "the health of the financial sector in our economy" with Japan. Stern notes bankers have "a little concern" about commercial loan credit quality. Jordan raises a forward-looking lender-risk concern. McDonough covers Japanese banks, which is foreign. US banks are seen as healthy with minor concerns. Score below 50, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: McTeer says "The dollar is somewhat stronger." The Greenbook projects a steady or modestly stronger dollar (Broaddus), though Broaddus and Makin raise depreciation as a risk. Direction is modest strengthening, so the score is above 50, modestly.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services and housing are only mentioned incidentally (McTeer's real estate capacity, steel and wallboard prices, DRAM prices). No clear assessments, so N/A. Expectations are discussed: Gramlich says surveys "do not show much evidence of acceleration" and Rivlin says "low inflation expectations", while Broaddus warns they could turn up. Anchored, roughly at target or slightly low.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Chairman recommends no change and a symmetric directive, and the Committee agrees. Most voters see "steady as she goes". There is a visible hawkish camp: Hoenig and Poole want to tighten now, Broaddus, Guynn and Meyer lean toward an asymmetric (tightening) bias, and Parry, Minehan and Gramlich are "asymmetric in head". Greenspan says the next move is "exceptionally likely" to be up. The result is hold with a tightening lean: lean hawkish, 55-60.
- Disagreement: No formal dissents (11-0). Poole says he "would, with respect, dissent" if he were voting, and Hoenig would tighten. Several others would prefer asymmetry but accept symmetry. The disagreement is mostly about timing and tactics, plus Poole's disagreement about the inflation target. The economic assessments are broadly shared. Broad agreement with notable reservations: about 30.
- Financial stability emphasis: Moderate to limited. Minehan (froth, "when the party stops", Japan lessons), Rivlin (bubble crash risk), Jordan (lender risk from productivity dynamics), Hoenig and Poole (insurance/credit disturbances), Brazil and Japan banking. It is a theme of several participants but not the dominant topic; the meeting is mostly about growth, labor and inflation. Around 40.
- Forward guidance: The directive stays symmetric, and nothing hawkish is announced. But many say the next move is likely up and Meyer says policy "should be biased toward tightening going forward". The debate is explicitly about not signaling it now (the new disclosure policy, Kosovo, market reaction). Score mildly above neutral: 55.
- Uncertainty: Moderately elevated. Parry cites "large uncertainties". Minehan and Gramlich say it is murky whether a new era exists or the NAIRU has shifted. Forecasts have been "consistently wrong", and the Taylor rule is called into question. Kosovo and international risks are noted, but the language is mostly standard-to-elevated, not "uncharted territory". Around 48.

**Polarity checks.**
- Inflation low: score < 50.
- Equity concern: the dimension's higher = more stress/depressed valuation. Participants see overvaluation, so the score is low.
- FC loose: < 50.
- Dollar: stronger means > 50.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP > 50.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50.
- CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 (level favorable; "wider than a year ago" is direction only).
- BANKING_STRESS < 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS N/A.
All satisfied.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) vs LABOR (74): gap of 14, which is under 15. The tight labor market is stated more strongly than the output-gap evidence, because participants emphasize productivity-driven supply growth raising potential.
- OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION (72): gap of 12, which is under 15. Spending is far above trend, partly offset by supply expansion.
- FC (30) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (40): consistent, both loose.
- TREASURY N/A.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (36) vs EXPECTATIONS (46): the parent is low on current prints and expectations are anchored near target, so they are consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member assessment. INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: no sustained member assessment.

**Text problems.** A few passages in Poole's FedEx/UPS remarks (around source lines 1024-1038) and one in McDonough's China remarks (source line 666) have gaps or removed words. They are anecdotal and do not affect the scores. Staff answers to member questions are absent, as expected.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "with inflation effectively at zero"; Ferguson, "Inflation seems to have disappeared, at least currently and as far as one can see in the pipeline." Center of gravity: current inflation is very low and, while the 2000 upturn worries many, the current state governs. Direction: below neutral because the CPI is rising 0.1% a month and members call inflation absent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 61 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, "the U.S. economy already operating well above potential GDP"; Guynn, "our economy very likely is operating beyond sustainable levels". Center of gravity: most see demand beyond sustainable capacity, tempered by productivity-raised potential. Direction: above neutral because several explicitly say above potential or sustainable levels.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 76 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "labor markets are very taut. Initial claims are unbelievable."; Rivlin, "continued extreme tightness in labor markets everywhere". Center of gravity: unemployment at 3-4% in many districts, with workers hard to find everywhere. Direction: above neutral because of shortages and sub-4% rates.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 74 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, "domestic final demand ... close to 7 percent"; Greenspan, "private domestic final purchases are truly awesome". Center of gravity: far above trend spending, offset slightly by manufacturing and agricultural weakness. Direction: above trend because of extraordinary demand strength.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 76 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "cyclically adjusted rate of increase in productivity ... has been rising appreciably since the mid-1990s"; Moskow, "very heartening productivity growth". Center of gravity: productivity acceleration is a central theme, with only measurement caveats. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "credit is plentiful; and the boom in interest-sensitive sectors suggests that the cost of capital is too low"; Ferguson, "relative ease of access to credit". Center of gravity: credit freely available, the stock market exuberant. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only member questions to the Desk manager; no member assessment.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "spreads wider than a year ago but funding readily available"; Poole, "the credit market disturbances last fall, most of which have dissipated". Center of gravity: spreads wider than a year ago but conditions favorable. Direction: below neutral because the stated level is favorable and "wider than a year ago" is direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 20 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Minehan, "They all view economic conditions and financial markets as frothy, bordering on excessive"; Rivlin, "the stock market's apparently unwarranted continued upward price march may accelerate again to even more bubbly heights". Center of gravity: several see froth and bubble risk, with Minehan open to productivity justification. Direction: valuations stretched, so the score is low on this stress scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 34 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "the health of the financial sector in our economy versus the Japanese situation"; Stern, "bankers are expressing a little concern about the credit quality of their commercial loan portfolios". Center of gravity: US banks healthy with minor concerns. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-7 (Low).** Evidence: McTeer, "The dollar is somewhat stronger."; Broaddus, Greenbook projects "a modestly stronger dollar". Center of gravity: modest strength, with depreciation risk flagged as an alternative scenario. Direction: above neutral (modest appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** Incidental mentions only.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46.** Evidence: Gramlich, "Surveys of inflation anticipations do not show much evidence of acceleration"; Rivlin, "low inflation expectations". Center of gravity: anchored near or slightly below target, with upside risk noted by Broaddus.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "it is exceptionally likely that the next move the Committee will be making will be on the upside ... we have time"; Meyer, "monetary policy should be biased toward tightening going forward". Center of gravity: a hold with a symmetric directive, but a sizable hawkish minority and a general expectation of eventual tightening. Direction: modestly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Poole, "if I were a voting member I would, with respect, dissent"; Hoenig, "my preference is to unwind". Center of gravity: an 11-0 vote, with non-voting preferences for tightening and several voters who accept symmetry reluctantly. Direction: modest reservations, no formal dissents, differences over timing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "When can the punch bowl justifiably be taken away?"; Jordan, "The risk to financial stability depends on the ability of lenders ... to absorb those adverse surprises." Center of gravity: several participants address froth and lender risk but it is not the meeting's focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "I would bet that our next move is more likely to be an increase"; Ferguson, "we haven't in any sense prepared the markets for a policy move". Center of gravity: no tightening signal is sent, but the expected direction is up and the tilt question is live.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "there are large uncertainties"; Gramlich, "The evidence is still murky". Center of gravity: new-era productivity and NAIRU doubts, model failures and Kosovo, but the language is mostly measured.
