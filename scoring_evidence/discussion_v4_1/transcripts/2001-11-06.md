# v4.1 Discussion Scoring: 2001-11-06 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It contains the Chair's and members' questions during the staff Q&A (the answers are removed), the economic go-around by Reserve Bank presidents and Governors, a short staff-policy-question exchange, the Chair's policy recommendation (50 bp cut with a balance-of-risks statement toward weakness), the policy go-around, and a wording discussion of the press statement. A partial roll-call read-out is appended at the end (nine "Yes" entries listed; the speech shows every member who spoke supporting the 50 bp cut, and Gramlich votes "yes" in the text). The text is clean; only a few staff passages are blanked.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2001: weeks after September 11, an economy that participants all call a recession, rising unemployment, anthrax and terrorism worries, funds rate already cut 100 bp since September 10 and 2-1/2 percent before this meeting. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Nearly all District reports are negative: Parry (Twelfth, high-tech, travel), McTeer (Eleventh, border, air travel, "largest decline" in the Texas indicator), Minehan, Broaddus ("hunkering down"), Stern, Moskow ("unprecedented situation and uncertainty is very high"), Guynn, Hoenig, Santomero, Poole, McDonough, Jordan (more mixed, cocooning, consumer resilience), Ferguson, Gramlich ("almost everything that could go down, did"), Meyer, Kelley.
- Broaddus, Ferguson, Meyer: zero-bound argument, "preempt disinflation", move aggressively. Greenspan: "beyond disinflation and getting to the edge of a deflationary process", recommends 50 bp.
- Reluctant or lower-ease camp: Poole (preferred 25), Hoenig (no change or 25), Minehan (25 or no change), Guynn (modest move), Jordan (comfortable unchanged), Stern and Santomero (mild reservations on expectations), Guynn/Stern earlier ("further policy responses should be more measured"). All support the Chair's 50 bp; none dissents.

**4. Core dimensions, directions in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Core PCE is about 1-1/2 percent (Broaddus), forecasts of 1-1/2 percent (Parry), disinflation with a risk of deflation (Greenspan, Ferguson, Broaddus), "inflation is not a current worry" (Minehan), "prices remain subdued" (Guynn). Slight offsets (insurance premiums) are minor. Score about 24.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. "Growth is now far below potential and will likely be so for some time" (Minehan); "so much excess capacity" (Broaddus); capacity utilization "extremely low" (Kelley, Parry). Score about 26.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below midpoint. Payrolls falling sharply, unemployment up about 1-1/2 points already, projected peak about 6 percent (Broaddus), layoffs three times normal (McTeer), hiring intentions lowest since 1980 (Moskow), wage demands abating. Unemployment level is roughly 5.4 percent (members cite a 1-1/2 point rise from a low near 4 percent), so a level in the 31-45 band, with deterioration pushing toward the lower part. Score about 35.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below midpoint. Business fixed investment "abnormally low even for a recession" (McDonough), capital spending cuts everywhere, durable goods orders weak, consumer confidence off, retail holiday outlook poor, but autos very strong on zero-percent financing, housing and some retail resilient, Jordan's cocooning. Net weak. Score about 30.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed mainly in the statement wording and by Guynn and Meyer. Members expect security reallocation "may restrain advances in productivity for a time" while long-term prospects stay favorable; Guynn notes falling manufacturing productivity; Greenspan says Q3 measured productivity will look strong and structural gains of the last years persist. Modestly below trend near term. Score about 42, low-moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Meyer: financial conditions "have improved much less than would have been expected" given the funds rate cuts; Minehan: spreads and bank credit tightening suggest "financing conditions that may be tougher than the level of real short-term interest rates implies"; Stern: "some tightening in credit conditions" but decent credits fine; McDonough: tighter standards. Against that: long rates down, record bond issuance, equity market high, Gramlich says the interest-rate channel worked. No member calls conditions "accommodative" as conditions (policy is called accommodative, which is policy not conditions). Score slightly above neutral, about 53.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Discussed only through the Chair's and Poole's opening questions about settlement fails and bid-ask spreads (answers removed). The Chair sees fails as "a disruption to the normal market process" but Poole's follow-up implies market-functioning spreads are mostly back to normal. No member view of actual dysfunction beyond fails. Score in the normal range with Low confidence, about 47.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Minehan mentions spreads directly (tougher than real rates imply); Gramlich notes risk spreads at levels that could "normalize" in equities; Stern says reasonable terms for decent credits; Ferguson notes record bond issuance but interprets it as weak free cash flow. Level information is thin. Somewhat wide and less favorable, Low confidence. Score about 56.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Gramlich: "stock market is surprisingly high ... earnings-price ratio virtually the same as the real AAA bond yield ... analysts' forecasts implausibly optimistic"; Ferguson: strength "may well erode quickly"; Greenspan: rally looks like a bear-market rally, "I wish I could take greater comfort". McTeer and Minehan take comfort in the market. Valuation view is modestly rich/vulnerable. Score about 38 (lower = more overvaluation concern per the scale).
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks "sound and have proved largely resilient" (Guynn); credit officers describe CRE portfolios as "excellent" and consumer portfolios better than expected (Jordan); but increased delinquencies and tighter standards (McDonough), C&I loss risk in auto-supplier exposure rose "dramatically" (Moskow), Japanese bank interest rate risk (McDonough). Below midpoint, healthy with pockets. Score about 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only passing references: Ferguson lists "perhaps even the level of the U.S. dollar" among markets attesting to expected rapid rebound; Gramlich notes lower rates do not necessarily lower the dollar. This implies a firm dollar level but no stated movement. Score just above 50 with Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services, housing and expectations are not discussed as such. All N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** INFLATION_PRESSURE low = low pressure, matches deflation talk. OUTPUT_GAP low = slack, matches. LABOR low = looser, matches. BANKING_STRESS low = healthy, matches Guynn and Jordan. EQUITY_VALUATION low = overvaluation concern; evidence is "surprisingly high ... implausibly optimistic", matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: a 50 bp cut, with a bias toward weakness, Chair and most members strongly dovish; the policy go-around features Broaddus, Ferguson, Meyer, Gramlich, Parry arguing for aggressive ease and the zero bound. Several favored 25 bp or no change but accepted 50. Strong dovish. About 20.
- Disagreement: zero formal dissents, unanimous vote. But at least five members (Poole, Hoenig, Minehan, Guynn, Jordan) explicitly say they preferred a smaller or no move and are going along; Stern and Santomero voice smaller reservations. The disagreement is over tactics and expectations management, not over the economic outlook (the Chair: "I don't think any of us has a big dispute on the economic outlook"). Per rubric, zero dissents with 4-5 reluctant supporters scores 40-50 at most; given the shared economic view, lower end. About 38.
- Financial stability attention: moderate-low. Treasury fails, Japanese bank risk, financial-conditions pass-through (Meyer at length), equity valuation (Gramlich, Greenspan), bank credit. Not dominant; economy and policy dominate. About 35.
- Forward guidance: Chair keeps the bias toward weakness; market expects another 25 bp; Poole, Stern, Guynn worry about built-in expectations of more ease; Meyer muses on signalling that rates will rise later than markets expect; Guynn wants policy to be "nimble" in raising later. Easing bias. About 17.
- Uncertainty: "unprecedented situation and uncertainty is very high" (Moskow), "great uncertainty" (Ferguson), "not much easier today ... to know what to make of" the data (Jordan), two states of the world and unparameterizable downside (Meyer), "extraordinary developments" the models cannot capture (Greenspan), Santomero "sufficiently unsure". High, many participants. About 72.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 26 < 50 with "far below potential": satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53 > 50: no "accommodative/supportive" description of conditions was given, so no violation; tightening language from Meyer, Minehan, Stern supports above 50. CREDIT_SPREADS 56 > 50: Minehan's spreads language implies somewhat wide, level inferred, direction not used alone; Low confidence. BANKING_STRESS 38 < 50 with "sound": satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 54 > 50: the level is described as supportive of a rebound; no explicit depreciation; Low confidence. TREASURY_STRESS stays in the normal range: satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 26 vs LABOR 35: 9 apart, fine. OUTPUT_GAP 26 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 30: 4 apart, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 56: co-move, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury near normal, fine. INFLATION parent has no sub-scores, so no check.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: no member discussed category-level price components. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: the only expectations discussed are market policy-rate expectations, not inflation expectations. Productivity, Treasury, equity, banking, dollar are scored because participants touched them, with confidence set by how little they said.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 ±6 [High]**
- Broaddus: "the core PCE inflation rate ... is currently about 1-1/2 percent ... we should ... prevent that ... from falling much below 1 percent."
- Greenspan: "we are beyond disinflation and getting to the edge of a deflationary process."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is low and falling with deflation risk named by the Chair. Inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe disinflation, not price pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 26 ±6 [High]**
- Minehan: "growth is now far below potential and will likely be so for some time to come."
- Broaddus: "an environment where we have so much excess capacity."
- Center of gravity: Recession with large slack. Output gap is below neutral (bright-line satisfied).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±6 [High]**
- Broaddus: "the 1-1/2 percentage point increase in unemployment that has already occurred will weaken household confidence further."
- Moskow: "labor market conditions ... are deteriorating rapidly ... Manpower survey ... largest year-over-year drop since 1980."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment in the mid-5s and rising quickly, wage demands abating. Labor is below neutral tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 30 ±6 [High]**
- McDonough: "Business fixed investment is abnormally low even for a recession."
- Moskow: "Both businesses and consumers are described ... as hunkering down" with "the major exception ... light vehicle sales."
- Center of gravity: Business investment and most consumer spending weak, partly offset by autos and housing. Below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 ±8 [Low]**
- Greenspan reading the statement: "the appropriate reallocation of resources to enhance security may restrain advances in productivity for a time."
- Guynn: "contacts note falling productivity, as more resources are diverted to heightened security."
- Center of gravity: Near-term measured productivity restrained, long-term favorable. Modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 53 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Meyer: "financial conditions have improved much less than would have been expected based on the magnitude of the cumulative decline in the federal funds rate."
- Minehan: "spreads and commercial bank credit tightening--suggest financing conditions that may be tougher than the level of real short-term interest rates implies."
- Center of gravity: Easy policy offset by sticky long rates, tighter bank standards and wide-ish spreads, partly counter by strong equities and issuance. Slightly above neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 47 ±10 [Low]**
- Greenspan: "fails seem to be a disruption to the normal market process."
- Poole: "the spreads that would indicate how the market itself is functioning, such as bid-ask spreads, are back pretty much to where they were?"
- Center of gravity: Settlement fails noted but market functioning treated as near normal. At neutral/normal, no dysfunction described by members.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 ±8 [Low]**
- Minehan: "spreads and commercial bank credit tightening ... tougher than the level of real short-term interest rates implies."
- Stern: "decent quality credits can still be accommodated on reasonable terms."
- Center of gravity: Modestly wide/less favorable on Minehan's level language, tempered by Stern. Above neutral, scored from level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Gramlich: "The stock market is surprisingly high ... earnings-price ratio is now virtually the same as the real AAA bond yield ... analysts' earnings forecasts that are still implausibly optimistic."
- Greenspan: "patterns that historically would suggest a rally in a bear market."
- Center of gravity: Participants see equities as rich and vulnerable, a valuation worry. Below neutral on this scale (modestly above fair value).

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Guynn: "the banking and financial sectors are sound and have proved largely resilient."
- Moskow: "the risk of loss in C&I loan portfolios ... with significant exposure to middle market auto suppliers has increased dramatically."
- Center of gravity: System sound with pockets of rising loss risk. Below neutral stress (bright-line satisfied).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 ±8 [Low]**
- Ferguson: "Several forward-looking financial markets ... and perhaps even the level of the U.S. dollar--attest to a market expectation of a rapid rebound."
- Gramlich: "a drop in interest rates doesn't necessarily lower domestic currency values."
- Center of gravity: Only passing mentions implying a firm dollar. Slightly above neutral, low confidence.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A** (not discussed by members).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 ±5 [High]**
- Greenspan: "I come out in favor of 50 basis points ... and to continue in any case a bias toward economic weakness."
- Broaddus: "What we need to do now is to preempt disinflation ... fairly aggressively"; Meyer: "policy ... set to err on the side of stimulus."
- Center of gravity: Strong dovish, large cut with easing bias; the smaller-move camp (Poole, Hoenig, Minehan, Guynn, Jordan) pulls slightly up from the floor. Direction: well below the midpoint.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Poole: "I can support the recommendation, although I certainly came into this meeting favoring a move of 25 basis points ... I'm not going to dissent."
- Hoenig: "I would be comfortable with no change or a 25 basis point change today ... I will certainly follow your lead."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous vote, but about five reluctant supporters disagree on size and expectations; the outlook itself is broadly shared. Moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Greenspan and Poole open on Treasury fails and market-functioning spreads; Meyer on financial conditions pass-through "failure of the declines in short-term interest rates to pass through."
- Gramlich on equity valuations and the stock market's fragility.
- Center of gravity: Several members address financial conditions and markets meaningfully, but as context for the economy and policy, not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 17 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Greenspan: "the expectation that we will do another 25 basis points will be built into the market. I'm not sure that's all bad."
- McDonough: "a 50 basis point cut, with the balance of risks still toward inadequate growth."
- Center of gravity: Easing bias retained and more ease expected, with a few members worried about built-in expectations and Guynn urging future nimbleness. Mild-to-strong easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 ±7 [High]**
- Moskow: "We're in an unprecedented situation and uncertainty is very high."
- Meyer: "two-states-of-the-world problem ... we would be hard pressed to parameterize the downside risks"; Jordan: "it's not much easier today ... to know what to make of either the economic reports or the anecdotal information."
- Center of gravity: Many participants emphasize deep uncertainty, unprecedented shocks and models that cannot capture the situation. High.
