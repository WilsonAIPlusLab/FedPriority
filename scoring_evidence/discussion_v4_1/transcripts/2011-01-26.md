## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document handling: not special-topic. The file holds an economic go-round, a policy go-round on alternatives B/C, and a closing vote read-out (unanimous Yes, 11 of 11). The organizational items, the structural-unemployment briefing discussion, the Desk/financial-conditions Q&A and the closing communications/ethics discussion were not used for condition dimensions except where members stated views of conditions. Staff answers are missing from the input.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-6 (High)**
- Moore: "core PCE inflation to remain at or a bit less than 1 percent both this year and next"; Pianalto: "inflation clearly remains very low"; Rosengren: "Inflation remains well below my inflation target"; Yellen: "notably below the 2 percent level"; Dudley: "underlying pressures outside of commodities are still very subdued."
- Offsets: Plosser "I see the risks clearly to the upside", Lacker "inflation outlook is also firmer", Fisher "inflation is at an inflection point"; Bernanke's summary says core "remain[s] quite subdued" and "essentially no domestically generated inflation." Level below target, commodity and import-cost pressure rising.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-5 (High)**
- Evans: "substantial slack in labor markets"; Moore: "significant slack in labor and goods markets"; Rosengren: "output well below potential through 2012"; Dudley: "so far away from our dual mandate"; Raskin: "considerable slack remains." Gap persisting though growth is 3.5-4%.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 21 +-5 (High)**
- Lacker: "We're at 9½ percent unemployment"; Rosengren: "at least four years before we reach full employment" and "too few jobs, not too few skilled workers"; Yellen: "the labor market remains quite weak"; Bernanke: "employment-to-population ratio... less than 58 percent". Minor offsets (skills-mismatch anecdotes from Lockhart, "signs of labor shortages in North and South Dakota" from Kocherlakota) do not change the level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-6 (High)**
- Bernanke: "Consumption spending has been relatively robust recently, and investment in equipment and software and exports have also been relatively strong"; Lacker: "Consumer spending growth has picked up"; Bullard: "Holiday retail sales were quite robust."
- Offsets: Yellen: housing and nonresidential construction "mired in gloom"; Bernanke: "consumption fundamentals still remain somewhat weak... labor income has been quite anemic"; Raskin: "consumer confidence has [not] returned"; Moore: housing "moribund"; state and local fiscal drag. Net near trend, slightly below.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 56 +-8 (Low)**
- Lockhart: "gains in labor productivity have not been exhausted and will continue to take priority over hiring"; Bernanke: "Much investment is aimed at productivity enhancement"; Fisher: investments "earmarked to drive productivity." Indirect, no explicit growth-rate assessment.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-6 (High)**
- Bernanke: "Financial conditions remain supportive"; Warsh: "improvements in financial markets, which continue to be highly supportive of the real economy"; Fisher: "there is more than enough liquidity in the system... flush with funds"; Yellen: program "had a modest but positive impact on financial conditions." Offset: credit "tight for smaller firms" (Bernanke summary, Duke, Tarullo).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Warsh: yield-curve steepness (30y-2y at 398 bp, widest in history) "is probably mostly about good economic developments"; he worries about sovereign funding costs and term premia, but describes no liquidity or auction problems. Orderly yield movement only.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 44 +-10 (Low)**
- No stated level of US spreads. Direction: Bernanke: "the decline in credit spreads" (as part of an easing pattern). Availability, which carries the weight under V4.1.1: Duke: "Large companies have credit; small companies have not"; Fisher: franchises "able to raise capital now"; Tarullo: credit "readily available but perhaps so abundant and cheap" in one segment with "some frothiness" in leveraged loans, and scarce for small businesses. Mixed availability; the score rests on direction plus availability, hence Low and wide band. Euro-area sovereign/bank spreads (Kocherlakota, Rosengren CDS) not used for US spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-6 (Low)**
- Price move only, no valuation view: Fisher: "the U.S. stock market has done brilliantly"; Bullard: program "contributed to a rally in equity markets"; Bernanke: "the increase in equity markets, the decline in equity volatility." Default side-of-move reading.

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 +-6 (High)**
- Duke: "Credit quality continues to improve, with some banks showing quite dramatic improvement"; Lacker: banking contacts "reporting improvements in loan quality"; Bernanke: "U.S. banks have had better asset quality and are making more loans, but they face problems, including servicing and mortgage documentation issues, loss of fee income." Concerns: Raskin on loss reserve adequacy; Hoenig on ag-loan concentration; European banks as a transmission channel.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 32 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Bullard: program "contributed to downward pressure on the trade-weighted value of the dollar"; Bernanke: "the decline in the dollar"; Hoenig: "lower exchange rates"; Yellen: fears of "an outsized decline in the dollar... have not materialized" (a modest decline, not a collapse).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58 (value only)**
- Fisher: retailers "budgeting for 8 percent increases on the apparel side"; Plosser: floor coverings "5 percent price increase", baking prices up; Bernanke: inflation "from commodity prices and from import prices." Offset: Rosengren: "price declines... motor vehicles, household appliances, and apparel"; Kocherlakota/Yellen: firms have "little or no pricing power."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** No member discusses services prices.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Members discuss house-price and foreclosure conditions, not rents or shelter inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 (value only)**
- Bernanke: "Overall inflation expectations seem to have normalized close to the mandate-consistent level"; Dudley: five-year, five-year forward TIPS "at acceptable levels"; Raskin: "Inflation expectations have remained anchored"; Lacker: near-term measures "moved up by roughly a percentage point since the summer."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- The meeting continues the $600 billion LSAP program with "extended period" language unchanged (alternative B), supported by Bernanke ("I would advocate maintaining the status quo"), Dudley ("patience is appropriate"), Yellen ("bar for not completing... should be high"), Rosengren ("expect to complete the entire purchase program"), Evans ("aggressive monetary policy accommodation"). Hawkish tilt from a sizeable minority: Lacker ("at the March meeting... seriously going to want to consider scaling down"), Hoenig ("begin to remove policy accommodation slowly"), Plosser ("begin removing policy accommodation considerably sooner"), Kocherlakota ("tightening by the end of 2011"), Fisher ("eager to normalize"). No one proposes tightening now. QE-era anchor plus a growing exit-planning minority.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Zero dissents; unanimous 11-0 vote. Real disagreement short of dissent: Plosser and Fisher state they "would have not supported QE2" but vote Yes; Lacker and Hoenig (non-voters) doubt the program's contribution ("I seriously doubt that our asset purchases have made a material contribution") against Bullard/Evans/Bernanke who credit it; debate on slack versus growth rates as an inflation guide; debate over inflation-target wording and the $80 billion pace (straw vote of 10 to drop it, Bullard objecting).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-8 (High)**
- Hoenig: extended farmland-value presentation, "problems we may be buying across a number of asset classes"; Tarullo: "early signs of some frothiness", leveraged loans, ETFs, tools "far preferable to monetary policy"; Rosengren: money market funds and Spanish bank CDS; Dudley: Europe and state/local finances; Warsh: euro-area refinancing and sovereign funding costs; Lockhart: contagion exposure; Bernanke summary includes a financial-stability paragraph. Several members address it at length alongside the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Statement keeps "exceptionally low... for an extended period"; Bernanke: "a bit of patience is probably a good idea"; Warsh: "overcompensate in March" with an upbeat paragraph while "keeping first paragraph of B." Lean toward hawkish: Plosser and Hoenig want exit-language changes; Kocherlakota asks for staff analysis by April on dropping "extended period." Guidance still easing-leaning with exit preparation emerging.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 46 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Kocherlakota: "considerable range and a considerable amount of uncertainty" on natural rate; Lacker: estimates from "4¼ percentage points to 0.6 percentage point away"; Evans: "need to see several more months of consistently better data"; Fisher: "I could be too optimistic"; Kocherlakota's three scenarios ("monetary policy is an exercise in contingency planning"); Europe and housing risks. Risks described as "more balanced"; uncertainty is meaningful but not extreme.
