# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1998-03-31

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening procedure, members' questions to staff (Desk, Greenbook, productivity, dollar, oil pass-through), the economic go-around (Jordan, Parry, Moskow, Boehne, Guynn, Hoenig, Minehan, McTeer, Broaddus, Stern, Poole, Rivlin, Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips), the Chairman's summary and policy recommendation, the policy go-around, and a partial roll-call read-out at the end. Staff turns are removed, so some members' questions appear without the answers. Staff content is ignored.

**Era context.** Spring 1998: very strong expansion with low unemployment, soaring equity prices, low measured inflation helped by oil, import prices and methodological changes, and an Asian crisis whose effects have so far been small. The text drives the scores, not this backdrop.

**Speakers and views.**
- Nearly everyone reports a very strong domestic economy and very tight labor markets, with the Asian drag so far limited. McTeer (oil, Texas) is the exception.
- Inflation is low now (Greenspan: "eerily calm"; Parry: core about 2 percent). Most see risks tilted to the upside from tight labor markets and the possible unwinding of temporary factors (oil, dollar, benefit costs, imports).
- Financial conditions are described as stimulative: Minehan ("extremely accommodative"), Ferguson ("very stimulative"), Meyer, Phillips. Risk spreads are narrow (Ferguson, Greenspan).
- Equities are seen as overvalued (McDonough, Phillips, Greenspan).
- Speculative real estate and slack underwriting are discussed by Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig, McTeer, Minehan and Broaddus.
- Policy: the Chairman recommends no change but a return to an asymmetric (tightening) directive. Most support him. Jordan wanted a 25bp hike now and voted No. Broaddus (not shown in the roll call) also preferred action today. Minehan came prepared to vote for a hike and to dissent if asymmetry was not adopted, but then supported the proposal. Hoenig would accept the proposal but feels pressure to act soon. Gramlich would have supported a hike. Phillips and Kelley say it is not yet time. A long side-discussion covers how and when to announce the asymmetry (policy speech vs. immediate release).

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see measured inflation as low and near or slightly below the objective ("very little evidence of inflation"; "core inflation around 2 percent; further modest progress toward price stability"). Cost pressures are building and the risk is on the upside, but the score reflects current conditions, so slightly below 50: 44.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Poole: "bumping along ... above and beyond capacity"; Meyer: "beyond the point of long-run sustainable capacity"; Moskow: "currently high resource utilization rates"; Greenspan: "essentially unsustainable". Countering this, NAPM shows no shortages and there is "no evidence of tightness in facilities". The bright line requires above 50: 60.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Described by almost every speaker as "very tight", "exceptional tightness", "extremely tight", with 10 percent job openings in Dayton and wage anecdotes. The unemployment rate is not stated; the language puts it well above 50: 74.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Broaddus: consumer spending "going great guns"; Kelley: "tremendous momentum"; Jordan: "spending has been very robust, business fixed investment on a sharp uptrend"; Greenspan: "extraordinary domestic demand". Net exports are a drag, which tempers the score: 70.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Anecdotes from Boehne, Guynn, Hoenig and Stern describe strong gains that are not fully captured in statistics. Jordan and Greenspan note the first-quarter measured figure is weak or unreliable. Greenspan: 2.3 percent over four quarters, a modest slowdown. Net perception is somewhat above trend: 60, wide band.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Accommodative and stimulative, so below 50. Minehan: "financial markets, which are extremely accommodative"; Ferguson: "a very stimulative environment"; Meyer: "credit availability is ample ... loan pricing is aggressive": 24.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The only Treasury discussion is Jordan's question on intermediate Treasuries trading through the funds rate (an expectations question, not dysfunction). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is very narrow. Ferguson: "Risk spreads for private debt instruments are quite narrow"; Greenspan: "yield spreads have fallen to unbelievable numbers": 18.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants see equities as stretched. McDonough: "equity markets are overpriced"; Greenspan: low equity premium, unrealistic profit-margin implications, "perceived as overvalued"; Phillips: "may be too good to be true": 22.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banking is not described as stressed. There are concerns about lowered credit standards, "shoddy underwriting by syndicators" (Jordan), the "slack attitude of lending officers" (Greenspan) and speculative real estate lending. This is the minor-concerns zone, below 50. The Japanese bank discussion (Greenspan, McDonough, in staff Q&A) adds a little: 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciation noted. Greenspan: "particularly strong trade-weighted dollar" in recent days; Moskow: "dollar's recent appreciation"; McTeer: "strong dollar". Modest strength, above 50: 60.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: falling import prices, weak commodities, lower oil, "core commodities" inflation low (Parry, Phillips, Greenspan). Score 32.
- SERVICES: Minehan cites "selected price increases in service industries" and wage and benefit increases of about 4 percent "particularly in services". Phillips notes the spread between core commodities and services inflation. Score 58.
- HOUSING: Jordan reports "home prices are rising very rapidly" and rising commercial rents; others report housing strength and speculative building. This is asset/house-price and commercial rent talk rather than shelter CPI. Score 60, discussed but indirect.
- EXPECTATIONS: Hoenig cites "bond markets signaling benign inflation expectations"; Ferguson cites "low inflation expectations". Score 42.

**Tier 3 check.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Hold at the meeting, but the Chairman and a large majority adopt an explicit tightening bias. Several would act now (Jordan dissent, Broaddus, Minehan prepared, Gramlich willing), and Hoenig, Poole and Moskow urge pre-emption. This is the "hold with tightening bias" band pushed to its upper end by the number who wanted to move: 63.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Jordan, wanting a 25bp hike now). Broaddus (non-voter) and Minehan (who held back from dissenting) leaned the same way. The disagreement is about timing and the announcement procedure, not about economics. The assessment is largely shared. 31.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Speculation in real estate and equities, lax underwriting, narrow spreads, overvaluation and "vulnerability" are raised by about a third of the members and are central to Greenspan's closing assessment and Minehan's and Jordan's cases for acting. A prominent but not dominant theme next to the growth/labor/inflation focus: 50.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Clear tightening signal. Greenspan: "we are running out of time ... the clock is starting to tick"; McDonough: "we will not hesitate to move"; Boehne: "we will have to ... make a policy adjustment and probably more than once". Much discussion of a speech to prepare markets. The signal is not public through the directive, and timing depends on data (Asia, stocks): 72.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Mostly confident descriptions of strength. Uncertainty is about productivity measurement, the persistence of supply shocks, Asia and the stock market, but most speakers do not stress deep uncertainty. Greenspan says there is "too little uncertainty in this system". Moderate: 36.

**Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; evidence says accommodative; low score matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; narrow spreads mean low. EQUITY_VALUATION (higher means more stress/depressed; overvaluation is low): matches. BANKING_STRESS: lax standards and healthy banks mean low. DOLLAR: appreciation means above 50. OUTPUT_GAP: above potential means above 50.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP 60 (>50), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 24 (<50), CREDIT_SPREADS 18 (<50), BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50), DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 (>50), TREASURY_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 60 vs LABOR 74: a 14-point gap, within the 15-point limit. The staff-led view that goods markets show no shortages keeps the output gap below labor. OUTPUT_GAP 60 vs CONSUMPTION 70: a 10-point gap, acceptable. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 24 and CREDIT_SPREADS 18 co-move (both loose). Treasury stress is N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 against sub-components (goods 32, services 58, housing 60, expectations 42): the average is about 48 and members stress that current inflation is low, so the parent is consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning discussion by members, only a yield-curve expectations question.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 44 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "We have very little evidence of inflation"; pressures on product prices "eerily calm"; Parry: "core price inflation probably should come in at a rate of around 2 percent." Center of gravity: inflation is low now but with upside risk. Direction: slightly below neutral because measured inflation is low and members say it has not accelerated.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 60 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Poole: "Economic activity is bumping along ... above and beyond capacity"; Meyer: "initial point beyond sustainable capacity". Center: the economy is running above sustainable capacity. Direction: above neutral because members describe operation beyond capacity.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 74 ±6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan: "exceptional tightness in labor markets ... getting tighter"; Phillips: "The labor market remains extremely tight." Center: near-universal agreement on very tight labor. Direction: above neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 70 ±7 High.** Evidence: Broaddus: consumer spending "going great guns"; Kelley: "tremendous amount of momentum. Consumer confidence is sky high." Center: strong, wealth-effect-driven demand, partly offset by net exports. Direction: well above trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 60 ±10 Moderate.** Evidence: Boehne: "one story after another ... the end is nowhere in sight"; Jordan: measured first-quarter productivity "shocking ... a residual". Center: strong gains seen in anecdotes, measured data weaker. Direction: above trend.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 24 ±6 High.** Evidence: Minehan: "financial markets, which are extremely accommodative"; Ferguson: "a very stimulative environment from a monetary and financial standpoint." Center: loose, supportive conditions. Direction: below neutral (looser).
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 18 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson: "Risk spreads for private debt instruments are quite narrow"; Greenspan: "yield spreads have fallen to unbelievable numbers." Center: spreads at compressed levels. Direction: below neutral (low stress).
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 22 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: McDonough: "The equity markets are overpriced in my view"; Greenspan: equity premium "quite low relative to any recent period". Center: overvaluation concern. Direction: below neutral (overvalued end).
- **BANKING_STRESS 38 ±10 Low.** Evidence: Jordan: bankers cited "shoddy underwriting by syndicators"; Greenspan: "the slack attitude of lending officers has become a potential concern." Center: banks sound with lax standards. Direction: below neutral. Low confidence because the comments are indirect.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 ±8 Low.** Evidence: Greenspan: "a particularly strong trade-weighted dollar"; Moskow: "the dollar's recent appreciation". Center: modest appreciation noted mostly as a disinflationary factor. Direction: above neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 32; INFLATION_SERVICES 58; INFLATION_HOUSING 60; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 42.** Evidence: Phillips: "spread between core commodities inflation and services inflation does remain"; Minehan: "selected price increases in service industries"; Jordan: "Home prices are rising very rapidly"; Hoenig: "bond markets that are signaling benign inflation expectations".
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 63 ±6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan: "I would recommend and put 'B' asymmetric on the table"; Jordan: "A 1/4 percentage point increase in the federal funds rate now would deliver a very important message." Center: hold plus tightening bias with a sizable minority preferring action now. Direction: above neutral.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 31 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Jordan voted No in the roll call; Minehan: "prepared to dissent if we did not at least move to asymmetry"; Broaddus: "I would still prefer to take action today." Center: one dissent on timing with shared economic assessments. Direction: moderate-low disagreement.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 50 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig: condominiums "people were standing in line to buy"; Greenspan: "the vulnerability of the economic system is far greater". Center: speculation and asset valuation prominent but secondary to growth/labor/inflation. Direction: moderate attention.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 72 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "I think the clock is starting to tick"; McDonough: "we will not hesitate to move". Center: explicit tightening bias and preparation of markets. Direction: tightening.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 36 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry: "it is uncertain in my view whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure"; Greenspan: "There is too little uncertainty in this system." Center: ordinary caveats about Asia, productivity and stocks, with confident views about near-term strength. Direction: somewhat elevated but not high.
