# v4.1 Discussion Scoring: meeting of 1996-07-03 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript for a two-day meeting. It has a short Q&A with staff, a Reserve Bank and Board economic go-around, a long long-run inflation-goal discussion, the long-term M2/M3 range decision, the policy discussion and directive vote, and a swaps/intervention discussion. Staff turns are removed, so many questions appear without their answers. Two unlabeled roll calls close the file. Some PDF garbles are present (for example "who comes from", "Texas excess spent more"). None of them changes the content scored.

**Era context.** Mid-1996. Growth is strong, unemployment is about 5.5%, and measured core inflation is flat or falling. I scored only what participants say.

**Speakers.** Greenspan, McDonough, Boehne, Jordan, Kelley, Lindsey, McTeer, Meyer, Phillips, Rivlin, Stern and Yellen are the voters. Broaddus, Parry, Melzer, Minehan, Hoenig, Moskow and Guynn are the non-voters. Most of the economic content is in the go-around and in the policy round.

**Core dimensions, direction stated before the number.**

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE.** Participants see inflation as near the middle of the scale.
  - CPI is about 3% but is described as biased upward. Core PCE is about 2%.
  - Several members say core inflation is "well contained" (Yellen) and falling (Greenspan, Meyer, Kelley).
  - Others (Parry, Melzer, Minehan, Stern) see the risk of a rise. Hawks cite wage pressures, steel, and food and corn.
  - Current pressure is therefore a little below to near the midpoint, and the risks are on the up side. Score about 47.
- **OUTPUT_GAP.** Q2 growth is 4% or more, unemployment is "admittedly below" staff NAIRU (Meyer), and growth is "above trend".
  - Parry says the economy is "stretching beyond the full utilization of its resources".
  - Kelley notes that capacity utilization is flat at about 82%.
  - Meyer says utilization is high but "not definitively" overheating.
  - Modestly above potential. The bright line requires a score above 50, so I score 55.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Labor markets are described as tight by many (Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Melzer, Stern, Hoenig).
  - Wisconsin unemployment is 3.6%, and seven large states are below 5%.
  - National unemployment is about 5.5%, which is near or slightly below NAIRU.
  - The UE anchor says about 45 and the participants' language says tight, so I score 53.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.** Spending is above trend.
  - Q2 growth is 4% or more.
  - Light vehicle sales are at the best pace since 1988 (Melzer).
  - Housing is strong, and business fixed investment is rising at a double-digit rate (Phillips).
  - Participants expect a slowdown, but the current level is strong. Score 62.
- **PRODUCTIVITY.** The measured macro figure is about 1% (Jordan, McDonough, Greenspan). Anecdotes of strong micro gains conflict with it.
  - Modestly below trend as measured, with measurement doubts. Score 44 with a wide band.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.** Conditions are loose.
  - Stern: "generally permissive. Credit is readily available on attractive terms."
  - Lindsey: credit "much easier to get". Minehan: "almost any project can get financed".
  - Phillips: the cost of equity capital is "quite favorable".
  - Long rates are up, but members still describe supportive conditions. The bright line requires a score below 50, so I score 37.
- **TREASURY_STRESS.** N/A. There are no comments on market functioning. Jordan's bond-market question concerns yields only.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS.** Spreads are not quoted.
  - Credit availability is described as ample and competition as intense (Minehan, Lindsey, Stern).
  - Delinquencies are rising, but that is credit quality and not a spread level.
  - By the level reading, availability points below normal. Score 33 with low confidence.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION.** Valuation concern is present.
  - Minehan reports the IPO market is "frothy".
  - Hoenig speaks of "artificiality" in some markets.
  - Meyer notes a mild correction is built into the staff forecast.
  - Stern cites wealth effects from the "run-up in stock prices".
  - Modest to moderate valuation concern. Score 33.
- **BANKING_STRESS.** Banks are healthy with some pockets of concern.
  - Lindsey: banks "well reserved" and credit problems will not affect "the integrity of the banking system".
  - McTeer: ag banks "pretty good". Phillips: banking markets are "well positioned".
  - Minehan says bank loan growth is subdued by merger restructuring.
  - Members raise Mexico's banks and consumer delinquencies as pockets of stress. Below 50, score 35.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** Yellen cites "a somewhat stronger dollar" and Meyer "the appreciation of the dollar". The dollar is modestly stronger, so by the bright line the score is above 50. Score 58.

**Sub-components.** Goods, services and housing price pressures are not isolated by members, so they are N/A. Inflation expectations are discussed with mixed views:
- Yellen: long-term expectations are "quite positive".
- Phillips: "inflation psychology has lessened".
- Melzer: embedding in expectations is a "growing risk".
- Stern: a "significant inflation premium" remains in long rates.
- Net, anchored and near the midpoint. Score 50.

**Tier 3, in words first.**

- **Lean.** The Committee holds the funds rate and adopts an asymmetric directive toward firming.
  - Broaddus (50bp), Parry (25 to 50bp), Melzer (50bp), Hoenig and Minehan (move now) are non-voters who favor tightening immediately.
  - Stern, a voter, wants 25bp now.
  - Greenspan, McDonough, Meyer, Rivlin, Yellen, Boehne, Kelley, McTeer and Jordan prefer to hold. Guynn, a non-voter, prefers to hold.
  - This is a hold with a tightening bias and a strong minority for moving now. Score 60.
- **Dissent.** There are two roll calls, and the read-out does not label them. I infer by order that the first is the M2/M3 ranges (No: Lindsey, Yellen) and the second is the directive (No: Stern).
  - The inference is supported by Yellen's statement that she has dissented on the range issue before, by Lindsey's argument for a technical adjustment, and by Stern's stated preference for a 25bp hike.
  - The policy directive therefore has one hawkish dissent, plus several non-voters who would have moved now. There was also open debate about the inflation goal, the measure to use and opportunism versus deliberate disinflation.
  - Moderate differences with distinct camps. Score 43.
- **Financial stability.** Lindsey gives a long account of consumer debt, delinquencies, loan-to-value ratios and household fragility. Jordan raises rising corporate financial stress, and Minehan and Hoenig flag froth. Most other members do not discuss it.
  - A limited but real discussion by a few members, in an era where the typical anchor is 1 to 10. Score 27.
- **Forward guidance.** Asymmetric toward firming. Greenspan thinks it likely the Committee moves in August or later and favors a phone call before any intermeeting move. Several members decline to treat it as a commitment (Boehne, Jordan, Guynn).
  - Mild to moderate tightening bias. Score 66.
- **Uncertainty.** Greenspan says "something disturbingly wrong with this picture". McDonough speaks of "very considerable uncertainty". Yellen: "a period of great uncertainty". Rivlin: "none of us really knows what is happening". Boehne: "we do not know how much different the future will be".
  - Several emphasize unusual uncertainty about the model. Score 66.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 (55), consistent with "above trend". FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (37), consistent with "permissive/favorable". CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 (33) under the level reading. BANKING_STRESS below 50 (35), consistent with "well reserved". DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (58), consistent with "stronger dollar". TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure, and the evidence says contained with up-side risk, so just below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter, and evidence says tight, so above 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and evidence says loose, so below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and evidence says abundant credit, so below 50. Higher EQUITY_VALUATION means more concern, and the froth comments point to modest concern.

**Coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53) are within 15 points.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (55) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (62) differ by 7.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (37) and CREDIT_SPREADS (33) co-move.
4. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (47) is consistent with the one sub-component scored, expectations (50).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: market functioning not raised. INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: members describe the aggregate and specific items (steel, corn, food, energy) but do not characterize each category.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 47 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Core inflation in the CPI and PPI remains well contained". Parry, "substantial risk that underlying inflation... is set on a gradual upward trend".
- Summary: current readings are contained or falling, with up-side risk.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Parry, the economy would be "stretching beyond the full utilization of its resources". Meyer, growth "significantly above trend" in Q2 and unemployment "below the staff estimate of NAIRU".
- Summary: modestly above potential.
- Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "labor markets in the District have tightened further". Moskow, Wisconsin unemployment "3.6 percent".
- Summary: tight in anecdotes, with national unemployment near 5.5%, around NAIRU.
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Melzer, "light trucks and autos are selling at a pace unmatched since 1988". Phillips, business fixed investment "double-digit rate of increase".
- Summary: spending is above trend, though a slowdown is expected.
- Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 44 +-10 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Jordan, productivity "stuck at 1 percent or 1.1 percent". Greenspan, anecdotal "significant acceleration" in manufacturing but macro data show 1%.
- Summary: measured growth is modestly below trend, with strong micro anecdotes.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 37 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Stern, "financial conditions seem to be... generally permissive. Credit is readily available on attractive terms." Minehan, "almost any project can get financed".
- Summary: loose and supportive.
- Direction: below neutral (tight = above).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-10 (Low).**
- Evidence: Lindsey, "Credit is much easier to get than it was 18 months ago". Minehan, "keen competition continue[s]".
- Summary: abundant credit points to below-normal spreads, but no spread levels are given.
- Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-10 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Minehan, the IPO market "has gotten frothy". Hoenig, "a certain degree of artificiality".
- Summary: modest valuation concern.
- Direction: above fair value, so below 50 on the scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Lindsey, "the banks are well reserved for any reasonably expected losses". McTeer, "The condition of the ag banks is pretty good".
- Summary: healthy with pockets of concern (consumer credit, Mexico).
- Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "a somewhat stronger dollar". Meyer, "the appreciation of the dollar".
- Summary: modest appreciation noted in passing.
- Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.**
- Evidence: Yellen, "long-term inflationary expectations also are quite positive". Melzer, "embedded in expectations, which we see as a growing risk".
- Summary: broadly anchored, with some worry.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "accepting alternative 'B'... would nonetheless accept an asymmetric bias toward tightening". Broaddus, "a solid case can be made for an increase of 50 basis points".
- Summary: hold with a tightening bias, and a strong minority wanting to move now.
- Direction: hawkish side of neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 43 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, Stern No on the directive roll call and Lindsey and Yellen No on the range roll call (by inference from order). Parry, Broaddus, Melzer and Hoenig would move now, but two thirds of the speakers accept holding.
- Summary: one policy dissent, two on ranges, camps on timing, and debate about the inflation goal.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 27 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Lindsey, "the price we are paying is the increasing fragility of the underlying financial structure of the household sector". Jordan, "more financial stresses in the economy than seem to exist today".
- Summary: one extended presentation and a few other mentions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: McDonough, "an asymmetric directive toward firming is appropriate". Minehan, "set the groundwork for a move in August".
- Summary: a tightening bias, with the August meeting as a likely decision point.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 +-8 (High).**
- Evidence: McDonough, "a period of very considerable uncertainty". Rivlin, "none of us really knows what is happening".
- Summary: many members stress that the model and the NAIRU are in question.
