# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1994-12-20 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It holds the Chair's opening procedure, members' questions during the staff Q&A, the go-around on economic conditions (district reports plus national views), the policy go-around, the Chair's summary and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns have been removed. Questions to the staff, procedure and staff-model numbers quoted back (for example the "staff model" simulations) are not scored. Where members report their own bank's model or forecast, that counts as a participant view.

**2. Era context.** Late 1994, after a year of tightening. Members refer to the November 15 move of 75bp, the funds rate at 5-1/2 percent, and a total of 250bp of tightening. Growth is surprisingly strong, with Q4 GDP seen near 5 percent. Inflation data are still benign. Many members expect further tightening. The backdrop is only context. Every score below comes from what members say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish and early-move camp: LaWare (wants alternative C, symmetric, "without delay"), Broaddus (wants +25bp now), Hoenig (wants action now), Parry (prefers +50bp but supports B), Minehan (not whether but when, asymmetric B).
- Wait, with asymmetric tightening bias: McDonough, Melzer, Moskow, Kelley, Phillips, McTeer, Greenspan.
- Wait, with a symmetric directive: Blinder, Lindsey, Yellen, Stern, Forrestal.
- Jordan: skeptical of forced slowing, would not dissent against asymmetric. Lindsey adds fiscal commentary. Kelley and Lindsey flag consumer debt.
- Almost all describe a very strong economy, with risks heavily on the upside for growth and inflation, and with final-goods inflation still contained.

**4. Directional signals, stated before scoring.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Measured inflation is contained: Jordan cites CEOs assuming zero price increases, McTeer reports retail selling prices below a year ago, LaWare cites widespread discounting. But input and intermediate costs are rising (Moskow, Stern, Minehan, Forrestal), CPI is seen near 3.2-3.3 and rising (Forrestal, McDonough), and many fear an acceleration (Stern, Moskow, Broaddus, Parry). The current level is the "somewhat elevated" kind, so the score sits at 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Hoenig: economy "beyond long-term potential" and "perhaps even an overheating economy". Moskow: GDP "slightly above potential". LaWare: "well above potential". Blinder: "pushed a bit past the NAIRU", and he stresses the overshoot is small and recent. Bright line requires more than 50. Net 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Tight, with widespread shortages (Hoenig, Moskow, Broaddus, Stern, Forrestal, Jordan). Michigan unemployment is the lowest since 1969 (Moskow), Eighth District is at 4.7 percent (Melzer), and Jordan reports Fourth District metro areas at 4 percent or less. The Chair calls it a "very rapidly tightening labor market". Wages are not responding much (Greenspan, Phillips, Jordan). Phillips gives the national rate as 5.6 percent, and Blinder says the economy is only a little past the NAIRU, which tempers the score. The 5.6 percent national rate would point toward the 40s on the objective anchor, while the participants' description is of a market tighter than the NAIRU. I weight the participants' description and the district levels, which gives 60.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend. Q4 growth near 5 percent, very strong holiday sales, a record for heavy trucks, capex expansions, strong orders (Greenspan), and "the economy is clearly very strong; it's really roaring" (Broaddus). Offsets are softening single-family housing (Stern, Forrestal, Broaddus), the auto slowdown, and worries about consumer debt (Kelley, Lindsey, Phillips). Net 66.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Phillips mentions a "productivity kick" from re-engineering and technology. Kelley mentions "much higher productivity". Greenspan says productivity is positive and contains unit costs. Forrestal and Jordan raise the possibility of higher potential growth. The tone is above trend but speculative. Score 58.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed, leaning accommodative. Greenspan describes "an easing of credit terms in the banking sector" and "continued ease in bank lending terms". Phillips says banks are now willing to lend. McTeer cites "credit availability". Offsets are higher long rates, a falling stock market, and real rates "not low by historical standards" (Yellen, McDonough). Bright line: "easing credit" and "supportive" language means less than 50. Score 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as a Treasury functioning issue by members. Thin year-end markets are mentioned in passing (McDonough, Phillips), and the bond rally is described as "heartening" (Melzer) and a credibility gain (Broaddus). Yield moves alone are not dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Parry gives a level. Municipal spreads that rose after the Orange County bankruptcy "generally have fallen back to the levels that prevailed before". California state spreads are still higher than before. Bank lending terms are easing (Greenspan). The level is about normal, with a pocket of widening, so 48 with Low confidence. The bright-line rule is satisfied because no "wide" or "compressed" level is stated for the broad market.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Yellen mentions "a decline in the stock market, which will bring wealth effects". Phillips calls it an "orderly correction", and mentions year-end mutual fund statements. No valuation view. Default range, 52, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Parry says exposure of Twelfth District banks to Orange County is "minimal". Phillips says the credit crunch is over and banks are willing to lend. Greenspan is concerned about ease in lending terms, not stress. Bankers Trust is a worry for markets but not for system soundness. Healthy, so below 50: 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen: "for once the dollar has been appreciating". Lindsey says the November move took pressure off the foreign exchange market. Brief. Appreciating means above 50: 58, Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components. Goods: input and materials costs up (paper, cardboard, steel, fertilizer), but final goods prices contained and heavy discounting; 54. Services: not discussed as such; N/A. Housing: rents rising in multifamily markets (Forrestal) and commercial rents (Broaddus), but no shelter inflation discussion; N/A. Expectations: members worry about "inflation psychology" (Jordan) and a risk of deterioration (Parry, Greenspan), while the bond market is read as showing credibility; 55.

**5. Polarity verification.** LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means tighter; the evidence says shortages, so above 50 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; the evidence says ease in credit terms, so below 50 matches. BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress; the evidence says minimal exposure and willing lending, so below 50 matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; the evidence says normal or back to pre-event levels, so about 50 and slightly below matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds at 5-1/2 percent, which is alternative B, with an asymmetric directive toward tightening. A consensus holds that more tightening is coming (Broaddus: "very high probability"; Moskow: "almost inevitable"; Kelley: "overwhelmingly likely"). A substantial camp wants action now (LaWare, Broaddus, Hoenig, and Parry at +50bp). Others stress patience and lags (Blinder, Lindsey, Yellen, Stern, Forrestal). It is a hold with a tightening bias and a hawkish tail, so 60.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (LaWare, wanted C symmetric). Several others preferred a move but voted yes (Broaddus, Parry), and Hoenig, a non-voter, also argued for acting now. The Chair calls the symmetric versus asymmetric split "very sharp", but the Committee shares a view of the economy and the debate is about timing and wording. Score 40.
- Financial stability attention: Orange County, Bankers Trust, thin year-end markets, mark-to-market risks, consumer debt, and credibility in the bond market get real but secondary space; the staff presentations on Orange County are not in the text. Several members touch it (Parry, Phillips, McDonough, LaWare, Kelley, Lindsey). Score 38.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric toward tightening, an expectation of a move "by the middle of January" (Parry, Moskow), and the Chair's view that the odds of moving shortly after the new year are "quite high", with a conference call before any intermeeting move. Some also push for inflation objectives. Score 68.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "great uncertainty", Phillips "considerable uncertainty", Yellen unsure how much tightening is needed, Broaddus notes "considerable risks" and calls the forecast exceedingly difficult, and Greenspan warns against trusting model simulations. Several members say it is hard to read lags and the current strength. Score 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 is above 50 (above potential language). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 is below 50 (easing credit terms). CREDIT_SPREADS 48 is below 50 with no stated high level. BANKING_STRESS 33 is below 50 (minimal exposure, willing to lend). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 is above 50 (appreciating). TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 62 versus LABOR_TIGHTNESS 60: aligned (2 points).
- OUTPUT_GAP 62 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 66: aligned (4 points).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 versus CREDIT_SPREADS 48: both near neutral and slightly loose; consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, which is fine in a normal-market period.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 versus sub-components (goods 54, expectations 55): consistent with a parent slightly above them given the pervasive upside-risk concern.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members did not discuss Treasury market functioning (staff and Desk content removed). INFLATION_SERVICES: no member discussion of services prices as a category. INFLATION_HOUSING: no discussion of shelter inflation; the rent mentions concern real estate activity.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, robust growth "threatens to generate a noticeable increase in CPI inflation later in 1995"; McTeer, "little evidence of price rises on final goods and services". Center of gravity: inflation is contained in the data but pressure is building and the risks are seen as upside. Inflation pressure is above neutral because cost pressures are widespread and most members expect acceleration, though not yet visible.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 62 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Hoenig, "operating beyond long-term potential levels"; LaWare, "well above potential". Center of gravity: the economy is above potential, with only Blinder framing the overshoot as small and recent. The gap is above neutral by bright-line rule and by the language ("overheating" is raised by Hoenig and Broaddus).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "labor shortages across the board, from the skilled to the entry level"; Greenspan, "very rapidly tightening labor market". Center of gravity: shortages are widespread and local unemployment is very low, but wages are not responding and the national rate (5.6 percent per Phillips) keeps it from rising higher. Labor is tighter than neutral because members describe the economy as at or beyond the NAIRU.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 66 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, "the economy is clearly very strong; it's really roaring"; Moskow, holiday sales "running very strong". Center of gravity: demand and capex are well above trend with housing and autos softening at the margin. Spending is above neutral because the central narrative is surprisingly rapid growth.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "productivity kick that we are getting from re-engineering and technology investment"; Kelley, "much higher productivity". Center of gravity: productivity gains are seen as above trend by several members, but the claim is speculative. Above neutral because the comments are positive.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 44 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "an easing of credit terms in the banking sector"; Phillips, "significant willingness by banks to lend". Center of gravity: credit is readily available, offset by higher long rates and a falling stock market. Conditions are below neutral (looser) because bank credit is easy.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed by members as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Parry, municipal spreads "generally have fallen back to the levels that prevailed before"; California state spreads "remain higher". Center of gravity: spreads are back to normal with an isolated pocket of widening. Slightly below neutral, with only one member speaking to levels.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Phillips, "quite orderly correction this year"; Yellen, "a decline in the stock market". Center of gravity: price moves are noted without a valuation view, so the default range applies. At neutral because there is no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, exposure of Twelfth District banks "has been and continues to be minimal"; Phillips, "now we are seeing significant willingness by banks to lend". Center of gravity: banks are healthy and lending, and the concern is loose rather than tight lending standards. Below neutral because the system looks sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Yellen, "for once the dollar has been appreciating". Center of gravity: a brief mention of appreciation, linked to moderating inflation. Above neutral by the direction rule.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 54.** Input prices (paper, cardboard, steel, fertilizer) rising while final goods prices are held down by discounting (LaWare, McTeer). **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Jordan, "inflation psychology"; Parry, further delay "would lead to a deterioration of inflationary expectations"; offset by the view that the bond market shows credibility gains.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Broaddus, "a very high probability that we are going to have to do something more"; LaWare wants to "snub the brakes again without delay". Center of gravity: the Committee holds with a tightening bias, with a sizable minority wanting to move now. Hawkish lean is mild because the majority waits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: the roll call has one "No" (LaWare); Greenspan, "a very sharp split on symmetric versus asymmetric". Center of gravity: one formal dissent plus several reservations on both sides, with shared views on the economy. Moderate differences on timing and wording.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "I don't think we have yet seen all of the implications of the Bankers Trust situation"; Parry on Orange County exposures. Center of gravity: several members address it as a risk to timing but it is not the focus. Limited to moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, odds of a move "no later than our next meeting, are quite high"; Moskow, "further tightening is almost inevitable". Center of gravity: the asymmetric directive and open talk of a January move signal tightening ahead. A clear tightening bias without a commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "great uncertainty about where the economy is going"; Phillips, "considerable uncertainty as we go into 1995". Center of gravity: several members stress uncertainty about the strength, the lags and the stance, while others speak with conviction on direction. Elevated but not extreme.
