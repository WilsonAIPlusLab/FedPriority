## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** A member/participant conference-call transcript, read to the last line (line 1277, "END OF SESSION"). There is no `=== Vote read-out ===` block and no roll call anywhere in the file. The Chairman closes with "this has been an hour and a half meeting" and "I look forward to seeing you all on Tuesday."

**Era context.** Early transcript format (1993). The call is a preparation session for a congressional hearing the following Tuesday. The Chairman says his testimony the previous Wednesday "went very well" and that he came away uneasy about the legislative mood in the House Banking Committee.

**Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, Governors Angell and Lindsey, and Presidents Melzer, Keehn, Syron, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, McTeer, Jordan and Boehne. Mr. Laware and Mr. Mattingly (legal) speak, and Mr. Winn gives logistics. Several unnamed speakers also appear.

**What was discussed.**
- The main item is the disclosure of FOMC transcripts, and the discovery that raw, unedited transcripts have been kept for more than 10 years. Participants discuss FOIA exposure after the Department of Justice changed its stance, and whether to move to a Memorandum of Discussion with a 3-year lag.
- They discuss whether to release or edit past transcripts, whether to destroy transcripts going forward (Melzer), and how to word the Chairman's testimony.
- They discuss the awkwardness of Presidents' written statements saying they kept no notes (Keehn, Parry, Jordan), and Lindsey's objection to retroactively changing the rules.
- Winn gives the hearing logistics: 16 witnesses, the seating plan, transportation, and the likely question on Presidential appointment of Reserve Bank Presidents.
- Winn's only reference to the economy is that members should be ready to answer, "in a paragraph," what is going on in the economy in their area. No member answers it.
