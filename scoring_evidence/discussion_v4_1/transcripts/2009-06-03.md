# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting date 2009-06-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It is a short, unscheduled joint FOMC-Board meeting, not a regular meeting. The text is about 19 KB and has no economic go-around and no policy go-around. Staff turns are removed. What is left is:
- Chairman Bernanke's opening and his closing remarks.
- A procedural vote to approve Brian Sack as SOMA manager.
- Members' questions to the Desk and to the staff.
- Bernanke's assessment of the recent rise in Treasury yields and mortgage rates.
- A longer statement by President Lacker.

This is a special-topic meeting about market developments and communication. It is not an edge-case "all N/A" meeting, because members do assess yields, the dollar, equities, inflation breakevens and the outlook. Coverage is narrow, so confidence is mostly Low to Moderate. I treat it as an anomaly in the flags.

**2. Era context.** The text places the meeting in the 2009 post-crisis period. The Committee is running large-scale asset purchases (a $300 billion Treasury program, plus TALF and MBS programs). Long yields and mortgage rates have risen sharply since the previous meeting. I take the macro backdrop only from what members say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Bernanke (Chair) gives the main assessment.
- Lacker gives a policy and communication argument.
- Dudley (Vice Chair) speaks on CMBS ratings and the TALF and asks about the equilibrium funds rate.
- Kohn, Fisher, Rosengren, Pianalto, Bullard and Lockhart ask questions only.

Questions are mostly procedural or about program mechanics. They are treated as weak evidence of attention, not of views.

**4. Dimension-by-dimension direction statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Bernanke says breakevens "suggest a still well-controlled rate of inflation, pretty close to what our target would be." He ties the rise in breakevens to "reduced fear of deflation." Lacker lists "reduced downside risks to inflation." Participants see pressure at or slightly below the midpoint: near target, with deflation fear receding. I score it slightly below 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP. There is no explicit gap or slack language. There is only "the better outlook" and "improvement of the economy," which imply a recovering economy still in a deficient-demand state. The direction is below potential, inferred. Low confidence. I score it below 50, on the Direction Rule, with a wide band.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Never discussed. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Only "better growth prospects" (Lacker) and "better outlook" (Bernanke). There is no spending or investment content. I score it N/A. The outlook improvement is already captured under OUTPUT_GAP.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Dudley refers to "the easing in financial conditions that we've seen." Bernanke cites "the reversal in the flight to quality." Lacker cites "improving credit market conditions." Against that, yields and mortgage rates have risen "significantly." Conditions are easing from crisis levels, partly offset by higher long rates. No member calls them accommodative or tight. Near neutral, slightly above 50. Low confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS. There was a "Treasury market selloff." On a few days Bernanke saw "a bad combination" that was "indicative of a flight from U.S. debt" and "somewhat worrisome." His overall view is that the move is "mostly a benign development." No liquidity impairment, auction failure or settlement problem is described. Per the gotcha, this is yield movement and not dysfunction, so 45-55.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Spread levels are not discussed. Lacker mentions "improving credit market conditions." Dudley says legacy CMBS sell "at 5 cents on the dollar" in an illustrative example, and S&P CMBS downgrades are pending. That is a direction-only signal (improving) with no level. Per the CREDIT_SPREADS rule it goes at 45-55 on the side of the move, with Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Equity price moves are mentioned only as part of the yields/dollar/equity pattern ("equity prices go up," "a fall in equity prices"). There is no valuation view. Default 45-55.
- BANKING_STRESS. Not discussed. Lockhart's CRE question is about risk to the outlook and does not address banks. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Bernanke: "a decline in the dollar" on the bad days, and "We are seeing the dollar decline and the yields rise at the same time that equity prices go up." The direction is weakening, so the score must be below 50. The magnitude is modest (a decline, "somewhat worrisome").
- Inflation sub-components. Expectations are discussed (breakevens near target, rising from deflation fear). Goods, services and housing are not discussed. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: higher means more above potential. The evidence is a recovering economy still below potential, so the score is below 50. This matches.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher means a stronger dollar. The evidence is a declining dollar, so the score is below 50. This matches.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure. The evidence is "pretty close to" target with deflation fear receding, so the score is a little below 50. This matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean. There is no policy action. Bernanke "would not recommend any change in our policy," to "wait and watch." The asset purchase programs and the near-zero funds rate stay in place. Lacker agrees that the programs should not be altered "right now," and adds that expanding purchases to damp yields "would be pretty clearly the wrong thing to do." He says some reasons for higher yields "might want [us to] consider backing off or reducing the scale of our purchases." Bernanke points to the June presentations on the exit strategy. The stance is maximum accommodation held in place, with no easing bias and some hawkish tilt from Lacker. I score it moderately dovish: it is a QE-era meeting where the answer to "should we expand?" is no.
- Dissent. There is no vote on policy. Only the SOMA manager appointment and the 2010 schedule are approved, without objection. Lacker agrees with the Chair and differs only on emphasis in communication. Near-consensus.
- Financial stability emphasis. The whole meeting is about the market reaction: Treasury yields, mortgage rates, TALF, CMBS ratings, flight from the dollar. Members' attention to financial conditions is high. The coverage of financial-stability themes (banking system, systemic risk) is thin. Moderate-high attention.
- Forward guidance. The message is wait-and-see, "we are comfortable," and no defense of a particular rate. The Chair would defer program changes until the June discussion of exit strategy. There is no directional signal on rates. Lacker adds an anti-monetization stance that leans slightly hawkish on balance sheet size. Neutral, with a slight hawkish tilt.
- Uncertainty. Lacker cites "our uncertainty about just how transmission mechanisms are working right now" and "uncertainty and confusion about our potential reaction function." Bernanke says it "is not clear" what role inflation expectations play. The Chair also expects to learn more by June. This is real uncertainty about transmission and about the cause of the yield moves, but it is not framed as deep macro Knightian uncertainty. Moderately elevated.

- Direction confirmation: disagreement is minimal. Nobody dissents, and Lacker's remarks are supportive additions to the Chair's view.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +-10 [Moderate confidence]**

- Center of gravity: market and financial conditions are the sole subject of the meeting, but the discussion is about yields and program mechanics and not about systemic risk.
- Direction confirmation: attention is moderately high. Financial markets are the meeting's focus, though the treatment is technical and short.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 +-8 [Moderate confidence]**

- Center of gravity: wait-and-see, with a plan to discuss the exit strategy in June. No directional rate signal.
