# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2006-01-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening procedural items, members' questions to staff (staff answers removed), a go-around of economic views, a short policy round on the 25 bp move and statement wording, and a vote read-out. It is Chairman Greenspan's final meeting, so many remarks are tributes; these were ignored. Discussion of the voting procedure for the statement is procedural and ignored for conditions.

**2. Era context.** Early 2006, a tightening cycle at 25 bp per meeting; the funds rate goes from 4.25 to 4.5 percent. Backdrop: weak preliminary Q4 GDP, energy prices up again, core PCE around 2 percent, cooling housing. I scored only what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Hawkish-leaning: Moskow (core inflation above 2 percent a problem, funds rate needs to rise a bit over 5), Lacker (inflation bulge, do not lower the near-term yield curve), Hoenig (inflation risk the greater risk, "inclined to move"). Dovish or cautious: Yellen (downside risks, lower inflation view, near the peak), Minehan (risks more balanced, rate that "neither stimulates nor restrains"), Ferguson ("at least possible" to move in March), Pianalto and Olson (flexibility). Middle: Santomero, Stern, Poole, Guynn, Geithner, Kohn, Bies, Fisher (growth strong, inflation contained). Greenspan: consensus for 25 bp, "further policy firming may be needed", data dependence from here.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above the comfort level. Core PCE was 1.9 percent over twelve months and 2.2 percent in Q4 (Yellen, Lacker, Bies). Moskow wants 1.5 percent, Geithner says "somewhat higher than we would be comfortable with", Hoenig expects 2.25 to 2.5 percent. Others (Yellen, Minehan, Fisher, Kohn) expect moderation and cite limited pass-through. Above 50 but not by much: 56.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Kohn says the economy operates "in the neighborhood of its long-run, sustainable potential"; Geithner "near potential"; Yellen "near full employment" with "trend-like growth"; Hoenig sees 2006 growth about 0.5 point above trend, falling back to trend. Bright line: "near potential" requires 48-52. Score 51.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment about 5 percent (Moskow, Santomero), "near full employment" (Yellen). Minehan sees remaining labor capacity; Hoenig notes some shortages. The 46-52 band applies. Score 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q4 was weak but viewed as temporary; Q1 rebound expected (4.7 percent cited). Fisher reports robust demand, Lacker "healthy" consumption and "fairly robust" investment, Hoenig "solid, though not spectacular". Housing is cooling and Bies notes borrowing caution. Net slightly above trend, 53.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Pianalto cites the 3 percent structural productivity growth in the baseline and "healthy productivity gains" from capital spending. Geithner mentions that productivity "could disappoint" and Stern a possible negative Q4 productivity figure. Thin evidence, near to slightly above trend: 55, Low.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Geithner "financial conditions...still seem quite supportive of continued expansion"; Hoenig "generally supportive financial conditions"; Moskow worried about "a lot of money chasing investments" and a price of risk "driven too far" down; Yellen and Ferguson stress low term premiums. Supportive means below 50 (bright line): 35.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The term premium, flat curve and long-end pension demand are discussed at length, but as demand-driven and orderly with no dysfunction. Yields are not dysfunction. Score low (35), Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Moskow: "risk spreads are quite low by historical standards". Level is low, so below 50 (bright line). Geithner only warns risk premiums "could rise sharply". Score 25.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only passing references to global equity price rises and no valuation view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Little direct discussion. Guynn: banks pulling back on construction lending and "a handful of small community banks may actually be at risk" (hurricane areas); Ferguson: corporate balance sheets in good shape; Bies: household debt-service worries as a lender. No system-level concern, mild pockets: 38, Low.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Geithner only asks the staff about the exchange rate assumption in the external balance forecast. No member view on the dollar's movement. N/A.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Unanimous support for a 25 bp hike (to 4.5 percent) and for "further policy firming may be needed" with "well" dropped. Members say the funds rate is within the neutral range (Hoenig, Ferguson, Yellen) and want flexibility (Pianalto, Olson, Santomero, Yellen). Moskow, Lacker and Hoenig lean hawkish, Minehan and Ferguson lean dovish. Hike plus near-neutral, data-dependent forward stance: lean hawkish at 55.
- Dissent: All ten voting members voted yes on the directive. Lacker voted against the foreign currency authorization, a recurring procedural position unrelated to the economic view, mentioned briefly. Differences of emphasis (March "probable" per Geithner vs "at least possible" per Ferguson, 50-50 per Hoenig and Kohn). 14.
- Financial stability emphasis: Moskow (liquidity, private equity, low risk spreads), Yellen (housing overvaluation, low term premia), Ferguson (term premium, housing), Bies (household debt service, ARMs), Kohn (housing froth), Guynn (community banks), plus the extended pension-demand and term-premium exchange at the opening. Moderate: 42.
- Forward guidance: "further policy firming may be needed" is a mild tightening bias, but "may well" was softened, and Greenspan says "we have run the string" and the data now decide. Mild tightening bias with data dependence: 55.
- Uncertainty: Ferguson says "considerable uncertainty", Geithner "considerable" and wants more uncertainty conveyed, Guynn "considerable uncertainty" on flood insurance, Yellen caution on Q4. Otherwise normal caveats; many members are fairly confident. 42.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 51 (near potential, 48-52, satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35 (supportive, below 50, satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 25 (low level, below 50, satisfied). BANKING_STRESS 38 (below 50, satisfied). TREASURY_STRESS 35 (no dysfunction, satisfied). DOLLAR N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 51 vs LABOR 50 vs CONSUMPTION 53: within 15. FC 35 and SPREADS 25 co-move below 50, consistent. FC vs TREASURY: no crisis, low-normal. INFLATION 56 vs sub-components (goods 52, expectations 52): consistent.

**9. N/A.** EQUITY_VALUATION: no valuation views. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only a staff question. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as price categories (housing remarks concern activity and house prices, not shelter inflation).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-6 High.**
Evidence: Yellen: "core PCE inflation over the past twelve months—at 1.9 percent—has come in higher than I would like"; Geithner: "Underlying inflation is still somewhat higher than we would be comfortable with"; Lacker: Q4 core PCE "2.2 percent".
Summary: Core inflation near or modestly above 2 percent, with some members expecting moderation. Direction: above neutral because core is above preferred levels and risks seen as modestly to the upside.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 51 +-4 High.**
Evidence: Kohn: economy "somewhere in the neighborhood of its long-run, sustainable potential"; Geithner: "With the economy near potential".
Summary: Near potential with growth around trend. Direction: at the midpoint, marginally above (Hoenig, Geithner).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +-5 Moderate.**
Evidence: Yellen: "The economy is near full employment"; Santomero: unemployment forecasts "about 5 percent".
Summary: Roughly balanced labor market near NAIRU. Direction: at neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 +-6 Moderate.**
Evidence: Fisher: the Southeast, Texas and West "enjoying robust growth"; Hoenig: consumer spending "solid, though not spectacular".
Summary: Solid with a temporary Q4 dip and cooling housing. Direction: slightly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 55 +-8 Low.**
Evidence: Pianalto: "maintain the 3 percent rate of structural productivity growth"; Geithner: "productivity growth could disappoint".
Summary: Mentioned by few members, baseline good. Direction: slightly above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: Geithner: "financial conditions, of course, still seem quite supportive of continued expansion"; Moskow: "this may have driven the price of risk down too far".
Summary: Supportive, ample liquidity. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-10 Low.**
Evidence: Greenspan and Minehan: flat curve reflects pension-fund demand for long-dated securities, "not...a comment about the economy"; Ferguson: low term premium.
Summary: Orderly market, demand-driven yield effects, no dysfunction. Direction: normal and calm.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: Moskow: "risk spreads are quite low by historical standards"; Yellen, Hoenig: low term premiums.
Summary: Spreads below normal. Direction: below neutral by stated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Only passing mention of global equity prices rising (Kohn, Ferguson), no valuation view.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 Low.**
Evidence: Guynn: "a handful of small community banks may actually be at risk"; "Banks are now clearly pulling back on their construction lending".
Summary: Localized pockets, system not discussed as stressed; household debt-service concern (Bies). Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Only a staff question on exchange-rate assumptions (Geithner).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52.** Fisher reports little pricing power and apparel deflation, but Poole and Kohn note commodity prices up, Lacker prices elevated; nets close to stable.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Ferguson: expectations "remained stable or edged even somewhat lower"; Geithner: "reassuringly stable" but "at the higher end of comfort"; Moskow worries about drift above 2 percent. Anchored, slightly upward concern.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-6 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we move 25 basis points and that we adopt the language...'further policy firming may be needed'"; Santomero: "close to being done".
Summary: A hike with a near-end, data-dependent outlook; hawkish tilt from Moskow, Lacker, Hoenig offset by Yellen, Minehan, Ferguson. Direction: modestly hawkish (tightening bias, hike delivered).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 14 +-5 High.**
Evidence: Roll call all yes on the directive; Lacker: "I'd like to vote against the foreign currency operation authorization" (long-standing procedural dissent); Ferguson "at least possible" vs Geithner "probable" for March.
Summary: Near consensus on action and wording, with only emphasis differences on pace and risks.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Moskow: "a lot of money chasing investments out there"; Bies on household debt service and ARM repricing; Yellen on "possible overvaluations in housing markets and low term premiums".
Summary: Several members address liquidity, risk pricing, housing and household balance sheets meaningfully, but it is not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 +-7 Moderate.**
Evidence: Greenspan: "the critical part being 'further policy firming may be needed'"; Hoenig: wants "50-50 odds" and the removal of "well"; Greenspan: "the data are going to determine".
Summary: Mild tightening bias with strong flexibility and data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-8 Moderate.**
Evidence: Ferguson: "there is considerable uncertainty around this baseline"; Geithner: "The uncertainty around this forecast still seems considerable".
Summary: Several members stress uncertainty (housing, term premium, energy) but most are fairly confident in the baseline; somewhat elevated.
