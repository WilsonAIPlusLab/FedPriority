# v4.1 Scoring: Transcript Discussion, Meeting 1996-11-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed): staff Q&A questions by members, a full round-the-table economic go-around, the Chairman's policy view, a policy go-around on the directive ("B" asymmetric toward restraint), and a roll-call read-out (12 Yes, no No votes). One passage in President Jordan's go-around is garbled by extraction (Reserve Bank name and speaker blanks); it concerns steel and a pastry firm anecdote and does not affect scoring.

**Era context.** Late 1996, long expansion, unemployment 5.2 percent, CPI inflation near 3 percent, a Q3 slowdown in spending, a large stock market run-up. Scored only from what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Hoenig, Melzer, Guynn, Stern, McDonough, Meyer, Yellen, Greenspan: labor markets tight, wage anecdotes rising but broad measures benign, upside inflation risk, concern that core inflation drifts up once BLS methodology adjustments are stripped out.
- Jordan: nominal GDP growth about 4 percent, no mechanism for higher inflation, risks on the downside; banker stories of consumer credit deterioration.
- Lindsey, Rivlin, Minehan, Hoenig, Melzer, Yellen: stock market valuation and asset-price bubble worry.
- Policy: Broaddus and Melzer advocate +25bp; Minehan and Hoenig could go with +25bp but accept the Chairman; the rest back "B" asymmetric; Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, Kelley, Yellen, Meyer, Stern lukewarm about the asymmetry itself.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above the comfortable level (CPI ~3 percent, "violating an implicit upper bound" per Melzer, upward drift ex-methodology per Hoenig, Moskow, Minehan) but recent data are benign and firms cannot raise prices. Above midpoint, modestly: 56.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Economy slowed "to trend growth" (Meyer); "resource utilization rates are high and slack is limited" (Moskow); unemployment "may yet prove to be below NAIRU" (Meyer); Yellen says capacity pressures are not intense. Slightly above potential: 53. Bright line (above potential means >50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "Labor markets remain very tight", outright shortages, signing bonuses, UE 5.2 percent, Missouri 3.9 percent. The rubric UE table would put 5.2 near 46-52, but the participants' language is uniformly "very tight"/"robust" and the NAIRU estimate is about 5.6, so UE is below NAIRU. Above midpoint: 62.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q3 consumer spending stalled, final sales collapsed (Meyer), housing cooling; rebound expected, retail "satisfactory", business investment holding up, holiday season expected healthy. Net near trend, slightly soft: 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed: measured productivity near zero in Q3 is "not believable" (Jordan), Phillips skeptical, Stern thinks growth likely underestimated, Meyer/McDonough say no evidence productivity is absorbing labor costs. Measured numbers read as weak; participants distrust them. Below midpoint with wide uncertainty: 42.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Low long-term rates, strong stock market, financing available (Phillips), cost of external capital low; offset by tighter consumer lending standards (Yellen, Guynn). Net accommodative: 38. Bright line (accommodative means <50) satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Long rates fell; no discussion of market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spreads discussed (consumer credit quality and lending standards are covered under banking). N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Heavy valuation concern: Lindsey (price implies 13 percent profit growth), Rivlin ("clearly overvalued"), Minehan ("major asset price bubble"), Hoenig, Yellen ("euphoria"). Elevated valuations, strong concern: 22 on the rubric's polarity (low = overvaluation concern).
- BANKING_STRESS: Jordan's bankers: rising delinquencies, bankruptcies, much higher 1997 charge-offs, commercial real estate worries, margin concern; Guynn: banks paying closer attention to credit quality; Yellen: lenders tightening. Emerging stress mainly in consumer credit, system not described as impaired (agricultural banks "very good financial condition" per Moskow): 54.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed. N/A.
- Sub-components: GOODS: firms cannot raise prices, commodity, steel, aluminum, copper prices falling: 35. SERVICES: McDonough's labor-intensive services link is clear but the relevant ECI portion is "not yet" moving: 52. HOUSING: only passing price remarks, no inflation discussion: N/A. EXPECTATIONS: bond rally implies diminished expectations (Broaddus, Stern), but Melzer and Greenspan warn about credibility/no regime change: 47.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Hold with tightening bias; two members want +25bp, two more could accept it, several say they feel less asymmetric than before. Lean hawkish: 57.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Unanimous vote. Two explicit calls for a hike (Broaddus, Melzer) plus two "could accept"; three or four reluctant about asymmetry; differences are about tactics, with shared economic assessment. 32.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Stock market valuation is Lindsey's main topic; Minehan, Melzer, Hoenig, Yellen, Rivlin raise it; consumer credit/bank quality (Jordan, Yellen, Lindsey) is a recurring second thread. Several address it meaningfully but not dominant: 42.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Asymmetric directive retained, Greenspan wants to stay through December, Parry says tightening likely in the future, but many note "breathing room". Mild tightening bias: 60.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Wage data puzzles, productivity, consumption rebound, inventories, political and market risk; Greenspan urges "humbleness"; but standard-caveat tone, risks "more symmetric". Somewhat elevated: 38.

**Polarity checks.** Equity: higher = more equity stress/depressed prices; participants say overvalued, so low (22). Financial conditions: accommodative means low (38). Banking: some stress, so near 50-55. Labor: tight means high.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (>50, consistent with "limited slack"); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (<50); BANKING_STRESS 54 (no "sound/well-capitalized" language from members as a system-wide statement); others N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 62: a 9-point gap, within 15; the economy has slowed to trend while labor remains tight. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION 47: 6. FC vs credit spreads: spreads N/A. FC vs Treasury: N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 56 vs sub-components (goods 35, services 52, expectations 47): parent is higher than the sub-average because members emphasize the expected upward drift in core CPI and the CPI level near 3 percent; accepted with moderate uncertainty.

**N/A justification.** Treasury functioning, credit spreads, dollar, and housing inflation were not addressed by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +-7 (Moderate).** Melzer: "we are in the process of violating an implicit upper bound on acceptable inflation"; Stern: inflation "has turned out to be less of a problem than I feared". Center of gravity: CPI near 3 percent with an expected upward drift but benign recent data. Above neutral because the level is above the comfort zone.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +-6 (Moderate).** Moskow: "resource utilization rates are high and slack is limited"; Meyer: "the economy has slowed to trend growth". Slightly above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-6 (High).** Stern: "Labor markets remain very tight... outright labor shortages"; Melzer: "All in all, labor markets remain robust." Above neutral, UE 5.2 percent below the NAIRU estimate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-6 (Moderate).** Meyer: "collapse of final sales in the third quarter"; Phillips: "Business fixed investment... has been holding up better than most forecasts." Near trend with a soft Q3 and expected rebound; slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 +-10 (Low).** Jordan: "It is simply inconceivable that there was no productivity growth at all in the third quarter"; Phillips: "I continue to be skeptical of the productivity numbers." Measured weak, members distrust it.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Phillips: "the cost of external capital is low as a result of low interest rates and the rise in stock prices"; Yellen: "lending standards tightened". Below neutral (accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** **CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** **DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** (Not discussed.)

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 22 +-8 (Moderate).** Lindsey: "The biggest risk relates to the current stock market valuation based on 13 percent growth in profits"; Rivlin: "the clearly overvalued stock market". Strong overvaluation concern, low on this scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 54 +-8 (Moderate).** Jordan: "They all said that charge-offs would be much greater in 1997"; Guynn: banks "beginning to pay closer attention to credit quality". Emerging consumer-credit stress, system not described as impaired.

**Sub-components.** GOODS 35 (Moskow: "materials costs are coming down rather than rising"). SERVICES 52 (McDonough: in labor-intensive services "prices follow rather quickly" but that alarm bell "is not yet ringing"). HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS 47 (Broaddus: "inflation expectations have diminished in a material way at least for now"; Greenspan: "no evidence" of a change that "killed the inflation expectations bugaboo").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-6 (High).** Broaddus: "I would still favor a 1/4 point increase"; Boehne: "monetary tinkering at this point would be likely to do more harm than good". Hold with a tightening bias, a minority wanting a hike. Lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 +-7 (High).** Unanimous roll call. Melzer: "I am going to advocate a modest increase"; Jordan: "I wish we were not already there". Disagreement over tactics, not over the assessment; 0 formal dissents.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Minehan: "We are risking a major asset price bubble"; Lindsey's stock valuation discussion. Several address it, not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60 +-7 (Moderate).** Greenspan: "I think we ought to stay where we are, at least through the December meeting. I would recommend 'B' asymmetric"; Lindsey: "we are going to be there for quite a while". Mild tightening bias, tempered by "breathing room".

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Greenspan: "The wage structure is extraordinarily dynamic and not easy to read"; Rivlin: "mixed signals... more difficult to tell what will happen next". Somewhat elevated; standard caveats about wages, productivity and consumption.
