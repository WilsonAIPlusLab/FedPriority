# v4.1 Scoring: Member Discussion (transcript), meeting date 2010-05-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, v2 format (staff turns removed). It is an emergency weekend videoconference with one agenda item: whether to reopen the foreign-currency (dollar liquidity) swap lines with the ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, Bank of Canada and Swiss National Bank. There is no economic go-around and no policy-rate go-around. There is a Q&A, then a go-around where each member states support or opposition to the swap authorization, then a procedural vote (10 yes, no no votes) read out by the Secretary.

**Era context.** Spring 2010, a European sovereign-debt flare-up (Greece, Portugal, Spain). The ECB is about to announce open-ended market interventions, and Ecofin is meeting. I score only what participants say in this text.

**Speakers and views.**
- Chairman Bernanke (framing and Q&A): reports ECB actions and personal appeals from central bank heads, and says the swaps would be "substantively helpful" for interbank flows and for confidence. He says there is "not a whole lot more" the Fed can do, because the fundamental issue is European fiscal stability.
- Tarullo: no substantial impact on the US financial system yet, but "such an impact isn't seen until the moment it occurs"; expects "a lot of unpleasantness in markets tomorrow."
- Dudley: swaps will "mitigate the bad feedback loop" between sovereign risk and European banks; they treat a symptom, not the cause.
- Kohn: "a very dangerous situation"; "the beginnings of a generalized flight from risk"; private sector "remembers September and October of 2008."
- Yellen: "an incredibly perilous moment"; "forceful, coordinated global effort."
- Warsh: "this is a mess"; expects "a disorderly move in the euro" and a pullback from European financial institutions; expects massive flows into US safe havens; "very tough period."
- Bullard: non-zero probability of euro/ECB disintegration; it is "irrational" for markets to worry about institution backing given government guarantees.
- Lockhart: contagion risk "is real."
- Evans: Europe "likely to be on fire by our June meeting."
- Hoenig: the US needs to supply dollars so this "doesn't unwind on us into a global disaster."
- Lacker: historically opposes swaps as a matter of regime choice, but will not oppose now. He raises sterilization (would like to sell MBS), political risk, and the point that Treasuries are "getting scarce" with flight to quality.
- Plosser: sterilization asymmetry, "in effect we're doing quantitative easing."
- Fisher, Pianalto, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Duke: supportive; stress communications, transparency and politics.

**Real-economy dimensions (inflation, output gap, labor, consumption/investment, productivity).** Not discussed by participants as US conditions. The remarks that touch them are not assessments of those dimensions:
- The ECB sterilization remark is Chair's description of the ECB's intent.
- Evans asks whether Europe/Germany can avoid recession (a question).
- Hoenig says "we want to maintain" the US recovery.

None gives a level of slack, inflation, labor tightness or spending. All N/A, including the four inflation sub-components. The Chair's note that ECB sterilizes to avoid "spillover into inflation expectations" is a description of the ECB, not a US or euro-area expectations reading, so INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is N/A.

**Financial dimensions, direction first.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as tighter than normal (above 50). Evidence: "middle of a crisis" (Rosengren), "generalized flight from risk" (Kohn), "incredibly perilous moment" (Yellen), "unpleasant spiral" (Tarullo), trouble funding for some European banks (Lacker). Magnitude is below Sept 2008-type freeze, because Tarullo says no substantial US impact yet and the stress is mostly prospective and European/dollar-funding. Score about 74.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The only content is Lacker's "Treasuries are getting scarce. Their price is going up because of the flight to quality," and Warsh's expectation of "massive flows into U.S. safe-haven securities." That is a flight-to-quality yield move, not dysfunction. Per the Treasury rule, 36-50. Score 42, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are never named. Level information is indirect: European banks having "trouble funding" in dollars, markets "going to that adverse equilibrium," ECB acting in "dysfunctional or illiquid" markets, a penalty-rate facility needed. Participants imply reduced access and elevated stress, so above 50. Score 66, wide band, Low confidence. Bright-line rule satisfied (>50 on a stated stressed level, not on direction alone).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Not discussed. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see European banks as under significant stress ("health of the European banks," Dudley; the banking system "will now get asked the hardest questions," Warsh; "problems are acute in some European institutions," Tarullo). US banks are described as having recapitalized (Warsh) with no substantial US impact yet (Tarullo). Net above 50, mainly European. Score 63, Moderate confidence. Bright-line satisfied (stressed language prevails).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar and euro are discussed. Participants worry about further euro depreciation (Chair's question to Nathan; Warsh's "disorderly move in the euro"), and expect safe-haven flows into US assets. Chair says "I don't think the dollar is going to suffer vis-a-vis the euro." Direction is dollar strengthening or at least not weakening, so above 50, modestly. Score 60, Low confidence. No current level of the exchange rate is given, so the band is wide.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: Policy is a liquidity facility (uncapped fixed-rate-tender swaps at a 1-point penalty), not a rate decision. It is supportive and easing in character (Plosser: "in effect we're doing quantitative easing"), with Bernanke noting a possible need to "respond to the macroeconomic conditions of the United States, as necessary." Lacker and Plosser raise sterilization, and Lacker wants to sell MBS at some future date, which is a mild hawkish counterweight. No one argues tightening. Net slightly dovish, Low confidence: 38.
- Disagreement: Unanimous vote of 10 yes. Lacker is a reluctant supporter ("I still oppose them... I won't oppose this now"), and Plosser and Bullard have small tactical points (sterilization, announcement style). Zero formal dissents, one reluctant, differences are tactical. Score 15.
- Financial stability emphasis: The entire meeting is about contagion and global financial stability. Dominant, 90.
- Forward guidance: No rate or balance-sheet guidance for the US. Faint signals only: sterilization and "asset sales" are mentioned as something to discuss soon (mild tightening-direction), versus "not a whole lot more" the Fed can do and the readiness to respond to macro conditions as needed. Net near neutral, slightly easing: 45, Low confidence.
- Uncertainty: Many participants stress how dangerous and unpredictable the situation is (Kohn, Yellen, Warsh, Bullard on non-zero euro disintegration, Evans "on fire", Tarullo on hidden impact, Warsh on another videoconference being likely). Offsetting that, the participants are fairly confident the swaps are the right step. High but not extreme: 72.

**Polarity verification.**
- Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter. Evidence is crisis and flight to risk, so tighter. Matches.
- Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress. Evidence is funding trouble, so more stress. Matches.
- Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress. Evidence is European bank stress. Matches.
- Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means more dollar strength. Evidence is euro-depreciation fear and safe-haven demand. Matches.
- Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN means more hawkish. Evidence is liquidity provision and the QE analogy, with a minor sterilization counterweight, so below 50. Matches.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 74 (>50, "crisis/perilous", no "accommodative" language) OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 66 (>50) OK.
- BANKING_STRESS 63 (>50 for stressed European banks) OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 (>50) OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS 42, no dysfunction described, OK.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT are all N/A, so those pairs do not apply. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (74) and CREDIT_SPREADS (66) are both above 50 and co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus TREASURY_STRESS: TREASURY_STRESS is lower at 42, which is consistent with a flight-to-quality bid for Treasuries rather than Treasury market malfunction. Inflation sub-dimensions are all N/A, matching INFLATION_PRESSURE N/A.

**N/A justification.** Real-economy and inflation dimensions: participants give no assessment of US conditions on these. EQUITY_VALUATION: equities never mentioned. PRODUCTIVITY: never mentioned. Inflation sub-components: never discussed.

**Special-topic note.** The rubric's special-topic clause (operations) would call for all-N/A. I judged this closer to the emergency-meeting case ("score only what is discussed, use N/A liberally"), because members do make substantive assessments of financial stress, contagion risk, banks and the euro, and the Tier 3 dimensions are scoreable. Real-economy dimensions are N/A for the reason above. The alternative reading (all N/A) is flagged in ANOMALY_NOTE.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: N/A.** No participant assesses US or euro-area inflation. N/A because the topic is not raised by members.

**OUTPUT_GAP: N/A.** Evans asks whether Europe can avoid recession, but this is a question, not an assessment of slack. N/A.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: N/A.** Not discussed.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 74 ±8, Moderate confidence.**
- Evidence: Kohn: "We saw on Thursday and Friday the beginnings of a generalized flight from risk, and the United States will not be immune." Rosengren: "we're in the middle of a crisis." Yellen: "an incredibly perilous moment."
- Center of gravity: Members see a sharp, contagion-prone global financial stress episode that has not yet hit the US system materially (Tarullo), so tight but not frozen.
- Direction: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because members describe crisis conditions, flight from risk and funding trouble.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Lacker: "Treasuries are getting scarce. Their price is going up because of the flight to quality." Warsh: "massive flows into U.S. safe-haven securities."
- Center of gravity: Treasuries are bid in a flight to quality, with no dysfunction (no liquidity or auction problems) described.
- Direction: TREASURY_STRESS is below the elevated range, in normal-functioning territory, because only yield and price moves are described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 66 ±12, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Lacker: European institutions "have trouble funding." Kohn: the private sector is "going to that adverse equilibrium as fast as they can."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are not quantified, but funding access is described as impaired for stressed European banks and markets as dislocated enough for the ECB to intervene, so credit stress is well above normal.
- Direction: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because the level of funding stress is described as high (reduced access), not merely because of direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Not discussed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 63 ±10, Moderate confidence.**
- Evidence: Dudley: "the intersection of the sovereign risk problems with the state of the European banks." Warsh: "the banking system in Europe will now get asked the hardest questions." Tarullo: "problems are acute in some of the European institutions."
- Center of gravity: European banks are seen as stressed and a source of contagion, while US banks are described as having recapitalized and not yet affected.
- Direction: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because the banking stress that is discussed is European and substantial, partly offset by US soundness.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±8, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Warsh: "a disorderly move in the euro" and "massive flows into U.S. safe-haven securities." Bernanke: "I don't think the dollar is going to suffer vis-a-vis the euro on these announcements."
- Center of gravity: Members worry about euro weakness and safe-haven demand for US assets, which implies modest dollar strengthening, with no stated level.
- Direction: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the expected and feared euro move is dollar-strengthening.

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** None discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 ±10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Plosser: "in effect we're doing quantitative easing in the United States." Lacker: "I'd love to use this as an opportunity to argue for selling an equivalent amount of MBS, but we can wait until another date."
- Center of gravity: The action is an unlimited dollar-liquidity backstop supported by essentially everyone, with only sterilization and future MBS sales as a minor hawkish counterweight, and no rate discussion.
- Direction: Below neutral (dovish-leaning) because the Committee's actual direction is expanding liquidity support, not tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 15 ±5, High confidence.**
- Evidence: Vote: ten yes, no dissent. Lacker: "I still oppose them, but this is not the time... So I won't oppose this now."
- Center of gravity: Near-consensus, with one reluctant supporter and tactical differences (sterilization, announcement style).
- Direction: Disagreement is at the low end of the scale.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 90 ±5, High confidence.**
- Evidence: The agenda is dollar funding and contagion from European sovereign and bank stress. Hoenig: "make sure this doesn't unwind on us into a global disaster." Dudley: "the propagation mechanisms and these amplifying dynamics."
- Center of gravity: The whole meeting is about containing financial contagion; essentially every participant addresses it.
- Direction: Attention is at the dominant end of the scale.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 45 ±10, Low confidence.**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I'm not sure there's a whole lot more that the Federal Reserve can do... other than... respond to the macroeconomic conditions of the United States, as necessary." Bernanke on sterilization: "we need to talk very soon about the availability of the reserve-draining tools as well as... potential asset sales."
- Center of gravity: No rate or balance-sheet guidance was given; only a readiness to respond to macro conditions and a flagged future sterilization discussion, netting close to neutral.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral, because the overall posture is supportive and no tightening signal is given.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 ±8, Moderate confidence.**
- Evidence: Warsh: "I think this is a very tough period in front of us... another videoconference before our next regularly scheduled FOMC meeting." Bullard: "the probability has clearly moved off zero" of euro disintegration. Tarullo: "such an impact isn't seen until the moment it occurs."
- Center of gravity: Many members stress an unpredictable, fast-moving situation with tail risks (euro breakup, contagion), while still expressing conviction that the swaps are appropriate.
- Direction: Uncertainty is well above the normal level.
