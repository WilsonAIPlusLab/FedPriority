# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2005-11-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of participants' turns: staff-Q&A questions, a go-around of regional/Board assessments, a second round on the policy recommendation and the statement wording, and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed (many questions appear without the answers). The text is clean. A few passages are question fragments with no answer, but nothing is garbled to the point of changing the content.

**2. Era context (kept subordinate to the text).** Hurricanes Katrina, Rita and Wilma, an energy price spike, an ongoing sequence of 25bp funds-rate increases (the rate is 3.75% going into the meeting and goes to 4%), and discussion of whether the funds rate is entering a "neutral range" and how to change the statement language.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish-leaning: Fisher ("I feel quite hawkish even relative to President Lacker"), Lacker (concern over pass-through, TIPS drift, wage-expectations survey), Poole (inflation risks asymmetric to the upside), Guynn ("inflation risk... is my major concern"), Moskow (costs of inflation above forecast outweigh costs of below), Pianalto, Santomero, Hoenig.
- More benign on inflation: Yellen ("no indication of the '70s style wage-price spiral"), Kohn (price and compensation data "raise the odds that the pickup could be a bit smaller"), Ferguson (close to the neutral rate; "vigilant... not vigilantes"), Minehan (cautious about overdoing it), Stern, Geithner (inflation skewed slightly to the upside but core near 2%).
- Everyone supports +25bp and the "alternative B" statement. The disagreement concerns how many more hikes, whether to keep "accommodation" and "measured pace" language, and communication.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core readings are low (core PCE 1.3-1.5%, which Yellen and Lacker call favorable), but headline is boosted by energy, many anecdotes of pass-through and pricing power (Fisher, Moskow, Pianalto, Poole, Hoenig), the Greenbook has core rising to 2.3-2.4%, and Poole/Lacker/Guynn fear the upside. "Somewhat elevated, expected pass-through" corresponds to 51-60 or slightly above, because of the breadth of the anecdotes. Score 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as modestly above potential or at the edge of it. Yellen: "close to full employment, with a good deal of momentum"; Kohn: "resource utilization is high" and growth not slowed to trend; Hoenig: GDP "above trend"; Geithner: just above potential; Guynn: "about trend". Staff forecast has growth slightly below potential later, but that is future. Bright line: above trend, so > 50. Score 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Close to full employment (unemployment about 5%); Moskow "labor markets have tightened"; Minehan skilled labor "hard to find and expensive"; but Kohn "inconsistent with tightness" for wage growth and ECI 2.5%; Stern, Minehan hiring caution. Near NAIRU, slightly tight. Score 54.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption resilient ("growth sound in mid-3 percent range", Bies; Poole retailers stronger than expected; Fisher Wal-Mart +4.2%) but energy-related risk to consumption (Guynn, Moskow, Pianalto, Kohn). Investment solid with a "mystery" of a low investment/GDP ratio (Ferguson). Net modestly above trend. Score 56.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Geithner "productivity growth strong"; Kohn "productivity growth in the third quarter was strong"; Moskow says firms have pushed productivity as far as it can go; Bies mentions a potential slowdown. Mildly above trend. Score 57.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Kohn: "Financial conditions have tightened... unambiguously restraining" (above 50 for that statement); Hoenig: "Financial conditions remain supportive" (below 50 for that statement); Geithner expects only modest tightening; Ferguson cites "ample liquidity". Net near neutral, a hair on the easy side. Score 48.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Only Greenspan's questions to staff about yield correlations (a question, not a stress statement); no participant assessment of Treasury market functioning.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Kohn: "risk spreads have widened just a little since the middle of the summer, suggesting that investors are a bit less confident... even if they're still too confident." The widening is direction; "still too confident" is a level statement that spreads are low. Level governs, so below 50. Geithner notes investors "take more duration risk". Score 42, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Kohn says stock prices "dropped a little"; Poole/Moskow discuss the staff's stock-market assumption (no valuation view). Kohn's "still too confident" gives slight valuation concern. Default band. Score 47, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Bies: "overall credit quality and underwriting" fine, "we think overall things are in pretty good shape", delinquency rates stable, vast majority of banks follow sound procedures; some concern on home equity lines, card practices, and a bankruptcy spike (front-loaded by the new law). Olson: bank activity back to normal near the storm. Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50. Score 35.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Kohn: "the appreciation of the dollar in the first half of the year"; "the exchange rate has firmed". Appreciation is a bright-line > 50. Mild and passing. Score 57, Low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; Kohn says tighter, Hoenig says supportive, so net near 50 and slightly easy. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; "still too confident" means low spreads, so below 50. BANKING_STRESS: "pretty good shape" implies low stress, below 50. DOLLAR: appreciation implies above 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: unanimous 25bp hike; most say "a few more increases" (Yellen), "continue tightening" (Moskow), "at least a couple more policy firmings" (Kohn). Fisher and Lacker are hawkish about pass-through. Ferguson, Minehan and Greenspan are more cautious about overshooting, and several note the funds rate is entering the neutral range. Moderate hawkish, about 66.
- Dissent: Zero dissents, all ten voters in favor, all discussants support +25 and alternative B. Differences are in emphasis (inflation view, number of future hikes, whether to keep "accommodation"), plus Guynn's discomfort with the "potential" wording. The Board vote on the discount rate is unanimous. Broad agreement with minor differences, about 20.
- Financial stability: Housing froth and home equity lending (Minehan, Kohn, Bies), consumer credit and bankruptcy trends and banking guidance (Bies), Olson on bank recovery, Kohn on financial conditions and spreads, and Greenspan on sovereign yield correlations and CDS (as questions). Limited, a few participants as context. About 28.
- Forward guidance: Keeps "measured pace" and "accommodation" wording; the discussion points to "further firming likely necessary" (Geithner's proposal, Yellen's examples) and an expectation of more firming, but the Chair and others plan to introduce greater uncertainty and less promise in the statement and prepare for the end of the cycle. Mild to moderate tightening bias. About 66.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan: "a very large element of uncertainty"; "our grasp on what's going on in the world is a little more tenuous"; Yellen: "considerable uncertainty still clouds the outlook"; Santomero: "wider band of uncertainty"; Kohn: "very open question", r* imprecise; Geithner: market certainty about the path "has to change". Some of it is rhetorical. About 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 (> 50, satisfied); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 (the statements point both ways; "supportive" and "tightened" both appear and net near neutral; no unambiguous bright-line breach because the dimension is mixed); CREDIT_SPREADS 42 (< 50, "still too confident"); BANKING_STRESS 35 (< 50); DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 (> 50); TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 and LABOR 54 are within 15 points. OUTPUT_GAP 55 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 56 agree. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 and CREDIT_SPREADS 42: both slightly easy, coherent. TREASURY_STRESS N/A in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 against sub-components (goods about 60, services about 52, expectations about 55): the parent is a bit higher than the weighted sub-component average because the anecdote-heavy pass-through discussion is broader than the sub-scores capture. Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant assessment of Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices and construction costs discussed, but not shelter/rent inflation; N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Fisher, "price pressures are building" and "inflation pressures creeping up like little cat's feet"; Yellen, "core PCE... remains near the upper end of my comfort zone... risks tilted somewhat to the upside"; Lacker, core PCE "right where I think we would like it to be" but "unsettling signs". Center of gravity: core is still low, but energy pass-through and pricing-power anecdotes are widespread. Direction confirmation: above neutral because most participants regard inflation as the main concern while core readings remain modest.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Yellen, "close to full employment, with a good deal of momentum"; Hoenig, "real GDP will grow at above trend"; Kohn, "a time when resource utilization is high". Center of gravity: modestly above potential now, expected to moderate. Direction: above neutral on the above-trend wording.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 ±5 [Moderate].** Evidence: Moskow, "labor markets have tightened"; Kohn, wage trend "seems inconsistent with tightness in labor markets". Center of gravity: near or slightly beyond full employment. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bies, growth "at a sound pace in the mid-3 percent range"; Poole, October same-store sales "much stronger than they would have anticipated". Offsetting: Guynn, "downside risks to consumer spending still remain elevated". Center of gravity: solid and slightly above trend with energy-related risk. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Geithner, "productivity growth strong"; Kohn, "productivity growth in the third quarter was strong". Center of gravity: modestly strong. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 48 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Kohn, "Financial conditions have tightened... making it unambiguously restraining"; Hoenig, "Financial conditions remain supportive". Center of gravity: mild tightening from a loose starting point, balanced by "supportive" and "ample liquidity". Direction: roughly neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Kohn, "risk spreads have widened just a little... even if they're still too confident". Center of gravity: widening is small and the level is judged low. Direction: below neutral because the stated level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: Kohn, "stock prices have dropped a little"; "still too confident". Center of gravity: price moves without a valuation view. Direction: at or slightly below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bies, "we think overall things are in pretty good shape"; delinquency rates "fairly stable". Center of gravity: sound, with minor consumer-credit and home-equity concerns. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±7 [Low].** Evidence: Kohn, "the appreciation of the dollar in the first half of the year"; "the exchange rate has firmed". Center of gravity: modest appreciation noted in passing. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60.** Evidence: Fisher, caustic soda $450 to $800 a dry ton, polyethylene 38 to 55 cents; Poole, "general merchandise prices are still falling a little" and no increases on goods sourced from China. Net: input/commodity goods pressure, finished goods mixed.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 52.** Evidence: Bies, "some of the slowdown has been in prices of services"; Fisher, American Airlines fares +8%, UPS rate increases. Net: mixed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** House prices discussed as asset prices; shelter inflation not addressed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Evidence: Lacker, TIPS compensation "bouncing around in the neighborhood of 2.6"; Yellen, "no significant pressure... from increased inflation expectations"; Ferguson, "contained". Net: contained but with a mild upward drift.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 66 ±5 [High].** Evidence: Yellen, "a few more increases, including one today, seem to me likely to be required"; Kohn, "it's probably going to take at least a couple more policy firmings". Center of gravity: consensus for +25bp and for more to come, but near the neutral range and with talk of stopping. Direction: above 50 (tightening).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±6 [High].** Evidence: unanimous vote; Guynn, "I certainly can support your recommendation"; Ferguson on a different view for the statement language. Center of gravity: broad agreement with differing emphasis on inflation risk and statement wording. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Bies, long remarks on consumer borrowing, home equity lines, and guidance on mortgages and CRE; Minehan, housing "bubble-like characteristics". Center of gravity: several participants address credit and housing as context. Direction: not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 ±7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Geithner, replace the last sentences with one stating "further monetary policy firming is likely to be necessary"; Greenspan, the statement will move toward "greater uncertainty... not promising as much going forward". Center of gravity: current wording keeps measured firming; discussion points to more firming but a less committal statement. Direction: tightening bias, moderated.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "a very large element of uncertainty regarding how this process is emerging"; Yellen, "considerable uncertainty still clouds the outlook". Center of gravity: elevated but partly standard hedging about hurricane effects, pass-through and r*. Direction: elevated.
