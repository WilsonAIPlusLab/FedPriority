# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 1998-11-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). Staff turns have been removed, so some Q&A fragments (e.g. "Peter Fisher" speaking lines, answers by Mr. Lindsey) are partly missing. The first third of the file (roughly lines 1-1070) is directive-wording redesign, TIPS purchases, Desk/fed funds volatility, swap lines and repo authorization. That is procedure and Desk operations, not conditions or policy views, so I use it only where members say something about conditions (e.g. M2 growth, TIPS illiquidity). The economic go-around (Poole through Kelley) and the policy round (Greenspan recommendation through Kelley) carry the scoring. Mr. Lindsey's policy briefing is staff content and is ignored. His statements (banks better capitalized, futures odds, Senior Loan Officer Survey) are not used for scoring.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 1998, after the Asian and Russian crises and the LTCM-period credit disruption, with two intermeeting-style easings already done (September and mid-October). The question is a third 25bp cut plus a move to a symmetric directive. I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (recommends ease 25bp and symmetry; inflation receding; stock-market bubble worry), McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Meyer, Rivlin, Boehne, Moskow, McTeer, Kelley (support ease). Minehan, Poole and Hoenig support it reluctantly. Parry, Stern, Guynn and Broaddus prefer holding. Jordan votes No.

**4. Directional signals (core).**
- Inflation: participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Moskow: "Price pressures remain benign despite tight labor markets." Greenspan: core PCE "barely more than 1 percent," core CPI "somewhat under 2-1/2 percent," "the inflation rate is still falling." Upside notes are tight labor and rising wages (Kelley "inflation is still a major potential threat," Hoenig "less favorable inflation outlook"). Net about 34.
- Output gap: above the midpoint, modestly. Poole: "output has overshot full employment"; "The economy currently is fully employed"; Meyer: second half "growth at or above trend"; Kelley: "Economic activity is still very strong"; Minehan: "about at potential." Many see slowing ahead, but that is growth rate, not level. Bright-line (>50 given "overshot"/"above trend") satisfied. About 54.
- Labor: above midpoint, tight. Jordan "severe shortage of people," Stern "Labor markets remain very tight," Hoenig "still very tight," Gramlich "unemployment at below 5 percent for seven quarters." Greenspan notes no further tightening. About 67.
- Consumption/investment: consumer and housing strong (Broaddus, Stern, Rivlin, Greenspan "Consumer markets continue to be strong"); autos 16.5 million in October; but manufacturing, capex plans and BFI weakening (Ferguson, Greenspan, Minehan). Net slightly above trend, about 56.
- Productivity: Moskow "productivity growth has been strong enough to largely offset those gains"; Parry "higher trend growth in productivity"; Greenspan "so has output per hour." Above trend, about 61, Moderate.
- Financial conditions: tighter than normal but easing. Meyer "financial conditions, at least outside of equity markets, have nevertheless significantly deteriorated since July"; Gramlich "risk spreads are still high, and the real federal funds rate is still high"; Boehne "not yet back to normal." Counter: Jordan and Guynn report no credit crunch locally; Stern bankers "willing and able." No member calls conditions accommodative. About 58.
- Treasury stress: members do not describe Treasury market dysfunction. The TIPS illiquidity premium and Desk fed funds volatility are about a niche instrument and operations. N/A.
- Credit spreads: level reading. Parry "risk spreads are still elevated"; Broaddus "Risk premia, of course, are still higher than they were before August" though "a number of yield spreads have narrowed significantly"; McTeer "Risk and liquidity premiums have declined since October 15 but remain high"; Greenspan: spreads "have stopped easing." Stated high level, so >50. About 62.
- Equity valuation: overvaluation concern. Parry "the stock market is still overvalued according to most models"; Greenspan "concerns about an asset bubble are not without validity"; Kelley fears a crash if it "should boom to new highs"; Hoenig "bubble economy syndrome." Low score on the stress/concern polarity (overvalued end). About 24.
- Banking stress: mixed. Concerns about money center banks and year-end pressure (McDonough, Moskow's Chicago contacts, Greenspan on backup lines) versus strong loan growth and willing regional bankers (Stern, Jordan, Minehan, Broaddus "Bank credit rose at a 27 percent annual rate"). No "sound/healthy" statement, so no bright-line forcing. About 52, Moderate.
- Dollar: depreciation noted as a positive impulse (Parry "the decline in the value of the dollar"; Meyer "depreciation of the dollar"). Bright-line (<50). About 35.
- Sub-components: goods falling (Greenspan manufacturing prices weakening, commodity prices, steel; Moskow); services moderate (wages rising, Boston medical care ~9%); housing rising faster than average (Greenspan on owners' equivalent rent, Minehan on home prices and rents); expectations low (Broaddus "very low inflation expectations," Parry "low inflation expectations").

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher inflation = more pressure: evidence is subdued, so below 50, OK. Equity: evidence is overvaluation concern; on this scale overvalued is the low end, so below 50 OK. Dollar: depreciation, below 50 OK. Credit spreads: elevated level, above 50 OK.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the decision is a 25bp ease; a majority of speakers back it, several only reluctantly; about five prefer to hold; no one argues for tightening. Forward lean is a pause (symmetric). Moderate dovish with a hold-leaning minority: 37.
- Dissent: one formal No (Jordan) among voters. Non-voting presidents Parry, Stern, Guynn and Broaddus prefer holding; Minehan, Poole and Hoenig support reluctantly ("considerable reluctance," "I am nervous about it," "not going to dissent"). Disagreement extends partly to assessment (Poole "fully employed" and "pouring gasoline" vs Rivlin/Ferguson downside insurance). About 47.
- Financial stability emphasis: prominent. Credit crunch, spreads, year-end funding, Brazil contagion, stock-market bubble and the Chair's explicit reluctance about equities recur across most speakers: about 68.
- Forward guidance: move to symmetric, Meyer "signals the pause," Greenspan's draft statement "we have gone as far as we are going to go," Poole hoping to signal easing is done. Neutral to slightly easing-lean residual: 47.
- Uncertainty: "financial markets remain fragile" (Minehan), Ferguson's two-scenario framing, Brazil "close to 50/50," Greenspan "more difficult call," "Something different is going on," Poole "growth of money is unexplained." Elevated but not extreme: 58.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP >50 (54) ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: no accommodative/tight bright-line language from members; 58 reflects "tightened, not normal." CREDIT_SPREADS >50 on stated high level (62) ok. BANKING_STRESS: no "sound" language, 52 allowed. DOLLAR <50 (35) ok. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** Output gap 54 vs labor 67: 13 apart (<15). Participants see tight labor but output only modestly above potential given productivity gains and slowing growth. Output gap vs consumption 56: aligned. FC 58 and spreads 62: both above normal, coherent. Treasury N/A, consistent with no Treasury dysfunction discussion. Inflation 34 vs sub-components: goods 30, services 52, housing 60, expectations 40 average about 45, parent lower because the Chair's core PCE and multiple members emphasize benign/falling inflation overall; the services/housing components are CPI-flavored (Chair argues housing should be excluded). Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members talked about TIPS operations and Desk funds-rate volatility, not Treasury market functioning or stress.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 34 +-7 (High confidence).** Evidence: Moskow, "Price pressures remain benign despite tight labor markets"; Greenspan, "the inflation rate is still falling." Center of gravity: inflation viewed as subdued, with tight labor markets a lingering upside caveat. Inflation is below neutral because multiple members, led by the Chair, describe disinflation and low expectations.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Poole, "output has overshot full employment"; Meyer, "growth at or above trend" in the second half. Center of gravity: economy modestly above potential with slowing ahead. Above neutral because of the "overshot"/"above trend" language; Minehan's "about at potential" holds it close to 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 67 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Jordan, "severe shortage of people"; Gramlich, "steady unemployment at below 5 percent for seven quarters." Center of gravity: very tight labor markets with wage pressure modest. Above neutral on level of tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Rivlin, "Consumers are spending cheerfully"; Ferguson, "Business fixed investment fell in the last quarter." Center of gravity: strong consumers and housing offset by weakening capex and manufacturing. Slightly above trend net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 61 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "productivity growth has been strong enough to largely offset those gains"; Parry, "higher trend growth in productivity." Center of gravity: productivity running above trend. Above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer, "financial conditions, at least outside of equity markets, have nevertheless significantly deteriorated since July"; Boehne, markets "not yet back to normal." Center of gravity: tighter than normal but improving, with a minority seeing no credit crunch. Above neutral because credit conditions remain somewhat tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "risk spreads are still elevated"; McTeer, "Risk and liquidity premiums have declined since October 15 but remain high." Center of gravity: spreads high in level though narrowing from the peak. Above neutral on the stated high level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 24 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "the stock market is still overvalued according to most models"; Greenspan, "the concerns about an asset bubble are not without validity." Center of gravity: valuations viewed as rich, with bubble/crash concern central to the policy debate. Below neutral (overvaluation end of the scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "year-end pressure on the money center banks... could well exacerbate the problem"; Stern, "bankers are both willing and able to accommodate the demand." Center of gravity: pockets of year-end and counterparty worry at large banks, but lending strong. Near neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "the decline in the value of the dollar"; Meyer, "depreciation of the dollar is certainly a positive." Center of gravity: dollar has weakened. Below neutral because depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30.** Greenspan: manufacturing "prices have been weakening quite measurably"; Moskow on steel price cuts.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 52.** Rising compensation, Minehan on medical costs up nearly 9 percent, offset by productivity.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 60.** Greenspan: "housing costs are rising faster than average"; Minehan on home prices and rents.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Broaddus, "very low inflation expectations"; Parry, "low inflation expectations."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I think the clear thing to do at this point is to move down and go symmetric"; Meyer, "cut the funds rate a quarter point today." Center of gravity: majority backs a further 25bp ease, with forward lean a pause and a sizable hold-leaning minority (Broaddus, Stern, Guynn, Parry). Dovish side of neutral, tempered by pause and reluctance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 47 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Jordan No in the roll call; Poole, "I will support your recommendation, but with considerable reluctance"; Minehan, "I am nervous about it." Center of gravity: one formal dissent plus several non-voters preferring a hold and three reluctant supporters. Notable but tactical disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Gramlich, "the period of the credit crunch is not entirely behind us"; Kelley, "I worry more today about the potential for very serious mischief that could arise in the stock market." Center of gravity: credit markets, year-end funding, bubble worries and international contagion shape most of the policy round.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 47 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer, the symmetric directive "signals the pause"; Greenspan's draft, "we have gone as far as we are going to go." Center of gravity: shift from easing bias to symmetry, a pause signal. Roughly neutral, slightly easing-leaning because the move is still an ease and the Chair says the Committee is poised to move lower if needed.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Ferguson, two scenarios, "The question is which one we believe"; Greenspan, "Something different is going on." Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty about Brazil, markets, money growth and the slowdown, but members are fairly confident about inflation. Elevated.
