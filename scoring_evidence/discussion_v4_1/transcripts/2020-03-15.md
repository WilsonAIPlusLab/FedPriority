# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2020-03-15

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: opening remarks and Desk Q&A, the economic go-round, the policy Q&A, the policy go-round, Board votes on rates, and the roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed. It is an emergency (Sunday) meeting, so N/A is used for topics participants did not address. The document is long (well over 500 words), so confidence is not forced to Low.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** The text itself says this is a global pandemic with a social-distancing shutdown, an oil price war, and a Treasury/MBS/commercial paper liquidity breakdown. The package under consideration is a 100bp cut to the 0-25bp range, at least $500bn of Treasury and $200bn of MBS purchases, discount window changes, swap lines, zero reserve requirements, and conditional guidance.

**Speakers.** Powell, Clarida, Williams, Quarles, Bowman, Brainard, Rosengren, Harker, Mester, Barkin, Bostic, Evans, Bullard, Kashkari, George, Kaplan, Daly. All give an economic view and a policy view.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see pressure as below the midpoint. Clarida says "the net effect of the virus is likely to be disinflationary". Daly says "we were already running below our target, and we now face considerable disinflationary pressures". George expects "significantly lower ... inflation". Powell says inflation "was still lagging our objective but close to 2 percent". Below target and heading lower, so about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as far below potential going forward. Powell: the economy will "shrink in the second quarter, maybe by a substantial number". Bullard: Q2 could be "as much as 10 percent, at an annual rate, decline". Kaplan: "a substantial contraction". Several members say the economy started the year strong, and some (Bullard, Quarles) expect a quick rebound. The sharp expected contraction dominates, so below 50 (about 24), consistent with the COVID Q2 anchor but not fully there because the hit is still a forecast.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: The level entering the shock was very tight ("unemployment at a 50-year low", Clarida, Powell). Members now expect hiring freezes and layoffs, but say few layoffs have occurred yet (Bostic: "To date, we have not seen significant layoffs"). Daly and others report early layoffs. The level is still tight but deteriorating, so about 47. This diverges from OUTPUT_GAP by more than 15 points. The explanation is that the labor data have not yet moved while output is expected to collapse, and the score reflects the current level, not the expected path.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as sharply below trend. Mester reports a retailer with "an average sales decline of 60 to 70 percent". Evans says consumers "could pull back substantially". Daly says people are not buying durables. Below trend and heading to contraction, so about 24.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not discussed beyond a passing remark that contacts "expect all will be down" (Mester). N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Clarida says "financial conditions have tightened considerably, volatility has exploded, and liquidity in many markets has evaporated". Bostic refers to "tightening financial conditions". Powell: "severe illiquidity". Very tight, about 86.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Williams: "extraordinarily low ... levels of liquidity, similar to what we saw in the financial crisis". Rosengren: "financial firms complain they cannot even trade in off-the-run Treasury securities". Brainard: dislocation between cash and futures, off-the-run versus on-the-run spreads. This is actual dysfunction, about 82.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Clarida: "Credit spreads are widening". Kaplan: "Even investment-grade companies are seeing their credit spreads gap out" and high-yield spreads blow out. Rosengren: the corporate bond market "has shut down", the MBS-Treasury spread has "significantly widened", and CP access is lost. These are level statements of severe stress (wide, reduced access), so well above 50, about 80.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members mention a stock market effect (Bowman: "the measurable effect so far has been to securities markets and the stock market"), Rosengren calls for "support [for] asset prices", and Kaplan notes bank stocks fell about 20 percent in a day. No valuation judgment is made. Per the rule, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55, so about 56 with Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Rosengren: banks are "well capitalized and meeting liquidity requirements" but are hoarding. Bowman: "Banks' capital and liquidity ratios have been strong". Brainard cites "strong capital and liquidity buffers". Williams: "not the challenges that banks themselves are having". There is stress at nonbank lenders, bank stock selloffs and credit-line drawdowns. The "well-capitalized/sound" language forces the score below 50, so about 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Clarida describes investors fleeing "to the safety of Treasury securities, dollars, and bank reserve deposits". Daly cites "a stronger dollar" as a disinflationary force. Dollar funding strains are mentioned (Brainard, Mester). Strengthening and demand for dollars, so above 50 (about 63) with Low-to-Moderate confidence because the evidence is brief.
- Inflation sub-components (goods, services, housing, expectations): not discussed by participants. N/A.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Emergency easing. Almost every participant supports 100bp to the lower bound (Mester preferred 50bp), at least $700bn of asset purchases, and many additional facilities. Kaplan, Bostic, Quarles, Bowman and Harker voice reservations about size and communication but remain on the easing side. Bowman said she wanted "a much smaller rate cut" and Quarles and Bostic raised concerns, but none argues against easing. This is the emergency-dovish band, about 4.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (Mester, who wanted 50bp). Several expressed reservations (Kaplan preferred 50bp but will support 100bp, Bostic argued for 50bp and for dropping the dual-mandate phrase, Quarles "with some reservation", Bowman "would have preferred a much smaller rate cut", Harker on QE perception). There were also disagreements on the TAF versus the discount window, and Kashkari and Daly wanted stronger forward guidance. The differences are mostly about size and tactics, not the economic assessment. This matches the March 2020 anchor at about 40.
- Financial stability attention: Dominant. Williams ("market dysfunction across a number of key ... cornerstones"), Powell ("liquidity and market functioning are job one"), Brainard, Kaplan, Rosengren and Daly all devote major portions of their remarks to it. About 91.
- Forward guidance: The proposed guidance keeps rates at the lower bound until the Committee is "confident that the economy has weathered recent events and is on track" to meet its goals. Kashkari, Evans and Daly say it should be stronger. Brainard and Williams describe it as conditional. The purchases are described as "at least" and open-ended. This is a strong, near-threshold easing commitment, about 5.
- Uncertainty: Powell: "the level of uncertainty and fear is unlike anything I can recall"; Clarida: "profoundly uncertain ... very nonnormal distribution"; Evans: "This is truly the Knightian uncertainty situation"; Williams and Daly repeat the Knightian framing. About 92.

**Bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 24 is below 50 (below potential/contraction). Pass.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 86 is above 50 (tightened considerably). Pass.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 80 is above 50 (wide level language and the bond market "shut down"). Pass.
- BANKING_STRESS 40 is below 50 (banks "well capitalized", "strong" buffers). Pass.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 63 is above 50 (flight to dollars, stronger dollar). Pass.
- TREASURY_STRESS 82 is above 60 on actual dysfunction (illiquidity, off-the-run trading impaired). Pass.

**Polarity verification.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: higher means more hawkish. The evidence is emergency easing, so a very low score is right.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more pressure. The evidence is disinflation, so below 50.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. The evidence is wide spreads and frozen markets, so high.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (24) versus LABOR_TIGHTNESS (47): diverge by 23, explained above (the labor market was still tight in the data, while the output collapse is forecast).
2. OUTPUT_GAP (24) versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (24): consistent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (86) versus CREDIT_SPREADS (80): consistent, both high.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (86) versus TREASURY_STRESS (82): consistent in a crisis.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE versus sub-components: sub-components are N/A, so there is nothing to compare.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, the four inflation sub-components: participants did not address them in any scoreable way.

**Text problems.** Some turns are missing between staff and member speech (for example Desk answers are removed, leaving orphan "Sure." lines), and a few speaker labels are mis-assigned in the Q&A (for example "CHAIR WILLIAMS" and a Barkin/Kashkari/Harker label on an answer). Nothing garbled enough to prevent scoring. The vote read-out lists Mester as "No" and all other voters "Yes" (Powell, Williams, Bowman, Brainard, Clarida, Harker, Kaplan, Kashkari, Quarles).

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Clarida, "the net effect of the virus is likely to be disinflationary"; Daly, "We were already running below our target, and we now face considerable disinflationary pressures".
- Summary: Inflation is seen as below target and being pushed lower by the shock.
- Direction: Below neutral because participants describe disinflation, not price pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 24 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell, "the outbreak will cause the economy to shrink in the second quarter, maybe by a substantial number"; Bullard, "a bad Q2 could be as much as 10 percent, at an annual rate, decline".
- Summary: A sharp contraction well below potential is the shared expectation, with a debated recovery timing.
- Direction: Below neutral because activity is expected to fall far below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 ±12 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Clarida, "unemployment at a 50-year low"; Daly, "we're starting to see it in hours and even in layoffs".
- Summary: Tight entering the shock, now starting to loosen, with large layoffs expected but not yet widespread.
- Direction: Just below neutral because the tight starting level is offset by emerging layoffs and hiring freezes.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 24 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Mester, a department store retailer with "an average sales decline of 60 to 70 percent across the nation"; Evans, "businesses could pull back even more on cap-ex ... consumers could pull back substantially".
- Summary: Spending and investment are collapsing in the exposed sectors and spreading.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe sharp pullbacks.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 86 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida, "financial conditions have tightened considerably, volatility has exploded, and liquidity in many markets has evaporated"; Powell, "severe illiquidity".
- Summary: Conditions are described as crisis-like.
- Direction: Above neutral because conditions are very tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 82 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Williams, "extraordinarily low ... levels of liquidity, similar to what we saw in the financial crisis"; Rosengren, "Financial firms complain they cannot even trade in off-the-run Treasury securities".
- Summary: Actual dysfunction in the Treasury and MBS markets is the central concern.
- Direction: Above neutral because liquidity has failed, not merely yields moving.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 80 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Kaplan, "Even investment-grade companies are seeing their credit spreads gap out, and access to CP has been said to be going away"; Rosengren, "the corporate bond market has shut down".
- Summary: Spreads are wide and access is impaired across investment-grade and high-yield.
- Direction: Above neutral because the level is wide and access reduced.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bowman, "the measurable effect so far has been to securities markets and the stock market"; Kaplan, regional banks "sold off by a little bit more than 20 percent that day".
- Summary: Price declines are noted with no valuation judgment, so the score stays near the default band.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because the moves are declines, but no valuation view is stated.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "despite banks being well capitalized and meeting liquidity requirements, their behavior is more consistent with hoarding"; Bowman, "Banks' capital and liquidity ratios have been strong".
- Summary: The banking system is described as sound, with stress located in nonbank credit and lending behavior.
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as well capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 63 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Clarida, investors "fleeing to the safety of Treasury securities, dollars, and bank reserve deposits"; Daly, "a stronger dollar".
- Summary: A flight to dollars and a stronger dollar are mentioned in passing.
- Direction: Above neutral because the dollar is described as strengthening.

**Inflation sub-components:** all N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 4 ±3 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "I strongly support the proposed reduction in the federal funds rate target by 100 basis points and the proposed QE purchases"; Evans, "our aggressive moves today demonstrate a do-what-it-takes approach".
- Summary: Near-unanimous emergency easing, with the dispute only over 50bp versus 100bp.
- Direction: Strongly dovish because the Committee is cutting to zero and launching large purchases.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Mester, "I favor a 50 basis point reduction at this time"; the roll call records one "No" (Mester). Quarles, "with some reservation".
- Summary: One formal dissent plus several voiced reservations about size and communication, with agreement on the economic assessment.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement; tactical, not about the diagnosis.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 91 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell, "liquidity and market functioning are job one"; George, "market functioning and financial stability our highest priority".
- Summary: Market functioning rivals or exceeds the macro assessment in emphasis.
- Direction: Dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 5 ±3 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell, the guidance "says we'll keep the funds rate there until we're confident that the economy has weathered recent events and is on track to achieve our dual-mandate goals"; Kashkari and Daly, the guidance "could be and should be stronger".
- Summary: A strong, outcome-based easing commitment, with some members wanting it stronger still.
- Direction: Strongly easing signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 92 ±4 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans, "This is truly the Knightian uncertainty situation"; Clarida, "profoundly uncertain ... a very nonnormal distribution".
- Summary: Nearly every participant stresses unquantifiable uncertainty.
- Direction: Extreme.
