# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2020-07-29 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It covers: (a) a long go-round on the draft Statement on Longer-Run Goals and Monetary Policy Strategy (no vote); (b) members' questions in staff Q&A; (c) a financial-stability go-round; (d) the economic go-round; (e) the next-morning questions on the Desk/market survey; (f) the policy go-round; (g) roll-call read-out (10 Yes votes, no No votes, no dissent). The input is long (roughly 5,400 lines). No garbled passages affected scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2020, COVID-19 pandemic recession and partial reopening, policy rate at the effective lower bound, large asset purchases, 13(3) facilities. The scores below rest on what members say, not on what I recall about this meeting.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Powell, Vice Chair Williams, Governors Clarida, Brainard, Quarles, Bowman, and Presidents Rosengren, Kaplan, Daly, Kashkari, Evans, Bullard, Harker, Mester, Barkin, Bostic, George.

**4. Directional signals (core).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as well below the midpoint (target). Powell: core "running at about 1 percent"; Kashkari: "far below our target"; Williams: "about 1 percent"; Brainard: "receded further below our 2 percent objective". Supply-side price rises in some sectors (Daly, George) are noted but described as outweighed by the demand shock. Below target, disinflationary, but not a deflation spiral, so mid-to-high 20s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint, large. Powell: Q2 GDP "11 percent below its pre-COVID level", "a substantial hole remains"; Clarida: output gap "around 4 percent" by end-2021 and "very deep hole". Partial rebound since April keeps it above the trough anchor (15-22): mid 20s.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Far below the midpoint. Unemployment about 11 percent (nearly 15 percent adjusted per Powell), payrolls about 14-15 million below the peak. Score about 20.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend overall. Consumer spending rebounded strongly in May-June (retail sales near February level, autos, housing strong) but stalled in July and services are "far below normal"; capital goods demand "moribund" (Evans). Net below trend: about 35.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing remarks (work-from-home productivity losses, structural slow productivity as a driver of low r*). No assessment of productivity growth itself: N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed but on the loose side. Kaplan: "financial conditions overall are relatively loose, except" for small and midsize firms; Brainard: capital markets "generally accommodative" though bank lending "tightened notably"; Williams: "stable and favorable financial conditions". Bank lending standards (SLOOS) are tight. Net below 50, about 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market discussed. March was "once-in-a-century storm" and is now described as functioning fine (Barkin, Clarida); concern is about renewed dislocation ahead (Brainard, George, Clarida). Present functioning normal, forward-looking worry: about 40.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Clarida: IG and HY spreads "retraced their sharp selloffs in March", trading range consistent with recovery; Kaplan: programs "substantially helped narrow credit spreads"; Evans: bond markets for big firms "as good as they could be"; Rosengren: concern is bank risk spreads rather than market spreads. Large-firm spreads near normal or better; small-firm and bank credit tight. Level reading: below or near normal, no stated high level, so slightly below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Daly: stock market "so out of whack", valuations "especially high" once uncertainty is counted; Barkin: "valuations seem rich"; Bostic: markets "disconnected" from Main Street, vulnerable to correction. Valuation concern is on the rich side (low end of this scale): about 30.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Entered the crisis well capitalized (Daly, Quarles: "strong and resilient", "bent, but not broken"; Brainard: "high levels of capital and liquidity"), but loan-loss provisions, tight lending standards, and adverse scenarios where about 75 percent of assets fall below buffers (Kashkari, Brainard), plus George's "near-term banking stress is likely to be significant". Bright-line: "well-capitalized / resilient" language means below 50; emerging-stress language pulls toward 50. Score 46.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a mention of the dollar's reserve-currency role (Kaplan), no movement discussed: N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods, services, housing not assessed as such (George's component remark and Brainard's goods/services spending remark are about activity or breadth, not price levels): N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS discussed substantively (see below).

**5. Polarity verification.** OUTPUT_GAP: higher means more above potential; evidence says deep slack, so low. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 11 percent unemployment, so low. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher is more stress; spreads retraced and large-firm access is good, so slightly below 50. EQUITY_VALUATION: per rubric table, rich/stretched valuation is low. BANKING_STRESS: higher is more stress; "well capitalized" plus emerging loss concerns, so just below 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Hold at the lower bound with a very accommodative stance; nobody argues for tightening. Many say more accommodation will likely be needed (Daly: "err on the side of doing more and sooner"; Quarles: "even potentially increasing" accommodation). The draft framework also moves toward deliberate overshooting. Strongly dovish, but no immediate action: about 20.
- Disagreement: Unanimous vote, zero dissents. Substantive differences are on framework wording (average inflation language, "shortfalls vs deviations", financial-stability paragraph: Mester, George, Bostic, Harker, Bowman, Quarles, Bullard), on yield curve control (Brainard in favor of keeping the option, Harker, Bowman, Quarles skeptical), on date versus outcome-based guidance, and on optimism versus pessimism about the virus. Tone is collegial and everyone says they support. 0 dissents plus several expressed reservations: about 30.
- Financial stability attention: A full financial-stability go-round by roughly 15 members, plus a financial-stability paragraph debate in the framework discussion, plus Rosengren's "knockout" clause and bank-lending discussion in the policy round. Prominent to dominant: about 72.
- Forward guidance: Strongly easing. Existing guidance holds rates at the lower bound; Clarida floats calendar-plus-threshold guidance through December 2023; many prefer outcome-based guidance tied to employment; no one signals tightening. About 12.
- Uncertainty: Very high. Powell: uncertainty "extraordinarily elevated"; Evans: "hard to imagine a more uncertain environment"; Bowman: "extremely uncertain"; Bullard: "extraordinarily high"; Clarida on nonlinear virus-economy dynamics and multiple equilibria; scenario-based outlooks. About 80.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (slack/deep hole): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 ("accommodative", "loose", "favorable"): satisfied (38). CREDIT_SPREADS: no stated elevated level, spreads retraced, score below 50 (42): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 ("well-capitalized", "resilient"): satisfied (46). DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. TREASURY_STRESS: no current dysfunction, score 40 (not above 60): satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 24 vs LABOR 20: consistent. OUTPUT_GAP 24 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 35: 11-point gap, within tolerance; spending rebounded faster than employment, so a little higher is reasonable. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 42: close. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: normal functioning, fine. INFLATION_PRESSURE 27 vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 35: expectations described as drifting down but not unanchored, so a bit higher than realized inflation.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment of productivity growth. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no dollar movement discussed. GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING inflation: no category-level price commentary scoreable.

**Inflation expectations.** Clarida: SPF 5-year PCE at 1.7 percent, "uncomfortably low", risk of "difficult-to-reverse decline"; Brainard: market-based measures "moved below their typical ranges", survey measures mixed; Williams: "do not appear to be coming unanchored"; George: downshift "seems unlikely for now"; Quarles: "some measures... decreased", surveys mixed. Below target, with downward-drift concern but no unanchoring: 36.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Powell, "core inflation is running at about 1 percent"; Kashkari, "Inflation is running far below our target."
- Summary: Members see inflation well below target and COVID as a disinflationary shock.
- Direction: Below neutral because inflation is far below 2 percent with weak demand.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 24 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Powell, "a substantial hole remains... Q2 being 11 percent below its pre-COVID level"; Clarida, "output gap of around 4 percent" by end-2021.
- Summary: Deep shortfall from potential, partially recovered from the April trough.
- Direction: Below neutral because of large slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Powell, unemployment "in double digits... nearly 15 percent" adjusted; Mester, payrolls "down almost 15 million jobs".
- Summary: Unemployment about 11 percent and far from full employment.
- Direction: Below neutral because of very high unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±8 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Bowman, "consumer spending rose sharply in May and June"; Evans, "demand for capital goods is moribund"; Williams, spending "may be stalling... in July".
- Summary: Strong but incomplete consumer rebound, stalling in July, weak business investment, levels below normal.
- Direction: Below neutral because spending and investment remain below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**
- Only passing mentions (Evans on work-at-home productivity losses); no assessment of productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Kaplan, "financial conditions overall are relatively loose, except" for small and midsize firms; Williams, "stable and favorable financial conditions".
- Summary: Market conditions accommodative; bank credit tight for smaller borrowers.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because markets are described as loose and supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Barkin, "Treasury security and MBS markets are, in fact, now functioning fine"; Brainard, "market makers could be unwilling or unable to intermediate... dislocation" ahead.
- Summary: Present functioning normal after March; concern is about future dislocation.
- Direction: Normal-range because there is no current dysfunction, only forward worry.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Clarida, "IG and high-yield spreads have retraced their sharp selloffs in March"; Evans, bond market conditions for large automaker "as good as they could be".
- Summary: Large-firm spreads back to a recovery-consistent range, small-firm and bank credit tight; level reading is slightly below normal.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because spreads are described as retraced and access is good for large issuers.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Daly, "How is the stock market so out of whack?" and "current valuations look especially high"; Barkin, "valuations seem rich".
- Summary: Several members see rich valuations and correction risk.
- Direction: Below neutral on this scale (low = valuation concern) because valuations are viewed as stretched.

**BANKING_STRESS: 46 ±8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Quarles, banks "strong and resilient... bent, but not broken"; George, "near-term banking stress is likely to be significant".
- Summary: Banks entered the crisis well capitalized but face expected losses and tight lending.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because capital and liquidity are described as strong, with emerging-stress pockets.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** (no dollar movement discussed)

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36**
- Evidence: Clarida, SPF at "1.7 percent over the next five years", "uncomfortably low"; Williams, expectations "do not appear to be coming unanchored".
- Summary: Below target and drifting lower, not unanchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Daly, "err on the side of doing more and sooner"; Quarles, accommodation "even potentially increasing".
- Summary: Hold at the lower bound with a broad expectation of more support; nobody argues for tightening.
- Direction: Strongly dovish because there is no tightening voice and the framework builds in overshooting.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 ±7 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Roll call of 10 Yes votes with no dissent; but Mester, George, Bostic, Harker and Bowman voice reservations about "average inflation" wording, and members split on yield curve control.
- Summary: Unanimity on the vote, with moderate reservations on framework language and tools.
- Direction: Low-to-moderate because there are no formal dissents but several expressed reservations.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 ±8 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Dedicated financial-stability go-round (Rosengren, Mester, Bostic, Kaplan, Kashkari, Brainard, Quarles, Daly and others); Rosengren, "I would like to have a 'knockout' clause related to financial stability."
- Summary: A prominent topic with extended contributions from most members, rivaling the economic assessment.
- Direction: High because most members devoted extended remarks to it.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 ±5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Clarida, guidance could say "exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least until December '23"; Williams, "Monetary policy will need to remain accommodative for quite some time."
- Summary: Easing-biased guidance with discussion of strengthening it further.
- Direction: Strongly easing because all forward signals point toward lower-for-longer.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 80 ±6 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Evans, "it is hard to imagine a more uncertain environment"; Powell, uncertainty "extraordinarily elevated".
- Summary: Nearly all members stress virus-dependent, scenario-based, nonlinear uncertainty.
- Direction: Very high because most emphasize deep uncertainty.
