# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2013-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion: a first-day session (Desk questions, a testing proposal for an overnight reverse repo facility, questions to staff, a quarterly financial-stability round, the economic go-round, the Chair's summary) and a second-day policy go-round on three statement alternatives (A, B, C), ending with the roll call. Staff turns are removed, so many member questions appear without answers. Text is clean; no passage was too garbled to read.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Large-scale asset purchases are ongoing at a monthly pace of $85 billion. The unemployment rate is a little above 7 percent, inflation is below 2 percent, and long-term rates have risen roughly 100 bp since May. The live question is whether to taper by about $10 billion now (C) or wait (B).

**Speakers and views.**
- Doves and waiters:
  - Rosengren: financial conditions "much tighter than I would have liked"; economy "fragile".
  - Evans: inflation too low, downside risks, "restrictiveness of financial conditions is a significant downside risk".
  - Kocherlakota: wants more accommodation, a 6 percent threshold, and a "relentlessly goals-oriented" approach.
  - Yellen, Dudley, Tarullo, Lockhart, Bullard: back B (no taper) on ambiguous data, tightening conditions and fiscal risk.
  - Powell and Stein: prefer C but vote B.
  - Bernanke: supports B.
- Pro-taper camp: Plosser, Williams, Pianalto, Lacker, Fisher, George (only George is a voter). Their case is credibility, forward guidance and the June framework.
- Fisher stresses that the economy is booming in Texas and that credit is easing outside real estate. Stein stresses uncertainty about productivity.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (below target) because Bullard says "running well below the Committee's 2 percent target", Lockhart finds the long run of sub-2 core PCE "troubling", and Kocherlakota and Evans see it staying low. A few note firming (Pianalto, Fisher, Bernanke: "recent inflation has picked up slightly... inflation is quite low"). Score about 36.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Tarullo: "an economy still in need of greater aggregate demand", "the amount of slack that still likely remains". Slack is expected to close only by about 2016 (Yellen). Bright-line "slack remains" means < 50. Score 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. The unemployment rate is above 7 percent and has fallen partly because of falling participation. The employment-to-population ratio is flat (Kocherlakota), and payroll growth is "disappointing" (Rosengren, Evans). Dissenting colour: Fisher's Texas boom, George's labor shortages, Williams's hiring indicators. Score 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly below trend, mixed. Consumer spending has "decelerated noticeably" (Evans), household spending ex-autos has slowed (Yellen), and businesses are hesitant to hire or invest. Offsets are autos, housing and the Verizon and AT&T investment examples. Score 41.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Lacker sees no acceleration. Stein is "capitulating" on productivity because output is weak while employment improves. Dudley and Yellen note it is hard to interpret. Perceived productivity growth is below trend. Score 37, moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed, near neutral. Many describe a "tightening in financial conditions" through long rates and mortgage rates (Rosengren, Evans, Dudley, Yellen, Bernanke). Fisher and Powell dispute this, citing easing credit standards, strong credit flows, low financial stress, and "abundant credit". Bernanke notes house and stock prices offset the rates. The tightening is rate-driven and concentrated in housing. Score 53 (slightly above neutral).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields and term premiums are discussed as a policy-expectations and term-premium move (Williams, Kocherlakota, Yellen). Evans mentions dealers' unwillingness to make markets post-Dodd-Frank in passing. No auction or functioning problems. Rule: yield rises are not dysfunction. Score 45, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Fisher: junk spread "under 4 percent" and "barely above 2 percent" on his two measures, and "continued to narrow". Stein: corporate spreads "haven't moved". Lockhart: "comforting decline" in corporate bond spreads. Yellen and Dudley say the BBB and equity risk premia are high, a counter-signal. Spreads are narrow or normal and favorable, so below 50. Score 40.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Prices are up and have held up in the face of higher rates (Williams, George, Powell). Dudley and Yellen say the equity risk premium is "very high", a mild undervaluation signal. No bubble or crash talk. Score 49, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: George flags earnings pressure at community and regional banks (low net interest margins, interest rate risk). Tarullo: financial firms "strengthened substantially". Powell: banks reluctant to lend to non-pristine credit. Healthy banks mean below 50. Score 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Williams: dollar "appreciated only very modestly". Bernanke: "A stronger dollar works in the other direction". Fisher: "pretty much even". Modest appreciation means above 50 (bright line). Score 52, thin evidence.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS and SERVICES: Dudley mentions a decomposition without giving levels, so N/A. HOUSING: only house prices are discussed, not shelter inflation, so N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Dudley says "pretty well anchored above the current core PCE rate" but the 5y5y drifted down. Bullard cites a two-year breakeven of 1.35 percent. George says "inflation expectations remain stable". Score 44.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The debate is whether to trim purchases by $10 billion or hold at $85 billion. The Committee holds the pace, signals patience, and plans to look at strengthening guidance in October. The B camp is the majority of voters, but a sizable pro-taper minority exists and Kocherlakota wants more easing. This is a QE-era "how much to buy" debate, so the 15-25 anchor applies, nudged up for the sizable pro-taper minority. Score 25.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (George, in favor of C). Of the non-dissenting voters, Powell and Stein state a preference for C but vote B. Of the non-voters, Plosser, Williams, Pianalto, Lacker and Fisher argue for C. Kocherlakota argues for much more accommodation. There are distinct camps, but the disagreement is mainly about tactics and signaling, with some divergence on the economic read (Fisher vs. Yellen and Tarullo). The tone is collegial ("best session"). Score 48.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: A dedicated quarterly financial-stability round (emerging markets, Puerto Rico, farmland, leveraged loans, reach for yield, supervisory actions), plus extended debate about whether financial conditions have tightened. Prominent but not dominant. Score 62.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Threshold guidance (6.5 percent) remains. B signals patience and a willingness to keep purchases going. Many want guidance strengthened to stress a low path for rates, with a SEP median of 2 percent for 2016. Yellen: "very patient in removing accommodation". The tapering-later presumption is a mild hawkish counterweight. Score 14.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Lockhart: "persistent ambiguity", "too many [question marks]". Stein: "I really don't have a clue" on productivity. Bernanke: "uncertainty higher now than normal". Dudley: "highly uncertain" on the housing impact. Also fiscal and debt-ceiling risk, and "completely shattered" confidence in predicting market reaction. Elevated but not extreme. Score 58.

**Polarity checks.** Higher = more of the named construct on every line. LABOR_TIGHTNESS below 50 matches slack language. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 matches narrow or stable spreads (higher = more stress). BANKING_STRESS below 50 matches "strengthened". HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN below 50 matches holding the pace with an easing bias. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE low matches patience.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 36: below 50, satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53: no uniform "accommodative" or "tight" characterization; the tightening is the focus but is contested.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 40: below 50, satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 38: below 50, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 52: above 50, satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS 45: in the 45-55 band, satisfied.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 36, LABOR 33 and CONSUMPTION 41 are all within 15 points.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 40 diverge by 13. The tightening participants describe is in long Treasury and mortgage rates, while corporate spreads were narrow or flat. That is why FC is only slightly above neutral and spreads are below.
- FC and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is near normal, consistent with a rates move that is not dysfunction.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 44: consistent.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed by participants with directional content.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Bullard: "Inflation has been running well below the Committee's 2 percent target." Lockhart: core PCE below 2 percent "for nine consecutive years... I find this troubling." Center of gravity: inflation is below target and expected to return only gradually. Direction: below neutral because most see it as low, with some noting a modest firming.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Tarullo: "an economy still in need of greater aggregate demand"; "given the amount of slack that still likely remains in the economy." Yellen: slack "largely disappeared" only by 2016. Center of gravity: material slack persists. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Kocherlakota: "essentially no change in the employment-to-population ratio... reclassification of those who are not employed." Rosengren: "The unemployment rate declined for the wrong reason again." Center of gravity: unemployment above 7 percent with weak participation. Direction: below neutral. Some pockets are tight (Texas, Tenth District).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 41 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Evans: "Consumer expenditures have decelerated noticeably." Yellen: "household spending outside of motor vehicles appears to have slowed over the summer." Offset: Pianalto and George cite autos and housing strength. Center of gravity: soft consumption, strong autos and housing, hesitant business investment. Direction: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 37 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stein: "I'm beginning to revise down my estimate [of productivity growth]." Lacker: "I see no reason to expect productivity growth to accelerate." Center of gravity: low productivity growth is the reconciliation of weak GDP and falling unemployment. Direction: below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 53 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Rosengren: "significant tightening in financial conditions... 10-year rate has risen more than 100 basis points." Fisher: "outside of real estate, credit conditions are, if anything, easing, not tightening." Center of gravity: rates and mortgage costs are tighter, while credit availability and spreads are easy. Direction: slightly above neutral; contested.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 45 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Williams: the rate spike was "mostly in term premiums." Evans: "dealers are unwilling to engage in those transactions." Center of gravity: a large yield rise but orderly. Direction: at the normal band; no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Fisher: "junk debt... has continued to narrow... barely above 2 percent" on one measure. Stein: "corporate credit spreads... hasn't moved." Counter: Yellen on the BBB premium being high. Center of gravity: spreads narrow or steady and credit availability easy. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 49 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: George: "Stock markets have remained up in the face of higher interest rates." Dudley: "The equity risk premium is very high." Center of gravity: no valuation alarm. Direction: roughly neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Tarullo: "financial firms having been strengthened substantially." George: "earnings pressure that comes from low net interest margins" at community and regional banks. Center of gravity: sound system with pockets of earnings and interest-rate-risk concern. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 ±5 (Low).** Evidence: Williams: "the dollar has appreciated only very modestly." Fisher: "we're pretty much even over the last several months." Center of gravity: stable to slightly stronger. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Evidence: Dudley: expectations "pretty well anchored above the current core PCE rate", with the 5y5y drifting down. Bullard: the two-year breakeven "as low as 1.35 percent." Direction: slightly below anchored-at-target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 25 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Yellen: "I judge it wiser not to move today"; "yet greater patience on our part will be required in removing accommodation." Kocherlakota: "We should provide more accommodation until, explicitly, either unemployment or inflation is hitting its target." Counter: Plosser: "The small taper... is trivial"; Lacker, Fisher, Williams for a $10 billion trim. Center of gravity: purchases continue at the full pace and patient guidance, with a minority for a small taper. Direction: dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Roll call has one No (George, preferring C). Powell: "I would have a preference for C"; Stein votes B "with some reluctance". Fisher: "We seem to be finding excuses not to move." Center of gravity: one formal dissent, several reluctant B supporters, two clear camps on tactics, collegial tone. Direction: notable disagreement mostly about timing and signaling.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Dedicated round: Rosengren on emerging markets and Puerto Rico, Kocherlakota on reach-for-yield, George on farmland and leveraged loans. Fisher's lengthy discussion of whether conditions tightened. Center of gravity: a standing financial-stability round plus extended debate about financial conditions. Direction: prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Yellen: "we're very patient in removing accommodation as headwinds gradually abate." Williams: stronger guidance that "creates some space between the 6½ percent threshold and the actual liftoff." Bernanke: look "very seriously in October at how we might strengthen the guidance." Center of gravity: a strong easing and patience signal with the threshold regime intact. Direction: dovish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Lockhart: "there are simply a lot of question marks—too many." Bernanke: "Why is this uncertainty higher now than normal?... the nature of the recent data... and fiscal developments." Stein: "I really don't have a clue." Center of gravity: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty about data, productivity, market reaction and fiscal events. Direction: elevated.
