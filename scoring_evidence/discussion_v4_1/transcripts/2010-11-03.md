# FOMC member discussion, 2010-11-03 (v4.1 rubric plus V4.1.1 clarifications, v411)

Part 1: omitted in this run

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: "core inflation remains extremely low, and disinflation has been broad-based"; inflation "in the vicinity of 1 percent for the duration of the forecast period."
- Rosengren: "gradual disinflation ... my forecast expects this disinflationary trend to continue"; Pianalto: "the disinflation process is most likely not yet finished"; Evans: "continued low inflation, low enough that monetary policy has an obligation to address it"; Moore: PCE inflation "around 1 percent for the next few years."
- Pushback: Plosser says 1 to 1.5 percent is "very near my inflation objective"; Lacker says "inflation is not too low"; Hoenig "I do not agree that inflation is too low"; Fisher says the trimmed mean is "holding steady" at about 1 percent. Chairman's summary: "Inflation has drifted down some this year."
- Most participants put inflation well below target and still drifting down; a minority calls it low but acceptable.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: "enormous slack in the labor market"; "a long, slow slog."
- Evans: "mired in a liquidity trap of immense proportions"; "substantial resource slack persisting into the future"; Lockhart: "persistent slack"; Moore: "significant slack in labor and goods markets"; "struggling to climb out of a very deep hole."
- Raskin: "the magnitude of excess capacity." Chairman: the outlook "remains fairly drab"; "we've lost a year of economic growth."
- Below potential (bright line satisfied). Members also say the recovery continues (Hoenig "continues to improve steadily"; Plosser "modest recovery"), which keeps the score at the top of the 16-30 band.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 +-5 (High)**
- Raskin: "our 9.6 percent unemployment rate"; Evans: "no business, large or small, reported any meaningful hiring plans at any relevant horizon."
- Kocherlakota: share of the labor force unemployed over 52 weeks "about 0.4 percent in 2007, and now that number is 2.9 percent"; Yellen: "quit rate is unusually low ... a measure of great fear in the labor market."
- Chairman's summary: unemployment "likely to decline, at best, very slowly—it may even increase in the near term." High unemployment, a very slack labor market.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: businesses "are reluctant to hire and invest"; "Outside of housing, where conditions are dismal ... growing, but only at a snail's pace."
- Lockhart: "little spending premised on domestic demand growth"; Fisher: firms report "little interest in ... committing to large-scale cap-ex here"; Duke: residential investment recovery removed from her forecast.
- Offsets: Lacker: "Consumer spending keeps advancing"; Hoenig: durable goods spending "risen nearly 6 percent"; Lockhart: Q3 GDP gave "some encouragement" in consumption and business investment. Chairman: "weak tone of consumer confidence and spending."
- Net: below trend and sluggish.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-10 (Low)**
- Fisher: "Almost all the businesses I talked to are expanding investment in productivity enhancement"; Kocherlakota: "firms have experienced sharp declines in unit labor costs"; Lockhart: "productivity gains by firms that have been 'banked.'"
- Against: Yellen: "even with some slowdown in productivity growth"; Warsh: "Harm is being done to the supply side ... I fear that potential GDP has really slipped"; Tarullo worries about capacity "atrophy."
- Mixed; roughly at trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-8 (High)**
- Yellen: "financial conditions have eased meaningfully"; Dudley: "Financial conditions have become much more accommodative, bond yields are down, equity prices are up."
- Warsh: "The credit markets are flying"; Fisher: firms "flush with liquidity", "ready access to cheap money." Chairman's summary: "Financial conditions have improved somewhat."
- Offsets: SME credit is constrained (Pianalto "lending to small and medium-sized enterprises remains constrained"; Dudley "Small businesses see the banks as continuing to constrain credit sharply"; Lockhart "a cautious and restrained credit environment"); Duke says the mortgage market is not "anywhere close to healed"; Evans and Rosengren say disinflation raises real rates.
- Accommodative overall (bright line < 50); the split between large and small borrowers keeps the score in the 31-45 band.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Low)**
- Lacker: a "dramatic fall in Treasury yields"; Kocherlakota: real yields "shockingly low", which he reads as risk aversion.
- Functioning is discussed only as a prospective risk: Lacker asks what "market dysfunction would constitute" if purchases exceed Desk capacity; Bullard: "We may have to be prepared to back off ... if we get market functioning problems"; Warsh worries the Desk becomes "a price-maker."
- Yield moves with no current dysfunction described: normal, quiet functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Fisher: "spreads against risk-free instruments are historically narrow"; "Junk yields are at their lowest level since October of 2007"; "Corporate debt markets are robust, including junk markets."
- Warsh: "spreads are compressing ... I haven't heard such positive reports about the credit market since early 2007." Chairman's summary: "Credit spreads have tightened."
- Offsets: Duke: "Elevated spreads between retail mortgage rates and MBS yields"; availability for SMEs is constrained (Pianalto, Dudley).
- The stated level is narrow (V4.1.1 rule 3: level governs, so < 50). The mortgage-spread and SME-availability caveats keep the score off the extreme.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-10 (Low)**
- Valuation and froth views: Fisher: margin debt "fast approaching levels that prevailed before the Nasdaq implosion"; LBO multiples "echoes the peak of the pre-crash craze"; "the beginnings of speculative activity in stocks"; Hoenig: "we'll also get asset price inflation"; Duke: "anxious about the potential for asset bubbles"; Warsh is "less convinced of their durability."
- Price-only statements: Bullard: "equity market valuations rising"; Chairman: "a 13 percent increase in the S&P 500."
- A few participants voice valuation concern (V4.1.1 rule 2, valuation view present); most only report rising prices. Modestly above fair value.

**BANKING_STRESS: 56 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Dudley: put-backs could "lead to renewed safety-and-soundness concerns"; possible ratings "downgrades ... even be funding issues for certain institutions"; community bankers' "considerable angst about profitability."
- Moore: "the degree to which the banking sector will suffer further damage from past mortgage securitization activities"; Pianalto: "banking conditions still have a way to go before we can say that they have normalized."
- Duke: "improving credit quality and weak demand for new loans"; bankers "suffering from their own post-traumatic shock"; Yellen: the financial system "appears to be healing but remains highly vulnerable."
- Healing but with emerging and residual stress, and cautious lending: mildly above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-6 (High)**
- Bullard: "the dollar depreciating during the intermeeting period"; Hoenig: "the declining value of the dollar"; Dudley: "the dollar has been slightly weakened"; Chairman's summary: "the dollar is down."
- Chairman: "The dollar is not particularly weak—it is about where it was in the mid-1990s in real terms."
- Modest depreciation (bright line < 50).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55**
- Plosser: "a sharp rise in the number of manufacturers reporting higher input costs"; Fisher: Chinese suppliers' bids for fall 2011 ask for "30 percent increases"; Warsh: "The pass-through of commodity prices into final goods ... is real."
- Offset: Fisher's contacts lack "pricing wherewithal to pass on these cost increases of more than, say 2 percent."
- Mild upward pressure.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 50**
- Warsh: "increases in prices for rental cars ... airline fares ... room rates across hotels."
- Offsets: Yellen: "wage trends are extremely subdued"; Pianalto: nonmarket PCE components "running higher than most of the market components."
- Thin and mixed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 52**
- Only evidence: Warsh's "anecdotes of increases in commercial and residential rents." One speaker.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44**
- Anchored: Yellen "continue to be well anchored"; Moore "well-anchored"; Lockhart "relatively stable ... not moving outside historical bounds."
- Downside risk: Bullard says expectations drifted "lower and lower ... during much of 2010"; Yellen "cannot completely dismiss the risk that inflation expectations could follow actual inflation down"; Lockhart puts the deflation probability at "about 20 percent—elevated."
- Recent rebound: Pianalto "Inflation expectations have since recovered"; Warsh "They've moved up."
- Anchored, but with a downside tilt.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 +-6 (High)**
- The Committee adopts alternative B: $600 billion in Treasury purchases at $75 billion a month. Supporters include Rosengren, Pianalto, Lockhart, Evans ("would much prefer to be more aggressive"), Moore, Bullard, Yellen (expects "roughly $1 trillion"), Tarullo, Raskin and Dudley.
- Opponents and reluctant supporters: Lacker ("too-nervous-to-support-alternatives-B-or-A camp"), Plosser ("supportive of alternative C"), Fisher ("firmly against"), Hoenig (dissents), Kocherlakota ("willing to go along"), Warsh ("with the greatest reluctance").
- Chairman frames it as "a very conservative, middle-road approach ... not going to do 'shock and awe.'"
- A QE launch backed by a clear majority: strong dovish. A sizeable hawkish minority keeps it out of the emergency range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 62 +-7 (High)**
- Vote read-out: Hoenig "Respectfully, no"; all other voters yes. One formal dissent.
- Three non-voting presidents oppose outright (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher). Kocherlakota is concerned and only goes along. Warsh: "if I were in the chair of most people around this room, I would dissent"; he warns he will "have no choice but to dissent ... at future meetings." Duke and Lockhart state reservations.
- The disagreement reaches the economic assessment: deflation risk, whether inflation is too low, structural unemployment. Warsh also clashes with Bernanke and Dudley on wording ("the fervency of the Bernanke-Dudley views"). Chairman: "views seem somewhat more dispersed this time."
- Distinct camps and significant division.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Dudley's "few thoughts on financial stability": mortgage put-backs, ratings downgrades, the Volcker rule. Yellen: "highly vulnerable to shocks ... evidence of asset price bubbles in some markets."
- Fisher at length on junk yields, margin debt and LBO multiples: "the greens are playing very fast." Warsh: "the credit markets are flying ... since early 2007, and that didn't seem to go so well."
- Hoenig: "This course sows the seeds of instability"; Tarullo: "the risk of excessive leverage developing in some markets"; Duke on asset bubbles and the mortgage market; Moore on banks. The Chairman flags the new Office of Financial Stability and says the Committee must "be very aggressive in monitoring financial stability."
- Several participants address it meaningfully; it is not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-6 (Moderate)**
- The statement keeps the "extended period" language. Purchases run through mid-2011, and paragraph 5 leaves room for more: Yellen says it "leaves open the possibility of purchases beyond $600 billion and suggests a bias towards ease"; Rosengren: "I also view alternative B as providing an easing bias"; Raskin: "An even larger purchase program could be necessary, and alternative B signals that possibility."
- Conditionality: "The Committee will regularly review" is moved up into paragraph 3. Dudley: "the threshold for rolling it back is significantly higher than the one for doing more." The "mid-2012" date language from A4 is not adopted.
- Strong easing bias with extended accommodation ahead; date-based guidance was not adopted.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-8 (High)**
- Chairman's summary: "Uncertainty is high, and it seems to be a pervasive issue for both the economy and for the Committee."
- Yellen: "I continue to judge the uncertainty attached to my projections as unusually high"; "abnormally high risk to the outlook." Kocherlakota: "Uncertainty remains a dominant problem, if not the dominant problem."
- Moore: "subpar economic growth ... has shaken my confidence"; Bullard: "the error bands around our forecast are gigantic"; Lockhart: "a lot of uncertainty associated with this policy action"; Warsh: risks "unknown, uncertain, and potentially large"; Hoenig's contacts: "an exceptionally uncertain business climate."
- Many participants stress unusual uncertainty about both the outlook and the policy tool, at the top of the elevated band.
