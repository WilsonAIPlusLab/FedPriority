# FOMC member-discussion scoring v4.1 — transcript discussion, meeting 2011-11-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) a long policy-framework discussion (flexible inflation targeting, conditional commitments, price-level and nominal-income targeting, publishing rate paths in the SEP); (b) the economic go-round (Lockhart through Raskin) and the Chairman's summary on the second morning; (c) the policy go-round on statement alternatives A1/A2/B/C; (d) a vote read-out. This is a regular policy meeting with a standard economic and policy go-around, so it is not a special-topic meeting and I score all dimensions. A staff-style trial-run briefing (labelled MS. MESTER) and the Q&A fragments were left in the text. I ignore that staff content and score only members' own views.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2011: post-crisis recovery, policy rate at zero, forward guidance "at least through mid-2013", Operation Twist under way, euro-area crisis dominant. I let the text drive the scores, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers.** Bernanke, Dudley, Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin and presidents Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Williams, Lockhart, Pianalto, George, Bullard, Lacker, Plosser, Fisher.

**4. Directional signals by dimension.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as roughly at or slightly below target and moderating. Core PCE is about 1.5-1.6 percent (Yellen, Williams) and wage pressure is absent. Headline is still elevated at 2.9 percent y/y (Lacker), with upside worries from George, Lacker and Kocherlakota. Net: slightly below 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as well below potential, with a gap of about 5-6 percent (Williams, Evans, Raskin's "worst slump since that of the 1930s"). A minority (Bullard, Kocherlakota, Lockhart) think it is smaller, but still negative. Below 50 (bright-line satisfied).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is about 9 percent (8.7 percent end-2011 per Williams), with only slow improvement expected. A structural-versus-cyclical debate was held, but the level is slack. Well below 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q3 final demand and consumption were a positive surprise, and investment is growing (Bernanke: nonresidential investment up 16 percent). Offsetting this: weak sentiment, flat income, a falling saving rate, weak housing, and growth of about 2.5 percent. Net slightly below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Williams cites slower potential growth from lower trend TFP and capital deepening. Fisher cites CEOs investing in productivity. Modestly below trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants describe "fragile financial condition" (Rosengren), elevated volatility, worsened liquidity in wholesale funding markets (Bernanke's summary), impaired credit availability and tight mortgage credit. Somewhat tight, above 50. There are partial offsets (Duke's balance sheet repair, Fisher's "significant liquidity").
- TREASURY_STRESS: No member describes Treasury market dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Raskin says "risk premiums remain elevated". Sovereign spreads are rising and Italian spreads are at new highs, and MBS spreads are mentioned. A stated elevated level means above 50, but the evidence is thin. Somewhat above 50, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price-move and volatility commentary (rally, volatility, Fisher's S&P 600 downside scenario), with no valuation view. Default near 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: U.S. banks are described as relatively sound, with strong capital (Duke, Bernanke's summary). European banks are stressed and MF Global failed, which raised concern about wholesale-funded dealers (Dudley). Net: below 50 but not far.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: One passing mention of "a depreciating dollar" (George). Below 50 per the bright-line, low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods are flat after a spike (Yellen: 0.2 percent over three months, versus about 3.5 percent before). Services are steady at 2 percent (Yellen). Housing and rents are rising despite the glut (Fisher, Dudley). Expectations are widely "well anchored" with some upside-risk worries.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher output gap means more above potential; evidence says well below, so the score is low. Higher labor tightness means tighter; evidence says 9 percent unemployment, so low. Higher inflation pressure means more; evidence says core is below 2 percent but headline is high, so slightly below 50. Higher financial conditions means tighter; evidence says stressed and volatile, so above 50. Higher credit spreads means more stress; evidence says elevated, so above 50. Higher banking stress means more stress; evidence says U.S. sound and Europe stressed, so just below 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee decides to hold (alternative B), keeping "at least through mid-2013" on top of the August and September easing. Bernanke says there is a "case for further action". The easing camp is Evans (A1, dissent), Rosengren, Yellen, Tarullo (tempted to dissent, wants MBS), Raskin and Dudley (escalation options, MBS). The hawkish and resistant camp is Plosser (would like C), Lacker, Fisher, George, Bullard and Lockhart (who favor waiting). Net: hold with a dovish tilt, so 41-50 or a bit below.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Evans, dovish) in the vote (9-1 on the roll call as read). Reservations: Tarullo ("considerable reluctance"), Plosser (prefers C, "willing to go along"), Kocherlakota ("willing to support"), Lacker (opposed the Twist). The framework debate shows real differences in views on the dual mandate, NAIRU, and structural versus cyclical unemployment. Moderate-to-notable disagreement.
- Financial stability emphasis: Prominent. Europe, MF Global, Dudley's liquidity-backstop argument, Yellen's long European risk section, George's imbalance worry, and Rosengren's and Fisher's market and pension comments. It is a major theme but does not rival the economic discussion.
- Forward guidance: Date-based low-rate guidance is retained. Extension and threshold-based strengthening were widely discussed, and a package is planned for January. Strong easing bias, with no tightening signal.
- Uncertainty: Williams ("Knightian uncertainty... unknown unknowns"), Bernanke ("very wide confidence band"), Dudley, Pianalto and Yellen. High, but the central forecast is stable.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 (30). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS above 50 (60). CREDIT_SPREADS "elevated" gives above 50 (58). BANKING_STRESS "capital strong" gives below 50 (44). DOLLAR_STRENGTH "depreciating" gives below 50 (40). TREASURY_STRESS is N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 and LABOR 24 differ by 6, which is fine. OUTPUT_GAP 30 and CONSUMPTION 40 differ by 10, which is fine because spending was mixed but gaps are large. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 60 and CREDIT_SPREADS 58 co-move. Treasury is N/A, so there is no tension. INFLATION_PRESSURE 46 against sub-components 45/50/58/48 is consistent.

**9. N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS: members did not discuss Treasury market functioning. The Treasury-related remarks concern Twist efficacy and purchase capacity, not dysfunction.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 46 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "The latest three-month reading on core PCE inflation is now back down to 1½ percent." Lacker: "The PCE figure for September was 2.9 percent year over year."
- Center of gravity: Core is below target and expected to ease, headline is elevated but fading, and most expect about 2 percent or below.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because core and wage pressure are low, partly offset by the high headline reading and upside-risk voices.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Williams: "our estimate of the current output gap is around 5 percent." Raskin: "The economy remains mired in the worst slump since that of the 1930s."
- Center of gravity: Large slack is the shared premise, with only a minority arguing it is smaller.
- Direction: Below neutral because output is well below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Pianalto: "unemployment will decline slowly by roughly ½ percentage point per year, leaving it at 7½ percent at the end of 2014." Kocherlakota: unemployment "around 8½ percent at the end of 2012."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment is about 9 percent and falling slowly. The structural discussion (purple squirrels, skills mismatches) does not change the slack reading.
- Direction: Below neutral because unemployment is far above any estimate of the natural rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "strong growth in final demand in Q3." Raskin: "I'm not optimistic that households are ready to be an engine of growth." Bernanke: "Total nonresidential investment... grew 16 percent at an annual rate."
- Center of gravity: Third-quarter spending was better, but most expect subpar sustained growth of about 2.5 percent, with income and the saving rate casting doubt on sustainability.
- Direction: Below trend (sluggish) on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 37 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "slower labor productivity growth owing to both a lower estimate of trend total factor productivity growth and the subdued pace of capital accumulation." Fisher: CEOs are "driving productivity significantly through technology investment."
- Center of gravity: Potential growth is judged lower than previously thought, though firms are investing for productivity.
- Direction: Modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "This highlights the fragile financial condition as we try to make our own forecast." Bernanke (second-day summary): "Liquidity has worsened in some markets, including wholesale funding markets."
- Center of gravity: Volatility, European strain, impaired mortgage and small-business credit, partly offset by healthy U.S. corporate and bank balance sheets.
- Direction: Above neutral (somewhat tight).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Raskin: "credit availability remains impaired, and risk premiums remain elevated." Yellen: "spreads on Italian debt have reached new highs."
- Center of gravity: Members mention elevated risk premiums and wide sovereign spreads, with little direct commentary on corporate spreads.
- Direction: Above neutral because a high level is stated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Raskin: "volatility in the stock market." Fisher: "I am still of the belief that we could see an S&P 500 trading at 600" under a European shock.
- Center of gravity: Only price-move and volatility comments, with no valuation judgment.
- Direction: At neutral (default range).

**BANKING_STRESS: 44 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Duke: "Capital levels are strong... this capital strength seems more real than it did in 2008." Dudley: MF Global "reinforce[s] people's anxiety about firms that are wholesale funded."
- Center of gravity: The U.S. banking system is viewed as sound, with European banks and wholesale-funded dealers as the stress pocket.
- Direction: Below neutral because U.S. banks are seen as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: George: "a depreciating dollar" listed among supports for inflation not falling below target.
- Center of gravity: A single passing reference to dollar weakness.
- Direction: Below neutral (depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 45.** Yellen: core goods prices "practically flat, rising at an annualized rate of only 0.2 percent." Fisher: gasoline and food drove headline.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 50.** Yellen: "services prices have been rising steadily at a 2 percent pace."
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Fisher: "very robust demand translating into the price of rentals." Dudley: "rents are rising despite the fact that we have a glut of unsold homes."
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48.** Williams: "inflation expectations remain well anchored." Pianalto: expectations "remain low and stable." George and Lacker flag upside risks.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I see a case for new action... I do support alternative B." Evans: "I cannot support alternative B today" (wants A1 plus asset purchases).
- Center of gravity: The Committee holds with the existing easing (Twist, mid-2013 guidance) while a sizable group wants more, and a smaller hawkish group (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, George) resists further action.
- Direction: Dovish-leaning hold, a mild easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans votes No (vote read-out). Tarullo: "with considerable reluctance, I can support alternative B." Plosser: "Obviously, there's a part of me that very much would like to see alternative C."
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent, several reluctant supporters on both sides, and open disagreement on framework and on the size and nature of slack.
- Direction: Notable but contained disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 66 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: European spillovers are "the number one risk affecting the outlook." Dudley: "we may need to think about bringing back a better liquidity backstop for our financial system if Europe really gets into great difficulty."
- Center of gravity: Europe, MF Global, liquidity, housing finance and imbalance concerns recur across most go-round statements.
- Direction: Prominent topic, but short of dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota on the proposed "six to seven quarters" duration language; Yellen: "at least" conveys "policy firming might not commence until significantly later than mid-2013." Evans: low rates while "the unemployment rate exceeds 7 percent."
- Center of gravity: Date-based zero-rate guidance is retained, and the debate is about extending or strengthening it with thresholds.
- Direction: Strongly easing-biased signal, with no tightening hint.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Williams: "Knightian uncertainty... the unknown unknowns that seem to have persisted at a high level." Bernanke: "there should be a very wide confidence band around our forecast for growth in 2012."
- Center of gravity: Many participants emphasize European tail risk, model uncertainty and uncertain slack.
- Direction: High uncertainty.
