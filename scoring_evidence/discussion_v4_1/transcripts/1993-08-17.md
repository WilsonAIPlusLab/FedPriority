# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - transcript discussion, meeting 1993-08-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed, so the foreign-exchange and domestic desk reports, the staff economic briefing and the staff answers to member questions are missing; only members' questions and remarks remain). It contains procedural openings (welcome to the new New York Fed president, election of the Vice Chairman, minutes approval), the economic go-around, a short policy-technical Q&A on what an asymmetric directive means, the Chairman's policy proposal, a go-around on the directive, and a roll call.

**2. Era context.** Summer 1993, an expansion that is slow ("slow motion recovery"), a just-enacted budget package, a Committee that has held the funds rate at 3 percent for about a year with an asymmetric (tightening-leaning) directive adopted in May. Scored from the text only.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan; Vice Chairman McDonough; Governors Mullins, LaWare, Kelley, Phillips, Angell, Lindsey; Presidents Keehn, Parry, Forrestal, Boehne, Broaddus, Syron, Hoenig, Stern, McTeer, Melzer, Jordan. The Fed staff questions are from members only.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- Inflation pressure: Participants see inflation as above the level they find acceptable (core 3 to 3-1/2 percent "settled", Mullins; "much too high a level", Kelley; "unacceptable", Jordan on the Greenbook forecast) but with pricing pressure restrained at the business level (Keehn: input costs up 1 percent or less; Stern: price increases not sticking) and recent monthly data favorable (Mullins: core CPI 2.4 percent over five months vs 4.6). Net: modestly above the Committee's price-stability aim, no acceleration. Score slightly above 50 (about 55).
- Output gap: Participants see slack: "slack in labor markets" (Parry, Phillips, Hoenig), "we don't see an overheated economy" (Phillips), moderate 2-1/2 percent growth, a weak first half (Mullins). Forrestal speculates growth could move "above potential at least for a while" in the future, which is a forecast, not the present. Below 50 (about 40).
- Labor tightness: Unemployment 6.8 percent nationally (Mullins), 9.8 percent in California (Parry); firms refuse to add full-time staff (Keehn, Forrestal, Boehne). Phillips: job growth only slightly more than handles demographics. Below 50, in the 5.5-7 percent band (about 35).
- Consumption/investment: Mixed. Mullins: second-quarter consumer spending near 4 percent, business fixed investment up 13 percent; housing improving; but auto sales easing and confidence receding (Mullins, LaWare, Phillips), investment hesitancy. Net at or slightly below trend (about 47).
- Productivity: Discussed by Phillips and Angell as a puzzling fall-off in the first half (Angell: business-sector productivity down 1-1/2 and 2-1/2 percent in two quarters). Below trend (about 33), with wide uncertainty because members call it a puzzle.
- Financial conditions: Mullins says rates "look pretty low", real short rates 2 to 3-1/2 points below neutral, "coincident indicators of easier conditions in the financial system"; Angell says a 3 percent rate "provides lots of liquidity"; Broaddus: improved financial conditions are "the main thing the economy has going"; Phillips: equity and bond markets strong; Kelley: banks want to lend. Accommodative, so below 50 (about 33).
- Treasury stress: Members discuss long-rate levels, the yield curve and a temporary "hiccup" in long rates after the Committee's signals (Chairman, McDonough). That is yield movement and its explanation, not market functioning. N/A (see item 9).
- Credit spreads: Mullins cites "low quality spreads on bonds, and very low spreads on junk bonds"; the Chairman says "yield spreads have come down". Level is low (bright-line: below 50). About 28.
- Equity valuation: Mullins sees "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices, especially the weak stocks"; Angell mentions "bubble events"; Phillips says "the equity market is strong". Mild valuation concern, so in the 31-45 band (about 38). One passage ("very low IPO volumes, the lowest in 15 years") sits oddly beside the speculative-excess context and may be a transcription error, so confidence is Low.
- Banking stress: Kelley "banks are strong again and seem to want to lend"; Phillips "banks are in better shape"; Stern and Melzer flood surveys show little disruption, with a few small ag banks possibly troubled. Healthy with minor concerns (about 30).
- Dollar strength: Phillips calls "the weakness of the dollar" an anomaly; Angell stresses a roughly 20 percent yen appreciation and a "precarious" dollar/yen rate. Weakening dollar, bright-line below 50 (about 30).

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher inflation pressure means more inflation; evidence is above-acceptable but restrained, so slightly above 50. Credit spreads: higher means more stress; evidence is "very low spreads", so low. Banking stress: "strong", "better shape", so low. Dollar: depreciation language, so below 50. Financial conditions: higher means tighter; evidence is "easier", "accommodative", "pretty low" rates, so low.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: No change to the funds rate (3 percent); the decision is whether to move the directive from asymmetric (tightening-leaning) back to symmetric. The Chairman proposes symmetry as a "tactical, not strategic" move and says short-term real rates will have to move higher at some point. The roll call is unanimous for symmetry. Hawkish pull: Angell (wants rates higher at the first window), Mullins (accommodative stance, "work ahead"), Broaddus and Jordan (prefer asymmetric). Dovish pull: LaWare, McTeer, Phillips (fiscal drag, weaker economy). Dropping the tightening bias is a mild dovish move; the underlying hold and the stated medium-term upward path are mildly hawkish. Net slightly below balanced, about 47.
- Dissent: Twelve voters, zero formal dissents (Broaddus and Jordan are not on the roll call but voice preferences for asymmetry; Broaddus "pretty strongly"). Reservations: Stern (over-interpretation, relying on the latest price data), Broaddus and Jordan (non-voting), LaWare (preferred symmetric even earlier, now reluctant on signal risk), Angell (wants higher rates), Syron (disingenuousness point toward symmetry). Disagreement is largely about signalling tactics, not economics. About 30.
- Financial stability emphasis: Not a focus. Mullins gives a paragraph on speculative excess and easier financial conditions; Angell mentions bubble events and liquidity; the bond-market rally and long rates get significant attention as financial conditions; flood effects on banks get attention from Stern and Melzer. Era anchor is 1-10 for 1993-96; the attention to bond-market conditions and speculative excess lifts it somewhat. About 18.
- Forward guidance tone: Chairman and many members say the next move is likely up in the longer run ("short-term real rates will have to move higher", "tactical, not strategic"), but the directive formally becomes symmetric and no move is expected for months. Mild tightening bias without a signal of action: about 56.
- Uncertainty: Mostly standard "risks about balanced" (Keehn, Parry, Syron, Boehne), plus uncertainty about fiscal and health-care effects (Hoenig "very uncertain future", Forrestal, Broaddus, LaWare "uncertainty inevitably inhibits growth"), and puzzles on productivity and M2 (Angell, Phillips). Somewhat elevated but not unusual: about 33.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 with "slack" language: satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 with "easier", "accommodative": satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS stated level "very low": below 50, satisfied. BANKING_STRESS "strong", "better shape": below 50, satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH weakening: below 50, satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 40 and LABOR 35 move together (5 apart). OUTPUT_GAP 40 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47 are 7 apart (consumption and investment held up despite slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 and CREDIT_SPREADS 28 co-move (both loose). Treasury N/A. Inflation 55 against sub-components: goods 38 (restrained input and selling prices), expectations 58 (members worry about expectations above acceptable); weighted result is consistent with a mildly above-target reading because the level, not the momentum, drives the parent score.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only yield levels and a transient "hiccup" in long rates are discussed; no functioning or liquidity commentary. INFLATION_SERVICES: no services-specific price commentary by members. INFLATION_HOUSING: Forrestal and Syron mention home price firming but not shelter or rent inflation; treated as not discussed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 +-7 (High-Moderate; recorded Moderate)**
- Evidence: Mullins, "inflation apparently has settled down in the 3 to 3-1/2 percent range"; Kelley, "Inflation now seems to be trendless, albeit at much too high a level"; Keehn, "restrained the price pressures seem to be".
- Center of gravity: Level seen as above what is acceptable but without acceleration and with recent favorable monthly prints.
- Direction: Above neutral because the level (3 to 3-1/2 percent) is repeatedly called too high, tempered by restrained business pricing.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Phillips, "We don't see an overheated economy... if you look at the slack in labor markets"; Parry, "current slack in labor markets".
- Center of gravity: Moderate growth of about 2-1/2 percent with slack still present.
- Direction: Below potential because members cite slack and no overheating.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Mullins, unemployment "6.8 percent or 6.85 percent"; Keehn, restraint on hiring "has become something of an absolute mindset among a growing number of CEOs".
- Center of gravity: Unemployment about 6.8 percent with firms avoiding permanent hires.
- Direction: Below neutral because unemployment is elevated and hiring weak.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Mullins, "consumer spending advanced at almost a 4 percent annual pace... Business fixed investment advanced at a 13 percent annual rate" but "both consumer and business confidence continue to recede"; Boehne, "2-1/2 percent is the number".
- Center of gravity: Spending and investment solid in the second quarter, with confidence and the fiscal package expected to restrain.
- Direction: Near and slightly below neutral given the mixed picture and anticipated drag.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 33 +-10 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Angell, "decline in productivity for the business sector of 1-1/2 and 2-1/2 percent two quarters in a row"; Phillips, "the fall-off in productivity in the first half of this year seems anomalous".
- Center of gravity: Productivity weak in the first half, viewed as a puzzle.
- Direction: Below trend because measured productivity fell.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Mullins, "coincident indicators of easier conditions in the financial system" and rates "2 to 3-1/2 percent below a neutral stance"; Angell, "a 3 percent rate provides lots of liquidity in the financial markets".
- Center of gravity: Low real rates, a bond rally and willing lenders, with no sign of tightness.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser), consistent with the accommodative bright line.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Yield and curve discussion only (McDonough on the 2- to 30-year spread at 235 basis points; the Chairman on a temporary "hiccup" in long rates). No market-functioning commentary.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Mullins, "low quality spreads on bonds, and very low spreads on junk bonds"; Chairman, "yield spreads have come down".
- Center of gravity: Spreads described as low and falling.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is very low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-9 (Low)**
- Evidence: Mullins, "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices, especially the weak stocks"; Phillips, "The equity market is strong".
- Center of gravity: Mild valuation concern, concentrated on weaker stocks; one supporting phrase about IPO volume looks garbled.
- Direction: Below neutral on this scale (stretched valuation concern) but modest.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Kelley, "banks are strong again and seem to want to lend"; Phillips, "Banks are in better shape".
- Center of gravity: Banks healthy; flood-related problems limited to a few small ag banks (Melzer).
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as strong.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Angell, "a 20 percent appreciation of the yen" with a dollar/yen rate that is "precarious"; Phillips, "The weakness of the dollar is another anomalous situation."
- Center of gravity: Dollar weak against the yen.
- Direction: Below neutral because the dollar is depreciating.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38 (value only).** Keehn, a manufacturer expects "a reduction in their outside purchase prices and costs this year of about 1 percent"; steel price increase "clearly not going to stick".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 58 (value only).** McDonough, inflationary expectations "a great deal higher than I believe this Committee should find acceptable"; Broaddus, survey evidence "inflation expectations in some quarters may actually have worsened"; the Chairman says expectations have not gone up.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 47 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Chairman, "we can afford at this stage to move back to symmetry... we have moved tactically, not strategically"; Angell, "more important than going back to asymmetric toward tightness would be for us to look for windows to get the fed funds rate up".
- Center of gravity: Hold at 3 percent and drop the tightening bias, with a hawkish minority and an expected eventual rise.
- Direction: Slightly below balanced because the directive bias is removed, offset by stated medium-term tightening intent.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Roll call has twelve Yes votes; Broaddus, "I would favor pretty strongly sticking with an asymmetric directive"; Jordan, "I'm not sure where I come out."
- Center of gravity: No formal dissents; a handful of reservations and two non-voters preferring asymmetry, mostly about signalling.
- Direction: Above the near-consensus floor but below distinct camps.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Mullins, speculative excess and "easier conditions in the financial system"; Angell, "an opportunity for certain bubble events because money is scurrying here and there".
- Center of gravity: Brief but real references from two members, plus attention to the bond market and flood effects on banks.
- Direction: Low attention overall.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Chairman, "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher"; McDonough, "highly unlikely that we will change the fed funds rate" before the next meeting.
- Center of gravity: Neutral near-term directive, longer-term message of eventual tightening.
- Direction: Mildly hawkish forward signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 33 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "we see a very uncertain future and pretty modest growth"; Keehn, "the risks are about evenly balanced".
- Center of gravity: Standard caveats plus fiscal and health-care policy uncertainty, and unresolved puzzles on productivity and M2.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated.
