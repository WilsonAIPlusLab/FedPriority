## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It is very short, roughly 1,000 words in total and mostly one long statement by the Chairman. It is not a standard go-around. Staff turns are removed, so what remains is the Chairman's introductions, three short member questions (Parry, Broaddus), a Chairman monologue on a possible intermeeting policy move, brief agreement from the Vice Chairman and Mr. Parry, and scheduling. Per the edge-case rule for very short discussions, confidence is Low or at most Moderate throughout.

**Era context.** The text refers to a pending/ongoing war with Iraq, oil price effects from Nigeria and Venezuela, a "bursting bubble", and corporate governance concerns. I take the backdrop only from the text.

**Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan gives all the substantive content. Vice Chairman McDonough and Mr. Parry say they agree with the Chairman's analysis. Parry proposes an abbreviated go-around at a later meeting. Parry's and Broaddus's questions to staff are procedural or about data mechanics. The one substantive point in Parry's question is the phrase "eliminating excess capacity".

**Core dimensions, direction first.**

This is an energy price shock, not an assessment of inflation pressure. I score a near-neutral value with wide bands and Low confidence.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as below potential. Parry asks about "progress in eliminating excess capacity". Greenspan says the economy has "edged off" and that GDP between December and March may not have been positive. This means a gap below 50 (bright-line satisfied), but it is only modestly described, so about 38.

- PRODUCTIVITY: "The productivity data are obviously very good." This is above trend, but the phrase is brief. About 68, Moderate.
