# v4.1 scoring: transcript discussion, meeting date 2004-01-28

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. About the first half is a first-day deliberation on Committee communications (risk-assessment wording, early release of minutes, role of projections), plus organizational housekeeping (elections, warehousing, GNMA). The second half (second-day morning) holds members' questions to staff, the economic go-around (District reports and national outlook, Parry through Bies), the Chairman's policy opening, the policy go-around, the press-statement discussion and a roll-call read-out. The communications material is procedure/institutional design and is not scored except where it reveals the policy stance. The scored material is the go-around and the policy round.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Early 2004, funds rate at 1 percent, a "considerable period" commitment in place, jobs data weak but output strong. Members' own text carries the scoring.

**Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan; Vice Chair Geithner; Governors Bernanke, Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kohn, Olson; Presidents Broaddus, Guynn, Hoenig, McTeer, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Pianalto, Poole, Santomero, Stern.

**Dimension reasoning (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Core PCE is about 1 percent (Parry: "uncomfortably low levels"; Kohn: "core inflation rate in the neighborhood of 1 percent"; Broaddus: risk of "further disinflation"). Counter-currents: Hoenig sees a rise of up to half a point, Pianalto hears about price increases, Poole warns of an inflation surprise, Geithner sees a "rising, if still small, probability" of moving higher. Net: clearly below target, with risk of a turn. Around 28.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Slack persists though it is expected to close ("employment gap" Broaddus; Kohn: "We haven't seen any closing of the output gap"; Hoenig: gap "as little as 1/2 percent ... as much as 2 percent"). Geithner expects growth "somewhat above" potential growth, which is a rate statement, not a level. About 38.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Payrolls disappointing, jobless recovery, unemployment cited at 5.3 to 5.9 percent, "labor markets remain soft", "hemorrhaging in the factory sector". A few positive hints (claims, hours, bankers unable to find workers). About 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend. GDP growth of 5 percent plus, capital spending plans surging, consumption "buoyant", housing strong. Greenspan: "extraordinarily benign". Not described as unsustainable. About 67.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Far above trend. "Astounding productivity gains" (Kohn), 5 percent rates, Greenspan "historically unprecedented" if it continues, "new trend in productivity". About 84.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose (below 50). Geithner: "very accommodative"; Kohn: "even more stimulative"; Minehan: "extremely accommodative financial markets"; Greenspan: "pumping very considerable liquidity". About 17.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yields and futures were discussed as expectations/credibility signals, not as functioning problems. Treasury market dysfunction not discussed: N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Compressed (below 50). Minehan: "very low credit spreads"; Geithner: "very low spreads to Treasuries"; Greenspan: "yield spreads continue to fall". About 17.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities are rising and considered rich-ish but explicitly "not yet ... bubble" (Greenspan); Minehan sees them "not out of line with profits"; Gramlich/staff exchange on earnings-price ratio and "new bubbles". Valuation concern modest-to-moderate. About 36.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy, improving. Olson: "bottoming out", bankers talk of "watch lists" rather than losses, but willing to take more risk; Pianalto: "credit quality ... very good". Low stress, minor caution on risk-taking. About 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has depreciated (Kohn "depreciated nearly 5 percent"; Gramlich on current-account adjustment; Geithner "dollar decline"). Below 50. About 28.
- Inflation sub-components: GOODS touched (Pianalto steel and commodity price rises; Moskow mixed price-hike stories; Poole fuel surcharges) against weak retail prices: about 42. SERVICES not discussed: N/A. HOUSING: a house-price-to-rent exchange occurs only as asset-valuation context, not as shelter-inflation commentary: N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Greenspan's long-bond comment, Poole's "expectations term", Geithner's regional survey; no unanchoring either way: about 43.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Chairman proposes holding at 1 percent and replacing "considerable period" with "patient". All who spoke supported the rate. On language, most supported the change (several wanted it dropped entirely, Broaddus would have kept "considerable period"). Greenspan argues "interest rates are too low", while Kohn and Gramlich-opening remarks stress caution and accommodation. Net: hold with a modest tightening-leaning exit from an easing commitment. Lean hawkish but mild: about 54.
- Dissent: No dissent, unanimous vote. Differences are about wording (Moskow, Bies, Olson, Pianalto prefer dropping rather than "patience"; Broaddus reluctant), about the cost of disinflation vs inflation (Kohn/Broaddus vs Greenspan/Minehan), and, in the communications part, about transparency design. About 20.
- Financial stability emphasis: Prominent in Greenspan's remarks (spreads, liquidity, snapback), Geithner, Ferguson (carry trade, risk pricing), Minehan, Kohn (credit risk), Olson (bank lending), Gramlich (bubbles, external imbalance). Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not dominant. About 52.
- Forward guidance: Retirement of the "considerable period" commitment replaced by "patient", with the downward tilt on inflation risks kept and explicit "baby steps"/"not a call to arms". Mild tightening-direction signal: about 57.
- Uncertainty: Considerable uncertainty about productivity, labor-force participation and payrolls, the output gap and Phillips curve (Pianalto "little confidence in forecasts of ... inflation"; Kohn "considerable uncertainties"; Ferguson torn between risks). Offset by Stern ("uncertainty ... has diminished") and Geithner ("range of uncertainty ... has narrowed"). Net somewhat elevated: about 38.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential and score < 50: yes (38). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative and score < 50: yes (17). CREDIT_SPREADS "very low" and score < 50: yes (17). BANKING_STRESS healthy and score < 50: yes (30). DOLLAR depreciation and score < 50: yes (28). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Polarity checks.** INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher = more pressure; evidence says low, so low score. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter; evidence says slack, so low. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says very loose, so low. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = wider; evidence says compressed, so low. PRODUCTIVITY: higher = more growth; evidence says exceptional, so high. HAWKISH: higher = tighter; evidence says hold with mildly less accommodative signaling, so just above 50.

**Coherence checks.** OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (34): within 15. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (67): 29 points apart; explained by the fact that strong growth is closing a gap that is still negative in level, and members stress that growth is strong while employment and resource utilization remain slack. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (17) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (17): consistent. TREASURY_STRESS N/A: consistent with normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) vs sub-components (goods 42, expectations 43): parent lower because the two scored sub-components are from partial and anecdotal comment while headline core readings of about 1 percent dominate member language; accepted.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction discussed. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no member commentary on those price categories.

**Text-quality note.** Staff turns are removed, so some member responses to staff questions appear without the question (for example in the Q&A). Extraction otherwise reads cleanly. The roll-call read-out at the end lists only nine "Yes" lines and no dissent; the missing remaining voters are an extraction truncation, not a sign of dissent (Chairman and others voiced support in the transcript and nobody objected). Hard line breaks and a few garbled hyphenations were ignored.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Parry: "core PCE inflation to come in at 1 percent this year ... inflation will decline further to uncomfortably low levels." Kohn: "with the core inflation rate in the neighborhood of 1 percent." Center of gravity: inflation well below target with downside-risk worry, small upside voices. Direction: below neutral because core is about 1 percent and disinflation is the leading worry.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn: "We haven't seen any closing of the output gap." Broaddus: "employment gap will be eliminated ... not completely until the end of next year." Center: gap negative but narrowing under 5 percent growth. Direction: below neutral because slack remains.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 34 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Pianalto: "something unusual is happening both on the supply side as well as the demand side" with firms "adamant about not expanding worker head counts." Hoenig: "Labor markets also remain soft." Center: unemployment above 5.5 percent and weak payrolls. Direction: below neutral because of slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 67 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Parry: "economic activity is on a path of robust growth ... GDP to grow about 5 percent." Greenspan: "outlook seems extraordinarily benign." Center: broad-based, strengthening spending and investment, above trend. Direction: above neutral because growth far exceeds trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 84 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn: "astounding productivity gains in recent quarters." Greenspan: "if that goes on much longer, it will become historically unprecedented." Center: exceptional productivity gains, much discussed. Direction: above neutral because gains are far above historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 17 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Geithner: "financial conditions are now very accommodative." Kohn: "financial conditions have become even more stimulative." Center: multiple members call markets extremely supportive. Direction: below neutral (loose) because spreads are tight, equity up and rates down.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No discussion of Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 17 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Minehan: "very low credit spreads"; Greenspan: "yield spreads continue to fall." Center: very compressed, with worry about snapback. Direction: below neutral because the stated level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 36 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan: "not yet at a point where 'bubble' is the appropriate word"; Minehan: "rising equity markets are not out of line with profits." Center: modestly rich, watched. Direction: below neutral (valuation concern) but mild.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Olson: "bottoming out ... now talking about watch lists"; Pianalto: credit quality "has been very good." Center: healthy system, some risk-taking caution. Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 28 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn: "the dollar has depreciated nearly 5 percent on the broad index." Geithner: "The fact that the dollar decline has been so benign." Center: depreciation, discussed as supportive and an external-imbalance risk. Direction: below neutral because the dollar weakened.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42.** Evidence: Pianalto: steel and commodity prices "rising sharply" but "have not found their way into retail prices"; Moskow: several firms unable to push price hikes. Mixed, slightly below stable.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43.** Evidence: Greenspan wonders whether the long-bond curve shows no expected inflation rise or "looking in a mirror"; Geithner notes regional survey expectations higher. Roughly anchored, slightly below target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 54 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan: "interest rates are too low," proposing "patience"; Kohn: "very cautious about moving from the very accommodative stance." Center: hold at 1 percent, begin to remove easing commitment. Direction: slightly above 50 because the signaled change loosens the pledge and a tightening bias is argued by the Chair, offset by strong dovish caution.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: unanimous support for the rate; Broaddus: "if it were my call, I would leave the 'considerable period' language ... a little longer. ... I could certainly accept your recommendation." Center: no dissents, wording-level preferences. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan: "we are undoubtedly pumping very considerable liquidity into the financial system"; Ferguson: "the dollar carry trade has become extremely well entrenched." Center: several participants, including the Chair, treat valuations/spreads as a substantive worry. Direction: moderate-to-prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Santomero: "These are baby steps ... we need an exit strategy"; Ferguson: "keeping the inflation balance a little to the downside ... starting to remove the 'considerable period' commitment." Center: mild shift away from an easing pledge, explicitly not an imminent tightening. Direction: modestly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn: "considerable uncertainties around this most likely outcome"; Stern: "uncertainty around the forecast has diminished." Center: elevated on productivity/labor/gap, offset by confidence in growth. Direction: somewhat elevated.
