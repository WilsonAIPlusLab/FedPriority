# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2016-03-16 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: economic go-around (day 1), Chair's summary, then policy go-around (day 2) and vote read-out. Staff turns removed; members' questions in the staff Q&A remain (recession-probability models, inflation-expectations surveys, dealer survey).

**2. Era context.** Spring 2016, three months after the December 2015 liftoff. Early-year financial turmoil (China/RMB, oil, bank stocks, negative-rate worries) has largely reversed. Score rests on what participants say, not on this backdrop.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Yellen, Vice Chair Dudley, Governors Fischer, Brainard, Powell, Tarullo, Presidents Williams, Rosengren, Bullard, Harker, Kaplan, Evans, Mester, Lockhart, George, Lacker, Kashkari. Vote read-out: 9 yes, 1 no (George).

**4. Dimension directions.**
- Inflation: Core PCE 1.7% in January, headline 1.3%; "inflation is still running below our 2 percent target" (Kashkari), "reasonably close" (Fischer). Participants see inflation pressure below target but rising, with expectations a concern. Below midpoint (~40).
- Output gap: Growth "modestly above potential" (Powell), "modestly above-trend" (Tarullo), but the "output gap ... basically closed" (George) and "employment gap is essentially closed". Net at/near potential; bright line "near potential" 48-52. Score 49.
- Labor: UE 4.9%, "close to the maximum sustainable level of employment" (Fischer), "essentially closed" (George), quits back to pre-recession. Some residual slack (Yellen, Tarullo, Kaplan, Brainard), others tight (Mester, Lacker, Williams, Harker). At/near NAIRU: 52.
- Consumption/investment: Household spending "moderate", "relative bright spot"; business fixed investment weak, energy capex and net exports drag. Net slightly below to at trend: 46.
- Productivity: Williams, Powell, Harker discuss; productivity growth very weak (~0.5% five-year average, "slow growth is real"). Well below historical trend: 30.
- Financial conditions: tightened sharply Jan-Feb, then eased; Powell "about where they were in December"; Evans "financial conditions are more restrictive" than December; Mester "only a bit tighter than anticipated". Slightly tighter than neutral: 54.
- Treasury stress: no market-dysfunction language (only passing "flight to quality" into Treasuries). N/A.
- Credit spreads: Brainard "pronounced widening", BBB +75bp since mid-2015, HY more; Evans "widened considerably"; Yellen BBB +30bp since December; Powell "come back in significantly but remain far from fully recovered"; Mester "narrowing of credit spreads". These are almost all direction statements with no explicit level word ("wide"/"elevated"/"tight"). Per v4.1 rule, direction only: 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Score 55.
- Equity valuation: Price moves discussed (declines, recovery) but valuation view only from Dudley ("things don't look overvalued relative to long-term bond yields") and Kaplan ("overallocated to risk assets"). Default 48, Low confidence.
- Banking stress: Rosengren worries large banks "may be more financially fragile than I had assumed despite ... capital positions"; Tarullo attributes bank stock declines to profitability skepticism rather than funding; Dudley notes negative rates squeeze margins; Mester notes CRE lending aggression. No one calls banks stressed or healthy outright. Mixed: 47.
- Dollar: Appreciation through 2014-15 is a continuing drag (Brainard, Kashkari, Kaplan, Yellen), but the dollar has recently retraced ("2 percent below" winter value, "essentially unchanged since December"). Concern remains about further appreciation. Bright line: dollar not depreciating on net; 54, Moderate-Low.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods/services/housing not discussed as categories (only passing durable-goods contribution to January core, George; health care payments) so N/A. Expectations: market-based compensation down, surveys edging down, "fragile" (Evans, Bullard, Brainard, Yellen, Kashkari), with some counter-arguments (Lacker, Mester, Harker, Powell). Below target / drifting: 38.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher inflation pressure = more inflation; evidence says below target, so <50 matches. Higher financial conditions = tighter; evidence slightly tighter, 54 matches. Credit spreads higher = more stress; widened, 55 matches. Dollar higher = stronger; recent retrace but still strong, 54.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: Committee holds (Alt B, 9-1). Nearly all expect further gradual hikes but cite global risk, inflation expectations, asymmetry at the ELB; SEP path lowered. Hawkish tail: George dissent for 25bp; Lacker favors C but supports B; Bullard sees case for moving; Mester, Lockhart, Harker, Rosengren, Williams want April live. Dovish tail: Evans, Tarullo, Kashkari, Brainard, Dudley. Balanced, hold with slight caution: 48.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (George, hawkish). Reservations: Lacker (C), Williams ("falling behind the curve"), Bullard (SEP criticism, argued for moving), Mester ("would have advocated" moving). Some disagreement on risks (Tarullo vs. Williams, Powell vs. Williams on dots and path), but statement unanimously welcomed on language. Moderate: 37.
- Financial stability emphasis: financial volatility, spreads, bank stock moves, negative-rate effects, and market endogeneity are central to the economic round and the risk-management argument for holding. Several participants at length (Rosengren, Brainard, Evans, Powell, Dudley, Fischer). Prominent but not dominant: 62.
- Forward guidance: Alt B keeps April and June open; gradual increases still expected; data dependent; no pre-commitment (Dudley, Fischer "not take April or June off the table"). Mild tightening bias but explicitly patient: 52.
- Uncertainty: "uncertainty about global growth has increased" (Fischer), "volatility of the volatility", "elevated" (Kashkari), "highly uncertain" (Brainard), "considerable uncertainty" (Evans). Counter: Mester "roughly balanced", Lacker confident. Elevated: 56.

**7. Bright-line check.** Output gap 49 (in 48-52 band, near potential). Financial conditions 54 (>50; no "accommodative" about conditions). Credit spreads 55 (direction only, widening, in 45-55). Banking 47 (no "healthy/sound" or "stressed" verdict). Dollar 54 (not depreciation). Treasury N/A.

**8. Coherence.** Output gap 49 vs labor 52 vs consumption/investment 46: within 15. FC 54 and spreads 55 co-move. Treasury N/A (no dysfunction). Parent inflation (40) vs expectations (38): consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** Treasury stress: no participant describes Treasury market functioning. Inflation goods/services/housing: no category-level discussion. Everything else has at least some discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 +-6 [High]**
Evidence: Kashkari: "inflation is still running below our 2 percent target"; Brainard: core PCE "stuck in the vicinity of 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 percent for the past three years", now 1.7 in January.
Center of gravity: Below target but edging up; many expect gradual return.
Direction: Below neutral because inflation is under 2 percent and participants worry about persistence of undershoot.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 49 +-4 [Moderate]**
Evidence: George: "output gap that is basically closed"; Powell: "growth ... around 2 percent, modestly above potential."
Center of gravity: Economy about at potential with a modest overshoot expected.
Direction: At neutral (near potential) because the gap is described as closed with slight above-trend growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 +-4 [High]**
Evidence: Fischer: "We are close to the maximum sustainable level of employment"; Tarullo: "some slack remaining, though ... considerably less."
Center of gravity: Near full employment at 4.9% UE; camps differ on residual slack.
Direction: At/slightly above neutral because many see the employment gap closed while others see a little slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-5 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Yellen: "household spending continues to be a relative bright spot ... a little less so" after retail sales revisions; "spending and production indicators for other sectors ... weak" (cap goods flat, drilling contracting).
Center of gravity: Moderate consumer and housing growth offset by weak business investment and net exports.
Direction: Slightly below/at trend because strength in households is netted against clear weakness elsewhere.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 30 +-6 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Williams: "the slowdown in business-sector labor productivity since 2004 has actually been larger than is reported"; Powell: five-year average of 0.5 percent.
Center of gravity: Productivity growth viewed as genuinely weak, well below historical trend.
Direction: Below neutral because members describe a persistent real slowdown.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 54 +-5 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Evans: "financial conditions are more restrictive" than in December; Powell: "tightening in financial conditions has been unwinding ... conditions are now about where they were in December."
Center of gravity: Sharp tightening in Jan-Feb largely reversed; residual tightening from spreads and risk premiums.
Direction: Slightly above neutral because conditions remain somewhat tighter than anticipated.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (not discussed as a functioning issue).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 +-7 [Low]**
Evidence: Brainard: "pronounced widening of corporate bond spreads ... triple-B spreads have increased about 75 basis points"; Powell: "Spreads have come back in significantly but remain far from fully recovered."
Center of gravity: Spreads widened notably since mid-2015 and partly retraced; no level word given.
Direction: Slightly above neutral per direction-only rule (45-55, side of the move, Low confidence).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-6 [Low]**
Evidence: Dudley: "Things don't look overvalued relative to long-term bond yields"; Yellen: "equity valuations are down only a little since our December meeting."
Center of gravity: Price swings discussed, valuation concern modest.
Direction: Near neutral because there is price commentary but no valuation concern either way.

**BANKING_STRESS: 47 +-8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Rosengren: "major banks may be more financially fragile than I had assumed despite the improvements ... in capital positions"; Tarullo: bank equity declines tied to profitability skepticism rather than funding.
Center of gravity: One notable worry about bank fragility, countered by another member's different reading; capital improved.
Direction: Near neutral, because mixed signals with sound capital but market-implied fragility.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 +-8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Brainard: dollar "now 2 percent below its trade-weighted value earlier in the winter"; Kashkari: "potential for further appreciation of the dollar"; Yellen: "dollar appreciation and slow foreign growth continues to hold down exports."
Center of gravity: Lagged strong-dollar drag persists; the dollar has retraced a little recently.
Direction: Slightly above neutral because the cumulative strength and its drag dominate the discussion, though the recent move was a pullback.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A** (no category-level discussion).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38**
Evidence: Evans: "persistently low readings on inflation breakevens ... suggest that longer-run inflation expectations may have already slipped below our inflation target"; Mester: "reasonably well anchored."
Center of gravity: Market measures low and surveys edging down; members split on how much weight to give, but the worry is downward.
Direction: Below neutral (below target drift).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 48 +-5 [High]**
Evidence: Yellen: "further gradual increases in our target range will be appropriate if the labor market continues to improve" alongside "the case for leaving the target range unchanged ... seems clear"; Lacker: "I favor alternative C."
Center of gravity: Hold with a gradual-hike expectation and a reduced rate path; hawks and doves both present.
Direction: Slightly dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 37 +-6 [High]**
Evidence: Vote 9-1 with George dissenting for a 25bp hike; Lacker "I favor alternative C"; Williams: "increasingly concerned that we are falling further 'behind the curve.'"
Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus several vocal reservations on both sides, mostly about risk weighting and tactics.
Direction: Moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [High]**
Evidence: Brainard: "the endogeneity of financial conditions"; Evans' VAR work equating spread widening to "multiple federal funds rate increases"; Rosengren on bank fragility.
Center of gravity: Market volatility and financial conditions run through nearly every member's remarks and the case for holding.
Direction: Prominent topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 +-7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Fischer: "We should be careful not to say anything that takes either April or June off the table"; Dudley: statement "neither pre-commits us to a move at the April or June meeting nor rules it out."
Center of gravity: Patient, data-dependent, with gradual increases still expected.
Direction: Roughly neutral with a slight tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 56 +-7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: Fischer: "my uncertainty about global growth has increased"; Kashkari: "the uncertainty continues to be elevated"; Evans: "considerable uncertainty and downside risks emanating from international economic conditions."
Center of gravity: Most stress global and inflation-expectations uncertainty; some (Mester, Lacker, Williams) see risks as balanced.
Direction: Elevated.
