# v4.1 scoring: transcript discussion, meeting 2008-10-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds: a long staff Q&A on emerging-market swap lines, open market operations and the funds market; a roll-call on the swap resolution; Reserve Bank/Board go-around on conditions (evening of day 1 plus the morning of day 2); the Chairman's summary; the policy go-around on alternatives A (-50bp) and B (-25bp); the Chairman's long policy remarks; and a vote read-out (unanimous Yes on a cut of the funds rate target to about 1 percent). Large parts (swap-line questions, IOER mechanics) are procedure or technical and are ignored for scoring except where members state views on conditions and policy.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2008, weeks after the Lehman failure, with a coordinated intermeeting cut on October 8. I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Nearly all members speak. Strongly dovish: Yellen, Kohn, Geithner, Rosengren, Pianalto, Bernanke. Cut but with qualms or tactics emphasis: Evans, Kroszner, Warsh, Stern, Lockhart (prefers 25bp), Fisher (open-minded, ends supporting A), Duke (torn between A and B, supports A), Lacker (supports 50bp, wants IOER/target clarity). Prefer to hold: Hoenig, Bullard, Plosser (Plosser will not dissent).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint and falling. Commodity prices plunging, a stronger dollar, slack, "no inflationary tail" (Fisher), "deflationary forces are ascendant" (Geithner), Yellen fears inflation "falling below the level consistent with price stability". Some push back (Plosser, Pianalto, Lacker doubt a big output gap will deliver disinflation, and the Chairman notes some see supply-side effects) but even they expect core to drift down. Magnitude: strong language, not yet a deflation spiral. About 27.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential, strongly. "Full-blown recession", "substantial resource gaps remain open throughout the projection period" (Evans), "among the largest postwar recessions" (Bernanke). Lacker and Plosser argue potential is also damaged, which shrinks the gap, but the economy is still described as far below the level of activity. About 17. Bright line (<50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Labor market weak and deteriorating. UE expected to peak above 7 to 7.5 percent (Rosengren, Kohn, Plosser 7.25), "shedding head count dramatically" (Fisher), the Chairman notes claims have not yet shown the full weakness and that UE started from 4.4 percent. Current level is still moderate (mid-6s at most) but the direction and forecast are sharply loose. About 28.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Collapsing. "Sudden stop", "capital spending is dead in the water", real consumption falling, consumer confidence "from 61 to 38", durables "hit particularly hard". About 15.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only brief remarks: Warsh expects productivity and trend growth to fall, Pianalto "slower productivity growth" from reallocation, Plosser likens financial dislocation to a negative productivity shock. Cyclical productivity itself is not assessed, but the direction mentioned is below trend. About 35, low confidence.

**Financial.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Very tight. "Tremendous tightening of financial conditions" (Kohn), "financial conditions will remain quite tight", "credit intermediation process ... is fundamentally broken" (Warsh), Bernanke on a "very broad based tightening". Partial improvement noted (LIBOR-OIS narrowed, CP better) but "we're still not back" to pre-Lehman (Rosengren). About 86. Bright line (>50) satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only incidental: Geithner "the world is a little short of the risk-free asset", Kohn on longer Treasury yields rising on supply, Bernanke saying TIPS are "distorted by illiquidity". No account of Treasury market dysfunction by members. Score near the normal-to-mildly-elevated range, low confidence: 52.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is high. Yellen: Baa +200bp, low-grade +700bp; Kohn: investment-grade +200 to 250bp; Bernanke: "spreads remain much wider", EMBI 280 to 850bp; Warsh: "spreads across asset classes are likely to stay far wider than historical norms". Some narrowing in recent days, but the level is wide and availability constrained. About 86. Bright line (>50) satisfied.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities down about 20 percent over the intermeeting period "even after the 900 point increase"; Bernanke: "declined sharply amid conditions of low liquidity and extraordinary volatility"; Rosengren expects further forced selling (hedge fund NAV triggers). Mostly price-move description plus concern about disorderly selling, little valuation commentary, so I sit above the neutral band but only moderately. About 63.
- BANKING_STRESS: Significant stress. Failures (Lehman, AIG, WaMu, Wachovia), "Banks have probably not reserved sufficiently for the credit losses to come" (Bernanke), tightening on every dimension (Kohn), CRE and construction lending dim (Duke). Offsetting: capital injections and guarantees, Duke's bankers report C&I book fine, Lacker/Hoenig say solid borrowers still get credit. About 76. Bright line (>50) satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar "appreciated nearly 10 percent" (Yellen, Kohn), "appreciate very sharply" (Bernanke); consequences for net exports discussed. Appreciation, notable. About 70. Bright line (>50) satisfied.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: plunging commodity and import prices, retail discounting (Lockhart), firms losing pricing power; low. Services: not discussed, N/A. Housing: house prices in freefall is not shelter-cost inflation and rents are not discussed, N/A. Expectations: coming down in surveys and TIPS, Lacker notes the Greenbook five-year inflation falling to 1.0, Geithner fears an "abrupt change in inflation expectations into deflationary territory"; drifting below target, 30.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: A 50bp cut to 1 percent with strong support for more and for QE-style work: Yellen "put as much stimulus ... as soon as we can", Kohn "almost impossible at this point to go too far", Geithner "don't see a good case for monetary policy gradualism", Bernanke "do whatever it takes". Minority prefers to hold (Hoenig, Bullard, Plosser, with Lockhart for 25), but their reasons are partly that liquidity/QE is already the policy, not tightening. Plosser's talk of future aggressive hikes is a distant exit concern. Strong dovish. 13.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: No formal dissent; the roll-call is unanimous. But three participants (Hoenig, Bullard, Plosser) say they would prefer to hold, Plosser votes with "a very reluctant yea" and Stern "reluctantly" on the swaps, Duke is torn, Lockhart prefers 25, Fisher is undecided until the end, and there is a tense exchange between Geithner and Plosser over blame for lost confidence. Economic assessment is shared; the disagreement is about tactics and effectiveness. "Zero dissents but several reluctant" supports 35 to 45. 38.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Dominant; essentially every statement is about the credit crisis, swap lines, liquidity facilities, spreads, bank guarantees, TARP, hedge fund redemptions. 90.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Strong easing bias. Kohn: "I think it will call for further easing at the next FOMC meeting"; Bernanke: prepared to "move as low as makes sense", staff to define the "effective zero"; Geithner: "keep them low enough long enough". Offsetting: Bernanke says December may allow standing still, and members stress eventual exit, but there is no tightening signal. 10.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Very high. "Uncharted waters" (Evans), "never seen a situation like this" and "vaguest notion" (Kohn), "a great deal of uncertainty" (Evans, Plosser, Bullard "explosion of uncertainty"), Bernanke "I don't think we know what the bottom is", volatility "up dramatically" (Kroszner). 84.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; wide spreads = high. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: tight = high. BANKING_STRESS: failures, under-reserving = high. DOLLAR: appreciation = above 50. INFLATION_PRESSURE: falling, below target = low. OUTPUT_GAP: deep slack = low. HAWKISH_DOVISH: easing = low. FORWARD_GUIDANCE: easing bias = low.

**6. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP <50 yes; FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS >50 yes; CREDIT_SPREADS >50 (stated wide level) yes; BANKING_STRESS >50 yes; DOLLAR >50 yes; TREASURY_STRESS near normal (no dysfunction described) yes.

**7. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 17 vs LABOR 28: 11 apart, acceptable (labor lags; UE level still moderate). OUTPUT_GAP 17 vs CONSUMPTION 15: aligned. FC 86 vs spreads 86: aligned. FC vs Treasury stress 52: Treasury dysfunction was not described by members, so it does not co-move; explained by low confidence and limited discussion. Inflation parent 27 vs goods 22 and expectations 30: consistent.

**8. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING not discussed as price pressures by participants.

**Text note.** A few staff-answer fragments were removed (e.g. "552 U.S.C. (b)(4)" redaction in Plosser's turn and many speaker turns with no text); these do not affect scoring. Roughly the first third of the document is swap-line procedure.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Fisher "There is no inflationary tail, or if there is, it is completely flattened"; Geithner "deflationary forces are ascendant around the globe". Center of gravity: inflation falling fast toward and below price stability. Direction: below neutral because commodity prices, slack and dollar all push inflation down.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 17 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Evans "substantial resource gaps remain open throughout the projection period"; Bernanke "could easily be among the largest postwar recessions". Center: deep and widening slack. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Fisher "shedding head count dramatically"; Rosengren "unemployment rate peaking above 7½ percent". Center: labor market loosening fast from a moderate level. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 15 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Evans "new capital spending is dead in the water"; Bernanke "consumer confidence ... from 61 to 38". Center: spending and investment collapsing. Direction: well below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-12 (Low).** Evidence: Warsh "I expect productivity to fall perhaps even more than in the Greenbook"; Pianalto "slower productivity growth". Center: brief mentions of weaker productivity and potential. Direction: below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 86 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn "tremendous tightening of financial conditions"; Warsh "credit intermediation process ... is fundamentally broken". Center: crisis-level tightness, slightly eased from mid-September. Direction: above neutral (tight).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 52 +-12 (Low).** Evidence: Geithner "the world is a little short of the risk-free asset now"; Bernanke "TIPS market is distorted by illiquidity". Center: incidental remarks, no described dysfunction. Direction: roughly neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 86 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Yellen "low-grade corporate bonds are up a staggering 700 basis points"; Warsh "spreads across asset classes are likely to stay far wider than historical norms". Center: very wide spreads and constrained availability, only grudging improvement. Direction: above neutral on stated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 63 +-10 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn "equity prices are down about 20 or 22 percent over the intermeeting period"; Bernanke "declined sharply amid conditions of low liquidity and extraordinary volatility". Center: sharp, disorderly decline with forced-selling concern, little valuation commentary. Direction: above neutral (depressed/stressed).

**BANKING_STRESS: 76 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bernanke "Banks have probably not reserved sufficiently for the credit losses to come"; Kohn "Banks tell us that they're tightening across every dimension". Center: system stressed, supported by guarantees and capital but still fragile. Direction: above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 70 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen "the dollar has appreciated nearly 10 percent against the currencies of our trading partners"; Bernanke "the dollar to appreciate very sharply". Center: sharp appreciation hitting net exports. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 22.** Commodity and import prices plunging, retail discounting. **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 30.** Evidence: Bernanke "Inflation expectations have come down, both in the surveys and in the TIPS market"; Geithner "abrupt change in inflation expectations into deflationary territory".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 13 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn "it is almost impossible at this point to go too far"; Bernanke "we're going to do what it takes". Center: large majority for a 50bp cut and more to come, QE-style tools in view; the hold camp is a minority. Direction: strongly dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Plosser "I will not dissent against a 50 basis point cut" after "I would prefer not to move today"; Hoenig "alternative A is not going to solve much"; unanimous roll call. Center: no formal dissent but about three explicit preferences to hold, torn and undecided members, one sharp exchange. Direction: moderate differences about tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 90 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke "we were dealing with what might have been a true systemic crisis"; swap lines, TARP, liquidity facilities and funding markets dominate throughout. Center: the meeting is about the financial crisis.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 10 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn "I think it will call for further easing at the next FOMC meeting"; Geithner "keep them low enough long enough". Center: clear easing bias and preparation for a lower bound. Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 84 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Evans "We clearly are sailing in uncharted waters"; Kohn "have only the vaguest notion of how it will play out". Center: most participants stress deep uncertainty. Direction: very high.
