# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 2002-01-30 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Transcript of participants' turns only (staff turns removed). It contains: (a) opening organizational business and a seminar-style Q&A on the zero-bound / quantitative-policy papers; (b) members' questions during the staff chart show (staff answers removed); (c) the economic go-around (Moskow through Bies); (d) a short Q&A on policy alternatives, then the policy go-around where the Chairman recommends no change in the funds rate and a risk statement weighted toward weakness, and each member states support or reservations; (e) a long procedural discussion about disclosing FOMC vote tallies and dissenters (procedure, scored only for disagreement tone); (f) a vote read-out with ten "Yes" and no dissent. One passage (around line 1684) has a company name blanked out in the text; it does not affect scoring.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Early 2002, after a shallow recession that participants date as ending around Q1 2002, with an Enron / accounting-confidence overhang. Participants talk about "bottoming out", "turning the corner", and a recovery led by an inventory swing.

**Speakers and views.**
- Regional presidents Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Poole, Broaddus, Santomero, McTeer, Stern, Hoenig, Guynn, Jordan, Vice Chair McDonough, Governors Gramlich, Ferguson, Olson, Bies, and the Chairman. Nearly all report improving data: claims falling, manufacturing surveys turning up (Philadelphia BOS +15), capital goods orders firming, strong Q4 consumer and housing spending, a very large inventory liquidation that must slow. Most also say the recovery will be moderate, with consumer sustainability, weak capital spending, equity-market and earnings risk, Enron-related confidence loss, state/local fiscal stress, and a weak labor market as downside risks.
- Policy: Chairman recommends no change and keeping the risk statement weighted toward weakness. Hoenig, McDonough, Poole, Stern, Jordan, Guynn, Gramlich, Ferguson, Moskow, Parry, Santomero, Minehan, Broaddus, Bies, McTeer, Olson all support the rate decision.
- Nuances: Poole would have preferred a balanced-risk statement; Parry thinks risks "appear balanced"; McTeer supports the bias "somewhat reluctantly"; Jordan is uneasy about the wording. Gramlich says "far less need for an expansionary monetary policy" and wants to stand pat; Santomero wants a pause and to start preparing markets for a reversal; Guynn says further easing "would appear to hamper our ability to return the funds rate"; Broaddus says we may "have eased as much as we're going to need to ease". At the other end, Minehan is "attracted, though reluctantly" to the "inflation cushion" scenario (more easing sooner), and Ferguson wants a possible cut left "on the table".

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below target and contained: "inflation has remained negligible" (McDonough), "how little evidence there is of price increases" and a fall in nominal GDP (Bies), "inflation and inflation expectations still well contained" (Broaddus), a Greenbook-like PCE near 1.3 percent (Minehan, Parry), plus extended discussion of the dangers of too-low inflation. Santomero says inflation or deflation "is not a problem at the moment". Below the midpoint, in the "contained / modest / running below 2 percent" band, around 29.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Ferguson cites a persistent output gap of about 1.8 percent of GDP shrinking to 1 percent over eight quarters (slack, in his usage); Minehan: "two years of growth below potential", unemployment well above the non-inflationary level; Parry: "slack in labor and product markets"; Bies: growth "significantly below ... potential". Recession ending, so not deep. Score 36 (bright-line satisfied, below 50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below full employment. Unemployment "at or near 6 percent" in the forecast, "above anyone's estimate of the NAIRU" (Ferguson); layoffs continue, hiring delayed, temp demand weak, "labor markets continue to be fairly easy ... no real signs of wage pressures" (Hoenig). Offsets: claims falling, regional pockets (Stern district ~4 percent, Minehan region about 2 points below the nation, a bank with 1,500 openings). Score 32.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net slightly below trend. Consumer spending and housing "remarkably strong" in Q4 (Guynn, Bies, Broaddus, Stern), but participants stress this was borrowed from autos incentives and warm weather, and business capital spending is weak/delayed ("hunkering down", "capital spending tightly controlled"), with signs of a bottom in orders. Nets to 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by several members as strong: "productivity growth has been very well maintained" (Moskow), Greenspan on the 3.1 percent Q4 rate ("cyclically adjusted ... a remarkable number"), Ferguson on strong tech returns keeping "labor productivity growing relatively strongly", Santomero on the secular upward trend. Above trend, 63.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed but leaning supportive. Bankers "flush with liquidity", "no evidence of a credit crunch" (Santomero, Guynn), low rates and mortgage refinancing freeing cash (Olson). Offsets: "high risk premiums in credit and capital markets" (Minehan), Kmart's financing drying up on Enron spillover, selective tightening for low-quality borrowers, equity-market and accounting-confidence worries. Below 50, around 42 (bright line: supportive means below 50).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only the Chairman's remark that long-term rates would rise if the risk statement changed, and Jordan's term-spread question. No dysfunction is described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only direct level statement is Minehan's "the high risk premiums in credit and capital markets". Santomero/Guynn say credit is available. A level described as high pushes above 50 under the v4.1 rule; thin evidence, so 56 with Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members discuss equities as pricing in an earnings turnaround that may not come: Bies on PE ratios requiring double-digit earnings growth amid no pricing power; Greenspan: "the implied stock market valuation in the Greenbook is just not right"; McDonough: the stock market is forecasting a much stronger recovery than anyone anticipates, a correction of "some significance" possible; Jordan/Gramlich/Ferguson on Enron-driven risk premium. Valuations seen as somewhat stretched relative to profits (modestly above fair value), not a bubble. Score 35.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy: "our [banking system] certainly at the moment is extremely strong" (McDonough), Minehan lists "the health of the banking system" as reassuring, Olson finds delinquencies flat except subprime, bankers flush with liquidity; a few Orlando hotel loans in trouble (Guynn). Below 50, 30 (bright line satisfied).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only a passing reference by McTeer to "the strong U.S. dollar" and a Gramlich point that rate cuts are unlikely to lower the currency enough to lift net exports. No discussion of dollar moves. Scored as a barely-above-neutral reading with Low confidence (54), because the one descriptor used is "strong".

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods, services, housing: not discussed (N/A). Expectations: Broaddus "inflation expectations still well contained"; Santomero less optimistic on the longer run; Poole/Parry/Minehan debate the target. Anchored, with a tilt to the low side given the low-inflation debate: 45.

**Tier 3 check in words.**
- Policy lean: Committee holds the rate and retains a weakness bias; most want a pause, a few (Poole, Parry, McTeer, Jordan) would like to drop the bias, Minehan/Ferguson keep easing on the table, Gramlich/Santomero/Guynn/Broaddus lean to the view that easing is done. Net: hold with a mild easing bias, 44.
- Disagreement: unanimous vote (10 Yes in read-out, zero dissents). Differences are over the wording of the risk statement and whether more easing is appropriate. Several mildly reluctant supporters. The long transparency discussion shows differing views on procedure but not on policy. Low-moderate, 28.
- Financial-stability attention: Several members (Jordan, Ferguson, Bies, Gramlich, McDonough, Moskow, Minehan) raise Enron/accounting confidence and equity risk, plus debt burdens, state/local finances, bank health; this is context for the outlook, not the main topic. 35.
- Forward guidance: The statement keeps risks weighted toward weakness, so the formal signal still leans easy, but members openly discuss that the next move is likely tightening and that the Committee should prepare the market (Santomero, Poole, Broaddus). Net mild easing lean with data dependence: 40.
- Uncertainty: "the level of uncertainty remains high" (Moskow), "Uncertainty remains high" (Minehan), Guynn on difficulty in judging recovery strength, Enron as an "emerging risk", Greenspan on a possible "different type of recovery". Elevated but not extreme and partly rhetorical: 52.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 (<50, below potential): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 (<50, supportive/accommodative): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 56 (>50, stated high risk premiums): ok. BANKING_STRESS 30 (<50, strong/healthy): ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 54 (>50, "strong dollar"): ok. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Polarity checks.** Inflation: higher means more pressure; evidence is "negligible / little evidence", so below 50: consistent. Labor: higher means tighter; evidence is slack, so below 50: consistent. Productivity: higher means more growth; evidence is strong gains, so above 50: consistent. Banking stress: higher means more stress; evidence is healthy banks, so below 50: consistent. Equity: higher means more stress/concern about the equity market; mild overvaluation concern is in the 31-45 band, so 35: consistent.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 versus LABOR 32: within 15 points. OUTPUT_GAP 36 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 42: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 versus CREDIT_SPREADS 56: a 14-point gap with FC below and spreads above 50; this reflects that the only spread-level remark (Minehan) is a single mention of high risk premiums while most descriptions of credit availability are supportive; spreads confidence is Low. Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 29 versus expectations 45: expectations anchored near target while realized pressure is low, consistent with the "well contained" language.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not discussed. INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: no member discusses these categories (health care cost increases noted by Guynn are not a category assessment).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 29 +-6 (Moderate confidence).** Evidence: "inflation has remained negligible" (Vice Chairman McDonough); "I continue to be quite surprised by how little evidence there is of price increases" (Bies). Center of gravity: inflation is low and contained, with debate about how low is too low. Direction: below neutral because members describe negligible price increases and a Committee debate about deflation margins.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: "After about two years of growth below potential, inflation is quite low, and the unemployment rate is well above" the non-inflationary level (Minehan); output gap "from about 1.8 percent of GDP down to about 1 percent" (Ferguson). Center: shallow recession, persistent slack through the forecast. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 32 +-5 (High).** Evidence: "unemployment that appears likely to be at or near 6 percent ... above anyone's estimate of the NAIRU" (Ferguson); "It's still rather easy to find new employees, and there are no real signs of wage pressures" (Hoenig). Center: slack labor market, deterioration easing. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: "I'm particularly struck by the remarkably strong consumer spending and housing numbers" (Guynn); "we have not heard any reports that suggest firms are increasing investment" (Moskow). Center: strong consumers (partly borrowed from incentives) offset by weak business capital spending. Direction: slightly below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 63 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: "productivity growth has been very well maintained" (Moskow); "we had a 3.1 percent annual productivity growth rate in the fourth quarter" (Greenspan). Center: productivity seen as strong. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: "our banking and business contacts report no evidence of a credit crunch" (Santomero); "the high risk premiums in credit and capital markets" (Minehan). Center: generally supportive credit availability with some risk-premium and confidence concerns. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: "the high risk premiums in credit and capital markets" (Minehan); "some banks have tightened terms selectively for the lowest credit quality borrowers" (Santomero). Center: one explicit statement of a high level, offset by good credit availability. Direction: slightly above neutral on the stated level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: "the implied stock market valuation in the Greenbook is just not right" (Greenspan); "To get the PE ratios that the market is pricing in ... would imply huge increases in unit sales" (Bies). Center: equities priced for an earnings recovery members doubt, a correction risk, but no bubble talk. Direction: modestly above fair value.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: the US banking system is "certainly at the moment ... extremely strong" (McDonough); delinquencies "roughly the same as in 2000 and 1999" with subprime weak (Olson). Center: healthy banks, isolated weak spots. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: "appreciated ... in terms of the strong U.S. dollar" (McTeer, about the peso). Center: a single passing reference; no discussion of dollar moves. Direction: marginally above neutral on the "strong" descriptor.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45.** Evidence: "inflation and inflation expectations still well contained" (Broaddus). Center: anchored, low side of target debate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 44 +-5 (High).** Evidence: "I recommend ... no change in the funds rate ... I would prefer to stay with the balance of risks toward weakness" (Greenspan); "a pause would be a good idea" (Santomero); Minehan "attracted, though reluctantly" to more stimulus. Center: unanimous hold with a mild easing bias retained, a few members wanting a balanced statement, a few open to more easing. Direction: slightly dovish of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-7 (High).** Evidence: ten "Yes" votes, no dissent; Poole "would have preferred a move to a balanced risks statement"; McTeer supports the bias "somewhat reluctantly"; Gramlich versus Ferguson on need for easing. Center: unanimous with modest differences on wording and on whether more easing is warranted. Direction: low-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: "an emerging risk ... is what I view as a fallout from the Enron debacle" (Jordan); "confidence in the capital markets due to the Enron scandal" (Bies). Center: several members raise accounting-confidence, equity and debt-burden risks as context. Direction: limited to moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: weakness bias retained; "We need to begin preparing the public and the market for the inevitable change in our policy course" (Santomero); "we need to continue to be in a position to ease policy further if necessary" (Broaddus). Center: formal bias still easy, members expect eventual tightening. Direction: easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: "the level of uncertainty remains high" (Moskow); "Uncertainty remains high, however" (Minehan). Center: several members describe elevated uncertainty about recovery strength and Enron effects, mostly in standard language. Direction: elevated.
