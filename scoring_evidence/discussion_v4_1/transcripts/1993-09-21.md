# v4.1 scoring: member discussion (transcript), meeting 1993-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: members' questions during the staff Q&A, the economic round, the Chairman's closing assessment, the one-by-one "B symmetric" policy go-around, and the vote read-out. Early-era (1993) format with no formal policy go-around beyond the directive. Some passages show the speaker's words only because staff replies were removed; I scored participants' views only. No passage was garbled enough to prevent scoring.

**Era context (not used to override text).** Early expansion after the 1990-91 recession. The funds rate had been at 3 percent for about a year. Members describe moderate growth, sluggish job growth, moderate inflation, and a sharp fall in long-term rates.

**Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: suspects the GDP data understate output and overstate the productivity decline. Says "we may be not all that far from potential," growth "over 2 percent, maybe 2-1/2." He favors no change and a symmetric directive.
- Forrestal, Keehn, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Boehne, Stern, Melzer, Syron: moderate growth near the Greenbook. Intense price competition (Keehn). Weak hiring (Keehn, Forrestal, Boehne). Tight labor in parts of the Ninth District (Stern). Broaddus expects slightly stronger growth and welcomes lower long rates.
- Jordan: Fed framework and fiscal-policy critique, with a price-stability orientation. Melzer: worries that policy may be stimulative and that inflation may eventually need to go lower.
- McDonough: concern about the level of equity prices, mutual fund redemption risk, and foreign developments.
- Angell, Kelley, Phillips, Mullins: Angell notes CPI still at 3.1 percent and higher productivity trends. Kelley sees balanced risks but a lack of clarity about what to worry about. Phillips sees the banking system and financial markets as stronger, the stock market as more problematic than the bond market, and the PPI and commodity prices as disinflationary. Mullins sees moderate expansion, core CPI 3.3 percent over 12 months, slack that should re-establish disinflation, and an eased financial system with monetary policy "doing what it can do to support growth."

**Dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as moderate, modestly above a price-stability objective (core CPI about 3 percent per Angell and Mullins), but with softness in prices (PPI, intense competition, commodity prices retreating) and slack expected to bring disinflation. Slightly above the midpoint: 48. The scale's 50 is a 2 percent target. The era's 3 percent core is somewhat above that, while members describe progress and low pressure, so the net is a bit below 55. I use 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as slightly below potential. Greenspan says "not all that far from potential." Melzer says "real growth around potential." Mullins refers to "plenty of slack." Bright-line: below potential, so below 50. Score 44.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment at 6.7 percent (Parry), employment growth sluggish, firms not hiring permanent staff (Keehn), job quality a concern (Phillips). A few pockets of tightness (Stern, Mullins "strong hours suggest pent-up labor demand"). Below full employment, score 35.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Moderate trend growth; consumer spending "increasingly solid" (Broaddus) but retail disappointing in some districts, third quarter Greenbook growth 1.2 percent, business investment healthy (Mullins, McDonough). About at trend, slightly below: 46.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Angell says productivity trends are "very, very high." Phillips notes "a marked improvement so far in the '90s." Greenspan disputes the measured first-half decline. Above trend, score 58, moderate confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Substantial easing in the capital market environment (Mullins), mortgage and long rates sharply lower, credit and banking system stronger (Phillips). Complaints about small-business credit from Forrestal. Loose: 35. Bright-line: easing so below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Long yields decline discussed, but no dysfunction described. The yield fall is not dysfunction. N/A is the right choice.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed in terms of spreads or levels. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: McDonough: growing concern about the level of the Dow and sustainability. Phillips: stock market "more problematic than the bond market... but at a higher level," no "visions of tulips." Modestly elevated valuation concern: 38.
- BANKING_STRESS: Bank earnings very strong (Hoenig), improved banking sector (Syron), banking system stronger (Phillips). Healthy, some residual pockets. Bright-line: below 50. Score 30.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Mullins: "the dollar has eased some." Jordan mentions the exchange rate in the Honda pricing context without a direction. Modest ease, low confidence: 40. Bright-line: weakening, so below 50.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services and housing not discussed as inflation categories (health care and building materials mentions are fragmentary). Expectations: Mullins cites the Michigan long-term series souring, and Jordan discusses regime credibility, but no clear anchored/unanchored statement. All N/A.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: All 12 voters, and all speakers in the go-around, support no change and a symmetric ("B") directive. Some members voice concerns on both sides (Melzer on stimulus and the need to lower inflation, Mullins "still pushing it," Jordan, versus Boehne expecting pressure for easier policy and Phillips on risks). Balanced: 50.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Unanimous vote, same view on policy. Substantive differences on the model and framework (Jordan, Mullins vs staff), and on whether risks are balanced, but not about the stance. 12.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: McDonough devotes a long passage to equity and mutual fund fragility, Phillips adds stock market and foreign financial market risks, Syron mentions nervous fund managers, bank lending conditions come up in several district reports, and long rates are discussed at length by several members. Moderate by 1993 standards: 25.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Symmetric directive, "remain alert" (Angell), no tilt signaled. 50.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Standard caveats and balanced risks, with Kelley emphasizing unknown unknowns and several members puzzled by data inconsistencies (Angell, Greenspan, Syron on consumption versus income). Somewhat elevated: 35.

**Polarity checks.** Higher = more of the construct in each case. Financial conditions at 35 (looser than neutral) matches "eased" language. Banking stress 30 matches "stronger/healthy." Output gap 44 matches "slack/not far from potential."

**Cross-dimension coherence.** Output gap 44 vs labor 35: gap of 9, under 15. Output gap 44 vs consumption 46: consistent. Financial conditions 35 with credit spreads N/A and Treasury stress N/A: no conflict. Parent inflation 50 with sub-components all N/A: no check available.

**N/A justification.** Treasury stress, credit spreads, and the four inflation sub-components were not addressed with scoreable content by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Angell: "the CPI running at 3.1 percent"; Keehn: "a very, very intense pricing environment." Mullins: core CPI "up at a rate of 3.3 percent" but the recent six months at 2.5 percent.
Summary: Moderate inflation, somewhat above a stable-price objective but with disinflationary signals.
Direction: at the midpoint because upside (core CPI near 3 percent) is offset by subdued price signals.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 ±7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Greenspan: "we may be not all that far from potential"; Mullins: "while there's plenty of slack in the economy."
Summary: Modest slack, closing slowly.
Direction: below neutral because members refer to slack and growth around potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Parry: unemployment "6.7 percent"; Keehn: "no one I have talked to plans to add permanent employees."
Summary: Elevated unemployment and weak hiring, with only scattered tightness (Stern).
Direction: below neutral because unemployment is above 6 percent and hiring is weak.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 ±7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Broaddus: "consumer spending is looking increasingly solid"; Forrestal: retail "quite disappointing."
Summary: Moderate-pace spending, mixed across districts.
Direction: near neutral, slightly below, given third quarter Greenbook growth and cautious sentiment.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Angell: "productivity trends very, very high"; Phillips: "a marked improvement so far in the '90s."
Summary: Productivity seen as strong, though recent measured data are disputed.
Direction: above neutral on trend language.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 ±7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Mullins: "substantial easing in the capital market environment"; "I think the financial system has eased."
Summary: Easy conditions from low rates and falling long yields, with some small-business credit complaints.
Direction: below neutral (looser) because of the eased conditions language.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: McDonough: "growing concern... about the level of the DOW"; Phillips: stock market "at a higher level."
Summary: Valuations seen as high enough to worry some members, but not a bubble.
Direction: below the neutral point because of elevated-valuation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±7 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Hoenig: "Banks' earnings are doing extremely well"; Phillips: "The banking system is stronger."
Summary: Healthy, improving banks.
Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±10 (Low).**
Evidence: Mullins: "the dollar has eased some."
Summary: A single passing mention of modest dollar softening.
Direction: below neutral because of the weakening.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A.**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 50 ±4 (High).**
Evidence: Greenspan: "I don't see any inclination... to move off where we are"; every member then said "B symmetric."
Summary: Unanimous support for no change and a symmetric directive.
Direction: at neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 (High).**
Evidence: 12 "Yes" votes; all members stated agreement with "B symmetric."
Summary: Consensus on policy; differences are over models and the framework only.
Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: McDonough: "growing concern in the financial markets about the level of the DOW"; Phillips on foreign financial market risks.
Summary: One extended passage plus scattered mentions, in a meeting otherwise focused on the real economy.
Direction: above the 1993 baseline, still limited.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 ±5 (High).**
Evidence: Angell: "we need to remain alert"; symmetric directive.
Summary: No tilt signaled.
Direction: neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Kelley: "I don't know what I should be most concerned about"; Greenspan: "Something is wrong with the data system."
Summary: Balanced risks with some data and outlook puzzlement.
Direction: somewhat elevated.
