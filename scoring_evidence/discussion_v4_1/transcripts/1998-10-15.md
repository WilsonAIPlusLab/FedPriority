# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1998-10-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript of a short conference-call meeting (about 9,000 words). Staff turns are removed. It opens with members' questions, then a financial-markets report from the Vice Chair (a Desk/NY Fed-side account given by a participant), a short go-around of District reports, and a policy discussion. The Chair proposes an intermeeting federal funds cut of 25 bp, members respond, and the discount rate is discussed. There is no formal vote (the Chair says it is "not for a vote"). The document is not a special-topic meeting. Scoring is not N/A across the board.

**Era context.** 1998, in the aftermath of the Russian default and the LTCM rescue, about two and a half weeks after an earlier easing. The text shows a financial-market seizure alongside a still-solid domestic economy. I score what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- McDonough gives a long, detailed account of dramatically wider risk spreads, illiquidity and a run to quality.
- The Reserve Bank presidents and governors (Boehne, Stern, McTeer, Hoenig, Minehan, Guynn, Parry, Moskow) report District conditions. They find fundamentals largely unchanged but a sharp rise in anxiety and uncertainty, with some pullback in investment.
- Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson and Gramlich debate policy. Rivlin and Gramlich favor acting now. Kelley is cautious about timing.
- Poole, Minehan and Guynn voice reservations about acting between meetings or about the discount rate.
- Jordan says policy is "too expansionary."
- Greenspan proposes a 25 bp cut and says he sees "a funds rate in the area of 4-1/2 to 5 percent" as appropriate.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- **INFLATION_PRESSURE.** Members say almost nothing about inflation. The only items are the PPI, which Greenspan calls a "significant aberration," Poole's worry about "bad PPI news," Stern's "squeezes on earnings," and Greenspan's remark that "inflation expectations fell." Participants see pressure as modest and not a concern, slightly below the midpoint. Confidence is Low.
- **OUTPUT_GAP.** Participants see "general strength in the domestic economy" (Rivlin), "persistence of tight labor markets," and fundamentals "pretty much in place." They also see downside risk ahead, with a slowing expected and no deterioration yet. The economy is slightly above potential now. Participants never state this explicitly, so I keep it modest at 55. The bright-line rule is satisfied, since nothing says "below potential."
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Moskow says "labor markets are still very tight." Stern cites "tight labor markets." There is no unemployment figure in the text. Labor is tight but not described as extreme, so the score is moderately above 50.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.** The evidence is mixed. Consumers are strong (Rivlin), residential real estate is "very strong" (Minehan), and consumer lending continues (Hoenig). Against that, investment is pulling back in energy, export-sensitive industries and some manufacturing, decisions are being delayed, and Moskow's contacts plan lower 1999 capex. Net is about at trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY.** Not discussed (the Lucent capital-for-labor anecdote is not a productivity assessment), so N/A.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.** Participants see conditions as very tight. Junk issuance is "completely dried up," investment-grade issuance is down "very markedly," and the Chair says liquidity is "clearly under stress." Ferguson says markets have "seized up in important ways." Conditions are well above 50. The bright-line rule is satisfied (tight, restrictive and strained map to above 50). This is conditions, not the policy rate.
- **TREASURY_STRESS.** The Treasury market is specifically discussed. McDonough reports the on-the-run versus off-the-run 30-year spread at about 27 bp against a usual 4-5, long-bond volatility at 23 versus a usual 5, and "a rather serious drying up of liquidity." Meyer adds that "market participants not standing behind the bid-ask spreads." This goes beyond yield movement to liquidity impairment, so the score is above 60. It is not at the auction-failure level.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS.** The levels are stated explicitly as very wide. The BBB spread is 6.3 points versus a normal 3.2. The 10-year swap spread is 95 versus 50. Ford 10-year is 124 versus 75. The level is clearly high, so the score is well above 50. Hoenig's point that the spread widening partly reflects lower Treasury yields does not change the level reading. Minehan notes a few cash-rich buyers see the spreads as "bargains," which does not offset the broad picture.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION.** There is no valuation commentary, only price moves. The S&P is about 102 percent of its year-ago level while bank and investment-bank stock indexes are down 20-25 percent. Greenspan mentions a prior "stock market wealth effect," and Rivlin mentions a possible "sharp drop in the stock market." Per the rule (price moves without valuation commentary), the score stays near the midpoint, slightly above 50, with Low confidence.
- **BANKING_STRESS.** Banking and securities firms are discussed. Evidence of stress: financial-firm stocks are down, rating agencies have "an extremely high anxiety level," more downgrades are possible, "other banks ... will have to report some losses," and fed wire was kept open late. Evidence against: payments systems are "functioning okay," regional banks show a "responsible slowdown" with "nothing close to any rationing of credit" (Guynn), and bank lending is absorbing business from capital markets. Stress is emerging and concentrated in securities firms, so the score is above 50. No "sound" or "healthy" language triggers the bright line.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** The only currency mention is the Brazilian real (Gramlich) and a "catastrophe on the international scene" (Rivlin). The dollar is not discussed, so N/A.
- **Inflation sub-components.** Goods, services and housing are not discussed, so N/A. Expectations get one passing reference (Greenspan: the real funds rate rose "as inflation expectations fell"), so I give a value only, slightly below target.

**Tier 3 check.**
- **Policy lean.** Dovish. The Chair proposes an intermeeting 25 bp cut and nearly everyone supports it. McTeer prefers 50. Gramlich says 50 "could be defended." Rivlin would do 50 on the discount rate. Kelley says policy "is on an easing path and ... we have further to go." Jordan alone says policy is too expansionary. Poole, Guynn and Minehan have timing reservations but would not object. The lean is moderate dovish, an actual cut with an easing path ahead, so roughly 28.
- **Dissent.** There was no formal vote and there are no formal dissents. Guynn says he "would not dissent" but would prefer to hold off. Reservations about timing and message came from Kelley (initially), Hoenig ("uneasy"), Poole, Guynn and Minehan, and Jordan restated his view that policy is too expansionary. McTeer wanted a larger cut. There was a visible spread on the discount rate (0, 25 or 50). All accepted the funds rate move. That is a moderate-differences meeting with several reservations and no formal dissents, so about 32.
- **Financial stability emphasis.** Dominant. McDonough's long report, the Chair's remarks on liquidity, Meyer, Ferguson, Gramlich, McTeer ("It is all about troubled financial markets") and Kelley all center on financial conditions. It rivals or exceeds the economic assessment, so about 88.
- **Forward guidance.** Easing bias but no explicit commitment. Evidence: Boehne's "we are open to further declines," Kelley's "further to go," Meyer's "lower federal funds rate in the first half of next year," Greenspan's note that the move can be reversed. About 22.
- **Uncertainty.** Very high. Language includes "enormous uncertainty and uneasiness" (Guynn), "a higher degree of uncertainty" (Meyer), and Greenspan's "never seen anything like the current situation." Gramlich claims uncertainty has been resolved toward the downside, which is a minor counterpoint. About 77.

**Polarity checks.**
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress, my evidence says spreads are very wide, so the score is high. This matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter, the evidence says tight, so the score is high. This matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH: higher means more hawkish, the evidence says easing, so the score is low. This matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE: higher means more tightening, the evidence says easing, so the score is low. This matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP is 55, above 50, with no below-potential language. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above 50 with tight language. CREDIT_SPREADS is above 50 on stated high levels. BANKING_STRESS is above 50 with stress language and no "sound/healthy" statement. DOLLAR_STRENGTH is N/A. TREASURY_STRESS is above 60 because of described liquidity impairment, not yield moves.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 55 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 63 differ by 8, within 15.
- OUTPUT_GAP 55 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50 differ by 5, within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 80 and CREDIT_SPREADS 85 co-move.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 80 and TREASURY_STRESS 68 co-move, as expected in a stress episode.
- The inflation parent has only one weak sub-component reference and no conflict.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the latest PPI number is clearly a significant aberration." Greenspan: "We purposely allowed the real federal funds rate to rise as inflation expectations fell."
- Summary: Inflation is mentioned only in passing and not seen as a concern.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral, because the one data point is dismissed as an aberration and expectations are described as having fallen.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rivlin: "general strength in the domestic economy, especially in the consumer sector." Stern: "persistence of tight labor markets" and "the fundamentals ... seem to be pretty much in place."
- Summary: A solid economy running modestly above potential, with a slowdown expected.
- Direction: Above neutral, because members describe strength and tight resource use and no one describes slack. The magnitude is held small because no explicit gap language is used.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Moskow: "the labor markets are still very tight." Stern: "tight labor markets and squeezes on earnings."
- Summary: Labor markets are tight, with no unemployment figure given.
- Direction: Above neutral, because the explicit descriptors are "very tight" and "tight." Some layoff notices (Minehan) are isolated.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rivlin: "general strength in the domestic economy, especially in the consumer sector." Guynn: "a fairly decided pullback in export-sensitive industries and companies." Moskow: contacts "planning lower levels of capital spending, hiring, and expenses for 1999."
- Summary: Strong consumers and housing offset investment delays and pullbacks.
- Direction: At neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 80 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the degree of liquidity in the overall financial system is clearly under stress." Ferguson: markets "seem actually to have gone beyond a state of concern and uncertainty and to have seized up in important ways."
- Summary: Capital-market access is sharply restricted and liquidity is impaired.
- Direction: Well above neutral (tight), because issuance has collapsed and risk aversion is extreme.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 68 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: McDonough: "Even in the most liquid market of the world, the U.S. Treasury market, there is an incredible broadening of spreads between on-the-run securities and the most recent off-the-run securities ... a rather serious drying up of liquidity." Meyer: "market participants not standing behind the bid-ask spreads as wide as they are."
- Summary: The Treasury market shows actual liquidity impairment but not auction failure.
- Direction: Above neutral, because liquidity and bid-ask problems are described rather than yield moves alone.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 85 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: McDonough: BBB spread to Treasuries "usually about 3.2 percentage points and it is now at about 6.3." McDonough: "no financial officer who is not smoking pot would actually try to issue in this kind of market."
- Summary: Spreads are roughly double normal, with issuance shut.
- Direction: Above neutral on explicit level statements, not direction words.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 54 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: McDonough: the S&P 500 is "about 102 percent of its level" a year ago while the money center bank index is "just under 80 percent" and investment banks "about 75 percent." Rivlin: possible "sharp drop in the stock market as earnings come in more gloomy."
- Summary: Price moves are noted with no valuation view, so near neutral with a slight tilt for financial-sector stress.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because of the stress in financial-sector stocks.

**BANKING_STRESS: 57 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: McDonough: rating agencies have "an extremely high anxiety level about the financial services firms," and "some other banks ... will have to report some losses." Guynn: regional banks show "a responsible slowdown in lending ... nothing close to any rationing of credit to legitimate borrowers."
- Summary: Stress is concentrated in securities firms and hedge-fund counterparties, with the commercial banking system functioning.
- Direction: Above neutral (emerging stress), because ratings pressure, expected losses and tighter standards are described, tempered by functioning payments systems.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A**

**INFLATION_GOODS: N/A. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "as inflation expectations fell."
- Summary: A single past-tense reference to falling expectations.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral, with value only.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I would be inclined to move the funds rate down by 25 basis points." Kelley: "our policy is on an easing path and that we have further to go." McTeer: "I would prefer 50 basis points today rather than 25."
- Summary: An intermeeting easing with near-unanimous support and some wanting more. Jordan's view that policy is too expansionary is the lone opposite view.
- Direction: Below neutral (dovish), because the Committee is cutting and expects to ease further.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Guynn: "If I had a vote ... I certainly would not dissent. But ... I would prefer to hold off taking action until the next FOMC meeting." Hoenig: "I don't think there has been enough change since the September meeting to warrant the rate cut ... I would not object."
- Summary: No vote and no formal dissents. Several expressed reservations on timing and the discount rate, and a preference for 50 bp from McTeer.
- Direction: Moderate differences about tactics, and no disagreement about the economic assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 88 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: McTeer: "It is all about troubled financial markets right now." Kelley: "if those markets should implode ... they could very easily and promptly take the real economy with them."
- Summary: Financial-market stress is the dominant topic, with the policy case built on it.
- Direction: Very high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Boehne: "the message we are sending is that we are open to further declines." Meyer: "if the story is that we feel we need to have a lower federal funds rate in the first half of next year, it probably would be good to get there a little faster."
- Summary: Mild-to-strong easing bias with no commitment, plus Greenspan's remark that a reversal is possible.
- Direction: Below neutral (easing), because members expect further cuts and see risks to the downside.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 77 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Guynn: "enormous uncertainty and uneasiness." Greenspan: "I have never seen anything like the current situation."
- Summary: Nearly every speaker stresses unusual uncertainty about the effect of financial turmoil on the real economy. Gramlich is the one voice saying uncertainty has resolved toward the downside.
- Direction: High, because the uncertainty is described as genuine and unprecedented, not rhetorical.
