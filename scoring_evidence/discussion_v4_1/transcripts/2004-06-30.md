# FOMC member-discussion scoring v4.1: transcript discussion, meeting dated 2004-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). The file holds a two-day record. It opens with the evening-before special seminar on prospective external adjustment (members' questions to staff, with staff answers removed, so many Chairman lines are fragments). It then has the morning session: members' questions on the staff chart show, the economic go-around, the Chairman's policy proposal, the statement-wording exchange, and the roll call (12 Yes, no No votes).

**Era context.** Mid-2004: expansion has become self-sustaining after a period of very low rates. The Committee is about to begin tightening. The text itself says the funds rate is "highly accommodative" and that a 25bp increase is to be taken today. I score what participants say, not what I recall.

**Speakers and views.**
- Moskow: extremely broad-based expansion, emerging price pressure, mostly temporary. Wants flexibility to move faster.
- Minehan: solid growth, risks to inflation on the upside, time to start raising rates, drop forward commitments.
- Santomero: self-sustaining expansion, rising trend in inflation, "take action today", language "overstayed its welcome".
- Pianalto: solid year, strong productivity, inflation risks shifted toward creeping up, move today and keep going.
- Hoenig: "inflationary updrift", policy very accommodative, move deliberately and systematically.
- Yellen: growth above potential, significant slack remains, markup large, inflation risks roughly balanced, funds-futures path is "measured".
- McTeer: reduced slack, pricing power, "behind the curve", wants to drop "measured".
- Stern: favorable growth, inflation low but not as low as anticipated.
- Poole: strong transportation and industrial demand, inflation risks quite skewed to the upside.
- Guynn: imbalances and widespread pass-through, steeper path likely needed, concerned about twin deficits.
- Lacker: balanced expansion, transitory inflation likely, gap still sizable, markets nervous about upside inflation.
- Geithner: strong expansion, more concern on inflation, risks somewhat to the upside, wants a signal that larger steps are possible.
- Kohn: gradual closing of the gap, risks to inflation "not quite balanced", favors keeping "measured pace" plus a statement of commitment.
- Gramlich: no good evidence the Fed is behind the curve, NAIRU about 5 percent, risks roughly balanced, wants a "Plan B".
- Ferguson: downside real-side surprises, inflation at the upper end of the price-stability zone, gradualism.
- Bies: broad-based sustainable growth, strong corporate finances, profit share very high, wants to act promptly.
- Bernanke: inflation rise mostly goods and energy, likely to moderate, temporize with gradual increases.
- Olson: banks' asset quality "best ever", signals have unfolded as planned.
- Greenspan: profit-margin arithmetic, core PCE slightly above 2 percent, long-term inflation expectations "remarkably contained", proposes +25bp with "measured" retained plus a qualifying sentence.

**Directional statements, core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Core inflation has risen ("substantial uptick", "somewhat above our range of comfort", "upper end" of the price-stability zone). The Chairman's own figure is core PCE slightly above 2 percent. Many call it largely temporary, others see broader pricing power. The gotcha says transitory language does not lower the score, so the score is above 50 but not high.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below the midpoint. Yellen says growth will "exceed potential, bringing the economy gradually back toward full employment". Lacker says "the output gap is still sizable". Gramlich says the 5.6 percent unemployment rate "does not represent full capacity". Kohn says gap closes "in the latter part of 2005". McTeer and Hoenig say the gap is small or closing fast, but that is a minority view. Bright line: "below potential / slack remains" must be < 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: below the midpoint on level (unemployment 5.5-5.6 percent), though tightening rapidly ("emerging tightness", "labor is in very short supply" in pockets). Score the level: UE 5.5-7 percent maps to 31-45, upper end.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: above trend. GDP about 4.5 to 5 percent, "business investment is accelerating", consumer spending at a moderate pace. Offset by Ferguson's downside surprises and Kohn's "solid rather than ebullient".
- PRODUCTIVITY: above trend on structural productivity (Pianalto, Kohn, Yellen "robust rate"), with the Chairman noting a recent actual slowdown. Net modestly above.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: below the midpoint. Kohn: "Financial conditions remain supportive of above-trend growth, but they have tightened in recent months". Bies: firms access capital markets "on favorable terms". Bright line: "supportive" must be < 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. The Treasury market is mentioned only as foreign share of holdings, yield-curve shape and a flat ten-year forecast. No functioning problem is raised by a member.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: no spread levels are quoted. Credit access is described as favorable (Bies), asset quality as best ever (Olson), commercial loan demand strong (Lacker). Level reading: favorable and not wide, so below 50, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A. Minehan notes regional stock indexes outperformed, but no valuation view is offered.
- BANKING_STRESS: low. Olson: asset quality "the best ever" and "getting better". Bright line: "healthy" must be < 50. Mild caution on commercial real estate (Olson, Bies).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: dollar discussed mostly in the external-adjustment seminar as prospective depreciation risk (Yellen "The dollar could fall further", Guynn "additional pressure on the dollar"). Direction is a feared or possible decline, not appreciation. Score below 50 with low confidence. Bright line satisfied (depreciation or weakness < 50).

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: a 25bp hike to begin removing accommodation, with near-unanimous support. Many (McTeer, Geithner, Hoenig, Guynn, Poole, Santomero) want flexibility for larger steps. Gramlich, Ferguson, Bernanke and Yellen favor a gradual path. Guynn says a steeper path may be needed. Moderate hawkish.
- Dissent: no formal dissent, 12 Yes. Differences exist over the wording ("measured"), the speed, and the gap and NAIRU assessment. A few members (Poole, Minehan) are uncomfortable with the statement language but accept it. Broad agreement.
- Financial stability: moderate. The seminar on external imbalances (disorderly adjustment, foreign portfolio exposure), the financial stability section of the chart show, household and business debt, commercial real estate, carry trades and maturity mismatches. It is still not the dominant topic of the policy go-around.
- Forward guidance: retain "measured" but add a sentence saying the Committee will respond as needed. Further tightening is signaled, with the possibility of 50bp in August. Mild-to-moderate tightening signal.
- Uncertainty: elevated on inflation dynamics and the gap ("so little we know about inflation dynamics", "forecasts are hazardous", "particularly so today", "not sure we know" in the terrorist scenario). Not extreme.

**Polarity checks.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: higher means more inflation pressure; participants see core inflation above 2 percent, so 60 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: higher means more above potential; the evidence is slack remains, so below 50 matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means tighter; UE is 5.6 percent, so below 50 matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; the evidence is "supportive", so below 50 matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; the evidence is favorable access, so below 50 matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress; the evidence is "best ever" asset quality, so below 50 matches.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher means stronger dollar; the evidence is risk of further depreciation, so below 50 matches.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: 43, below 50, satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40, below 50, satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: 35, below 50, satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: 25, below 50, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40, below 50, satisfied.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (43) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (42): consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP (43) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (58): the difference is 15, at the threshold. Strong growth above potential-growth pace is closing a still-open gap, so spending is above trend while the level of activity is still below potential. The Greenbook closes the gap only by early 2006.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (35): both below 50, consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is N/A in a normal market, consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) vs sub-components: goods 62, services 48, expectations 53. A weighted average of about 54 is below the parent score. The parent reflects the aggregate uptick of core inflation above 2 percent and widespread pass-through reports (Guynn, McTeer, Minehan), which the staff-style decomposition understates. The gap is modest and within the uncertainty band.

**N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning comments by members.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: no valuation commentary by members.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: members discuss house-price valuation (Ferguson, Lacker, Bies) and real estate, but not shelter or rent inflation. The only mention of housing services is Bernanke's remark that services inflation, with or without housing, decelerated, which is not enough to score separately.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Geithner: "significant acceleration in underlying inflation to a level somewhat above our range of comfort." Greenspan: core PCE "slightly above a 2 percent annual rate." Ferguson: "we're probably at the upper end of that zone [of price stability]."
- Center-of-gravity: modestly above target, with an upward trajectory; many say the uptick is partly temporary but few deny it.
- Direction confirmation: inflation pressure is above neutral because core inflation has risen above 2 percent and pricing power is broadening in the District reports.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Lacker: "The output gap is still sizable." Yellen: "the pace of growth will exceed potential, bringing the economy gradually back toward full employment." Kohn: economy reaches "full utilization in the latter part of 2005."
- Center-of-gravity: most see remaining slack that is closing; McTeer and Hoenig argue it is smaller than the staff thinks.
- Direction confirmation: the output gap is below neutral because the economy is still below potential and "slack remains".

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Gramlich: "the present 5.6 percent rate of employment does not represent full capacity economywide." Lacker: "several of our contacts are reporting signs of emerging tightness in labor markets."
- Center-of-gravity: unemployment at 5.5-5.6 percent, participation and workweek low, with pockets of shortages and a rapid pace of hiring.
- Direction confirmation: labor tightness is below neutral because the level of unemployment is above the NAIRU estimate.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Santomero: "business investment is accelerating, and consumer spending continues to rise at a moderate pace." Hoenig: GDP growth "around 4¾ percent this year."
- Center-of-gravity: growth of 4.5 to 5 percent with accelerating business investment, tempered by Ferguson's downside surprises and Kohn's "hardly a barnburner".
- Direction confirmation: spending is above trend because growth is well above potential growth.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Pianalto: a CEO reports "productivity has been increasing at 10 percent annual rates recently." Greenspan: "a very significant slowing in productivity" in April and May data.
- Center-of-gravity: structural productivity growth is viewed as robust, with a recent cyclical slowdown noted by the Chairman.
- Direction confirmation: productivity is above neutral because members describe strong structural gains.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kohn: "Financial conditions remain supportive of above-trend growth, but they have tightened in recent months as markets have come to anticipate our actions." Bies: companies can "access capital markets and financial institutions for funds on favorable terms."
- Center-of-gravity: still supportive though less so as rate expectations rise.
- Direction confirmation: financial conditions are below neutral (looser) because members call them supportive.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Not specifically discussed by members as a functioning issue (mentions limited to foreign share and yield levels).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±8 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bies: firms can fund "on favorable terms." Lacker: "consistent reports of strong commercial loan demand."
- Center-of-gravity: credit availability is favorable; no spread levels are quoted.
- Direction confirmation: credit stress is below neutral because credit is described as readily available, with no wide spreads mentioned.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A
- No participant offers a valuation view.

### BANKING_STRESS: 25 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Olson: two of three bankers described asset quality as "the best ever" and "getting better." Bies: debt-equity ratios "down to levels last seen in the 1970s."
- Center-of-gravity: very healthy banks, with mild watch items in commercial real estate.
- Direction confirmation: banking stress is below neutral because banks are described as healthy with excellent asset quality.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Yellen: "The dollar could fall further." Guynn: "by falling behind the curve we risk ... additional pressure on the dollar."
- Center-of-gravity: members treat dollar depreciation as a risk and a factor in imported goods inflation, with no recent appreciation cited.
- Direction confirmation: the dollar is below neutral because depreciation is the feared direction.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 62 [Moderate]
- Evidence: Bernanke: more than 100 percent of the core PCE acceleration "can be accounted for by higher inflation or less deflation in goods prices, particularly durable goods."
- Direction confirmation: goods inflation is above neutral because goods prices have turned up and commodity pass-through is widely reported.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 48
- Evidence: Bernanke: "inflation in services excluding energy ... has actually decelerated in the past six months." Guynn reports personal-service firms raising prices.
- Direction confirmation: services inflation is near neutral because of the mixed evidence.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Housing prices were discussed only as a valuation matter, not shelter inflation.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 53
- Evidence: Greenspan: long-term expectations "remarkably contained." Lacker: short-run expectations "have moved up in recent months" while longer-horizon compensation "drifted higher."
- Direction confirmation: expectations are slightly above anchored because short-term measures rose while long-term measures held.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 66 ±6 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Greenspan: "we are going to move ... 25 basis points today. We have to leave open the possibility that we may wish to move 50 basis points in August." McTeer: "we are behind the curve"; Pianalto: "move the fed funds rate up today and continue to do so for a while."
- Center-of-gravity: consensus for beginning tightening, with a significant group wanting readiness for faster steps, offset by a gradualist group.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is hawkish because a majority favors a rate increase now and a path back to neutral.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 ±6 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Roll call: all twelve voters Yes. Poole accepts the statement while sitting "on a very pointed fence ... about language." Minehan: "there's going to be some scrutiny" of the wording.
- Center-of-gravity: unanimous action, disagreement limited to statement wording, pace, and the gap/NAIRU assessment.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is low because there are zero dissents and the differences concern tactics.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±10 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Bernanke commends the financial-stability report section and asks about "the tails of the distribution." Greenspan: "we have to be careful not to move too fast and risk destabilizing the financial system by enforcing maturity mismatches."
- Center-of-gravity: a seminar on disorderly external adjustment plus several members on household debt, commercial real estate and carry trades, but not the central focus of the go-around.
- Direction confirmation: attention is moderate because several participants address it meaningfully but the policy discussion is driven by inflation.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 ±7 [High confidence]
- Evidence: Greenspan: retain "likely to be measured" but add a sentence that "the Committee will respond to changes in economic prospects, as needed." Geithner: helpful if markets built in "a somewhat greater expectation that we could move in larger increments this year."
- Center-of-gravity: further increases signaled, at a measured pace, with explicit readiness to go faster.
- Direction confirmation: the forward signal is hawkish because more tightening is expected and the easing-leaning commitment language is being qualified.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Ferguson: "how unfortunately little we know about inflation dynamics ... humility ... is very important." Bernanke: forecasts "seem particularly [hazardous] today."
- Center-of-gravity: multiple members emphasize unusual uncertainty about inflation dynamics and the size of the gap, but outlook for growth is held with confidence.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is above normal because several participants stress the difficulty of reading inflation and slack.
