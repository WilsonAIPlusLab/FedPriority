# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript discussion, meeting date 2009-01-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, participants only (staff turns removed, so staff questions appear without answers). It covers: organizational votes, a long afternoon discussion of the Fed's balance sheet and credit facilities (TALF, AMLF, MBS and Treasury purchases, Treasury-Fed "accord"), the economic go-around, the policy and statement go-round (alternatives A and B), a straw vote, the roll call, and the long-term projections.

**2. Era context.** Federal funds rate target already at 0-1/4 percent. Financial crisis in its second year, rapid contraction, banks under heavy stress, deflation worries, "credit easing" and balance-sheet policy are the live tools. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Nearly everyone describes a very sharp, widespread contraction (Lockhart, Hoenig, Yellen, Fisher, Evans, Pianalto, Rosengren, Kohn, Dudley, Warsh, Duke, Bernanke).
- Optimistic outliers on timing only: Plosser, Stern. Even they say "one of the worst in the postwar period" (Plosser) and expect a weak first half.
- Banking stress is the dominant concern: Warsh ("effectively insolvent"), Dudley ("probably in worse shape today than last fall"), Rosengren ("Banking continues to be stressed"), Bernanke ("tremendous concerns about the stability of the banking system"), Yellen, Hoenig.
- Inflation: falling, deflation-trap worries (Yellen, Bullard, Rosengren, Kohn, Fisher, Lockhart, Bernanke "disinflation is a concern"). Hoenig and Warsh doubt that deflation is the top risk. Plosser and Evans see less deflationary paths. Expectations are described as fragile (Lacker) or anchored (Plosser).
- Policy: all want to keep 0-1/4. Rosengren, Evans (marginally), Lockhart and Duke (marginally) prefer A (add $250bn MBS). Most prefer B (wait for Treasury/fiscal clarity). Bullard wants "very aggressive" later. Lacker prefers buying Treasuries instead of TALF and says he would dissent. He is the only "No".

**4. Directional statements (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint, with deflation risk. Core PCE about 1 percent and falling (Yellen), "inflation has fallen significantly" (Bernanke summary), "disinflation... deflationary trap" (Bullard, Yellen). Meaningful deflation risk, but not an acute spiral, so about 20.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Far below potential. "Very deep and protracted downturn" (Yellen), "worst postwar recession" globally (Bernanke), it will take "many years before the economy returns to potential" (Yellen), unemployment "well above the NAIRU" (Rosengren). Around 15, GFC trough anchor.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very slack. "Severe hemorrhaging in jobs" (Yellen), unemployment above 8 percent heading to 8.5 percent (Plosser, Yellen, Kohn), large layoffs (Fisher, Evans). About 17.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Contracting sharply. Worst holiday season in a company's history (Yellen), capex cuts "average a third" (Fisher), business fixed investment down "just over 20 percent" in Q2 (Pianalto), "consumption, business equipment spending, and housing are all falling rapidly" (Kohn). About 15.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only an aside from Plosser ("productivity shock on financial intermediation") and potential growth numbers from Pianalto. No real discussion of productivity growth, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tight. "Credit conditions... extremely tight" (statement read-out), "very tight credit conditions" (Bernanke), Kohn on "firmer financial conditions", credit to non-high-quality borrowers "very expensive". Small improvements in money and mortgage markets only. Bright line requires above 50. About 80.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction is described. Members discuss low long-term Treasury yields and the demand for Treasuries (Rosengren: "the one asset that everybody wants to buy"; Kohn: "rates are already low"). That is yield level and flight to quality, not dysfunction. I score a low normal-functioning number with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Wide by level. "Credit spreads remain breathtakingly large" (Yellen), spreads "very, very large" (Rosengren), BBB yield 2 points above September (Kohn), ABS 500-600bp over LIBOR (Lacker), BB borrower at 18 percent. Plosser says spreads should be expected to stay elevated in a recession, which still implies a high level. Bright line above 50. About 82.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity prices keep falling, financial stocks "crushed", single-digit share prices (Warsh), Dudley on a high equity risk premium. Participants see depressed, distressed valuations (not a bubble). About 68.
- BANKING_STRESS: Severe, with solvency concerns. About 84.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has strengthened recently (Kohn: "a strengthening of the dollar pointing to somewhat firmer financial conditions"; Fisher: exports declined "because of the stronger dollar"). Bright line requires above 50. Few mentions, so modest, about 62.
- Inflation sub-components: goods price declines in used cars and apparel and energy/commodities (Yellen, statement) so goods low. Services mentioned only via sticky-price services disinflation (Yellen) and Lacker's service-sector wage index decline, so low but weakly evidenced. Housing inflation (shelter costs) is not discussed in the current period (house prices falling is not shelter inflation), so N/A. Expectations: discussed at length, described as stable so far but fragile with downside risk, "diversity" (Bernanke), expected to "move down rather than up" (Bernanke), so a bit below anchored, about 40.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress; wide spreads, so high. BANKING_STRESS higher = more stress; solvency worries, so high. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher = tighter; tight, so high. EQUITY_VALUATION higher = more depressed/stress; participants describe depressed, so above 50. OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR, CONSUMPTION all low. All match.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: strongly dovish. Zero rate maintained, balance sheet expanding, extension of facilities, debate is about how much more easing and when (A vs B). Nobody argues for tightening; the "hawkish" voices (Fisher, Hoenig, Plosser, Lacker on monetization/credit programs) are about balance-sheet composition and communication, not rate tightening. Lacker thinks liftoff may come in 2010 rather than 2012, a mild signal only. About 18.
- Dissent: one formal "No" (Lacker) on TALF/credit allocation, with an earlier "No" on the agency-guidelines suspension. Sharp, extended exchange between Lacker and Dudley (market failure vs no market failure, "Pareto efficient", "buggy whip", "disequilibrium is a non sequitur... like astrology"), which is a framework-level disagreement, plus real splits over A vs B and over adding the inflation sentence (straw vote 9 to 6). But otherwise unanimous on the thrust of policy. About 42.
- Financial stability attention: dominant. The first afternoon is about facilities and bank-rescue design, and the go-around is heavily about banks, TARP, CRE and financial markets. About 85.
- Forward guidance: "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for some time" and readiness to expand MBS and buy Treasuries; Bernanke "we could be at zero for quite a long time"; Taylor-rule remark implying negative rates through 2011. Strong easing bias, no explicit date/threshold. About 11.
- Uncertainty: very high. "Uncertainty remains high" (Bernanke), "well outside the range of normal experience... model-based forecasts should be viewed with greater skepticism" (Dudley), "extremely abnormal environment" (Pianalto), "forecasting... is guesswork" (Plosser), "no operational handbook" (Pianalto), "uncharted waters" (Bernanke quoting press). About 80.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 15 (<50, "below potential") OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 80 (>50, "tight") OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 82 (>50, stated high level) OK. BANKING_STRESS 84 (>50, "stressed") OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 (>50, strengthening) OK. TREASURY_STRESS 40 (no dysfunction, <=55) OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 15 vs LABOR 17 vs CONSUMPTION 16: within 15 points. FC 80 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 82: co-move. FC 80 vs TREASURY 40: acceptable since Treasury market is not described as dysfunctional (flight to quality, low yields). Parent INFLATION_PRESSURE 22 vs sub-components goods 25, services 35, expectations 40: parent is lower because the discussion centres on deflation risk and falling core inflation; sub-components are thinly evidenced.

**9. N/A.** PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed as productivity growth. INFLATION_HOUSING: shelter inflation not addressed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 +-5, Moderate-High (High)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "I expect core PCE price inflation to come in at about 1 percent this year and then edge down a bit more"; Bullard: "I am very concerned about deflationary expectations possibly taking hold."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is low and falling with a real deflation-trap risk, though Hoenig, Warsh and Plosser downplay it.
- Direction: below neutral because core inflation is around 1 percent and heading lower with slack.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 15 +-5, High**
- Evidence: Yellen: "We are in the midst of a very deep and protracted downturn"; Bernanke: "this seems very likely to be the worst postwar recession."
- Center of gravity: Very large and growing slack across all speakers.
- Direction: below potential; "many years before the economy returns to potential" (Yellen).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 +-5, High**
- Evidence: Yellen: "severe hemorrhaging in jobs and a sizable jump in the unemployment rate"; Fisher: CEO "plans to cut 17,000 jobs."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment above 8 percent heading to 8.5 percent, layoffs broad.
- Direction: far below full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 16 +-5, High**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Consumption, business equipment spending, and housing are all falling rapidly"; Pianalto: "a steep decline of just over 20 percent in the second quarter" in business fixed investment.
- Center of gravity: Spending and investment are contracting sharply with capex cut by a third.
- Direction: well below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**
- Not discussed as a topic.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 80 +-6, High**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Very tight credit conditions are, in turn, affecting both households and firms"; Kohn: "financial markets remain severely disrupted and fragile."
- Center of gravity: Credit very tight despite modest improvement in money and mortgage markets.
- Direction: tighter than neutral; "extremely tight" in the policy statement.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-10, Low**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "Treasury securities are the one asset that everybody wants to buy now"; Kohn: "Rates are already low."
- Center of gravity: Treasury market discussed for yields and purchase policy, with no dysfunction reported.
- Direction: at or below neutral; flight to quality, not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 82 +-6, High**
- Evidence: Yellen: "credit spreads remain breathtakingly large"; Rosengren: "spreads that are very, very large."
- Center of gravity: Spreads at crisis-level highs, with only slight easing; ABS spreads 500-600bp.
- Direction: above neutral on stated high level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 68 +-8, Moderate**
- Evidence: Warsh: "Single-digit share prices for a large class of financial institutions"; Dudley: "risk premium for the U.S. equity market remaining high with lower equity prices."
- Center of gravity: Participants see equities, especially financials, as sharply depressed and distressed.
- Direction: depressed (stress) side of neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 84 +-5, High**
- Evidence: Dudley: "the banking system is probably in worse shape today than it was last fall"; Warsh: financial institutions "perceived by markets to be effectively insolvent or nearly so."
- Center of gravity: Severe systemic stress, rising losses, repeated rescues, solvency questions.
- Direction: far above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +-8, Moderate**
- Evidence: Kohn: "a strengthening of the dollar pointing to somewhat firmer financial conditions"; Fisher: exports declined "because of the stronger dollar."
- Center of gravity: Dollar has recently strengthened, mentioned by few.
- Direction: above neutral on appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 25.** Yellen: price declines "in categories of goods with highly flexible prices, like used cars and apparel"; falling energy and commodity prices (Hoenig, statement).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 35.** Yellen: disinflationary pressures also in sticky-price goods and services; Lacker: "declines in the wage indexes in the manufacturing and services areas." Thin evidence.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Shelter inflation not discussed for the current period.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Pianalto: Michigan expectations "do not seem to be shifting either up or down yet"; Bernanke: expectations "will move down rather than up"; Lacker: not "as well established as we want."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 18 +-6, High**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "I see little harm in expanding our program of purchases of mortgage-backed securities"; Pianalto: "I have a predisposition to provide further monetary stimulus."
- Center of gravity: Maximum accommodation at the zero bound; debate is only on how much more and when; no one wants tightening.
- Direction: strongly dovish; hawkish-sounding remarks concern balance sheet composition and monetization, not tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-8, Moderate**
- Evidence: Lacker: "I would respectfully dissent because I would prefer to expand the monetary base by purchasing U.S. Treasury securities"; Dudley: "that is a fundamental basis of the disagreement" (after Lacker calls the economy Pareto efficient).
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus a sharp framework-level exchange, but broad agreement on the direction of policy and statement.
- Direction: above the near-consensus range because of the dissent and heated Lacker-Dudley debate; below deep split.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 85 +-6, High**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "The most important news on the financial side... is the worse-than-expected results in the banking system"; Warsh: "we are back at maybe the most precarious state in financial markets" at a regular FOMC meeting.
- Center of gravity: Facilities, bank rescue design, TARP and CRE dominate nearly all of the discussion.
- Direction: very high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 11 +-5, High**
- Evidence: Statement: "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for some time"; Bernanke: "we could be at zero for quite a long time."
- Center of gravity: Strong easing bias with readiness to expand MBS and possibly buy Treasuries; no explicit date.
- Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 80 +-7, High**
- Evidence: Dudley: "We are well outside the range of normal experience"; Pianalto: "no operational handbook yet exists."
- Center of gravity: Most speakers stress unusual uncertainty about recovery, banks, policy tools and inflation expectations.
- Direction: very high.
