# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2014-10-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It contains: members' questions in the staff Q&A (market inflation compensation, reverse-repo testing, the dollar), a short financial-stability comment round, the economic go-around, the Chair's summary, the policy go-around on the draft statement (alternatives A/B), a long discussion of the longer-run goals statement (symmetry, "balanced approach", financial stability, consensus forecast), and a roll-call read-out. Many staff answers are missing, so Q&A passages are one-sided fragments; none looked garbled.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2014: the Committee is ending asset purchases at this meeting and holds the funds rate near zero with "considerable time" forward guidance. Members report a turbulent intermeeting period (an October risk-off episode, a sharp Treasury rally, a stronger dollar, weaker Europe and China, falling oil). I score only what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Yellen; Vice Chairman Dudley; Governors Fischer, Tarullo, Powell, Brainard; Presidents Williams, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Bullard, Evans, Fisher, Lockhart, Mester, Lacker, Plosser, George. The main economic view is shared: US growth near 3 percent, labor market improving, inflation below target on energy and the dollar, downside risk from abroad. Kocherlakota (inflation credibility; wants continued purchases), Evans (low-inflation risk; objects to paragraph 2 wording) and Brainard (patience, ZLB asymmetry) lean dovish. Plosser, George and Lacker lean hawkish (policy rules say tighter, earlier liftoff, "behind the curve" risk). Bullard worried about inflation expectations but accepted ending QE.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Core and total PCE are about 1.5 percent (Rosengren, Yellen), "well below our 2 percent objective", energy and the stronger dollar will push it lower, and Evans sees an undershoot "for at least another three years". Most expect a gradual return. Lacker notes an uptick in Richmond price indexes, and George and Fisher cite wage firming, which offset slightly. Score around 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. Growth is about 3 percent, "somewhat above trend" (Mester) and Plosser expects "somewhat stronger-than-trend growth", but the level is still below potential: Yellen says "appreciable slack still remains", Brainard sees "continued slack", and Mester says little underutilization remains. Net below 50, close to it: 43. Bright line (below potential means under 50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below midpoint but improving. Unemployment is 5.9 percent (Rosengren), Yellen says slack is diminished but not gone, wage growth is only starting to pick up, and Brainard stresses part-time work and participation. Anchor for 5.5-7 percent unemployment is 31-45. Score 42.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Roughly at trend to slightly above. Job gains, lower gasoline prices, higher confidence, strong equipment orders (Lacker, Tarullo) against soft retail sales and weak housing (Brainard, Plosser). Net 54.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only a qualitative remark by Tarullo on capital spending and productivity. Not a productivity assessment, so N/A.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Members repeatedly say "very accommodative" (Dudley), "highly accommodative" (Powell), "highly supportive of economic growth" (Yellen). Slightly tighter than last meeting. Bright line (accommodative means under 50) satisfied. Score 32.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Rosengren and Brainard flag the abrupt move in the 10-year ("massive price move and huge volumes in a market that is among the most liquid... for reasons that we still don't really understand ... somewhat concerning"), and Rosengren worries about dealer positioning. Members do not describe dysfunction. Yield moves alone are not dysfunction under the rubric. Score 47, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only is widening ("increases in credit spreads", Yellen), but the starting level was thin (Williams: "how thin high-yield spreads were"; Fischer: premiums "far too low and yields are compressed"). Fischer also notes a high-yield spread near 6 points as a high yield. Members view credit as easing (Dudley, Lockhart: credit readily available). Level is below normal, so under 50: 40, low-moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities fell then "almost back where they started"; Powell says the market "cuts neither way". Earlier worries about complacency, no valuation call now. Default near neutral: 46.
- BANKING_STRESS: Dudley: "Banks are well capitalized and tending to ease lending standards"; asset-quality-review read as manageable; Powell: mortgage standards loosening for most. Bright line (well-capitalized means under 50) satisfied. Score 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Discussed extensively as a continued appreciation (Brainard, Evans, Lockhart, Powell, Fisher). Bright line (appreciation means over 50) satisfied. Members debate effect size, not strength. Score 66.

**6. Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Action is ending QE3 and holding the near-zero rate with "considerable time" retained and data-dependence sentences added. Majority support alternative B; Dudley expects the market to hear it as slightly hawkish and sees June as most likely liftoff. Dovish edge: Kocherlakota (alternative A: keep purchasing, rate commitment until inflation outlook at 2 percent), Evans, Rosengren (later liftoff), Brainard ("holding our fire for a few more meetings"). Hawkish edge: Plosser, George, Lacker, Mester; Fisher wants "considerable time" gone. Tapering ending is already expected. Net lean dovish-to-balanced: 46.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Kocherlakota votes No). Reservations and statement-wording objections from Evans, Fisher, Plosser (who dissented last time but now supports as a transitional step), Mester, Lockhart. Differences in assessment of inflation expectations are real but they converge on policy; the long framework discussion is collegial. 36.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Substantial: a dedicated financial-stability comment round (wholesale funding data, leveraged loans, Pimco redemptions), repeated discussion of the volatility episode and Treasury market move, and a large section on whether financial stability belongs in the goals statement. It does not rival the macro assessment. 48.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: "Considerable time" kept and tied to the end of purchases, but the two new sentences stress data dependence and liftoff timing. Several want to drop the phrase, Dudley says some risk that liftoff slips later, Fischer says "never say never" on QE. Easing lean with data dependence, but close to neutral: 40.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Global-growth risks, dollar effects and market-based inflation measures are seen as hard to read; Tarullo and Brainard want a reference to increased uncertainty about foreign prospects, Dudley is "totally uncertain" about post-liftoff dynamics. But the domestic baseline is held with reasonable confidence ("my view ... has changed very little"). Moderately elevated: 40.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP under 50 (slack remains); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS under 50 (accommodative); CREDIT_SPREADS under 50 (level thin, direction widening only); BANKING_STRESS under 50; DOLLAR_STRENGTH over 50; TREASURY_STRESS 47 (no dysfunction described). All satisfied.

**8. Polarity and coherence.** Output gap 43 vs labor tightness 42 vs consumption 54: gaps of 11-12, within 15, consistent with solid spending but slack remaining. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 32 and CREDIT_SPREADS 40 co-move on the loose side; the spread score is higher because of the recent widening, consistent. Treasury stress near normal with accommodative conditions, fine. Inflation parent 32 vs sub-components (goods 30, expectations 40; services and housing not discussed) consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: no assessment. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not specifically discussed by members (wages and the Fed's own price measures discussed in aggregate only). INFLATION_GOODS is scored because members repeatedly cite imported goods and commodity price declines.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen: "both total and core PCE inflation are running at about 1½ percent... a substantial deceleration in headline PCE prices from lower energy costs." Evans: "inflation will underrun our 2 percent objective for at least another three years." Center of gravity: below target and set to dip further, with most expecting a gradual return. Direction: below neutral because inflation is well below 2 percent and falling on energy and the dollar.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 43 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen: "appreciable slack still remains in the labor market"; Mester: "moderate growth of about 3 percent, which is somewhat above trend." Center of gravity: near-trend-plus growth closing a gap that is not yet closed. Direction: below neutral because slack is explicitly said to remain.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 +-6, High.** Evidence: Rosengren: "unemployment rate at 5.9 percent"; Brainard: "potential for slack to be somewhat larger than indicated by the unemployment rate." Center of gravity: strong payroll gains but slack remains and wages are not broadly accelerating (Yellen: "not yet seeing broad-based evidence of stronger wage gains"). Direction: below neutral at 5.9 percent unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Plosser: "consumption will grow slightly above trend"; Lacker: "new orders for nondefense capital goods, excluding aircraft, are up more than 8 percent." Offset: Brainard: "the housing recovery remains painfully slow." Center of gravity: solid spending, helped by cheap gasoline, with housing the weak spot. Direction: slightly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +-6, High.** Evidence: Dudley: "financial market conditions are slightly tighter... but they're still very accommodative"; Powell: "financial conditions remain highly accommodative." Center of gravity: a short bout of volatility that has largely reversed. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 47 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Brainard: "a massive price move and huge volumes in a market that is among the most liquid in the world for reasons that we still don't really understand"; Rosengren on the 10-year falling below 2 percent. Center of gravity: an unexplained but one-off move, with no stated dysfunction. Direction: near normal; yield and price moves alone do not count as dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Williams: "how thin high-yield spreads were"; Yellen: "increases in credit spreads... credit conditions seem to be easing somewhat." Center of gravity: spreads widened from thin levels and credit availability is described as good. Direction: below neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Powell: "Equities are almost back where they started, and the equity market, I wouldn't say, cuts either way right now"; Fischer: complaints that "risk premiums are far too low" before the correction. Center of gravity: no valuation verdict now; earlier complacency worries eased. Direction: roughly neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Dudley: "Banks are well capitalized and tending to ease lending standards"; Dudley on the European stress test: "the net capital need for the banking system appears very manageable." Center of gravity: healthy US banking system, with only data-gap concerns on wholesale funding (Rosengren, Tarullo). Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 66 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Brainard: "the continued appreciation of the dollar"; Powell: "a stronger dollar"; Williams: "the appreciation of the dollar and the slowing demand from abroad will cut into our net exports a bit." Center of gravity: a clear, notable appreciation that members treat as a headwind to growth and inflation. Direction: above neutral on appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30, N/A for uncertainty/confidence fields.** Evidence: Williams: "Cheaper imports stemming from the stronger dollar... falling commodity prices will all exert disinflationary pressures." Yellen: "lower non-energy commodity and imported goods prices."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not specifically addressed by members.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Evidence: Kocherlakota: "five-year, five-year forward inflation breakevens... lower than all but 1 percent of the daily observations in the previous 10 years"; Mester: "survey-based measures... remain quite stable near 2 percent." Center of gravity: surveys anchored near 2, market measures lower and read mainly as risk and liquidity premiums, with a vocal minority worried about drift down. Below neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Williams: "I support alternative B... asset purchase program has done its job"; Kocherlakota: "I favor alternative A... keeping the federal funds rate target range at its current level, at least until the one- to two-year-ahead inflation outlook has returned to 2 percent." Center of gravity: QE ends and the rate stays at zero with patience emphasized, balanced by hawkish voices. Direction: slightly below 50 (hold with mild easing bias).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-7, High.** Evidence: roll call: "President Kocherlakota No"; Fisher: "the objectionable calendar guidance 'considerable time'... I will support option B." Center of gravity: one formal dissent plus several stated reservations about wording, without sharp exchanges.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Kocherlakota: "I continue to see financial-stability analysis as vital in evaluating whether the current path of policy is appropriate"; Brainard on financial stability as "an implicit third leg of our responsibilities." Center of gravity: a dedicated round plus the framework discussion, but the economic assessment stays primary.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Dudley: "June as remaining, still, the most likely date, with some risk that the date could slip later"; Lockhart: "the 'considerable time' formulation... consistent with... liftoff in the third quarter of 2015." Center of gravity: patient, data-dependent guidance with a hint of hawkishness in the added data-dependence sentences (Dudley: "read... as a little bit hawkish"). Direction: below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Brainard: "the added uncertainty and that downside risk validate our strategy of remaining patient"; Dudley: "I'm just totally uncertain about how these things are actually going to evolve." Center of gravity: uncertainty is highlighted about global growth, markets and post-liftoff dynamics, but the domestic baseline is held with reasonable confidence.
