# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2011-09-21 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed), covering a two-day meeting. Day one holds the staff Q&A, a go-round on policy tools and communications (balance sheet tools, IOER, forward guidance, a numerical inflation objective, unemployment and inflation thresholds, SEP rate paths), and the economic go-round. Day two holds the Chair's summary of the economic go-round, the Chair's own assessment, questions on the IOER, MBS reinvestment and statement wording, then the policy go-round on Alternatives A, B and C, a straw poll on paragraph 3 versus 3', and the roll-call (7 yes, 3 no). The text reads cleanly. No garbled passages affect scoring. Some staff-answer turns are missing, so a few questions appear without their answers.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2011, with the funds rate at the zero bound, an extended-period / mid-2013 date guidance already in the statement, and the debate is over additional easing through a maturity extension program (MEP / "Twist"). I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish / easing camp: Bernanke (recommends B as a "bridge"), Yellen, Williams, Evans, Rosengren, Raskin, Dudley, Tarullo, Duke, Pianalto (B with caution). Evans, Yellen, Rosengren and Raskin say more is needed. Dudley says he would prefer a larger LSAP. Rosengren and Evans press for conditional guidance.
- Hawkish / hold camp: Fisher (cannot support A or B, wants C), Kocherlakota (C), Plosser (C), Lacker (C, non-voter), George (additional accommodation "somewhat premature"), Lockhart (devil's advocate for C, then B with 3'), Bullard (supports B but against MBS reinvestment today and in favor of 3').
- Economy: almost all see slow growth, rising recession risk (about one-third), dismal consumer sentiment, deteriorating labor market, and Europe as the main financial threat. Inflation is the point of disagreement: Lacker, Plosser, Lockhart, Bullard and George stress persistent inflation (headline PCE near 3%, core about 2 to 2.25%), while Williams, Yellen, Rosengren, Dudley and Evans see receding inflation and heavy slack.

**4. Directional signals for core dimensions (Direction Rule).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above the midpoint. Headline is "up close to 3 percent" (Bullard), core is about 2 to 2.25% and has "surprised on the high side every single time" (Lacker), and Bernanke says "inflation is too high but I do think that it will moderate". Doves see it as receding and the medium-term outlook as under 2%. Mixed, net modestly above target, about 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. "Extraordinary slack in the labor market" (Yellen), "a ton of resource slack" (Evans), "the output gap story is still fundamentally a solid one" (Tarullo), growth "below potential" (Rosengren). Dissenting voices (Lacker, Kocherlakota) say slack may be smaller than measured, but they do not claim the gap is closed. About 30.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Far below the midpoint. Unemployment 9.1%, long-term unemployed 43% of the unemployed, payrolls flat, hours falling. Firm-level skill-mismatch anecdotes do not change the level. About 20.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Consumer spending "anemic" (Yellen, real PCE 0.4% annualized since March), sentiment at record lows, firms cutting investment plans conditionally. Bright spots in exports, equipment and autos temper it. About 32.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not assessed by participants (only passing references such as Pianalto's "continued productivity growth" as an inflation restraint). N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Above the midpoint (tighter). Bernanke: "Financial conditions have deteriorated... considerable tightening" and "strained - even reminiscent of 2008 in some dimensions" (his summary). Yellen: "many other indicators of financial conditions have tightened". Offsets are low Treasury yields and accommodative policy, and stress has not reached freeze levels. About 66.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss low yields from safe-haven flows (Rosengren, Yellen) and worry about market function if the Fed owns 70% of some long CUSIPs (Dudley), but no one describes dysfunction. Orderly, with a low-yield flight to quality. About 38, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is wide. Yellen: "spreads on high-yield bonds have reached recession levels, CMBS spreads have similarly widened, CDS spreads on several large banking organizations... close to... Lehman". Dudley: mortgage basis "wider than it has been at any time in the past six years, except for the heart of the crisis". Williams: European spreads "comparable to... September 2008". Stated high levels, so above 50. About 66.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss declines and volatility (bank stocks down markedly, "volatile equity market") but little valuation commentary. Fisher: US equities "if not richly priced, fairly priced". Concern about the declines is real, so modestly above neutral. About 57.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banks are described as sound (capital and liquidity "much greater than a few years ago" per Dudley and Evans; Duke: credit quality "back to historic norms", "no longer scary, but... gloomy"; Tarullo: "way, way better shape than in 2007"). Offsets: European banks acutely stressed with dollar-funding mismatches, US bank CDS above 300 (Rosengren), bank stock prices sharply down, contagion fear. Mixed, system sound with stress pockets. About 48.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Little direct assessment. Bernanke: "to the extent that we continue to have financial stresses, the dollar tends to benefit". George: "depreciating dollar" in the baseline. Plosser and Raskin raise hypothetical appreciation. Mixed, near neutral, Low confidence. About 52.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more credit stress; evidence = wide, recession-level spreads, so score above 50 matches. BANKING_STRESS: US banks "well capitalized"/sound push below 50, European stress pushes up, net slightly below 50. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: high unemployment means low tightness, so a low score matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: A majority of participants (including the Chair and a majority of voters) favor additional easing via the MEP plus MBS reinvestment, with several wanting more and flagging more aggressive tools if conditions worsen. A hawkish minority opposes any easing on inflation and efficacy grounds. Net: moderate dovish, with the dovish side leaning toward more. Lean about 32.
- Disagreement: Three formal dissents (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser) with distinct reasons (harm to savers and "do no harm"; credibility absent a stated willingness to tolerate higher inflation; ineffectiveness and the Europe risk). A fourth hawk, Lacker, a non-voter, argued sharply for C and likened tolerating higher inflation to the 1970s. There were sharp exchanges (Lacker versus Dudley and Bernanke on the political backlash and on the 1970s; Evans versus Bullard on models; Tarullo versus Lacker on the mandate), plus disagreement about the economics (slack, inflation persistence). Significant division. About 74.
- Financial stability attention: Very high. Europe, European bank dollar funding, money market funds, bank CDS and a long contingency-planning discussion run through nearly every speaker. About 72.
- Forward guidance: The existing statement promises exceptionally low rates through mid-2013 (date-based). Many speakers want it strengthened through state-contingent thresholds or a duration. No participant calls for any tightening signal. Strong easing signal. About 10.
- Uncertainty: "uncertainty, uncertainty, uncertainty" (Plosser, reporting business contacts), uncertainty "one of the highest levels in 50 years" (Williams), unreliable forecasts, doubts about measuring slack and about how policy transmits, "how little we know" (Bernanke). High uncertainty. About 70.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 (<50, "slack"), consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 66 (>50, "strained"/"tightened"), consistent. CREDIT_SPREADS 66 (>50, stated wide levels), consistent. BANKING_STRESS 48 (US "sound" yet neutral overall because of European and CDS stress; I allow slightly below 50 but not lower, given the emphasis on acute stress pockets). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 52 (Low confidence; the only directional statement about the current dollar is mixed). TREASURY_STRESS 38 (no dysfunction, below 60).

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs LABOR 20 (10 apart; fine, the labor measure is more extreme at 9.1% unemployment). OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs CONSUMPTION 32 (aligned). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 66 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 66 (co-move). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 66 vs TREASURY_STRESS 38: Treasury market is orderly and benefits from safe-haven demand, so no co-move is required. INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 vs sub-components (goods 58, services 55, housing 60, expectations 46): average about 55, consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: participants did not assess productivity trend. The four inflation sub-components are scored because members discussed core goods, OER / rents, and expectations.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "Every Tealbook this year has forecast a decline in inflation during 2011, and inflation has surprised on the high side every single time." Bernanke: "I agree that inflation is too high, but I do think that it will moderate." George: "Price pressures are broad based, and inflation recently has been above 2 percent."
- Summary: Headline and core run above 2% with persistent upside surprises, but about half the room expects moderation and slack to hold it down.
- Direction: Above neutral because of the stated above-target readings, held to the low 60s at most by the receding-inflation camp.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "I expect the extraordinary slack in the labor market to push wage growth and core inflation down." Evans: "there's a ton of resource slack." Rosengren: "The economy has been growing below potential."
- Summary: Broad agreement on substantial slack; only some question its size.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe the economy as well below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Raskin: share unemployed 27 weeks and longer "now at 43 percent, well above the 15 percent historical average." Kocherlakota's projection for unemployment is "just over 8 1/2 percent by the end of next year"; Evans cites "9.1 percent unemployment."
- Summary: Unemployment above 9% with deteriorating hours and payrolls; skill-mismatch anecdotes are marginal.
- Direction: Well below neutral given the level of unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "consumer spending is anemic... real PCE has advanced at an annual rate of only 0.4 percent, a truly glacial pace." Evans: "The ice keeps getting thinner"; firms plan "a 30 percent reduction in capital expenditure spending" if needed.
- Summary: Weak consumption and cautious business spending, with pockets (exports, equipment, autos) preventing a collapse score.
- Direction: Below trend because spending is described as anemic and sentiment as at record lows.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**
- Evidence: Only passing references (Pianalto, "continued productivity growth" restraining inflation).
- Summary: Not assessed.
- Direction: Not applicable.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 66 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Financial conditions have deteriorated. If you look at financial conditions indexes, for example, they show considerable tightening." Yellen: "many other indicators of financial conditions have tightened over recent weeks."
- Summary: Conditions are tighter and strained, though not frozen.
- Direction: Above neutral because multiple speakers describe tightening and stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "Safe-haven flows have pushed Treasury yields toward historically low levels." Dudley worries "we would own as much as 70 percent of the outstanding CUSIPs in some of the longer-dated Treasury issues" and function effects, which is prospective.
- Summary: Low yields reflect safe-haven demand, with no described dysfunction.
- Direction: Below the stress threshold because yields moved on flight to quality rather than market breakdown.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 66 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "spreads on high-yield bonds have reached recession levels, CMBS spreads have similarly widened." Dudley: "the mortgage basis... is now wider than it has been at any time in the past six years, except for the heart of the crisis."
- Summary: Spreads are described at wide levels, in places near crisis readings.
- Direction: Above neutral because stated levels are high, not just rising.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "the stock prices of financial institutions have declined markedly." Fisher: US stocks "if not richly priced, fairly priced, and therefore, has a lot of downside."
- Summary: Participants see sharp, volatile declines and bank equity weakness, with little explicit view that equities are cheap or rich.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because the declines worry members but are orderly.

**BANKING_STRESS: 48 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "beyond much lower bank share prices, U.S. banks have not been greatly affected... their capital levels and liquidity buffers are much greater." Rosengren: "Bank of America and Morgan Stanley both have credit default swap rates above 300."
- Summary: US banks sound with real stress pockets and heavy exposure to European strain.
- Direction: Near neutral, balancing sound US capital and liquidity against stressed European banks and market pressure on large US banks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 +-7 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "To the extent that we continue to have financial stresses, the dollar tends to benefit." George: "a depreciating dollar" in her baseline.
- Summary: Mixed and tangential; no assessment of the current dollar level.
- Direction: Roughly neutral, with a slight tilt up from safe-haven flows.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58 [Low]** Fisher: "some pressure in core goods"; Dudley says the pressure from commodity pass-through and auto supply disruptions is temporary.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 55 [Low]** Fisher: "some in core services... owners' equivalent rent and rents"; wage-driven service pressure is "very subdued" (Dudley).

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 60 [Low]** Lacker: "owners' equivalent rent has been accelerating"; Dudley discounts it because of the unsold-home overhang.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46 [Moderate]** Williams: "inflation expectations have remained remarkably well anchored"; Lacker calls it "a mixed picture"; Pianalto: Cleveland measure dips "just under 1 1/2 percent."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I would propose to recommend alternative B... a bridge toward whatever future action this Committee may find to be appropriate." Yellen: "I strongly support taking actions at this meeting to foster a stronger recovery"; Lacker: "I do not think we now want or need more monetary stimulus. Accordingly, I support alternative C."
- Summary: Majority, including the Chair, favors modest easing (MEP and MBS reinvestment) with several wanting more, against a hawkish minority.
- Direction: Dovish because most advocate additional accommodation; the easing is limited (not QE) and a sizable minority opposes it.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 74 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll-call 7-3 (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser against). Fisher: "I cannot support an extension, and I will not vote for alternative B as it is stated, nor alternative A." Lacker: tolerating higher inflation "bears... more than a passing resemblance to the strategy pursued in the late 1960s and 1970s."
- Summary: Three formal dissents with different reasoning, a fourth hawk arguing sharply, and disputes over economics and the framework.
- Direction: Strongly divided, well above the one-dissent baseline.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "My biggest concern is that we will have at least a serious threat of a financial crisis this fall." Bernanke: "financial instability looks to be rearing its ugly head once again."
- Summary: Europe, bank funding, money funds and contingency planning recur across most speakers, nearly rivaling the economic assessment.
- Direction: Prominent attention (not direction-specific).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 10 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Existing guidance of exceptionally low rates through mid-2013 is retained; Evans wants "aggressive forward guidance" with "7 percent for unemployment and 3 percent for medium-term inflation"; Kocherlakota proposes "interest rates will remain extraordinarily low for the next 12 quarters."
- Summary: Date-based low-rate commitment, with most advocates of change wanting it strengthened or made state-contingent; no tightening signal.
- Direction: Strongly easing-biased; capped from the lowest band because no stronger new guidance was adopted and a minority wants no further easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 70 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Plosser: business contacts report "uncertainty, uncertainty, uncertainty." Williams: "Uncertainty in U.S. financial markets has soared to one of the highest levels in 50 years."
- Summary: Many participants stress unusual uncertainty about Europe, forecasts, slack and policy transmission.
- Direction: High because most speakers emphasize difficulty, though the room still holds baseline forecasts.
