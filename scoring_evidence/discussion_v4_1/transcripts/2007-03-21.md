# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2007-03-21 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, v2 input (staff turns removed). It holds: members' questions during the staff Q&A (subprime, alt-A, Eurodollar pricing), the economic go-around (Yellen through Mishkin), the Chair's summary and remarks, the policy go-around on alternatives A/B/C and the statement wording, a long second-day discussion of a numerical inflation objective and a forecast-narrative trial run, and a vote read-out (10 Yes, 0 No). The inflation-objective and communications material is not an assessment of conditions; I score the economic go-around, the Q&A comments, and the policy go-around.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2007. Housing correction and subprime stress dominate the news, business investment is weak, unemployment about 4.5%, core inflation stuck above comfort zones, funds rate 5.25%, inverted yield curve. Markets price in easing. This is only background; scores come from what members said.

**3. Speakers.** Yellen (downside risks, private-equity "bubble", inflation upside), Moskow, Plosser, Hoenig (hawkish, strong District), Fisher (fatter recession tail and stubborn inflation), Lacker (hawkish, "will need to tighten this year"), Pianalto, Minehan, Lockhart, Stern, Poole, Geithner, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Bernanke (summary and closing remarks).

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Core PCE about 2 1/4 to 2.3, core CPI 2.7 (Plosser: "troubling"; Moskow: "too high for my taste"; Bernanke: "general disappointment with recent inflation readings"). Some soften this (Mishkin "not nervous", Kroszner "by no means out of control", Stern "not alarming"), and most expect slow moderation. Clearly above comfort zones but not accelerating: about 62.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is below potential (Yellen: "substantially below potential"; Kohn: "modestly below potential"; Moskow: "below potential in '07"). But the level of output is near potential (Kohn in the policy round: "output is still running close to potential; in level terms, it might even be beyond potential"). Lacker sees no "easing in resource utilization". Level near potential, so about 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above midpoint. Unemployment 4.5% (Kohn: "low by historical standards"), "labor markets remain tight" (Moskow, Lacker, Hoenig, Bernanke), skilled-labor shortages (Plosser, Hoenig, Minehan). Some softening expected, wage acceleration not broad (Lacker, Bernanke on NAIRU). About 62.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below the midpoint on net. Housing contracting, capital spending "persistently weak", "deepening puzzle", retail flat, consumption to slow (Bernanke); offset by solid consumer income and spending in most Districts, strong exports and government spending. About 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Weak recent productivity (Geithner: "significantly below 2 percent" for ten quarters; Kohn, Mishkin, Bernanke on possible trend slowdown or labor hoarding). Most call it cyclical. Modestly below trend: about 38.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below 50 (loose/supportive). Kohn: "financial conditions that remain quite supportive of growth"; Yellen: "low long-term yields, ample liquidity"; Warsh: "still favorable"; Bernanke: "interest rates are low, stock prices are pretty strong, and liquidity is good". Offset by recent tumult and tightening mortgage credit. About 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss yield-curve inversion, term premia and Eurodollar futures pricing, but not any dysfunction (Geithner: "overall liquidity seems fine"). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: LEVEL is low. Yellen: "generally low level of compensation for risk"; Geithner: "lower levels of risk premiums and credit spreads"; Warsh: "credit-risk spreads unusually low". Subprime spreads widened (Lacker), but that is direction, off a compressed base. Below 50: about 30.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed as tumult and downside risk (Kohn: "distinct downside risk to the staff's assumption of continued increases in equity prices"; Warsh; Bernanke puzzled by the subprime/stock link), with a few valuation hints ("priced for perfection", private-equity "bubble"). No clear valuation verdict; slightly toward rich: about 44.
- BANKING_STRESS: Sound. Geithner: "very little concern ... about substantial losses in the core of the financial system"; Lacker: no risks "to the financial safety net"; Pianalto: large-bank CEOs not worried. Stress pockets are in subprime lenders and possibly funds. Below 50: about 35.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar has fallen (Kohn: "The dollar has fallen"; Yellen; Pianalto: "dollar depreciation is finally having an effect"). Passing mentions. Below 50: about 35.

**5. Polarity checks.** Credit spreads: higher = more stress; evidence says compressed, so below 50, matches. Banking stress: healthy means below 50, matches. Dollar: depreciation means below 50, matches. Financial conditions: supportive means below 50, matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Near-unanimous hold at 5.25%. Inflation named the "predominant" concern, and many (Hoenig, Lacker, Plosser, Moskow) are wary of signaling easing, yet the tightening bias is removed (symmetric "future policy adjustments"), Geithner and Yellen stress downside risk, Poole wants to let the market do stabilization. Lacker says "we will need to tighten this year". Net: balanced to slight hawkish lean, about 55.
- Dissent: Vote 10-0 (voters present: Bernanke, Geithner, Hoenig, Kohn, Kroszner, Minehan, Mishkin, Moskow, Poole, Warsh). No dissents. Reservations: Hoenig and Lacker want to keep the firming bias; Mishkin initially wanted the old language; Stern/Plosser wanted alternative C wording; debate on "principal" vs "predominant" and on whether to mention financial conditions. Views on the growth outlook vary (Hoenig robust, Fisher/Poole/Geithner uneasy). The second-day inflation-objective debate (Lacker 1%, Mishkin 2%, Hoenig/Minehan/Moskow against any target) is real disagreement but not on policy. Score about 28.
- Financial stability emphasis: Prominent. Opening Q&A is largely on subprime/alt-A, Yellen and Fisher on private-equity excess, Warsh at length on market tumult and credit risk, Geithner on structured credit, and an extended wording debate on referring to financial conditions in the statement. Several participants at length; not dominant over the economic assessment. About 56.
- Forward guidance: The statement drops "additional firming" for "future policy adjustments will depend on ... both inflation and economic growth", with inflation still the predominant concern. Direction-neutral, data-dependent, with some members worrying it will be read as easing; Geithner says path still above market. About 50.
- Uncertainty: Many members say uncertainty about growth has increased, tails are fatter (Bernanke's summary: "uncertainty has increased, and that the tails of the distribution have become fatter"; Geithner "greater uncertainty"; Plosser; Moskow; Kroszner; Kohn). Not extreme, not Knightian. Elevated: about 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 (level near potential, inside 48-52; no "above/below potential" statement about level that contradicts it). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 (<50, "supportive"). CREDIT_SPREADS 30 (<50, level low). BANKING_STRESS 35 (<50, sound). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 (<50, depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 vs LABOR 62 (12 apart, within 15; growth slowed but labor stays tight, level of output near potential). OUTPUT_GAP 50 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 42 (8). FC 36 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 30: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: N/A. Inflation parent 62 vs sub-components (goods 58, services 60, housing 54, expectations 52): average about 56; parent sits a bit above because members repeatedly call the core readings too high and persistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members discussed rate pricing, not market functioning. Sub-components are given because members mentioned each at least briefly.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 62 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "I find the upward trend in core inflation over the past year, from 2 to 2 3/4 percent, troubling." Moskow: "This inflation rate is too high for my taste."
- Summary: Core inflation clearly above comfort zones with upside risks, but expected to ease slowly and not accelerating.
- Direction: above neutral because most members call recent readings disappointing and above desired levels.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "output is still running close to potential; in level terms, it might even be beyond potential." Yellen: growth "substantially below potential."
- Summary: Growth pace below potential, level of output about at potential, utilization not easing.
- Direction: at neutral because level is near potential while only the growth rate is subpar.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "further tightening in our labor markets ... shortages of skilled labor"; Kohn: "at 4 1/2 percent, the unemployment rate is low by historical standards."
- Summary: Unemployment about 4.5% and widespread tightness reports.
- Direction: above neutral because of low unemployment and reported shortages.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "The slowdown in capital investment drew more concern"; Lacker: "outlook for consumer spending remains fairly healthy."
- Summary: Housing and capex weak, consumer solid but expected to slow.
- Direction: below neutral because net spending is below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner: productivity growth "has, over the past ten quarters, come in significantly below 2 percent"; Kohn on "slower growth of productivity".
- Summary: Recent productivity weak, mostly viewed as cyclical.
- Direction: below neutral because growth is below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "financial conditions that remain quite supportive of growth"; Yellen: "low long-term yields, ample liquidity."
- Summary: Conditions loose/supportive despite recent volatility and mortgage tightening.
- Direction: below neutral (loose) because members call conditions supportive/favorable.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no market-functioning discussion; only rates/term premium pricing).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "generally low level of compensation for risk"; Warsh: markets "in which credit-risk spreads have been unusually low."
- Summary: Spreads viewed as unusually low; subprime widening is a localized move.
- Direction: below neutral because the stated level is low (direction of recent move not used).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "distinct downside risk to the staff's assumption of continued increases in equity prices"; Warsh: markets "priced for perfection."
- Summary: Price drop discussed; mild hint of rich valuations, no strong valuation verdict.
- Direction: slightly below neutral (mild overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner: "very little concern ... about substantial losses ... in the core of the financial system"; Lacker: no "broader risks to ... the financial safety net."
- Summary: Core system seen as sound; stress confined to subprime originators.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "The dollar has fallen"; Pianalto: "dollar depreciation is finally having an effect."
- Summary: Dollar weaker; passing mentions only.
- Direction: below neutral because of depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58.** Plosser: "prices for industrial goods continue to move up"; Fisher: "pickup in core goods inflation", import prices "neutral to slightly inflationary".
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 60.** Bernanke: medical costs "have, indeed, proved to be a risk"; Geithner: surprises "concentrated in medical services"; wage pressures noted by Plosser.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 54.** Bernanke: "rents and owners' equivalent rent ... have not yet slowed much"; Fisher: "tamer OER readings"; Kohn expects rent increases to unwind.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Mishkin: "inflation expectations seem to be very solidly grounded"; Lacker: "centered at or above 2 percent"; Moskow: private sector "betting that our inflation objective is 2 percent or higher".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "We should continue to emphasize inflation risk ... I would counsel patience on the rate"; Lacker: "I do believe we will need to tighten this year ... this doesn't seem like a propitious time."
- Summary: Unanimous hold, inflation the predominant concern, tightening bias removed but members resist signaling easing.
- Direction: slightly hawkish of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote 10-0; Hoenig: "I also continue to favor statement language that reflects an explicit bias toward firming"; Lacker: "We should not be loosening the sense of our tightening bias."
- Summary: Unanimous vote with wording reservations from a few and varied growth views; the separate inflation-target debate adds some disagreement.
- Direction: low-to-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 56 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "Could this market volatility ... lead to the kind of credit crunch that would have meaningful downside risks to the economy?"; Yellen: "the bubble in private equity ... raise the risk of a sharp retrenchment in credit."
- Summary: Subprime, private equity and market tumult discussed by many and the statement wording turned partly on financial conditions, but economic assessment still leads.
- Direction: moderately prominent.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Statement read-out: "Future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth"; Geithner: "our expectations about what makes sense for policy are still above the market's expectations."
- Summary: Move from firming bias to data-dependent symmetry, offset by an inflation-first message.
- Direction: neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "uncertainty has increased, and ... the tails of the distribution have become fatter"; Plosser: "my uncertainty of that forecast has increased somewhat."
- Summary: Multiple members say uncertainty is up, tails fatter, but no deep or Knightian language.
- Direction: elevated.
