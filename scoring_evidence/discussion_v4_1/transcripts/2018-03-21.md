# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2018-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: members' questions to staff, the economic go-round (Harker, Bullard, Rosengren, Brainard, Barkin, Quarles, Mester, Evans, Kaplan, George, Bostic, Williams, Kashkari, Dudley, Powell summary), a short second-day exchange, the policy go-round (Rosengren through Dudley, plus Powell's closing), and a roll-call read-out. All votes are Yes (8 of 8 listed, no dissent). The text is clean; the only quirks are the staff-answer turns that were removed, leaving some orphaned questions and "MR. EVANS. Okay." fragments. These do not affect scoring.

**Era context.** This is a meeting where participants describe a strong, fiscally boosted expansion, very low unemployment and inflation approaching 2 percent. I score the text, not the backdrop.

**Speakers.** Powell (Chair), Dudley (Vice Chair), Brainard, Quarles, and the presidents. Views are broadly aligned on a stronger outlook. Evans and Kashkari prefer a more patient stance. Harker and Bullard stress patience and data dependence. Rosengren and Williams lean toward a steeper path. Dudley, George and Williams favor four hikes in 2018.

**Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as roughly at or slightly below target and firming. Core PCE is 2 percent over six months, but 1.5 percent over 12 months (Kashkari). Barkin says it is "effectively at our target". Harker does not expect 2 percent this year, and many expect only a modest overshoot in 2019-20. Most business contacts report restrained pass-through. That is near midpoint, slightly below 50. Score 48.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy above potential. Williams: "above full employment" with growth "considerably in excess of the pace of potential". Rosengren: GDP "exceed potential by almost 1 percentage point". George: "operating beyond its potential". Brainard: "growing above its trend rate". Dissenting nuance from Kashkari and Quarles about possible hidden slack. Bright-line: above potential means above 50. Score 60.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 4.1 percent with forecasts of 3.5-3.7 percent. Williams: "taut". Barkin: "tight and getting tighter". Kaplan: labor shortages are the biggest constraint. Wage growth is muted and some (Kashkari, Quarles) doubt how tight it is. The level is in the tight band. Score 64.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q1 spending is soft (Powell: consumption slowed from 4.25 to 1.5 percent), but members call it temporary. Sentiment is at highs, capex plans are strong (George: a survey high), and fiscal stimulus is expected to lift spending. Bostic and Kaplan are more cautious on tax effects. Net modestly above trend. Score 57.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Williams: growth "very slow through the fourth quarter". Mester: "productivity growth ... has been so low". Kaplan: "sluggish productivity". Barkin and Quarles are hopeful of improvement but it is not yet visible. Modestly to well below trend. Score 38.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Brainard: "financial conditions remain supportive". Mester: "remain accommodative". Powell: "remain accommodative despite a modest net tightening". Dudley: "still very easy". Bright-line: accommodative means below 50, with some tightening since January. Score 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only rising yields, Treasury supply and waning foreign appetite (Brainard, Kaplan, Kashkari), plus Evans's question on money-market spreads. There is no dysfunction. Normal functioning. Score 42, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Brainard: spreads "widened somewhat ... they still remain quite compressed by historical standards". Level governs, so below 50. Score 33.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Brainard: "valuations are stretched"; Bullard: "high by traditional valuation metrics"; Dudley expects a tougher equity outlook but found the correction "mostly reassuring"; Evans's contacts called it a "healthy" reappraisal. Elevated to somewhat elevated, concern moderate. Score 33.
- BANKING_STRESS: Brainard: "the banking system is well capitalized", liquidity risks declined. Bright-line gives below 50. One speaker only. Score 27, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Brainard: real trade-weighted dollar down about 10 percent, "continued dollar weakness". Kashkari: "recent dollar depreciation", "broad dollar has been depreciating". Depreciation means below 50. Score 30.
- INFLATION sub-components: GOODS: rising input and import prices and tariff pass-through (Harker, Mester, Evans, Kashkari "import price inflation ... 3½ percent") but little pricing power (Barkin, Kaplan). Score 54. SERVICES: George expects health-care services prices toward 2.5 percent, wages modest. Score 52. HOUSING: not discussed, N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Evans "too low", Bullard "still too low", Kashkari "remain low", Brainard hopes to "re-anchor", Barkin "stable". Slightly below target. Score 42.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: A 25 bp hike is supported by all; the median SEP shows about three hikes in 2018 and a steeper path. Dudley, George and Williams want four. Rosengren wants language ready for faster moves. Against that, Bullard, Harker, Evans, Kashkari, Kaplan, Bostic and Quarles stress patience, and Evans and Kashkari would have preferred holding or alternative A. Powell: "gradual", "modestly steeper". Moderate hawkish with a clear gradualist, data-dependent counterweight. Score 64.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero formal dissents (unanimous roll call). Reservations: Evans preferred a hold (conceded to consensus); Kashkari preferred A (nonvoter); Bullard warns against a hawkish tone; Rosengren wants more urgency. Disagreement is on pace and the neutral-rate/slack debate, not the action. Score 28.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Questions on the volatility unwind (Kashkari, Evans), Brainard and Kaplan on imbalances and lending standards, Dudley on equity and fiscal risks, Bullard on yield curve and equity valuations. Several members address it meaningfully but it is not dominant. Score 40.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: "Further gradual increases" remains the guidance. Many (Bullard, Williams, Mester, Barkin, Brainard, Dudley) want to remove the "below longer-run levels" sentence soon, a tightening signal, while Bullard and Harker want more emphasis on data dependence. Mild to moderate tightening bias. Score 66.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Powell: "considerable uncertainty" on inflation impetus and "real uncertainty" on the natural rate. Kashkari: "our gauge is broken". Harker: uncertainty "abnormally high". Brainard: fiscal stimulus of this nature is untested; trade and tariff uncertainty (Bostic, Evans). Elevated but most speakers are confident in the baseline. Score 40.

**Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; evidence says accommodative, so score is below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; compressed spreads give a score below 50. BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress; well-capitalized gives a score below 50. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: depreciation gives a score below 50. OUTPUT_GAP: above potential gives a score above 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 60 (above 50), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (below 50), CREDIT_SPREADS 33 (below 50), BANKING_STRESS 27 (below 50), DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 (below 50), TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction, in 36-50). All satisfied.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 60 and LABOR 64 are within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 60 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 57 are close. FC 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 33 co-move. FC and Treasury stress: normal times, Treasury stress near the normal band. INFLATION_PRESSURE 48 is consistent with sub-components (goods 54, services 52, expectations 42).

**N/A.** INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed shelter or rents.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 48 ±5 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Barkin: "for now it's effectively at our target." Kashkari: "on a 12-month basis ... core PCE is at 1.5 percent."
Summary: Inflation is seen as about at target and firming, with 12-month readings still below. Direction confirmation: slightly below to at neutral because 12-month readings are below 2 percent while 6-month readings are at 2.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±6 (High)**
Evidence: Williams: "All signs point to an economy operating above full employment." Rosengren: "real GDP to exceed potential by almost 1 percentage point this year."
Summary: Most members see growth above trend and the economy beyond full employment, with a few doubting how much. Direction confirmation: above neutral because "above potential/above trend" language is widespread.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 64 ±6 (High)**
Evidence: Barkin: "Labor markets are tight and getting tighter." Kaplan: labor shortages "are also ... the biggest constraint to growth".
Summary: Unemployment of 4.1 percent heading to 3.5 percent with widespread shortages, though wage growth is muted. Direction confirmation: above neutral on the level of unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 ±7 (Moderate)**
Evidence: George: "cap-ex plans ... at their highest level in the 24-year history of our survey". Powell: consumption "appears to have slowed significantly ... to an estimated 1½ percent".
Summary: Strong sentiment and investment plans and fiscal tailwinds offset by a soft Q1 treated as transitory. Direction confirmation: above neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 ±8 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Williams: "Productivity growth has been very slow through the fourth quarter of last year." Mester: "productivity growth over the expansion has been so low."
Summary: Productivity is seen as weak so far with hope but no data of improvement. Direction confirmation: below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±6 (High)**
Evidence: Brainard: "financial conditions remain supportive of economic growth." Powell: "financial conditions remain accommodative despite a modest net tightening".
Summary: Loose-to-neutral conditions with a slight tightening from volatility and rates. Direction confirmation: below neutral because "supportive/accommodative/very easy" language dominates.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±8 (Low)**
Evidence: Brainard: "waning foreign appetite for the anticipated growing supply of U.S. Treasury securities". Kaplan: debt market participants "pare back on duration".
Summary: Supply and yield-level concerns only, no functioning problem. Direction confirmation: near the normal band, not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±6 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Brainard: "corporate bond spreads have widened somewhat ... they still remain quite compressed by historical standards."
Summary: Slight widening from a compressed level, so still below normal. Direction confirmation: below neutral because the stated level is compressed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 ±7 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Bullard: "equity valuations are high by traditional valuation metrics." Brainard: "valuations are stretched".
Summary: Valuations seen as elevated, with mixed views on how much concern the correction revealed. Direction confirmation: below neutral on the scale (elevated valuations).

**BANKING_STRESS: 27 ±6 (Low)**
Evidence: Brainard: "the banking system is well capitalized."
Summary: One clear statement of a healthy system. Direction confirmation: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 ±6 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Brainard: "depreciation of the exchange rate in real trade-weighted terms of around 10 percent". Kashkari: "The broad dollar has been depreciating since a peak at the start of 2017."
Summary: The dollar has weakened over the past year. Direction confirmation: below neutral because of depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 54.** Evidence: Harker: manufacturers "reporting rising prices"; Kashkari: "Import price inflation has been running at about 3½ percent." Offset by Kaplan's pricing-power limits.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 52.** Evidence: George: health-care services prices "should rise toward 2½ percent in 2018". Wages modest.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Evans: "inflation expectations remain too low"; Bullard: market-based expectations "still too low today".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 ±6 (High)**
Evidence: Williams: "my projection is four funds rate increases this year and another four next year." Bullard: "I think it's premature to bake in that much tightening."
Summary: Everyone backs a 25 bp hike and a gradual path with a steeper tilt in the SEP, while a sizable minority urge patience. Direction confirmation: above neutral, moderate hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 ±7 (High)**
Evidence: Evans: "My preferred policy decision for today is to maintain the funds rate"; Kashkari: "I would prefer alternative A." Vote: all Yes.
Summary: Unanimous action with a handful of expressed reservations about pace. Direction confirmation: low-to-moderate disagreement, no formal dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±8 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Brainard: "we should be particularly attentive to the risk of financial imbalances." Dudley: "a risk of a much bumpier ride a couple of years down the road".
Summary: Several members take up volatility, valuations and imbalances but it is not the central theme. Direction confirmation: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 ±7 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Brainard: "a well-telegraphed path to eliminating the forward guidance in paragraph 4". Bullard: "the only words that matter ... 'further gradual increases.'"
Summary: Gradual increases continue and the dovish-leaning sentence is nearing removal, with calls for stressing data dependence. Direction confirmation: above neutral, tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 (Moderate)**
Evidence: Powell: "There is, however, considerable uncertainty about just how strong that impetus is". Kashkari: "our gauge is broken."
Summary: Notable uncertainty about slack, the natural rate, fiscal effects and trade, but baselines are held with confidence. Direction confirmation: somewhat elevated.
