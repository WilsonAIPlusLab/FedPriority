# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - 1995-07-06 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). The input holds members' questions to staff (foreign exchange and the Mexican SDR transaction, the chart show, the long-run monetary ranges), the full economic go-around from the first day (Vice Chairman McDonough through Governor Blinder), the second-day debate on the Humphrey-Hawkins M2/M3 ranges, a Chairman's policy opening, a policy go-around, directive wording and the press release, and a vote read-out with three roll calls. Staff turns are removed, so some member questions appear without the staff answers. A few passages have gaps, for example the names that follow "including" in McDonough's Japan paragraph and a missing speaker in "told me that the market disappeared". I score members' views only.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1995. Members describe a slowdown after a very strong 1994 and the 1994 tightening. Real GDP is described as flat to contracting in Q2, with an inventory adjustment under way. Core CPI is running about 3.8% year to date and CPI about 3.5%, but inflation is seen as peaking. The funds rate is about 6%. I score what the text says, not what I recall of the period.

**3. Speakers.** All 12 Reserve Bank presidents' voices are present, plus governors Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, Yellen and Blinder, Chairman Greenspan and Vice Chairman McDonough. Main views:
- Weak or flat activity is widespread. Boehne, Forrestal and Stern say the risk of recession has risen; Yellen and Blinder say risks are lopsided to the downside; Phillips says "downside risks are stronger".
- Inflation is elevated (3.25-3.5% CPI forecasts at several banks) but "may be peaking cyclically" (Kelley, Blinder, Greenspan). Melzer, Hoenig and Broaddus remain hawkish-minded.
- Financial conditions are seen as supportive and as an "automatic stabilizer" (Yellen, Stern): the bond rally, stock market strength, easy credit, a sound banking system.
- Policy: the Chairman proposes -25bp with an asymmetric directive. Blinder, Yellen, Forrestal, Parry and McTeer would have preferred 50bp. Melzer, Minehan and Hoenig preferred no change. Hoenig votes no.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as above the price-stability objective (core CPI 3.8%, CPI about 3.5%; Melzer "3 percent just isn't good enough"), but easing and expected to have peaked. I score the current level, modestly to clearly above, not the forecast: about 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as slightly below potential and heading lower. Q2 GDP is contracting, there are references to "growth below trend", "excess capacity likely to develop", and "greater slack already in evidence in both product and labor markets" (Yellen). Blinder has the unemployment rate going from "a tad below" to "a bit above" the natural rate. Below potential, so the score is below 50, but only slightly: 43.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: National unemployment is 5.7% (Moskow) against a NAIRU of about 5.9-6.1 (Parry). Several districts report very low unemployment and skilled-worker shortages (Jordan, Melzer, Forrestal), while payrolls are flat and wages are in check. Near NAIRU, slightly loose: 47.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption was sluggish in Q2 (final sales fell; Blinder: retail sales up 0.4% over six months). Housing and autos are stabilizing. Business fixed investment and equipment orders are "holding up remarkably well". Net below trend: 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned briefly by Jordan ("productivity increases that are being sustained") and Phillips ("productivity improvements are holding up"). Slightly above trend, low evidence: 55.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as supportive: long rates are "down significantly", equities are "riding high", credit is "flowing nicely", credit standards are "exceptionally easy" (McTeer), and banks are strong. I score conditions, not the 3% real funds rate (policy). Below 50: 35.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The bond market appears only through yield moves. Broaddus remarks that a 15bp jump on a housing report was "remarkable", and the rally is called an automatic stabilizer. No dysfunction is described, so the score stays in the normal range: 40, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No spread levels are given. Credit availability is ample ("exceptionally easy credit standards", "credit is still flowing nicely, maybe... a little too strongly", "no credit crunch in sight"). Hints of rising default rates (Yellen, citing Lindsey) and a persistent premium on California state debt are minor. Level reading: below normal, so under 50: 38, Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: The stock market is "riding high" and its rally cushions the economy, but there is no valuation-concern language; Blinder fears a crack if the Fed does not ease. Default range, slightly rich: 46, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: "Banking system is in relatively good shape" (Forrestal), "banking system is strong" (Phillips), "health of the banking system" (Minehan), loan quality "very high" (Melzer), "favorable banking conditions" (Hoenig). Healthy means below 50: 27.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar "has declined significantly in real terms" (Melzer); "the depreciation of the dollar has left American goods very well priced" (Yellen). Lindsey and McDonough worry about the yen strengthening against the dollar if a bold move is made. Dollar weaker, so below 50: 30.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- Banking: higher means more stress. Evidence says the banks are healthy, so a low score of 27 matches.
- Dollar: higher means more strength. Evidence says depreciation, so below 50 matches.
- Credit spreads: higher means more stress. Evidence says easy credit, so below 50 matches.
- Financial conditions: higher means tighter. Evidence says supportive, so below 50 matches.
- Output gap: higher means more above potential. Evidence says below potential, so below 50 matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee moves toward easing. A 25bp cut is the "general consensus", with an asymmetric (easing) directive. Several members wanted 50bp, and Lindsey and Moskow expect 50-100bp in total. A minority (Melzer, Minehan, Hoenig) prefers no change, and Jordan is lukewarm ("cancel half the insurance"). Moderate dovish: about 31.
- Dissent: one formal policy dissent (Hoenig). Melzer and Minehan state they would have preferred no change but will not dissent. Blinder, Yellen, Forrestal, McTeer and Parry preferred 50bp. The vote read-out has three roll calls. By content, the first is M2 (Blinder and Yellen no, consistent with their stated preference for raising the range), the second is M3 (unanimous), and the third is the policy action (Hoenig no, consistent with his no-change stance). There is also a clear but civil disagreement about the Broaddus price-stability proposal and about whether to ease at all. Moderate: about 38.
- Financial stability emphasis: the bond rally, the stock market and the banking system are treated as a cushion, and market reaction to 25 versus 50bp (yen, bond market) is a major policy consideration. Overseas financial weakness (Japanese financial institutions) and Orange County are mentioned. The topic is limited but real: about 25.
- Forward guidance: the asymmetric directive and Greenspan's remark that the market will expect another move ("the Fed never moves only once", "we could add another 25 in August") signal an easing lean. Parry, Melzer and Kelley want it made clear that reversal is possible. The press release is minimal. Easing lean with data dependence: about 34.
- Uncertainty: Phillips says "the most difficult of my tenure... so many conflicting economic signals" and describes a fork in the road. Blinder gives a 30-60% range for recession and "highly vulnerable" economy. Several others stress downside risk and "great uncertainty" (Melzer). Greenspan says "some uncertainty" and that the answer will be clearer by the next meeting. Elevated but not extreme: about 54.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 (below potential, so under 50): satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35 (supportive and easy, so under 50): satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (easy credit, so under 50): satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS 27 (sound, so under 50): satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 30 (depreciation, so under 50): satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 (no dysfunction described, so not above 60): satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 47: a 4-point difference, fine.
- OUTPUT_GAP 43 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 42: fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: co-move, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35 vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: normal times, fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 vs sub-components (goods 52, housing 55, expectations 57): roughly consistent; services is N/A.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no member discusses services prices as such. Wage commentary is scored under labor.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Moskow, "the core CPI has increased at an average annual rate of 3.8 percent so far this year". Melzer, "CPI inflation accelerated to 3-1/2 percent". Kelley, "inflation may be peaking cyclically".
- Summary: inflation is modestly to clearly above objective but seen as peaking.
- Direction: above neutral because current CPI of 3.25-3.5% is above what members call price stability.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 43 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Parry, "the economy is flirting with recession"; Parry, "excess capacity that is likely to develop later this year"; Yellen, "greater slack already in evidence in both product and labor markets"; Hoenig, "a couple of quarters of growth below trend".
- Summary: a slowdown that has opened modest slack, with the gap expected to widen.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe below-trend growth and emerging slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Moskow, "unemployment rate... is 5 percent now compared... to the 5.7 percent rate nationally"; Jordan, "unemployment rates of under 3 percent" in many counties; Yellen, "unemployment in the vicinity of the NAIRU".
- Summary: national labor market is near NAIRU, with weak payrolls and regional tightness offsetting.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because national unemployment is a bit above or at NAIRU and job growth is stalling.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "consumption, which has been sluggish this quarter"; Blinder, "business fixed investment, which has been holding up remarkably well".
- Summary: weak consumer and inventory-driven slowdown, partially offset by solid equipment spending and a housing turn.
- Direction: below neutral because net spending is below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 55 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Jordan, "the productivity increases that are being sustained"; Phillips, "the productivity improvements are holding up".
- Summary: brief mentions of solid productivity.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because productivity is described as sustained.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "the equities market is riding high, and long-term interest rates are down significantly"; McTeer, "exceptionally easy credit standards"; Yellen, bond and stock rallies "functioning simply as automatic stabilizers".
- Summary: market conditions are a supportive cushion.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because rates fell, equities rose, and credit is available.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "15 basis point jump in the long bond rate... was remarkable"; Stern, bond market "automatic stabilizer".
- Summary: only yield moves discussed; no dysfunction.
- Direction: at the normal-functioning range because only yield changes are described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: McTeer, "exceptionally easy credit standards throughout the District"; Kelley, "credit is still flowing nicely, maybe... a little too strongly"; Parry, "persistent premium on California state debt".
- Summary: credit is ample; spread levels are not stated.
- Direction: below neutral because the level of availability is easy.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-7 [Low]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "the equities market is riding high"; Blinder, "I would expect the stock market to crack as well" if the Fed does not ease.
- Summary: rising stocks cited as support, with no explicit valuation concern.
- Direction: roughly neutral, slightly rich.

**BANKING_STRESS: 27 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Forrestal, "the banking system is in relatively good shape"; Phillips, "the banking system is strong. There is no credit crunch in sight."
- Summary: several members describe a healthy banking system.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "the value of the dollar has declined significantly in real terms"; Yellen, "depreciation of the dollar has left American goods very well priced".
- Summary: the dollar is weaker, and members worry a bold move might push the yen up.
- Direction: below neutral because the dollar has depreciated.

**Inflation sub-components**
- INFLATION_GOODS 52: mixed, with finished-goods price increases "have moderated" (Hoenig), steel price hikes rolled back (Jordan, Moskow), but paper and chemicals still rising and "sharp increases in import prices" (Melzer).
- INFLATION_SERVICES N/A: not discussed.
- INFLATION_HOUSING 55: Forrestal reports "rising rental rates and occupancies", with the rate of increase expected to slow; brief regional mention.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 57: Broaddus says "longest-term inflation expectations are super sensitive" and need "a firmer anchor"; Melzer says expectations declines are critical to credibility.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 31 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "move the funds rate downward by 25 basis points"; Blinder, "I strongly feel we should be easing today"; Greenspan, "A cut of 25 basis points with asymmetric language seems to be the general consensus."
- Summary: a majority favors easing now, some want more, with a hawkish minority (Melzer, Hoenig, Minehan) reluctant.
- Direction: dovish (below 50) because the Committee is easing with an easing-biased directive.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "that leaves me inclined to leave policy unchanged" (and votes no on the third roll call); Melzer, "I would not dissent on that even though that is not what I would favor"; Blinder, "50 basis points would be better".
- Summary: one formal policy dissent, several stated reservations in both directions, and two no votes on the M2 range.
- Direction: moderate disagreement; the reasoning differs by camp but the tone is cooperative.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "we need to cement in place the existing financial conditions that are already working to provide the critical cushion"; McDonough, a 50bp move "would be very likely to destabilize financial markets".
- Summary: financial conditions matter as a cushion and as a tactical constraint, but financial stability is not a focus.
- Direction: limited attention, a context rather than the topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 34 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I am marginally in favor of going asymmetric" and a 25bp move "create an expectation in the marketplace that indeed we will move again"; Parry, "we should be prepared to reverse course and raise rates".
- Summary: an asymmetric easing directive with an expected further move, qualified by data dependence.
- Direction: easing lean (below 50).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 54 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Phillips, "the most difficult of my tenure... so many conflicting economic signals"; Blinder, recession probability "in the 30 to 60 percent range"; Melzer, "great uncertainty with respect to the economic outlook".
- Summary: several members stress mixed data and downside risk, but most still hold a baseline of a pause.
- Direction: elevated, above the normal range.
