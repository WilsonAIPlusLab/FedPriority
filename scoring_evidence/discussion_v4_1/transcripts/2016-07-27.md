# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2016-07-27 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed), with speaker labels and a roll-call read-out at the end. It has three blocks:
- Day 1 morning: a long "Long-Run Framework for Monetary Policy Implementation" discussion (money-market operating framework, liquidity facilities, stigma, floor vs corridor). This is pure procedure/institutional design. It holds no assessment of conditions, so I ignored it except for a few asides.
- Day 1 afternoon: members' questions in the staff Q&A, a short financial-stability comment round (Rosengren, Evans), and the economic go-round.
- Day 2: the policy round on statement alternatives A/B/C, then the vote read-out.

The staff answers are absent, so the Q&A fragments are one-sided. I found no garbled passage that hides meaning.

**Era context.** Late July 2016, after the Brexit vote. The unemployment rate is 4.9 percent, core PCE is about 1.6 percent year over year, and growth is about 2 percent. The May payroll report was weak (11k) and June was strong (287k). The Committee has hiked once, in Dec 2015. I score what the text says.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish camp: Williams, Lacker, George (alt C, hike now; George dissents), and Mester ("strong case for raising the funds rate"). Rosengren supports B but wants a September default. Kaplan and Harker lean forward.
- Patient camp: Tarullo, Brainard, Evans, Kashkari, Lockhart, Powell, Dudley.
- The Chair wants a wait-and-see posture with September "live".
- Bullard and Fischer are in between: B today, a September move acceptable if the data cooperate.

**Dimension directions (before numbers).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below target but edging up. Core PCE is 1.6 percent. Brainard says it has been "below the target for 88 of the past 92 months". Williams and Powell see underlying inflation close to 2 percent. Mester notes prices-received doubling. This is slightly below 50, around 43.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Near potential. Staff gap is about zero. Lacker cites an LW gap that is large and positive. Williams expects growth above his 1.6 percent potential. Brainard and Evans see slack still left. "Near potential" falls at 48-52, so I use 48.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Near full employment. Mester says "we have met our maximum-employment goal". Williams sees "limited remaining slack". Brainard sees room to go on prime-age EPOP. Slightly above 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumption is "strong"/"robust" (Brainard, Lockhart). Business investment is weak and housing mixed. Net about at trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Below trend. Powell says "awful productivity growth", Bullard says "exceptionally low", and Williams says a slow productivity trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Accommodative and easier. Powell says "significant loosening", Mester says "more accommodative", and Williams says "more favorable". This must be below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield moves were mentioned, with no dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads narrowed (Rosengren, Kashkari) and corporate debt is oversubscribed (Evans). Level information is thin but points favorable. I score slightly below 50 with low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Prices are at record highs. Kaplan is worried about P/E near 19, Tarullo says "one might argue" overvalued, and Bullard says bubble risk is not heightened. Modestly above fair value.
- BANKING_STRESS: US banks look sound (Harker: strong loan growth, falling delinquencies). Concerns are CRE concentration (Rosengren) and European and Italian banks (Brainard, Powell, Kashkari). Net mildly below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: About 15 percent appreciation since mid-2014 still weighs on demand, but the dollar is roughly flat in 2016, with a "modest increase" post-Brexit (Brainard). Modest strength, above 50.
- Inflation sub-components: goods, services and housing were not discussed as such, so N/A. Expectations are discussed extensively: low market measures worry Brainard, Evans and Kashkari. Dudley is less worried, and George finds them consistent with goal. Mildly below target.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the outcome is hold (alt B), but a large share favor tightening soon. Three or four want to move now or are close to it, and the Chair keeps September live. This is a hold with a tightening bias, so lean hawkish, about 55.
- Dissent: 9-1, with George dissenting for a 25bp hike. Williams and Lacker (non-voters) also preferred C. Brainard, Tarullo and Kashkari argued the opposite way (Tarullo explicitly on risk-management). The camps are distinct, but there are no sharp exchanges and everyone accepted B. Moderate, about 42.
- Financial stability: a dedicated comment round (Rosengren CRE, Evans reach-for-yield), plus Kaplan, George, Tarullo, Mester and Williams weighing it in policy. Dudley's long discussion of financial conditions adds to it. The framework session also frames financial stability and liquidity backstops. Moderate-to-prominent, about 55.
- Forward guidance: statement B is gradual, data dependent, with near-term risks "diminished" and September open. Members debated and rejected a stronger signal. Mild tightening bias, about 57.
- Uncertainty: Brexit and Europe leave "murky" long-run effects, but most say near-term uncertainty subsided. Brainard says uncertainty about the cyclical position is "relatively large". Fischer says "we know very little" about Brexit's course. Somewhat elevated, about 40.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 48: "near potential" rule, passes.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 35: "accommodative"/"easier", so below 50, passes.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 44: direction only (narrowed) plus mild favorable-level hints. Below 50 on the side of the move, passes.
- BANKING_STRESS 45: "healthy/sound" for US banks gives below 50. Europe is a tail concern, not a US system problem. Passes.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57: appreciation and "stronger dollar" language, so above 50, passes.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, no dysfunction discussed.

**Polarity checks.** Higher inflation pressure means more price pressure. My evidence says below target but rising, so slightly below 50, which matches. Higher labor tightness means a tighter labor market. The evidence says near full employment, so about 50, which matches. Higher FC means tighter, and the evidence says looser, so below 50. Higher credit spreads means more stress, and the evidence says spreads narrowed in a favorable market, so below 50. Higher dollar means stronger dollar, and the evidence is mild appreciation, so above 50. Higher equity score means more depressed-valuation concern, while the overvaluation concern (Kaplan) points below 50, which matches.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 48 and LABOR 52 differ by 4, fine.
- OUTPUT_GAP 48 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50, fine.
- FC 35 and CREDIT_SPREADS 44 both below 50, consistent.
- FC with TREASURY: Treasury is N/A, normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 43 and EXPECTATIONS 40, consistent.

**N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: only yield levels were discussed.
- INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed these categories (house prices were discussed as an activity indicator, not shelter inflation).

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Brainard: "core inflation has now been below the target for 88 of the past 92 months." Williams: "we remain on course to return to 2 percent inflation."
- Summary: Core is 1.6 percent, rising gradually toward target, with the camp split on how near 2 percent it already is.
- Direction: below neutral because inflation is still short of 2 percent, although the gap is narrow and closing.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 +-5 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Lockhart: "near full employment but with some absorbable slack remaining." Lacker: an estimated output gap "large and positive".
- Summary: Participants roughly see the economy near potential, with views spanning a little slack (Brainard, Evans) to a small overshoot (Williams, Lacker).
- Direction: just below neutral, within the near-potential band.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 +-5 [High].**
- Evidence: Mester: "we have met our maximum-employment goal." Brainard: the prime-age employment-to-population ratio "suggest there is still room to go."
- Summary: The unemployment rate is 4.9 percent, quits and hires are high, and wages are edging up, with a minority seeing remaining slack.
- Direction: at or slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-5 [High].**
- Evidence: Chair Yellen: "solid growth in consumer outlays compensating for softness in most other categories of spending." Powell: "Consumption alone is not likely to be a strong enough engine."
- Summary: Strong consumers net against weak business investment and flat housing, giving roughly 2 percent growth.
- Direction: at trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 27 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Powell: "the awful productivity growth we have had." Bullard: "productivity growth remains exceptionally low."
- Summary: Many participants take low productivity as a defining feature of the current regime.
- Direction: well below historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-5 [High].**
- Evidence: Powell: "2016 has actually been a time of significant loosening in financial conditions." Mester: "financial conditions are now more accommodative than they were at the time of our June meeting."
- Summary: Low yields, record equity prices, easier lending standards and a flat dollar point to accommodative conditions, partly offset by a stronger dollar earlier.
- Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No participant described market dysfunction (only yield levels). Kaplan noted no flight-to-quality episode after Brexit in the money-market context, which is not Treasury dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 44 +-8 [Low].**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "credit spreads have narrowed." Evans: "high-quality debt is routinely oversubscribed."
- Summary: Direction is clearly narrowing, and favorable-level cues (oversubscription, easy mortgage standards) put it modestly below normal, offset by the SLOOS CRE tightening (Brainard).
- Direction: below neutral, with low confidence because no member states a spread level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Kaplan: "market P/E is getting close to 19 times earnings in a period when corporate earnings are actually declining." Williams: "equity prices have risen to all-time highs."
- Summary: Valuation concern is real but confined to a few voices, with Bullard seeing no heightened bubble risk.
- Direction: below neutral (modest overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 45 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Harker: "strong loan growth and high profitability as well as a continued decline in delinquencies." Rosengren: "financial-stability risks associated with CRE are elevated."
- Summary: US banks are broadly sound, with CRE concentration worries and European bank fragility as pockets of concern.
- Direction: slightly below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Powell: "The 15 percent or so appreciation of the dollar since mid-2014 has been weighing on demand." Brainard: "a modest increase in the exchange rate" after the vote.
- Summary: Past appreciation is a headwind, but the dollar has been flat to modestly firmer in 2016 and Bullard downplays it.
- Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A** (not discussed as categories).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Evans: expectations "continue to bounce along at extremely low levels." Dudley: "I'm a little bit less worried now than I was earlier."
- Summary: Market and survey measures are below pre-crisis norms, with some improvement and one dissenting voice (Lacker) worrying about upside unanchoring.
- Direction: below target-anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Mester: "there's a strong case for raising the funds rate." Brainard: "I would need to see some combination of greater momentum in economic activity and progress on inflation."
- Summary: The outcome is a hold, but a substantial share advocate a hike now or in September, and the Chair keeps September live.
- Direction: above neutral (lean hawkish), tempered by the patient camp.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-7 [High].**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President George No." Tarullo: "I would prefer to wait for more convincing evidence that inflation will get to, and then remain around, the 2 percent target."
- Summary: One formal dissent, two non-voters favoring the dissenting alternative, and a split on assessments, but a civil debate with all accepting B.
- Direction: above the one-dissent baseline, below distinct-camps territory.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "I remain concerned that the financial-stability risks associated with CRE are elevated." Kaplan: "I believe excesses are building, although not to the point at which I'm alarmed."
- Summary: A dedicated comment round, repeated policy-round references and Dudley's long financial-conditions discussion, short of dominating the economic assessment.
- Direction: moderate-to-prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Chair Yellen: "I would prefer to be more in a wait-and-see posture, in which every meeting, particularly September, is 'live'." Powell: "the path of tightening is likely to be gradual and dependent on the momentum of the economy."
- Summary: Data-dependent, with September open and "diminished near-term risks" language, but a strong signal was explicitly rejected.
- Direction: mildly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Brainard: "When uncertainty about the cyclical position of the economy is relatively large, as it appears to be at present." Fischer: "We know very little about what's actually going to happen."
- Summary: Brexit and European banks leave murky medium-term risks, but most members say near-term uncertainty has subsided and risks have diminished.
- Direction: somewhat elevated.
