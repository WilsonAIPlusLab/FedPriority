# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript discussion, meeting 2001-01-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion, two-day meeting (Jan 30-31). Staff turns are removed, so many Q&A passages show only members' questions. The first third (about 1,400 lines) is a closed joint session on the SOMA portfolio study (how the Fed should hold assets if Treasury debt disappears), the foreign-currency and domestic-operations authorizations, and some Desk Q&A. It contains no assessment of conditions and is ignored for scoring except incidental remarks. The scored content is the economic go-around (Parry through Poole, Day 1 afternoon), Greenspan's Day 2 opening assessment, and the policy go-around (all members support 50bp and a weakness-weighted balance-of-risks statement). The vote read-out is 10 Yes, 0 No. Some turns are fragmentary because of staff removal, but the member content is clear.

**2. Era context.** Late January 2001, one month after a surprise 50bp inter-meeting cut on Jan 3. The expansion has slowed abruptly: manufacturing contraction, an inventory correction, collapsing consumer confidence, an energy problem. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan frames the slowdown as an inventory and capital-stock adjustment in a just-in-time, high-tech economy. He sees a V-shaped recovery as most likely, flags an energy shock and elevated Baa-Aaa spreads, and recommends 50bp with a weakness-weighted risk statement. Regional reports are gloomy (Moskow, Jordan, McTeer, Guynn, Stern), moderate (Santomero, Minehan, Poole, Broaddus, Hoenig) or relatively upbeat (McDonough, Parry on employment). Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson and Kelley give national assessments. Hoenig and Jordan say policy has been too tight. Jordan, Meyer and Broaddus urge care about expectations of further easing.

**4. Directional statements (Direction Rule).**
- Inflation: participants see pressure as below the midpoint. Evidence: "inflation apparently well contained" (Broaddus), "inflation expectations remain well anchored... inflation could ease some" (Hoenig), "core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent" (Parry), "inflation is not a problem" (Minehan), "wage pressures seem to be easing" (Moskow). Offsetting: energy cost pressure (Greenspan), New England prices "rising more rapidly", input price pressures in the Second District. Net: modestly below the midpoint, not deflationary.
- Output gap: participants see the economy as below its (higher) potential growth path, with a sharp slowdown. Evidence: "sub-trend growth" (Kelley), "below-trend growth... unwinding some of the excesses of output relative to potential" (Meyer), potential growth "above 4 percent" and GDP slowing 2-1/2 points below it (McTeer). The level is only mildly below potential because labor markets are still tight and the economy had been above potential. Net: slightly below 50. The bright-line rule is satisfied (<50, "below trend").
- Labor: participants see the market as still tight but loosening. Evidence: "labor markets remain tight... still remarkably tight" (Broaddus), "unemployment still very low" (Kelley), District unemployment 3.8% up from 3.3% (Moskow), "full employment recession". Unemployment under 4% implies above 55. Hoenig and Jordan report easing and layoffs. Net: about 60.
- Consumption and investment: below trend. Evidence: durables growth fell to minus 5%, business fixed investment "at or near zero" (Kelley), "consumer spending... lackluster" (Stern), industrial production falling, Chicago PMI 40.2 (Moskow). Housing and some construction hold up. Net: well below trend, not collapsing (about 30).
- Productivity: strongly positive. Evidence: "continuing leap in structural productivity growth" (Greenspan), "persistence of the ongoing productivity improvement" (McDonough), "productivity will hold up" (Ferguson).
- Financial conditions: somewhat tight. Evidence: "tighter credit terms... high cost of financing for all but the most highly rated firms" (Minehan), loan standards "continue to be tight" and commercial paper "anything but healthy" (Ferguson), Baa-Aaa spreads wider than a year ago (Greenspan). Offsetting: markets rebounded after the Jan 3 move, "risk spreads have fallen and equity values have risen" (Parry), refinancing booming, easing already priced in (Poole). Net: somewhat above 50, bright-line satisfied (">50", tight standards).
- Credit spreads: level elevated. Greenspan wants Baa-Aaa spreads to "come down to where they were a year or so ago". Parry says risk spreads have fallen since the cut (a direction, not a level). Level reading: elevated, so >50.
- Equity valuation: valuation concern. Evidence: P/E "still well over 20, matching the previous high of 1992" (Kelley), P/E "not by any means depressed" (Greenspan), "excesses in equity valuations" (Meyer). Valuations are somewhat rich after the correction, not distressed. Below 50 on the stress scale.
- Banking stress: low stress. Evidence: "banking sector... relatively strong" (Ferguson), "banks are still healthy" (Kelley), "banking system appears to be strong" (Poole), "banking sector remains a source of strength" (Minehan). Offsetting: some tightening of standards, rising delinquencies, "skittishness in the banking industry" (Greenspan). Net: below 50 (about 35).
- Treasury stress: no current discussion. The 1998 off-the-run reference is historical, and the portfolio discussion concerns Treasury supply, not dysfunction. N/A.
- Dollar: only "projected depreciation of the dollar" in the staff baseline (Meyer) and Parry's unchanged-dollar assumption. Not a member assessment of dollar moves. N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.** Credit spreads: higher = more stress; wide spreads (Greenspan's wish for narrowing) mean >50, which matches 62. Banking: healthy banks mean low stress, which matches 35. Equity: rich valuation with concern means lower on this scale, which matches 38. Labor: tight and low unemployment mean higher, which matches 60.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: every member supports a 50bp cut (100bp in four weeks) and a risks-toward-weakness statement. Several urge front-loading, and Hoenig and Jordan say policy is too tight. A few (Jordan, Meyer, Broaddus, Minehan) urge caution on further easing. Strongly dovish, but not emergency or QE.
- Dissent: unanimous, 0 dissents. Differences are limited to recession probability, whether to say more easing is coming, and the size of the future path. Jordan and Meyer worry about market expectations of further cuts.
- Financial stability attention: limited to moderate. Credit standards, the commercial paper market, spreads, banks and equity valuations are raised by a handful of members, mostly as context for the real-economy slowdown. The portfolio-study portion concerns asset-side operations, not stability.
- Forward guidance: the retained balance-of-risks-toward-weakness language signals an easing bias. Meyer and Jordan push back on pre-committing to further cuts.
- Uncertainty: many say forecasts are unreliable ("we don't have the capacity actually to forecast a recession", Greenspan; "so little confidence in forecasting", Stern; Guynn; Gramlich "grain of salt"). Divergent scenarios (V-shaped recovery vs. recession) and elevated recession probabilities, but no "uncharted territory" language.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 44 (<50, "below trend") OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 (>50, tight standards) OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 62 (>50, elevated level stated) OK. BANKING_STRESS 35 (<50, "healthy"/"strong") OK. DOLLAR N/A. TREASURY N/A.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs LABOR 60: a 16-point gap. Explanation: labor is still tight in level terms (unemployment under 4%) while growth has dropped below potential and a gap is only starting to open.
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs CONSUMPTION 30: a 14-point gap, within tolerance. The sharp spending fall is a flow, while the gap is a level that was previously positive.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 62: co-move, OK.
- FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A, OK.
- Inflation parent 42 vs expectations 47: roughly consistent (expectations anchored, current pressure a bit below).

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member comment on current Treasury market functioning. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only a staff-forecast reference. INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: no member-level assessment of these categories beyond scattered energy and local-rent anecdotes.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "With inflation apparently well contained, at least for now" (Broaddus); "inflation expectations remain well anchored... inflation could ease some" (Hoenig). Summary: pressure is contained and expected to ease, with energy costs a partial offset. Direction: below neutral because members describe inflation as contained and wage pressures easing.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "two quarters of sub-trend growth already registered" (Kelley); "below-trend growth... unwinding some of the excesses of output relative to potential" (Meyer). Summary: growth has dropped well below trend, so slack is opening from a position that was above potential. Direction: below neutral because of below-trend language, though labor markets keep the gap small.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +-6 [High].** Evidence: "Labor markets overall remain tight; although not as tight as they were, they're still remarkably tight" (Broaddus); District unemployment "3.8 percent in December; it had been 3.3 percent in October" (Moskow). Summary: unemployment is still under 4% but loosening and layoffs are rising. Direction: above neutral because unemployment is under 4%; the score is trimmed for the loosening trend.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 30 +-7 [High].** Evidence: durables growth "fell to minus 5 percent... business fixed investment... at or near zero" (Kelley); "consumer spending was lackluster" and "manufacturing sector is clearly in difficulty" (Stern). Summary: consumption and capex have dropped sharply, with housing and some construction holding. Direction: below neutral because spending is contracting versus trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "the continuing leap in structural productivity growth" (Greenspan); "belief in the persistence of the ongoing productivity improvement" (McDonough). Summary: productivity growth is seen as structurally high. Direction: above neutral because gains are described as a leap.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: "tighter credit terms... high cost of financing for all but the most highly rated firms" (Minehan); "commercial paper market... anything but healthy... conditions and standards... continue to be tight" (Ferguson). Summary: credit is tight for non-top-tier borrowers, partly relieved by the cut and a market rebound. Direction: above neutral on tight standards and strained CP market.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a current market condition.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "if Baa-Aaa spreads were to come down to where they were a year or so ago" (Greenspan); "risk spreads have fallen" since the cut (Parry). Summary: spreads are above where they were a year ago and credit is tight outside top tiers, though they have eased since January 3. Direction: above neutral on the stated elevated level. The Parry narrowing is a direction and does not set the side.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: P/E "still well over 20, matching the previous high of 1992" (Kelley); "price-earnings ratios, which are not by any means depressed" (Greenspan). Summary: after the decline, valuations are still seen as rich. Direction: below neutral on this stress scale because of residual overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: "the banking sector still seems to be relatively strong" (Ferguson); "the banks are still healthy and open for business" (Kelley). Summary: the banking system is sound with minor deterioration in credit quality. Direction: below neutral because banks are described as strong and healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Only a staff-forecast reference.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** No member-level assessment.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 [Moderate, value only].** Evidence: "inflation expectations remain well anchored" (Hoenig). Summary: anchored at roughly target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 +-5 [High].** Evidence: "Another 1/2 point cut at this meeting would go far toward nipping in the bud our recession" (McTeer); "I think we can and should move decisively and quickly" (Guynn). Summary: unanimous 50bp easing, front-loading, and the view that policy is still too tight, with mild caution from Jordan, Meyer and Broaddus. Direction: dovish because members advocate substantial easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 13 +-4 [High].** Evidence: roll call 10 Yes, 0 No; "I support your recommendation" from nearly every member. Summary: unanimity, with differences only about the future path and recession risk. Direction: low disagreement because there are zero dissents and shared diagnosis.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: CP market and loan standards (Ferguson); Baa-Aaa spreads and "skittishness in the banking industry" (Greenspan). Summary: a handful of members raise credit and market conditions as context, but the focus is the real economy. Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 24 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: "retaining the balance of risks statement to the down side" (Greenspan); "it is far too early to suggest that the balance of risks is anything other than to the down side" (Ferguson). Summary: an easing bias is retained, though Jordan and Meyer caution against promising more. Direction: dovish forward signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "we don't have the capacity actually to forecast a recession" (Greenspan); "I have so little confidence in forecasting how the economy will perform" (Stern). Summary: many stress forecast unreliability and a wide range of outcomes (V-shape vs. recession), without Knightian language. Direction: elevated uncertainty.
