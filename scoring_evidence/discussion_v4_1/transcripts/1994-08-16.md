# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring, v4.1 + V4.1.1 (label: v411), meeting 1994-08-16, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion. It has the round-table (economic go-around), the Chairman's policy summary and recommendation, a policy go-around, a statement-wording exchange, and a vote read-out at the end (read to the last line: 12 Yes, no No votes, no dissent). Staff turns are removed, so the Desk and Greenbook Q&A is one-sided. It has a policy vote and a stance discussion, so it is NOT special-topic (V4.1.1 rule 1). Page markers and a few blank-name passages (Jordan's retailer names) were ignored; none changed meaning.

**Era context.** Mid-1994, during a tightening cycle, with the funds rate at about 4.25 percent after 125bp of increases since February. Members talk about core inflation near 3 percent, an expansion near capacity, and a 50bp move proposed together with a discount-rate increase. I scored from the text only.

**Speakers.** Broaddus, Forrestal, Parry, Hoenig, Minehan, Boehne, Melzer, McTeer, Stern, Lindsey, Conrad, Jordan, Phillips, Blinder, Kelley, McDonough, LaWare, Yellen, and the Chairman (whose long summary is a Committee member's own view and is scored).

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as above the level they consider noninflationary, and building. Core CPI is about 3.1 percent (Phillips, Blinder). Firms can pass through price increases for the first time in a long while (Forrestal, Minehan, Phillips). Melzer says "risks are clearly on the side of rising inflation". Greenspan: "3 percent is not noninflationary". Counterweights: low unit labor costs, retail competition, and the overall CPI being flat (Kelley, Phillips, Boehne, McTeer). Modestly to clearly above, with pressure rising. I score 64.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Mixed on the level, uniformly "at or near capacity". "Growth ... above potential" (Phillips, Hoenig) and "at or very, very near capacity ... momentum to carry us past" (Kelley) point above. Yellen says output "has not yet passed potential, although we are approaching it", and Blinder says "not quite fully employed, surely close". The level is at or just above potential, so I score 53. Bright line: "above potential" language exists for growth, and I stay above 50; Yellen's "not yet passed" is the only below-potential reading, and it is a near-potential reading.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "Full employment" (McTeer's economists), "little slack" (Conrad), widespread shortages of unskilled and semi-skilled labor, sign-up bonuses (Forrestal, Jordan), but little wage acceleration. Unemployment is about 6 percent (Blinder's NAIRU debate is 5.6 to 6.3). The rubric's UE anchor would put this in the high 30s to 40s, while the participants' language is near-NAIRU with shortages. I take a compromise slightly above neutral, 52, with wide UNC. Coherence with OUTPUT_GAP (53) is fine.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong overall. Strong investment, orders and profits (Yellen, Greenspan, Phillips), strong retail rebound (Forrestal), but housing and consumer durables are moderating (Broaddus, Boehne, Stern), and Lindsey expects the consumer to spend less. Above trend but decelerating. I score 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Phillips (the "supposed new trajectory" in the 1990s that keeps wages out of prices), Kelley (optimistic, "a little egg on its face" from the latest numbers), and Greenspan (finds a second-quarter productivity decline "utterly noncredible"). Roughly at or modestly above trend, with measurement doubt. I score 54.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Credit is readily available and not tight. Banks have eased standards, competition for loans is "very aggressive" (Hoenig, McTeer, Minehan), business and consumer loans are up 17 and 23 percent (Melzer), money and credit conditions are "quite consistent with rising demand" (Melzer), and the bubble has been "defused" (Greenspan). Offsetting: higher long rates, a steeper yield curve, and a market that was fragile earlier in the year (Phillips). Somewhat loose. I score 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Phillips speaks to it ("bond market has withstood considerable volatility ... haven't had any major accidents") and Greenspan ("moving of securities from weaker hands to stronger hands"). Volatility, but orderly. Normal functioning, I score 38 with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No participant uses spread, yield or premium wording about credit. Availability and lending language exists (standards eased, aggressive competition, banks "making some loans that should not be made", strong C&I growth). Under V4.1.1 rule 3 this may support a score on availability only: ample availability points below 50. This score rests on availability. I score 35, Low confidence, UNC +-12.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Phillips says the stock market is "showing a great deal of resilience ... off the January highs, but not ... fragility" and that earnings support it. No valuation view; this is a price-state remark. Per rule 2, 45 to 55 with Low confidence. I score 49.
- BANKING_STRESS: No stress. Banks are expanding loans aggressively with loan-to-deposit ratios rising (Hoenig, with some liquidity interest at the discount window), and some bankers worry they are making loans they should not (McTeer, Minehan). Healthy banks, with some risk-taking and liquidity concerns. Below 50: 36.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen cites "the decline in the value of the dollar", and Forrestal lists "a weak dollar". Depreciation is below 50: 35 (bright line met).

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: autos up 3 to 4 percent with incentives cut by 40 percent (Conrad), steel up 5 percent (Jordan), materials and paper pressures. Retail competition limits some pass-through. I score 60. Services: not discussed, N/A. Housing: rents rising and tightening in the Southeast (Forrestal), shortages and rising house prices in Memphis area (Melzer). I score 58. Expectations: Lindsey sees "a lot of expectations of inflation in the household sector"; Melzer says the Fed has "a ways to go" on credibility; the Fed is "at the curve or maybe ahead of the curve" (Phillips). I score 58.

**Tier 3 check.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee unanimously approves a 50bp increase, with all members saying further tightening is "clearly called for" (Greenspan) or "necessary" (Minehan). Several wanted 25 or fewer (Minehan: a slower pace, Blinder: growth should be higher, Yellen: somewhat less than the Greenbook assumes), but everyone supports 50. The forward lean is tempered: symmetric directive, "oars out of the water", and a possible easing next (Phillips, McDonough). Large current action with a moderated forward lean gives 72.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Unanimous 12-0 on the vote. Disagreement in the economic discussion is moderate (Blinder on NAIRU and growth, Jordan's skepticism of inflation from growth and his probing of the Chairman's logic, Kelley's "not sure how much imminent danger"). Minehan and Melzer differ on statement nuance. I score 16.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Brief to limited: bank lending behavior (Hoenig, McTeer, Minehan, Jordan), market resilience (Phillips), and Greenspan's concern about the market reaction to 25 versus 50bp and the "bubble defused". It is a context topic. I score 22.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The agreed statement says the actions are "expected to be sufficient at least for a time", with a symmetric directive and an explicit refusal to tie hands. This is a pause signal after a large hike, with a chance that the next move could be easing (Phillips, McDonough, Yellen). Mildly below neutral: 44.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Standard-to-somewhat-elevated caveats: Yellen "I confess uncertainty" on potential, Stern uncertain about the capacity-price relationship, Phillips "I don't think we know", Greenspan "who knows" and doubts about GDP data. Mostly confident conviction on direction. I score 36.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (above 50, near-potential: ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 (accommodative/easy availability, below 50: ok). CREDIT_SPREADS 35 (availability easy, below 50: ok). BANKING_STRESS 36 (sound/healthy-expanding, below 50: ok). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 (depreciation, below 50: ok). TREASURY_STRESS 38 (orderly, not above 55: ok).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 52 vs CONSUMPTION 60: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 and CREDIT_SPREADS 35 co-move (both loose). FC and TREASURY 38: normal. INFLATION_PRESSURE 64 vs sub-components 60, 58, 58: roughly consistent; the parent is slightly higher because the core measure and breadth of the pass-through discussion, which are not captured in a single sub-component.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no participant discusses services prices. All other dimensions are scored.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 64 +-6 Moderate-to-High (reported High).** Evidence: Melzer: "the risks are clearly on the side of rising inflation"; Phillips: "core CPI for the second quarter actually edged up to 3.1 percent ... a 3 percent level ... still does constitute a form of tax". Summary: Inflation is clearly above what participants call noninflationary and pressures are building, with some offsets. Above neutral because participants stress pass-through and price risks on the upside.

**OUTPUT_GAP 53 +-5 High.** Evidence: Phillips: "The growth in the last four quarters has been above potential"; Yellen: "output has not yet passed potential, although we are approaching it". Summary: Economy at or just above potential, with momentum to go higher. Above neutral modestly.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 52 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Forrestal: "experiencing labor shortages, not only for skilled people but for semi-skilled and unskilled"; Conrad: "There appears to be little slack". Summary: Near full employment with shortages in lower-skilled jobs, but the unemployment level near 6 percent and little wage response keep it near neutral. Slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60 +-6 High.** Evidence: Yellen: "strength of new orders for durable goods and the strong profit projections suggest continuing strength in investment"; LaWare: "the current brisk rate of growth". Summary: Strong spending and investment, with housing and consumer durables moderating. Above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 54 +-8 Low-to-Moderate (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley: "I'm still optimistic that productivity is going to continue to increase at a significant secular rate"; Greenspan: "I find utterly noncredible the probability that, in the real world, productivity declined in the second quarter." Summary: Seen at or modestly above trend, with data doubts. At or just above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig: "both the larger and smaller banks ... have eased their credit standards"; Melzer: "money and credit conditions that are quite consistent with rising demand and inflation". Summary: Credit is plentiful and conditions are somewhat loose, despite higher long rates. Below neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS 38 +-10 Low.** Evidence: Phillips: "The bond market has withstood considerable volatility ... we haven't had any major accidents". Summary: Volatile but orderly. Normal functioning, below the stress threshold.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 35 +-12 Low.** Evidence (availability only, no spread wording): McTeer: "competition for loans is getting very aggressive and the large banks are so aggressive on pricing and credit standards"; Hoenig: "banks want to make loans". Summary: Ample credit availability and aggressive loan pricing. Below neutral; rests on availability.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 49 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Phillips: "The stock market ... is showing a great deal of resilience. We clearly are off the January highs". Summary: Price-state remark with no valuation view. At neutral, slightly below because of the January decline offset by resilience.

**BANKING_STRESS 36 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig: "very strong loan demand across the board ... loan-to-deposit ratios have increased"; McTeer: bankers "wonder if they aren't making some loans that should not be made". Summary: Expanding, competitive, healthy banks with mild liquidity and underwriting concerns. Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen: "the decline in the value of the dollar"; Forrestal: "a weak dollar". Summary: The dollar is described as having declined. Below neutral (depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS 60, INFLATION_HOUSING 58, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 58, INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Evidence: Conrad: "price pressures have most assuredly increased in the motor vehicles area"; Jordan: steel "put through a 5 percent price increase ... and it has held"; Forrestal: "rental markets have tightened ... and rents are rising"; Lindsey: "a lot of expectations of inflation in the household sector".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 72 +-6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan: "another upward notch in rates is clearly called for"; Broaddus: "I enthusiastically support your proposal". Summary: A unanimous 50bp hike plus a discount-rate increase, with a tempered forward stance. Hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 16 +-5 High.** Evidence: Vote read-out: 12 Yes, no dissent; Jordan: "I came in on the fence between 'B' and 'D.'". Summary: Unanimous vote, with differences confined to size, nuance and the economic read. Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 22 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan: "the bubble for all practical purposes had been defused"; Phillips: "Currency markets also have withstood significant volatility". Summary: Market reaction and bank lending behavior are context, not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 44 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan's proposed statement: "these actions are expected to be sufficient at least for a time"; McDonough: "the next policy move should be an easing of policy". Summary: A pause signal after the hike with no commitment. Slightly below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 36 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen: "With respect to how close the economy is to potential, I confess uncertainty here"; Phillips: "I just don't think we know." Summary: Normal-to-somewhat elevated caveats on the NAIRU, the data and the next move, with strong conviction on direction.
