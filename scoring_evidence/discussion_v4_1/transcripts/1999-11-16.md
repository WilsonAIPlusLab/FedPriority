# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting 1999-11-16 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: members' questions during staff Q&A, the economic go-around, the Chairman's policy recommendation (25bp increase plus symmetric directive), a go-around on the recommendation, a debate on the draft press statement, and a later discussion of directive/disclosure procedure (Working Group memo, Chairman's authority between meetings). The roll-call read-out shows all ten listed voters "Yes". Staff turns are removed, so some staff questions have no visible answers; the text is readable, with no garbling that affects scoring.

**Era context.** Late-1999 expansion: strong growth, very low unemployment, an acceleration in measured productivity, rising energy prices, Y2K year-end concerns, and a Committee that had already tightened (June, August) and adopted an asymmetric directive at the prior meeting. This backdrop is noted only; scores come from what members say.

**Speakers.** Jordan, Minehan, McTeer, Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Hoenig, Guynn, Stern, McDonough, Poole, Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, Boehne, Greenspan. Nearly all report very tight labor markets and strong growth, with some slowing in housing and autos. Most see upside inflation risk, while a minority (McTeer, Parry, Poole, Boehne, Stern) stress benign actual inflation and uncertainty.

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly to somewhat above comfortable and building. Measured core inflation is still about 2 percent, and McTeer, Poole, Parry and Boehne note benign results. Against that, many cite the core PPI, the NAPM prices-paid and delivery indexes, health care costs, energy, import prices and wage anecdotes. Meyer: "overall inflation has increased significantly this year"; Broaddus: "somewhat behind the inflation curve" (vigorously rejected by McDonough and Greenspan). Level is modestly above, with rising direction. Score 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Ferguson: the economy "is still at this stage exceeding that limit [the speed limit]". Gramlich: demand "a little too much demand pressure even for our new economy". Greenspan: "growth of demand exceeds the growth of supply". Bright line requires >50. Potential has been revised up, so the excess is moderate. Score 62.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Unemployment is about 4.1-4.2 percent (McTeer 4.1 percent, Ferguson "ticked down"); Minehan reports 2.9 percent in New England; "very, very tight" (Moskow); Honda will not build in Ohio for lack of labor. UE below 4.5 percent and "severe shortage" anecdotes put this in the very-tight band. Wage pressure is not generalized (Poole, Guynn, McTeer), which restrains it from going higher. Score 74.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong but moderating at the margin. Third-quarter GDP 4.8 percent, "overall spending growth is currently still very strong" (Broaddus), strong equipment spending (Ferguson). Housing, autos and refinancing are softening (McDonough, Guynn, Greenspan). Above trend overall. Score 63.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Clearly and significantly above trend; the central theme. Greenspan cites 7.6 percent annualized for the quarter and 4.4 percent from the income side; Minehan: "higher productivity"; McTeer: "stronger now than it was early this summer". Score 80.

**Financial dimensions.**

- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Members do not call conditions tight or loose. Greenspan describes a rise in long-term real rates, a stock market flat over six months, and "firming of conditions more generally in U.S. financial markets". Minehan notes rates "backed off a bit" and the stock market "remains positive". McDonough reports tightening bank credit standards. Mixed, mildly firmer than before. No bright-line trigger. Score 53, wide band.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Treasury yields appear only as level/real-rate commentary; no market functioning discussion by members (Y2K liquidity refers to the year-end generally).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Greenspan mentions "the spread on BBB-rated obligations versus Treasuries has widened" - direction only, no level. Per the v4.1 rule, direction-only goes 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Score 53.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed as price moves and wealth effects (stock market "leveling off", "new up leg" per Kelley, wealth effect per Greenspan), with no valuation-concern language. Default band. Score 48, Low-Moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks described as sound: Guynn "credit quality generally seems to be improving", McDonough "continued improvement in delinquency rates ... much virtue in the banking sector", with small pockets (agriculture, health care, energy independents violating covenants). Below 50. Score 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Current dollar roughly stable ("the dollar hasn't gone anywhere", Greenspan), with members expecting depreciation (Minehan "more likely to depreciate than not", Broaddus, Kelley "lower dollar", Gramlich "likely fall in the dollar"). Direction expectation is weakening, so below 50. Score 40.

**Inflation sub-components.** Goods: rising crude/intermediate PPI, import prices, energy, NAPM prices, but non-oil import prices are still down year over year (McTeer), so 62. Services: health care premiums and wages are discussed, but this is a cost story without a clear services price reading; N/A. Housing: Minehan notes home prices up 8 percent in New England and McDonough notes double-digit rent increases in NYC, scored 57 (price evidence is stronger than for shelter inflation). Expectations: split. Jordan says expectations of rising inflation "do not have any dissenters" among contacts; Boehne and Poole say expectations are well anchored and that low expectations reflect trust in the Fed. Net 52.

**Tier 3.**

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The decision is a 25bp increase endorsed by essentially everyone speaking, which is the current action for a moderate hawkish stance. Forward lean is reduced by the symmetric directive, which was widely preferred partly for Y2K reasons; several (Moskow, Broaddus, Minehan, Parry) say they feel asymmetric. Parry would have held but kept a tightening bias. Hoenig, Broaddus and Guynn lean to earlier action. Score 64.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero dissents in the vote (all "Yes"). Reservations: Parry (a non-voter) prefers to hold; Stern says the hike may be "unnecessary"; Boehne finds the move "close"; Poole says the move does not matter much; several wanted asymmetry; sustained wording disputes over the press statement. Views on risk differ (hawkish camp vs. McTeer/Parry/Stern/Boehne), but the prescription converges. Score 24.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Some attention to Y2K year-end liquidity (Guynn's director probe, McDonough, Boehne on calming markets), credit quality, the wealth effect and real long rates (Greenspan), and the current account deficit. This is context, not focus; the meeting is overwhelmingly about growth, labor and inflation. Score 28.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Symmetric directive, with the Chairman saying a December move is not credible and the statement language being revised to avoid implying that tightening is complete or that more is coming. Members' private lean is upward (Parry, Moskow), but the official signal is neutral, data-dependent. Score 52.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Greenspan: "we really do not know how this system works ... The old models just are not working", "a lot of uncertainties"; Stern: "we are a bit at sea", "my uncertainty ... has increased"; Ferguson: "great deal of uncertainty about where policy is going to go next year"; Kelley on the productivity parameters; Minehan nonetheless says the direction of the next move is "less uncertain than ever". Elevated, with no uncertainty about direction in the hawkish camp. Score 60.

**Polarity checks.** OUTPUT_GAP: higher = more above potential; evidence is above, so >50, matches. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter; evidence is very tight, matches. BANKING_STRESS: healthy language gives a low score, matches. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: expected depreciation gives <50, matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: direction-only widening gives 52-55, Low confidence, matches the v4.1 rule.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP >50 (62), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS has no accommodative/tight wording (53), CREDIT_SPREADS direction-only (53), BANKING_STRESS <50 (32), DOLLAR_STRENGTH <50 (40), TREASURY_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs LABOR 74: a 12-point gap, within 15; labor is the most extreme because potential was revised up by productivity, which mutes the output gap. OUTPUT_GAP 62 vs CONSUMPTION 63: aligned. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 53: aligned. FC vs TREASURY: N/A. INFLATION 58 vs sub-components (goods 62, housing 57, expectations 52): consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member discusses market functioning. INFLATION_SERVICES: no clear services-price statement.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 58 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Meyer, "overall inflation has increased significantly this year"; McTeer, "deflators ... have been stable in the 1 to 1½ percent range". Center of gravity: benign measured core inflation but widely perceived building pressure. Inflation pressure is above neutral because most members expect core inflation to creep up and list multiple price pressure points.

**OUTPUT_GAP 62 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Ferguson, "the economy is still at this stage exceeding that limit at a time when labor markets are already stretched thin"; Gramlich, "a little too much demand pressure even for our new economy". Output is above potential because demand growth outpaces the revised supply growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 74 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "Our labor markets are still very, very tight"; Minehan, unemployment 2.9 percent in New England, "Labor markets are very tight". Labor is far above neutral given unemployment near 4 percent and shortage anecdotes.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 63 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Ferguson, "overall third-quarter GDP barreled ahead at an estimated annual rate of 4.8 percent"; Broaddus, "Overall spending growth is currently still very strong". Spending is above trend with interest-sensitive softening.

**PRODUCTIVITY 80 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "something profoundly important has happened here" (4.4 percent income-side, 7.6 percent quarterly extrapolation); Minehan, "Productivity increases are real." Productivity is well above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 53 +-9 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the firming of conditions more generally in U.S. financial markets"; Minehan, "interest rates have backed off a bit, the stock market remains positive". Mixed, around neutral to slightly firmer.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS 53 +-6 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the spread on BBB-rated obligations versus Treasuries has widened". Direction only, with no level information, so a mild move above 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Kelley, "the market seems to be moving into a new up leg"; Greenspan, "the stock market is unchanged over the last six months". Price/wealth-effect discussion without valuation concern; treated as roughly neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS 32 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn, "credit quality generally seems to be improving"; McDonough, "continued improvement in delinquency rates. There is much virtue in the banking sector". Banks are healthy with isolated pockets.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "the dollar is more likely to depreciate than not"; Broaddus, "greater-than-expected depreciation of the dollar". Expected weakening against a currently stable dollar.

**Sub-components.** GOODS 62 (PPI, NAPM, import and energy prices; Guynn, "import prices are increasing"); SERVICES N/A; HOUSING 57 (Minehan home prices; McDonough NYC rents); EXPECTATIONS 52 (split between "well anchored" per Boehne and "moving up" per Jordan's contacts).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 64 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the benefits of moving outweigh those of standing pat ... a 25 basis point increase today"; Moskow, "I think the risks of not moving are much higher." Moderate hawkish: tightening endorsed by nearly all, offset by the symmetric directive.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 24 +-6 (High).** Evidence: roll call, all voters "Yes"; Stern, "there is some chance that this action may be unnecessary"; Parry, "I would prefer to leave the funds rate unchanged". Broad agreement with some reservations and a wording dispute.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 28 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn, directors probed "potential liquidity problems at financial institutions" at year-end; McDonough, "markets are always somewhat illiquid" at year-end. Limited attention, context only.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "the directive ... is symmetrical with regard to the outlook for policy over the near term"; Meyer, wants to avoid "turning a tightening into what appears to be an easing". Neutral formal signal with a latent tightening tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 60 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we really do not know how this system works. It's clearly new. The old models just are not working"; Stern, "we are a bit at sea here". Elevated model/structural uncertainty with high conviction on direction among hawks.
