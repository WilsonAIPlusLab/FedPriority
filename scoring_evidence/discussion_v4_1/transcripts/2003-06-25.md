# FOMC member-discussion scoring (v4.1 rubric + V4.1.1 clarifications), meeting 2003-06-25 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Document type: not special-topic. Day 1 is a long session on policy at the zero bound (deflation, nontraditional tools, money market funds), but day 2 holds a full economic go-around, a policy round and a roll call (12 voters). The Day 1 session is not used for the condition dimensions, apart from what members say about bank and market conditions.
- Policy lean: dovish. The Chairman proposes a 25 bp cut with an easing-bias statement ("we may not have completed our easing"). Most members support it; Parry prefers 50 bp. Several others (Broaddus, Santomero, Gramlich, Kohn) say 50 bp is defensible.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Parry, No, wanted 50 bp, the more dovish side). Reservations from Broaddus, Santomero, Guynn (statement wording) and Gramlich (a 50 bp message-of-fear argument). Disagreement is about size and wording, not about the economic assessment.
- Economy: members describe weak realized activity (jobless recovery, excess capacity, persistent output gap) with a hoped-for pickup (housing, fiscal stimulus). Consumers and housing hold up; business capex and hiring are weak.
- Inflation: very low (core PCE about 1%); the concern is disinflation and, for some, deflation risk. No participant sees upward price pressure.
- Financial conditions: described as accommodative and supportive (equities up, rates down, risk spreads narrowed, dollar weaker). Banks are healthy with margin compression. Treasury stress is N/A (yield moves only, no functioning issue).
- Guidance: easing bias and "easier for longer" emphasis, with an explicit wish to keep the 75 bp floor notion off the table. Uncertainty is elevated ("uncharted waters", unusual uncertainty about slack, "don't understand what is holding the economy back").
- Limits: staff answers to member questions are missing, and some Q&A fragments are cut mid-exchange. This does not change the member-view scoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 +-6 (High).** Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Evidence: Parry, "core PCE prices expected to increase about 1 percent both this year and next"; Broaddus, core inflation could fall to "pretty close to incipient deflation"; Stern, "difficult time constructing a credible story that has any appreciable acceleration of inflation". Center of gravity: low inflation with downside risk, no upward pressure. Direction: below neutral because inflation is far below the desired range and the debate is about disinflation. Not in the deflation-spiral band because members call spiral risk "very low".

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-6 (High).** Persistent slack is expected. Evidence: Minehan, "it will take some time to close the output gap"; Pianalto, "the Greenbook does characterize the economy as operating far below potential"; Parry, "substantial excess capacity would remain... through the end of next year". Moskow, "resource gaps will not close much until we move into next year". Direction: below potential (bright line satisfied), though forecasts of above-potential growth next year keep it from going lower.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 32 +-6 (High).** Weak labor market, unemployment about 6%. Evidence: Minehan, "unemployment remaining around 6 percent"; Moskow, "the lack of hiring is very troublesome"; Ferguson, "unemployment rate hangs up above the NAIRU through next year". Direction: below neutral. Coherent with the output gap (within 15 points).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-7 (High).** Consumers and housing are solid; business investment is weak, so the net is slightly below trend. Evidence: Stern, "Homebuilding, home sales, and home improvement spending remain the foundation of growth"; Guynn, "unable to find any significant sentiment on the part of businesses to invest except to replace depreciated equipment"; Bies, "what is missing is obviously business capital expenditures". Direction: below neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 66 +-8 (Moderate).** Strong productivity gains are cited repeatedly. Evidence: Broaddus, "Strong productivity growth still appears to be restraining job growth"; Guynn, cost-cutting investments cut labor costs "as much as 30 or 35 percent... the outlook for continued productivity gains is indeed real"; Kohn, "rapid productivity growth is boosting profits and income and economic potential". Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6 (High).** Accommodative and supportive. Evidence: Bernanke, "policy is accommodative and the financial markets are supportive"; Kohn, "Easier financial conditions are not just occurring in the United States"; Broaddus, "overall financial conditions are really quite conducive to increased business investment". Direction: below 50 (bright line satisfied). Ferguson's doubt is whether conditions are "indicative of growth", not whether they are loose.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Members discuss falling long-term yields and policy expectations (Kohn, McTeer, Ferguson "search for yield") but never any Treasury market functioning, liquidity or auction problem. Yield moves alone are not dysfunction, so there is nothing to score.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 45 +-10 (Low).** Direction-only wording plus availability. Evidence: McTeer, "a narrowing of risk spreads, reflecting less perceived risks"; Ferguson, "risk spreads are down"; Guynn, "Business credit terms have eased". Per V4.1.1 this is direction of narrowing (45-55 band on the side of the move), supported by availability language (Pianalto: "current price and availability of credit" are not impediments). No level is stated. Direction: at or slightly below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-8 (Low).** Price move only, no valuation view. Evidence: Ferguson, "Equity prices are up"; Guynn, "stock market indexes up significantly"; Broaddus, "the stock market rally". Per V4.1.1, price move with no valuation view scores 45-55 on the side of the move. Direction: roughly neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-7 (Moderate).** Healthy with margin pressure. Evidence: Olson, "Asset quality has improved... coverage of loan-loss reserves is appropriate by any measure", "record profits" while "interest rate margin declined"; Pianalto, bankers' "loan-to-deposit ratios are quite low and their liquidity ratios are quite high". Bies raises floors on loan rates and credit-risk coverage as a forward concern. Direction: below 50 (bright line satisfied).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-6 (Moderate).** Dollar depreciation. Evidence: Kohn, "lower interest rates, increase equity prices, and depreciate the dollar"; McTeer, "a considerable weakening of the dollar, especially against the euro"; Moskow, "a weaker dollar had helped boost orders". Direction: below 50 (bright line satisfied). Stated as a decline helpful to growth, not a sharp collapse.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36.** Guynn, "continued softness in goods prices"; Pianalto, "declining prices for the products they are producing"; Hoenig, goods prices "basically flat" with "a bit of increase in the cost of goods".

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 50.** Only Guynn mentions "increases in prices for services" offsetting goods softness. Thin evidence, near neutral.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 40.** Rents are soft in places: Stewart, Manhattan "nonstabilized rents off about 20 percent from their 2001 peaks"; Minehan, "declining rents"; Parry, price appreciation "moderated" in some areas. Home purchase markets are strong, which offsets slightly.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Anchored but with downside concern. Santomero, "Long-run inflation expectations have remained stable"; Gramlich, measures "perhaps off a bit but not careening toward deflation"; Parry, "decline in expected inflation".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 +-5 (High).** The majority favors an easing cut. Evidence: Greenspan, "the reduction should be 25 basis points... we may not have completed our easing"; Parry and Broaddus prefer 50 bp; Ferguson, "reestablish an accommodative policy". Moderate dovish band; no member argues for hold or tightening, and the main debate is 25 versus 50 bp.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-6 (High).** One formal dissent (Parry, No, preferred 50 bp) in a 12-member vote. Reservations: Broaddus ("a tough call", prefers 50 but supports 25), Santomero ("I would have preferred a bit more decisive action"), Guynn (wording of the disinflation risk language), Gramlich (doubts the message-of-fear argument against 50). Disagreement is on size and communication, not on the economics. Fits "1 dissent or 2-3 reservations".

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +-10 (Moderate).** Day 1 spends much of a long session on money market fund viability, bank capital and margins, spreads and the effects on intermediaries of near-zero rates (Poole, Santomero, Bies, Olson, Greenspan on private credit spreads). Day 2 returns to financial conditions as a policy channel (Kohn, Bernanke, Ferguson). The attention is real but is framed as policy mechanics rather than a stability threat.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-7 (Moderate).** Easing bias and "easier for longer". Evidence: Greenspan, "convey the notion that we may not have completed our easing"; Bernanke, "willingness to keep policy easy so long as there is a risk of further disinflation"; Kohn, "another serious conversation in August about whether we need to move further". Some members want to signal temporariness (Guynn) or eventual return to neutrality (Minehan), but the signal is mild easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 (Moderate).** Many members stress unusual uncertainty. Evidence: Bernanke, "more than the usual degree of uncertainty about the amount of slack in the economy"; Gramlich, "the uncharted waters uncertainties"; Kohn, "I really don't understand entirely what is holding the economy back"; Parry, "we face considerable uncertainty about the future strength of the economy". Greenspan on nontraditional policy: "we don't know how the market will respond". Not the extreme band because most members hold a central forecast of recovery.

Cross-checks: OUTPUT_GAP (32) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32) move together; CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42) is within 15 of the gap. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) and CREDIT_SPREADS (45) are both at or below neutral, which is coherent given the weak evidence on spreads. Sub-components average about 42, consistent with parent INFLATION_PRESSURE of 24 only loosely, because the parent reflects the strong "well below desired range" language while the sub-component evidence is thin.
