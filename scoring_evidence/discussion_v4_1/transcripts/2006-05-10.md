# FOMC member discussion, 2006-05-10 (v4.1 + V4.1.1)

## Part 1: Conclusions

- Not special-topic: full economic go-around, policy go-around and a roll-call vote on a 25bp funds-rate increase to about 5 percent.
- Policy lean: moderately hawkish. A 25bp increase, a statement saying "some further policy firming may yet be needed," and wide concern about inflation and inflation expectations. Two non-voters (Poole, Fisher) wanted 50bp, while Yellen, Olson, Hoenig and Pianalto lean toward a pause soon.
- Dissent: zero formal dissents (11 yes votes, no no's). Real differences over 25 vs 50bp, pause timing and the "inflation expectations remain contained" wording. Intensity is low to moderate.
- Economy: participants see growth strong now, expected to moderate toward potential, housing cooling, resource utilization high (little slack, unemployment 4.7 percent). Inflation is at or above the top of the comfort range (core PCE 2.4 percent over six months) and inflation expectations are rising.
- Financial conditions read loose or neutral: narrow spreads, abundant liquidity, easy loan standards, a weaker dollar, higher long rates. Bies adds a mortgage-lending concern.
- Treasury stress is scored low, because rising long rates and TIPS moves are not dysfunction (Bernanke mentions only TIPS liquidity effects).
- Uncertainty is elevated but mixed (Geithner, Minehan, Stern, Kroszner, Bernanke stress it; Warsh and Poole are confident). Forward guidance is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias that stays data dependent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 66 +-6 (High).** Participants see inflation above the comfortable range and rising.
- Evidence: Lacker, "the inflation picture has become substantially more troubling," core PCE "up at a rate of 2.5 percent over the past three months." Stone, "inflation running at the top of the range that I consider consistent with price stability." Bernanke, "we are at the upper level of what might plausibly be called the region of price stability."
- Summary: Core near or above 2 percent, broadening cost pressures, risks to the upside, but not accelerating sharply (Kohn, Yellen are more sanguine).
- Direction: above the 50 midpoint, clearly above the comfort zone but not extreme.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-5 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Geithner, "little or no slack is left in resource utilization." Moskow, "Resource utilization measures currently are only modestly on the tight side." Yellen, "in the vicinity of full employment and not noticeably beyond it."
- Summary: Slightly above potential now, with growth expected to average around or slightly below potential.
- Direction: modestly above potential, so above 50 under the bright line.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 56 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Warsh, "low unemployment rate, which is still at 4.7 percent." Stone, "labor markets tight." Moskow, "skilled workers are in short supply."
- Summary: Unemployment near the natural rate, skilled-labor shortages widespread, wage pressure mixed (ECI muted).
- Direction: slightly tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Warsh, "consumption was up 5½ percent in the first quarter. Business capital expenditures were up about 16½ percent." Fisher, "shift ... to business-investment-driven growth."
- Summary: Strong recent spending and capex, partly offset by an expected moderation (housing, energy) that members describe as still prospective.
- Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 53 +-6 (Low).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Data on both productivity and labor compensation are largely reassuring." Minehan, "productivity growth has been trending lower, it remains quite healthy." Geithner, "unlikely that productivity growth is going to accelerate significantly."
- Summary: Productivity is seen as healthy and near-to-slightly-above trend, discussed briefly.
- Direction: around neutral, slightly above.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Fisher, "There's a lot of liquidity in the system." Stern, survey of loan officers "indicating further easing of standards and terms for C&I loans." Minehan, "longer end of the yield curve has turned up, tightening financial conditions somewhat, though ... credit spreads remain narrow."
- Summary: Easy credit and liquidity, partly offset by higher long-term rates. Overall supportive.
- Direction: below 50 (loose/supportive), as the bright line requires.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "nominal ten-year Treasury rate is up about 75 basis points." Bernanke, TIPS markets "fairly concentrated, and therefore, liquidity issues sometimes play a role in these high-frequency movements."
- Summary: Yield and TIPS-breakeven moves are discussed with no description of dysfunction. Scored in the normal range.
- Direction: at or just below neutral (orderly market).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Minehan, "credit spreads remain narrow." Poole, "Relatively low risk spreads, making it easy for firms to raise capital." Geithner, "hard to find evidence in credit spreads ... of a substantial growth slowdown."
- Summary: Spreads are stated to be narrow or low.
- Direction: below 50 (compressed), per the level rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Warsh, "profitability-driven growth in the S&P and in the Dow as opposed to some sort of P/E multiple expansion." Poole lists "Strong stock market" among inflation warning signs.
- Summary: A mild valuation view (earnings-driven, not multiple expansion), so near fair value with no bubble language.
- Direction: roughly neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Fisher, "the credits, as reported by banks, look to be in very, very good shape." Bies, "growing ingenuity in the mortgage sector is making me more nervous ... interest rate risk embedded in their negative amortization loans."
- Summary: Banks sound overall, with a pocket of concern in nontraditional mortgages (Bies) and foreclosure notes (Hoenig).
- Direction: below 50 (healthy with minor concerns).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Yellen, "the sharp drop in the dollar over the same period." Kohn, "The decline in the dollar is a bit worrisome." Minehan, "the dollar falling."
- Summary: Members consistently describe a weakening dollar, a modest to notable decline.
- Direction: below 50 (depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 65.** Stone, "Prices for industrial goods are rising"; Poole and Guynn on commodity and construction-material surges (copper "four times"); energy at $3 gasoline.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 62.** Lacker, service-sector current price measure "higher than at any time since 2003," expected six-month service inflation 3.2 percent "higher than at any point since our survey began."

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 60.** Bernanke, "owners' equivalent rent ... is going to be an upside risk for us" because rents begin to rise after house prices; house-price appreciation itself is slowing.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62.** Geithner, "a material rise in long-term inflation expectations"; Lacker, "inflation expectations have deteriorated markedly" but "contained"; Kroszner, no sign of unanchoring.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 +-5 (High).**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "I recommend a 25 basis point increase today." Statement, "some further policy firming may yet be needed to address inflation risks." Poole and Fisher favored 50bp; Yellen, "I strongly favor a pause in our campaign" after this move.
- Summary: A hike with majority concern about inflation and a tightening bias, tempered by reluctance to commit beyond data.
- Direction: hawkish (above 50), moderate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Roll call: 11 yes, no dissents. Poole and Fisher (non-voters) advocated 50bp; Lacker said "any statement today that moves the market's expected policy path down is going to cause inflation expectations to move up," while Yellen and Olson lean toward a pause.
- Summary: Unanimous vote and wide agreement on 25bp, with some difference in emphasis (pause vs more, wording) but little disagreement on the economics.
- Direction: low to moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Bies on negative-amortization and ARM risks; Moskow, directors concerned about liquidity "bordering on speculative"; Stern on hedge funds and private equity moving into credit; extensive talk of long rates, term premiums and TIPS.
- Summary: Several members address financial-market and lending conditions meaningfully, but the meeting centers on inflation and growth.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Statement, "some further policy firming may yet be needed" with "extent and timing ... depend importantly on the evolution of the economic outlook." Bernanke, "it is important that we not signal that there is a definite pause." Yellen, B "does leave open the door."
- Summary: Retains a firming bias but explicitly emphasizes flexibility, an extent-and-timing hedge, and possible pauses.
- Direction: mild-to-moderate tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Geithner, "the limits of our knowledge ... matter more than they have in the recent past." Kroszner, "we are in somewhat uncharted territory." Minehan, "I am more uncertain than I have been about where policy should be." Warsh and Poole sound confident.
- Summary: Several members stress elevated uncertainty (housing, expectations, resource utilization), others do not.
- Direction: elevated.

Cross-checks: OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR (56) and CONSUMPTION (60) move together within 15 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) and CREDIT_SPREADS (28) are both loose. INFLATION_PRESSURE (66) is consistent with the sub-components (60-65). Bright lines: output gap above 50, financial conditions below 50, spreads below 50, banking below 50, dollar below 50, Treasury stress in the normal range.
