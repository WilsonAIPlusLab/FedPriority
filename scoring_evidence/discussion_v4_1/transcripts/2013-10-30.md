# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2013-10-30

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: economic go-around (Plosser through Powell), a policy-planning go-around on tapering, forward guidance, market interventions, IOER cuts and communication (two sessions, Oct 29 and 30), then the current-policy go-around on Alternative B wording, a straw vote on the statement's first sentence, and the roll call. Staff turns are removed, so the staff Q&A at the start shows only members' questions with fragmentary exchanges. The Chairman's summary of the economic go-round is member-side content and is used as a corroborating summary. Extraction looks clean; I found no garbled passages that change meaning.

**2. Era context.** Fall 2013: open-ended QE3 at $85bn/month, a 6.5 percent unemployment threshold, the October government shutdown and debt-ceiling standoff, the September non-taper decision, and a go-round on how to wind down purchases. I score what the participants say, not what I know of the period.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Chair Bernanke: growth about 2 percent, labor market improving slowly, inflation below target, financial conditions eased since September, fiscal drag large. Wants to keep accommodation and communicate three principles.
- Dovish or patient camp: Kocherlakota (wants a lower threshold, Alt A, more accommodation), Evans, Rosengren (no change until 3 percent GDP and 200k jobs are in the data), Bullard (continue a "successful program"), Lockhart, Yellen, Dudley, Williams.
- Taper-sooner camp: Plosser ("time has come to end our LSAP program"), Lacker ("economic conditions warrant reducing the pace"), George (dissents), Pianalto (favored slowing in September), Fisher ("I don't like the program"), Powell (leaning toward winding down by end of 2014).
- Tarullo and Stein: hold now, planning for March, worry about costs.

**4. Dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Kocherlakota: "running at about 1.2 percent". Bullard: "running well below our target". Rosengren: "inflation well below our target is not acceptable". Many expect a slow rise. Score about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below potential. Yellen calls the labor market "unacceptably weak" and growth "stuck around 2 percent". Chair: "slack is playing some role". Rosengren: "five years of a very sluggish recovery". Bright line (<50) satisfied. About 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: slack remains. Unemployment is 7.2 percent, participation is depressed, and payroll gains are slowing. Some pockets of skilled-labor shortage are reported (Kocherlakota, Lacker, George), but the national picture is slack. About 32.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: below trend and sluggish. Consumer sentiment is "surprisingly low", capex is delayed, housing is slowing, autos are strong, and growth is "muddling along" at 2 percent. About 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed (Lacker, Bullard, Yellen). Growth is 0.7 percent over three years, "little evidence of a sustained rebound", with a structural-damage scenario. Below trend, about 35.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: eased significantly since September, partly retracing the summer tightening. Dudley: "financial conditions have eased significantly". Williams: little evidence of tightening since spring, aggregate measures "looser". Chair: "financial conditions have eased". Supportive, so below 50 (bright line). About 38. Evans and Powell note the earlier tightening, so I stay above the very loose range.
- TREASURY_STRESS: yields and term premium are discussed (the 100bp summer rise, swaption vol down), but no dysfunction. Stein says "market function is not a problem". Normal functioning, Low confidence, about 42.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: favorable level. Williams cites "shrinking junk bond yield spreads" and credit available for smaller firms. Fisher cites narrowed triple-C spreads. Dudley says banks are "tripping over themselves" to lend. Level below normal, so below 50. About 33. A CMBS spread rise is noted but called non-binding.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: "record highs" (Williams), Fisher's concern that policy aids the S&P and Nasdaq, Powell and Stein on reach for yield. Some valuation unease but mostly price commentary. About 40, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: George describes compressed margins, a search for yield, thin loss coverage after reserve releases, and CRE competition. Dudley and Chair describe credit availability and banks as willing lenders. Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50. About 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Williams "a depreciating dollar", Dudley "dollar is a bit softer", Bullard describes the stronger dollar after June and the reverse after September. Recent direction is weaker, so below 50. About 40.
- Inflation sub-components: goods is lightly discussed (energy and gasoline down, crop prices lower, little pricing power) at about 38. Services and housing-rent inflation are not discussed as such (house prices are not shelter inflation), so N/A. Expectations are described as "stable" (Chair, George) with market measures near 1½ to 1¾ percent (Bullard), at about 44.

**5. Polarity check.** For the sub-components the evidence is low price pressure, and the scores are below 50, so the polarity matches. For FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS the evidence is easing and supportive, so below 50 is correct. For CREDIT_SPREADS, favorable credit means low stress, so below 50 is correct.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds QE at $85bn with no taper (9-1 vote). The majority position is hold, with a taper "in March at the earliest", and the biggest unresolved debate is how to communicate the exit. Several members want an immediate taper (Lacker, George, Plosser, Pianalto), several want more accommodation (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Bullard). A QE-era hold with the exit still in the future leans dovish, about 35.
- Dissent: one formal hawkish dissent (George). Lacker, Plosser, Fisher and Pianalto voiced taper-now preferences without a vote. Dovish members pushed for a lower threshold. The first sentence of the statement was split in a 6-5 straw vote. The disagreement extends to costs versus efficacy and to the outlook, but it is mostly about tactics and the tone is collegial. About 42.
- Financial stability: a recurring but not dominant topic. George on banks and search for yield, Stein on the carry trade and QE costs, Powell on suppressed volatility and a financial-stability knockout, Dudley and Chair on financial conditions and the signaling channel, and the Chair's survey on costs including financial stability. Moderate, about 48.
- Forward guidance: the existing threshold-based guidance is kept ("highly accommodative for a considerable period"). Many want to strengthen it or lower the threshold, and none favors tightening. Taper is deferred to at least March. Strongly easing, about 12.
- Uncertainty: Yellen opens with "the fog is especially dense", describes four scenarios and "great uncertainty", and repeats "overly optimistic predictions". Rosengren and Lockhart cite persistent forecast errors, and data are distorted by the shutdown. Elevated to high, about 60.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 (<50, "slack"). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (<50, supportive). CREDIT_SPREADS 33 (<50, low level). BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50, sound with minor concerns). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 40 (<50, depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction, in the normal range). All satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs LABOR 32: 4 points apart. OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs CONSUMPTION 40: 4 points apart. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 33: co-move (both loose). FC vs TREASURY: no crisis, Treasury near normal, consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 vs sub-components (goods 38, expectations 44): the sub-components sit somewhat above the parent because they are lightly discussed and the parent is anchored on the explicit 1.2 percent PCE reading, so no change.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed as price categories by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kocherlakota, "It is running at about 1.2 percent"; Bullard, "I still see inflation running well below our target of 2 percent." Summary: broadly shared view of low inflation with a slow expected rise. Inflation pressure is below neutral because members repeatedly say it is under target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "the current state of the labor market is unacceptably weak"; Dudley, "we still seem stuck around 2 percent". Summary: sustained slack and sub-potential growth. The gap is below neutral because growth is trend-like while labor and inflation goals are unmet.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 32 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Pianalto, "the unemployment rate dipped another tenth, to 7.2 percent"; Chair, "wage growth has also been slow, suggesting that slack is playing some role." Summary: improving but still slack, with scattered skilled-labor shortages (Kocherlakota, Lacker). Labor is below neutral because unemployment is above 7 percent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Lockhart, "Business investment appetite and intentions remain soft"; Rosengren, consumer sentiment "surprisingly low". Summary: muddling along near 2 percent with delayed capex, housing slowing and strong autos. Below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Lacker, "averaged 0.7 percent per year for the past three years"; Bullard, "little evidence of a sustained rebound in labor productivity growth". Summary: weak productivity is a central debate (structural damage). Below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions have eased significantly over the past few months"; Williams, measures are "little changed or even looser than earlier this year". Summary: supportive and eased since September, though the summer tightening was only partly reversed. Below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Stein, "I think we have learned that market function is not a problem"; Rosengren on the rate increase of "over 100 basis points". Summary: large yield moves but orderly markets. Normal functioning, so below 50.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Williams, "shrinking junk bond yield spreads"; Fisher, spreads "narrowed on triple-C credits". Summary: favorable credit with ready availability. Level below normal, so below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Williams, "record highs in the stock market"; Fisher, the policy "is aiding and abetting the rich and the quick" while he cites the S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Summary: stocks are high, with some valuation unease but little explicit valuation commentary. Slightly rich.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: George, "net interest margins compress and the search for yield continues"; Dudley, banks "tripping over themselves in their willingness to do business". Summary: a sound system with margin and risk-taking concerns. Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Williams, "a depreciating dollar"; Dudley, "the dollar is a bit softer". Summary: recent mild depreciation. Below neutral by the direction rule.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Evidence: Bullard, "consumer energy prices will follow" crude down; Dudley, "very little price pressure in terms of inputs".

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed as a category.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** House prices were mentioned, but not rents or shelter inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Evidence: Chair, "Inflation expectations are stable"; Bullard, market-based expectations "a bit less than 1½ percent" over two years.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 35 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Rosengren, "no change in monetary policy until we see economic data consistent with achieving full employment"; Lacker, "I still believe economic conditions warrant reducing the pace of our asset purchases." Summary: most hold QE and defer a taper to March or later, with a vocal minority wanting to end it sooner. Lean dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-7 (High).** Evidence: George, "I do not support alternative B"; the 6-5 straw vote on the first sentence. Summary: one formal dissent plus several unvoted taper-now preferences and real disagreement about costs and efficacy, but a collegial tone. Moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell, "the idea that monetary policy can ignore that and leave it to macroprudential tools just is not credible to me"; George, banks' "search-for-yield incentives". Summary: several members engage meaningfully, notably on the carry trade, reach for yield and the planned cost survey, but it is not the center of the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "crossing the threshold does not imply an imminent tightening"; Yellen, policy must "continue to provide accommodation for a long time". Summary: threshold guidance retained, with debate about strengthening it and no tightening signal. Strong easing bias, tempered by an eventual taper.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "It's challenging to make monetary policy in the fog, and at the moment that fog is especially dense"; Lockhart, "increased uncertainty and greater caution". Summary: many cite the shutdown's distortion of data, repeated forecast misses and competing structural scenarios. Elevated.
