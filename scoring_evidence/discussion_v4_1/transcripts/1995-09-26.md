# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 1995-09-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It contains the Chair's and members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic round-table, the policy round (Chairman's proposal then a go-around), and a separate go-around on a pending bill (the "Mack bill" on price stability). A vote read-out is appended: 11 names, all "Yes". Staff turns are removed. Two things are out of the scoring scope: the long Mack-bill and Humphrey-Hawkins institutional discussion (no view on conditions, so it does not move the economic dimensions) and procedural remarks. The text is clean apart from page markers and one unreadable gap (the Daiwa remark by McDonough, "somewhat less than ..."), which does not affect any score.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 1995, after a mid-year inventory correction. Members talk about a hoped-for soft landing. Unemployment is about 5.6 percent. CPI inflation is around 3 percent. The federal funds rate is 5-3/4 percent after an earlier easing. Fiscal restraint and a possible default ("train wreck") are on the horizon. I score what members say, not what I know happened later.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Forrestal: the economy is good but he senses hidden softness. He thinks potential is higher than assumed and wants an immediate 25 bp cut.
- Parry: growth is picking up and inflation is not heading down. He is reluctant to cut.
- Broaddus: the economy is solid, risks have shifted to the upside, and wage pressure is rising in the Carolinas. He is "a little nervous" about running near full capacity.
- Boehne: moderate growth, with business confidence "broader than deep."
- Hoenig: the economy is strong, growth is slightly below trend, and he is less optimistic than the Greenbook on inflation.
- Minehan: mixed bag, risks balanced to slightly down.
- Moskow: a Seventh District pickup, tight labor markets, intensifying wage pressure at the low end, and receding input-price pressure.
- McTeer: moderate pace, the recession probability has receded, and he sees room for a further ratchet down in inflation.
- Melzer: inflation will not fall and market expectations are flat. He views policy as "somewhat restrictive" and appropriate.
- Stern: strong, tight labor markets, no bias in the risks.
- McDonough: risks slanted to the downside, with weakening consumer demand in the Second District.
- Jordan: tight labor markets and skepticism that fiscal restraint requires an offset.
- Yellen: trend growth, prices well contained, and a real funds rate that is too high ("termites in the basement").
- Lindsey: a negative fiscal impulse, and a real funds rate that is "clearly" too high.
- Kelley: upside risks, and the Committee is in a good position to wait.
- Phillips: demand held up, markets improved, inflation reasonably well controlled, and slow growth near term.
- Blinder: on a soft-landing path, a small gap opening up, and a real funds rate above equilibrium. He does not argue for a move today.
- Greenspan: inventory drag is still present, downside risks arrive in 1996, the real funds rate is probably above equilibrium, and it is unwise to move today because of the budget debate. He proposes "B" symmetric and says any next move should be made with the Committee.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as contained and well behaved ("well contained," Yellen; "well behaved," Forrestal; "reasonably well controlled," Phillips). Several also say it is stuck near 3 percent CPI and not falling (Parry, Hoenig, Melzer), and some say material and building prices are rising. That is modestly above a price-stability definition, but not pressured or accelerating. I put it just above the midpoint, about 52. Polarity: higher means more inflation pressure, and the evidence of "modestly above objective, contained" matches a score just above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth of 2 to 2-1/2 percent is described as "slightly below trend" (Hoenig), below potential in the Greenbook (Forrestal, Blinder: "a small gap has opened up"), at "trend" (Yellen), with inventory drag still present (Greenspan). Against that, Broaddus says "close to full capacity in many industries," and Parry says there is no excess capacity. The center of gravity is slightly below to at potential, so I put the score just under 50 at 46. This is consistent with the bright-line rule (below-potential language is below 50, and nobody says above potential in aggregate).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Many tight local markets (Carolinas, Seventh District at 4.6 percent, Minnesota near record lows, "could not hire anybody") are offset by 5.6 percent national unemployment (Lindsey), job insecurity restraining wages, and Blinder's projected unemployment above the natural rate. At 5.6 percent, the table points to 31-45, but the discussion repeatedly calls it near or at the natural rate with pockets of tightness, so I use 47.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Moderate and about at trend. Housing is recovering, autos rebounded in August, and back-to-school sales were good. Offsetting this, Second District consumer demand is weak, business investment is slowing, and inventories are being worked down. Net is 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only raised as an open question (Kelley's list of questions, Jordan's staff question on the capital stock). No assessment of the level. N/A.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: The main statement on conditions is Phillips: markets "generally have improved" and there is "continued availability of relatively low cost capital." Bank credit growth has slowed and bankers are more cautious on commercial real estate (Hoenig, Forrestal). Statements that policy is restrictive or the real funds rate is high (Blinder, Yellen, Melzer, Greenspan) are policy stance, not market conditions, and I do not score them here. Direction: slightly supportive, below 50. Score 46. Bright-line holds (supportive means below 50).
- TREASURY_STRESS: The Daiwa fraud involved unauthorized sales of custody-account Treasuries, but no one describes market dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: One passing mention that the stock market improved, with no valuation view. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: The Daiwa branch fraud (about $1.1 billion in losses, failure of internal controls, late notification to regulators) is discussed at length, but as a single-institution supervisory failure. Members otherwise describe bankers as cautious on commercial real estate, with loan growth slowing and nothing about capital or solvency. No system stress is described. I score it just below neutral at 42, Low confidence. Bright-line: nothing stressed or undercapitalized is said, and nothing healthy is said either, so the below-50 placement rests on absence of stress.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Phillips says the dollar has "improved" since the last meeting (read as appreciation), while Blinder says the anti-EMU campaign "always seems to weigh on the dollar." Direction is mixed to slightly firmer. Score 52, Low confidence. Bright-line: the only directional statement from a member (improved) is appreciation, so the score is above 50.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: finished-goods prices flat, materials rising (Forrestal), packaging easing with building materials up (Moskow), grain prices up. Net about stable, 50.
- Services: not discussed. N/A.
- Housing: not discussed as inflation. N/A.
- Expectations: Melzer says long-horizon forecasts show no decline from current levels. Parry says market inflation expectations came down a bit. Anchored but not falling toward price stability. 55.

**7. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds with a symmetric directive (B). The Chairman and nearly everyone see the real funds rate as above equilibrium and the next move as probably an easing, but not now because of the fiscal debate. Forrestal wants a 25 bp cut today. Yellen and Blinder are "in sympathy" with him. Phillips sees "room to ease sometime this fall." Melzer, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus and Kelley are content with the current stance and would ease only on evidence of falling inflation. No one argues to tighten. This is a hold with a mild easing bias, 40.
- Disagreement: the vote read-out is all Yes (11 of 11), so zero formal dissents. Forrestal (a non-voter this year) openly argues for a cut today. There are camps on whether fiscal policy is a legitimate input to monetary policy (Blinder, Lindsey vs Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan) and on whether the real rate is too high. These are differences in timing and emphasis, so the score is 25.
- Financial stability: a substantial Daiwa exchange (supervision), some default or "train wreck" worry (Yellen, Phillips), the sweep-accounts discussion (monetary aggregates, not stability), and a Lindsey remark on bubbles inside the Mack-bill discussion. This is brief to limited in a 1990s meeting, 14.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive, but the Chairman says policy should not stay frozen, the next move will be taken by the full Committee, and several signal an easing next. Easing lean with data dependence, 36.
- Uncertainty: fiscal outcome called a "step into the unknown," the train-wreck scenario is not fully understood, and inventory dynamics are unclear. At the same time the baseline is broadly agreed ("too good to be true," but the economy "doing well"). Normal to somewhat elevated, 40.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (46) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (47) are close. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (50) differ by 4, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (46) with CREDIT_SPREADS N/A: nothing contradictory.
- TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, which is consistent in a calm market.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (52) is consistent with the sub-components (goods 50, expectations 55).

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY (raised only as a question), TREASURY_STRESS (no dysfunction described), CREDIT_SPREADS (not discussed), EQUITY_VALUATION (one passing mention, no valuation view), INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING (not discussed).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "prices remain well contained." Parry: "I don't anticipate any progress unfortunately this year and next in reducing inflation below last year's rate." Melzer: little prospect of substantial gains toward price stability.
- Summary: Contained but stuck near 3 percent CPI, a bit above the objective.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because members see no progress toward price stability but also no acceleration.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 46 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Hoenig: growth "slightly below trend, but in the 2 to 2-1/2 percent range." Blinder: "a small gap had opened up between actual and potential GDP." Counter: Broaddus: "operating at close to full capacity in many industries."
- Summary: Slightly below to at potential, with the gap widening slowly in forecasts.
- Direction: below neutral because several members describe growth under potential, offset by pockets of full capacity.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "District labor markets remain tight," unemployment 4.6 percent, "a full percentage point below the national average." Lindsey: "we are at 5.6 percent unemployment already."
- Summary: Pockets of tightness, but near the natural rate nationally with wage pressure restrained by insecurity.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the national rate is above 5.5 percent and projected to drift up.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: spending reports "consistent with continued growth ... at a pace close to trend." Phillips: "consumer spending and housing have shown improvement," although "business investment growth is slowing somewhat."
- Summary: A trend-pace expansion with housing and autos firm and investment cooling.
- Direction: at neutral because strengths and weaknesses offset.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Only mentioned as an open question (Kelley, Jordan).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 46 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Phillips: markets "generally have improved ... This means a continued availability of relatively low cost capital." Hoenig: "Growth in bank credit has slowed."
- Summary: Mildly supportive market conditions, with the real-rate-too-high remarks treated as policy stance rather than conditions.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the only direct description of conditions is supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** The Daiwa matter involved Treasury securities but no one describes market dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A.** Not discussed by members.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Stock market mentioned once as improved, no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-10 (Low)**
- Evidence: McDonough on Daiwa: "we do not take kindly to the management of a bank that has this information and doesn't share it with regulators for two months." Forrestal: "bankers are now becoming much more cautious about financing these activities."
- Summary: A single-institution fraud and some lending caution, with no system stress described.
- Direction: below neutral because there is no description of capital problems, failures or tightening standards at system level.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Phillips: markets improved, "the dollar, the stock market, the bond markets." Blinder: anti-EMU events "always seem to weigh on the dollar."
- Summary: Slightly firmer dollar since the last meeting, with a mixed outlook.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because the one directional report is an improvement (appreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 50.** Finished-goods prices flat while materials and building materials rose (Forrestal, Moskow).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Melzer: market participants "do not expect inflation to decrease from current levels." Parry: market expectations "have come down a bit."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "policy should be unchanged and symmetrical," while "we are probably at a real funds rate level that is higher than the long-term equilibrium." Forrestal: "my preference would be to move by 25 basis points today."
- Summary: A hold with most members seeing an eventual easing and one wanting it now. Hawkish voices (Melzer, Parry, Hoenig) only argue against easing, not for tightening.
- Direction: below neutral because no one advocates tightening and the next move is widely expected to be a cut.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Vote read-out shows 11 of 11 "Yes." Forrestal: "I hesitate to disagree with you, but I will anyway." Yellen and Blinder: "very much in sympathy" with his analysis.
- Summary: Unanimous vote, one open preference for a cut, and debate about fiscal policy as a consideration.
- Direction: low because there are no formal dissents and disagreement is on timing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 14 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Extended Daiwa exchange (McDonough, Greenspan, Minehan). Yellen: "a default on the government debt could touch off financial repercussions."
- Summary: A supervision episode and default worry, otherwise little attention to financial conditions.
- Direction: low-to-brief attention, typical of the mid-1990s.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 36 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "Policy should not remain frozen if we determine that the economy is in a state where we have to move." Phillips: "We should have some room to ease sometime this fall."
- Summary: Symmetric directive, but with a lean toward a later easing and Committee consultation.
- Direction: below neutral because the signaled next step is easing, tempered by data dependence and the fiscal wait.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Blinder: the fiscal step is "into the unknown ... It could all work out fine, or it could work out to be a catastrophe." Phillips: "we don't fully understand the implications of a potential train wreck."
- Summary: The baseline is agreed, but fiscal outcomes and inventories are called hard to judge.
- Direction: somewhat elevated, concentrated on fiscal policy.
