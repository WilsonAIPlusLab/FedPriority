# v4.1 scoring: transcript discussion, meeting 1997-11-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It contains the members' questions during the staff Q&A, the Reserve Bank go-around, the Board members' comments, the Chairman's summary, the policy go-around and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. The input is long (about 1,900 lines) and I read all of it. The text is clean apart from page markers. A few interjected questions have lost their answers because staff speech was removed, which does not affect scoring. The Q&A about primary dealers, swap lines, the BLS revision and similar topics is procedure and was ignored.

**Era context.** Late 1990s boom: strong growth, very low unemployment, low and falling measured inflation. Asian financial turmoil (Hong Kong stock market, Korea, Brazil and Argentina) and a sharp US stock market drop two weeks earlier are the new elements. I score only what the participants say.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair) gives the productivity argument and the policy summary. McDonough, Meyer, Rivlin, Phillips, Kelley, Ferguson and Gramlich are Board members and the Vice Chair. The presidents are Hoenig, Parry, Jordan, Broaddus, Moskow, Minehan, McTeer, Guynn, Stern, Melzer and Boehne.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see measured inflation as low and still falling (Hoenig: "core rate of inflation that continues to decline"; Phillips: "pressures on resources... have not shown through to price inflation"; Kelley: "inflation indexes continue to come in remarkably subdued"). They also see building pressure from wages, health costs and tight labor markets (Jordan: "buildup of inflationary pressures"; Meyer: "growing risk of higher inflation"; Gramlich: inflation "heating up... at a very slow rate"). Current pressure is therefore at or near the midpoint, slightly above on the forward-looking build. About 50 to 52.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Broaddus: "growing at a rate well above the staff's estimate of potential... 2-1/2 percent", "factory utilization rates... above past noninflationary levels". Moskow: "above-potential growth". Phillips: the current rate of growth is not sustainable. Bright line: above 50. Language is "clearly above" but not "overheating", so about 61.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants say it is extremely tight. Broaddus: "just about everybody who is willing and able to work is already working", labor demand "outstripping the sustainable supply... by a factor of maybe 2 or 3". Greenspan: "extremely tight labor market". Moskow: unemployment below 4% in district states. Parry: unemployment "even further below most estimates of the natural rate". Jordan: hiring bounties and shortages across many occupations. National unemployment is about 4.7% by the members' own references, which would map low in the table, but the language and regional rates below 4% push it higher. I score about 68.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Melzer: PCE up 5.7% and final sales up 5% in Q3; Phillips: "business fixed investment has been strong"; Guynn: investment "stronger than I expected". Offsets are stock-market-wealth worries and an expected slowdown, but the current reading is robust. About 67.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Heavily discussed. Greenspan argues that productivity is accelerating and that the staff's slowdown is too pessimistic. Rivlin sees "a sustained spurt". Ferguson cites "two quarters of productivity growth in the range of 2-1/2 to 3 percent". Meyer and Rivlin disagree on how much of this persists, but the perceived level is above trend. About 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants say they are supportive. Meyer: "overall financial conditions still seem to be highly supportive... more supportive than at the beginning of the year". Stern: "credit availability is ample". Minehan: "very accommodative capital markets". Ferguson: "capital has been readily available to borrowers on very reasonable terms". Bright line: below 50. The stock market drop and Asian turmoil temper this somewhat. About 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only brief mentions: a flight to quality, the narrowing spread between nominal and indexed notes, and long rates declining or insensitive. No dysfunction is described, and no member discusses Treasury market functioning. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No explicit spread statements. Level is inferred from terms and availability: Boehne says "bank margins are thin and the terms are very liberal"; Hoenig and Jordan say banks are pressing to lend, and Jordan says bankers are financing weak projects. Stern says credit is ample. Bright line (level governs): below 50. About 28, with limited direct evidence, so Moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed at length. Jordan: the Greenbook's 20% decline looks too mild if earnings are expected to fall, so he implies a more severe correction is warranted. Phillips: "froth". Greenspan expects a contraction when margins are squeezed. Boehne notes bullish attitudes that "could very well become excessive". Members see valuations as stretched but are mixed, since the recent drop was framed as volatility rather than a settled view. About 35, with a wide band.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed, leaning healthy. Minehan: banks "very profitable... well capitalized". Against that, Jordan reports speculative construction lending, undercapitalized borrowers and 125% LTV mortgages. Boehne reports lenders forgetting the lessons of the late 1980s and early 1990s, and credit card delinquencies are stable at a higher level. Bright line: "well-capitalized" means below 50. Healthy with some minor credit-quality concerns, about 36.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar appreciation is mentioned as background. Rivlin: "the strong dollar were helping us fight inflation... unlikely to go on rising much longer". Parry: "the dollar's appreciation over the last year". McDonough: "a stronger dollar". Bright line: above 50. The mentions are brief, so about 60.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Broaddus says goods prices at wholesale and retail "generally have been flat"; Meyer notes outright declines in durable goods prices; Boehne cites an inability to raise prices; Parry and Minehan mention falling computer prices. About 38.
- SERVICES: Health care costs are expected to rise (Broaddus, Hoenig, Jordan), trucking rates are rising, and business services firms pass on more of their costs (McTeer). About 56.
- HOUSING: Real estate boom talk concerns construction and asset prices, not shelter inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Hoenig: "persisting expectations of stable inflation". Boehne: "I do not sense an increase in inflationary psychology". Meyer: long-term expectations "have not changed much". Anchored near 50, so 47.

**Tier 3, in words first.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The decision is to hold with an asymmetric tightening directive ("B asymmetric"). Nearly all members say they would tighten were it not for the Asian turmoil. Broaddus wants a 1/4 point hike now and Melzer prefers C but accepts B. Meyer and Parry would have moved or want to soon. Rivlin and Kelley are more reluctant about tightening. Hold with a tightening bias is the 51-60 band; the strong latent hawkishness puts it at the upper part of that band. About 58.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent on the policy vote (Broaddus voted No on the directive, wanting an immediate hike). Broaddus also voted against swap line renewal, a separate non-policy matter. Reservations or different emphasis come from Melzer, Meyer and Parry. Differences are about timing, and the assessments of the economy broadly agree. This fits the typical one-dissent anchor. About 33.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: The Asian crisis, stock market volatility, the mutual fund stress study (Minehan), contagion to Brazil and Argentina (Guynn), speculative lending and asset prices (Jordan, Boehne), and the "international citizenship" argument (Gramlich) all feature. The decision to hold is justified primarily by financial-market fragility. This is a prominent topic though not dominant over the economic assessment. About 62.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The asymmetric directive toward tightening is retained. Greenspan: "we have no real choice about maintaining an asymmetric directive toward tightening"; "Whatever we believe we may have to do in December or later". Several members want to act soon after conditions calm. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias. About 68.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Many members stress uncertainty: Phillips ("forecast errors are extremely high", "the jury is still out"), Rivlin (interconnections "added greatly to the uncertainty"), Minehan (unable to forecast strength and low inflation together), McDonough (the "degree of uncertainty has increased"), McTeer ("the level of uncertainty is high"), and the productivity trend debate. Rhetorical hedging is small relative to substantive doubts. About 62.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 (61) satisfies "above potential". FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (30) satisfies "supportive". CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 (28) from low-level, liberal-terms language, no "wide" or "elevated" statements. BANKING_STRESS below 50 (36) satisfies "well capitalized". DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (60) for appreciation. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A because there is no dysfunction to score.

**Polarity checks.** Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter; evidence says supportive and easy, so a low score matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress; evidence is thin margins and ample credit, so a low score matches. Higher EQUITY_VALUATION means more stress or undervaluation concern, and overvaluation concern scores low; the evidence of froth and an expected larger correction fits a score below 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress; healthy banks give a low score.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP (61) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): within 15, consistent.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (61) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (67): within 15, consistent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) and CREDIT_SPREADS (28): co-move, consistent.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, no conflict.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (51) versus sub-components (goods 38, services 56, expectations 47): consistent with a near-midpoint reading. Measured inflation is low while labor-market pressure is high.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: members only mention bond-yield spreads and long-rate behavior, nothing on functioning. INFLATION_HOUSING: no discussion of shelter or rent inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 51 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Hoenig, "a core rate of inflation that continues to decline"; Jordan, "a buildup of inflationary pressures and... the inflation risks are decidedly on the upside". Summary: measured inflation is low while labor and wage pressures point up, so it nets near neutral. Direction: at neutral, with balanced pulls.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 61 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: Broaddus, "growing at a rate well above the staff's estimate of potential longer-term growth"; Moskow, "above-potential growth this quarter and out into 1998". Summary: the economy is running clearly above potential. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: Broaddus, "just about everybody who is willing and able to work is already working"; Greenspan, "an extremely tight labor market". Summary: very tight, with shortages across regions and unemployment below estimates of the natural rate. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 67 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: Melzer, "personal consumption expenditures at a 5.7 percent annual rate"; Phillips, "Business fixed investment has been strong". Summary: well above trend spending, with wealth-effect worries only a forward risk. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Ferguson, "two quarters of productivity growth in the range of 2-1/2 to 3 percent"; Rivlin, "a sustained spurt in productivity increases". Summary: members see productivity improving, with debate on persistence (Meyer is more skeptical). Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: Meyer, "overall financial conditions still seem to be highly supportive... more supportive than at the beginning of the year"; Minehan, "very accommodative capital markets". Summary: conditions are easy despite equity volatility. Direction: below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a market-functioning topic by participants.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Boehne, "bank margins are thin and the terms are very liberal"; Stern, "credit availability is ample". Summary: credit is abundant and terms are easy, with no explicit spread levels quoted. Direction: below neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +-10 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Jordan, the Greenbook's 20% decline looks mild given the earnings outlook, so "we would have a more severe correction"; Phillips, "plenty of activity, or froth". Summary: valuations are seen as rich, with the recent drop viewed as a volatile correction. Direction: below neutral (overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 36 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Minehan, banks "very profitable. They are well capitalized"; Jordan, "all the potential borrowers were significantly undercapitalized" in speculative building. Summary: banks sound, with lending-quality worries at the margin. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Parry, "the dollar's appreciation over the last year"; Rivlin, "the strong dollar were helping us fight inflation". Summary: the dollar is seen as having strengthened, mentioned in passing. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Broaddus, "goods prices at both the wholesale and retail level generally have been flat"; Meyer, outright declines in some durable goods prices.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 56.** Health care cost increases expected (Broaddus, Hoenig, Jordan); business services passing on costs (McTeer).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Hoenig, "persisting expectations of stable inflation"; Boehne, "I do not sense an increase in inflationary psychology".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: Greenspan, "we have no real choice about maintaining an asymmetric directive toward tightening"; Broaddus, "I strongly favor a modest tightening move today". Summary: hold now, strong underlying desire to tighten, restrained by Asia. Direction: above 50, lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-6 [High].**
Evidence: roll call, Broaddus "No"; Melzer, "I favor alternative C... but I could accept 'B'". Summary: one formal dissent and some reservations, with agreement on the economic assessment. Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Gramlich, "international citizenship... struggling currencies"; Moskow, "were it not for the financial volatility stemming from events in Southeast Asia". Summary: market turmoil and lending excess are a prominent, policy-relevant theme, alongside the real economy.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +-7 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Greenspan, "Whatever we believe we may have to do in December or later"; Vice Chairman, "'B' asymmetric". Summary: the retained asymmetry points toward tightening once the turmoil passes.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 [Moderate].**
Evidence: Phillips, "the forecast errors are extremely high"; McDonough, "the degree of uncertainty has increased". Summary: many cite uncertainty on Asia, productivity and the inflation outlook.
