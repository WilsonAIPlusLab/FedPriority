## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Staff turns are absent from the input; only participant statements are used. Vote read-out at the end was read: nine Yes votes, no dissent.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 +-5 Moderate
- Bullard: core PCE "now at 1.6 percent... little inflation pressure in the U.S. economy"; Kashkari: "we still have not reached our 2 percent target... may not even be making progress"; Brainard: "troubled by the lack of progress toward our inflation target"; Mullinix: "inflation is essentially returning to its target"; George: "inflation remains very near the Committee's objective"; Powell/Mester/Dudley: March softness mostly "transitory"/"one-off".
- Score: most members see core inflation below 2 percent but expect a return to target; no one sees excess pressure.

OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-5 Moderate
- George: "current economic output is above its potential and expected to exceed it by greater margins"; Mullinix: "real GDP as growing above the underlying trend"; Kaplan: "above-potential GDP growth"; Williams: "economy appears on track to outpace potential"; Bullard: "balanced growth"; Kashkari: "difficult for me to take too seriously the concerns that the economy may be overheating".
- Score: modestly above potential (bright-line > 50).

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +-5 High
- Unemployment 4.5 percent; Fischer: "almost 1/2 percentage point below the staff's estimate of the natural rate"; George: "little or no slack remaining in labor markets"; Williams: "we've already overshot full employment"; Mester: "labor market conditions remain strong"; Kaplan: "reports of labor shortages are becoming even more frequent"; Yellen: wage growth "still remains subdued".
- Score: tight, UE below most natural-rate estimates, wage pressure only modest.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +-6 Moderate
- Powell: consumption "rose at an annual rate of only 0.3 percent" but "business investment posted its first solid increase since 2015"; Brainard: "consumer spending was surprisingly weak" yet "underlying fundamentals remain favorable"; Mester: "Business fixed investment picked up"; Gooding: "concerned about the outlook for consumer spending".
- Score: weak Q1 consumption offset by firm investment and solid fundamentals, netting near trend.

PRODUCTIVITY: 30 +-6 Moderate
- Powell: "very weak performance of productivity over the past six years"; Mester: "Productivity growth remains low"; Mullinix: "low real-wage growth to low productivity growth"; Bullard: "about 1/2 percent per year productivity improvement".
- Score: well below historical trend.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-6 High
- Brainard: "Financial conditions have eased significantly, on net"; George: "financial conditions appear to have eased over the past six months"; Mester: "accommodative monetary and financial conditions"; Dudley: "our recent moves to remove accommodation have not tightened financial conditions"; Powell: risk premiums "compressed".
- Score: loose/easing (bright-line < 50).

TREASURY_STRESS: 32 +-8 Low
- Harker: "The Treasury securities market is well functioning"; Kashkari: "I don't see a market-functioning issue arising"; Mester: yield declines reflect "flight-to-quality flows" (orderly). Only brief, indirect member comments.
- Score: normal functioning, no dysfunction.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-7 Moderate
- George: "low levels of implied volatility and compressed risk spreads raises some concerns that financial markets may be somewhat complacent and underpricing risk"; Powell: "risk premiums in the prices of equities, corporate debt, and other financial assets are compressed". Stated level (compressed) governs; Harker adds "Loan growth has been strong" (availability, supporting).
- Score: below normal (bright-line < 50).

EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-7 Moderate
- Yellen: "potential for a correction in the stock market, in view of the fact that valuations... appear somewhat 'rich'"; Brainard: "'rich' asset valuations as a source of notable concern"; George: asset prices "elevated and, by some measures, near all-time highs"; Fischer: "prices of risky assets are high"; Bullard: "should the air come out of the current valuations".
- Score: modestly-to-clearly above fair value, concern expressed but not alarm.

BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-6 High
- Brainard: "well-capitalized core financial institutions"; George: "capital ratios at the bank holding companies that are high by historical standards"; Fischer: "summary of financial stability in the United States is encouraging"; Harker: "delinquency rates at all-time lows"; Kaplan: some worry about small banks "taking duration risk"; Williams: low foreign-bank price-to-book questions.
- Score: healthy with minor concerns (bright-line < 50).

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 +-6 Moderate
- Yellen: "the dollar has depreciated modestly since our previous meeting"; Williams: "The fall in the dollar over the intermeeting period"; Dudley: "the dollar trajectory is now starting to have some consequences for the inflation outlook"; Mester: "the rate of dollar appreciation has tempered".
- Score: modest depreciation (bright-line < 50).

INFLATION_GOODS: 52 (Dudley: "nonfuel import prices are now rising—about 1 percent over the past year" versus -2.5 percent a year earlier; Harker: "no price pressures" in his contact's business; Mullinix: "higher commodity prices" concern).
INFLATION_SERVICES: 42 (Powell/Yellen/Gooding: March decline from "wireless telephone services"; Dudley: "lodging away from home"; ECI and wages "firming a bit"; Yellen: wage growth "still... subdued").
INFLATION_HOUSING: 62 (rests on house prices rather than rents: Mester "acceleration in prices"; Kaplan "run-ups in home prices"; George "house prices are likely to continue rising"; Fischer "house prices starting to rise more rapidly"; Fischer: prices "within the range suggested by their rents").
INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 (Brainard: market-based measures "remain quite low by historical standards", surveys "edged down"; Kashkari: "flat to down"; Mester/George: "stable... near 2 percent"; Dudley: NY Fed survey "rose to 2.9 percent"; Gooding/Brainard: public may see target as a ceiling).

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +-6 Moderate
- Policy: hold at alternative B, unanimous; Yellen: "keeping the target range unchanged... leaving the door open for a hike in June"; Mester: "very uncomfortable if that pause turned into a longer delay"; Rosengren: "three, rather than two, increases" and balance-sheet announcement this summer; Williams: "three more rate increases this year"; Dudley: "there's more for us to do"; Powell: "continued gradualism"; Bullard: "consider delaying a further rate hike"; Kashkari: "not appropriate to raise the funds rate unless we see more progress toward our inflation objective"; Evans: "policy has some work to do". Balance sheet runoff plan broadly supported (Bullard wants faster).
- Score: hold now with a clear tightening bias, one-to-two dovish voices.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 High
- Vote: nine Yes, no dissent. Differences of emphasis: Bullard ("somewhat overcommitted to a sequence of policy rate moves"), Kashkari (June conditional on inflation) versus Rosengren/Williams (overheating risk); Mester prefers cessation over phase-out; Fischer and Kashkari/Evans sparring on the word "wealth" and on a "long march"; floor versus corridor reservations (Harker, George, Mester).
- Score: broad agreement, minor differences, zero dissents.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 Moderate
- A dedicated financial-stability round at the start (Rosengren on GSE multifamily exposure, Williams on price-to-book, Fischer, George, Brainard, Kaplan on bank ROE); Powell: "risk premiums... compressed... argues for tighter monetary policy"; George: asset prices elevated; Mester: avoid "buildup of risks to... financial stability". Mostly absent from the economy and policy go-rounds apart from asset valuations.
- Score: several participants address it meaningfully.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +-6 Moderate
- Yellen: "a further increase in the target range at our next meeting will probably be appropriate" but "not... baked in the cake", "preserve our optionality"; gradual removal of accommodation (Mester, Powell, Kaplan); reinvestment phase-out to start later this year with bullets released at June (Yellen, most members; Williams "before the end of the year"); Brainard: "further removal of accommodation will be appropriate later this year".
- Score: mild-to-clear tightening signal ahead (further hikes and balance sheet reduction), tempered by data dependence.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-6 Moderate
- Evans: "My uncertainty over all of these forecasts is a bit higher than it was in March"; Powell: outlook "broadly unchanged, albeit less certain" and "highly uncertain" neutral rate; Kashkari: "considerable uncertainty about both the natural rate of unemployment and longer-run trend in labor force participation"; Harker: "large amount of uncertainty" about fiscal policy; Yellen: "risks... roughly balanced"; Williams: risks "well balanced". Many hedges are routine; fiscal and r* uncertainty are real.
- Score: somewhat elevated.
