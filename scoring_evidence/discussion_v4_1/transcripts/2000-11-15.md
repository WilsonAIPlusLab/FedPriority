# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2000-11-15

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Participants' turns from a transcript: opening procedure, members' questions during the staff Q&A, the economic go-around (Hoenig through Meyer), a second Q&A after the break, the Chairman's proposal and the policy go-around on the "balance of risks" language, then the vote read-out. Staff answers are removed, so some questions are unanswered in the text. Parts of a few turns (McDonough on Japan, Poole on UPS) have gaps from extraction, which does not affect any score.

**Era context.** Late 2000. The text shows a long, strong expansion that has slowed to a moderate pace. Unemployment is about 3.9-4%, oil is high, and core inflation is edging up. Credit is tightening. The funds rate is 6-1/2 percent, and the live issue is whether to keep the "risks toward inflation" statement or move to "balanced". This only frames the reading; the scores come from what members say.

**Speakers.**
- Hawkish side on the tilt: Greenspan (keeps the inflation tilt but adds softening language), McDonough, Parry, Kelley, Guynn, Stern, Moskow, Santomero, Pianalto, Minehan, Broaddus, Meyer.
- Prefer a balanced statement but accept the proposal: Poole, Hoenig, McTeer, with Ferguson and Gramlich as reluctant supporters.
- Everyone supports an unchanged funds rate.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the comfortable level and rising, not extreme.
  - Core CPI and core PCE are up about 1/2 point from their cycle lows (Moskow).
  - The ECI is up 4.6 percent, the biggest four-quarter rise in ten years (Broaddus).
  - Many call the measures "worrisome" or "disturbing" (Gramlich, Ferguson).
  - Offsets: Greenspan sees benign expectations and a labor-cost story that is overstated. Hoenig calls underlying inflation "relatively modest". Goods prices are subdued.
  - Clearly above target with broadening pressure but no acceleration to alarm: low-to-mid 60s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Meyer says explicitly "output is above potential", and the unemployment rate is below the NAIRU. Growth has moved below trend (Santomero, Moskow), and Pianalto reports the Greenbook sees the economy "in balance". Level modestly above potential, growth cooling. The bright line requires > 50 (Meyer), so 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "Exceptionally low" unemployment (Meyer), 3.9 percent (Santomero), 3.2 percent in the Tenth District, Iowa at 2.3 percent. The Manpower survey shows record demand for labor, and workers are scarce everywhere. Participants call it "exceptionally tight" (Broaddus). UE below 4 percent: 77.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is slowing from a very high pace, with retail softness and a "knife-edge" for investment (Ferguson). Santomero says the economy is "still robust", Kelley says "substantial strength", and Gramlich says consumption is holding up. This nets to roughly trend or slightly below: 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Productivity growth "held up remarkably well" (Parry) and Stern reports optimistic business contacts. Offsets: Q3 slowed to 3.8 percent, and structural estimates were revised down (Broaddus, Minehan). Above the historical trend but cooling: 62.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Financial conditions have tightened" (Hoenig). Meyer reports "deterioration in financial conditions" and tighter lending standards, and Greenspan reports a "remarkable tightening" of junk, investment-grade and banking markets. Greenspan notes it is "not 1998" and that costs, not availability, are what is affected. Somewhat tight: 62.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Greenspan's and Poole's questions touch this (market structure if easing is needed, curve shape and expectations). No dysfunction described; Poole's comment is about expectations. Normal functioning, low confidence: 42.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Members describe spreads that "have increased" and "widening" (Minehan, Hoenig, Gramlich, Meyer), "wider quality spreads, especially for below investment-grade", a "sharp rise in borrowing costs for low-rated borrowers" (Gramlich), and reduced access (Guynn, Parry). No explicit statement of the level in words like "wide" or "elevated"; the language of reduced access and sharply higher costs for low-rated borrowers points to a somewhat wide level, short of 1998. Level reading: 62, moderate confidence. Bright line satisfied (> 50 on stated levels of cost and reduced access, no "compressed" language).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Kelley says stocks are "still quite elevated by historical standards" apart from the most egregiously overvalued names. Others mention a stalled or falling market, volatility and a risk of further declines (Gramlich). The valuation view is mildly rich, and the decline is orderly: 43.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Minehan reports small banks "as profitable and healthy as ever" and some deterioration at larger banks. Parry mentions problem loans to large borrowers while small business and middle-market portfolios hold up. Guynn calls the tightening "markets are working". Lending standards are tightening. Sound system with emerging caution: 47.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "The dollar remains strong" (McTeer). The strong dollar is hurting tourism (Guynn) and "the dollar is high now" (Gramlich), with McDonough giving a long account of why the dollar has strengthened. A forecast depreciation is discussed as a future risk, and Ferguson mentions "continuing depreciation of the dollar" as a marginal tightening factor, which conflicts with the other statements. The level is strong: 64, moderate confidence.
- Sub-components. Goods: manufacturers cannot pass through costs and goods CPI ex food and energy is up only about 1/2 percent a year (Pianalto, Hoenig), against energy surcharges: 38. Services: services are the "culprit" for accelerating core CPI (Pianalto), with health-care costs: 66. Housing: not discussed as housing inflation: N/A. Expectations: long-term measures have "not worsened" and TIPS are unchanged or lower (Greenspan, Gramlich), short-term measures somewhat up, with credibility concerns (Poole, Meyer, Santomero): 52.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: all hold at 6-1/2 percent, with the inflation tilt retained but paired with language acknowledging weakening. Five members want a balanced statement. McTeer expects the next move to be easing, while Kelley sees tightening as the most likely next move. A hold with a mild tightening bias: 55.
- Dissent: the vote is unanimous with no formal dissents. Poole, Hoenig and McTeer prefer a balanced statement but have no vote on the proposal in the read-out (the roll lists ten voters, all Yes). Ferguson and Gramlich are reluctant supporters ("a close call for me"). Greenspan says there is no serious difference about the policy rate, only about the statement. Moderate differences on tone and statement, not on action or assessment: 30.
- Financial stability emphasis: Financial and credit conditions come up in many go-around turns (Hoenig, Guynn, Minehan, Ferguson, Meyer, Greenspan). Greenspan says tightening is "what has changed the most", and McDonough raises Argentina, Japan and "eerie calm". This is a meaningful topic but alongside the main inflation-versus-growth debate: 52.
- Forward guidance: the retained inflation tilt with softening language is a mild tightening-bias signal, but market easing expectations are a concern for the proposal's wording: 57.
- Uncertainty: Gramlich ("considerable uncertainty", "can't even see its basic direction"), Ferguson ("as hazy as it has been"), Minehan ("a great deal of uncertainty"), Guynn ("considerable uncertainty"), Greenspan (the NAIRU is unknown, data are hard to read). Elevated, though most members express fairly firm views: 52.

**Polarity checks.** Higher output gap = more above potential: Meyer says above, so > 50. Higher financial conditions = tighter: members say tightened, so > 50. Higher credit spreads = more stress: members say wider spreads and reduced access, so > 50. Higher dollar = stronger: members say strong, so > 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: members mostly describe a healthy system with some caution, so < 50. Higher EQUITY_VALUATION = more stress/concern about depressed prices: Kelley's "elevated" view is below 50. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH = more hawkish: hold with an inflation tilt, so just above 50.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 and LABOR 77 differ by 22 points. This is explained by labor's level being the clearest objective anchor (UE below 4 percent, far below NAIRU) while members describe the output gap as modestly above potential and narrowing as growth slows. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (55 vs 47) are within 15 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 62 and CREDIT_SPREADS 62 co-move. TREASURY_STRESS stays near normal as conditions are tight but not in crisis. Parent inflation 65 is consistent with services high (66), goods low (38) and expectations anchored (52).

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discusses housing or shelter costs as inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 65 +-6, High.** Evidence: Moskow, "higher inflation is already more than a risk... both core CPI and core PCE inflation are running 1/2 point above their lows for the cycle". Broaddus, ECI "up 4.6 percent... the biggest four-quarter increase in ten years". Summary: clearly above comfort and rising across measures, tempered by anchored expectations and benign goods prices. Direction: above neutral because most members call the core trend rising and worrisome.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Meyer, "below-trend growth when output is above potential, as I believe is the case, is desirable"; Moskow, outlook "only slightly below that of potential". Summary: modestly above potential in level with growth cooling below trend. Direction: above neutral per the explicit "above potential" statement, though the Greenbook framing is "in balance" (Pianalto).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 77 +-5, High.** Evidence: Broaddus, "the labor market is still exceptionally tight"; Moskow, Iowa's 2.3 percent unemployment and Manpower "record high demand for labor". Summary: unemployment around 4 percent or lower with scarce workers everywhere. Direction: well above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Santomero, "the economy is still robust"; Ferguson, "investment spending is on a bit more of a knife-edge". Summary: a slowdown from very high growth, with robust levels but softening retail and capital goods. Direction: about at trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "productivity growth held up remarkably well in the face of slower real GDP growth"; Broaddus, structural projections revised downward. Summary: above trend and strong but possibly peaking. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 +-6, High.** Evidence: Hoenig, "financial conditions have tightened"; Greenspan, "remarkable tightening... of the junk bond markets... investment-grade markets, and very particularly the banking markets". Summary: somewhat tight but not 1998-style stress. Direction: above neutral (tightened).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-10, Low.** Evidence: Poole on the curve showing an expected-easing pattern; Greenspan asking about market structure. Summary: no dysfunction, only expectational commentary. Direction: normal functioning (below midpoint by rubric).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Gramlich, "a sharp rise in borrowing costs for low-rated borrowers... a widening of credit spreads"; Meyer, "wider quality spreads, especially for below investment-grade bonds". Summary: somewhat wide and widening, with reduced access, short of 1998. Direction: above neutral on the level implied by costs and access.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Kelley, the stock market "is still quite elevated by historical standards and holding up well so far"; Gramlich, "stock prices could decline further". Summary: mildly rich valuations with an orderly decline. Direction: below neutral (valuation still elevated).

**BANKING_STRESS: 47 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Minehan, "small banks are as profitable and healthy as ever, larger ones are seeing some deterioration"; Guynn, "markets are working". Summary: sound system with tightening standards and some large-credit problems. Direction: slightly below neutral (healthy).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 64 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: McTeer, "The dollar remains strong"; Gramlich, "while the dollar is high now". Summary: strong dollar with expected future depreciation. Direction: above neutral.

**Sub-components.** GOODS 38 (Pianalto: goods CPI ex food and energy up only 1/2 percent a year, manufacturers cannot raise prices). SERVICES 66 (Pianalto: "The culprit is the services sector"). HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS 52 (Greenspan: TIPS "about where it was... if anything it is lower"; Gramlich: short-term measures worsened to some degree).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +-5, High.** Evidence: Parry, "an unchanged funds rate" and retain "balance of risks is toward higher inflation"; McTeer, "my guess is that our next move will be an easing move". Summary: a hold with a retained inflation tilt, with a minority preferring balance. Direction: slightly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Gramlich, "It is indeed a close call for me"; Greenspan, "I don't believe there is a really serious difference of opinion about where policy ought to be currently". Summary: a unanimous vote with five members preferring a balanced statement, and disagreement confined to wording. Direction: low-moderate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson, "This focus on financial and credit conditions is important because investment... has been a pillar"; McDonough on Argentina and "eerie calm". Summary: several members discuss credit tightening and external fragility meaningfully, but it is not the main topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "stay with the balance of risks toward inflation statement"; Kelley, "The Greenbook and at least some of us here see a further tightening as the most likely next move". Summary: a mild tightening bias retained, softened by acknowledgment of weakening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8, Moderate.** Evidence: Gramlich, "How do we stay ahead of the curve when we can't even see its basic direction?"; Ferguson, "the future is as hazy as it has been". Summary: several members stress elevated uncertainty, though most are fairly firm on policy.
