# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) - 2015-04-29 (transcript discussion)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-5 (High)**
- Yellen: "Both core and headline PCE inflation rates are running well below 2 percent and are likely to do so for the rest of the year"; Kocherlakota: "inflation is far below target"; "signs ... we may be facing a prolonged period of inflation running substantially below our target".
- Partial offset: Mester "Core inflation is modestly firming"; Lacker "we are emerging from this inflation dip"; Dudley "inflation is starting to stabilize"; Brainard "underlying trend rate of only 1.4 percent".
- Below target, with headline near zero and core around 1.3-1.4 percent. Several participants see stabilization, so the score sits a little below the 2013-19 anchor.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 42 +-5 (High)**
- Williams: "an economy still operating below potential"; Dudley: "still-sizable slack in the labor market ... continue to close the output gap"; Kocherlakota: "continued shortfalls in aggregate demand relative to supply".
- Fischer/Powell: unemployment "not that much above many estimates of the natural rate" / "we're close to the natural rate".
- Below potential but closing ("slack remains"), so the score must be under 50 and is.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +-5 (High)**
- Fischer: "The unemployment rate remains at 5.5 percent ... not that much above many estimates of the natural rate"; Williams: "still some considerable slack in the labor market"; Brainard: "slack remains".
- Tighter signals: Lacker on "shortages of skilled workers" and wage pressures that "appear to have broadened"; Bullard says labor markets are "relatively strong viewed in historical perspective". Yellen's summary: "aggregate measures suggest very little pickup in wage growth".
- UE 5.5% sits at the edge of the 31-45 band. Most participants still describe slack, though a few see local tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen summary: "everyone remarked on the unexpected weakness we've seen in a broad range of spending and production indicators"; Yellen: "outright declines in real equipment outlays"; "retail sales having been soft for several months".
- Brainard: "Drilling investment looks to have dropped at an annualized rate of 50 percent"; "subpar data on retail sales have now persisted for four months". Offsets: Lacker "I share this Tealbook's optimism on consumer spending"; Lockhart positive real estate investment reports.
- First-quarter spending and investment were below trend, and most participants expect a consumption-led rebound.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +-10 (Low)**
- Dudley: "unless one thought that the productivity growth trend would remain extraordinarily poor"; Rosengren: "changes in demographics and productivity since the financial crisis"; Kocherlakota: "be very cautious before accepting" claims of "a permanent downward shift in the productive capabilities".
- The discussion is brief and indirect. It leans toward weak trend productivity, so the score is modestly below 50.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Williams: "highly accommodative monetary policy will continue to boost spending"; "abundance of financing that can be obtained at very generous terms"; Yellen: "low borrowing costs", "mortgage rates are low".
- Offsets: Powell refers to "the tightening in financial conditions that we have already experienced" through the dollar channel; Evans says "I am not convinced our current policy stance is all that accommodative" (a policy-stance point).
- Credit is generally easy, with some dollar-driven tightening. "Accommodative" requires a score under 50, and it is.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Brainard: "episodes of severe volatility, like the wild swings we saw in Treasury bond yields on the morning of October 15", but "bid-ask spreads are in line with historical norms"; Kocherlakota: "changes in the structure of the Treasury market could amplify the effects of an initial rate move".
- Current functioning is normal and the concern is prospective (liquidity vulnerability), so the score is under 50 and no dysfunction is described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-10 (Low)**
- This score rests on availability and terms language, not on any spread wording. Williams: "abundance of financing that can be obtained at very generous terms" (CRE); Holcomb: "bankers report flat demand and unchanged standards across all major loan categories"; Prichard: commercial loan growth "picked up substantially"; Yellen: "low borrowing costs".
- Rosengren's worry about "a rapid increase in the yield on corporate bonds" at liftoff is about the future and does not state the current level.
- Availability is ample, so the score is somewhat below normal. Per V4.1.1 §3 it gets CONF Low and UNC +-10.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A**
- Yellen mentions "record equity prices" only as a wealth and saving channel. Bullard's concern about "asset price bubbles" is about generic assets in the future and gives no current equity valuation view (V4.1.1 §2).

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Low)**
- Williams: "large banks' liquidity ratios continue to improve"; Lacker: "large banks have a ton of reserves"; Brainard: "overall risks to financial stability in the United States remain moderate". Holcomb notes "some reports of adverse spillovers from energy to financial services".
- Banks are described as healthy, with a minor energy-exposure concern, so the score is under 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 66 +-5 (High)**
- Mester: "the sharp appreciation of the dollar"; Brainard: "the surge in the dollar", "the stronger dollar is weighing significantly on activity"; Kocherlakota: "the high value of the dollar"; Dudley: "the dollar's sharp appreciation has led to a deterioration in U.S. net exports".
- Recent pause: Brainard "the dollar's sharp ascent seems to have stalled"; Yellen "the broad dollar exchange rate actually fell a little bit".
- The dollar is strong after a sharp appreciation, and participants weigh it as a notable headwind, so the score is above 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 33**
- Tarullo/statement: "decreasing prices of non-energy imports"; Mester: "Prices of finished goods continue to be stable despite ... declining costs for nonlabor inputs"; energy and import prices are cited by many as holding inflation down.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 42**
- George: "Inflation in some sectors, such as health care, have also notably slowed". Tarullo implies that underlying inflation outside housing is soft. The evidence is thin.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 56**
- Tarullo: "if you pull out housing inflation ... then you have an awful lot of continuing questions about how much inflation there is", which implies housing is the firmer component. Williams: Bay Area housing "very hard to come by and obviously very dear".

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44**
- Williams: "Long-run survey expectations ... remain well anchored"; Dudley: "fears I had of inflation getting unanchored to the downside seem to have diminished"; Kocherlakota: "Market-based longer-term inflation expectations remain low", plus "some slippage in Michigan survey measures"; Brainard: market-based measures "still materially below the levels that prevailed until the middle of last year".
- Surveys are anchored and market-based measures are soft, so the score is slightly below the at-target midpoint.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 49 +-5 (High)**
- The Committee held, with broad support for alternative B. Dovish side: Kocherlakota "I favor alternative A"; Evans says liftoff will come "sometime in 2016"; Rosengren is "very skeptical that these conditions can be met by June"; Tarullo says June is "very unlikely"; Yellen says firming conditions are "unlikely ... by June".
- Hawkish side: Lacker says "it's very important not to take June off the table"; Bullard: "shading in the direction of slightly higher interest rates"; Fischer: "we should seriously be contemplating lifting off soon".
- This is a pre-liftoff hold with June kept open but the probability shifted later, so the score is near balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-7 (Moderate)**
- The policy vote was unanimous, 10-0, in the read-out at the end of the file. The non-voter Kocherlakota preferred alternative A, and Evans was "sympathetic" to it. Participants differed on the inflation outlook, the level of r* (Bullard vs Williams/Evans) and on reopening the inflation target (Rosengren/Kocherlakota vs Fischer). Timing views ranged from June to 2016.
- Broad agreement on today's action came with moderate differences on timing and assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 +-7 (High)**
- A dedicated financial-stability segment covered several topics. Rosengren raised "the problem of liquidity in the secondary market for corporate bonds". Kocherlakota said a near-term increase "could lead to financial instability". Williams spoke on market liquidity. Brainard warned that lack of liquidity "could pose threats to financial stability".
- In other rounds, Bullard warned that low rates are "a recipe for asset price bubbles", Tarullo described negative European yields with "potential for ... significant turmoil", and George cited the risk "that longer-term rates could increase sharply approaching liftoff".
- Several participants addressed the topic meaningfully. It was not the dominant theme.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 45 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Lacker/Tarullo: "every meeting should be live"; Powell: "I would avoid calendar-based guidance. I would let the data speak". Fischer and others say "June remains on the table", while Brainard says "the probability of liftoff has shifted from June to later in the year". Bullard: normalization "would be very small and very gradual".
- The signal is data-dependent with eventual tightening ahead, but recent data pushed it later and the path is described as gradual, so the score is slightly below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-6 (High)**
- Yellen: "I am more worried about downside risk and am more uncertain about the outlook"; "new data will confuse rather than clarify". Dudley: "I'm more uncertain about the growth outlook ... a lot of crosscurrents ... really difficult to assess". Lockhart: "the greater uncertainty I perceive at this juncture". Mester: "difficult to do in real time ... remain agnostic". Bullard: "the value of r* is highly uncertain".
- Multiple participants say outright that uncertainty is elevated, so the score falls in the elevated band.
