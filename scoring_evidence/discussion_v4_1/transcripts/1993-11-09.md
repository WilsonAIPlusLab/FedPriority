# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (+ V4.1.1 clarifications) — 1993-11-09 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion extracted from an FOMC transcript (staff turns removed). The whole file (629 lines, read to the last line) is a telephone conference call. Its only subject is Mexico: pressure on the peso ahead of the U.S. House vote on NAFTA, and a request from the Bank of Mexico for an enlarged/activated Federal Reserve swap line (largely "window-dressing", in the Chairman's words). There is no economic go-around, no discussion of U.S. growth, labor, inflation or domestic financial conditions, and no discussion of the U.S. monetary policy stance (funds rate, directive, bias).

**Special-topic determination (V4.1.1 rule 1).** The test is whether the document contains (a) a vote or roll call on a monetary policy action (target rate, directive, policy statement, balance-sheet operation) or (b) a go-around or sustained discussion of the policy stance.
- (b) is clearly absent: nobody discusses the stance of U.S. monetary policy.
- (a): there is **no `=== Vote read-out ... ===` block** at the end of the file; the text ends with the Chairman saying "That is satisfactory with me" after members converge on raising the Mexican swap line by up to $2.3 billion (to $3 billion), temporary for about 90 days, with authority delegated to the Foreign Currency Subcommittee. Members reach informal consensus but no recorded vote appears. Even if a vote was taken, the action is a foreign-currency swap-line facility for a foreign central bank. It is not a monetary-policy action of the kind the clarification lists. A swap line is technically on the balance sheet, so this is a judgment call. I read "balance-sheet operation" in the clarification as a monetary-policy balance-sheet action (e.g., asset purchases), not FX support for another country.
- **Conclusion: treated as special-topic** (foreign-currency operations / Mexico swap-line conference call). HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and DISSENT_INTENSITY are N/A. Condition dimensions are N/A unless the text actually supports them. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS, FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE and UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED are scored only where participants say something explicit.

**2. Era context.** Late 1993, early-transcript era. Backdrop not used to override the text; the text contains nothing about U.S. macro conditions.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (frames the peso pressure, praises Mexican policy as "remarkably sound", calls the request "window-dressing"); McDonough (supports, argues the rate was not artificial and that capital flight is a "psychological battle"); Angell (procedural/legal questions; notes peso depreciation could affect the NAFTA vote; raises the "real question" of eventual use); Lindsey (most skeptical: "why increase the swap line?", "let those people who are irrational lose money", calls it "a charade to get through next Wednesday", proposes expiry on Wednesday); Syron (skeptical of intervention generally but supports this case); Forrestal, Boehne, McTeer, Kelley, Phillips, Keehn (supportive or asking procedural questions).
