# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 — Meeting 2020-06-10 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, v2 input (staff turns removed). It spans two sessions: day one holds a go-around on policy tools (forward guidance, asset purchases, yield curve control), members' questions to staff, and the economic go-around. Day two holds the policy go-around and the vote read-out (10 "Yes", no dissent). Staff answers are absent, so several member questions stand without replies. I ignored that and the procedural remarks, the racial-injustice statements and the motions.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2020, a few months into the pandemic shutdown. I score only what participants say. They describe the steepest output decline on record, a May jobs report that surprised sharply to the upside, financial markets that have recovered from March, and policy at the effective lower bound.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chair Powell and Vice Chair Williams: highly accommodative policy for years; wait before specific guidance; caution about one good jobs report.
- Clarida, Brainard, Daly, Kashkari, Evans: worry about low inflation and inflation expectations; want stronger, outcome-based guidance and a package of tools.
- Harker, Kaplan, Barkin, George, Quarles, Bostic: skeptical of yield curve control; some say further action is premature or warn of costs (risk-taking, zombie firms, reach for yield).
- Rosengren and Bullard: stress credit spreads, 13(3) facilities and financial-crisis risk. Rosengren expects a second wave.
- Bowman and Mester: gradual recovery, downside risks, bank credit and small-business stress.

**4. Directional statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as far below the 2 percent target. Core PCE is about 1 percent, April saw the largest one-month decline on record (Kashkari, Clarida), and Daly calls it a "deflationary downdraft". The outlook is 1.4 to 1.7 percent by 2022. This is well below the midpoint but not a deflation spiral, so I place it in the 11-25 band.
- OUTPUT_GAP: far below potential. Clarida: "output gap of at least 7 percent" at end-2020, Q2 GDP down about 35 percent annualized. The recovery has begun, but it starts from a trough. This is the COVID Q2 anchor area, so about 20.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: very slack. Unemployment is 13.3 percent, the "true" rate is near 20 percent (Powell), and 21 million are unemployed. May's gain is a small share of the losses (Mester). Level reading, about 16.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: spending and investment are far below trend but rebounding (card spending near year-ago levels per Harker and Bostic, auto sales up). Investment is deferred (Evans, Barkin, George). The net is deeply below trend with a bounce, so about 25.
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed only in passing and mixed. Barkin: "Productivity will likely be hurt by new health measures". George and Bullard: staffing and work-from-home efficiencies. Net near trend, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: eased greatly since March. Clarida: "easing of financial conditions"; Brainard: "financial markets are buoyant"; Powell and Williams: "highly accommodative"/"supportive". Offsetting are tighter bank lending standards (Harker, Quarles) and wide private spreads (Rosengren). Accommodative language means the score must be below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: market functioning has been restored. Rosengren: "problems created by panic selling are now behind us". Clarida: markets "functioning well". Bostic: asset purchases "worked well this spring" for Treasury market functioning. Yield rises after the jobs report are not dysfunction. Normal functioning, score 36-45.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level reading. Rosengren states that private-sector spreads over Treasuries are "particularly large at present" and "elevated", so this is above 50. Brainard and Clarida report improvement and record issuance, with high yield improving "to a lesser degree". Net: somewhat wide but improving. This is a level above normal, so the score is above 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: concern about stretched valuations. Brainard reports market participants' "puzzlement" with the equity rally above 3,000 on the S&P. Bowman: equity moves "out of step with realistic revenue expectations". Kaplan: "speculative nature". Kashkari disagrees and ties prices to a low r*. Valuation concern means a lower score on this scale, modestly stretched.
- BANKING_STRESS: banks are sound and a source of strength (Quarles). The concerns are a pullback in new lending, possible CRE and small-business losses, and illiquidity turning into insolvency (Clarida, Bostic, Mester). Mixed, leaning healthy.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: George cites "the elevated level of the foreign exchange value of the dollar" weighing on exports. Kashkari: "relatively strong dollar" pulls down prices. Brainard: swap lines normalized dollar funding abroad. Two brief mentions of strength put the score above 50.
- Sub-components. Goods: low oil prices, flat or falling manufacturing input prices, but selected meat price increases (Kashkari, Brainard, Mester, George), so mildly low. Services and housing: no inflation-specific discussion, so N/A. Expectations: Clarida says TIPS-implied inflation fell from about 2.5 to about 1.75 percent, Kashkari says 5y5y is 1.56 percent, and several fear drift lower, so below target and at risk of unanchoring.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- Labor: higher means tighter. Evidence says very slack, so the score is low. Matches.
- Inflation pressure: evidence says far below target, so the score is low. Matches.
- Credit spreads: higher means more stress. Rosengren says elevated, so it is above 50 even though others say improving.
- Equity: higher means more undervaluation or stress. Participants worry about overvaluation, so the score is below 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds at the effective lower bound (roughly $80 billion a month of Treasury purchases, unchanged guidance). Most discussion is about how to add more accommodation (QE, threshold guidance, yield curve control). Several want to act boldly and early (Daly, Kashkari, Brainard, Evans), while a minority sees further action as premature (Kaplan, Barkin, George, Bostic, Quarles). No one argues for tightening. This is strongly dovish.
- Dissent: unanimous vote, no dissents. There are differences on tactics: yield curve control, timing and form of guidance, wording of the asset-purchase paragraph (George, Barkin, Mester), and whether policy is accommodative at all (Kashkari, Williams, Evans). These are tactical rather than a split on the economics.
- Financial stability: prominent but not dominant. Topics include 13(3) facilities, credit flows, spreads, reach for yield, equity puzzlement and escape clauses, and Bullard's two goals of avoiding a financial crisis and a depression. The pandemic and the policy tools take most of the time.
- Forward guidance: a strongly easing signal. Powell: "highly accommodative" for "some time"; the dots mean rates low "through the end of 2022"; Bostic: "zero rates as far ahead as the eye can see". Explicit thresholds are not yet adopted, so it is not the most extreme score.
- Uncertainty: very high. Powell: "how little we know"; Clarida: uncertainty "remains extreme"; Brainard: "extraordinarily uncertain"; Mester, Barkin, Williams and others agree.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP below 50: ok.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (accommodative/easing language): ok.
- CREDIT_SPREADS above 50 (stated elevated level): ok.
- BANKING_STRESS below 50 (banks sound, source of strength): ok.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 (strong or elevated dollar): ok.
- TREASURY_STRESS in 36-45 (functioning restored, no dysfunction): ok.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 20 vs LABOR 16: within 15 points, fine.
- OUTPUT_GAP 20 vs CONSUMPTION 25: fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (eased) vs CREDIT_SPREADS 56 (modestly wide): they diverge slightly. This reflects participants' split: broad conditions are accommodative and markets buoyant (policy-supported), while Rosengren and Brainard note private spreads still above normal. That is why the spread score sits only just above 50 and the financial-conditions score only moderately below.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal-market functioning is consistent with eased conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 24 vs expectations 30 and goods 38: consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING are N/A because participants gave no services or shelter price commentary. Productivity gets a Low-confidence score because it was mentioned in passing by three participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kashkari: "Core PCE on a 12-month basis is only around 1 percent. April saw the largest one-month decline on record." Daly: "we have undeniably been hit by a deflationary downdraft that pushes us even further from our price-stability goal."
- Summary: well below target and still falling, but no outright price-level decline.
- Direction: inflation pressure is below neutral because core inflation is about 1 percent against a 2 percent goal.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 20 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "an output gap of at least 7 percent of GDP" at the end of 2020. Powell: staff projects "35 percent annualized shrinkage in the second quarter".
- Summary: deep gap, with recovery just beginning.
- Direction: below potential because activity collapsed and the gap persists into 2022.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "a 13.3 percent unemployment rate, near its postwar highs". Powell: "the true unemployment rate being close to 20 percent."
- Summary: massive slack, with the May rebound a fraction of the losses.
- Direction: below neutral because unemployment is far above full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 25 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Harker: card purchases up "1.5 percent" versus a year ago after the March-April decline. George: Michigan survey share saying it is a bad time to buy durables "soared to its highest level in the 70-year history of the survey".
- Summary: far below trend overall, with spending bouncing in the sectors that reopened and investment on hold.
- Direction: below trend because the Q2 collapse is only partly recovered and uncertainty is deferring investment.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 46 ±12 [Low]**
- Evidence: Barkin: "Productivity will likely be hurt by new health measures, like distancing in the workplace." George (contacts): staffing cuts "based on the productivity they've realized with fewer workers".
- Summary: passing and mixed remarks net to roughly trend.
- Direction: near neutral because the mentions pull in opposite directions.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "this easing of financial conditions is at least in part the direct consequence of economic policy decisions". Powell: policy should "promote and sustain highly accommodative financial conditions". Offset: Harker, "credit standards have indeed tightened".
- Summary: markets are buoyant and conditions much easier than in March, though bank lending is cautious.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because of the broad easing and supportive market tone.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "Both these markets have settled down. The problems created by panic selling are now behind us". Bostic: asset purchases "worked well this spring to improve the functioning in the Treasury securities market."
- Summary: past dysfunction has been repaired, with orderly trading and only yield moves now.
- Direction: below the elevated range, in normal-functioning territory.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 56 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "the spreads embedded in private-sector interest rates relative to Treasury rates are particularly large at present". Brainard: Fed backstop "is credited with supporting the record level of issuance in corporate bond markets".
- Summary: spreads are still above normal but narrowing, with open issuance.
- Direction: above neutral because the one explicit level statement is elevated; the improvement language only describes direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "market participants expressed some puzzlement over the buoyant tone in equity markets". Bowman: equity prices "seem out of step with realistic revenue expectations".
- Summary: several participants see stretched valuations, with Kashkari the main dissent, tying prices to a low r*.
- Direction: valuations are viewed as above fair value, so the stress/undervaluation score is below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "banks will continue to be a source of strength" and credit is flowing under existing lines, but "business loans have leveled off at smaller banks". Clarida: "illiquidity" may "morph into a problem of insolvency".
- Summary: the system is sound, but there are growing worries about new lending and later credit losses.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy, tempered by lending pullback concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: George: "the elevated level of the foreign exchange value of the dollar". Kashkari: "low oil prices and the relatively strong dollar".
- Summary: brief mentions of a strong dollar as a headwind and a drag on prices.
- Direction: above neutral because the dollar is described as strong or elevated.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38** (value only). Evidence: Kashkari, "flat and falling input prices in manufacturing"; Mester, selected beef, poultry and pork price increases. Mildly low, with some food upside.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** (not discussed).

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** (not discussed).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 30** (value only). Evidence: Clarida, TIPS-implied expectations "down from about 2½ percent several months ago" to about 1¾ percent; Kashkari, 5y5y "only around 1.56 percent". Below target with a risk of drifting lower.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 18 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Daly: "we should err on the side of doing more and doing it boldly and earlier". Powell: "I see some appeal in the familiarity of an open-ended purchase program".
- Summary: policy is held at the lower bound with large ongoing purchases, and the debate is about how much more easing to add.
- Direction: strongly dovish, and I did not go lower because there was no immediate new easing action and a minority urged waiting.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 26 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: all 10 voters "Yes". George: "the language in paragraph 5 may leave too much ambiguity"; Barkin: the purchase memo "might be a bit premature"; Kashkari: "I don't think we're providing much accommodation".
- Summary: unanimous, with real but tactical differences on yield curve control, timing and form of guidance, and asset-purchase wording.
- Direction: above the near-consensus band because several participants voice reservations, yet below moderate because they all support the statement and none dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Bullard: goals include to "support the financial sector to mitigate the risk of a financial crisis". Mester: "I would consider financial stability escape clauses in threshold forward guidance."
- Summary: many address credit, asset prices, 13(3) facilities and reach-for-yield risks, but it is secondary to the pandemic and the policy tools.
- Direction: moderate-to-prominent attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 10 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Bostic: "the modal case to be zero rates as far ahead as the eye can see". Powell: "keep policy at the effective lower bound until a particular rate of inflation is achieved" has appeal.
- Summary: a strong, extended-accommodation signal, with threshold guidance being discussed but not yet adopted.
- Direction: well toward easing; I kept it above the explicit-threshold extreme because the statement's guidance is unchanged.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 86 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "we continue to be reminded of just how little we know and how much uncertainty there is". Brainard: "the forward path of the virus and the economy remain extraordinarily uncertain."
- Summary: nearly every participant stresses extreme, scenario-dependent uncertainty.
- Direction: very high on the uncertainty scale.
