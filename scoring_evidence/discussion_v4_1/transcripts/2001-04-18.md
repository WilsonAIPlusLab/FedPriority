# FOMC member-discussion scoring (v4.1) - transcript discussion, meeting 2001-04-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It is not a standard go-around: it is a single-round policy discussion of one proposal (a 1/2 point funds rate cut, with the "balance of risks weighted toward economic weakness" language kept). The Chairman opens with a short data summary, then each member comments in turn, then there is a vote read-out and a short exchange on the press statement. Staff replies (Q&A on trade data, GDP estimate, directive, press release text) are removed, so some member questions have no visible answer. Total length is only a few thousand words, so the economic content is thin, and Financial/inflation dimensions are mostly not covered.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2001, after a sharp slowdown in activity, an inventory correction, falling capital spending and equity earnings estimates, and a series of rate cuts. I note this but score only what the text says.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (proposes the cut; data summary), McDonough (supports; world economy weaker), Minehan (supports; consumer erosion), Parry (supports; weakening District, real funds rate high), Hoenig (supports; growth 1 to 1.5% vs potential of 3% or better; "split about 50/50" on whether the bottom is in; caution about going too far), Stern (supports; below potential for several quarters; careful how far to push), Guynn (supports; be ready to reverse), McTeer (supports; no worry about over-stimulus), Santomero (supports; consumer and capex concern), Moskow (supports; must be ready to act "decisively on the up side"), Pianalto (supports; District not deteriorating, cautious outlook), Meyer (supports but calls the intermeeting timing "a mistake, though not a big mistake"), Poole ("I do not favor the move", but votes yes with reservations), Ferguson (mixed data; "too early to call a bottom"; risks to the downside), Kelley (supports; shares timing concern), Gramlich (supports; economy too weak, unemployment forecast rising to 5.2%).

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants barely discuss U.S. inflation. Hoenig cites "yesterday's CPI numbers and the related outlook on prices" as supporting a lower rate (benign), Parry notes a high real funds rate given consensus CPI estimates, Moskow speaks only of acting up "if and when inflationary pressures become excessive" (a contingent, not a current concern). Participants see inflation as somewhat below to near the midpoint, not a worry. Weak evidence: ~43, wide band, Low confidence.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as below potential: Hoenig, Q1 growth "only 1 percent or maybe 1-1/2 percent ... against a potential of 3 percent or better"; Stern, "grow below potential for at least several more quarters"; Gramlich, "the economy is too weak." Score below 50 (bright line satisfied). Magnitude "somewhat to considerably": ~36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see labor as loosening: Ferguson says job-loss figures were "a surprise"; Gramlich says a forecast unemployment rate of 5.2% by Q4 is "just too high." Level of unemployment is not stated for now, but rising toward 5.2% implies roughly 4.5% to 5%. Near-NAIRU to slightly slack: ~44, Low-Moderate confidence.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Weak and weakening: consumer confidence lower (Greenspan), consumers "may be pulling back" (Ferguson), consumption "has lost all its momentum ... close to zero in the second quarter" (Meyer), capex "weakening" (Greenspan, Santomero, Pianalto "curtailed or put on hold"). Offsets: housing/construction "still quite strong" (Ferguson, flattening), some retailers and restaurants not cutting plans (Moskow). Net below trend, not collapsing: ~33.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed in the statement review. Greenspan: "very little to suggest any significant change in the rise of long-term structural productivity"; measured Q1 productivity "probably weakened" and "could very well be a small negative"; Minehan doubts confidence in the structural trend. Structural trend perceived intact at a good pace, measured productivity weak, net roughly at trend: 50, wide band, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Almost no discussion of credit availability. Stock prices "significantly higher this morning" (Greenspan, McDonough on Intel/TI), Parry says the real funds rate is "rather high" relative to a weak economy, Pianalto wants policy "not contractionary." Hoenig's "more accommodative stance" describes policy, not conditions. Nothing says conditions are tight or easy. Neutral with wide band, Low confidence: 49. (Bright-line: no "accommodative/tight" statement about conditions, so no violation.)
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A (not discussed; Poole's one-year bond remark is an arithmetic point about expectations, not market functioning).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A (not discussed).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity moves are mentioned only as price moves/rationale for the market reaction (higher on Intel/TI, benign equity futures, earnings estimates falling). No valuation commentary, so default 45-55 range; included at Low confidence: 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A (no exchange rate discussion).
- Inflation sub-components: all N/A (no goods/services/housing/expectations discussion).

**5. Polarity verification.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50: higher = more above potential; evidence says growth well below potential, so below 50 is right. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter; evidence is loosening toward 5.2% unemployment, so mildly below/around 50 is consistent. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: higher = stronger; evidence says weak, so low. All match.

**6. Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: All ten voters approve a 50 bp intermeeting cut, the balance-of-risks language stays tilted to weakness, Ferguson says the market may be right that rates must go "even lower." Offsetting: Stern, Guynn, Hoenig, Moskow all say to be careful about how far to push and ready to reverse; Poole opposes the timing. Strong-to-moderate dovish with some hedging: ~24.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero formal dissents (10 Yes). Two clear reservations about the intermeeting timing (Meyer: "a mistake, though not a big mistake"; Poole: "I do not favor the move" but votes yes "with the reservations that I expressed before"), plus mild concern from Kelley and Ferguson on timing. The disagreement is tactical (timing/communication), not about economics. Unanimous vote with 2 real reservations: ~30.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: No financial stability discussion. Some attention to market reaction, equity moves and communication/volatility (Poole, Meyer). Brief mentions only: ~12.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The retained weakness-weighted risk language implies further easing possible; Meyer says the market expects another 50 to 100 bp; Poole asks whether the market will expect a May 15 cut; Ferguson says lower rates may be needed. Counterweight: Stern/Guynn/Hoenig/Moskow emphasize caution and readiness to reverse. Mild-to-moderate easing bias: ~22.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Mixed data, "too early to call a bottom" (Ferguson), District views split "50/50" on whether the bottom is in (Hoenig), "simply a forecast" (Stern), unclear what rate level supports the expected recovery (Ferguson), Greenspan warning not to express "some extraordinary degree of uncertainty" in the statement. Moderately elevated but ordinary for a downturn, mostly rhetorical-to-genuine mix: ~42.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 on "below potential" language: ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 49, no bright-line trigger. CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, no rule triggered.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs LABOR 44: diverge by 8, within 15 (labor starts from a low unemployment level, ~4.5%, so slack there is milder). OUTPUT_GAP 36 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 33: close. FC vs CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads N/A. FC vs TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Inflation parent vs sub-components: sub-components N/A.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, and the four inflation sub-components were not raised by participants. Note that the discussion is a short intermeeting-style session without a full economic go-around, so the thin evidence for INFLATION_PRESSURE, FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS, PRODUCTIVITY, EQUITY_VALUATION and LABOR_TIGHTNESS is flagged with Low or Low-to-Moderate confidence.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "given yesterday's CPI numbers and the related outlook on prices, I think the lower rate is appropriate"; Moskow, "act just as decisively on the up side if and when inflationary pressures become excessive."
- Summary: Inflation is mentioned only as a permissive background to easing.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral, because the CPI and price outlook are cited as supporting a cut and no one sees price pressure as a current problem.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "growing at only 1 percent or maybe 1-1/2 percent ... against a potential of 3 percent or better"; Stern, "the economy is likely to grow below potential for at least several more quarters."
- Summary: Growth is well below potential and is expected to stay below for several quarters.
- Direction: Below neutral because participants repeatedly describe growth below potential and a too-weak economy.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Gramlich, forecast "has the unemployment rate rising to 5.2 percent by the fourth quarter. I think that's just too high"; Ferguson, job loss figures "were a surprise."
- Summary: Labor market is loosening from a roughly full-employment level.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because unemployment is rising toward 5.2%, which members regard as too high.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Meyer, "consumption has not been well maintained; it has lost all its momentum. It is going to be close to zero in the second quarter"; Pianalto, capital spending plans "have been curtailed or put on hold."
- Summary: Consumer momentum is gone and capex is being cut; housing and some retail and dining plans are partial offsets.
- Direction: Below neutral because spending and investment are soft and weakening.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "very little to suggest any significant change in the rise of long-term structural productivity"; Greenspan, measured productivity "could very well be a small negative."
- Summary: Structural trend viewed as intact, measured Q1 weak; nets to about trend.
- Direction: At neutral because the strong structural view and weak measured data offset.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 49 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "Stock prices ... are significantly higher this morning"; Parry, "the real funds rate is rather high now relative to where we might want it to be."
- Summary: No real discussion of credit conditions; offsetting hints (higher equities vs high real policy rate).
- Direction: At neutral because nothing says conditions are tight or easy.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** - Not discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A** - Not discussed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: McDonough, today's equity rise "is pinned basically on both Intel and Texas Instruments saying that life is not quite as grim"; Greenspan, earnings estimates "continue to fall."
- Summary: Equity moves are noted as price news without valuation commentary.
- Direction: At neutral because there is no valuation concern either way (default for price moves without valuation view).

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** - Not discussed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** - Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A** - Not discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "a 1/2 percentage point reduction in the funds rate and maintain the language indicating that the balance of risks is weighted toward economic weakness"; Ferguson, "we'll have to go even lower on the funds rate before we've reached the end of this easing process."
- Summary: A large intermeeting cut is supported by all voters, with caution about reversing later tempering the dovish lean.
- Direction: Dovish because the Committee is easing aggressively and sees further downside risks.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Poole, "I do not favor the move ... I voted yes, but with the reservations that I expressed before"; Meyer, "I think it's a mistake, though not a big mistake."
- Summary: Unanimous vote, two explicit reservations on timing and communication, no disagreement on the economic assessment.
- Direction: Low-to-moderate disagreement because there are no formal dissents and the differences concern tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 12 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole, the move will "increase market uncertainty and market volatility"; McDonough on equity markets rising on Intel/TI.
- Summary: Only brief references to market reaction; no financial stability discussion.
- Direction: Low attention to financial stability or conditions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan keeps balance of risks "weighted toward economic weakness"; Meyer, markets expect "another 50 to 100 basis point decline in the funds rate between now and the middle of the year"; Stern/Guynn, "we need to be ready to reverse this easing."
- Summary: Weakness-weighted risk language and market expectations of more cuts, tempered by calls for readiness to reverse.
- Direction: Easing bias because further cuts are signaled and expected, with limited tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson, "it is too early to call a bottom"; Hoenig, views "split about 50/50 between those who feel we're at a bottom ... and those who are still expecting some further weakening."
- Summary: Typical downturn uncertainty about the bottom and the right rate level, without extreme language.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated because the data are mixed and members disagree on whether a bottom is in.
