# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 2013-07-31 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion with staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) a short opening block with the reverse-repo facility discussion and questions on the economic situation; (b) the economic go-around on day one; (c) a policy go-around on day two about the draft statement (alternatives A, B, C, paragraphs 2, 4 and 5), the Chairman's three questions (plan for asset purchases, putting it in the statement, forward guidance on rates), and the drafting of the statement; (d) a vote read-out. The operational reverse-repo talk and the statement drafting are procedure and are not scored except where they reveal views on conditions or policy. The text reads cleanly. A few staff-answer gaps leave short fragments, such as lone "Yes" turns. None of them affects scoring.

**2. Era context.** The Fed is in its large-scale asset purchase (QE) program at $85 billion a month, with a rate threshold of 6.5 percent unemployment. The Committee is working out how to signal a later reduction in purchases. Long-term yields have jumped since the June meeting. The text itself says the unemployment rate is 7.6 percent, core PCE inflation is about 1 to 1.1 percent, and growth is about 2 percent with a weak first half.

**3. Speakers.** Bernanke chairs. The main voices are Dudley, Yellen, Powell, Stein, Tarullo, Raskin, Duke and the Reserve Bank presidents. Lacker, Bullard, Fisher, Plosser, George and Pianalto lean toward reducing purchases or toward minimal communication. Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Dudley and Yellen worry about premature tightening. Williams, Lockhart and Powell support the plan and want few changes.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as clearly below target. They cite core PCE near 1 to 1.1 percent, "disturbingly low" (Evans), and "lowest three-year average in half a century" (Kocherlakota). Most expect a transitory-factor rebound, but "transitory" does not reduce the score. The score is well below 50, at about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as below potential. Growth is about 2 percent, the unemployment rate is 7.6 percent, and "economic growth remains very slow" (Raskin). Substantial slack remains, and a second-half pickup is only a forecast ("forecast of hope", Evans). The score is about 35.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as slack. The unemployment rate is 7.6 percent, with part-time work for economic reasons, a low employment-to-population ratio, and Dudley's view that "the labor market is still quite weak". Payrolls of about 200,000 a month are the offset. The score sits in the 16-30 band, at about 28.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as below trend. Consumer spending is "pretty sluggish" outside autos (Evans), and Powell says the spending narrative was "badly damaged". Autos and housing are strong, and sentiment is up. The net is about 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: This is discussed. Dudley calls productivity growth "terrible", Yellen says it was negative in the first half, and Lacker says it is below 1 percent. The score is well below trend, about 25.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: This is mixed. Evans says conditions "have become more restrictive than they have been for quite a while". Dudley says they "have tightened considerably since May". The Chairman's summary says conditions "have become more restrictive, which is a concern". Against that, Fisher, Williams, George, Stein and Duke point to rising equities and house prices, easier SLOOS lending standards, and levels that "feel kind of reasonable". The tightening is a move in rates and term premiums from very easy levels, with credit supply still positive. Given the explicit "restrictive" language from the Chair and several voting members, I score slightly above 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yield movements are not dysfunction. There are references to a dealer survey saying market functioning "deteriorated somewhat" and to lower liquidity (Fisher). Powell describes a "crush at the exit door" in bond funds and in MBS. This is mostly agency MBS and collateral scarcity, not Treasury auction or settlement failure. The score is near 50 with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Fisher says triple-C credits are "back to trading below 6 percent", spreads have narrowed, and some of the credit market is "frothy". BAA yields are up about 70 basis points, which is a direction. Members stress that credit is ample (SLOOS, Williams on lending standards). The level reading is somewhat below normal, so the score is below 50 (about 40) under the level rule.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity prices are up. The Chairman says the wealth-to-income ratio is "above long-run norms", and Yellen warns "paper wealth gains" could prove transitory. There is no explicit valuation alarm. This is a default-to-neutral score, slightly below 50 for the stretched-wealth remarks, with Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are healthy. Duke says they are "well capitalized, overflowing with liquidity, and anxious to lend", and Bullard says banks are "substantially healthier". The score is well below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. The dollar and exchange rates are not discussed. Fisher's "dollar weekly flow" refers to fund flows.

**5. Polarity checks.** Banking: healthy means low stress, so the score is below 50, which matches. Credit spreads: higher means more stress, and spreads are below normal, so the score is below 50, which matches. Financial conditions: higher means tighter, and many members say tightened, so the score is slightly above 50, which matches. Output gap: slack means below 50, which matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The decision is to hold at $85 billion a month, with a contingent plan to reduce purchases starting in September, a 7 percent unemployment stopping marker, and the 6.5 percent rate threshold unchanged. The majority is "broadly comfortable" with the taper plan, but wants minimal change now. George wants an explicit signal now. Pianalto, Lacker, Bullard (hold, but he is uncomfortable with the plan) and Plosser want to reduce purchases or are skeptical of QE. Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Dudley and Yellen worry about tightening too soon. QE is still running at $85 billion, so the stance is accommodative and the lean is dovish to balanced.
- Dissent: George votes No on the read-out. There are no other formal dissents. Real differences exist over the statement language, the 7 percent marker, whether to change forward guidance, and Bullard's discomfort with the plan. The Chairman says "the consensus was to chill".
- Financial stability: This is prominent. It covers the post-June rate move, Stein's "recruitment" and financial-stability talk, Powell on exit risk, George's concern about a rapid rise in rates being destabilizing, Fisher on froth, and the reverse-repo money-market discussion.
- Forward guidance: The forward signal is to reduce purchases "later this year" if the data cooperate, and the stance remains highly accommodative. Rate guidance is unchanged, and "considerable period" before any rate rise is stressed. Several members want to clarify a reaction function below the 6.5 percent threshold, and none argues for tighter guidance. The signal is a mild easing-leaning taper plan.
- Uncertainty: Many participants stress uncertainty. Evans says "considerable room for doubt", Tarullo says "clarity seems less likely", Lockhart has "anxiety about the balance of risks", and there is uncertainty over the Fed's reaction function, fiscal timing, and sequestration effects.

**7. Bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: "below"/"slack" language, so the score is below 50. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: the "restrictive" language gives above 50; no member says "accommodative" or "easy" as the overall verdict on conditions. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: the stated level is "frothy"/"narrow", so below 50. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: "well capitalized", so below 50. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction in Treasuries, so the score is not above 60. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28) differ by 7, which is within tolerance.
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40) differ by 5, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (52) and CREDIT_SPREADS (40) differ by 12 and point in different directions. The explanation is that the tightening came through long-term rates and term premiums, with spreads from a compressed base and lending standards still easing, so the two measures differ in level.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury stress is near the normal range, consistent with a mild tightening.
- Parent inflation versus sub-components: services (33) and expectations (46) are consistent with a parent score of 30.

**9. N/A justification.**
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed.
- INFLATION_GOODS: only gasoline and the blend wall are raised, as energy; there is no goods-price assessment, and Fisher's "keeps a lid on price pressures" refers to consumer behavior generally.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: members discuss house prices and sales, not rents or shelter inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-5 (High)
- Evidence: Evans: "core PCE inflation will increase a bit from its current disturbingly low level". Kocherlakota: "PCE core inflation has averaged less than 1½ percent per year over the past three years."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is clearly below target and expected to recover only slowly.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because members report core PCE near 1 percent and a small recovery expected.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +-6 (High)
- Evidence: Raskin: "economic growth remains very slow." Evans: "There is nothing really tangible in the data at the moment that points to an acceleration in economic activity."
- Center of gravity: The economy runs below potential, with a hoped-for pickup that is not yet visible.
- Direction confirmation: The output gap is below neutral because slack remains and growth is about 2 percent.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-5 (High)
- Evidence: Dudley: "the labor market is still quite weak." Raskin: "Labor market conditions ... are still far from normal or acceptable."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment is 7.6 percent with broad underutilization, even though payroll gains are steady.
- Direction confirmation: The labor market is below neutral tightness because of the high unemployment rate and weak participation and employment-to-population measures.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-6 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Evans: "outside of autos, consumer spending is looking pretty sluggish". Powell: "the spending aspect of it has been badly damaged".
- Center of gravity: Spending is soft apart from autos and housing, below trend overall.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is below trend because most members describe soft consumption, partly offset by strength in autos and housing.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 25 +-7 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Dudley: "Productivity growth has been terrible." Yellen: "labor productivity growth was actually negative during the first half of the year".
- Center of gravity: Productivity is seen as running far below trend, with an expected but uncertain rebound.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is below neutral because of reported negative growth.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 +-8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Evans: "financial conditions have become more restrictive than they have been for quite a while." Fisher: "according to the SLOOS, we're seeing bankers a little more liberal with credit, and financial conditions there haven't tightened."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are viewed as having tightened through rates, balanced by easy credit, rising equity and house prices.
- Direction confirmation: Financial conditions are slightly above neutral because several members and the Chairman's summary describe tighter, more restrictive conditions, partly offset by supportive credit and asset prices.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 48 +-8 (Low)
- Evidence: Fisher: dealers "view market functioning conditions as having deteriorated somewhat ... higher volatility and a reduction of liquidity in agency MBS". Powell: "Investors took their money out of bond funds, and there was a crush at the exit door."
- Center of gravity: There was volatility and some liquidity thinning, mostly in MBS, with no Treasury dysfunction.
- Direction confirmation: Treasury stress is near neutral because yield moves and some thinner liquidity are not dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-8 (Low)
- Evidence: Fisher: "triple-C credits are back to trading below 6 percent, and the spreads have narrowed." Stein: levels of rates and spreads "feel kind of reasonable and grounded in fundamentals".
- Center of gravity: Spreads widened after June from compressed levels but are described as narrow or frothy at the level.
- Direction confirmation: Credit spreads are below neutral because the stated level is narrow or frothy, not wide, and credit is available.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-7 (Low)
- Evidence: George: "Equity prices have risen." Chairman: "The wealth-to-income ratio is close to 6, which is above long-run norms."
- Center of gravity: Equity strength is noted, with a mild "stretched wealth" caveat and no valuation alarm.
- Direction confirmation: Equity concern is roughly neutral to slightly below, because the discussion is about price gains, not stress.

### BANKING_STRESS: 26 +-5 (High)
- Evidence: Duke: "Banks are well capitalized, overflowing with liquidity, and anxious to lend." Bullard: "District banks are substantially healthier than they were one year ago."
- Center of gravity: Banks are healthy and lending, with tight standards in mortgages and cards as the only caveat.
- Direction confirmation: Banking stress is below neutral because members describe banks as well capitalized and healthy.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- Not discussed by participants.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
- Gasoline and oil appear only as energy; there is no goods-price assessment.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 33
- Evidence: Yellen: "The two key components of core PCE prices that have shown unusually low readings lately are medical services prices and the nonmarket component of the PCE."
- Single-speaker evidence, so this is a rough value below neutral.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- House prices and sales are discussed; rents and shelter inflation are not.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46
- Evidence: Rosengren: "well-anchored expectations will draw inflation back toward the Fed's 2 percent goal". Lockhart: "Either inflation numbers have to firm or inflation expectations should adjust downward."
- Expectations are stable, with a slight downside tilt.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 36 +-7 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Yellen: a reduction at the September meeting "is not baked in the cake but is instead contingent". George wants "a more explicit signal that the pace of asset purchases will be reduced this year, as early as September".
- Center of gravity: The Committee holds at $85 billion a month with a contingent taper plan most members accept, and prefers minimal statement change.
- Direction confirmation: The lean is dovish to balanced because QE continues and tightening-too-soon worries dominate, tempered by broad acceptance of a later reduction.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 +-7 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President George No" with all others Yes. Bullard: "I'm not comfortable with the plan". Bernanke's summary: "the consensus was to chill and to do minimal language changes".
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent and clear differences on tactics, with broad convergence on the day's action.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is moderate because there is one dissent and visible camps on communication and the pace of tapering.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Stein: "the interplay of monetary policy and financial stability ... is the tradeoff between ... having rates very, very low versus ... an elevated probability of a sharp widening of rates and spreads". Powell: "the process of exit itself will provoke a sharp and damaging tightening in financial conditions".
- Center of gravity: The post-June rate move and the risks of exit and market dynamics occupy a large share of the discussion.
- Direction confirmation: Emphasis is above the moderate band because several participants discuss it at length, though the macro assessment remains the main subject.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 17 +-6 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Yellen: "it will still be a considerable time before we begin to remove policy accommodation". Chairman: "the end of the asset purchase program does not mean the proximate increase in rates".
- Center of gravity: The forward signal is a measured taper later this year with rates held low well beyond, and many members would consider strengthening rate guidance.
- Direction confirmation: The tone is below neutral because the signal stresses extended accommodation, even with the purchase-reduction plan.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate)
- Evidence: Tarullo: "clarity seems less likely in the next few months than I would have thought". Evans: "there is considerable room for doubt; this pickup is just a forecast."
- Center of gravity: Many members stress forecast uncertainty, fiscal timing and the Fed's reaction function, without Knightian language.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is elevated because multiple participants describe unusual difficulty in reading the outlook.
