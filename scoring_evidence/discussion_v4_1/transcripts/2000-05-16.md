# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 + V4.1.1) - meeting 2000-05-16 (transcript, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, read to the last line (the vote read-out is at the end). Staff turns are removed, so staff answers to member questions are missing; only the members' questions and statements survive. The file holds: a short Desk/swap-renewal exchange at the start (with a formal dissent by Mr. Broaddus on renewing the Mexican swap line), questions on the Greenbook, the economic go-around (all Reserve Bank presidents, First Vice President Stone, governors), the Lindsey policy briefing and Q&A, the Chairman's recommendation, the policy go-around, and the roll call. It is a normal policy meeting, not special-topic: there is a stance go-around and a monetary policy vote. Not special-topic under V4.1.1 rule 1.

**Era context.** Spring 2000: very strong growth and tight labor markets after the earlier gradual tightening, with a stock market that has become volatile. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.** Hawkish voices: Broaddus, Guynn, Parry, Meyer, Kelley, Gramlich, McDonough, Moskow. Gradualists: Hoenig, McTeer, Ferguson, Stone (more cautious on how aggressive). Skeptics on the inflation threat: Stern (uncertain; labor-slack variables do not forecast inflation since 1984), Greenspan (unit costs falling, productivity accelerating, "little evidence of a pickup" in underlying inflation, yet recommends 50bp). Jordan wants to move 50bp and says a pause is near.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: members see inflation as rising from a low level, "the beginning of a pickup", core CPI 12-month about 2.2-2.3%, core PCE 1.8-1.9% and rising, the ECI jumping, firms trying to raise prices. Many call it "worrisome", "red flag", "inflection point". Greenspan sees only a margin-driven rise. Above 2% target and rising but not broad-based acceleration. Modestly to clearly above: 62.
- OUTPUT_GAP: "unsustainable growth in demand", "large imbalance between the levels of actual and potential output" (Meyer), capacity utilization 81.4% the highest in two years (Ferguson), "demand growing faster than supply" (Stone). Clearly above potential: 68. Bright-line (>50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "exceptionally tight", "extremely tight" (Guynn), "near impossibility of finding labor" (Minehan), unemployment at all-time lows, 1.9% in Columbus IN, "lowest unemployment rate since 1970" (McDonough), Hoenig's district about 3%. Very tight, near-unprecedented in anecdotes: 82.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumption "red-hot" (Moskow), investment bounced back "considerable strength" (Guynn), "unbelievable domestic demand" (Poole). Some tentative moderation signs in housing, autos, retail sales (Hoenig, McTeer, Jordan), but evidence called marginal. Well above trend: 73.
- PRODUCTIVITY: discussed at length. Greenspan: "productivity ... is still accelerating" and rejects the 1.5% staff first-quarter number (his profits-based estimate is 6%); Jordan: "productivity still is helping us out"; Gramlich: first-quarter productivity "mildly disappointing"; Kelley: productivity "continues to hold unit cost increases at bay". Above trend on balance: 68.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Tightening evidence: corporate bond rates "up noticeably", issuance cut back, bank loan terms tightening (Ferguson), construction financing harder (Stern), banks tightening credit standards (McDonough), stock decline and "greater stringency in credit markets" (Lindsey), real long rates up (Greenspan). Counter: Poole sees "no real restraint ... as yet". Somewhat tight: 57. No accommodative/easy language is used as a description of markets, so the bright-line is not engaged.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only touched via the Treasury buyback/on-the-run-off-the-run discussion (Greenspan: liquidity premium "not altogether desirable", Jordan: Treasury market "difficult to interpret"). No dysfunction described. Normal functioning: 42, Low.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No explicit spread level is stated. Ferguson/Greenspan give corporate bond yields up and issuance down, loan terms and availability tightening, a shift from bonds to bank loans/commercial paper. That is availability-based (V4.1.1 rule 3), so Low confidence and UNC at least 10. Mild tightening of availability: 56.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation view stated: Poole, "the stock market is still very elevated by historical standards", volatility "unprecedented", "shaky". Also reports of a decline (Stern, McTeer, Minehan). Elevated valuation concern on the table (stretched/rich band, with fragility): 32.
- BANKING_STRESS: McDonough: banks tightening standards and delinquency rates improving ("speeches ... for bankers to be prudent may just be having some effect"); Ferguson: bank conditions tightening. System seen as sound, brief. Below 50: 36, Low.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Kelley: "the dollar remains remarkably strong"; Gramlich: current account deficits "without the depreciation of the dollar". Strong, not newly moving: 62, Low. Bright-line (>50 for strength) satisfied.
- Inflation sub-components (value only): goods (input costs, plastics, paper, energy surcharges, but pass-through limited) 58; services (amusement park admissions, insurance premiums, hospital wages) 62; housing (Bay Area home prices up 25%, NYC rents and selling prices pushed up) 66; expectations (split: Broaddus, Gramlich, Stone on one-year and TIPS spread rising vs long-run surveys stable, Ferguson sees no increase) 58.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Chairman recommends 50bp (up from 25bp) with asymmetric (inflation-tilted) language; nearly all endorse it, with Hoenig and McTeer preferring 25bp, Jordan wanting to be near a pause. Strong hawkish step, with some gradualist reservations: 76.
- Dissent: The roll call on the policy action shows every listed voter Yes (10 names listed, all Yes; Stone, who spoke as alternate, is not on the read-out lines, so I flag the read-out as possibly incomplete). Formal dissent exists only on the swap renewal (Broaddus, a non-policy vote). Reservations: Hoenig ("1/4 point seems right"), McTeer ("would have preferred 1/4"), Jordan on language, Stern on inflation uncertainty and Poole/Minehan disagreement on stock-market risk. No dissent on the stance and agreement on direction: 28.
- Financial stability emphasis: Stock market vulnerability (Poole), credit tightening, IPO market, emerging-market worries (McDonough), market expectation management dominate parts of the policy debate. Moderate: 38.
- Forward guidance: Asymmetric language retained, "rates are going to have to continue to rise" (Poole), with caveats on how far (Jordan, Ferguson, McDonough). Tightening bias: 76.
- Uncertainty: Stern "more uncertain than usual" on inflation; Lindsey and Greenspan on structural change, NAIRU and productivity; many "don't know how far". Moderate-elevated rather than extreme: 47.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 68 (>50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 (no accommodative/tight conflict). CREDIT_SPREADS 56 (availability-based, direction of the move; no stated "narrow/low" level; V4.1.1 availability rule). BANKING_STRESS 36 (<50, sound). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 (>50). TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 68 vs LABOR 82: diverge by 14, within 15; labor anecdotes are more extreme than the output-gap language. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION 73: within 5. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 56: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: normal. INFLATION_PRESSURE 62 vs sub-components (58, 62, 66, 58, average about 61): consistent.

**N/A justification.** None of the 15 Schema A dimensions is N/A. Treasury stress is scored (Low) because the Treasury market is discussed directly, though not as stress.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 62 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "recent data certainly have raised a red flag about inflation ... the previously established downward trend has ended at a level above price stability"; Meyer, "we are seeing ... the beginning of a pickup in inflation rates for both compensation and core consumer prices". Summary: rising, modestly-to-clearly above target. Direction: above neutral because most members see an upward drift, though Greenspan sees no underlying deterioration.

**OUTPUT_GAP 68 +-7 High.** Evidence: Meyer, "a large imbalance between the levels of actual and potential output"; McDonough, "the unsustainable growth in demand". Summary: clearly above potential. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 82 +-6 High.** Evidence: Guynn, "They now have to be characterized as extremely tight"; Minehan, "the near impossibility of finding labor". Summary: near-unprecedented tightness across districts. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 73 +-7 High.** Evidence: Moskow, "the consumption sector remained red-hot through the first quarter"; Guynn, "the signs point to considerable strength". Summary: well above trend with only tentative interest-sensitive softening. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 68 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "productivity ... is still accelerating"; Gramlich, the first-quarter change "mildly disappointing". Summary: members see strong underlying gains, with one soft quarterly print. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Ferguson, "corporate bond rates have increased noticeably ... conditions on both loan rates and terms or availability do seem to be tightening"; Poole, "There has been no real restriction of monetary policy taking place". Summary: somewhat tighter, with disagreement on its extent. Direction: above neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS 42 +-12 Low.** Evidence: Greenspan on the on-the-run/off-the-run liquidity premium being "desirable" to narrow; Jordan, "the Treasury market is difficult to interpret". Summary: orderly market, buyback context only. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 56 +-12 Low.** Evidence (rests on availability and yields, not an explicit spread): Ferguson, "issuance of corporate debt has also been cut back ... forcing borrowers back to banks"; Lindsey, "the recent greater stringency in credit markets". Summary: mild tightening of credit availability. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 32 +-10 Moderate.** Evidence: Poole, "the stock market is still very elevated by historical standards ... The market is shaky"; Stern, "some of the air has gone out of the NASDAQ". Summary: valuations seen as elevated, with fragility. Direction: elevated-valuation side.

**BANKING_STRESS 36 +-10 Low.** Evidence: McDonough, "further tightening in credit standards and continued improvement in delinquency rates"; Ferguson on bank loan terms tightening. Summary: sound banks being prudent. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Kelley, "the dollar remains remarkably strong"; Gramlich, "current account deficits without the depreciation of the dollar". Summary: dollar seen as strong. Direction: above neutral.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 58, SERVICES 62, HOUSING 66, EXPECTATIONS 58 (value only; evidence above).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 76 +-6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan, "move the funds rate up 50 basis points, remain asymmetric"; McDonough, "we are not in the 'sweaty hands' period but in the 'cool heads' period". Summary: step-up to a 50bp hike with a tightening bias, tempered by gradualist voices (Hoenig, McTeer). Direction: strongly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 28 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Roll call of listed voters all Yes; Hoenig, "1/4 point with asymmetric language seems most appropriate"; McTeer, "I would have preferred 1/4 point, I can also agree to 1/2"; Broaddus dissent on the swap renewal. Summary: unanimous policy outcome with a few size/language reservations, plus one non-policy dissent. Direction: low-to-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 38 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Poole, "It is not going to help us if the market cracks"; McDonough on emerging-market stress. Summary: several members address markets and credit, but the meeting is about demand and inflation. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 76 +-7 High.** Evidence: Poole, "given what we know today, we believe that rates are going to have to continue to rise"; Gramlich, "I still think we have to go 50 basis points, with asymmetric language". Summary: inflation-tilted balance-of-risks language and expectation of further hikes, with Jordan resisting a June presumption. Direction: tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 47 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Stern, "I continue to be uncertain, perhaps even more uncertain than usual"; Lindsey, "the surprisingly favorable inflation and output performance ... has fostered a greater sense of uncertainty about economic relationships". Summary: several members stress uncertainty about how far to go and structural change, while many are confident on direction. Direction: elevated but not extreme.
