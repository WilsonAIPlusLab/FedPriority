# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 -- transcript discussion, meeting 2007-05-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It holds the members' questions to staff (staff answers removed), the economic go-around (Yellen through Mishkin), the Chairman's summary, a short second round in which Minehan, Kohn, Warsh and Kroszner speak after the break, the policy go-around (a hold at 5 1/4 percent, alternative B, with a long argument over statement wording), a discussion of the communications "dry run" on projections, and a vote read-out. The text has no PDF-extraction damage that affects meaning. Some staff-answer turns are missing, so a few of the Chairman's and members' remarks refer to answers that are not shown. I score only what members say.

**2. Era context.** Spring 2007. The funds rate has been 5 1/4 percent for many months. Growth is soft and housing is weak. Members worry about subprime, and inflation is somewhat above the comfort level. I score what the text says, not what came later.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Yellen: sluggish Q1, housing a continued drag, a fairly tight labor market, inflation expected to edge below 2 percent, policy "somewhat restrictive."
- Moskow: inflation risks dominate; he sees no meaningful slack in the projections.
- Fisher: bearish on growth, with inflation pressures from firms' pricing.
- Poole: soft anecdotes, with stock market and weaker dollar support.
- Minehan: inflation acceleration is the larger risk, and she cites "frothier" markets.
- Lacker, Pianalto, Plosser, Stern, Hoenig, Lockhart: inflation vigilance and a hold.
- Geithner, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner: downside risks to growth, risks to inflation, and concern about financial froth.
- Mishkin: Knightian uncertainty, with inflation risk related to productivity.
- Bernanke: "growthless job growth," housing as an inventory cycle, and inflation the predominant risk.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfort and target. Core is about 2.1 to 2.3 percent ("somewhat elevated"). The latest readings were better, but upside risks come from energy, commodities, the dollar and compensation. Score 58.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants describe growth as below potential for a while (Yellen "just a bit below"; Kohn "income growing at less than the growth rate of potential"; Hoenig "below trend"). They also describe resource utilization as high, with unemployment slightly below NAIRU (Kohn, Moskow "no meaningful slack"). The level of the gap is therefore close to potential, tilted slightly below by the below-potential language. Score 48 to satisfy the bright line.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: The labor market is above neutral. Members say "fairly tight," "very tight" (Lockhart, unemployment about 4 percent regionally and 4.4 percent nationally), and "strong, particularly for highly skilled workers" (Bernanke). Score 62.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend on net. Consumption is moderating toward about 2 percent, business investment is weak but showing glimmers, and housing investment is a large drag. Score 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length. Productivity growth was 1.6 percent in 2006, half the earlier pace, and structural trend estimates were marked down slightly. This is below trend and mostly judged cyclical. Score 38.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint, meaning loose. Kohn cites "supportive financial conditions," Bernanke "generally accommodative financial conditions," and Minehan "very accommodative financial markets." Score 28.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only a single reference, Warsh's remark that the MOVE index of Treasury volatility is at a nine-year low. That signals smooth functioning with no dysfunction. Score 25 with Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads are narrow and compressed (Kohn "narrow credit spreads"; Warsh "narrower spreads provide less of a shock absorber"; Poole "very narrow risk spreads"; Geithner "credit spreads...low"). The level is below normal, so the score is below 50. Score 20.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Concern about rich valuation. Markets are "frothier" (Minehan), "priced to perfection" (Warsh, Geithner, Bernanke), and Kohn expects equities to be soft despite the 6 percent rise. Moderate valuation concern, on the "somewhat elevated" side. Score 35.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks and borrowers are described as healthy. Poole's contact says "business cash flow and balance sheets are solid"; Poole also says "credit quality remains very high." Warsh says consumer credit quality is very strong. Offsetting concerns are covenant-lite lending, banks pushing leverage on private equity (Kroszner), and pressure on small and medium banks (Warsh). Healthy with minor concerns. Score 32.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar is declining slowly (Minehan "slowly declining dollar"; Kohn "the decline in the dollar"; Yellen "the dollar, which has fallen"; Geithner "dollar potentially weakening further"). Modest weakening, so below 50. Score 35.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- Higher inflation score = more inflation pressure. Evidence says somewhat elevated, so score above 50. OK.
- Higher credit-spread score = more stress. Evidence says narrow spreads, so a low score. OK.
- Higher financial-conditions score = tighter. Evidence says supportive and accommodative, so a low score. OK.
- Higher dollar score = stronger dollar. Evidence says weaker dollar, so below 50. OK.
- Higher equity score = more stress or undervaluation. Evidence says rich valuations and froth, so below 50. OK.
- Higher banking score = more stress. Evidence says healthy with small concerns, so low. OK.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds at 5 1/4 percent. The "predominant concern" remains inflation. Lacker expects to raise "when downside risks have sufficiently diminished," Hoenig wants "modestly tight policy" held, and Plosser would tighten if trend growth returns faster. Geithner wants to keep leaning against expectations of cuts. Kohn's own projection has slight easing but he weights inflation risk more. A hold with a tightening bias, so a mild hawkish lean.
- Dissent: The vote is unanimous, with no dissents. The language debate (Stern's alternative-C sentence, "on balance," "high resource utilization") ends with agreement. There is a real disagreement over the ultimate inflation objective (Poole, Lacker and Mishkin on 1 1/2 versus 2 percent; Kohn says the Committee has no comfort zone), which Lacker calls an unresolved dispersion. This is a disagreement about the objective, not about the current action.
- Financial stability attention: Geithner and Minehan raise froth early. Warsh and Kroszner speak about private equity, covenant-lite loans, narrow spreads and correlation. Bernanke's summary says "priced for perfection." Subprime is a recurring topic. Moderate, several participants at length.
- Forward guidance: The Chairman wants to keep the risk assessment sentence (inflation predominant) and drop forward-directing language. He explicitly notes the previous statement removed the suggestion that the next move was up. The signal is data-dependent with an inflation emphasis, so mildly hawkish.
- Uncertainty: Kohn says things are "more uncertain than usual"; Mishkin cites Knightian uncertainty on productivity and NAIRU; Kroszner lists five uncertainties; Lockhart says "significant uncertainty." Against that, the Chairman and Minehan say risks and uncertainties are a bit less pronounced than in March. Elevated but easing.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 48: below 50, consistent with "below potential" language, and within the "near potential" band.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 28: below 50, consistent with "accommodative" and "supportive."
- CREDIT_SPREADS 20: below 50, consistent with "narrow."
- BANKING_STRESS 32: below 50, consistent with "solid" and "very high credit quality."
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35: below 50, consistent with depreciation.
- TREASURY_STRESS 25: not above 55, and there is no dysfunction. OK.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62) differ by 14, under the 15-point threshold. The reason is that growth runs below potential while the labor market stays tight (the "growthless job growth" puzzle), so the two measures legitimately differ.
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43) differ by 5. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) and CREDIT_SPREADS (20) co-move. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: both low. OK.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (58) against sub-components: goods 60, housing 58, expectations 55. Consistent.

**9. N/A justification.**
- INFLATION_SERVICES: only Lacker's brief district comment on services price trends, with no usable national assessment. N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the rate is still higher than I am comfortable with ... I completely agree that inflation is our predominant concern"; Moskow, "inflation continues to run higher than most of us think is appropriate."
- Center of gravity: Core inflation is somewhat above target with upside risks, slightly better recently.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is above neutral because core is about 2.1 to 2.3 percent and described as "somewhat elevated."

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, real GDP growth "just a bit below my assessment of potential"; Kohn, "income growing at less than the growth rate of potential for several quarters ... unemployment rate is slightly below the NAIRU"; Moskow, no "meaningful resource slack over the projection period."
- Center of gravity: Growth is below potential but the level of utilization is still described as high, so the gap sits just under neutral.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because the below-potential language governs under the bright line, offset by tight utilization.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Lockhart, "Labor markets appear to have remained very tight in the District. The measured unemployment rate is around 4 percent ... versus 4.4 percent nationally"; Bernanke, "the labor market remains strong, particularly in the market for highly skilled workers."
- Center of gravity: Tight, with unemployment under 4.5 percent and skilled-labor shortages reported across Districts.
- Direction: Above neutral because multiple participants describe a tight market and unemployment is low.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "Consumption growth seems likely to moderate ... underlying growth is roughly 2 percent"; Poole, "the growth of credit card usage is slowing"; Kroszner, investment "more a glimmer of hope than something we can bank on."
- Center of gravity: Consumption is moderating, business investment is weak but improving, and residential investment is a drag.
- Direction: Below neutral because spending and investment are growing below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, productivity "rose 1.6 percent, about half as fast as the average pace set from 2000 through 2005"; Geithner, "productivity growth probably staying a bit below what we thought was trend."
- Center of gravity: Productivity has slowed, mostly viewed as cyclical, with some modest structural downgrade.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe growth below the historical trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "supportive financial conditions, especially for businesses; credit availability ... narrow credit spreads; low long-term rates"; Bernanke, "generally accommodative financial conditions."
- Center of gravity: Loose conditions that members both rely on and worry about.
- Direction: Below neutral (loose) because members call conditions supportive and accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 25 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh, the MOVE options index "is the lowest it has been in about nine years."
- Center of gravity: Very low Treasury volatility, no dysfunction mentioned, single remark.
- Direction: Below neutral because markets are calm; no market dysfunction was described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 20 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "these narrower spreads provide less of a shock absorber"; Poole, "very narrow risk spreads"; Geithner, "Credit spreads, other risk premiums, low levels of implied volatility ... imply a level of confidence ... a bit implausible."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are compressed and many members worry about underpriced risk.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan, investment contacts see markets "frothier right now than they had been over the past three or four years"; Bernanke, "Financial markets are priced for perfection."
- Center of gravity: Concern that valuations and risk pricing are rich, but no bubble language.
- Direction: Below neutral (valuation concern) because members call markets rich and priced for perfection.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole's bank contact, "business cash flow and balance sheets are solid"; Warsh, "Credit quality remains very strong across consumer credit."
- Center of gravity: Banks and borrowers look healthy; concerns are about lax covenants, leverage from private equity deals, and small-bank market share.
- Direction: Below neutral because members describe sound credit quality.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan, "a slowly declining dollar"; Kohn, "the decline in the dollar--which will support exports."
- Center of gravity: A modest, continuing decline in the dollar.
- Direction: Below neutral because the dollar is weakening.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60.** Higher energy, commodity and import prices and producers' price actions (Fisher, Minehan, Bernanke). Value only.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not meaningfully discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Owners' equivalent rent is "temporarily pushing up our measured inflation" (Kroszner, Yellen), with rising vacancy rates expected to help later (Bernanke). Value only.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Called "well anchored" (Yellen, Kroszner) but "too high" (Plosser) and at risk of drifting up (Geithner, Kohn, Warsh). Value only.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker, "When the downside risks have sufficiently diminished ... we're going to want to take action to bring inflation down; but in the meantime, we are on the sidelines"; Hoenig, "I am very comfortable with this modestly tight policy."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold, with inflation named the predominant concern and a few members expecting to tighten later.
- Direction: Slightly hawkish because a hold with a tightening bias fits the 51 to 60 band; Kohn's own slight easing assumption is a counterweight.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: The vote read-out shows ten Yes votes and no dissent. Lacker says the inflation forecasts portray "a Committee that has not come to agreement on its objective"; Kohn says "the Committee has never chosen such a comfort zone."
- Center of gravity: Agreement on the action and a broad outlook, with real but non-voting disagreement on wording and the long-run inflation target.
- Direction: Low because no dissent and few differing economic assessments.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Geithner, "a significant risk of a sharp deterioration in financial markets"; Warsh gives extended comments on private equity, covenant-lite deals and correlation; Kroszner on banks chasing private equity deals.
- Center of gravity: Several participants address froth, leverage and subprime at length, but the meeting remains focused on growth, inflation and wording.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "We wanted to retain a balance of risks that emphasized inflation risks, but we also wanted to get away from forward-directing language"; the directive keeps "predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate."
- Center of gravity: No commitment on direction, with a persistent inflation emphasis and a deliberate effort to avoid signalling a cut.
- Direction: Slightly hawkish because the statement retains the inflation concern while remaining data-dependent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 47 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "I committed the sin of thinking things were more uncertain than usual"; Mishkin, "the issue of Knightian uncertainty, not normal uncertainty"; Bernanke, "the risks and uncertainties seem a bit less pronounced than at the last meeting."
- Center of gravity: Several members stress unusual uncertainty (productivity, NAIRU, housing), but the group sees it as easing from March.
- Direction: Elevated but not high, so in the 46 to 60 band at its lower end.
