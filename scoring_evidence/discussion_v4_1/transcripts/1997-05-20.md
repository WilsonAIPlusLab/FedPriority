# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1997-05-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript: staff Q&A questions by members, the regional go-around, the Chairman's long policy statement (productivity hypothesis), policy go-around, and a roll-call. Staff turns are removed. Text is clean, with only page markers. No garbling affects scoring.

**Era context.** Mid-1997 expansion after a 25bp tightening at the prior meeting; Q1 growth very strong, Q2 expected to slow sharply, unemployment about 4.8-5%. Scores below rest on what participants say.

**Speakers.** Greenspan, McDonough, Governors Rivlin, Phillips, Kelley, Meyer, and Presidents McTeer, Parry, Jordan, Broaddus, Boehne, Hoenig, Guynn, Moskow, Stern, Minehan, Melzer.

**Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see measured inflation as low and well-behaved ("lower than almost all of us expected", Guynn; PPI down four months, McTeer). Yet most see pressure on resources building and risks to the upside (Parry: "modest upward trend"; McDonough: core CPI troubling, core services; Phillips: core CPI flat two years). So slightly above the midpoint: actual inflation benign, pressure building. Score about 54.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Parry says GDP is "about 1-3/4 percent" above potential and capacity utilization is above the historical acceleration level. McDonough says GDP is "somewhat above potential and likely to stay that way". Greenspan says slack is "not substantial". Others say first-quarter growth is unsustainable. Above 50 (bright-line), modestly: 59.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "Very, very tight labor markets" (Stern), signing bonuses and job fairs (Parry), 23-year low unemployment (Meyer), unemployment about 4.5-4.9%, entry-level wage jumps (Jordan). But wage pressure is modest and some districts (Moskow, Hoenig) report little acceleration. Score 66, since the level is tight (UE under 5%, below the NAIRU range some cite).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: First-quarter growth of 5.6% (real final sales 3.8%), strong fundamentals, wealth effects, record confidence, strong business fixed investment. Q2 is slowing sharply (retail sales soft, auto sales weak). Net above trend: 62.
- PRODUCTIVITY: The Chairman's central theme is an acceleration (about 3% in nonfinancial corporate), a hypothesis he stresses is not certain; Boehne, Phillips, Rivlin, Moskow and McDonough echo it. Above trend: 63, with a wide band because it is described as hypothesis.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Meyer: "supportive set of financial conditions", terms "attractive", market conditions "more favorable" since March. Phillips: "very favorable financial environment". Boehne: "more than ample liquidity". Accommodative, so below 50 (bright-line): 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed in terms of Treasury market functioning (Treasury cash balances during the staff Q&A are a tax-receipts matter, not dysfunction). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Phillips: "spreads on speculative-grade debt are particularly low", bank credit available; Boehne: lenders lending 30% more than a new acquisition is worth. Compressed, so below 50 (bright-line): 22.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: "Frothy asset markets" (McDonough), "irrational exuberance... more than the stock market" (Meyer), "booming financial markets" (Minehan), wealth effect, stock market rallied. Elevated valuation concern is on the low side of the scale: 30. It is discussed mainly in passing, so Moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are described as lax rather than stressed ("there are no standards", Jordan; "every deal was skinnier", fierce competition; commercial delinquencies low, Phillips). Low stress, with a note of excess risk-taking: 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar "remains on the strong side" (McTeer), "strong dollar" helped prices (Phillips, McDonough, Parry "higher dollar"), but recently the dollar fell sharply against the yen (staff Q&A, Chairman's question) and Meyer says it "first rose and then declined to below its initial level". Net: strong level, recent decline. Bright-line says appreciation above 50, depreciation below. The prevailing characterization is of a dollar that has strengthened since 1995 and still strong: 56, wide band, Low-Moderate confidence.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Strong dollar, falling PPI, steel price increases rescinded, import price pressure down (McTeer, Moskow, McDonough): 36.
- SERVICES: McDonough: rise in core services inflation is "troubling", the sector most susceptible to wage pass-through, but one month's data: 56.
- HOUSING: Not discussed as shelter inflation (commercial real estate only): N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Not directly discussed; Kelley says no inflation fear in the yield curve and Broaddus uses anchored expectations as a hypothetical. Weakly informative: 47.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The decision is no change with an asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive. Greenspan says another 25bp "more likely more" by year-end, and a July move is better than 50/50. Many prefer an immediate hike (Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Meyer prefers C, Parry, Guynn and Minehan prefer 25bp but accept B). Others (Boehne, Kelley, Phillips, McDonough) favor wait-and-see. Hold with a clear tightening bias and a hawkish skew in the room: 63.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Broaddus, for a hike, voted No). Several reluctant supporters (Parry "somewhat reluctantly", Guynn, Minehan, Stern, Meyer, Melzer is not stated but did not support by name). Disagreement is about timing and tactics rather than the economic assessment, since nearly all see upside inflation risks. Score 38.
- Financial stability emphasis: Moderate. Farmland speculation and lending standards (Jordan), speculative building (Broaddus, Boehne, Hoenig, Guynn, McDonough), credit-market excess (Minehan), "frothy asset markets", Meyer on financial conditions. A recurring side theme, not the focus: 42.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric directive, explicit Chairman probabilities (50/50 or better for July, very high for August, better than 50/50 for fall), "trajectory modest but definitely up". Clear tightening signal, softened by "modest" and "not today": 73.
- Uncertainty: Lots of discussion: Chairman calls his view "only a hypothesis", McDonough says high uncertainty and caution, Meyer details uncertainty about NAIRU and second-half growth, Minehan "agnostic", Rivlin "we do not know". Broaddus says not much more than usual. Elevated but not extreme: 55.

**Polarity checks.** Output gap above 50 matches "above potential". Financial conditions below 50 matches "supportive/favorable". Credit spreads below 50 matches "particularly low". Banking stress below 50 matches low delinquencies and no distress. Dollar slightly above 50 reflects a "strong dollar" level; flagged uncertain.

**Coherence.** Output gap 59 / labor 66 / consumption 62: within 15 points. Financial conditions 30 / credit spreads 22: co-move (both loose). Treasury stress N/A in normal times. Inflation parent 54 with sub-components 36/56/47 (goods low, services high): consistent with a weighted average near the high-40s to 50s; parent slightly higher due to broad pressure-building comments.

**N/A justification.** Treasury stress: Treasury market functioning not discussed. Inflation housing: not discussed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 54 ±6 Moderate.** "Recent inflation remains low by all conventional measures" (Guynn); "underlying inflation would show a modest upward trend" (Parry). Pressure building, level benign. Above neutral slightly because risks and resource pressure dominate while measured inflation is low.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 59 ±6 Moderate.** "The excess of real GDP over its potential level rose to about 1-3/4 percent" (Parry); "real GDP is now somewhat above potential" (McDonough). Above neutral.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 66 ±5 High.** "Very, very tight labor markets" (Stern); unemployment "to a 23-year low" (Meyer). Above neutral.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62 ±6 Moderate.** "Strong growth in both demand and production" (Phillips); "first-quarter... 5.6 percent" with Q2 softening (McTeer). Above trend.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 63 ±8 Moderate.** "productivity numbers that are accelerating significantly" (Greenspan, flagged as hypothesis). Above trend.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 ±7 Moderate.** "A supportive set of financial conditions" (Meyer); "very favorable financial environment" (Phillips). Below neutral (loose).
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.**
- **CREDIT_SPREADS 22 ±7 Moderate.** "spreads on speculative-grade debt are particularly low" (Phillips). Below neutral.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 30 ±8 Moderate.** "rather frothy asset markets" (McDonough); "irrational exuberance has affected more than the stock market" (Meyer). Elevated valuation concern.
- **BANKING_STRESS 28 ±8 Low.** "there are no standards. Bankers are just pirating each other's loans" (Jordan); commercial delinquencies low (Phillips). Low stress, lax-lending note.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH 56 ±9 Low.** "the dollar remains on the strong side" (McTeer); but dollar "declined to below its initial level" (Meyer). Slightly above neutral.
- **INFLATION_GOODS 36, SERVICES 56, HOUSING N/A, EXPECTATIONS 47.** (Evidence above.)
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 63 ±5 High.** "we are going to have to tighten policy somewhere along the line... another 25 basis points, more likely more" (Greenspan), with no move today. Tightening bias, a hawkish majority leaning to move now.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 38 ±6 High.** One No vote (Broaddus: "I think we need to move today"); Parry "somewhat reluctantly" supports. Moderate differences on timing.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 42 ±8 Moderate.** Speculative construction, farmland and lending laxity raised by many. Moderate attention.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 73 ±6 High.** "probability that we will have to move and should move in July is better than 50/50" (Greenspan); asymmetric directive.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 55 ±8 Moderate.** "only a hypothesis" (Greenspan); "a great deal of uncertainty" (McDonough); Broaddus "not that much more" than usual.
