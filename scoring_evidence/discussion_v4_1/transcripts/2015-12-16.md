## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Quotes are from participants only (staff turns are not in the input). Read to the last line, including the vote read-out.

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 (+-5, High)
- Brainard: "Inflation has remained persistently below our 2 percent target for six years now"; "The Phillips curve remains inert."
- Lyon: "Core PCE inflation has been quite stable over the past year, running close to 1.3 percent." Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation is currently at 1.3 percent."
- Many say the shortfall is transitory (Williams, Powell, Fischer, Yellen: "reasonably confident ... inflation ... will return to 2 percent"). Transitory language does not raise the current-level score.

OUTPUT_GAP: 47 (+-5, High)
- Yellen: "the economy is not quite back to full employment"; Brainard: "some further slack may remain"; Evans: "We see resource gaps closing in 2017".
- Offsetting: Williams "growth continuing above trend in 2016"; Powell "The amount of remaining slack is probably modest"; Tarullo "moderately above-trend pace of growth."
- Net is a small residual gap, slightly below potential with growth above trend and the gap closing.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 (+-4, High)
- Unemployment 5.0%: Williams "national unemployment at my estimate of the natural rate of 5 percent"; Mester "at or nearly at full employment"; Yellen "5 percent ... only a shade above the median of our projections of its longer-run sustainable level".
- Slack/softness side: Brainard "where are the margins of remaining slack?"; Powell "I don't yet see persuasive signs of an acceleration in compensation".
- Tight side: Lacker "The struggle to find qualified workers continues across a range of occupations"; Harker "a much larger fraction of firms are reporting difficulty finding workers".

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 (+-5, Moderate)
- Lacker: "consumer spending to continue to grow at around 3 percent"; Brainard: "domestic demand continues to increase at a moderate pace"; Yellen: "Motor vehicle sales have been robust, retail sales are advancing at a solid pace".
- Offsetting: Brainard "private fixed investment has decelerated"; Lockhart "a sharp reduction in anticipated capital expenditures"; Kaplan "hesitant to commit resources to capital spending"; energy and manufacturing weak.

PRODUCTIVITY: 34 (+-8, Moderate)
- Fischer: "structural labor productivity growth over the period from 2011 to 2014 declined to an average annual rate of 0.8 percent"; Bullard: "a world of low inflation and low productivity growth"; Dudley: "the underlying productivity growth trend seems to have fallen."
- Fischer notes upside risk to future productivity growth, but the current assessment is well below trend.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 51 (+-6, Moderate)
- Tightening side: Evans: "credit conditions have tightened somewhat since the previous FOMC meeting"; Kaplan and Powell on high-yield stress.
- Neutral or easy side: Dudley: "other than in the high-yield market ... financial conditions have not tightened appreciably"; Brainard: "credit conditions have mostly normalized"; Kaplan: "tight investment-grade spreads"; Mester: "highly accommodative monetary policy".
- Net is neutral. The rate decision itself is policy, not conditions.

TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Treasury market functioning was not discussed by participants (only inflation compensation in TIPS/Treasury yields and "bond yields have risen modestly"). No dysfunction raised.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 (+-8, Moderate)
- Level and direction: Evans: "spreads are up, and they've risen more since then", "healthy repricing of riskier assets"; Kaplan: "High-yield credit spreads have widened" but also "tight investment-grade spreads"; Tarullo: "spreads on many asset classes already widening"; Dudley: high yield "under considerable stress", much of it driven by energy.
- Wide in high yield (stated stress) but tight in investment grade, so the net is slightly above normal.

EQUITY_VALUATION: 37 (+-8, Moderate)
- Kaplan: "particularly with P/Es and market-cap-to-GDP measures at historically elevated levels. Some amount of market revaluation may be healthy."
- Brainard: "asset prices have reached or exceeded gauges of historical levels". Dudley: "equity markets are little changed". Only modestly above fair value, and no bubble language.

BANKING_STRESS: 30 (+-8, Moderate)
- Fischer: "The financial system is currently in good shape"; Lacker: bankers say "credit quality is improving"; Williams: community-bank multifamily lending, "loose lending standards and excessive leverage, remain largely absent".
- Small pockets of concern: TRID costs for community banks (Lacker), fierce competition for borrowers.

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 70 (+-5, High)
- Brainard: "a further 2 percent increase in the real dollar, bringing total strengthening up to about 16 percent since the summer of last year"; George: "the strong dollar"; Dudley: "the dollar is slightly firmer".
- Net exports drag and disinflation from dollar appreciation are cited widely (Mester, Kaplan, Powell, Lyon).

INFLATION_GOODS: 36
- Dudley: "core goods inflation moving higher as the effects of the stronger dollar ultimately dissipate" (implies currently depressed); Kaplan: "consumer pricing power is driving down the price of goods"; Harker: manufacturing "very little evidence of price pressures".

INFLATION_SERVICES: 55
- Dudley: "the gap between core services inflation and core goods inflation is unusually wide right now" (services firmer); Harker: nonmanufacturing price indexes "experiencing increases that accelerated somewhat".

INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Participants discuss housing activity and commercial real estate prices (Williams) but not shelter or rent inflation.

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36
- Brainard: "persistent underperformance of inflation may be beginning to have some effect on inflation expectations"; Lyon: "Market-based measures of inflation compensation remain near their historic lows"; Dudley: "inflation expectations in some danger of becoming unhinged to the downside"; Mester: "reasonably well anchored"; Fischer: "how little expected inflation has declined".
- Drifting down but mostly seen as not yet unanchored.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 (+-5, High)
- Action: the Committee raises the target range 25 bp at liftoff, and every voter (and the Chair) supports it: Yellen: "current economic conditions and the outlook, taken together, now warrant a modest increase"; Williams: "I unequivocally support alternative B".
- Forward lean is gradual and cautious: Dudley: "a go-slow strategy"; Powell: "a very gradual path"; Brainard: "somewhat greater weight on the possible regret associated with tightening too early"; Tarullo only "reluctantly" supports; Evans: "a tough call"; Yellen: "monetary policy will remain appropriately accommodative".

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 (+-6, High)
- Vote read-out: all nine voters Yes (Chair Yellen, Dudley, Brainard, Evans, Lacker, Lockhart, Powell, Tarullo, Williams); no formal dissent (the only objection was Fischer's about the word "unanimously" in the Board-vote note, resolved).
- Reservations: Tarullo: "I don't think it's appropriate policy to raise rates today ... I will reluctantly go along with alternative B", with an open warning that "I may have some differences with the majority of the Committee in the future"; Brainard leaning toward waiting but supporting; Lyon (non-voting) wanting "a more complete and explicit rationale".
- Substantive split on inflation outlook (Tarullo/Brainard/Lyon vs Lacker/Bullard/Williams) and on "gradual" language (Lacker, Bullard, Harker). Differences are in tone and path, not in the vote.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 (+-8, Moderate)
- Rosengren: "overall financial conditions might be conducive to sowing the seeds of such problems"; Williams on commercial real estate: "not yet flashing red, the potential for imbalances merits close monitoring"; Powell on leveraged finance and Third Avenue; Kaplan on high yield; Dudley: "high yield ... under considerable stress"; Fischer: "there are some signs of accumulating financial stability problems".
- Several participants address it at some length, but it is not dominant over the economic assessment.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 (+-6, Moderate)
- Statement language "gradual" twice, "gradual but data dependent" (Lacker, Bullard, Harker); Lockhart: "The SEP dot chart looks as though it will set up an expectation of rate increases on a schedule of every other meeting"; Powell: "I have no prior about March"; Yellen: "further moderate increases in the federal funds rate would probably be warranted".
- Begin normalization with a gradual, data-dependent, state-contingent path and reinvestment continuing: a mild tightening bias.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 (+-7, Moderate)
- Tarullo: "no convincing theory ... of how expectations are formed", "faith has trumped empirics"; Powell: "uncertainty about inflation dynamics"; Yellen: "heightened uncertainty about the economic effects of higher interest rates"; Lacker: "substantial uncertainty about the inflation outlook"; Dudley: "considerable risk from abroad."
- Many participants stress inflation-dynamics and external risks, but many forecasts are described as "boring" or "balanced" (Rosengren, Williams, Mester), so overall it is elevated, not extreme.
