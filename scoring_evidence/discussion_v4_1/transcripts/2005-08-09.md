# FOMC member-discussion scoring v4.1 -- transcript discussion, meeting 2005-08-09

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion from a transcript: members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic go-around (Moskow through Ferguson), the Chairman's summary, the policy go-around on the 25bp proposal and the Alternative B statement wording, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. I scored only what members say. A few passages are cut, such as unanswered Q&A questions and the directive reading, but nothing is garbled enough to affect scoring.

**Era context.** The text says the Committee is in a "measured pace" tightening campaign with the funds rate heading to about 4.25-4.5%. Unemployment is 5%, GDP growth is solid, oil is high, and housing is booming. A benchmark revision has just raised measured core PCE inflation. I score these from the text, not from the backdrop.

**Speakers and views.**
- Moskow: hawkish. He wants 4.5% before pausing, worries about liquidity chasing deals and about the revised inflation, and favors 25bp.
- Fisher: hawkish. Activity is strong, but he describes Chinese-sourced price competition.
- Yellen: the economy is "noticeably stronger," inflation is "foreboding," a "whisker of slack" remains, and policy should continue.
- Lacker: expectations are stable and policy needs flexibility.
- Santomero: wary of getting behind the curve.
- Stern: relaxed, "random walk" on inflation, stay on the path.
- Guynn: inflation is near the upper bound of his comfort level, housing may be cooling, and he is not worried about labor slack.
- Minehan: a weak district, but strong national growth. She is concerned about complacency, with "bond risk spreads very narrow," and wants policy to possibly accelerate and the "measured pace" language revisited.
- Pianalto: inflation is at the high end of her comfort zone, but she sees no intensifying pressure.
- Hoenig: growth is above trend, the gap is "approaching zero," the real funds rate is only 1.2%, and policy is "probably 100 basis points below" where people think it should be. He wants to drop "measured pace."
- Poole: the economy has a "good head of steam," and he sees a disconnect with labor-market anecdotes (no wage pressure).
- Geithner: risks are broadly balanced, perhaps slightly higher, and he supports keeping the path.
- Olson: fiscal issues.
- Kohn: the economy is a little closer to potential, but "some slack remaining," so stay on measured increases.
- Bies: mortgage and ARM reset risk, "tremendous amount of liquidity," and the need to push rates up further.
- Ferguson: the Greenbook inflation forecast is reliable, with upside risk.
- Greenspan: the path is right, no urgency to accelerate, and oil and housing wealth are the big unknowns.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfort and target. Core PCE was revised to about 1.9-2.25%, and several (Guynn, Pianalto, Hoenig, Moskow) say it is at the top of their comfort zone. Recent monthly data are benign, and expectations are stable. Level about 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as near potential. The gap is "shrinking to almost nothing" (Moskow), "approaching zero" (Hoenig), and "a whisker of slack" (Yellen). Kohn says "somewhat short" of potential and Minehan says slightly wider. Growth is above potential but the level is about at potential. Score 50. Bright-line: "near potential" falls in 48-52, so it is satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 5%, "at or near full employment" (Hoenig), with "less slack" (Yellen). Poole's contacts report no shortages, and Guynn expects future shortages. Near NAIRU, 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is strong. Kohn says consumption is "already quite strong" though partly borrowed from the future, Lacker says households keep expanding spending, and Ferguson says final demand is robust. Business investment is described as "a bit on the weak side" (Kohn) and recently strong (Lacker). Above trend, 61.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed. Structural productivity was marked down modestly, but it is still above late-'90s rates (Yellen), and actual 2005 data have been revised up (Kohn, Greenspan). Roughly at or slightly above trend, 54.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Accommodative and liquid. Moskow cites a "large amount of liquidity chasing financial deals," Bies cites "a tremendous amount of liquidity," Minehan cites low volatility and narrow spreads, and Hoenig says financial conditions are poised to support growth. Long yields have risen, but conditions are still easy. Score below 50, at 33. Bright-line: "accommodative" requires below 50, so it is satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members mention yields rising on policy expectations (Lacker, Kohn) and Minehan asked about a 10-year squeeze, but no dysfunction is described by a member. N/A (see N/A justification).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan says "bond risk spreads very narrow," and Geithner says credit spreads suggest a favorable view. The level is below normal, so the score is below 50, at 27. Bright-line: "narrow" requires below 50, so it is satisfied.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only Minehan (very low equity volatilities, risk-taking) and Geithner (favorable equity prices) speak to it. There is mild complacency concern but no overvaluation call. Default band, slightly toward elevated valuation, 44.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are seen as sound. Bies says "bankers as a whole ... are doing a good job at underwriting," with "lenders on the edges." Guynn notes banks exercising new caution in real estate lending, and Moskow says banks relax lending terms. Healthy with pockets of concern, below 50, at 38. Bright-line: satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Members refer to dollar depreciation (against the yuan after the July revaluation, and as a risk). Hoenig lists "dollar depreciation" as an upside inflation risk, and Minehan and Olson raise the risk of a large depreciation. The framing is weakening or a risk of weakening, so below 50, at 40. Bright-line: depreciation requires below 50, so it is satisfied.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: mixed. There is little pricing power and Chinese competition (Fisher), soft steel and auto price cuts (Pianalto), and oil pass-through (Yellen). Near neutral, 48.
- Services: not specifically discussed, N/A.
- Housing: only owners' equivalent rent in Santomero's district and the nonmarket PCE discussion, so N/A. Housing prices are discussed as asset prices, not as housing inflation.
- Expectations: "remarkably stable" and well contained (Lacker, Kohn), but at the upper end of Lacker's comfort zone. Anchored, about 52.

**Tier 3 check in words.**
- Policy lean: unanimous 25bp hike to 4.25%, continuing the measured pace. Moskow wants 4.5%, Hoenig and Minehan lean toward faster or dropping "measured pace," Bies wants higher, and Greenspan sees no urgency to accelerate. Moderate hawkish, 67.
- Dissent: zero dissents, a unanimous vote. The disagreement is on statement wording ("gradually," paragraph 4 order, "measured pace"). Hoenig says "a little behind the curve" but supports the action. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences, 20.
- Financial stability attention: several participants address it meaningfully (Moskow, Minehan, Guynn, Bies, Kohn, Greenspan on housing wealth, plus GSE hedging in the Q&A). Moderate, 46.
- Forward guidance: the "measured pace" language is retained, markets expect more tightening, and some want to move toward changing it. Policy will go higher with attention to the data. Mild-to-clear tightening direction, 72.
- Uncertainty: standard caveats about the output gap, oil, and housing wealth. Greenspan says "we don't really have a great deal of confidence in the outlook." Most members see risks as balanced and the outlook as good. Somewhat elevated, 35.

**Polarity checks.** Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence says liquid and easy, so a low score is consistent. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress, and the evidence says narrow, so a low score is consistent. Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means appreciation, and the evidence says depreciation, so a low score is consistent. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and the evidence says sound, so a low score is consistent.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 50 move together. OUTPUT_GAP 50 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 61 is an 11-point gap, within the 15-point limit and explained by strong consumption alongside a gap that is near zero, not above. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 and CREDIT_SPREADS 27 co-move. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, as in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 is consistent with the sub-components, with expectations anchored and goods near neutral.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS is N/A because the only mention is Minehan's question about a 10-year squeeze, whose answer was not retained, and members otherwise discuss yield moves only as policy-expectation effects. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING are N/A because they were not discussed as inflation components.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "the benchmark revision boosts inflation as measured by the core PCE to 1.9 percent ... at the top of my comfort zone." Hoenig: core PCE "now averaging about 2¼ percent."
- Summary: Measured inflation is modestly above comfort and the pressures are described as elevated, though recent monthly readings are benign.
- Direction: Above neutral because several members place inflation at the top of their comfort zone and Moskow calls the revisions "a serious concern."

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "the current degree of resource slack is small." Hoenig: "an output gap that is small and approaching zero." Yellen: "a whisker of slack probably still remains."
- Summary: Members see the economy as at or very near potential, with growth above potential closing the last bit of slack.
- Direction: At neutral because the level is described as near potential (the above-potential language refers to growth).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +-5 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "at or near full employment now." Poole: contacts "have no problem with labor availability."
- Summary: Unemployment is 5% with a slightly firmer labor market and no wage pressure in the anecdotes.
- Direction: At neutral because 5% unemployment is near NAIRU.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 61 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Kohn: consumption "is already quite strong." Ferguson: "Final demand seems to be robust, with consumers, housing, and to some degree business investment."
- Summary: Spending is above trend, helped by auto incentives and housing, with investment improving.
- Direction: Above neutral because spending is described as strong and robust.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 54 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "productivity still seems to be growing somewhat faster than the robust rates achieved in the second half of the '90s." Greenspan: "we are getting upward revisions in productivity."
- Summary: Structural productivity was cut modestly, but actual productivity is running firm.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because growth is described as still robust.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Bies: "There is a tremendous amount of liquidity in the market." Moskow: "large amount of liquidity chasing financial deals ... banks continue to relax lending terms."
- Summary: Conditions are easy, with abundant liquidity and risk-taking, despite higher long yields.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because members describe accommodative, liquid conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 27 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Minehan: "equity volatilities at a very low level and bond risk spreads very narrow." Geithner: "equity prices, credit spreads, and risk premia generally suggest a pretty favorable view."
- Summary: Spreads are narrow and risk appetite is high.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Minehan: "equity volatilities at a very low level ... reaching out the risk spectrum for return." Geithner: "equity prices ... suggest a pretty favorable view of this expansion."
- Summary: Mild complacency concern but no explicit overvaluation claim.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (somewhat elevated valuation) on thin evidence.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Bies: "Bankers as a whole I think clearly are doing a good job at underwriting these. We have some lenders on the edges." Guynn: banks "beginning to exercise a new caution in their real estate lending."
- Summary: The banking system is sound with pockets of risk in mortgage products.
- Direction: Below neutral because banks are described as sound with minor concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-8 (Low)**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "upside risk from higher energy prices and dollar depreciation." Minehan: risk of "a large depreciation in the dollar."
- Summary: The dollar is discussed in terms of depreciation and depreciation risk, with limited direct discussion.
- Direction: Below neutral because members refer to depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 48.** Evidence: Fisher on Chinese suppliers underbidding and Pianalto on soft steel and auto price cuts, against Yellen on oil pass-through. Near neutral.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed as an inflation component.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Evidence: Lacker: "inflation expectations have been remarkably stable ... at the upper end of my comfort zone." Anchored at roughly target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Moskow: "I continue to think that we should raise rates until we are comfortably in the middle of the neutral range ... about 4½ percent." Hoenig: "monetary policy is probably 100 basis points below what most people think it should be."
- Summary: All back a 25bp hike and continued tightening, with a hawkish minority wanting a faster path, while the Chair sees no urgency to accelerate.
- Direction: Hawkish because the majority favors tightening and some lean toward more.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-5 (High)**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "I think we're a little behind the curve. But I don't see a better solution ... so I support your recommendation." The roll call read-out shows only "Yes" votes.
- Summary: A unanimous vote with disagreement only on statement wording and pace.
- Direction: Low because there are no dissents and the differences are about emphasis.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 46 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Bies on option ARMs and subprime resets: "we could see a major impact on cash flows of both subprime and prime borrowers." Minehan: "The biggest risk markets face right now is their belief that there is no risk."
- Summary: Several members spend real time on liquidity, risk-taking, mortgage products and housing wealth, but it is not the dominant topic.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I think we're on the right path. I don't think there's any urgency to consider accelerating the path nor, of course, pulling it back." Geithner: "reinforce the sense that the fed funds rate needs to move higher."
- Summary: The statement is kept, with elevated inflation pressures and a message that rates will keep rising, while several push to loosen the commitment to "measured pace."
- Direction: Tightening direction because the signal is further increases, tempered by a data-dependent pace.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "I don't think we really have a great deal of confidence in the outlook implicit in this forecast." Kohn: "increased uncertainty about the strength of this channel."
- Summary: Standard caveats on the gap, oil and housing, with risks described as balanced and a generally confident outlook.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated, not high.
