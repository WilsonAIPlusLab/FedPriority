# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1993-02-03 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type and era.** Member/participant transcript for a two-day meeting (Feb 2-3, 1993). The input holds organizational items, member questions during staff briefings, the economic go-around, a long discussion of the annual M2/M3/debt ranges, the policy go-around, and a subcommittee discussion of FOMC confidentiality/openness. Staff answers are removed, so many questions appear without answers (ignored). A few passages are marked [unintelligible]; none block scoring. Era context: early recovery after the 1990-91 recession, a new Administration with a deficit-reduction/short-term stimulus package, weak job growth, corporate restructuring, and slow M2 growth. Scored from what members say.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), Corrigan (Vice Chair), Governors Angell, Kelley, LaWare, Lindsey, Mullins, Phillips, and Presidents Boehne, Broaddus, Forrestal, Hoenig, Jordan, Keehn, McTeer, Melzer, Parry, Stern, Syron.

**Core dimensions (direction first).**
- Inflation: participants see pressure as below/modest. Greenspan sees a "continued disinflation path"; Stern, Forrestal and Boehne see no price pressure. Counter-voices are Angell (commodity index rising, "slightly less optimistic than the staff"), Melzer (upside risk), Corrigan (the "speed factor") and Mullins on the Blue Chip view. Score below midpoint, around 38.
- Output gap: below midpoint. Members say real GDP is growing "below potential" (Parry) and speak of slack, weak employment, and drags from commercial real estate, defense and exports. Corrigan notes inflation relates to slack. Score around 40.
- Labor: below midpoint. Layoffs and restructuring dominate, "job growth is not evident" (Mullins), and Jordan reports small businesses not adding workers. Stern's Twin Cities help-wanted anecdote is a lone tight spot. Score around 32.
- Consumption/investment: above trend recently. Q4 GDP 3.8% with final sales 4.5% (McTeer), strong Christmas and January retail, rising investment, housing and trucks. Offsetting this is concern about sustainability and the saving rate. Score around 58.
- Productivity: above trend. Kelley cites 3% productivity growth in 1992, and McTeer and Greenspan stress rapid productivity gains through restructuring. Score around 66.
- Financial conditions: mixed, near neutral and slightly loose. Long rates have fallen a lot, "pretty low rates and high stock prices" (Mullins), and short rates are low enough (McTeer). Against that, bank and business lending is weak because of commercial real estate and bank capital caution (Greenspan, Jordan, Boehne). Score slightly below 50, so the bright-line (accommodative means below 50) is satisfied.
- Treasury stress: N/A. Members discuss falling long rates and fiscal expectations, not market functioning.
- Credit spreads: N/A. Members discuss loan volumes and bank caution, not spreads.
- Equity valuation: only a passing "high stock prices"; weak evidence. Default 45, Low confidence.
- Banking stress: Greenspan describes banks raising capital ratios, uncertain commercial real estate values, nonperforming loans and highly leveraged intermediaries. Phillips says the financial sector is in better shape, and Angell says net interest margins have strengthened. Mixed, so 52 with moderate uncertainty. No "sound" or "stressed" bright-line wording.
- Dollar: N/A. Only a foreign-currency reserves Q&A, with no discussion of dollar moves.
- Sub-components: goods get a value (steel price increases of about 2% from "very, very low levels", commodity prices up, but pricing otherwise soft). Services and housing are not discussed. Expectations are mixed: Jordan's contacts and Corrigan say people have not "bought zero inflation", while Stern says business is "resigned to price stability". Roughly anchored, about 50.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: every member votes for alternative B and a symmetric directive, with no change in the 3% funds rate. Greenspan sees "no reason" to change policy over the next 6 weeks. Angell is "inclined to believe we ought to be thinking about tightening" but lacks the "courage"; Melzer prefers asymmetric toward tightening but accepts symmetry; Broaddus sympathizes with Angell. McTeer says "tempting to call for further ease" but rejects it. Slight hawkish tilt from the minority, but the stance is hold. About 53.
- Dissent: the vote is unanimous (12 yes on the ranges and 12 yes on the directive). Differences are over tactics and framing: Angell prefers 1-5% for M2, Keehn would leave the ranges unchanged, Jordan wants M2 taken seriously, Corrigan objects to the "political" framing and to many Ms. No dissents, and Angell and Keehn explicitly decline to dissent. About 22.
- Financial stability emphasis: not a main topic. Greenspan's discussion of commercial real estate and bank lending, plus Boehne, Phillips and Syron comments, is a moderate but narrow segment; most time goes to money targets and confidentiality. About 15.
- Forward guidance: symmetric directive and "sit still"; a few members flag a possible tightening need (Angell, Melzer, Broaddus on future signaling). Near neutral, about 52.
- Uncertainty: widely expressed. Melzer says wide confidence intervals make policy basis "very difficult", Corrigan has no conviction on risks (a "bimodal" view), Kelley calls it a "close call", Keehn says uncertainties are "very, very significant", and there is a lot of doubt on velocity, M2 and fiscal policy. Elevated but not extreme: about 54.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50 matches "below potential" and slack. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below 50 and no member says tight overall; that is consistent. BANKING_STRESS is near 50 and no bright-line wording applies. CREDIT_SPREADS, DOLLAR and TREASURY are N/A.

**Coherence.** Output gap 40 vs labor 32 (gap 8, fine). Output gap 40 vs consumption/investment 58 (gap 18): spending is robust in recent data but members still see slack and a jobless recovery, with sustainability doubts, so I accept the divergence. Financial conditions vs credit spreads: spreads N/A. Inflation parent 38 is consistent with goods 52 (a small component) and expectations 50, because the parent is dominated by the "disinflation" narrative.

**N/A justification.** Treasury, credit spreads and dollar are not raised as market-price topics by members. Services and housing inflation are not discussed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 38 +-8 Moderate.** Greenspan: "continued disinflation path... glide down... to as close to a noninflationary environment"; Stern: "not much, if any, sign of inflationary pressures." Below target-like pressure with a minority worried about upside (Angell, Melzer). Below neutral because most report benign prices.

**OUTPUT_GAP 40 +-8 Moderate.** Parry: "real GDP growing below potential"; Corrigan: "inflation... related to slack in the economy." Slack remains, so below neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 32 +-7 Moderate.** Mullins: "job growth is not evident"; Keehn: "almost every company executive... expects to continue to reduce their employment." Slack in the labor market, so below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 58 +-7 Moderate.** McTeer: "strong consumption but investment was even stronger"; Greenspan: "definite investment push." Above trend in recent data, tempered by sustainability worries (Keehn, LaWare, Jordan).

**PRODUCTIVITY 66 +-8 Moderate.** Kelley: "very gratifying 3 percent in 1992"; McTeer: "rapid productivity growth that we've been getting." Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 +-8 Moderate.** Mullins: "pretty low rates and high stock prices"; Greenspan: commercial real estate uncertainty is "stunting the growth in lending." Slightly looser than neutral on rates, offset by constrained credit. Below 50, consistent with no member calling conditions tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not discussed as functioning/dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.** Spreads not discussed; availability discussed only through bank behavior.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 45 +-8 Low.** Mullins mentions "high stock prices" in passing with no valuation concern. Default range.

**BANKING_STRESS 52 +-8 Moderate.** Greenspan: banks "moving their book capital ratios up as rapidly as they have... proxy for uncertainty with respect to the reserves for nonperforming loans"; Phillips: financial sector "in better shape." Mixed, near neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.** No member discusses dollar movement.

**INFLATION_GOODS 52.** Keehn: steel prices to rise about 2% "from very, very low levels"; Angell: commodity index up, lumber up 43%.
**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 50.** Jordan: contacts "believe that inflation is going to pick up"; Stern: business people "increasingly resigned to price stability." Mixed, anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 53 +-6 Moderate.** All vote "B symmetric"; Greenspan: "no reason... to change the stance of policy"; Angell: "inclined to believe we ought to be thinking about tightening"; Melzer prefers asymmetric toward tightening. Hold with small tightening tilt from a minority, so slightly above neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 22 +-7 Moderate.** Unanimous votes; Angell: "I'm not going to vote against"; Keehn: "I certainly don't feel strongly enough about it to dissent." Differences over range width, M2 framing and press conferences, not policy stance.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 15 +-7 Moderate.** One extended Greenspan passage on commercial real estate and bank capital/lending, with brief support from Boehne, Phillips, Syron; otherwise a minor topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 52 +-6 Moderate.** Symmetric directive; Broaddus: "lot of sympathy for Governor Angell's comments"; Melzer: "very sensitive" to the Blue Chip inflation risk. Neutral with a faint tightening tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 54 +-8 Moderate.** Melzer: "wide confidence intervals... very difficult basis on which to make policy"; Corrigan: "I can't quite decide on which side is the greater risk"; Keehn: "uncertainties are very, very significant." Elevated across several members, though not extreme.
