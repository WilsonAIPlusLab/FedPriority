# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2014-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: economy go-round (first session), policy go-round (second session), staff Q&A questions from members, and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Procedural items (Board/FOMC motions, RRP testing resolution) are ignored.

**Era context.** Late-recovery period with the policy rate at zero, asset purchases just ended, and members debating how to word forward guidance ("considerable time" vs "patient") and when liftoff should come. The macro backdrop (strong payrolls, sharp oil drop, strong dollar, falling market inflation compensation) is only used where members themselves describe it.

**Speakers.** Chair Yellen, Vice Chairman Dudley, Governors Fischer, Tarullo, Powell, Brainard, and Presidents Lacker, Fisher, Rosengren, Evans, Williams, Lockhart, Mester, Bullard, Plosser, George, Kocherlakota. Views split into a hawkish-leaning group (Fisher, Plosser, Bullard, Lacker, George, Mester, Williams) and a cautious/dovish-leaning group (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Brainard, Tarullo, Lockhart, Powell on risks), with Yellen, Dudley, Fischer in the middle.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target and likely to fall further in headline terms ("persistence of inflation around 1 1/2 percent", Lacker; "12-month rate likely to drop below 1 percent early next year", Yellen; core "stuck at 1 1/2 percent", Dudley). Disagreement is about whether it returns, not whether it is currently low. Below midpoint, in the 26-40 band. Not as extreme as "meaningful deflation risk"; members such as Plosser say "I don't really think that deflation is the serious problem". Score about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants describe growth well above trend/potential growth ("real GDP growth to run well above potential output", Williams) but also remaining slack ("I still see a fair measure of employment slack remaining", Lockhart; "significant labor market slack", Rosengren; George's composite gap about 1 percent, to be closed within about a year). The level of the gap is still below potential and closing. Below 50, around 44. Bright-line: "below potential/slack remains" so must be < 50. Satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE 5.8 percent, "within hailing distance" of the natural rate (Fischer), but U-6 and long-term unemployment elevated, wage growth tepid, part-time for economic reasons elevated. Some see labor tightness in local pockets (Fargo 2.2 percent, Kocherlakota; Fisher's Dallas district 5.1 percent). Score the level, not the improvement: UE 5.5-7 percent corresponds to 31-45; near-NAIRU views push up. Score 44.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumer spending "surprisingly strong" (Williams), payrolls "robust", manufacturing and capex strong, with offset from energy-sector investment and net exports. Above trend but not boom. Score 60.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Not discussed by participants as a dimension (only incidental references to potential growth and technology orders). N/A.

**Financial dimensions.**

- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Policy "highly accommodative", "financial conditions don't tighten at all" since asset purchases ended (Dudley), credit availability "continuing to improve" (Dudley), higher stock prices (Powell), mortgage rates down (Fisher). Offset: junk-bond angst concentrated in energy ("real angst in the marketplace", Fisher) and EM selloff risk (Powell). Overall loose/supportive: below 50, score 38. Bright-line "accommodative/supportive/easy" gives < 50. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries discussed as pricing in lower oil prices and strong foreign demand (Powell, Dudley); no dysfunction. Yield moves are not dysfunction. Score 35, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only the high-yield market is discussed (Fisher: OAS up, panic "heavily concentrated with marginal energy operators"). No level statement relative to history is given by members; direction only (widening). Per the v4.1 rule, direction-only goes 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence; offset by Dudley's improving credit availability. Score 53, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only a passing mention ("higher stock prices", Powell) as a support for spending; no valuation commentary. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Dudley refers to a "better-capitalized banking system" and credit availability improving; Brainard "U.S. economy and financial system have become more resilient"; some concern about European bank exposure to Russia (Dudley). Healthy, with a minor external concern. Score 33, below 50 (bright-line satisfied). Moderate-low confidence given brief treatment.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar "continued its sharp appreciation" (Brainard), "stronger dollar" cited by many, concern about net exports. Strong appreciation: above 50, in the 66-80 band. Score 72.

**Inflation sub-components.**

- GOODS: Dollar appreciation will "lower non-energy commodity and imported goods prices" (Yellen's summary of members; Williams, Fischer), oil/commodities falling. Falling goods prices: 26.
- SERVICES: Not directly characterized as current services inflation (George's comment is a forward expectation). N/A.
- HOUSING: Housing is discussed as activity, not price/rent inflation. N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Survey measures stable and "anchored" for most; market-based measures fell, and several (Kocherlakota, Bullard, Evans, Brainard) worry about downward drift, with Staff-vs-Williams debate about anchoring "at 2 percent" vs below. Net: anchored, with a downside tilt. Score 42.

**Tier 3 check.**

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The decision is to hold at zero with "patient" language (alternative B) and the Chair defines patient as no move for at least the next couple of meetings; nearly all expect liftoff in 2015 and the statement is read as "modestly hawkish" (Dudley). This is hold with a tightening bias, but with a sizeable cautious wing (Kocherlakota wants more easing tools; Evans, Rosengren, Brainard, Tarullo prefer to wait; Tarullo sees premature liftoff as the bigger risk). Two hawkish dissenters want earlier action. Net: lean hawkish but moderate, 53.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Three formal dissents (Fisher, Plosser from the hawkish side, wanting earlier signal of liftoff; Kocherlakota from the dovish side, wanting stronger easing commitment/asset purchases). Different reasoning from opposite sides, plus reservations among supporters (Lockhart "with some apprehension", Evans, George, Brainard). Disagreement reaches assessment (inflation expectations, slack). Exchanges are civil, no heated personal clashes. Between "notable disagreement" and "significant division": 65.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Several members address financial conditions: oil-driven junk-bond stress and emerging market/Russia spillover risks (Fisher, Powell, Brainard, Dudley, Fischer), Dudley's long discussion of financial conditions as the transmission channel and taper-tantrum avoidance, Tarullo's mention of financial-stability reasons possibly motivating liftoff. Still secondary to the economy and the wording debate. Score 33.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: "Considerable time" is being replaced by "patient", to be defined as at least the next couple of meetings; January and March unlikely, April onward live; liftoff in 2015 expected. This is the start of normalization language but with explicit "patient" and strong data dependence, and a cautious tone. Between mild tightening bias and pre-liftoff: 56.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Normal-to-somewhat elevated. Fischer: "forecasts have very wide, not narrow, confidence bands"; Tarullo: "considerable amount of uncertainty"; Williams on oil; global risks (Europe, Russia, EM); model-sensitive decompositions of breakevens; several confident (Plosser, Powell on the baseline). Score 42.

**Polarity verification.** Higher = more of the named construct throughout. INFLATION_PRESSURE low because inflation is below target. DOLLAR_STRENGTH high because the dollar strengthened. BANKING_STRESS low because banks are described as better capitalized. CREDIT_SPREADS near 53 only on the weak directional basis, not a level claim.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (slack remains); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (accommodative/supportive); CREDIT_SPREADS in 45-55 with direction-only information; BANKING_STRESS < 50; DOLLAR_STRENGTH > 50; TREASURY_STRESS 35 with no dysfunction described. All satisfied.

**Coherence checks.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 44: aligned.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 44 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 60: 16 point gap. Explained: spending is running above trend (growth above potential), but the level of the gap is still negative after years of slack; the dimension measures pace vs trend while the gap measures level.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 53: modest divergence (15). Explained: spreads have only a weak directional (widening, energy-high-yield) signal with Low confidence, while the broader conditions discussion (credit availability, low rates, stocks) is supportive.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, Treasury stress low, consistent.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 32 vs sub-components (goods 26, expectations 42): consistent.

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, EQUITY_VALUATION (no valuation view), INFLATION_SERVICES (no statement of current services price pressure), INFLATION_HOUSING (housing discussed as activity, not prices).

**Text issues.** Many staff-Q&A turns appear as isolated member questions with the staff replies removed; a few fragments (for example Bullard's exchange with Mr. Kamin, "Which it clearly is.") are one-sided but do not affect scoring. Late in the file a small number of speaker lines are unattributed procedural. No passage was garbled enough to affect a score.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-5, High.** Evidence: Yellen: "the 12-month rate likely to drop below 1 percent early next year"; Dudley: "the core deflator has remained stuck at 1 1/2 percent". Center of gravity: inflation is clearly below target and the debate is only about its return. Direction: below neutral because headline and core are under 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Williams: "growth to run well above potential output through next year"; George: composite output gap "in the range of about 1 percent... about a year away from full capacity". Center: growth above trend closing a still-negative gap. Direction: below neutral because slack remains (Lockhart, Rosengren, Brainard).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 +-6, High.** Evidence: Fischer: unemployment "within hailing distance of the most recent estimates of the natural rate"; Lockhart: "I still see a fair measure of employment slack remaining". Center: strong improvement, level near but still short of full employment, wages tepid. Direction: below neutral by a small margin.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 +-6, High.** Evidence: Williams: "Consumer spending has been surprisingly strong"; Lacker: "investment in equipment and intangibles was strong in the past two quarters". Center: above-trend spending with oil-sector investment as a partial offset. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Dudley: "if financial conditions don't tighten at all, which has been the situation since we began to end the asset purchase program"; Dudley on credit availability "continuing to improve". Center: loose conditions with localized energy-credit stress. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Powell: "the Treasury market is, in fact, pricing in lower oil prices and stronger foreign demand for Treasuries". Center: orderly, demand-driven yield moves. Direction: low stress.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Fisher: "the real angst in the marketplace right now is in the junk bond market, and that is driven largely by the 18 percent that are marginal energy operators". Center: direction-only widening in one segment, no level claim; offset by improving credit availability. Direction: near neutral, slightly above on the weak directional signal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Only "higher stock prices" (Powell) as a spending support; no valuation view.

**BANKING_STRESS: 33 +-8, Low.** Evidence: Dudley: "the better-capitalized banking system"; Brainard: "the U.S. economy and financial system have become more resilient". Center: healthy US banks, minor European exposure worry. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 72 +-6, High.** Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar has continued its sharp appreciation"; Lockhart: "the impact of a stronger dollar on net exports". Center: sharp, repeatedly cited appreciation weighing on net exports. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 26.** Evidence: Yellen summarizing members: dollar appreciation "likely will lower non-energy commodity and imported goods prices"; Williams: declines in "energy prices, commodity prices, and import prices". Goods prices falling.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Williams: "I think they are actually anchored at 2 percent"; Kocherlakota: "the downside risk to our target may already be materializing". Anchored in surveys, drifting down in market measures.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 53 +-6, Moderate.** Evidence: Dudley: "I think we want the statement to be read as modestly hawkish"; Brainard: "remaining somewhat cautious on the removal of monetary accommodation". Center: hold at zero with "patient" language and liftoff expected in 2015, with hawkish and dovish wings. Direction: slightly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 65 +-6, High.** Evidence: Vote read-out: Fisher No, Kocherlakota No, Plosser No; Kocherlakota: "I'm unwilling to support alternative B" (wants stronger easing) vs Plosser: "I cannot support alternative B at this meeting" (wants earlier signal). Center: three dissents from opposite directions plus reservations among supporters, in civil tone.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 33 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Dudley on financial conditions as the transmission channel and avoiding a taper tantrum; Powell: "heightened risk of an emerging markets selloff or even a rout". Center: several members address it meaningfully, but as context and risk rather than the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen: "unlikely to begin the normalization process for at least the next couple of meetings"; Mester: "the time for lifting off from the zero lower bound is nearing". Center: patient with data dependence, liftoff approaching, signal modestly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +-7, Moderate.** Evidence: Fischer: "our forecasts have very wide, not narrow, confidence bands around them"; Tarullo: "there is a considerable amount of uncertainty that remains". Center: standard-plus caveats, global risks and model-sensitive inflation-compensation decompositions, but many with firm baselines.
