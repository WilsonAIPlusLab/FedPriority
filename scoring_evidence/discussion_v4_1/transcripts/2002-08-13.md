# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2002-08-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening questions during the staff Q&A (staff answers removed), a Reserve Bank/Board economic go-around (Moskow through Olson, then Bernanke through Jordan), a short policy round, and a vote read-out. Ned Gramlich, Kohn, and Bernanke speak; Kohn and Bernanke are in their first meeting.

**2. Era context.** Summer 2002: a weak recovery from the 2001 recession, equity declines, corporate-governance scandals, widening credit spreads, a funds rate of 1.75 percent, and low inflation. This is background only; scores follow the text.

**3. Speakers.** Nearly all participants report slower activity and downward-revised forecasts, with auto and housing strength, weak business investment, and weak labor markets. The staged financial-market concern comes from Greenspan, McDonough, Kohn, Ferguson, Olson, Minehan. Bernanke and Guynn downplay the financial headwinds. Policy views are described under Tier 3.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. "Inflation expected to remain well contained, core PCE about 1½ percent" (Parry); "little pricing power" (Moskow, Hoenig); "risks of inflation have receded" (Ferguson); Kohn: "inflation could fall further", at 1½ percent. Some isolated increases (steel tariff, health and insurance costs). Score about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. "Significant slack in resource utilization remains" (Parry); Ferguson cites a 1.7 percent output gap in 2002 and 1.3 in 2003; Kohn: margins of underutilized resources "could widen". Below potential, so bright line (<50) is met. Score about 34.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below midpoint. Unemployment about 6 percent (Kohn), "firms having no difficulty filling almost any position" (Hoenig), "labor is readily available" (Poole), employers reluctant to hire. Guynn's note that unemployment is below some old NAIRU estimates is a minor offset. Score about 33.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly below midpoint on net. Housing and autos strong (Bies: near record highs; Gramlich calls these bright spots), consumer spending resilient but confidence down; business investment weak ("hunkered down", Guynn; "very strong wariness", Olson), with a tentative rise in equipment and software. Net weighted toward investment weakness and a downshift. Score about 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend. "Structural productivity growth ... above 2 percent" (Broaddus); Greenspan cites 5.7 percent four-quarter and 4.0 percent in Q2 for nonfinancial corporations; Bernanke: productivity growth "most crucial element of the recovery". Score about 66.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Above midpoint (tighter). Equity prices down sharply, "financial market conditions have tightened in some respects" (Kohn), banks tightening terms, "corporate bond market has virtually dried up", net equity issuance negative and "conditions far less accommodative" (Ferguson), noninvestment-grade credit standards tighter (Hoenig). Offsets: mortgage rates at thirty-two-year lows, falling long yields, falling dollar (Bernanke, Bies). Bernanke thinks the financial headwinds "are not very strong". Words of "accommodative" apply to policy, not conditions, so the bright line is not triggered. Score about 60.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as dysfunction; Greenspan's remark about a marked decline in the ten-year yield is a flight-to-quality yield move, not dysfunction. The bid-ask spread discussion concerns Ford/GMAC debt (corporate), and market-maker reluctance (Kohn) is about corporate/risk markets. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Above midpoint on level. Greenspan: "major widening of spreads in a number of financial markets", "evidently significant flight to quality", Citigroup's spread wider than that of lower-rated Gillette (McDonough), bid-asked spreads on auto finance debt opened sharply, corporate bond market near shut, worst bond-portfolio losses for an insurer, "widening credit market spreads" (Minehan), "risk spreads were starting to open up" (Kohn). These indicate wide spreads with reduced access, not just direction. Bright-line (>50) met. Score about 68.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss a big drop, much volatility and concern, and Greenspan's note that equity premiums are above 1994 averages and prices may be below "fair" value by some measures, though fair value is ill-defined; Bernanke notes the market is "searching for a consensus valuation". Concern is about depressed, volatile, scandal-hit markets rather than overvaluation. Score about 60.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed, below midpoint. Healthy: Guynn (no credit crunch, problem assets low), Olson (loan quality strong), Bernanke ("banks are healthy"), McDonough (profitable, good capital, but risk-averse, a "fragility" in the sector, weak syndicated market, European insurers and Japanese banks impaired). Stress pockets: agricultural lenders (Jordan), one regional banking organization hit (Minehan), tighter lending standards. Score about 38.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Briefly touched: "falling dollar" (Bernanke), "decline in the dollar" (Jordan), Kohn: foreign weakness "has kept the dollar from falling". Direction is depreciation (bright line <50). Score about 36, low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says contained, so below 50 matches. OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR evidence say slack, so below 50 matches. CREDIT_SPREADS higher = more stress; evidence says wide, flight to quality, so above 50 matches. BANKING_STRESS healthy/sound, so below 50 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS tighter, so above 50 matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: all participants support leaving the funds rate at 1.75 percent. Greenspan and Vice Chair McDonough say it is premature to ease; the recommendation shifts the balance-of-risks statement toward economic weakness. Minehan says she would have favored an easing; Bernanke, Kohn, Ferguson want to be ready to ease (Greenspan, Moskow, Bernanke: any move should be aggressive, 50bp or more); Broaddus, Hoenig, Parry, Poole, Guynn, Santomero, Moskow lean toward waiting and see risks as more balanced or prefer to use press-statement wording. Net: hold with a clear easing bias. Lean about 37.
- Dissent: no dissenting votes (unanimous roll call). There are several expressed reservations about the tilt (Broaddus, Poole, Parry, Guynn, Hoenig, Santomero, Moskow), and Minehan (would have eased), but they are tactical/communication differences, with Broaddus the most explicit and others saying they can live with it. Score about 35.
- Financial stability attention: High. Greenspan's opening remarks centre on spreads, corporate-governance effects on bank and equity prices, McDonough on financial-sector fragility and Brazil banks, Kohn on market tightening, Ferguson on equity/bond issuance, Olson on bankers, Bernanke on market noise. Score about 62.
- Forward guidance: the balance-of-risks sentence moves toward weakness; participants say it will be read as a signal of a possible cut; Poole's alternative is rates staying put longer. Score about 33 (easing lean with data dependence).
- Uncertainty: "a lot of uncertainty" (Bernanke), Broaddus: "unusually large number of significant downside risks ... very difficult to quantify", Jordan: "uncertainties is the right word, not risks", Greenspan: factors we "cannot capture" in models, Ferguson, Bies "very unusual times". Elevated to high. Score about 66.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (slack). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS > 50 (conditions tightened, not described as "accommodative"). CREDIT_SPREADS > 50 (wide level stated). BANKING_STRESS < 50 (sound/healthy). DOLLAR_STRENGTH < 50 (depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs LABOR 33: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 34 vs CONSUMPTION 42: gap of 8, within tolerance (housing/auto strength lifts consumption). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 60 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 68: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A. INFLATION parent 32 vs sub-components (goods about 40, services about 52, housing about 62, expectations about 47): broadly consistent given that the parent is anchored to core PCE of 1½ percent and sub-component evidence is thin and mixed.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant describes Treasury-market dysfunction; only yield moves are mentioned.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: "we continue to expect inflation to remain well contained, with the core PCE price index increasing about 1½ percent next year" (Parry); "the risks of inflation, if anything, have receded" (Ferguson).
Center of gravity: inflation is low and falling, with isolated cost pressures. Inflation is below neutral because participants cite weak pricing power and the risk that it falls further.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: "significant slack in resource utilization certainly remains" (Parry); output gap "stays stubbornly high at 1.7 in 2002 and 1.3 in 2003" (Ferguson).
Center of gravity: slack is large and expected to persist. OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because the economy is below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: "firms having no difficulty filling almost any position" (Hoenig); "6 percent unemployment" (Kohn).
Center of gravity: soft labor demand, hiring reluctance, unemployment about 6 percent. Labor is looser than neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: "housing and auto sales remain near record highs" (Bies); "businesses still very much hunkered down, putting off both investment spending and additions to staff" (Guynn).
Center of gravity: resilient consumers and housing, weak business investment and downshifting. Spending is a little below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 66 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: "Structural productivity growth still appears to be running above 2 percent" (Broaddus); productivity "the most crucial element of the recovery" (Bernanke).
Center of gravity: productivity growth is strong and mentioned by many. It is above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±8 [High]**
Evidence: "financial market conditions have tightened in some respects" (Kohn); "conditions are far less accommodative than they were even a short time ago" (Ferguson).
Center of gravity: equity, bond, and bank credit conditions tightened with an offset from low mortgage rates; Bernanke judges the headwinds mild. Conditions are tighter than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no discussion of Treasury market functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 68 ±8 [High]**
Evidence: "a major widening of spreads in a number of financial markets" (Greenspan); "the corporate bond market has virtually dried up in the last month or so" (Kohn).
Center of gravity: spreads are described as wide with flight to quality and impaired access. Spreads are above neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 60 ±10 [Moderate]**
Evidence: "equity premiums that have risen in the latest period above their 1994 averages ... stock prices by some measures may be undervalued" (Greenspan); "searching for a consensus valuation without too much luck" (Bernanke).
Center of gravity: equity markets are depressed and volatile after the scandals, with concern about wealth and investment effects. Above neutral on the stress scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
Evidence: "banks are healthy" (Bernanke); "banks have been quite profitable. Their capital ratios are good" yet "fragility in the financial sector" (McDonough).
Center of gravity: banks are sound with risk aversion, tightened standards, and some stress pockets (agriculture lenders, one New England bank). Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 36 ±10 [Low]**
Evidence: "a falling dollar" (Bernanke); "the decline in the dollar" (Jordan).
Center of gravity: brief references to dollar depreciation. Below neutral because the dollar is described as falling.

**Inflation sub-components (values only)**
- INFLATION_GOODS 40: steel tariff price rises, but "prices of finished goods ... remarkably stable" (McDonough), tame commodity prices (Ferguson).
- INFLATION_SERVICES 54: health care, insurance, tuition costs rising (Moskow, Guynn, Poole).
- INFLATION_HOUSING 62: home prices strongly up ("up 15 percent", Stern; Greenspan on housing demand), though rents stabilizing or weakening.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47: "inflation expectations appear relatively unchanged" (Ferguson); anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 37 ±6 [High]**
Evidence: "I would recommend that we stay where we are with respect to rates today ... does shift the balance of risks statement toward weakness" (Greenspan); "I would have ... voted for an easing in policy" (Minehan).
Center of gravity: unanimous hold with an easing tilt and readiness to cut aggressively. Below 50 because the stance moves toward easing, but hold-the-rate pulls toward the 40s.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 ±7 [High]**
Evidence: unanimous roll call; "I wouldn't fall on the sword on this issue" (Hoenig); Broaddus: "I would not favor moving the tilt either".
Center of gravity: no dissents, but a sizable minority of reluctant supporters of the tilt change and one would-be easer. Moderate differences, mostly tactical.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 ±8 [High]**
Evidence: Greenspan's opening on the widening of spreads and governance effects on bank and equity prices; McDonough on "fragility in the financial sector".
Center of gravity: market conditions and credit are discussed by most participants and anchor much of the policy debate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 33 ±7 [Moderate]**
Evidence: "shifts the balance of risks statement toward weakness" (Greenspan); "if we do move, we should make a move of 50 basis points" (Moskow).
Center of gravity: the statement shifts toward easing with data dependence; no commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±8 [High]**
Evidence: "an unusually large number of significant downside risks ... very difficult to quantify" (Broaddus); "there's a lot of uncertainty" (Bernanke).
Center of gravity: many participants stress unusual uncertainty from governance, markets, Brazil, and geopolitics, though baseline forecast remains moderate recovery.
