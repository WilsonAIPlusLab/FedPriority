# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2001-08-21 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: the economic go-around (district reports and national views, Broaddus through Kelley), then the Chairman's policy proposal and a policy go-around, a short exchange on the press-statement wording, and a roll-call vote read-out. Staff turns have been removed, so only participants' questions and views remain. The text has page markers, which I ignored. I found no passage garbled enough to leave the meaning unclear.

**2. Era context.** Summer 2001. The text describes a year-old slowdown driven by collapsed high-tech capital spending and an inventory correction. Policy easing began early in the year and has reached 275bp, with 300bp total after today's move. Tax rebate checks are arriving, energy prices have fallen, and the foreign sector is weak (Argentina, Asia, Japan). Housing and consumer spending are holding up. I scored only what participants said.

**3. Speakers.**
- Broaddus sees faint signs of an inflection point and says the Committee should prepare the public for a possible reversal.
- McTeer says the news keeps disappointing and that a large action is called for on the economy alone.
- Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Stern, Guynn, Santomero and Hoenig give mostly weak district reports and a downside-tilted outlook.
- Jordan gives upbeat anecdotes from the Fourth District.
- McDonough sees a significant recession risk and warns about Argentina and China.
- Ferguson and Gramlich argue strongly for easing. Ferguson calls the baseline "more hope than reality"; Gramlich calls for a "monetary booster shot".
- Meyer presents FRB/US simulations showing financial conditions have not eased on net, and he also worries about overshooting.
- Kelley is edging toward symmetric risks.
- Greenspan describes absorption of capital losses and rising risk premiums, with household demand propped up by housing. He proposes a 25bp cut with risks weighted toward weakness.
- Poole discusses the forward-rate structure.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Parry expects core PCE just below 2 percent. Hoenig says "inflationary pressures continue to subside". McDonough says inflation and input costs "continue to recede". Greenspan sees "no pricing power" and a lower inflation rate ahead. Offsets are modest: Meyer has inflation "a bit above" his long-run target, Poole notes tight sales-associate labor and health costs up 20-22 percent, and Jordan notes tuition up 9-10 percent. Magnitude: "subsiding / no pricing power" gives roughly 12 points under 50, so about 38.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Growth is about 1 percent, below potential, with capacity utilization falling (Guynn) and unemployment set to rise to about 5 percent by 2002 (McDonough). Minehan notes two quarters more than 1 point below potential. Several members say the economy "will return to potential" only later. Meyer's remark that the economy is "close to capacity" is about the recovery phase. Score about 37.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Slightly below the midpoint and easing. Many report layoffs, easing labor markets, wage pressures subsided, and payroll declines (Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Hoenig, McDonough). Offsetting tight spots are Wal-Mart sales associates (Poole), skilled labor in Jordan's District, and unemployment described as "still relatively low" (Broaddus). Score about 43.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net below trend. Capital spending has collapsed ("serious investment collapse", "capital spending bubble that has collapsed"). Housing is strong and consumers are "moderate to good" but tiring in places. Business investment is weighted heavily. Score about 35.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mixed and thinly discussed. Gramlich says the productivity spurt is "partly undone" by data revisions. Minehan says using existing capital fully "augurs well". Poole ties the outlook to productivity. Roughly at trend, with low confidence. Score 50.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed, slightly tight. Meyer's simulations show conditions "actually have become more restrictive" if measured from mid-2000, because equity prices are lower and the dollar higher. Greenspan sees a rise in risk premiums in capital goods markets. Minehan and McDonough report tighter bank credit standards. Against that, Hoenig calls *policy* accommodative; that is policy, not conditions, so I did not score it as such. Meyer also says easing is meant to produce "a more accommodative overall set of financial conditions", but he finds that has not happened net. No member states that conditions are accommodative or tight in plain terms, so the bright-line rule does not bind. Score 54.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Poole addresses Treasury yields, covering the 5-10 year forward rate, long rates not falling, and an "unusual" long-end/short-end pattern. That is yield behavior, not dysfunction. Under the rule it scores 36-50. Score 42, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan reports bond financing "not much of a problem" but large lower-rated firms "shut out of credit markets", smaller healthy firms "facing higher rates", and difficulty renewing revolvers. McDonough reports tighter credit standards and rising delinquencies. No spread level is stated. This is reduced access in the lower-quality segments, so "somewhat wide / less favorable" (55-65). Score 57, Low confidence. The bright-line rule is not violated: no "narrow/compressed" language and no high-level language.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Discussed. Gramlich says tech S&P P/Es are high again, "possibly foreshadowing more declines", while P/E ratios on most stocks are "reasonably stable", and he says nobody knows how to interpret equities. Ferguson notes a "declining stock market". Greenspan discusses earnings and profits. Valuation views are mixed, with tech rich and the rest fair. Price falls are not valuation views. Score 47.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Santomero says bankers seek business among "well-capitalized firms" and "loan quality remains relatively good". McDonough reports rising delinquency and tighter standards. Guynn worries about home-equity and sub-prime lending. Pockets of stress, system sound. Score 44, Low confidence. Bright line: the "sound/healthy" language is about borrowers and loan quality, so I stayed below 50 but only just.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Stern flags "the recent decline in the dollar". Gramlich says "the dollar hadn't really gone down until a couple of weeks ago". Kelley cites "a somewhat lower dollar". These are recent declines. Meyer, Santomero and Guynn describe the dollar as high or appreciated over the longer period. The direction in the discussion is recent depreciation, so < 50 per the bright-line rule. Score 42.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Heavy discounting in retail, autos and trucking. Hotel, airline and retail pricing is weak; McDonough reports selling prices "flat to lower". Score 33.
- SERVICES: Mixed. Tuition is up 9-10 percent (Jordan), health benefits are up sharply (Poole, McDonough), and utilities are up, against hotel, airline and trucking discounting. Score 50.
- HOUSING: No participant discusses rents or shelter cost inflation; the talk is about home prices as asset prices, housing activity, and capital gains. Mark N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Broaddus says "we don't need a recession to contain inflation or inflation expectations". Poole warns of inflation pressure only if potential growth falls. Anchored near target. Score 46.
- Coherence: the parent at 38 sits above the goods sub-score of 33 and just below the services and expectations sub-scores of 50 and 46. Consistent given the heavy weight on discounting and subsiding pressures in the parent.

**7. Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee cuts 25bp with risks weighted toward weakness, and the vote is unanimous. McTeer says a large action is "called for", and Ferguson and Gramlich are strongly pro-easing. Several support it with reservations (Parry "close call", Stern, Guynn, Jordan, Broaddus, Minehan, Meyer). The mood is "moderate dovish", with an eye on an exit. Score 34.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: No formal dissents; 10 listed votes are all Yes. There are numerous reservations about lags and exit strategy, and Broaddus pushes for different statement wording, with Hoenig opposing a longer statement and Greenspan declining. Real divergence on assessments: Jordan and Broaddus see improvement, Ferguson and Gramlich see more downside. But everyone ends up in the same place. Score 28.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Moderate to limited. Meyer devotes a long passage to financial conditions and pass-through. Poole discusses the term structure. Greenspan discusses risk premia and capital losses. Guynn warns of home-equity lending risk. McDonough warns of "chaotic financial markets" from Argentina. Minehan raises credit availability. Several address it meaningfully but the main focus is the real economy. Score 40.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Statement keeps the weakness bias and the funds rate path is expected to ease further ("we have not as yet completed our easing process"), but there is explicit discussion that a bottom is near ("a meeting away, maybe two") and that an exit is needed. Broaddus's push to signal an end of easing is not adopted. Easing lean with data dependence. Score 33.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: McDonough cites "a very great deal of uncertainty"; Greenspan says "a good deal of uncertainty" and that the usual models do not apply; Ferguson and Kelley and others call the baseline hope rather than reality; Guynn and Hoenig note difficulty reading cyclical vs structural. Argentina is described as potentially chaotic. Elevated but not extreme. Score 55.

**8. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION means more pressure; the evidence says subsiding, so below 50 matches. Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential; evidence is below potential, so below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter; evidence is easing, so below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS means more stress; reduced access means above 50. Higher DOLLAR_STRENGTH means appreciation; the recent depreciation gives below 50. Higher HAWKISH means tighter; this is an easing meeting, so below 50.

**9. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 37 (< 50, consistent with "below potential"). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 54, since no member states plain "accommodative" or "tight" conditions. CREDIT_SPREADS 57 (> 50). BANKING_STRESS 44 (< 50, sound language but mixed). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 42 (< 50, recent weakening). TREASURY_STRESS 42 (in the normal range; yield behavior only).

**10. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 37 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 43: 6 apart, consistent with unemployment relatively low (4.5 percent area) while growth is subpar.
- OUTPUT_GAP 37 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 35: 2 apart, consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 54 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 57: co-move, both slightly tight.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS 42: normal-times pattern.
- Inflation parent vs sub-components: consistent as above.

**11. N/A justification.** INFLATION_HOUSING is N/A because no participant discusses rents or shelter cost inflation. Everything else was addressed by at least one participant.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "inflationary pressures continue to subside in our area". Greenspan, "there is no pricing power in markets across the nation... we will have a lower rate of inflation". Parry, core PCE "just below 2 percent both this year and next".
- Center of gravity: Participants see disinflationary pressure from slack and discounting, partly offset by health and tuition costs.
- Direction: Below neutral because slack and discounting dominate.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 37 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Parry, "growth of around 1 percent in the current quarter". McDonough, growth "below what we believe is potential, so the unemployment rate will rise". Guynn, "capacity utilization continuing to fall".
- Center of gravity: A year-long slowdown with output below potential and recovery repeatedly pushed back.
- Direction: Below neutral because participants describe slack and sub-potential growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Moskow, "less difficult to hire and retain workers in most areas and wage pressures have subsided further". Poole, Wal-Mart "finds labor market conditions still tight for sales associates". Broaddus, "unemployment still relatively low".
- Center of gravity: Labor markets are loosening from a tight level, with pockets of tightness.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because layoffs and softening dominate, and the level of unemployment is only moderate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Kelley, "serious investment collapse" with the consumer possibly "tiring". Santomero, "housing market remains robust and consumer spending remains moderate to good". Stern, "a capital spending bubble that has now collapsed".
- Center of gravity: Housing and consumer spending are holding up but business investment has collapsed, netting below trend.
- Direction: Below neutral because investment weakness dominates.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-9 (Low).**
- Evidence: Gramlich, "disappointing to see this productivity growth spurt... partly undone with new data". Minehan, using existing capital to its fullest "augurs well for future productivity".
- Center of gravity: Revised-down capital deepening against cost-cutting efficiency gains; roughly trend.
- Direction: At neutral because the positives and negatives roughly offset.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 54 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Meyer, "on net financial conditions have actually become more restrictive rather than more stimulative if we begin the accounting from mid-2000". Greenspan, "a significant rise in risk premiums in the capital goods markets".
- Center of gravity: Easing in short rates has been offset by lower equity prices, a higher dollar, and tighter bank credit, leaving conditions slightly tight rather than loose.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because no net easing in conditions is seen.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Poole, long-end forward rates "very, very unusual" relative to the short end, but no dysfunction described.
- Center of gravity: An orderly market with unusual curve behavior; no stress.
- Direction: Below neutral because only yield behavior is discussed.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 57 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Minehan, "Big firms with lower credit ratings are reportedly shut out of credit markets and smaller healthy firms are facing higher rates". McDonough, bankers report "tighter credit standards".
- Center of gravity: Credit access has tightened for lower-quality and smaller borrowers, though investment-grade bond financing is fine.
- Direction: Above neutral because access is reduced and rates higher for weaker borrowers; no spread levels were stated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Gramlich, tech S&P P/E ratios "again showing high price-earnings ratios, possibly foreshadowing more declines", while "price-earnings ratios on most stocks have been reasonably stable". Ferguson, "declining stock market performance".
- Center of gravity: Mixed valuation views with tech still rich and the broad market near fair.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because the tech overvaluation concern roughly offsets the concern about falling prices.

**BANKING_STRESS: 44 +-8 (Low).**
- Evidence: Santomero, bankers "actively seeking new business borrowers among well-capitalized firms. Loan quality remains relatively good." McDonough, "rising delinquency rates, and tighter credit standards".
- Center of gravity: Banks are sound but see rising delinquencies and tighten standards.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral because the system is sound with minor pockets of stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Stern, "the recent decline in the dollar raises at least a yellow flag". Kelley, "a somewhat lower dollar". Meyer, "the dollar is higher" over the easing episode.
- Center of gravity: The dollar has been high but is recently weakening, with the recent move the focus.
- Direction: Below neutral because the recent move discussed is depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 33.** Guynn, "deep discounting is being used to bolster sales"; McDonough, retailers report "selling prices and merchandise costs are flat to lower".

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 50.** Offsetting pressures: Jordan, tuition "up 9 to 10 percent"; Poole, Wal-Mart health-care costs "up 20 to 22 percent"; against discounting in hotels and travel (Guynn).

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** No rent or shelter-cost inflation is discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46.** Broaddus, "we don't need a recession to contain inflation or inflation expectations". Anchored, with slight downside risk.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 34 +-6 (High).**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I would put on the table a reduction of 25 basis points with the risks weighted toward economic weakness". Gramlich, "there does seem to be a need for a monetary booster shot". Parry, "a close call... I do support a 25 basis point cut... as some insurance".
- Center of gravity: A unanimous 25bp cut, with several supporters reluctant and focused on an exit, and a few (McTeer, Ferguson, Gramlich) wanting more.
- Direction: Dovish because the Committee is easing and keeping the weakness bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-6 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Vote read-out shows all listed voters "Yes". Parry, "for me the policy choice is a close call". Hoenig on statement length: "I do not want an expanded statement", against Broaddus's wish for an extra phrase.
- Center of gravity: Zero dissents but a handful of reservations about lags, exit and wording; economic views differ a bit (Jordan, Broaddus vs Ferguson, Gramlich).
- Direction: Low-to-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Meyer's long FRB/US simulation on financial conditions; McDonough, "we could have some very chaotic financial markets"; Guynn on home-equity and sub-prime lending risk.
- Center of gravity: Several participants address financial conditions meaningfully, though the real economy is the main focus.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 33 +-7 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we have not as yet completed our easing process"; also "we have to be clear that there is a bottom to this... a meeting away from that, maybe two". Broaddus's request for language that the easing cycle "may now be approaching an end" is not adopted.
- Center of gravity: The easing bias is retained but with explicit recognition that a bottom and an exit are near.
- Direction: Easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 (Moderate).**
- Evidence: McDonough, "a very great deal of uncertainty about this short-term growth outlook". Greenspan, the process is "largely without historical precedent... not what we have embodied in our standard econometric models". Ferguson, baseline "is a hope more than a reality".
- Center of gravity: Several participants emphasise unusual uncertainty and downside risk, but not Knightian.
- Direction: Elevated.
