# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting dated 2007-10-31

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript: the economic go-around (afternoon session of the first day), the Chair's summary, the policy go-around the next morning, the Chair's recommendation, a short communications and projections item, and the vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Members' questions to staff are procedural or probing and are not scored as views.

**Era context (not used to override text).** Autumn 2007. Housing correction, subprime and ABCP stress that began in August, a 50bp cut at the previous meeting, and a decision on a further 25bp cut.

**Speakers and views.**
- Hoenig: growth near 2.5 percent; inflation upside risk (dollar, commodities, TIPS); wants to hold and dissents.
- Plosser: markets improved; inflation risks tilted up; "troubled" by a cut; prefers to wait until December.
- Rosengren: downside risks; markets fragile; prefers to wait (but ends up on the yes side).
- Fisher: district strong, labor shortages; worried about food, energy and inflation; would not cut.
- Yellen: output near potential, inflation near objective, policy somewhat restrictive; favors a cut.
- Pianalto: weakness spreading; nonperforming loans rising; favors a cut.
- Lacker: housing worse; favors a cut with no tilt.
- Lockhart: favors a cut; heightened uncertainty.
- Evans: data better; risks two-sided; "close call"; supports a cut.
- Stern: supports a cut "with misgivings".
- Poole: "reluctantly" supports a cut; wants to signal it is enough.
- Geithner, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Bernanke: favor a 25bp cut with the "roughly balanced" language (alternative A).

**Dimension by dimension (direction in words first).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Participants see core inflation as contained and below 2 percent (Hoenig 1.8, Yellen 1.75, Geithner "below 2 percent", Stern "well contained"). Headline is pushed up by food and energy, and many see upside risks (Fisher, Warsh, Hoenig, Lockhart, Kohn, Chair "most participants saw inflation risks to the upside"). Net is slightly above midpoint: modestly elevated pressure and risk, not above target on core. Score 54.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Yellen: "output near potential". Kohn: "keep the economy near its potential". Rosengren: "very close to full employment". Others expect a few weak quarters of below-trend growth. This sits at potential, so the 48-52 band applies. Score 49.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. Yellen: unemployment "very near" full employment. Fisher: wages rising on "labor shortages". Evans: skilled-worker shortages but weak temp help. Chair: "less concern about tightness in labor markets", employment "reasonably solid". Balanced, near NAIRU (unemployment about 4.7 percent). Score 50.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Spending outside housing was "well maintained" and stronger than expected (Kohn, Evans, Stern). Residential investment is "miserable" and in "severe contraction". Consumer sentiment is down, card spending is flat in October (Warsh), nonresidential is slowing. Net is somewhat below trend. Score 45.
- PRODUCTIVITY. Only passing remarks: Lacker expects somewhat higher trend, Yellen cites a "slower rate of structural productivity growth", Hoenig sees potential growth declining. Mixed and slight. Score 46, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Improved since September and August but "not back to normal". Banks are tightening standards "across the board" (Yellen), the senior loan officer survey shows tightening, securitization is impaired, and jumbo and CRE financing is tight. Corporate funding for good borrowers is fine. Somewhat tight. Score 60.
- TREASURY_STRESS. Yellen: liquidity conditions and Treasury bill markets "still at times strained". Warsh: the Treasury bill market "quite volatile" and not back to normal. This is a limited signal of bill-market strain, not auction failure. Score 55.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Level reading. Spreads widened in the past two weeks and are described as elevated: the jumbo premium "remains elevated", CRE spreads "still very high" (Mishkin), "spreads are much wider" for CRE (Chair), "big, widening credit spreads" (Mishkin). Warsh says risk premiums were abnormally low before August. The Chair says commercial paper spreads are down for good borrowers. Net: above normal in housing and structured credit, improved elsewhere. Level stated high in several segments, so above 50. Score 62.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Equities are mentioned only in price terms (flat since June, rose since September, hit by write-downs). Warsh says credit spreads and equities may have "gotten ahead of underlying reality" two weeks ago. No real valuation view. Default near neutral. Score 48, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS. Mixed. Pianalto reports a "sudden and sizable" rise in nonperforming loans at large banks. Warsh says institutions are on defense and profitability is "called into question". Kroszner and the Chair say balance-sheet constraint concerns are somewhat less. Lenders "very skittish". Emerging stress with the system still functioning. Score 57.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Depreciation: "trade-weighted dollar celebrated a post-Bretton Woods low" (Fisher), "continuing dollar depreciation" (Pianalto), Warsh worries about a disorderly move. Weakening, so below 50. Score 26.
- Inflation sub-components. GOODS: Fisher on import prices from China, paper products and commodities; Hoenig on commodities. Scored modestly above 50. SERVICES: not discussed, N/A. HOUSING: shelter or rent inflation not discussed (house prices falling is not shelter inflation), N/A. EXPECTATIONS: TIPS measures have risen, surveys are stable, "anchored" but fragile (Hoenig, Geithner, Mishkin, Kroszner). Score 55.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean. A 25bp cut was adopted (alternative A) with "roughly balanced" risk language, a signal that easing is not open-ended. The majority favors some easing, but several are reluctant, and three or more other participants (Plosser, Fisher, Rosengren) lean toward holding. Moderate dovish with a pause signal. Score 38.
- Disagreement. One formal dissent (Hoenig). Reservations from Plosser (strongly: "troubled by a cut"), Fisher, Rosengren, Stern ("misgivings"), Poole ("reluctantly") and Pianalto (torn). Extended debate on the rationale and the wording. The disagreement is mostly about tactics and timing, with some difference on inflation versus growth risk. Score 42.
- Financial stability emphasis. Most participants discuss markets at length, in both rounds, and the Chair gives a long financial-markets segment. Prominent. Score 68.
- Forward guidance. The Committee deliberately moves to "roughly balanced" risks. Yellen: "this is not yet another step in a planned series of continuing rate cuts". Kohn: "preemptive but not open ended". Poole: "we've done enough". Lacker and Poole mention possible firming or taking cuts back. Net neutral, slightly easing residual. Score 48.
- Uncertainty. The Chair: "an unusual amount of uncertainty around the modal forecast... still a great deal". Lockhart, Geithner, Mishkin, Kohn, Pianalto and Stern all stress heightened uncertainty about financial effects and housing. Elevated to high. Score 62.

**Polarity checks.** Higher dollar score means a stronger dollar; the evidence is depreciation, so the score is low. Higher credit spreads score means more stress; the evidence is wider, elevated spreads in housing and structured credit, so above 50. Higher hawkish score means tighter policy; the evidence is a cut, so below 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 49 (at potential, 48-52). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 60 (tightening, above 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 62 (level stated high, above 50). BANKING_STRESS 57 (not described as sound or healthy). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 26 (below 50). TREASURY_STRESS 55 (no actual dysfunction beyond strain; at the cap of the 45-55 band).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 49 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 50 are consistent. OUTPUT_GAP 49 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 45 are within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 60 and CREDIT_SPREADS 62 co-move. TREASURY_STRESS 55 is mild, in line with the stress. INFLATION_PRESSURE 54 is consistent with GOODS about 58 and EXPECTATIONS 55.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not raised by participants.

**Text quality.** Clean text. Staff turns removed, so some answers to member questions are missing, but members' own statements are intact.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 ±6, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "core consumer inflation currently is at a level that I consider consistent with price stability"; Warsh, "considerably less sanguine about inflation prospects and risks than the Greenbook". Center of gravity: contained core with a headline and risk tilt up. Inflation pressure is slightly above neutral because most see upside risks while core is below 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 49 ±5, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "output near potential"; Kohn, "keep the economy near its potential". Output gap is at neutral because members see the economy near potential with some below-trend quarters ahead.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 ±6, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "unemployment is very near my best estimate of the full employment rate"; Fisher, "widespread reports of labor shortages". Labor market is at neutral: near full employment with offsetting soft spots in temp help and construction.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 ±6, Moderate.** Evidence: Kohn, "Spending outside of housing has been well maintained"; Poole, "the housing sector is obviously in miserable condition". Spending is below trend on net because the housing collapse and softening anecdotes outweigh resilient non-housing demand.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 46 ±8, Low.** Evidence: Lacker, "somewhat higher estimate of trend productivity growth"; Yellen, "a slower rate of structural productivity growth". Productivity is slightly below neutral to neutral: passing and mixed remarks.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "banks are tightening lending standards across the board"; Chair, "securitization remains impaired". Conditions are tighter than neutral because credit standards and housing financing tightened despite improved market functioning.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 55 ±7, Low.** Evidence: Yellen, "liquidity conditions and Treasury bill markets are still at times strained"; Warsh, "the Treasury bill market has been quite volatile". Treasury stress is slightly above neutral because of bill-market strain without dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Chair, "spreads are much wider" for commercial real estate; Mishkin, "spreads for commercial real estate are still very high". Spreads are above normal in the housing, structured and CRE segments, though they have eased from August peaks and are fine for good corporates.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8, Low.** Evidence: Yellen, "the stock market is roughly unchanged since June"; Warsh, credit spreads and equities may have "gotten ahead of underlying reality". Equity view is near neutral because only price moves were discussed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 57 ±8, Moderate.** Evidence: Pianalto, "a sudden and sizable percentage increase in nonperforming loans"; Warsh, "profitability of most of these financial institutions has been significantly called into question". Banking stress is above neutral because of emerging loan problems and lender caution, but no solvency worry.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 26 ±6, High.** Evidence: Fisher, "the trade-weighted dollar celebrated a post-Bretton Woods low"; Pianalto, "continuing dollar depreciation". The dollar is weakening, so below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58.** Evidence: Fisher on stiffening Chinese import prices and commodity costs; Hoenig, "commodity prices, not just energy, show a rising trend". **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Evidence: Hoenig, "gradual upcreep in the TIPS measures of expected inflation"; Mishkin, "inflation expectations are contained".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Chair, "I favor a cut... 25 basis points with alternative A"; Plosser, "I am troubled by a cut today". Majority favors a modest cut with a pause signal, so the lean is moderately dovish and is below neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig, "I think that we should hold where we are" (the one No vote); Plosser, "troubled by a cut". One dissent plus several reluctant supporters, mostly about tactics and timing.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 68 ±7, High.** Evidence: Warsh's extended discussion of capital markets, intermediaries and money funds; Mishkin on credit-market skittishness and the "downward spiral". Financial conditions are a prominent topic for most participants.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 48 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Yellen, "this is not yet another step in a planned series of continuing rate cuts"; Kohn, "preemptive but not open ended". Mostly neutral and data-dependent, with a slight residual easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±7, Moderate.** Evidence: Chair, "an unusual amount of uncertainty around the modal forecast... still a great deal"; Geithner, "a huge amount of uncertainty about what equilibrium is". Elevated to high uncertainty about housing, credit and the equilibrium rate.
