# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - transcript 2002-03-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed): opening Q&A on Argentina, a SOMA collateral/contingency-planning discussion, members' questions to staff, the economic go-around, a policy Q&A on a simulation exercise, the policy go-around, the vote read-out, and a disclosure-policy discussion. Staff answers are absent, so several members' questions are fragmentary. Score participants' views only.

**Era context.** Early 2002, after the 2001 recession and the post-September 11 easing; funds rate 1.75 percent. The text itself says incoming data have turned sharply positive and the Greenbook was revised up a lot. I score what participants say, not hindsight.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), McDonough (Vice Chair), Governors Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Olson; Presidents Jordan, McTeer, Santomero, Stern, Parry, Poole, Moskow, Minehan, Hoenig, Broaddus, Guynn. Common view: recovery under way, much better than at the January meeting, risks now about balanced, business sentiment still cautious, productivity strong, inflation low (core PCE about 1.5 percent), policy accommodative, and rates "unsustainably low" (Guynn) so tightening is coming. Hoenig and Broaddus would consider moving today. Others (Stern, Guynn, Moskow, Greenspan) see no urgency.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Parry's core PCE forecast is "just under 1 1/2 percent"; Minehan says "very, very little inflation in the economy"; Hoenig says price pressures "without question remain low"; Stern hears "very little about inflationary pressures"; Greenspan says discounting is "not cost based" and will reverse, but "we will not get very strong inflationary pressures." Santomero notes prices-paid indexes turned positive. Low but with upward-drift hints: about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. Ferguson: "the level of GDP will still leave an output gap for the forecast period"; Gramlich: "high unemployment, low capacity utilization"; Moskow/Ferguson cite significant excess capacity. Moskow's reference to the gap being eliminated by end-2003 is a staff-forecast discussion. Hoenig's sentence "While the output gap is positive..." reads oddly (likely garbled or a slip, since he goes on to say the gap will close), so I do not let it override the explicit slack language. Bright line: less than 50. About 37.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below midpoint. Unemployment is about 5.5 percent against a NAIRU of about 5.25 (Bies); Parry: "a buyer's market... little or no upward pressure on wage inflation"; Minehan: labor markets "much easier"; Hoenig: "Labor markets are slack"; Ferguson: unemployment "stubbornly high". Some stabilization signs. UE 5.5 falls in the 31-45 band. About 38.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net near the midpoint. Consumption is strong and "persistent" (Minehan, Gramlich, Bies; auto sales at 16 million), but business investment is weak with excess capacity (Moskow, Ferguson, Bies, Greenspan). Participants stress a recovery weaker than typical (Santomero, Moskow), though the inventory swing and the Greenbook project 3-4 percent growth. Net about 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend. Minehan: "surprising growth in productivity"; Gramlich: "productivity spurt"; Guynn: "sustained rebound in productivity"; Greenspan: productivity and structural change. About 68.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Members mostly talk about policy stance (real funds rate "at or below zero"), which is policy not conditions. On conditions: low mortgage rates support housing (Guynn), money supply growing "at a very strong pace" (Guynn), stock market "coming back" (Gramlich), but long-term rates rising (Jordan, Greenspan) and bankers tightening standards on commercial borrowers (McDonough). Net somewhat accommodative. Bright line (accommodative means less than 50). About 40, wide band.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Treasuries came up only as a SOMA supply/collateral question (shrinking Treasury supply), with no functioning/dysfunction commentary.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A. No discussion of spreads or credit pricing by members.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: One explicit valuation comment (Minehan: "the very heights of the equity markets and PE ratios suggest some level of fragility"), against others noting stocks recovering (Gramlich, Olson). Mild above-fair-value view with little weight: about 42, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only McDonough's bankers' remark (tighter standards, delinquencies holding the same, less consumer-loan demand), plus Poole on GSE low capital ratios. Neutral, thin. About 47, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Parry: "ongoing strength of the dollar" hurting district exports; Jordan: currency "continuing firm"; McTeer: strong peso. Bright line (strength means more than 50). About 58, low confidence (brief).

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS: only scattered anecdotes (steel tariffs, oil), not a goods-price assessment, so N/A. SERVICES: one insurance-pricing anecdote, N/A. HOUSING: no shelter-inflation discussion, N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Minehan "inflation expectations ... continue to be low"; Jordan: no change in inflation premium in bond yields; Parry: Blue Chip long-run forecasts imply inflation drift up from 1.5 percent. Well anchored, slightly below target; 42. Consistent with the parent score (32 versus 42, expectations slightly higher on Blue Chip concern).

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Hold at 1.75 percent, move the risk statement to balanced, and add "accommodative" language to prepare a May move. Many speakers (Jordan, Hoenig, Broaddus, Guynn, Santomero, Minehan, Parry, Gramlich, Poole, Moskow) say rates must rise soon; Hoenig and Broaddus would tighten now. A few want patience (Stern, Guynn, Greenspan). Lean hawkish to moderate hawkish with a hold: about 60.
- Dissent: Unanimous roll call. Differences are on timing (today vs. May vs. later), pace (25 vs. 50bp, intermeeting possibility), and on whether 1 percent-range inflation is the goal (Minehan, Bies). Jordan says the January bias constrained him from wanting an increase now. No economic disagreement. Disclosure-policy objection by Minehan is outside the policy decision. About 20.
- Financial stability emphasis: Argentina/Q&A, GSE capital and SOMA-collateral discussion are not stability assessments by members; the economic round has passing mentions of equity valuations, credit standards, state fiscal strains. Brief mentions: about 15.
- Forward guidance: "Accommodative" in the statement and balanced risks are explicitly intended to allow a May move; Greenspan is "by no means certain" about May and wants 25bp steps, Poole/Broaddus/Hoenig want the door open to intermeeting moves. Mild tightening bias: about 64.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan on a turning point where the slope of the upturn is "very difficult to estimate" and "incomplete knowledge about the changing overall structure"; Ferguson "a great deal of uncertainty"; "March madness" for forecasters; business pessimism versus data. But most express fairly confident improvement and balanced risks. Elevated, not extreme: about 48.

**Polarity checks.** Higher output gap = more above potential; evidence says below, score below 50 (matches). Labor: higher = tighter; evidence says slack, below 50. Financial conditions: higher = tighter; evidence is accommodative, below 50. Dollar: strong, above 50. Hawkish: tightening bias with a hold, slightly above 50 and well below clear tightening.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP less than 50 (37), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS less than 50 (40), DOLLAR more than 50 (58), TREASURY/CREDIT N/A, BANKING neutral (47, no "sound/stressed" language).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 37 vs LABOR 38 (close); vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47 (10 apart; consumer strength offset by weak investment, within 15). FC 40 with CREDIT_SPREADS N/A and TREASURY N/A: no contradiction. Parent inflation 32 vs expectations 42: consistent (parent reflects low current readings, expectations somewhat higher).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, and GOODS/SERVICES/HOUSING not discussed as assessments by members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "if we really have a number that begins with 1, there is very, very little inflation in the economy." Parry: core PCE "just under 1 1/2 percent."
- Summary: Low, well-contained inflation with only mild upward drift expected as margins reopen.
- Direction: below neutral because multiple participants describe price pressures as low and discounting present.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 37 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the level of GDP will still leave an output gap for the forecast period." Gramlich: "high unemployment, low capacity utilization."
- Summary: Economy is below potential but recovering quickly. (Hoenig's "output gap is positive" phrasing is unclear and not relied on.)
- Direction: below neutral because slack in labor and capacity is repeatedly cited.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 38 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Parry: "it remains a buyer's market... little or no upward pressure on wage inflation." Hoenig: "Labor markets are slack."
- Summary: Unemployment about 5.5 percent, above NAIRU, with early stabilization.
- Direction: below neutral because slack and elevated unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "the continued willingness of consumers to buy autos and houses." Greenspan: "consumer spending is going to be only modestly helpful at best" and capital investment is the weak link.
- Summary: Solid consumption, weak investment, inventory swing; roughly trend.
- Direction: near neutral, slightly below, because strong consumption is offset by weak business spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 68 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "the surprising growth in productivity." Gramlich: "the productivity spurt raised profits."
- Summary: Productivity growth is seen as well above trend and sustained through the downturn.
- Direction: above neutral because multiple members describe unexpectedly strong gains.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Guynn: "The money supply continues to grow at a very strong pace"; low mortgage rates fuel housing. Greenspan: expects long-term rates to rise as the economy rebounds.
- Summary: Conditions supportive, though policy-stance talk dominates and rates are starting to rise.
- Direction: below neutral because conditions are described as accommodative/supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (not discussed as market functioning)

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A** (not discussed)

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "the very heights of the equity markets and PE ratios suggest some level of fragility." Gramlich: "The stock market is coming back."
- Summary: One member flags rich valuations, others note recovery; little valuation debate.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the only valuation view is mild overvaluation.

**BANKING_STRESS: 47 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: McDonough: bankers "are enforcing tighter credit standards on their commercial borrowers, and delinquency rates are holding about the same."
- Summary: Single neutral bank-lending remark.
- Direction: near neutral because mixed signals, no stress or soundness language.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Parry: "the ongoing strength of the dollar" is hurting District exports. Jordan: currency "continuing firm in foreign exchange markets."
- Summary: Dollar firm/strong, noted briefly.
- Direction: above neutral because strength is described.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42**
- Evidence: Minehan on inflation expectations "to be low at the end of 2003"; Jordan: "inflation premium in bond yields remaining more or less steady."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "keep the funds rate target unchanged at 1 3/4 percent and... move the balance of risks statement to balance"; Guynn: rates "unsustainably low. They will soon need to begin to rise." Broaddus: "one could make a case for tightening policy today."
- Summary: Hold today, with a broad expectation of tightening beginning by May.
- Direction: above neutral because tightening bias, with the hold keeping it modest.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Unanimous vote; Jordan: "I think we have to have no action this time"; Hoenig "moving sooner, maybe even today, may mean that we will have to move less later."
- Summary: Unanimous, with timing and pace differences only.
- Direction: low because no dissents and agreement on the economic assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 15 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Minehan on equity fragility and state fiscal stress; Poole on GSE capital ratios.
- Summary: Passing mentions only.
- Direction: low attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: indicate "monetary policy as accommodative... would enable us to move in May"; Broaddus: market will see it as "a switch in the tilt by two notches to a tilt toward tightness."
- Summary: Statement change is meant to prepare tightening while keeping May open.
- Direction: above neutral because the signal points toward tightening ahead.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "it is very difficult to estimate the slope of the upturn"; Ferguson: "we're facing a great deal of uncertainty."
- Summary: Turning-point uncertainty is emphasized, but with risks seen as balanced and a generally confident improving outlook.
- Direction: elevated but short of high.
