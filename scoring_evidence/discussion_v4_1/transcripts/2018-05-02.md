# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2018-05-02 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant turns from a two-day transcript (a first-day session and a May 2 session). Staff turns are removed, so some member questions to staff appear without the answers. It has these segments:
- A Desk and IOER technical discussion.
- Member questions during the staff Q&A.
- A financial-stability go-round.
- An economic go-round.
- A policy go-round on statement alternatives B and C.
- A closing vote read-out (8 Yes, no No).

**Era context.** The text reads as a mid-expansion hiking cycle. Unemployment is 4.1 percent, core PCE inflation has just reached 1.9 percent and headline 2.0 percent. Fiscal stimulus is large, tariffs and trade tension are rising, and the yield curve is flattening. These came from the speakers' words, not from outside recall. The Committee holds the rate this meeting and signals a likely June hike.

**Speakers and views.**
- Williams: expansion "very strong", growth "well above potential", unemployment drifting to 3.5 percent, four hikes this year.
- Quarles: economy "in as good a place as it's been for a long time", no overheating, patient.
- Rosengren: unemployment more than a full point below full employment, wants at least three more hikes, worries about imbalances.
- Evans: labor markets "obviously tight" but not overheating, inflation expectations still too low, gradual path.
- Mester: above-trend growth, biased toward hiking, "more risk we'll move rates up too slowly".
- Barkin: tight but wages moderate.
- Brainard: gradual, balanced risks, resource utilization could reach multi-decade extremes, wary of declaring victory.
- Kaplan: strong Texas economy, three hikes as the base case.
- Harker: confident inflation will exceed target, now considering four hikes.
- George: strong District, upside inflation risk and downside risks over the medium term.
- Bostic: close to full employment, cap-ex pushed out by tariff fears.
- Bullard: inflation expectations too low, Phillips curve weak, worried about a yield-curve inversion.
- Kashkari: still some slack, wants a neutral stance.
- Dudley: inflation near target but partly transitory, the flattening yield curve is not a worry.
- Powell: summarizes a strong outlook with gradual removal of accommodation.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as roughly at the 2 percent target, perhaps slightly above the midpoint. Core PCE is 1.9 and headline 2.0, with input-cost pressures and pass-through rising. Many caution against declaring victory (Williams, Powell, Brainard, Dudley), and Bullard and Kashkari stress that expectations remain low. Slightly above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy above potential. Evidence is Williams "well above potential", Mester "above-trend growth", Barkin "Output growth is above trend", and Brainard "growing above its trend rate" with resource utilization possibly at multi-decade extremes. Kashkari and Bostic dispute how far above. Clearly above 50 but short of overheating.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the market as tight. Unemployment is 4.1 percent, headed below 4, with widespread reports of worker shortages. Kashkari argues slack remains and Barkin, Bostic and Brainard find wages moderate. By the rubric's unemployment anchor (3.8-4.5 percent) this is in the 53-65 band.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as modestly above trend overall. Q1 consumption was soft but expected to rebound, business investment is strong, and fiscal stimulus is supportive. Bostic reports cap-ex pipelines pushed out on trade fears. Net is a little above 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Participants see it as weak. Powell has "weak productivity growth", Kaplan "continued sluggish productivity", Barkin "productivity growth apparently limited", and Bostic discusses the productivity puzzle. Below 50.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as supportive and only slightly tighter. Brainard says conditions "remain quite supportive", Powell "still-accommodative financial conditions", and Dudley "financial markets are still easy". Several note a modest tightening (the 10-year yield above 3 percent, a firmer dollar, equities down slightly). The bright line requires below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury yields and the curve are discussed (the 10-year at 3 percent, flattening), plus money-market technicals. Nobody describes dysfunction, so this is normal functioning, 36-50.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Brainard says "Spreads on junk bonds and on leveraged loans, particularly, are near the low end of their historical range". Kaplan says "I don't take any comfort from the tightness of credit spreads". The level is low, so the bright line requires below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants see valuations as elevated. Brainard says "stretched asset valuations", Quarles "Asset valuations are still relatively high", and George says CRE and high-yield valuations are at "very high levels". Quarles also sees no speculative cycle. This is modest to clear elevated-valuation concern, so below 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see banks as well capitalized. Quarles says "banks appear well positioned to handle losses", and Brainard cites strong buffers and notes "bank lending has been healthy, and profits are strong". George flags liquidity risk at community banks as a small-scale pocket, "unlikely to be a systemic risk". Bright line requires below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Quarles says "The dollar has strengthened a bit", Brainard has the real trade-weighted dollar up about 3/4 point though far below its 2016 peak, and Powell cites "dollar strengthening". Appreciation means above 50, modestly.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- INFLATION_GOODS: Members describe rising input and materials costs (steel up 25-30 percent, shipping, tariffs) and growing pass-through, with a contact planning midyear price increases near 3 percent. Modestly above stable.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: Dudley details rapid rises in hospital and nursing-home services (3.3 percent) and financial services without payment (7.5 percent), which he calls transitory. Modestly above stable.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: Not discussed as an inflation topic, so N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Evans says they are "somewhat below levels consistent with" the target, Bullard says they are "still too low", and Brainard says underlying inflation is "somewhat below our target". Mester, Barkin and Kashkari call them anchored or rising from low levels. Slightly below target.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds at this meeting, with a June hike signaled. Nearly everyone supports continued gradual increases. Rosengren, Harker and Williams lean toward more, up to four or more hikes. Bullard, Kashkari, Evans and Brainard stress patience, a possible pause, and the yield curve. This is a hold with a clear tightening bias, mixed on pace, so moderately hawkish, around 60.
- Dissent: There were no dissents, and the vote was 8-0 with every voter in favor of B. There were real differences over the pace, the yield curve and inflation expectations, including Bullard directly disputing Rosengren. All support B, so this is broad agreement with some emphasis differences.
- Financial stability: A dedicated go-round included Quarles, Rosengren, Kaplan, George and Brainard at length, with business leverage, valuations, buffers and the countercyclical capital buffer. It also recurs in the policy go-round (Rosengren, Mester, George, Kaplan on yield-curve and financial-imbalance risk). Moderate.
- Forward guidance: Members debate removing the "below longer-run levels" language and the gradual-pace guidance. They want optionality and data dependence, and they keep "gradual increases" and a likely June hike. A moderate tightening signal.
- Uncertainty: Quarles has "a very big confidence interval" on the natural rate. Kaplan says "we don't know what we don't know". Harker says uncertainty "appears to be abnormally high". George says "considerable uncertainties over the medium term", and Bostic cites trade and fiscal unknowns. Most members still express confidence in the baseline. Somewhat elevated.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP is above 50 as required.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below 50 as required.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: the stated level is low, so the score is below 50.
- BANKING_STRESS is below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above 50 (appreciation).
- TREASURY_STRESS is 36-50 (no dysfunction).

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 60 and LABOR 63 are within 15 points. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 57 is consistent with OUTPUT_GAP.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 and CREDIT_SPREADS 30 both lean loose and co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS 42 sits in the normal range.
- The inflation parent (53) matches the sub-components (goods 60, services 57, expectations 44, average about 54).

**Polarity check.** Higher means more of the named construct.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: evidence says near or slightly above target, which implies a score of 53.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: members call conditions supportive or easy, meaning loose, so the score is below 50.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: low levels of spreads mean less stress, so the score is below 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: healthy banks mean low stress, so the score is below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: high valuations are scored low because the scale reads higher as more concern about undervaluation or distress. Members call valuations elevated, so the score is below 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: weak productivity gives a score below 50.

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_HOUSING is N/A because participants did not discuss shelter or rent inflation (housing activity came up, but not housing prices as an inflation input).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 53 +-5 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Williams, "with the 12-month core inflation rate at 1.9 percent and headline at 2 percent, we are now, in fact, closing in on our 2 percent inflation target." Evans, "a modest increase in inflationary pressure."
- Summary: Inflation is essentially at target with a mild upward drift and a modest overshoot expected, while many warn against declaring victory.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral, because input costs and pass-through are rising and an overshoot is expected, tempered by Dudley's transitory view.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Williams, "GDP growth this year and next to average about 2½ percent, well above potential." Brainard, "growing above its trend rate" with utilization possibly reaching "levels not seen in several decades."
- Summary: Most participants see growth running above potential with utilization tightening, though not yet overheating.
- Direction: Above neutral, because above-potential growth and unemployment below the natural rate are repeatedly cited.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 +-5 [High].**
- Evidence: Evans, "Labor markets, obviously, are tight." Williams, "ever-tightening labor markets... worker shortages have become a binding constraint."
- Summary: Unemployment is 4.1 percent and falling, with widespread shortages, though wages stay moderate and Kashkari sees residual slack.
- Direction: Above neutral, because unemployment is below most natural-rate estimates and shortages are widespread.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Brainard, "business investment continued last year's impressive gains" though "consumption posted anemic growth of 1.1 percent" in Q1. Powell, "continue to point to above-trend growth in both consumer spending and business investment."
- Summary: Strong business investment and fiscal support outweigh a transitory Q1 consumption dip, with trade fears delaying some cap-ex.
- Direction: Above neutral, because most expect a rebound and above-trend spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Kaplan, "continued sluggish productivity due to lagging education and skill levels." Bostic, "weak productivity growth."
- Summary: Productivity is repeatedly described as weak or limited, with only hopes of an investment-led pickup (Mester).
- Direction: Below neutral, because it is described as weak.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 +-5 [High].**
- Evidence: Brainard, "even with these recent moves, financial conditions remain quite supportive of aggregate demand." Powell, "financial conditions have tightened modestly."
- Summary: Conditions remain supportive though somewhat tighter over the intermeeting period.
- Direction: Below neutral (loose), because members call them supportive or easy.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-7 [Low].**
- Evidence: Brainard, "the 10-year yield increased 60 basis points so far and crossed the 3 percent threshold for the first time in four years." Dudley, "worrying now that we're about to commit a major policy mistake because the curve is flattening somewhat seems completely misplaced."
- Summary: The discussion covers yield rises and curve flattening, with no hint of impaired functioning.
- Direction: Normal functioning, because yield moves are rate-expectation driven.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Brainard, "Spreads on junk bonds and on leveraged loans, particularly, are near the low end of their historical range." Kaplan, "I don't take any comfort from the tightness of credit spreads right now."
- Summary: Spreads are tight by the speakers' own account, which is a vulnerability worry rather than a stress signal.
- Direction: Below neutral, because the stated level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 33 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Brainard, "against the backdrop of stretched asset valuations." Quarles, "Asset valuations are still relatively high."
- Summary: Valuations are viewed as elevated but without a speculative bubble, and the February selloff is seen as modest.
- Direction: Below neutral on this scale, because valuation concern is of elevation, not distress.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Quarles, "banks appear well positioned to handle losses that would occur in a major business downturn." Brainard, "bank lending has been healthy, and profits are strong by any measure."
- Summary: Large banks are described as well capitalized, and George's community-bank liquidity concern is a pocket, not systemic.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress), because the system is described as sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 +-5 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Quarles, "The dollar has strengthened a bit." Brainard, "up about ¾ percentage point from the March meeting... still much lower than its 2016 peak."
- Summary: A modest appreciation over the intermeeting period, from a lower base than the 2016 peak.
- Direction: Above neutral, because members describe appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60 [value only].** Evidence: Mester, manufacturers and builders "experiencing substantial input price increases and are finding little resistance in passing on price increases." Mixed with Bostic's "most of my contacts continue to report little to no pricing power."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 57 [value only].** Evidence: Dudley, hospital and nursing-home service prices at 3.3 percent and financial services at 7.5 percent year over year, judged largely transitory.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44 [value only].** Evidence: Evans, "inflation expectations remain somewhat below levels consistent with our symmetric 2 percent target." Bullard, "inflation expectations are still too low for comfort."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "at least three more tightenings this year, not two." Mester, "the burden of proof will lie on delaying a rate increase." Counterweight: Bullard, "we should at least have a contingency plan about what we would do and under what circumstances we would pause."
- Summary: A hold today with near-unanimous support for further gradual hikes and a June move, with some pushing for faster and a few urging patience.
- Direction: Above neutral (hawkish), because the Committee leans toward tightening, tempered by this meeting's hold and the patience advocates.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: The vote read-out shows eight Yes votes. Bullard, "I do not agree with that argument" (reply to Rosengren), alongside "I do support alternative B for today."
- Summary: A unanimous vote with real but contained debate over pace, the yield curve and the inflation measure.
- Direction: Low-to-moderate disagreement, because there are no formal dissents or reservations on the vote itself.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +-8 [High].**
- Evidence: A dedicated go-round by Quarles, Rosengren, Kaplan, George and Brainard on leverage, valuations, buffers and shadow-system risk. Rosengren, "financial markets could begin to look a bit more disorderly."
- Summary: A dedicated multi-speaker session plus recurring references, but secondary to the economic and policy discussion.
- Direction: Moderate attention, because several participants address it meaningfully.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +-6 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Dudley, "we still remain on a gradual path of removing monetary policy accommodation." Williams, "validates expectations for an increase in the target range in June."
- Summary: A June hike is signaled and gradual increases continue, while members want less explicit guidance and more optionality.
- Direction: Above neutral (hawkish), because normalization continues, tempered by the data-dependence push.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Kaplan, "We don't know what we don't know." Quarles, "I have a very big confidence interval around that" (the natural rate). Harker, "Uncertainty also appears to be abnormally high."
- Summary: Standard to somewhat elevated, concentrated on the natural rate, trade and fiscal policy, while the baseline outlook is held with confidence.
- Direction: Somewhat above normal caveats, because several raise the natural rate, trade and fiscal path as unknowns.
