# Part 2: Evidence and Scores (v4.1 + V4.1.1; SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

Document read to the last line, including the vote read-out (10 Yes, 2 No: Bullard, George). Staff turns are removed from the input, so some member questions have no visible answer.

## Core economic dimensions

INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 +-6, High.
- Rosengren: "core PCE index is now at a low for the historical series at 1.05 percent. Total PCE is now at 0.7 percent"
- Williams: "almost 85 percent of consumer expenditures are on goods and services for which inflation ... has slowed"; "inflation is running roughly at 1 percent"
- Yellen: "the trend in the readings ... is disturbing"
- Raskin: core PCE "the lowest number in the 50-year history of the series"
- Bernanke: "majority view is probably that what we are seeing is transitory"
- Score: well below target, widespread across members. Transitory language does not raise the score.

OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6, Moderate.
- Raskin: "far-fetched to think that the economy will run out of available capacity anytime soon"; "still substantial labor slack"
- Tarullo: "quite likely a substantial amount of room for further improvement"
- Williams: "still-considerable slack in labor markets"
- Evans: forecast has growth "accelerating to above potential in 2014 and 2015" (a forecast, not the current state)
- Score: below potential now, gap closing slowly. Bright-line satisfied (<50).

LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-5, High.
- Rosengren: "The unemployment rate is at 7.6 percent"; Evans: "We're at 7.6 percent"
- Dudley: "the labor market is still far from being healthy"
- Tarullo: "no evidence of wage inflation"
- Bernanke: "Wage inflation is nowhere to be seen"
- Score: UE above 7 percent with slack, improving. The level governs, not the pace.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-6, Moderate.
- Fisher: "Clearly, the consumer is carrying the economy"
- Williams: "everything related to housing is turning hot, hot, hot"
- Evans: "Investment has been sluggish"
- Dudley: "lack of momentum in terms of investment"
- Bernanke: "Capital investment is a weak spot"
- Score: consumers and housing resilient, business investment weak, net slightly below trend.

PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +-10, Low.
- Lacker: "Over eight-tenths of the difference between overall growth now and growth in the late 20th century is attributable ... to lower growth in labor productivity"
- Lockhart: contacts "investment instead for productivity gain"
- Score: one substantive statement, modestly below historical trend.

## Financial dimensions

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 47 +-7, Moderate.
- Dudley: "a tightening of financial market conditions", "roughly 50 basis point rise in long-term Treasury yields"
- Bernanke: "financial condition indexes, FCIs, remain moderate"
- Duke: "household balance sheets, corporate balance sheets, and the financial system have all strengthened, and ... are now quite supportive of recovery"
- Duke: "mortgage conditions are still extremely tight"
- Bullard: "St. Louis Fed's Financial Stress Index ... below-average financial market stress"
- Score: the rate backup tightened conditions, but members call overall conditions supportive and moderate.

TREASURY_STRESS: 46 +-8, Low.
- Dudley: "markets haven't been very stable recently", the backup is due to "leveraged hot money" exiting and "convexity hedging"
- Stein: "not necessarily unhealthy or unwelcome"; "if things were to go much further, or to become disorderly, that would begin to be a concern"
- Bullard: volatility "badly overplayed"
- Score: yield rise and volatility from policy expectations, no dysfunction described.

CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-8, Moderate.
- Fisher: high-yield spread "LIBOR plus 450 or so" against a standard of "LIBOR plus 900", "this is a very tight spread"
- Stein: "high-yield spreads have not widened in any way that I would call dramatic or scary"
- Raskin: "mortgage and credit spreads have widened"
- Bernanke: "credit spreads have not blown out"
- Score: level is tight (Fisher), recent widening is modest from that level. Level governs, so below 50.

EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-10, Low.
- Fisher: margin debt, "rate of increase, as we saw in 2000 and 2007"; "end of the 30-year bull market"; "growing bubbles"
- George: "distortions in asset prices"
- Bernanke: "monitor possibly excessive risk-taking and asset price distortions"
- Yellen: stock prices "up markedly" (as a positive)
- Score: valuation concern voiced by a few, modest and not widespread.

BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-6, Moderate.
- Duke: "Credit quality is improving rapidly"; "the financial system [has] strengthened"
- Bullard: District banks "modestly higher return on assets"
- Bernanke: "Credit quality is improving"
- Raskin and Stefani raise interest rate risk at banks and life insurers if rates rise suddenly (a contingent concern, not current stress).
- Score: healthy, low stress. Bright-line satisfied (<50).

DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-8, Low.
- Stefani: staff "identified the global slowdown and appreciation of the dollar as important sources of disinflation"
- Yellen: "real exchange rate" in the simulation (neutral)
- Score: a single mention of appreciation. Bright-line satisfied (>50).

## Inflation sub-components

INFLATION_GOODS: 25
- Stefani: "import prices and the core goods component of both PCE and CPI inflation have sharply decelerated"; Yellen: "core import prices have been essentially flat"

INFLATION_SERVICES: 33
- Stefani: "inflation in a number of PCE services categories has slowed noticeably" while "core CPI services ... stayed pretty close to 2 percent"; Bernanke: health-care costs

INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Members discuss house prices and housing activity, but not rents or shelter-cost inflation.

INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40
- Yellen: "TIPS-based five-year, five-year-forward inflation compensation is down 35 basis points"; Cleveland measure "below 1½ percent"
- Williams: "well-anchored inflation expectations"
- Rosengren: confidence in anchoring "certainly being stretched"
- Score: drifting down, below target but not yet unanchored.

## Discussion-specific dimensions

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 46 +-8, Moderate.
- Outcome is alternative B: no change in the $85 billion purchase pace, a plan to reduce purchases "later this year" and end around mid-2014, with the first cut contingent on data.
- Camp for C or earlier cuts: Lacker "my druthers would be that we go with alternative C"; Plosser "more inclined to argue for alternative C"; Duke "I actually prefer alternative C"; George wants a clearer signal to reduce "as early as September"; Powell "ready to support a reduction ... today"; Stefani, Fisher and Stein say C is their ideal.
- Camp for more accommodation: Bullard "option A"; Kocherlakota "drawn to some of the suggestions in alternative A", wait until July; Evans, Yellen, Raskin, Williams and Dudley resist an early cut.
- Plosser: "we will still be increasing the degree of policy accommodation" even with a reduction.
- Score: a tapering pivot discussed against ongoing QE and a no-action decision, so just below balanced.

DISSENT_INTENSITY: 55 +-7, High.
- Two formal dissents with opposite reasoning: Bullard (dovish, "defend its inflation target from the low side") and George (hawkish, wanted a clearer signal of near-term reduction).
- Distinct camps on timing and on the 7 percent stopping rule: Kocherlakota "we are about to do alternative C ... This is a big deal"; Bullard "I am going to have to break with you today"; Tarullo and Yellen "utterly rash" on the hard 7 percent rule.
- Duke: "the 19 participants in the room are about evenly split between these two directions."
- A straw vote on exit principles at the start (about 14 to 15 supported the language), and Chair noted several objections to the MBS wording.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 +-8, Moderate.
- A dedicated financial stability item: Rosengren on money market funds, Kocherlakota on systemic risk and the "Boom-bust" scenario, George on "reaching for yield", Fisher on "bubbles".
- Much of both days is about the rate backup, volatility, and the market reaction to communication (Dudley, Stein, Powell, Raskin, Lacker).
- Score: prominent topic, but not the whole meeting.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 40 +-10, Moderate.
- Bernanke: the Committee "would likely reduce the pace of purchases 'later this year'", continuing reductions "hitting zero by mid-2014", with unemployment "around 7 percent"
- He pairs this with the point that there is "a considerable period between the end of the asset purchases and the time that the rate thresholds are likely to be hit"
- Kept state-contingent; the 6.5 percent funds rate threshold is unchanged; Powell: "we will continue to add accommodation for several quarters"
- Score: a balance-sheet tightening signal heavily offset by easing rate guidance.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8, Moderate.
- Tarullo: "there is so much uncertainty out there"
- Powell: "the tails seem particularly wide to me"; "It's very, very hard to know"
- Kocherlakota: "households and firms remain uncertain"
- Yellen: "The risk that we will be disappointed yet again is considerable"
- Dudley: "I'm still uncertain about how the tug of war ... will play out"
- Stein: "we've got to be pretty humble about this"
- Williams: Brainard-style policy uncertainty
- Score: many members stress uncertainty about growth, inflation and market reactions, short of extreme.
