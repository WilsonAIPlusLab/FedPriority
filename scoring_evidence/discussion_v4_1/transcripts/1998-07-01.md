# FOMC member discussion, v4.1 score: meeting 1998-07-01 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, with staff turns removed. It contains: opening procedure and members' questions to staff (Japan, Russia, yen intervention, inventories, Greenbook forecast); the economic go-around (District reports plus national outlook) from McTeer through Ferguson; a long side-discussion of a staff paper on the zero bound on nominal rates; the morning session on long-run money and debt ranges (unanimous for no change); the policy go-around on the funds-rate and directive decision; and a long discussion of disclosure policy (tilt, directive, statements). Pure procedure, the zero-bound paper, the monetary-range debate and the disclosure debate are not conditions or policy-stance content, so I use them only where they bear on lean, dissent or uncertainty. Staff content (Lindsey's Bluebook remarks, Kohn, Whitesell and others) is ignored. Where Chairman Greenspan speaks as a participant (his own assessment), I score it.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1998, a long expansion with very low unemployment, Asian crisis (Japan weakening, yen sliding), Russia near collapse, GM strike, inventory buildup, strong stock market and a strong dollar. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish-leaning: Broaddus (would raise now, "may have missed a couple of opportunities"), Poole (would raise now), Parry, Hoenig, Jordan (precautionary firming should have occurred; policy "not appropriately calibrated"), Ferguson, Guynn, Moskow ("likely to have to tighten this year"), Minehan (reluctantly holds, "policy may well be behind the curve"), Kelley ("incipient inflationary trend... should be countered" but holds for international reasons), Meyer.
- Cautious or hold-first: Greenspan ("too risky to raise the funds rate at this point", retain upside asymmetry), McDonough, Rivlin, Gramlich, Boehne, Stern.
- Dovish outlier: McTeer (risks "relatively balanced", would remove the tightening bias, "minority of one"). Jordan says "we should ease policy" for the sake of the rest of the world but "do not have the luxury" and wants the domestic conditions that permit easing.

**4. Direction statements for the core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly above a comfortable level and drifting up. Meyer cites core CPI at 2.7% annualized over five months, Ferguson cites average hourly earnings up 4.3%, Parry cites shelter up about 7% in San Francisco, and Minehan, Hoenig and Poole expect an upturn. Offsets: Greenspan says core PCE is under 2% and the GDP chain index is about 1%, and McTeer, Boehne, Stern and Guynn see little price pressure and low pricing power. Above the midpoint but modest: mid-50s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Kelley says "operating at levels beyond its potential", Greenspan refers to "strong excess domestic demand", and Rivlin and Gramlich call first-quarter growth "unsustainable". Offset: a slowdown toward sustainable growth is expected, so modestly to clearly above (about 60).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Unemployment is 4.3% in April and about 4.5% on average, "below anyone's reasonable estimate of the NAIRU" (Ferguson); "extraordinarily tight" (Broaddus); 3.3% in the Seventh District and 1.7% in the Twin Cities; signing bonuses, BMW leases and similar anecdotes. Per the rubric, UE 3.8-4.5% with very tight language puts the score in the upper 60s to low 70s.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Members cite "robust" domestic final demand, "brisk" consumer spending, booming construction, and "unabated demand for consumer goods" (Ferguson). Offsets: a slowing is expected from inventories and net exports, and Q2 shows some softening. Above trend, but not boom-level: mid-60s.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length by Greenspan, Stern, McDonough, Rivlin, Jordan and Poole. They see accelerated productivity ("very nice productivity improvement since the last quarter of 1995", Greenspan on rates of return since 1993), with permanence uncertain. Above trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose. Meyer cites "continued supportive financial market conditions", Minehan "very accommodating financial markets", Kelley "credit is readily available on reasonable terms", Poole "very accommodative monetary and credit conditions", and Broaddus an "extraordinary amount of money looking for a home". Bright line: accommodative/supportive/easy puts the score below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as market functioning. Greenspan's safe-haven remark about long rates falling is a yield comment and not dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan says "credit continues to be available, though spreads have widened a bit, particularly for anything involving Asia". That is a direction word, but the level is implied favorable by Kelley's "readily available on reasonable terms" and Broaddus's worry that "credit standards may be slipping". Level reading: somewhat below normal, score below 50. Only one participant mentions spreads, so Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: No explicit valuation statements, but there are hints. Jordan: "less exuberant equity markets" and worry about asset-market excesses. Rivlin: "rational nonexuberance". Greenspan: earnings expectations of 13-14% over five years. The stock market is described as strong and resilient and possibly due for a correction. Mild valuation concern with no stress: slightly below the fair-value midpoint on this scale (more elevated valuation means lower score).
- BANKING_STRESS: Domestic banking is described as sound (Hoenig: "most banks had strong portfolios... able to absorb likely increases in delinquencies"; Jordan: bankers report strong loan demand). Banker worries about slipping standards (Broaddus) and Japan's bank-loss problem (Gramlich, Hoenig, Parry) are discussed, but the Japanese issue is foreign. Bright line: healthy means below 50, with some upward pull from the Japan discussion.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciation. Hoenig says the dollar "is and has been appreciating", Parry cites "the higher dollar", Rivlin says the dollar "is likely to stay strong", and the yen has depreciated sharply. Meyer expects an eventual reversal, but the current direction is appreciation. Bright line: above 50.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more price pressure: evidence is modest upward pressure, so above 50 matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter: evidence is very tight, so a high score matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS higher means tighter: evidence is accommodative, so low matches. CREDIT_SPREADS higher means more stress: evidence is availability favorable, so low matches. BANKING_STRESS higher means more stress: evidence is healthy domestically, so low matches. EQUITY_VALUATION on this scale is high for stress or undervaluation and low for overvaluation concern: evidence is mild exuberance, so low-to-middle matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds the funds rate and keeps an upside (tightening) asymmetry. Outright hike preferences exist (Broaddus, Poole; Gramlich would hike by more than 25bp if a window opens), and many others say risks are on the upside and "we are likely to have to tighten this year" (Moskow) but defer for international reasons. One member (McTeer) wants to drop the bias. A hold with a tightening bias and a hawkish tail puts the lean at 51-60, around 58.
- Dissent: the vote read-out has two roll calls. The first (after the money-range discussion, which the Chairman called a "fairly general consensus") is unanimous. The second (after the policy go-around, "B asymmetric") has one No: President Jordan. The read-out does not label which roll call is which; the order matches the agenda. Jordan's stated reasoning is mixed (he wants conditions that permit easing, and also says earlier precautionary firming was warranted), so the direction of his dissent is unclear. In addition, Broaddus and Poole prefer to raise now, Minehan supports "reluctantly", McTeer would drop the bias, and Hoenig and Guynn add caveats. One formal dissent plus a few reservations, with agreement on economics largely shared: moderate differences.
- Financial stability emphasis: heavy attention to international financial stress (Japan banks, Russia, contagion, Latin America, "wider world financial crisis" per Greenspan), some attention to credit standards and asset-market excess (Broaddus, Jordan), but no dominant US financial-conditions focus. Moderate, near the middle.
- Forward guidance: the directive keeps a tightening tilt, Greenspan says "I do not see how we could do otherwise than to respond" if labor-market inflation emerges, and Moskow expects to tighten this year. Mild tightening bias, with a "window of opportunity" theme. Score in the mid-60s.
- Uncertainty: widespread and strong. McDonough: "the word that comes to my mind is uncertainty"; Boehne: "the only part... I have a good deal of confidence in is the 'lot of uncertainty' part"; Greenspan: financial implosions are "virtually impossible" to forecast and models cannot capture vicious cycles; McTeer says his uncertainty rose "considerably"; Ferguson uses "uncertain". High but not Knightian.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 with "beyond its potential" language: satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 with accommodative/supportive language: satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS: level reading gives below 50 (favorable availability), with only a mild "widened a bit" direction note: satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 for sound domestic banks: satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH above 50 for appreciation: satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70) differ by 10 points, within the 15-point limit; labor is the more extreme because the unemployment level is the stronger anchor.
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (66): 6 points apart, fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) and CREDIT_SPREADS (40): both below 50, consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A, nothing to check.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (55) against sub-components: goods about 38, services about 65, housing about 68, expectations about 44. The weighted average is in the low-to-mid 50s, consistent with 55.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no participant discusses Treasury market functioning (only a passing safe-haven yield comment by the Chairman). Inflation sub-components are scored because members speak to goods, services, shelter and expectations specifically.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Meyer: "after a 2.7 percent annual rate of core inflation over the first five months of this year, the Greenbook projects only a 2.1 percent rate for the second half." Greenspan: "price pressures, while modestly accelerating, are not by any means overwhelming."
- Center of gravity: modest, rising pressure with a benign headline picture.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is above neutral because most members report a pickup in core CPI, wages and shelter, tempered by low pricing power and PCE-based measures.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "I do not think there is much doubt that our economy is operating at levels beyond its potential." Kelley: "it is very hard for me to see the domestic economy slowing spontaneously to or below any reasonable definition of sustainability."
- Center of gravity: demand outpacing supply, partly offset by an expected slowdown.
- Direction confirmation: Output gap is above neutral because members repeatedly describe growth and levels above potential or sustainable.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: Ferguson: "the unemployment rate averaged about 4.5 percent, which is probably below anyone's reasonable estimate of the NAIRU." Stern: unemployment in the Twin Cities "has now dropped to 1.7 percent"; Broaddus: "labor markets are still extraordinarily tight."
- Center of gravity: unemployment about 4.3%, many shortage anecdotes and bonuses.
- Direction confirmation: Labor tightness is above neutral because unemployment is below the NAIRU and shortages are widespread.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 66 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "Consumer spending is brisk, manufacturing is solid, and housing sales and starts are robust." Meyer: "the growth of domestic final demand remains robust."
- Center of gravity: strong consumption, housing and capital spending; Q1 "unsustainably rapid", Q2 slowing but still strong.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is above trend because members call demand robust and unabated, with a slowdown expected only from inventories and net exports.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "the period from 1993 forward does show a significantly higher rate of return than in the previous years" (leading to productivity acceleration). McDonough: "We have been experiencing very nice productivity improvement since the last quarter of 1995."
- Center of gravity: above-trend productivity gains, with permanence in doubt.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is above trend because several members credit an acceleration, while the durability uncertainty restrains the score.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Meyer: "continued supportive financial market conditions." Minehan: "We continue to have an atmosphere of very accommodating financial markets."
- Center of gravity: easy credit and buoyant markets, with worry about slipping standards.
- Direction confirmation: Conditions are below neutral (looser) because members call them accommodative, supportive and readily available.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- No participant discusses Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan: "credit continues to be available, though spreads have widened a bit, particularly for anything involving Asia." Kelley: "credit is readily available on reasonable terms."
- Center of gravity: a mild widening noted only for Asia-related credit, against generally favorable availability.
- Direction confirmation: Spreads are below neutral by level because availability is described as easy, and "widened a bit" describes direction only.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 ±9 [Low]**
- Evidence: Jordan: the Asian crisis fosters "less exuberant equity markets." Rivlin: "The stock market could turn to rational nonexuberance."
- Center of gravity: stocks viewed as high and wealth-supported with correction risk, but no explicit valuation call.
- Direction confirmation: This is slightly below neutral on the scale (some overvaluation concern, no stress) because members imply exuberance rather than distress.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "most banks had strong portfolios coming into the year and should be able to absorb likely increases in delinquencies." Broaddus: "concern on the part of bankers... that credit standards may be slipping."
- Center of gravity: domestic banks sound, with some standards concerns and a large foreign (Japan) bank problem.
- Direction confirmation: Banking stress is below neutral because US banks are described as strong, with Japan's problem a modest upward pull.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "the dollar, of course, is and has been appreciating." Rivlin: "the dollar also is likely to stay strong."
- Center of gravity: steady dollar appreciation against a falling yen, with a reversal expected later.
- Direction confirmation: The dollar is above neutral because it is described as appreciating and strong.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38**
- Evidence: McTeer: "signs of inflationary strain are lacking in goods markets"; Moskow: "special factors holding down goods price inflation."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 65**
- Evidence: Moskow: "prices for services being the driving factor"; Parry: services other than housing "increasing at a fast pace" in the Northwest.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 68**
- Evidence: Parry: "CPI housing shelter costs in the San Francisco Bay area have jumped about 7 percent"; Minehan: uptick in core CPI "especially as it reflects the cost of shelter."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44**
- Evidence: McTeer: "long-term inflation expectations continue to fall"; Parry: "diminished inflation expectations"; Hoenig revises his own near-term expectations up, which offsets slightly.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "an increase now in the funds rate would be prudent." Greenspan: "I would propose that if we decide to stay where we are today, we at least also retain an asymmetric directive to the upside."
- Center of gravity: hold now with a tightening bias, a hawkish tail (Broaddus, Poole, Gramlich on a bigger hike), and one dove (McTeer).
- Direction confirmation: The lean is above neutral because the decision is to hold but with an upside bias and many members expecting to tighten.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: second roll call shows "President Jordan No"; McTeer: "I am willing to be a minority of one... I would suggest a removal of the bias toward tightening." Minehan: "in some sense reluctantly".
- Center of gravity: one formal dissent plus several reservations over timing and the tilt; economics broadly shared.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is moderate because there is one No (with unclear direction) and several members preferring a different action, but most agree on the assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 ±10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "The danger of a wider world financial crisis, while still very small, has risen beyond the point of being de minimis." Broaddus: "credit standards may be slipping and we may be digging a hole for ourselves."
- Center of gravity: substantial attention to international financial fragility and some to asset-market excess, but economic assessment dominates.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is moderate, as several participants address it meaningfully without it dominating.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 66 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Moskow: "the risks remain definitely to the upside and... we are likely to have to tighten this year." Guynn: the asymmetrical directive is "a signal to others that we are not interested in giving back any of the ground that we have gained on inflation."
- Center of gravity: tightening bias retained, with a possible move when a window opens.
- Direction confirmation: Guidance is above neutral because the directive keeps its tightening tilt and several members flag future hikes.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 66 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: McDonough: "the word that comes to my mind is 'uncertainty'." Greenspan: "forecasting financial implosions and vicious cycles is virtually impossible."
- Center of gravity: many members stress unusual uncertainty and two-sided widening risks, without Knightian language.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is well above normal because many participants emphasize difficulty and scenario spread.
