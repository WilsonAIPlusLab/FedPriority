# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2019-06-19 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion with staff turns removed. The document holds (a) a long special-topic discussion of a standing repo facility (plumbing and balance-sheet design, not an economic assessment), (b) members' questions during the staff Q&A, (c) the economic go-around, (d) the policy go-around on alternative A/B, (e) a short wrap-up exchange about how to describe the "nine who did not write down cuts", and (f) a vote read-out (9 yes, 1 no; Bullard voted no). I scored the economic go-around, the policy go-around and the wrap-up. I ignored the repo-facility design discussion except where it touches financial stability. Questions in the staff Q&A carry little scoring content. Some staff answers are removed, so a few member replies read as fragments. This does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** The text describes a late-cycle expansion with a 50-year-low unemployment rate (3.6%), core PCE inflation of about 1.5-1.6% below the 2% target, a renewed US-China and Mexico tariff escalation since May 5, a sharp fall in Treasury yields and a partly inverted curve, and falling market-based inflation compensation. I scored what members say, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers.** Powell (Chair), Williams, Clarida, Quarles, Brainard, Bowman (Board/NY); Bullard, Rosengren, Daly, Evans, Mester, Harker, Barkin, Kaplan, Bostic, George, Kashkari (Reserve Bank presidents). Nearly all comment on trade uncertainty, weak business investment, strong consumers and labor market, low inflation and falling expectations.

**4. Directional statements by dimension.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. They cite core PCE at 1.5-1.6% (Daly, Brainard, Kashkari, Williams), "muted" and "stubbornly low" readings (Clarida, Harker, Quarles), and weak wages and falling commodity prices. They hold that low inflation may be persistent, not transitory (Daly, Kashkari, Evans). A few expect a rebound (Bowman, Quarles, Barkin, Bostic, Kaplan, via trimmed mean at 2%). The center is clearly below target but not deflationary: about 30.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Mixed but modestly positive. Brainard says growth is "somewhat above its potential rate", "above-trend growth", and "above-potential pace". Quarles: growth "above my estimate of potential". Williams: forecast "slightly above the potential rate". Barkin: "above trend". Powell: "growth near potential" and "above-trend real GDP growth". Clarida says the gap is "close to zero" and Kashkari "I don't think there's an actual positive output gap". Direction is slightly above 50 on net: about 52.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above the midpoint. Unemployment is 3.6%, "lowest in half a century" (Clarida, Mester, Quarles, Brainard, Bowman: "very strong", "extremely low"). Kaplan says "at or past full employment". Powell says "tight labor markets". George cites "challenges of finding workers". Offsets: May payrolls were weak, wages are not accelerating (Kashkari, Evans, Williams), and Kashkari sees residual slack. UE below 4% implies a score above 55. About 64.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong consumers (Barkin "real strength", Brainard "robust", Bowman, Quarles, Mester "solid") against weak business investment (Williams: possible "outright contraction", Clarida, Powell "stalled"), soft manufacturing, and weak housing and energy. Net is at or slightly below trend: about 48.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only briefly discussed. Quarles points to a 2.4% output-per-hour pickup as "a considerable acceleration after... sluggish growth". Daly cites 1.2% trend productivity growth. Rosengren says trade disruption could produce "large negative productivity shocks". Slightly above the midpoint on the one clear data point, but thin: about 54.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (looser). Brainard: conditions "only marginally tighter" and "still accommodative relative to historical norms"; later "supportive relative to their historical average". Powell: earlier "much more supportive financial conditions". Kaplan: "Financial conditions are very strong", "cost of capital is historically low". Bowman: "favorable". Daly notes conditions "tightening", but that is a smaller offset. About 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members discuss large yield falls (10-year down 40-45 bp) and curve inversion as recession signals (Bullard, Brainard, Kashkari, Rosengren). This is yield movement, not dysfunction. Brainard's "gloom in Treasury markets" is sentiment, not impaired functioning. The repo talk mentions month-end funds-rate spikes as minor. Normal functioning: about 42.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level is tight. Kaplan twice says spreads are "extremely tight" and "very tight", and the cost of capital is "historically low". Brainard says corporate spreads "haven't widened by commensurate amounts". Level below normal, so below 50: about 24.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation commentary leans toward rich, not depressed. Kaplan: "Markets remain near their all-time highs" and "high valuations". Rosengren: S&P "quite close to a record high". Daly and Brainard cite "already high asset valuations". Brainard and Rosengren also note volatility and a modest dip, but the valuation view is above fair value: about 38.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banking system health is not discussed as such. Bowman mentions ag lenders restructuring farm debt, which is a sector issue. The repo discussion is about reserve demand and liquidity design. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Daly lists "an appreciating dollar". Brainard: "the dollar has appreciated about 3/4 percent since our last meeting". George: tariffs add "upward pressure on the foreign exchange value of the dollar". Modest appreciation, above 50: about 58.
- INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: Goods and tariff pass-through are mentioned (Williams, Evans, Mester) but with mixed signals. Brainard notes "housing services and other market services" disappointing in one data release. These are too thin to score separately. N/A for all three.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Heavily discussed and falling. Michigan survey at a record low, 5y5y breakevens down 27-30 bp, risk-neutral probabilities skewed to the downside, and the view that markets read 2% as a ceiling (Kashkari, Evans). Several say they are not yet unanchored (Daly "I still have them anchored at 2 percent"; Mester "relatively well anchored"; Williams "already showing signs of fragility"). Below target and drifting lower: about 34.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; "extremely tight" means low stress, so the score is low. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; "accommodative/supportive" means a low score. DOLLAR: appreciation means above 50. EQUITY: higher = more equity stress or depressed prices; participants see rich valuations, so below 50. All match.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee held the funds rate (alternative B, 9-1) but dropped "patient" and signaled readiness to act. Powell says "the case for additional accommodation has strengthened" and that there is "broad, if not universal, agreement" on that. Eight participants wrote down cuts. Bullard wanted 25 bp now (alternative A). Kashkari wanted 50 bp plus a commitment not to raise until core inflation reaches 2% sustained. Williams, Brainard, Evans, Daly and Clarida lean toward cutting. Mester, Rosengren, Harker, George, Bostic, Barkin, Quarles, Kaplan and Bowman prefer to wait. Some of these still say the case for easing has strengthened. No one argues for tightening. This is a dovish lean with an immediate hold: moderate dovish, about 34.
- Disagreement: One formal dissent (Bullard, in favor of an immediate cut). A non-voter (Kashkari) wanted 50 bp. The room is split on timing (8 who wrote down cuts vs 9 who did not). Powell worries about a "split Committee" narrative. There are real disagreements about slack (Kashkari, Clarida: gap near zero vs. George: easing risks "overheating labor markets") and about whether low inflation is transitory. Most still support the statement. About 40.
- Financial stability attention: Mostly market-signal and risk-premium commentary (yield curve, equities, breakevens). Corporate debt and excesses are raised by Kaplan, Brainard, George and Daly, and the repo special topic has financial stability sub-themes (Rosengren, Williams, Evans). It is moderate, not dominant: about 42.
- Forward guidance: The statement drops "patient" and adds "act as appropriate", which the market reads as pointing to a July cut. Powell says "somewhat more accommodative monetary policy is likely to be appropriate". Several members want less forward guidance (Barkin, Rosengren, Bostic). Easing lean with data dependence: about 30.
- Uncertainty: Heavy use of "uncertainty", "bimodal outlook" (Rosengren, Mester), "too soon to tell" (Clarida, Kaplan), trade policy as a "new normal" (Bullard, Daly), "difficult to assign probabilities". It is not Knightian, but it is clearly elevated: about 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 52 (> 50 with "above potential" and "near potential"; mixed, in the allowed 48-58 range given both). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (< 50 for accommodative/supportive). CREDIT_SPREADS 24 (< 50 for "extremely tight"). BANKING_STRESS N/A. DOLLAR 58 (> 50 for appreciation). TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction described, 36-50 range).

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs LABOR (64): a 12-point gap, within 15; the labor level is tight but members doubt that it generates inflation, so the gap estimate is smaller. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (48): within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) and CREDIT_SPREADS (24): both on the loose side, consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, near 40, consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (34): consistent; sub-components N/A.

**9. N/A justification.** BANKING_STRESS: banking system health not discussed. INFLATION_GOODS/SERVICES/HOUSING: only passing and mixed remarks; no component-level assessment.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Daly: "headline and core PCE inflation are running at just 1.5 percent over the past 12 months". Brainard: "core PCE prices posted a 12-month increase of 1.6 percent, well short of our objective". Clarida: "inflation is muted-probably more muted than we would like."
- Summary: Core inflation is well below target and many expect it to stay low.
- Direction: Below neutral because core PCE is at 1.5-1.6% and pressures are "tugging in the wrong direction" (Daly).

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: growth "somewhat above its potential rate in the first half" and "above-potential pace". Clarida: with u* at 4%, "the output gap would be less than half of the staff estimate and could well be close to zero."
- Summary: Slightly above potential on net, with the gap size itself contested.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral; "above potential" language from Brainard, Quarles and Williams is offset by "near potential" (Powell) and a near-zero gap (Clarida, Kashkari).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 64 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Quarles: unemployment "flat at its lowest rate in half a century". Kaplan: "a labor market that I believe is either at or past full employment".
- Summary: Unemployment is at a 50-year low, with wages not accelerating and some dissent on slack.
- Direction: Above neutral because UE is 3.6% and members call the labor market "very strong".

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "consumer spending remains strong"; Williams: "we could see an outright contraction in business fixed investment this quarter".
- Summary: Strong consumers offset weak business investment and manufacturing.
- Direction: Near neutral; consumption above trend and investment below trend net to roughly trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 54 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "2.4 percent increase in output per hour... a considerable acceleration after the extended period of sluggish growth". Daly: trend productivity growth of "1.2 percent".
- Summary: Brief mentions, a recent pickup against a low trend.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral on the recent pickup only.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: conditions "only marginally tighter than in April and still accommodative relative to historical norms". Kaplan: "Financial conditions are very strong. Markets remain near their all-time highs."
- Summary: Conditions are supportive despite some tightening noted by Daly.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser) because members describe accommodative, supportive conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "The 10-year and the 2-year are both down 45 basis points... an extraordinary move in the bond market." Brainard: "10-year Treasury yield has come down about 40 basis points and is now below the federal funds rate."
- Summary: Large, signal-rich yield moves with no dysfunction reported.
- Direction: Near the normal functioning range; yield movement alone is not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 24 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kaplan: "Credit spreads are extremely tight." Brainard: spreads "haven't widened by commensurate amounts... as a result of... ongoing strong demand".
- Summary: Spreads are at a tight level with ample access to credit.
- Direction: Below neutral because the stated level is "extremely tight".

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "The S&P 500 index is quite close to a record high." Kaplan: capital markets "willing to give high valuations to companies... with little prospect of near- or medium-term profitability".
- Summary: Valuations are viewed as full, with only a modest dip and some volatility.
- Direction: Below neutral because the valuation view is rich, not depressed.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** (banking system health not discussed; only ag-loan restructuring from Bowman).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar has appreciated about 3/4 percent since our last meeting". Daly: "an appreciating dollar".
- Summary: Modest appreciation noted by three members.
- Direction: Above neutral because the dollar is described as appreciating.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A** (not assessed separately).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 34**
- Evidence: Kashkari: "Surveys of inflation expectations are hitting all-time lows... Market-based five-year, five-year inflation expectations are down around 30 basis points". Williams: "inflation expectations, already showing signs of fragility, could really become unmoored from our target".
- Direction: Below target and drifting lower, but members still call them not unanchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 34 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "the case for additional accommodation has strengthened, and I believe that there is broad, if not universal, agreement around this table on that." Bullard: "I propose that we cut the policy rate today by 25 basis points". Kashkari: "a 50 basis point cut combined with language that commits us not to raise rates".
- Summary: The Committee holds the rate but leans toward cutting, with one voter and one non-voter wanting an immediate cut and a majority wanting readiness to act.
- Direction: Dovish; no member argues for tightening, and the current action (hold) is less dovish than the lean.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President Bullard No". Powell: "you have a split Committee, which would be just the kind of thing journalists would like to write about."
- Summary: One formal dissent for a cut, a non-voter wanting 50 bp, and a split on timing, with differences extending to assessments of slack and persistence of low inflation.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement, mostly about timing and tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "current, elevated risky corporate debt and associated asset valuations are consistent with that pattern". Kaplan: "I'm particularly concerned about the relative amount of corporate debt, particularly in triple-B and weaker segments."
- Summary: Several members raise financial imbalances and market signals, but it is not the focus of the meeting.
- Direction: Moderate attention, with market signals and leverage concerns from about five or six members.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "somewhat more accommodative monetary policy is likely to be appropriate to achieve our baseline outlook." Brainard: language changes "should be read as signaling that a future cut in interest rates is on the table."
- Summary: The statement removes "patient" and signals readiness to ease, while keeping data dependence.
- Direction: Easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "the outlook seems more bimodal at this time". Kaplan: "my honest answer right now... is that it's too soon to judge."
- Summary: Many members stress trade-policy uncertainty and bimodal scenarios, though not "uncharted territory".
- Direction: Elevated, multiple participants emphasizing unusual uncertainty.
