# FOMC member discussion scoring (v4.1 rubric + V4.1.1 clarifications): meeting 2006-06-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process (short)

- Document type: two-day transcript with an economic go-around, a policy go-around and a roll call. It has a policy vote and a stance discussion, so it is NOT special-topic. The FOIA item and the communications-subcommittee report at the end are ignored for condition scoring.
- Policy lean: the voters back a 25bp hike to 5.25 percent, and the roll call at the end is unanimous (10 Yes). Forward language is deliberately softened toward about 50-50 odds for August, so the lean is moderately hawkish on the action but only mildly so going forward (60).
- Dissent: 0 formal dissents. President Hoenig, a non-voter, said he would hold at 5 percent. Yellen called it a "close call" and would have preferred a pause. There was real argument over statement wording and a sharp Geithner-Fisher exchange, so moderate-low (30).
- Conditions: core inflation is seen as clearly above comfort and broad-based; output near or slightly beyond potential; labor tight with few wage pressures; housing slowing; consumption softer; business investment strong.
- Financial: members split on whether financial conditions have tightened (Kohn, Yellen) or remain accommodative (Minehan, Moskow, Bies, Stern). Credit spreads are "very narrow by historical standards" even after some widening, so below 50 under the level rule. Banks are described as sound.
- Bright lines: OUTPUT_GAP 52 (near potential). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 (mixed). CREDIT_SPREADS 28 (narrow level). BANKING_STRESS 32 (sound). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 48 (mixed, Low). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.
- Coherence: OUTPUT_GAP 52 / LABOR 55 / CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 50 are within 15 points. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 28 differ by 20 because members call spreads explicitly very narrow while others cite rate-driven tightening. INFLATION_PRESSURE 68 is consistent with the sub-components (housing 72, services 60, goods 57, expectations 58).
- Uncertainty is a stated central theme (Bernanke: "increase of uncertainty and risk", "exceptionally complicated"), scored 62.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Lacker, "our worst three-month performance on the core CPI in more than eleven years, 3.8 percent"; Bernanke, "a broad-based acceleration and one that I think we should be concerned about". Center of gravity: core is clearly above comfort and broadening, though Bernanke says numbers are "historically not extremely high" and Kohn and Hoenig see partly transitory factors. Direction: above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-4 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stone, "slightly beyond full employment"; Minehan, "small to nonexistent output gap"; Bernanke, "output is slowing to something close to potential". Center of gravity: near potential with growth heading slightly below. Direction: at neutral (near-potential band).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "tight labor markets should eventually generate somewhat larger increases in wages"; Poole, a "dire" shortage of drivers but no wage response; Bernanke, "difficult to find skilled workers, but there are still few signs of wage pressures". Unemployment about 4.6 percent (Minehan, citing the staff forecast starting point). Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn, "Consumption appears to be less strong than it was in the early spring"; Moskow, demand for long-lead capital goods "as strong as he has seen in his thirty years". Center of gravity: soft consumption and housing offset by strong business investment. Direction: at neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "Productivity growth remains solid"; Minehan, "productivity growth remains strong"; Geithner, "Strong productivity growth keeps unit labor costs from accelerating sharply". Direction: above trend, mentioned in passing by several.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "considerable tightening of financial conditions over this year"; Minehan, "Financial conditions outside equity markets are accommodative"; Moskow, "financial conditions continue to be favorable". Bernanke noted "disagreement on the extent to which financial conditions are supportive". Center of gravity: split, with accommodative wording slightly dominant. Direction: about neutral, just below.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Members did not discuss Treasury market functioning; desk presentation and answers are not member text.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bies, "spreads are still very narrow by historical standards" and "Credit spreads are still very, very minimal"; Warsh, spreads "moved higher... but, again, not a lot by historical perspectives". Center of gravity: narrow level after modest widening; level governs. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 53 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Warsh, "equity markets have been off on the order of 7 or 8 percent"; Poole, "modest declines in U.S. equity markets". Price move only, no valuation view, so default 45-55 on the side of the move. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Stern, "the financial system remains sound and flexible"; Bies, "we're still seeing banks loosening lending standards". Direction: below neutral (low stress), brief discussion.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Poole, "some dollar appreciation since the May FOMC meeting"; Geithner, "The medium-term trajectory for the dollar seems likely to be down"; Stone, "as the dollar depreciates". Mixed recent rise and expected decline. Direction: about neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 57.** Guynn, goods prices "now not making a large negative contribution"; Bernanke, core goods show the same acceleration; Fisher, suppliers passing through costs.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 60.** Bernanke, core PCE services excluding OER shows the same acceleration pattern; Lacker, services price expectations at a record high in the Fifth District.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 72.** Lacker and Kohn on owners' equivalent rent; Guynn, "recent increase in owners' equivalent rent"; Yellen, "rents are finally moving up".

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 58.** Lacker, expectations "fluctuating around a level suggesting PCE inflation above 2 percent"; Geithner, long-term expectations "a little higher than we want"; Hoenig and Stone, "anchored". Contained but not comfortably at target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "I support the alternative B action of 25 basis points"; Lacker, "we may need to raise rates beyond today"; Poole, "50 percent odds that we would hold steady in August and 50 percent odds of another 25". Center of gravity: all voters support the hike, but the majority wants to keep options open and several would like to pause soon. Hoenig (non-voter) preferred a hold. Direction: hawkish side, moderate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-6 (High).** Evidence: roll call unanimous; Hoenig, "my preference... is to maintain the funds rate at 5 percent, and I would vote accordingly if I were a voting member"; Yellen, "on purely economic grounds I'd prefer to pause". Center of gravity: no formal dissents; a non-voting hold preference and reservations; disagreements mostly about statement wording and signaling (Lacker/Fisher vs Geithner/Kroszner/Warsh).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Warsh's extended discussion of markets, leverage and "club" deals and the "first coupon" risk; Bernanke, "Another potential nonlinearity is in financial markets"; Bies on housing, liquidity and credit availability. Center of gravity: several address markets meaningfully, but the meeting is mainly about inflation and the statement.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "the next move is more likely to be up than down. But it's a less definitive statement than it was before"; Bernanke, "keep our options open and... move slowly if we do move further"; the adopted text drops "some further policy firming may yet be needed". Center of gravity: mild tightening bias, deliberately de-emphasised and data-dependent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "a central theme of the speakers today was the increase of uncertainty and risk"; Kohn, "I'm also even less confident, if that's possible"; Yellen, "I see a lot of uncertainty right now about the prospects for both real GDP and inflation". Center of gravity: many members emphasize unusual difficulty and model uncertainty.
