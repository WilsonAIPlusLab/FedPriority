# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion, transcript, meeting 2001-04-11

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It is a short informational (unofficial) meeting, held by call. Chairman Greenspan opens by saying he will not put a rate cut on the agenda, and wants an informational meeting with an intermeeting window over the next 10 days. Participants then comment in a roundtable. There is no vote read-out and no formal directive. The text is about 4,500 words, so it is not a very short discussion. The content is narrowly focused: whether to cut now, later in the window, or only at the next scheduled meeting, plus anecdotes on conditions. Parry's passage has a dropped name ("I talked with the yesterday"). That does not affect any score.

**Era context.** Spring 2001, with an inventory correction, falling profits and weak capital goods orders. The federal funds rate is mentioned by Jordan as 5 percent. I take the scores from the text only.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (opening), McDonough, Poole, McTeer, Stern, Minehan, Guynn, Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Santomero, Hoenig, Jordan, Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson, Kelley.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Greenspan says pricing power is "even more constrained", margins are falling because firms cannot pass on cost increases, high-tech prices are falling faster and commodity prices are weak. McDonough also cites "lack of pricing power". Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint, but only modestly. No one discusses core inflation or targets directly. The score is about 36, with Moderate-to-Low confidence.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Ferguson says "there is still a notable output gap" and that growth could be "close to 1 percent" for several quarters. Meyer worries about "how long it will take us to get back to trend". Gramlich says the economy is weakening from a forecast that already has weak conditions. The gap is below potential, so the bright line requires < 50. The score is 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Little direct discussion. Moskow cites Rockford unemployment of 5.1 percent. Gramlich mentions a year-end unemployment rate of 5.2 percent, a staff forecast he quotes. Ferguson says the unemployment numbers might show some improvement, or that "risks are starting to spread". The labor market is roughly near full employment and softening, a little below the midpoint on balance. The score is 44, Low confidence.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Business fixed investment is the "major issue". McDonough: executives "postpone investment decisions". Hoenig: delays at Agilent and Intel. Greenspan: capital goods orders declined. Consumption and housing are "holding up" (Greenspan, Hoenig, Gramlich, Moskow's consumer travel is "very strong"). Net: weak investment outweighs resilient consumption, so below the midpoint. The score is 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Greenspan notes BBB-AAA spreads have "opened up further", Moskow notes Motorola is "frozen out of the commercial paper market", and there is equity volatility. Against that, Jordan says the funds rate is possibly "inadvertently restrictive", which is a policy remark, and not a statement of credit availability. No bright-line wording ("accommodative", "tight") is used about conditions. Somewhat tighter than neutral. The score is 56.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Greenspan mentions "rapid changes in both bond and stock markets" and Jordan discusses the yield curve. Neither describes market dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Greenspan says BBB-AAA spreads "have opened up further recently, implying that business risk has been rising". This gives direction only, with no information on the level. Under the v4.1 rule, direction-only language gets 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. A clear widening is 52-55. Score 54. The Motorola CP remark adds a little support.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss stock price moves and the stability of equity prices, plus the "Greenspan put" and "two days of really good stock market performance". There is no valuation commentary. Default range 45-55. Score 52, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Jordan relays bankers' views on the economy, not bank health. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: Greenspan notes high-tech prices falling at an accelerating pace and weak commodity prices. That is goods prices, soft, so goods is about 30. Services, housing and expectations are not discussed, so N/A.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Nobody argues for tightening. Everyone expects or wants easing. McTeer, Gramlich and Kelley would cut now or soon. Gramlich would do 50bp "as soon as we possibly can". Meyer expects a full percentage point by the end of June. Jordan says the funds rate is "probably too high". Santomero: "we clearly will be easing". Greenspan and most others prefer to wait for the next 10 days. This is a moderate-to-strong dovish stance, though no action is taken today. Score 24.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: No vote and no formal dissent. The disagreement is about timing and tactics (cut now versus later in the window versus the May meeting, and whether intermeeting moves are wise at all, from Poole, Minehan, Guynn and Broaddus). There is little disagreement about the economic assessment. Score 30.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Market stability is a recurring theme: Greenspan on how a cut would affect stock prices and "nascent stabilization", McTeer on bonds versus stocks and the "put", the BBB-AAA spreads, Motorola's loss of commercial paper access, and Poole's comments on market riskiness. Several participants address it, though much of the focus is capital spending. Moderate, 42.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The forward signal is easing: "likely to be moving rates down" (Ferguson), "we clearly will be easing" (Santomero), "further easing ahead of us" (Meyer). The deliberation is private, so it is not a public commitment. Mild-to-moderate easing bias. Score 16.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: "the uncertainties have intensified" (Parry), "downside risk ... extremely considerable" (McDonough), "durable stabilization or ... another leg of weakness ... I don't have the answer" (Stern), "a lot of uncertainty in the outlook and a number of crosscurrents" (Meyer), and a mixed picture in several Districts. Several participants stress it. Elevated. Score 58.

**Polarity checks.** Inflation: higher means more pressure; my evidence says constrained pricing, so below 50, which matches. Output gap: negative gap, below 50, which matches. Credit spreads: higher means more stress; the spreads widened, so a bit above 50, which matches. Financial conditions: higher means tighter; some tightening in spreads and CP access, 56, which matches. Hawkish/dovish: higher means more hawkish; easing is favored, so low, which matches.

**Bright lines.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (33), satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS has no accommodative or restrictive language about conditions, so no bright-line trigger. CREDIT_SPREADS has direction only, so 54 is within 52-55. BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A.

**Coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 33 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 44 differ by 11, within 15. The labor market is described only through a 5.1-5.2 percent unemployment rate, so it is near full employment, while the output gap view rests on the weak growth and investment commentary.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 33 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36 are consistent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 56 and CREDIT_SPREADS 54 co-move.
4. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, so there is nothing to compare.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 and INFLATION_GOODS 30 are consistent (the only sub-component scored).

**N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS, BANKING_STRESS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not raised in the sense of the dimension. INFLATION_SERVICES, HOUSING and EXPECTATIONS: not discussed by participants.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-8 (Low-to-Moderate confidence; coded Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "businesses have found their pricing power even more constrained ... unable to pass on unit wage and energy cost increases"; McDonough, "the lack of pricing power".
- Center of gravity: Weak pricing power and falling margins point to subdued price pressure, with no direct discussion of inflation levels.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because firms cannot raise prices.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Ferguson, "there is still a notable output gap"; "growth ... at a number starting with one".
- Center of gravity: Slow growth, weak profits and capital spending point to slack.
- Direction confirmation: The output gap is below potential because of the stated "notable output gap".

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 +-9 (Low)**
- Evidence: Moskow, "unemployment rate is 5.1 percent now"; Ferguson, unemployment numbers a "sign of some improvement" but "risks are starting to spread"; Gramlich, a forecast with "year-end unemployment rate at 5.2 percent".
- Center of gravity: Unemployment near 5 percent with softening prospects, little direct discussion.
- Direction confirmation: Labor tightness is slightly below neutral because unemployment is around 5 percent and expected to rise.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McDonough, "corporate executives feel they must postpone investment decisions"; Hoenig, "delays in capital expenditures by that company, by Intel, and by others"; Greenspan, "Personal consumption expenditures and homebuilding appear to be holding up".
- Center of gravity: Investment is weak and consumption is holding up, with the net below trend.
- Direction confirmation: Spending is below trend because weak business investment dominates the discussion.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 56 +-9 (Low)**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "spreads of BBB yields over AAA yields have opened up further"; Moskow, Motorola "has been frozen out of the commercial paper market".
- Center of gravity: Credit conditions for some firms are tighter and markets are volatile, though no one calls conditions tight overall.
- Direction confirmation: Financial conditions are slightly above neutral because spreads widened and some firms lost market access.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** No dysfunction described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 +-6 (Low)**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "spreads of BBB yields over AAA yields have opened up further recently, implying that business risk has been rising".
- Center of gravity: A widening is described, and no level is given, so the score sits just above neutral per the v4.1 rule.
- Direction confirmation: Spreads are at the top of the neutral band because of the widening (direction only, level unknown).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +-6 (Low)**
- Evidence: McTeer, "two days of really good stock market performance"; Greenspan, concern about "stock prices would fall markedly".
- Center of gravity: Price-movement discussion only, with no valuation view, so the default band applies.
- Direction confirmation: Equity valuation is at neutral because there is no valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A.** Banks cited only as sources on the economy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30.** Greenspan, "The fall in high-tech prices is accelerating and commodity prices remain weak." **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A.**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 +-6 (High)**
- Evidence: Meyer, "I wouldn't be surprised if we need to move a full percentage point between now and the end of June"; Gramlich, "50 basis points of easing as soon as we possibly can"; Jordan, "the funds rate is probably too high at 5 percent".
- Center of gravity: Almost everyone expects more easing, and the dispute is about when, which gives a moderate-to-strong dovish stance.
- Direction confirmation: The lean is dovish, since no one argues for tightening and several want a cut now.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: McTeer, "I think we really should cut the funds rate today" versus Greenspan, Poole and Guynn on waiting and avoiding intermeeting moves; Kelley, "I'd prefer to go ahead today".
- Center of gravity: There are no formal dissents and no vote, and the differences are about timing and tactics, not the outlook.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is low-to-moderate because the debate is about tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-10 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we will break whatever developing pattern for equity price stability may be currently emerging"; Poole, intermeeting moves add "to the riskiness of the short-run trading environment".
- Center of gravity: Market stability is a recurring consideration for the timing decision, though capital spending is the main topic.
- Direction confirmation: Moderate attention because several participants address market effects and credit conditions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 16 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Santomero, "I think we clearly will be easing as we go forward"; Ferguson, "over the next several meetings we are likely to be moving rates down".
- Center of gravity: The forward signal is easing, though it is a private deliberation.
- Direction confirmation: The signal is below neutral because the stated direction is lower rates.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Evidence: Parry, "the uncertainties have intensified"; Stern, "whether this is a durable stabilization or whether there is another leg of weakness ... I don't have the answer".
- Center of gravity: Several participants emphasize unusual uncertainty and downside risk, while others see mixed data and some stabilization.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is elevated because multiple participants stress it explicitly.
