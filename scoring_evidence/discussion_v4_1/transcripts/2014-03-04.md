# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2014-03-04 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript of a videoconference (not a regular meeting). The Chair opens, then a go-round of presidents and governors on one subject: how to revise the FOMC statement's forward guidance, because the 6.5 percent unemployment threshold is about to be crossed. The members discuss five staff-drafted options. There is no economic go-around and no policy decision or vote.

**2. Era context.** Early 2014, in the taper period. The Chair says the unemployment rate is 6.6 percent. Asset purchases are being tapered, the funds rate is at 0-1/4 percent, and inflation is running below the 2 percent goal. I use this only as background. Scores below rest on what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Chair Yellen (framing), Kocherlakota, Bullard, Rosengren, Lockhart, Fisher, Williams, Evans, Pianalto, George, Tarullo, Stein, Powell, Vice Chair Dudley. Vice President Weinberg (substituting for Lacker) has no text in the input, and neither Plosser nor Lacker appears. The extraction looks to have dropped or omitted a turn (see ANOMALY_NOTE). Several members refer to a Plosser alternative and a Lacker comment, but that is second-hand.

**Special-topic judgment (stated plainly).** The rubric says a special-topic meeting (framework, operations, communications) with no standard economic assessment should be scored N/A on all dimensions. This meeting is a communications meeting, so that rule applies in spirit. However, the five Tier 3 dimensions have real content: the members state a policy stance, argue about guidance wording, mention financial stability and express uncertainty. The Tier 3 text also says N/A only where there is "no standard discussion". I therefore:
- score the five Tier 3 dimensions;
- score only the Tier 1 dimensions that members describe in passing, all at Low confidence with wide bands;
- mark everything else N/A.

A strict reader could set every Tier 1 and financial item to N/A. The Tier 1 scores here are thin and rest on incidental remarks, not an assessment.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target. Yellen: the possibility of "inflation running persistently and significantly below our objective has become more prominent". Bullard cites "lower-than-expected inflation outcomes". Evans: "I doubt that inflation is going to increase very quickly... to 2 percent very soon". Kocherlakota's example is an inflation outlook of 1 1/2 percent. The level is below target but not described as deflationary. Magnitude: 26-40 band ("below target... running below 2 percent"). Score 35.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output below potential. Powell: indicators like the unemployment gap "are likely to understate the real resource gap". Yellen's "false dawns" and the "weaker tone of recent data" point to slack. Bullard says output growth is "relatively robust", which is a growth comment and not a level comment. Bright line: below potential means < 50. Score 40, Low confidence.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as below full employment but close to the 6.5 percent threshold. The unemployment rate is 6.6 percent (Yellen), which falls in the 5.5-7 percent band (31-45). "Complete recovery in the labor market" is judged to be more than a year away. Fisher/Evans: unemployment of 6 percent or a bit below. Evans says the unemployment rate has behaved unusually. Score 40.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Only fleeting remarks, "weakness in incoming data" (Rosengren) and "weaker tone of recent data" (Yellen), against "relatively robust" output growth (Bullard). No spending or investment discussion. N/A.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Yellen says forward guidance has been "keeping financial conditions accommodative". Rosengren does not want language "to tighten financial conditions". Dudley discusses financial conditions through the lens of guidance. Direction is accommodative, so the bright line gives < 50. Evidence is thin and about guidance, not market levels. Score 38, Low confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no participant describes these (Fisher mentions an equity-market correction only as a hypothetical definition of instability; Dudley says only that bond yields and equity prices depend on the rate path). All N/A.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Lean: No one argues for tightening. The Committee wants to keep policy "highly accommodative" for a considerable time after asset purchases end (Powell, Williams, Dudley, Evans). The taper continues. Kocherlakota wants an explicit 2 percent inflation floor (strongly dovish), while Bullard and Lockhart oppose a floor, though they too want to keep accommodation. There is no easing proposal and no tightening bias, so it is not as dovish as a QE-expansion debate. Lean dovish-to-balanced, hold steady with accommodation: 38.
- Dissent: There is no vote and no dissent. Disagreement is about wording and tactics (inflation floor vs none; post-liftoff paragraph 6 yes or no; link to asset purchases; financial stability vs financial developments; "complete recovery" language). A converging "emergent consensus" on option 5 (Tarullo, Yellen's summary). There is no disagreement about the economic outlook. Broad agreement, with some tactical camps: 24.
- Financial stability emphasis: One of the four framing questions is whether and how to mention financial stability. Roughly ten participants address it, mostly briefly and with a preference for a minimal reference. Moderate, but it is about statement wording. 45.
- Forward guidance: The entire meeting is about it. The settled signal is that rates stay near zero for a considerable time after asset purchases end, the unemployment threshold is dropped in favor of qualitative language, and post-liftoff rates are expected to stay low (paragraph 6, Evans on 2 percent in 2016). Removing the numeric threshold dilutes the commitment somewhat, but the signal is still strongly easing, with data dependence. 17.
- Uncertainty: Members stress uncertainty about how to measure the labor market (Bullard: "considerable uncertainty may attend the very notion of an employment shortfall"; Powell: "uncertainty about the size of the gap is unusually high"), about forecast reliability (Dudley, Pianalto) and about market reactions. Yellen references "false dawns". Stein: "we're basically in a good place". Most of this is uncertainty about communication and measurement, not about the outlook. Somewhat elevated: 40.

**7. Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 35: higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target, so below 50. Match.
- OUTPUT_GAP 40: "resource gap" and slack, below potential, so < 50. Satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 40: UE 6.6 percent, below full employment. Match.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38: "accommodative" means < 50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, TREASURY_STRESS: N/A, so no bright-line violation is possible.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: the latter is N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs CREDIT_SPREADS / TREASURY_STRESS: the latter two are N/A, so no conflict.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-components: all N/A.

**9. N/A justification.** CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: only passing "data weakness" remarks, with no spending discussion. PRODUCTIVITY, TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, BANKING_STRESS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not raised by participants. INFLATION_GOODS/SERVICES/HOUSING: not discussed. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: "well anchored" appears only as proposed statement language (a condition in drafts), not as a member's assessment of the current state. N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +-8 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Yellen: "the possibility of inflation running persistently and significantly below our objective has become more prominent." Evans: "I doubt that inflation is going to increase very quickly, and I don't think it's going to increase to 2 percent very soon."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is below target and expected to stay there for some time, with no pressure concern.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe inflation as running below the 2 percent goal.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Powell: "indicators, like the unemployment gap, are likely to understate the real resource gap." Yellen: the many "false dawns the economy has presented"; "weaker tone of recent data".
- Center of gravity: Slack remains, and the size of the gap is itself in doubt; there is no explicit gap estimate.
- Direction confirmation: Output gap is below neutral because members refer to a remaining resource gap and weak recent data.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Yellen: "The unemployment rate, at 6.6 percent, is very close to the threshold." Pianalto: "the labor market will continue to improve gradually."
- Center of gravity: Unemployment is around 6.6 percent and falling, still short of full employment, with complete recovery seen as over a year away.
- Direction confirmation: Labor market is below neutral tightness because UE of 6.6 percent is in the elevated range and members speak of an incomplete recovery.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: N/A
- No participant assesses spending or investment; only incidental data-weakness remarks.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Yellen: forward guidance "in keeping financial conditions accommodative and as having thereby supported the recovery." Rosengren: "I would certainly not want the language to tighten financial conditions."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are described as accommodative, with a desire not to disturb them; there is no discussion of market levels or credit.
- Direction confirmation: Financial conditions are below neutral (loose) because the Chair describes them as accommodative.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A
### EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A
### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- None discussed by participants.

### INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Powell: monetary policy "will remain highly accommodative for a considerable time after the end of asset purchases" is the "ballast" holding expectations. Kocherlakota: "the FOMC is highly unlikely to raise the target fed funds rate at least until the one- to two-year-ahead outlook for inflation has risen back to 2 percent."
- Center of gravity: Everyone wants accommodation maintained through the end of tapering and beyond, with a minority (Kocherlakota, Pianalto, Evans) favoring a firmer inflation floor; nobody argues for tightening.
- Direction confirmation: Lean is dovish because the discussion centers on how to keep policy accommodative, with no tightening bias.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Tarullo: "the somewhat emergent consensus, with a lot of variation... a basic minimalist approach." Yellen: "I have heard a considerable amount of interest in something along the lines of option 5."
- Center of gravity: No vote and no dissent; real differences over an inflation floor, post-liftoff guidance and "complete recovery" wording, but not over the outlook or the stance.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is low-to-moderate because differences are tactical and the group converges on one option.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 +-10 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Bullard: "a minimalist reference is appropriate." Dudley: "I strongly prefer financial market developments to financial stability." Fisher: "it is very difficult to define financial stability."
- Center of gravity: Financial stability and financial developments were one of four named questions and about ten members commented, mostly briefly and on wording.
- Direction confirmation: Attention is moderate because several participants address it meaningfully, but it is not the center of the discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 17 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Williams: the Committee "will consider... maintain the current range for the federal funds rate until significant further progress toward its goals is achieved." Evans: "Our SEP forecasts for 2016 have us at our mandate goals, and the funds rate is about 2 percent."
- Center of gravity: The signal is low-for-long guidance, with the numeric threshold replaced by qualitative language and rates expected to stay below normal after liftoff.
- Direction confirmation: Guidance tone is strongly on the easing side because the stated aim is to keep expectations anchored low; dropping the threshold only partly dilutes the commitment.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-10 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Bullard: "considerable uncertainty may attend the very notion of an employment shortfall." Powell: "uncertainty about the size of the gap is unusually high."
- Center of gravity: Several members stress uncertainty about measurement and communication, but most also say expectations are well aligned and "we're basically in a good place" (Stein).
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated because multiple members flag measurement difficulty, though it is mostly about communications rather than the outlook.
