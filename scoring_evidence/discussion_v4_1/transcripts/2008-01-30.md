# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2008-01-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It contains the organizational items, questions to staff, the economic go-around (Jan 29 afternoon), the risk-management Q&A, the policy go-around (Jan 30 morning), and a later staff session on financial-crisis policy issues (rating agencies, underwriting, supervision) that is mostly questions and not an economic assessment. The vote read-out at the end shows 9 Yes, 1 No (Fisher) on a 50 bp cut to 3 percent (alternative B).

**2. Era context.** Meeting of the Committee after an intermeeting 75 bp cut; housing downturn, financial turmoil, recession fears, elevated headline and core inflation. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Bernanke, Geithner, Kohn, Kroszner, Mishkin, Warsh; presidents Poole, Yellen, Lacker, Plosser, Lockhart, Hoenig, Stern, Fisher, Evans, Pianalto, Rosengren. Near-universal: growth very weak, recession risk 40-50+ percent, financial fragility, inflation "stubbornly high" and a concern. Disagreement: Plosser, Lacker, Poole, Hoenig, Fisher stress inflation/credibility and want a pause or a plan to reverse; Fisher dissents for no change; Yellen, Kohn, Mishkin, Geithner, Rosengren would do more or see little insurance in 50 bp.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint, clearly above target. Lacker: PCE averaged 2 3/4 percent over four years; Plosser: core PCE 2.4 percent in H2 2007, price indexes near record; Fisher: Frito-Lay, Wal-Mart, rail adjustment factor. Bernanke: "stubbornly high." Offsets: most expect moderation, Mishkin and Yellen are sanguine. "Clearly above target, persistent, concerning" is the 61-75 band; expected moderation is not to lower the score. Score about 64.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. Rosengren: "Growth well below potential for the first half"; Hoenig: "grow below our potential"; Plosser disputes Greenbook slack in 2010 but accepts weak near-term growth. Economy skirting recession, so modest-to-considerable slack; around 38.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment around 5 percent and rising (up 0.6 point off the low; Yellen sees 5 1/4 percent, natural rate 4 3/4). Layoffs up, hiring plans cut, though pockets of shortage (software engineers, Northern Virginia). Near NAIRU but softening: about 44.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Retail weak, big-ticket slump, capex delayed, housing collapsing, consumption "slowed", though some exports/IT/energy strong and Fisher/Evans see "anemic, not negative". About 33.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only one passing remark (Geithner, "incipient optimism on the productivity outlook"; Plosser trend 2 3/4). Barely discussed; score low confidence near trend, 46.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Tighter. Kohn: lenders "demanding much greater compensation for taking risk in nearly every sector"; Warsh: "significantly tighter lending standards"; Pianalto: first signals of a credit crunch; Bernanke: "Financial markets remain stressed." Offsets: liquidity improved (TAF), equity raising. Tight, stress emerging: about 70.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only a question from Rosengren about whether the TAF helped Treasury functioning; no dysfunction described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (level reading): Kohn: IG and junk spreads "increased", CMBS spreads "risen substantially", banks tightened; Warsh: "risk premiums widening across the board"; Rosengren: Capital One CDS from under 100 to over 400 bp; Pianalto: credit "less available and more expensive than it has been in quite a while." Counter on level: Poole says "risk spreads are rising from abnormally low levels"; short-term funding spreads improved (LIBOR-OIS half of September). The text gives a mix of level evidence, with availability reduced and several stated as materially wider; I place it somewhat wide, above 50, with moderate confidence: about 62.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity declines mentioned (wealth effects, Plosser on equity premium), no valuation commentary. Default near neutral, slightly stressed: 55, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Above midpoint. Warsh: "financial institutions as a group are, in my view, undercapitalized"; Stern: "impaired capital positions of large banks"; Rosengren: bank CDS up; monolines. Geithner offers that major US institutions' capital "look pretty good" relative to the early 1990s, and Pianalto/Plosser note some banks are fine. Significant stress with some offsets: about 68.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Passing references only (Warsh "a lower exchange value of the dollar"; Geithner on central banks loosening links to the dollar). Depreciation, brief: 35, low confidence.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence says wider/less availability, so above 50, consistent. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter; evidence says loosening from near-NAIRU, so just below 50. BANKING_STRESS: evidence says undercapitalized/impaired, so above 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Policy go-around supports a 50 bp cut (9 of 10 voters) after a 75 bp intermeeting cut; Mishkin would prefer 75, Kohn/Yellen/Rosengren/Geithner say 50 contains little insurance, Bernanke says cut below 3 and reverse is conceivable but further at this time not advisable. Hawkish counterweight: Plosser (on the verge of overshooting), Hoenig (stop at 3), Fisher (hold), Lacker (25 preferred), Poole (down 50 or 25 makes little difference, favors longer-run inflation focus). Majority easing with a vocal hawkish minority: moderate dovish, about 30.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (Fisher, a fully argued hold). Serious reservations from Lacker (would have chosen 25), Plosser (statement language, "verge of overshooting"), Hoenig, Poole; debate on statement paragraph 4 and on whether to plan the reversal. Disagreement extends to inflation assessment and framework (risk management vs. certainty equivalence), yet tone collegial and outcome near-consensus. 1 dissent plus 3-4 reservations: about 42.
- Financial stability emphasis: Dominant theme in the economic go-around (monolines, bank capital, credit crunch, "adverse feedback loop"), Desk/TAF discussion, a full afternoon session on rating agencies and supervision. Prominent to dominant: about 78.
- Forward guidance: Statement keeps "downside risks to growth remain" and "act in a timely manner"; Bernanke says not on autopilot, data-dependent, and will stress reversal when appropriate; Plosser/Hoenig want guidance neutral. Easing lean with data dependence: about 33.
- Uncertainty: "exceptionally fluid," "highly uncertain," models fail at turning points, humility, "castle in the sand," wide scenario range and recession probability about 50 percent, inflation uncertainty up. High: about 72.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP < 50 (below potential), yes. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS > 50 (tight/stressed), yes. CREDIT_SPREADS > 50 on stated reduced availability and "wider" levels; no "compressed/narrow" language except Poole's recollection of earlier low levels; consistent with level reading. BANKING_STRESS > 50 (stressed, undercapitalized). DOLLAR < 50 (depreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs LABOR 44 (6 apart, ok). OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs CONSUMPTION 33 (ok). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 70 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 62 (co-move, ok). FC vs TREASURY: N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 64 vs sub-components: goods 72, expectations 57, services/housing N/A; consistent with a parent in the mid 60s.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning only raised as a question, no assessment. INFLATION_SERVICES: not discussed by members as a category (core mentioned in aggregate). INFLATION_HOUSING: Bernanke only notes owners' equivalent rent was not discussed and can behave oddly; no assessment, so N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 64 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Lacker, "it is hard to put a good face on the recent numbers"; Bernanke, "Core inflation and headline inflation have remained stubbornly high and are a concern."
- Center of gravity: Inflation clearly above target and a broad concern, though most expect moderation.
- Direction: above neutral because core PCE ran 2.4 percent in H2 2007 and pass-through reports are widespread.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "Growth well below potential for the first half of this year results in additional slack"; Hoenig, "we are going to grow below our potential growth rate."
- Center of gravity: Weak growth, recession risk, modest-to-meaningful slack opening.
- Direction: below neutral because activity is below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen, labor market "has indeed been weakening since mid-2007"; Lacker, some regions "labor markets remain tight" for skilled workers.
- Center of gravity: Unemployment about 5 percent and rising, softening from near-NAIRU with pockets of shortage.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the market is loosening.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Pianalto, department store "January sales ... the worst ... in the past twenty years"; Lacker, firms "delaying capital spending projects."
- Center of gravity: Consumption and business investment clearly below trend, though not collapsing (Fisher: "anemic ... not negative").
- Direction: below neutral because spending is soft.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 46 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Geithner, "a little sign of incipient optimism on the productivity outlook."
- Center of gravity: One passing remark; roughly trend.
- Direction: near neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 70 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "depleted credit availability and significantly tighter lending standards"; Pianalto, "first signals of a credit crunch."
- Center of gravity: Credit tightening and market stress, partly offset by improved liquidity.
- Direction: above neutral because conditions are tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no participant assessment of Treasury market dysfunction).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Credit spreads on both investment-grade and junk bonds have increased ... Spreads on CMBS have risen substantially"; Poole, "Risk spreads are rising from abnormally low levels."
- Center of gravity: Spreads somewhat wide with reduced availability; the level evidence is mixed, so confidence is moderate.
- Direction: above neutral because several speakers describe materially wider spreads and curtailed credit, not just direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Nonfinancial corporations have experienced declines in equity prices"; Plosser on the equity premium rising.
- Center of gravity: Price moves discussed without valuation view; default near neutral.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because of declines and concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 68 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "financial institutions as a group are, in my view, undercapitalized"; Rosengren, "credit risk for financial institutions has grown."
- Center of gravity: Significant strain (capital, monolines, CDS), with Geithner noting capital looks decent against the early 1990s.
- Direction: above neutral because stress is rising.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh, "a lower exchange value of the dollar."
- Center of gravity: Brief mention of a weaker dollar.
- Direction: below neutral because the dollar has depreciated.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 72 [value only]** Fisher on food, rail, Wal-Mart, Tyson; Kohn on energy/food supply shock.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57 [value only]** Plosser, "Michigan survey ... measures are up"; Mishkin, "just no evidence that inflation expectations have gone up"; most say anchored with a risk of drift.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "I recommend a 50 basis point cut at this meeting, to 3 percent"; Plosser, "we are on the verge of overshooting."
- Center of gravity: A large cut with majority support and calls for more insurance, against a vocal hawkish minority.
- Direction: below neutral (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher, "recommending no change"; Lacker, "I could have chosen a 25 basis point cut today."
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent plus several reservations and a real inflation-versus-insurance debate within a largely collegial meeting.
- Direction: moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 78 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "adverse feedback loop—the interaction between a slowing economy and the credit markets"; Geithner, "dangerous self-reinforcing cycle."
- Center of gravity: Financial fragility dominates the assessment and a full session addresses financial-crisis policy.
- Direction: high attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 33 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "we stand ready to cut further if necessary"; Bernanke, "the Fed is not on autopilot for further rate cuts."
- Center of gravity: Retained downside-risk language with data dependence, plus commitment to reverse when appropriate.
- Direction: below neutral (easing lean, data dependent).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "exceptionally fluid"; Kroszner, "Our models have never been successful at assessing turning points."
- Center of gravity: Many emphasize difficulty, wide recession and inflation scenarios, model doubts.
- Direction: high uncertainty.
