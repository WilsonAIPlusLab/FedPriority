# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1997-02-05

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Transcript discussion of a two-day meeting (Feb 4-5). It contains the opening procedural items, members' questions to staff, the Feb 4 economic go-around, the Feb 5 morning discussion of the monetary-aggregate ranges and long-run inflation objectives, the policy go-around and a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. Names are partly garbled in places, and "Stem" is the extraction of "Stern". Procedure was ignored. I scored participants' views on conditions and policy.

**2. Era context (not used to override the text).** Strong economic expansion, low unemployment and low measured inflation. The funds rate is 5-1/4 percent, and the debate is about when to tighten. The text itself carries this: members describe a strong Q4 1996 (4.7 percent GDP growth), tight labor markets, benign realized inflation and upside risks.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish on timing: Parry (wants +25bp now), Minehan (wants +25bp now), Melzer (wants alternative C or "something in that direction"), Hoenig (worried about waiting) and Broaddus (accepts "B" asymmetric but says tighten "almost certainly" in March).
- Wait but lean to tighten: Greenspan (hold now, "March may be the appropriate time"), McDonough, Meyer, Moskow, Stern, Boehne, Rivlin, Phillips, Guynn and Kelley.
- Jordan prefers a higher funds rate in hindsight and focuses on communication and price stability.

**4. Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see realized inflation as modestly above the 2 percent target and benign (CPI about 3 percent, core CPI 2.6 percent). Prices are "somnolent" (McDonough), and the Chair sees "no credible evidence of increasing price pressures". They also see wage pressures building and upside risks, with the "inflation dragon nibbling". Above the midpoint but modestly, so 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint. Moskow says output is "somewhat above potential", Parry says output "already appeared to be above its long-run potential", Hoenig says the economy is "further beyond its long-run production capacity" and Meyer says "at or beyond capacity". Kelley dissents on utilization ("not much pressure on capacity utilization"). Score 59.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above the midpoint. "Tight labor markets are reported almost everywhere" (Rivlin), "very tight" (Moskow), and Meyer says unemployment is "about to move still further below NAIRU". The national rate is about 5.3 percent, with local rates of 3-4 percent. The UE anchor alone would give about 50, but the participants' language is strong, so 60.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Above trend. The Q4 surge is called unsustainable, but "the consumer is back" (Kelley), "robust economy" (Boehne), and business investment has been consistently underestimated (Phillips). Weighting the 1997 slowdown talk against the current strength gives 62.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mixed and discussed. Official data are "disappointing" (staff trend 0.9 percent, Phillips; Rivlin: "significantly mysterious"). Anecdotal and income-side evidence points to large gains (Jordan, Greenspan). Net near trend, so 46.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Accommodative. Minehan cites "a financing environment... conducive to excess" and "liquid financial markets". Phillips cites "low cost of capital, a ready availability of credit". Greenspan says markets "look just too benign". Bright-line (<50) satisfied, so 32.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A (see below).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Greenspan cites "sharp narrowing of junk bond yield spreads" and risk spreads "quite narrow... for quite a while". Level is compressed, so < 50 by bright-line rule. Score 24.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: High valuation concern. Greenspan's long argument concludes "the market is overvalued", and he would not invest "very forcefully". Minehan calls markets "frothy" and Phillips says "irrationally exuberant or fundamentally driven". Elevated-valuation band (16-30), so 20.
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar has strengthened recently (Parry, Moskow, Kelley, Phillips, McTeer), so > 50. Modest strength, so 60.

**5. Polarity check.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT mean more of each, and my evidence is for more. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS low = loose, and evidence says loose. CREDIT_SPREADS low = narrow, and evidence says narrow. EQUITY_VALUATION low = overvaluation concern, and evidence says overvalued, which is consistent with the rubric's table.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds at 5-1/4 percent with an asymmetric ("B") directive. The Chair and McDonough say this means tightening probably in March. Four or five members (Parry, Minehan, Melzer, Hoenig, and Broaddus in part) want action now or very soon. This is a hold with a strong tightening bias and several preferring an immediate move, so about 62.
- Dissent: Unanimous vote, with no formal dissent. Parry, Minehan and Melzer stated a preference for tightening now but supported or went along with the directive (the read-out is all Yes). Hoenig voiced concern about waiting. Stern, Moskow, Kelley and Phillips argued for patience. There was also a debate on explicit inflation targets and on M2 interpretation (Jordan, Meyer, McTeer outliers). Differences are over timing, not assessment. Score 33.
- Financial stability emphasis: The Chair spent a sizable passage on complacency, equity premia, junk spreads and a "stable, viable financial system". Minehan, Phillips and Guynn (complacency) touched on it, but most discussion is inflation and labor. About 33.
- Forward guidance: The Chair wants to "prime the markets" in Humphrey-Hawkins testimony. "We might be moving quite soon even in the absence of clear evidence" and "be prepared to move... at the next meeting". This is a clear tightening signal but with conditionality, so 76.
- Uncertainty: Considerable attention to forecast, NAIRU and wage-price uncertainty ("inscrutable", "enigma", "productivity... mysterious", "wait and see"). It is normal-to-somewhat-elevated, not unusual. Score 42.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 59 (>50, with "above potential" language). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 32 (<50). CREDIT_SPREADS 24 (<50, level described as narrow). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 (>50, appreciation). BANKING_STRESS and TREASURY_STRESS are N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 59 vs LABOR 60 vs CONSUMPTION 62: all within 15 and aligned. FIN_COND 32 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 24: both loose, coherent. TREASURY N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 vs sub-components (goods 45, expectations 55): the parent leans above because wage/upside-risk talk dominates, with goods subdued by supply shocks. Acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.**
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only long-term yield levels are mentioned, with no market functioning discussion.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only passing remarks on lending standards (McTeer: banks "just beginning to get serious about tightening their consumer credit lending standards"; Boehne: "caution from lenders"). Nothing on bank health.
- INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: Not discussed as price categories. Housing price anecdotes are regional and not about shelter inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "we expect the CPI to rise by a bit under 3 percent this year... significant risk of an increase in underlying inflation"; McDonough, "Inflation both for prices and overall labor costs remains somnolent". Center of gravity: realized inflation is modestly above target and benign, with building upside risk. Inflation pressure is above neutral because CPI is about 3 percent and wages are rising, but the pressure is not yet realized.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 59 ±6 High.** Evidence: Moskow, "output was still somewhat above potential"; Hoenig, "the economy is further beyond its long-run production capacity". Center of gravity: most see output above potential. The gap is above neutral because of repeated "above potential/capacity" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±6 High.** Evidence: Rivlin, "Tight labor markets are reported almost everywhere, wages are creeping up"; Meyer, "the unemployment rate is about to move still further below NAIRU". Center of gravity: labor markets are tight nearly everywhere. Labor is above neutral because of widespread shortage reports.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 ±6 High.** Evidence: Boehne, "we clearly have a robust economy"; Kelley, "the consumer is back". Center of gravity: strong spending that is expected to slow toward trend. Above neutral on current strength.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 46 ±10 Moderate.** Evidence: Rivlin, "The productivity data remain significantly mysterious; the low increases do not seem to fit"; Jordan, "indirect evidence that productivity gains are quite high". Center of gravity: official data weak, anecdotes strong. Roughly at trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Minehan, "a financing environment that obviously was conducive to excess"; Phillips, "the low cost of capital, a ready availability of credit". Center of gravity: loose, benign financial markets. Below neutral because of accommodative, easy language.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 24 ±7 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "the sharp narrowing of junk bond yield spreads against Treasuries"; Greenspan, "the various risk spreads, which are really quite narrow and have been that way for quite a while". Center of gravity: compressed spreads, a main source of the Chair's concern. Below neutral because the level is narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 20 ±6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan, "This then means the market is overvalued in that regard"; Minehan, "financial markets that are too one-sided and frothy". Center of gravity: valuation concern. Low in this polarity means overvaluation concern, so below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A.**

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±6 High.** Evidence: Parry, "The recent rise in the dollar and in long-term interest rates"; Kelley, "the recent movement of the dollar... the stronger dollar should tend to weaken exports". Center of gravity: modest appreciation. Above neutral on direction.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 45.** Evidence: Meyer, "decline in nominal import prices, and the sharper-than-normal decline in computer prices"; Jordan, steel prices expected lower; Broaddus, some pass-through in furniture, paper, electrical equipment. Net slightly subdued.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55.** Evidence: Melzer, "Expectations of 3 percent inflation seem to be fairly widespread"; Parry, surveys at "about 3 percent inflation one year ahead"; Greenspan and Guynn, complacency about inflation. Modestly above a 2 percent target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "I would prefer a 25 basis point increase in the funds rate to 5-1/2 percent"; Greenspan, "March may be the appropriate time... when we will have to move". Center of gravity: hold now with a strong bias to tighten shortly, and several favoring action now. Above neutral because the majority expects tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Melzer, "I favor alternative C or at least something in that direction"; Moskow, "the prudent thing to do is to wait longer"; unanimous vote read-out. Center of gravity: no formal dissent, but a clear split on timing between those wanting a move now and those wanting to wait. Moderate differences, so low-30s.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 33 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "the financial markets look just too benign"; Greenspan, risk spreads "quite narrow... suggest that product prices alone should not be the sole criterion". Center of gravity: limited but pointed discussion led by the Chair, with Minehan and Phillips adding. Limited discussion rather than a dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 76 ±6 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "we would have to prime the markets to anticipate that we might be moving quite soon"; McDonough, "we ought to be girding our loins for a probable tightening at the next meeting". Center of gravity: explicit effort to prepare markets for tightening. Hawkish, but conditional on data.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±8 Moderate.** Evidence: Meyer, "Uncertainty about the forecast, about the structure of the economy or specifically about the value of NAIRU, justifies... a more cautious approach"; Kelley, "the enigma of the consumer". Center of gravity: somewhat elevated uncertainty about NAIRU, productivity and wages, yet not unusual. Moderately elevated, not high.
