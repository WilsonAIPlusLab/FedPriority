# v4.1 scoring: member discussion (transcript), meeting 2010-08-10

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, one-day meeting. It has a short financial/economic Q&A (members' questions only; staff answers removed), an economic go-round (Plosser, Bullard, Rosengren, Pianalto, Fisher, Lacker, Yellen, Lockhart, Hoenig, Kocherlakota, Evans, then after a break Dudley, Kohn, Warsh, Duke, Tarullo), a Chairman summary, a short Desk Q&A on reinvestment, and a policy go-round on "alternative B" (stop the passive MBS runoff by reinvesting principal payments into longer-term Treasuries). It ends with a roll call (Hoenig No, all other voters Yes). Staff-reported content (Tealbook forecasts quoted by members, Desk views) is ignored except as members' own adopted view.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2010 recovery from the deep recession: unemployment about 9.5%, core PCE inflation about 1%, policy rate at the zero bound, a large balance sheet, and a debate on whether to do more. I score from what the members say, not from this backdrop.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Dovish and activist: Evans (wants a "far more aggressive version of alternative A" with substantial Treasury purchases), Bullard (enhanced QE, signal now), Yellen, Rosengren, Kohn, Dudley, Pianalto, Tarullo (all support B and want options for more), Bernanke ("we are too tight", "our nominal variables are below target").
- Cautious or opposed: Hoenig (wants rates raised toward 1%, option C, opposes rollover, dissent), Plosser (non-voter, would dissent; calls B "fine-tuning hubris"), Lacker (policy has already eased; prefers "B classic" without para 4), Kocherlakota (B without para 4; purchases ineffective, Fed not the right vehicle), Fisher (non-voter, cautions against B), Warsh (prefers MBS reinvestment, wants optionality, warns of misleading signals), Duke ("real reservations", but supports), Lockhart (supports "with a little apprehension").
- Economy: nearly all say the recovery lost momentum, with weak labor market and household spending, and the NIPA revisions show a deeper recession and a wider gap. A minority (Hoenig, Plosser, Lacker, Warsh) stress the recovery is intact and the data are noisy. Inflation is low, with deflation a tail risk to most and "remote" to the Chair.

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as well below the 2% objective: core PCE about 1% (Bullard, Kocherlakota, Yellen, Evans), "inflation tracking well below our objectives" (Kohn), "price pressures are nil" (Fisher), though a few note upward NIPA revisions and a core CPI uptick (Plosser, Lacker). Deflation risk is discussed but called remote. This is below the midpoint, in the "subdued, very low" band, roughly 24-28.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below potential. Kohn: "the recession was deeper and the output gap wider"; Tarullo: "badly underperforming"; Kocherlakota: per capita GDP "more than 7 percent below trend". Bright line satisfied (<50). The recovery is under way, so the 2010-14 anchor (35-42) applies, shaded lower for the wider gap: about 32.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: unemployment 9.5%, "no net improvement over the last 12 months" (Tarullo), employment-to-population at 1980s lows, employers reluctant to hire. A few pockets of tightness are noted (Dakotas) but are anecdotal. Anchor 15-22: about 18.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumers saving more, spending revised down, housing weak, state and local governments retrenching, capex confined to replacement. Offsets: equipment and software spending, manufacturing and shipping strength, Hoenig's list of positive trend data, and Evans's Deere and Caterpillar remarks. Net below trend, about 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: touched only in passing ("apparent slowdown in productivity growth" per Kohn, "end to the unusually high productivity growth during the recession" per Tarullo, firms "realizing efficiencies" per Fisher). No clear level judgment, so near neutral with Low confidence, about 50.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: members say conditions have eased and are accommodative or supportive since June (Plosser "very accommodative", Dudley "eased", Chair "weaker dollar and declining Treasury yields have further eased financial conditions", Fisher on easy access for large firms). Offsets: tight credit to small and some bank-dependent borrowers (Yellen, Kohn "still quite tight", Chair "credit constraints do remain a problem", the lack of mortgage refinancing). Bright line says supportive/accommodative means <50: about 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries discussed as a rally and falling term premium, with no dysfunction. Warsh: "The Treasury market is doing everything for us... working well"; Plosser: "markets seem to be functioning normally". Calm anchor 25-35: about 28.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level reading. Fisher: "spreads have pretty much returned to the norm", issuance back to 2006-07 levels, CP spreads near zero, a single-A 10-year priced about 67bp over Treasuries; Kohn: risk premiums down but "not back to where they were in April, so there's still a net tightening since the spring". Normal-to-slightly-favorable level, direction improving. Bank credit to small borrowers remains constrained (availability), pulling slightly up. About 44. Bright line: no high-level statement, so no >50 requirement; the language is "back to norm", so 44-48 is consistent.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: stocks are up on earnings and reduced European tail risk (Plosser, Lacker, Chair summary), with no valuation or stress commentary. Default neutral, Low confidence: about 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: Warsh "bank credit quality appears much improved"; Duke "credit quality continues to improve", but CRE losses "only a small fraction" recognized, weak loan demand, and consolidation pressure; Pianalto "commercial real estate exposures still lurk"; loan losses below the SCAP adverse scenario. Healthy with minor concerns, bright line <50: about 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: weaker. Dudley "the dollar weakening"; Kohn "the dollar has reversed most of its gain over the spring"; Warsh "a weaker dollar"; Chair "a weaker dollar". Bright line <50: about 38.
- Inflation sub-components: goods and services both weak (Yellen: weakness "widespread among both goods and services"; Fisher: nil price pressure except transportation and commodities; Kohn: rising commodity prices as a partial offset). Housing inflation not discussed, so N/A. Expectations: "stable or down slightly", "relatively well-anchored", with Bullard and Kohn flagging the TIPS decline as a warning sign: slightly below anchored, about 42.

**5. Polarity verification.** CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; the evidence says spreads are near normal and improving, so a score just under 50 matches. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: evidence is depreciation, so below 50 matches. BANKING_STRESS: "improving, healthy-ish" means low stress, so below 50 matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: easing and accommodative means looser, so below 50 matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: the decision is to stop passive balance-sheet tightening by reinvesting MBS runoff into longer Treasuries, a mild easing step, and the debate is about how much more QE to signal. Evans wants substantial purchases and Bullard an enhanced QE response; Bernanke says policy is "too tight" relative to the mandate. The hawkish side is a minority and mostly argues for the status quo (Plosser, Lacker, Kocherlakota, Fisher) or MBS-only reinvestment (Warsh); only Hoenig wants actual tightening. By the rubric note, a QE-rollover debate is dovish. Moderate-dovish, about 28.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Hoenig, who argues the economic assessment differs: the economy is improving and zero rates are risky). Several non-voters would dissent (Plosser says so explicitly), and Lacker, Kocherlakota and Fisher oppose paragraph 4. Warsh and Duke express strong reservations; Evans pulls in the opposite direction; the Chairman says "I don't have the full support of the Committee." Disagreement is both about tactics and about economic assessment (deflation risk, efficacy of QE). Rubric anchor 55-65 for 2010-11 QE debates: about 60.
- Financial stability/conditions attention: the Chairman opens by urging more integration of financial-stability analysis; Fisher, Lacker, Warsh, Duke, Kohn, Tarullo and Pianalto discuss credit conditions, bank health, spreads and financial-market response at length; Europe's sovereign stress is cited. It is a notable but not dominant topic next to labor, growth and balance-sheet policy: about 45.
- Forward guidance: "extended period" retained; B is explicitly a signal of openness to further action (Yellen: "signals our openness to moving in this direction"); Bullard wants firm, determined asset purchases; Yellen wants to consider further accommodation if the forecast is downgraded; Chair sees a framework for future purchases but "I don't anticipate bringing another large step back." The Kohn clause on long-run inflation was dropped. Easing bias with data dependence: about 24.
- Uncertainty: Yellen and the Chair call the outlook "unusually uncertain"; Plosser, Pianalto, Lockhart, Evans, Lacker, Fisher and Kohn all stress heightened uncertainty, policy uncertainty and downside-skewed risks; Hoenig "no one can predict the future". The Chair also notes one uncertainty measure looks normal, and most still see a modal recovery. Elevated but not extreme: about 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below 50, FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 (accommodative/eased), CREDIT_SPREADS at 44 (no high-level language, level near normal), BANKING_STRESS below 50, DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 (weakening), TREASURY_STRESS well below 55 (no dysfunction). All satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR 18: gap of 14, within 15; the labor score is anchored on the 9.5% level. OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 44: co-move, both modestly below neutral (loose conditions, near-normal spreads, tight small-business credit). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, low Treasury stress consistent. Inflation parent 26 vs sub-components (goods 33, services 30, expectations 42): parent is a bit lower than the sub-component average because members stress the shortfall from target at about 1%; acceptable.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_HOUSING is N/A: no member spoke to shelter or rent inflation. Other optional financial items were all touched on and are scored (equity and productivity at Low confidence).

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 26 ±6 [Moderate-High → High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "we are underrunning our inflation objective... failing by a full percentage point"; Fisher: "Price pressures are nil, except in the transportation and some commodity spaces."
- Summary: Core inflation about 1%, below target, deflation a tail risk but not the base case.
- Direction: below neutral because inflation is well under the 2% objective. (Confidence: High.)

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "the recession was deeper than estimated and the output gap was larger"; Kocherlakota: per capita GDP "still more than 7 percent below trend".
- Summary: Substantial slack, recovery slow, gap wider after revisions.
- Direction: below neutral because participants describe slack and a larger gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 ±4 [High]**
- Evidence: Tarullo: "the number of employed people in the country decreased by roughly 900,000" over the year; Kocherlakota: "Unemployment remains high at 9½ percent."
- Summary: Very high unemployment with no net improvement and low participation.
- Direction: below neutral; 9.5% unemployment is far from full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "consumers saving more, and home sales weak"; Lacker: "consumer spending has not been as robust as we thought".
- Summary: Household spending soft, capex mostly replacement, offset by strong equipment, manufacturing and shipping.
- Direction: below trend because the net picture is sluggish spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "Despite the apparent slowdown in productivity growth, payroll growth has been quite damped"; Tarullo cites "an end to the unusually high productivity growth during the recession".
- Summary: Mentioned only in passing as unusually strong earlier and now slowing, with no level judgment.
- Direction: at neutral because only a fading above-trend gain is mentioned.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "financial conditions in the United States have eased since the last meeting, with equities recovering a bit, the dollar weakening, and long-term interest rates having fallen"; Chair: "credit constraints do remain a problem."
- Summary: Conditions are easier and supportive, with small-business and mortgage credit still tight.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because easing and accommodative language dominates.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 28 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "Those markets are working well, not just in terms of market functioning but also in terms of prices"; Plosser: "markets also seem to be functioning normally for the most part."
- Summary: A rally in Treasuries without any hint of dysfunction.
- Direction: below neutral because markets are described as well functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 44 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "spreads have pretty much returned to the norm"; Kohn: risk premiums down but "still a net tightening since the spring."
- Summary: Spreads near normal and improving, with remaining tightness in availability for small borrowers.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the stated level is "back to the norm", not elevated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "Stock prices are up, reflective of better than expected earnings"; Kohn: "the rise in equity markets" aiding wealth effects.
- Summary: Price moves discussed without valuation concern.
- Direction: at neutral because no valuation view is offered.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "bank credit quality appears much improved"; Duke: banks "have recognized only a small fraction of the embedded losses" in CRE.
- Summary: Banks are improving and profitable with CRE and weak-loan-demand concerns lingering.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is described as improving and sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "the dollar weakening"; Kohn: "the dollar has reversed most of its gain over the spring".
- Summary: The dollar has weakened since the last meeting, retracing its spring gains.
- Direction: below neutral because participants describe depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 33.** Evidence: Yellen, "weakness in prices remains widespread among both goods and services"; Kohn notes rising commodity prices as a partial offset.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 30.** Evidence: Yellen's same remark, plus the wage-cut anecdote (base wage dropped 15%).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42.** Evidence: Chair: "stable or down slightly"; Bullard: expectations "have fallen at all horizons" on the Cleveland measure, yet Plosser sees them "relatively well-anchored".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "I favor an aggressively enhanced version of alternative A with substantial large-scale asset purchases"; Bernanke: "even if you are a pure inflation targeter... we are too tight."
- Summary: A majority of voters favor an easing step now (reinvest into Treasuries) and some want more, against a hawkish minority led by Hoenig and a status-quo bloc.
- Direction: dovish because the decision and debate concern additional accommodation.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 60 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Hoenig: "I very much oppose this policy"; Plosser: "if I were a voting member, I would dissent"; Bernanke: "I don't have the full support of the Committee."
- Summary: One formal dissent, several non-voting opponents, two members with strong reservations, and a member pushing for much more, with disagreement over the assessment as well as tactics.
- Direction: well above neutral because camps are distinct and span both flanks.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "I propose that we continue to expand and integrate financial stability analysis into these meetings"; Warsh: "the credit situation has turned in the United States."
- Summary: Credit conditions, bank health and market response get meaningful attention from many, but are secondary to growth and balance-sheet policy.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 24 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: reinvestment "signals our openness to moving in this direction" (renewed asset purchases); Lockhart: "I think the 'extended period' language remains appropriate, and I would hold that guidance."
- Summary: Extended-period language retained, balance sheet held constant, and a readiness to do more, with the Chair not promising another large step.
- Direction: easing bias with data dependence, so below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "my outlook, like yours, Mr. Chairman, is unusually uncertain"; Plosser: "considerable error bands around our forecast".
- Summary: Many emphasize heightened uncertainty and downside risk, but most still hold a modal recovery and some push back on the uncertainty narrative.
- Direction: above neutral because multiple participants call uncertainty unusual.
