# v4.1 score: FOMC member discussion, transcript (meeting 2011-08-09)

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Participants-only transcript of one meeting: questions during the staff Q&A, the economic go-round, the policy go-round, a drafting scramble over the statement, and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed. Procedure (farewells, motions) is ignored.

**Era context.** Summer 2011: a weak recovery with the policy rate at the zero bound and a large balance sheet. The text shows a sharp equity sell-off after the US rating downgrade, a European sovereign scare (Italy and Spain), GDP revisions, and a debate on whether to give date-based guidance. I score what participants say, not what I remember about the period.

**Speakers and views.**
- Dovish camp: Yellen (case for action "compelling", proposes an unemployment threshold), Evans (9% unemployment as a policy loss like 5% inflation), Williams, Rosengren, Raskin, Dudley, Tarullo, Duke, Pianalto, Lockhart (reluctantly, as a modest step) and the Chair (proposes "at least through mid-2013").
- Hawkish or "no more accommodation" camp: Fisher, Plosser, Kocherlakota (formal dissents), Lacker, Bullard and Hoenig (against action or date-based guidance, but Bullard and Hoenig do not dissent).

**Dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see it as slightly below or around target on balance. Headline inflation is moderating (oil and commodities falling), and trimmed-mean measures show it contained (Fisher, Bernanke). Core ran a bit above 2% in Q2 (Plosser, Kocherlakota), and several expect about 1.4-1.5% (Rosengren, Williams, Evans). The score sits a little below 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below the midpoint. Rosengren cites "large degree of resource slack", Kocherlakota a possibly wider gap, and Bernanke low resource utilization. Lacker, Bullard and Plosser argue some weakness is lower potential, so I stay out of the deep-recession range.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: well below the midpoint. Unemployment is about 9%, and the employment-to-population ratio is the lowest since 1983 (Rosengren, Raskin). The labor market is called "quite concerning" (Bernanke).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: below trend. Real PCE fell three months in a row (Yellen), sentiment is poor, and business investment is hesitant. Autos are an offsetting transitory bounce.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed as productivity growth, so N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: mixed. Equities are down sharply, bank stocks and CDS are weaker, and European funding is stressed (Dudley, Rosengren, Tarullo, Bernanke). On the other side, Treasury and mortgage rates fell (Lacker, Lockhart, Fisher), business credit has "healed" (Duke), and the Committee deleted the "more restrictive" sentence. The net is somewhat tight, above 50. The bright-line rule is satisfied: nobody calls conditions "accommodative", and Bullard's "accommodative" refers to policy.
- TREASURY_STRESS: yields fell on flight to safety, and no dysfunction was described, so this is a normal-market score with low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Dudley says "credit spreads wider", Fisher "slight widening in spreads", and bank CDS and the Italian and Spanish spreads are wider. No level is given, so by the level rule I score in the 45-55 band on the side of the move, with low confidence. Duke's standards "in the middle" for business credit pulls toward normal.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher puts the S&P at 19x against a normal of about 19.5, with more downside possible. Dudley notes the Shiller view that it is overvalued but rejects it. This is a sharp sell-off but roughly fair value, so a mild score above 50.
- BANKING_STRESS: split. Duke says the banking system is "in much better shape", and Bernanke says the US banking sector is "generally stronger". Dudley calls bank stock and CDS moves "most disturbing", Rosengren sees a possible troubled large-bank shock, and European bank funding is stressed. Score just under 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only "flight into safe-haven currencies" (Dudley), with no dollar direction stated, so N/A.
- Inflation sub-components:
  - Goods: commodities and oil are down, auto prices are receding, and import-price pass-through faded; core goods revisions were up (Plosser). Slightly below the midpoint.
  - Services: only a passing mention (Plosser), so N/A.
  - Housing: the rental market is strong (Rosengren) but this is not framed as inflation, so N/A.
  - Expectations: stable (Bernanke, Raskin) or drifting down (Pianalto, Rosengren), with an outlier saying policy "ratcheted up" expectations (Bullard). Slightly below the midpoint.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: dovish. A majority favors some further accommodation. The Chair proposes the "at least through mid-2013" language, Yellen, Evans, Williams and Rosengren want more, and QE3 and Twist are on the table. Hawks object. It is not the emergency-easing range, because the action actually adopted is modest guidance.
- Dissent: three formal hawkish dissents (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser) with different reasoning (political optics of a date, the inflation-commitment framework, ineffectiveness and distortions). Other reservations came from Lacker, Bullard and Hoenig. Disagreement runs to economics, not just tactics, and exchanges are pointed (Evans/Hoenig, Fisher "I cannot support that"). Rosengren: "If you're going to take three dissents".
- Financial stability emphasis: substantial but not dominant. Market turmoil, European funding, bank stock and CDS stress, and the downgrade were discussed by many, and a statement sentence on financial conditions was debated. The policy debate itself is mostly about guidance.
- Forward guidance: strongly easing and date-based ("exceptionally low levels... at least through mid-2013"), conditional on projections. Not the full threshold form, since Yellen's 7.5% unemployment proposal was dropped.
- Uncertainty: high. Lockhart ("a lot more uncertainty than whatever the Committee could consider normal"), Bernanke ("really and truly in this case, higher than usual"), Lacker ("uncharted waters"), Bullard ("90 percent confidence intervals are so wide as to be laughable"), Tarullo ("inflection point").

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: slack language gives a score below 50. Satisfied.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: tightening language gives a score above 50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: direction only, so inside 45-55 on the side of the move. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: mixed, with no blanket "sound" claim from the group. Score just under 50, so it does not violate the rule.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction described, so the score is below 50.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (32), LABOR_TIGHTNESS (24) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (31): all below 50, and none diverge by more than 15 points from the others except labor against the gap (8 points), which is fine.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (57) and CREDIT_SPREADS (54): co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS (42): sits in the normal band, consistent with the Treasury market being orderly.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (44): consistent with the goods (47) and expectations (44) readings.

**N/A.** PRODUCTIVITY, DOLLAR_STRENGTH, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING were not discussed in a scorable way.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bernanke, "it doesn't seem that high inflation is a near-term concern"; Plosser, "Core PCE inflation ran a bit more than 2 percent in the second quarter." Summary: headline is moderating and the core pickup is judged largely temporary, with Plosser and Kocherlakota the main voices of upside concern. Direction: below neutral because most expect inflation to settle below 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Rosengren, "Given the large degree of resource slack in the economy"; Raskin, "considerable slack in labor markets". Summary: large slack is the majority view, with a minority emphasizing lower potential. Direction: below potential because members describe slack and weak demand.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Raskin, "58.1 percent, the lowest level since 1983"; Bernanke, "labor market conditions are quite concerning." Summary: unemployment near 9% with a very low employment ratio. Direction: below neutral because of high unemployment and little hiring.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 31 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "real personal consumption expenditures have declined in each of the past three monthly readings"; Bernanke, "Consumer spending has been extremely restrained." Summary: weak consumption and hesitant investment, with an auto rebound seen as transitory. Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Tarullo, "Plummeting equity markets and tightening financial conditions"; Fisher, "Volatile? Yes. Restrictive? I'd question that." Summary: turmoil in equities, bank funding and sovereign markets offset by lower Treasury and mortgage rates, and the Committee deleted the "more restrictive" sentence. Direction: modestly tighter than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Bullard, "Treasury yields have remained extremely low... flight-to-safety"; Lacker, "Treasury rates... have fallen precipitously." Summary: orderly market with falling yields and no dysfunction. Direction: at or slightly below normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 +-6 (Low).** Evidence: Dudley, "credit spreads wider"; Fisher, "a slight widening in spreads". Summary: direction of widening only, no level. Direction: slightly above 50 because the move is a widening and no level is given.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Fisher, "we're at 19 times earnings... normal valuation is about 19 and a half... substantial downside left"; Dudley, "Shiller would say the stock market is actually very overvalued." Summary: sharp sell-off and heightened concern, but participants see valuation as roughly fair. Direction: modestly above neutral on stress and concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 46 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Duke, "The banking system is in much better shape"; Dudley, "bank stocks falling very, very sharply and CDS spreads widening." Summary: balance sheets are better but market stress and European funding are a live worry. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS: 47.** Evidence: Bernanke, "oil down almost 30 percent... automobile prices receding"; Plosser, "three months of upward revisions in prices for core goods and services."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Evidence: Pianalto, "inflation expectations... fell significantly across most durations"; Bernanke, "Survey and financial measures of inflation expectations have remained stable."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "I consider the case for policy action compelling"; Rosengren, "we would today be considering at least a 25 basis point easing." Summary: a majority wants more accommodation (guidance through mid-2013, with Twist, a peg and QE3 as options), while a hawkish minority resists. Direction: dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 70 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "It would be very unpleasant to have three dissents"; roll call shows Fisher, Kocherlakota and Plosser voting No. Summary: three formal dissents with different reasons, plus further reservations from Lacker, Bullard and Hoenig, and disagreement that reaches the diagnosis. Direction: well above the one-dissent norm.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Dudley, "bank stocks falling very, very sharply and CDS spreads widening"; Bernanke, "very severe stresses in the financial system". Summary: market and bank stress get sustained attention from many speakers but are secondary to the guidance debate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 +-4 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "likely to warrant very low policy rates at least through mid-2013"; Yellen, "prepared to employ its policy tools". Summary: the adopted signal is a calendar-date commitment to low rates plus a readiness to use more tools. Direction: strongly easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 64 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Lockhart, "a lot more uncertainty than whatever the Committee could consider normal"; Lacker, "we find ourselves in uncharted waters." Summary: many participants stress unusual uncertainty about growth, Europe and the models, but not Knightian.
