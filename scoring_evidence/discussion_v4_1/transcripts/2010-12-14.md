# Part 2: Evidence and Scores (participants only; staff turns absent from input)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 22 +-6 High.** Rosengren: core CPI "at 0.6 percent, is the lowest since 1957"; "continued disinflation"; Moore: "measures of underlying inflation are running below 1 percent"; Evans: "underlying trend in core PCE inflation is likely closer to 1 percent"; Yellen: "pace of core inflation still seems to be moving downward"; Bernanke: "inflation remains quite well contained... risks are still to the downside"; Plosser/Lacker/Hoenig see a gradual pickup, and Plosser says no sustained-deflation worry. Well below target, deflation risk "receded somewhat" but not gone.

**OUTPUT_GAP 28 +-6 High.** Kocherlakota: Tealbook gap "minus 6 1/2 percent", his own "closer to minus 4 percent"; Rosengren: "significant slack in the economy"; Moore: "significant slack in labor and goods markets"; Bernanke: "a deep hole". Below potential, slightly offset by Kocherlakota's smaller-gap view and an improving near-term outlook.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 17 +-5 High.** Bernanke: "unemployment rate is, after all, 9.8 percent"; employment-to-population "fallen from 63 percent to 58 percent"; Pianalto: "labor markets are still struggling"; Tarullo: quits "still nearly 20 percent below previous levels". Some modest improvement noted (claims, vacancies) but level remains very slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44 +-7 Moderate.** Lacker: consumption growth "averaged more than 3 percent over the last four months"; Fisher: ex-auto retail sales "up 5.2 percent" y/y; Moore: second-half growth "at or perhaps even a bit above trend", but "I don't think we'll see a surge in spending or hiring"; Lockhart: firms in "wait and see" posture on capital spending; Lacker: residential investment "still looks bleak"; Pianalto: strength concentrated "in the high end". Improving consumption, weak housing and cautious business investment net slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY N/A.** Only passing mentions (Rosengren/Fisher: firms using productivity gains to offset costs); no participant gives a view of the productivity growth level.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 +-8 Moderate.** Moore: "significant tightening of financial conditions over the past month, particularly a jump in real longer-term interest rates"; Dudley: bankers report "credit availability continues to improve"; Raskin: financial markets "could be recovering from their nervous conditions"; Duke: "credit conditions continue to improve slowly"; Bernanke: "Financial conditions during the intermeeting period have been mixed". Backup in rates and weak small-business credit offset improving availability and higher equities. Net near neutral, slightly easier.

**TREASURY_STRESS 44 +-8 Moderate.** Warsh: "change, level, and uncertainty with respect to longer-term Treasuries"; Yellen: "extraordinary backup in nominal Treasury and TIPS yields"; Dudley: backup partly "higher risk premium"; Lacker/Bullard: yield rise attributed to outlook, fiscal package, supply, reduced QE expectations. Yield moves and uncertainty only, no liquidity or functioning problems described, so normal functioning range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 56 +-9 Low.** Duke: Survey of Terms of Business Lending shows "spreads are still unusually wide" (attributed to loan-rate floors); Raskin: "risk spreads have narrowed" since the last meeting (direction only); Bernanke: refinancing "limited by tight terms and conditions"; Dudley: credit availability improving. A stated wide level (Duke) governs the side of 50; modest score because mixed direction and availability signals. CONF Low because the level statement is one member's remark on loan pricing.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 47 +-6 Low.** Raskin: "equity prices are higher"; Bullard: "increase in U.S. equity prices... continues to this day"; Yellen: "negative co-movements between U.S. equity prices and peripheral European CDS premiums". Price moves only, no valuation view (Hoenig's valuation worry is farmland, Fisher mentions only past warning). Default band on the side of the rise.

**BANKING_STRESS 57 +-7 Moderate.** Rosengren: "percent of bank assets in 3-, 4-, and 5-rated institutions had become quite elevated"; Pianalto: "a very large number of banks still have adverse ratings"; many small banks hold "large amounts of nonperforming assets"; Yellen: "Financial institutions remain quite vulnerable to losses related to real estate"; Dudley: S&P ratings risk "we're not out of the woods yet, but things look a little bit better"; Duke: bank credit quality "stable". Emerging but contained stress, partly improving.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 53 +-6 Low.** Dudley: "the dollar has been strengthening"; Bullard: earlier "decline in the dollar" (Aug-Nov) as easing side effect; Bernanke: "the dollar has not shown any indication of sharp decline"; Yellen: no "flight from the dollar". Recent mild strengthening after earlier decline; no concern expressed.

**INFLATION_GOODS 40.** Plosser: foreign suppliers "have raised prices for many products"; Moore: rising prices of materials and imports but "economic climate does not appear to support much pass-through"; Bernanke: commodity costs "substantially offset by lower labor costs"; Evans: slack "will prevent high commodity prices from bleeding" into inflation. Input costs up, final goods prices soft.

**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Only Bernanke's generic remark that slowdown is "well spread across categories of goods and services"; no services-specific view.

**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** House prices discussed as a wealth/credit risk; no rent or shelter inflation discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47.** Pianalto: five-year swap-implied inflation "settled near 2 percent"; Moore: "longer-term inflation expectations remain relatively stable"; Bullard: TIPS-based expectations up "40 basis point"; Raskin: could become unanchored downward if decline continues; Bernanke: "inflation expectations at levels close to the Committee's objectives". Anchored near target with mild downside worry.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 27 +-7 High.** Rosengren: "difficult to imagine why we would not purchase all $600 billion"; Evans: "Inflation is too far below our mandate and resource slack remains very large"; Yellen: "bar for stopping short of $600 billion should be set pretty high"; Dudley: "A very accommodative policy stance... is still fully appropriate"; Bernanke: "not too accommodative". Hawkish minority: Hoenig "additional monetary stimulus carries more long-term risk" and prefers C/D; Lacker "I still oppose" and favours C; Plosser "more inclined to support... reducing the pace of LSAPs"; Fisher "not a fan of QE2" but would not pare back. Nobody advocates A (expansion). Near-unanimous hold of an ongoing QE program, with a vocal hawkish minority.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 40 +-8 High.** Vote read-out: ten Yes, one No (Hoenig). Lacker prefers C but "won't bother trying to persuade"; Plosser "I can accept 'no change'" while holding reservations; Fisher "may well dissent in the future"; Dudley criticizes public dissent; disagreement extends to inflation outlook ("somewhat diverse") and LSAP efficacy, with Kocherlakota/Lockhart/Evans differing on framework. One formal dissent plus several non-voting reservations, mostly polite.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 58 +-8 Moderate.** Rosengren: European risks via "U.S. banks and money market funds"; Dudley: Europe, municipal bond market, rating downgrades of major U.S. firms; Hoenig: farmland "worry of mine" and asset-value allocation effects; Yellen: housing/bank feedback loop; Bernanke: financial stability memos, "no large growing imbalances". Several members address it at length, though real-economy assessment still leads.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 28 +-8 Moderate.** Bullard: "likely to continue this pace of purchases through the second quarter of 2011"; Evans: calendar-date guidance on "extended period" as extra easing tool; Kocherlakota: favours forward guidance and an IOER cut; Bernanke: "watching and waiting"; "could change sharply... we would need to be willing to respond". Hawkish counter-signals from Plosser/Hoenig/Kocherlakota on exit planning ("begin shifting our language") but the committee path is continued purchases with regular review; easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 48 +-8 Moderate.** Bernanke: "European sovereign banking problems have created significant uncertainties"; "Difficulties in measuring slack and in beating the random walk forecast make inflation predictions difficult"; "risks in both directions on the economic growth front"; Warsh: "level, and uncertainty with respect to longer-term Treasuries"; Duke: "very difficult for me to determine what the effect of the $600 billion... has been"; Kocherlakota: output-gap measurement. Elevated but mostly standard-caveat and measurement uncertainty, with confidence the recovery is improving.
