## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Document treated as a normal (not special-topic) meeting: it contains an economic go-around, a policy go-around and a roll-call vote on the directive. The organizational items at the start and the long communications / forecast-publication session at the end carry no condition content and were ignored for the condition dimensions.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +-5 (Moderate-High)**
- Hoenig: "core inflation is too high"; "2.6 year over year is elevated". Kohn: "core PCE... 2.3 percent"; "inflation remains elevated". Bernanke: owners' equivalent rent is "essentially the entire reason that inflation is remaining above our target zone". Moskow: "uncomfortable with the current rate of inflation"; Yellen/Pianalto/Stern: core "has come down in recent months", "moderating".
- Above the comfort zone but recently easing; modestly above target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-5 (Moderate)**
- Minehan: "the output gap is virtually eliminated"; "with virtually no output gap". Kohn: "in the neighborhood of full employment"; "high level of resource utilization". Yellen: growth "slightly below trend in 2007". Moskow: "modestly below potential in the first half".
- At potential, resource use high; slightly above 50 for the high-utilization language, offset by below-trend growth expected.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-5 (High)**
- Bernanke: "Labor markets remain taut, with continuing wage pressures in some occupations." Yellen: "labor markets are already arguably tight". Minehan: unemployment "below 5 percent"; "Skilled labor continues to be in short supply and expensive". Hoenig: "labor markets remain tight for us". Bies: "slack in the skilled labor force is getting very, very limited". Poole's FedEx contact "does not see any particular labor market pressures" (minority).
- Tight, with wage pressure noted by many.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +-6 (Moderate-High)**
- Minehan: consumption "likely growing at a pace of more than 4 percent... remarkably strong". Kroszner: "very, very strong" consumer; investment "declining, or at least not growing as we would expect". Kohn: "business investment spending has been weaker than we anticipated". Bernanke: "Consumption grew strongly in the fourth quarter"; "investment seems likely to grow at a moderate pace".
- Strong consumers net of weak housing and soft business investment; modestly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +-8 (Low-Moderate)**
- Kohn: "the extent of the slowdown in productivity growth, both in terms of trend and of actual relative to the lower estimated trend"; "sluggish rise in productivity". Geithner: "fairly comfortable with the staff view of around 2 percent or 2 1/2 percent". Pianalto: "continuing strength in manufacturing productivity" (as the usual story).
- Modestly below trend per the main voices; mixed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Minehan: "tighter though still reasonably accommodative financial conditions". Plosser: "monetary conditions, which I believe have been very accommodative". Warsh: "The tone of the markets appears to be exceptionally strong"; "powerful sources of liquidity". Bernanke: long rates rose ~30 bp, but "five-year and ten-year bond rates are 35 to 40 basis points below the fed funds rate".
- Accommodative / supportive, bright line (<50) satisfied; some tightening via higher long rates.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Only yield levels and the curve shape were mentioned (Poole: ten-year 4.49 to 4.88 on policy expectations; Bernanke: curve inverted ~30 bp). No participant discussed Treasury market functioning; yield moves alone are not stress.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Warsh: "Credit spreads continued to trend tighter; high-yield spreads were down about 100 basis points from September"; "Spreads have tightened, even as yields have trended back to 5 percent". Kroszner: "spreads seem to be low". Geithner: markets assign "quite low probabilities to even modest increases in risk premiums".
- Stated low level ("seem to be low") governs; below normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-8 (Low)**
- Warsh: "Stock prices... were up 84 percent in the past four years and up about 15 percent in 2006"; Kroszner: "equity markets seem to be positive"; Minehan: "rising equity markets". Price moves and wealth/indicator remarks only; no valuation or concern view, so default 45-55 on the side of the rise.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Bies: "Asset quality in the consumer sector as a whole is very good"; "with the exception of the subprime ARM mortgages, we feel very good about overall credit quality"; "not seeing any real concentrated risk, particularly in banking". Barron: "credit quality has softened but remains at very strong levels"; banks "concern about the number of speculative condominium projects in south Florida".
- Healthy with pockets of concern (subprime, Florida condos); below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 +-8 (Low)**
- Moskow: "the strength of foreign demand and the weaker dollar seem to be showing through to increased export demand". Kohn: restraint on demand from "the dollar exchange rate" (direction not stated). Minehan: uncertainty about "the strength of the dollar". Weaker dollar is the only explicit direction; sparse and mixed.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 47**
- Kohn: "Goods prices might have been held back by efforts to run off inventories"; Bernanke: "slowing in goods prices"; Fisher: Chinese import costs turning up "approximately 1 percent" after falling; Minehan: "declining import prices".

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 55**
- Barron: "price growth in services will continue to dominate core inflation"; Bernanke: "encouraging developments on the services side"; wage pressures in skilled occupations (Fisher, Hoenig).

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 55**
- Bernanke: "lack of any substantial moderation in shelter costs"; owners' equivalent rent the reason inflation is above target; Kohn: "Increases in rents are likely to moderate"; Minehan: "falling shelter prices".

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52**
- Yellen: "longer-term inflation expectations remain stable and well anchored"; Stern, Mishkin, Warsh: "well anchored". Lacker: "center of gravity a little higher, above 2 percent", survey/TIPS "not much below 2 1/2 percent for the CPI"; Moskow: markets may read inaction as "satisfied with 2 1/4 percent".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +-5 (High)**
- Policy held at 5 1/4 percent by all; Bernanke: "take no action and... maintain a bias toward further tightening". Plosser: "I do want us to consider tightening"; Lacker: "policy is likely to require a higher funds rate path at some point this year"; Mishkin: "some upward bias to the future path of the federal funds rate"; Moskow: "we have work to do". Several urge patience (Stern, Kohn, Hoenig); none advocate easing.
- Hold with a tightening bias; lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 +-5 (High)**
- Roll call: eleven "Yes" votes on the directive, no dissents. Differences were over statement wording (alternative B vs C, sections 2-4; Poole wanting to drop the old risk language, Plosser/Minehan/Lacker/Hoenig/Stern/Poole preferring C sections 2-3), the word "rebounding", and emphasis (Mishkin more worried about output than inflation). Minor emphasis differences only.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 36 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Bies at length on subprime ARMs, delinquencies and servicing exposure; Barron on Florida bank concerns; Warsh on credit spreads and market optimism; Geithner: markets "quite low probabilities to even modest increases in risk premiums than I think is probably justified"; Bernanke on "endogeneity of financial conditions". Several participants address it, but economic assessment dominates.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-6 (Moderate-High)**
- Bernanke: "our bias remains clearly to the upside and... we must certainly leave open the possibility of future tightening later this year"; statement keeps "the extent and timing of any additional firming". Geithner: "possible, maybe probable, that we will have to tighten further". Poole: "Possibly in June"; Hoenig: "I don't have a sense that we have a great increase coming". Mild tightening bias retained, data dependent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: "a great deal of uncertainty about inflation going forward". Minehan: "hard to tell... whether the recent readings on core inflation are the result of fundamentally lower inflation pressures or just luck"; Kroszner: "I don't have a lot of confidence that I understand why they have moved as they have"; Geithner: housing "Probably, but we can't be sure yet"; Hoenig: "considerable uncertainty remains". Standard-plus caveats, several puzzles raised; not unusual or extreme.
