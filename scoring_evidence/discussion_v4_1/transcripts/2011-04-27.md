# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Transcript Discussion, Meeting 2011-04-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It has these segments, in order: members' questions on the Desk/financial developments briefing; a long exit-strategy go-round (staff memo options 1 and 2, with four "true/false/uncertain" questions); the economic go-round; the Chairman's own outlook remarks (including a Taylor-rule exercise); the Chairman's summary of the economy; the policy go-round on alternative B/C wording; and a roll-call read-out. The input is long (about 4,950 lines) and reads cleanly. A few staff answers are missing, so some member questions appear without replies. I found no garbled passages. This is a normal meeting with an unusually large exit-strategy segment. The segment bears on policy lean and uncertainty, not on the state of the economy.

**2. Era context.** The text points to spring 2011: unemployment 8.8 percent, QE2 ($600 billion) nearing completion in June, a weak Q1 (1.7 percent GDP estimate), an oil and commodity price surge after Middle East/North Africa turmoil, the Japan earthquake, euro-area sovereign stress, the debt ceiling, and fiscal drag ahead. I scored what participants said, not what I recall about the period.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Doves/accommodationists: Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen, Raskin, Tarullo, Pianalto, Duke, Dudley, Lockhart. All support alternative B (hold, finish QE2, keep "extended period"). Their view is that underlying inflation is low, slack is large, and the headline spike is transitory.
- Hawkish voices:
  - Hoenig prefers alternative C: drop "extended period", stop purchases at $450 billion, and raise the funds rate to about 1 percent by year-end.
  - Plosser prefers C's language, wants reinvestment ended and the language changed, and sees exit starting in H2 2011.
  - Lacker sees "reasonably strong arguments" for C but supports B.
  - Kocherlakota sees rules calling for a rate rise in the fall.
  - Bullard wants a "small move": end purchases at $500 billion.
  - Fisher was "tempted to embrace alternative C and to dissent", and warns of inflation and speculative excess.
- Chairman: modal path is "go on hold for a short period", then gradual exit if things go as hoped. He argues policy is easy but not inappropriate given the economy. Staff Taylor-rule work implies the first rate increase early in 2012.

**4. Directional signals for core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see pressure slightly above the midpoint. Headline inflation is clearly high (Fisher: headline PCE at a 4.9 percent annualized pace in February; Lacker: "inflation is, indeed, too high right now"). The Chairman cites core PCE of about 0.9-1.2 percent, well below target. Upside risks and pass-through anecdotes are broad (Fisher, Lacker, Lockhart, Plosser). Others see transitory effects (Evans, Williams, Yellen). Net is modestly above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. The Chairman puts the gap at about 5 percent; Kocherlakota says "around minus 4 percent"; Tarullo argues for "a greater output gap"; Rosengren sees unemployment well above full employment through 2013. Bright-line: "slack"/"below potential" means below 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well below the midpoint. Unemployment is 8.8 percent, with "substantial slack", very low quits, and wages minimal. Pockets of tightness (specialized/tech workers per Bullard; Lacker's firms short of workers) are minor. UE above 7 percent puts the score in the 16-30 band.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend to modest. Consumer spending is "moderate" but drained by gasoline prices; confidence is weak; housing and construction are weak; state and local spending is falling. Equipment and software investment, manufacturing and exports are strong. Net is somewhat below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mixed and thinly discussed. Evans notes "softer growth in productivity"; Lockhart and the Chairman say firms are near the end of productivity gains; Yellen cites "healthy productivity growth"; Fisher cites efficiency gains already realized. Near or slightly below trend, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose. Yellen: accommodative policy has "caused credit and broader financial conditions to ease"; the Chairman: "Financial conditions have improved a bit further"; Fisher and Hoenig stress excess liquidity and easy credit. Williams notes lending conditions "still appear relatively tight", a small offset. Bright-line: easy/supportive means below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The Treasury market is not discussed as dysfunctional. The debt ceiling is raised as a risk (Dudley, Kocherlakota), but no current market stress is described and the Desk Q&A on repo/collateral is operational. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Credit is described as readily available. Duke: banks easing terms, "aggressive" C&I competition. Fisher: leveraged loan issuance at about the 2007 pace and "covenants even better than 2007". Spread levels are never quoted, and Duke cautions that credit is "close to as good as it's going to get" apart from housing. Below normal, so below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equities are mentioned only as strong or rallying (Chair: "Earnings are strong, and equities are doing well"; Plosser: markets "took all that ... in stride"). No valuation concern is voiced. Default near neutral, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy and improving. Duke: credit quality metrics are normalizing and classified assets are declining; Dudley: "healthier financial system". Offsets are Bullard ("an awful lot of banks ... in pretty weak condition") and Dudley on Greek bank exposure. Bright-line: sound means below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Weakening. Dudley: "a dollar that's actually weakened quite a bit over the past few weeks"; Bullard: "The dollar is weakening pretty substantially"; the Chairman: "the dollar has also fallen". Bright-line: depreciation means below 50.
- Inflation sub-components:
  - Goods: scored. Broad commodity and goods cost pass-through is cited (Fisher on Walmart, P&G and Kimberly-Clark price rises; Yellen on firms passing through input costs).
  - Services: not discussed separately, N/A.
  - Housing/shelter: not discussed as an inflation component (only a passing Texas rents remark), N/A.
  - Expectations: scored. "Stable" per the majority, but drifting up to the top of recent ranges (Lockhart, Plosser, Dudley, Bullard).

**5. Polarity verification.** I checked the polarity-sensitive dimensions.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter. Evidence is loose and easy, so the score is low, matching the rule.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. Evidence is ample and aggressive credit, so the score is low.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. Banks are described as healthy, so the score is low.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher means appreciation. The dollar is falling, so the score is low.
- OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means more above potential/tighter. Slack is large, so both scores are low.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The action under discussion is alternative B, which finishes QE2, keeps the "extended period" language and holds the rate at zero. Broad support is for holding (Bullard: "I am going to support option B"; Chairman: "broad support for alternative B"). The hawkish minority pushes for earlier signaling or exit (Hoenig, Plosser, Bullard, Kocherlakota, Lacker, Fisher). The Chairman's modal forecast is a pause, then gradual exit. The exit-strategy go-round dwells on how to remove accommodation but explicitly "does not necessarily signal any change in our stance". Net: hold with a slight dovish tilt from finishing the purchases, tempered by an active hawkish wing. Roughly balanced, a touch below 50.
- Disagreement: The vote read-out shows 10 "Yes" votes and no formal dissent. Fisher says he is "tempted ... to dissent" and does not. Hoenig (a non-voter in the read-out) prefers C. Bullard wants a smaller purchase total. There is open disagreement on what inflation is and on core versus headline (Evans vs Lacker; Bullard vs Chairman). There is a sharp Fisher-Evans-Tarullo exchange on "monetizing the debt". The exit-strategy views diverge widely (Chairman: "quite a bit of divergence"). Disagreement extends to the economic assessment, mainly inflation risk and the persistence of the commodity shock. No formal dissents; real reservations from several participants. That puts it in the moderate range.
- Financial stability attention: Fisher and Hoenig raise leverage and speculative excess, with the Fisher leveraged-loan and Energy Future Holdings anecdotes and Hoenig's royalty-trust story. Dudley covers European sovereign/banking contagion at length. Duke covers bank credit. The Chairman's summary notes "Risks to financial stability exist". Yellen and Dudley mention the debt ceiling and fiscal sustainability. This is moderate, several participants but not the dominant topic.
- Forward guidance: "Extended period" is retained. The Chairman expects to go on hold and then begin a gradual exit, and several participants want language changes (Plosser, Hoenig, Kocherlakota). No explicit tightening commitment. This is an easing lean tempered by a pivot toward exit, still data-dependent. Below neutral.
- Uncertainty: The language is elevated. The effects of asset sales are called highly uncertain (Plosser, Williams: uncertainty "far exceed[s]" that of the funds rate); models are "not a great guide" (Bullard); Tarullo says there are many geopolitical risks; Yellen says risks "keep me awake at night"; Rosengren says "significant uncertainty about the strength of the recovery"; the Chairman cites "a lot of uncertainty". The modal outlook is still stated with reasonable conviction. Elevated but not extreme.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### Economic conditions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 +-7 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Lacker: "we'd had so many months of inflation well above what we want that it was worth acknowledging ... that inflation is, indeed, too high right now." Chairman: "inflation has risen very significantly in recent months primarily because of energy prices ... Underlying inflation measures remain low."
- Center of gravity: Headline inflation is clearly elevated with upside pass-through risk, while core/underlying inflation is well below target and the spike is widely seen as transitory.
- Direction confirmation: Inflation pressure is modestly above neutral because of the headline surge and upside risks, offset by low core readings.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 34 +-5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "output gap was around minus 4 percent at the end of 2010." Chairman: staff estimate of the output gap "5.0 percent"; "we are still in a very deep hole."
- Center of gravity: Substantial slack persists and is expected to close only slowly.
- Direction confirmation: The output gap is below potential because of the large unemployment gap and the weak recovery.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 +-5 (High confidence)**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "The unemployment rate has fallen ... to 8.8 percent"; the "quiescence in wages and salaries is consistent with substantial slack." Pianalto: "there is still a lot of slack in labor markets."
- Center of gravity: Labor markets are improving but remain very slack at 8.8 percent unemployment, with minimal wage pressure.
- Direction confirmation: The labor market is well below neutral tightness, since high unemployment, low quits and weak wage growth all indicate slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Chairman: "growth in consumer spending remains moderate, and retailers in some areas are seeing increased sales and traffic"; "Investment in equipment and software is up." Williams: "resilience of consumer spending in the face of significant increases in food and energy prices."
- Center of gravity: Moderate consumer spending and solid business equipment investment are offset by high gas prices, weak confidence, and housing, construction and state/local drag.
- Direction confirmation: Spending and investment are slightly below trend, with a weak Q1 and a moderate recovery.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 45 +-10 (Low confidence)**
- Evidence: Evans: the DSGE model is "struggling with the apparent softer growth in productivity." Yellen: "healthy productivity growth and modest wage increases."
- Center of gravity: Few participants discuss productivity and their readings differ (softer vs healthy), so it is near trend with a slight lean below.
- Direction confirmation: Productivity is slightly below to near trend, but the evidence is thin and mixed.

### Financial conditions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +-6 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "Our accommodative monetary policy has caused credit and broader financial conditions to ease." Chairman: "Financial conditions have improved a bit further. Earnings are strong, and equities are doing well."
- Center of gravity: Conditions are broadly easy, and some participants worry that excess liquidity is fostering speculation.
- Direction confirmation: Financial conditions are looser than neutral (below 50), given accommodative and improving conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Treasury market functioning is not described as strained. The debt ceiling appears only as a potential future risk.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Fisher: "the pace of institutional leveraged loan issuance in the first quarter was about the same as the pace for the entire year of 2007"; Duke: "a few bankers characterized competition for C&I loans as aggressive."
- Center of gravity: Credit is readily available and loan terms are easing, indicating below-normal credit stress. Spread levels are not quoted.
- Direction confirmation: Credit stress is below neutral because credit is plentiful and loosening, so the level reading is below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-8 (Low confidence)**
- Evidence: Plosser: "Financial markets have taken all that potentially bad news in stride ... Earnings continue to be strong." Fisher: LSAP "has lifted stock prices and increased investor returns."
- Center of gravity: Equities are noted as strong, but no one voices a valuation concern or alarm.
- Direction confirmation: Equity concern is near neutral (slightly below) because prices are rising with strong earnings and no valuation worry is expressed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 37 +-7 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Duke: "Bankers ... are increasingly less worried about credit quality ... Classified assets are declining at larger banks." Bullard: "an awful lot of banks out there that are in pretty weak condition still."
- Center of gravity: Bank credit quality is improving and the system is viewed as healthier, with some weak banks and revenue concerns remaining.
- Direction confirmation: Banking stress is below neutral because credit quality is improving and the system is described as healthier.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 +-6 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Dudley: "We have a dollar that's actually weakened quite a bit over the past few weeks." Bullard: "The dollar is weakening pretty substantially."
- Center of gravity: The dollar has been depreciating notably.
- Direction confirmation: The dollar is weaker (below 50) because participants describe notable depreciation.

### Inflation sub-components

- **INFLATION_GOODS: 68.** Fisher: Walmart-approved increases ("dairy products up 15 percent, all Proctor & Gamble products up 5 to 7 percent"). Yellen: producers "passing through a portion of their higher input costs into the prices of a broad range of goods and services." Goods prices are rising fast on commodity costs.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed as a distinct category.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** No shelter-inflation discussion.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 56.** Lockhart: "expectations remain in the territory reasonably described as 'anchored,' ... have drifted higher and are at the top of the recent historical range." Williams: "inflation expectations ... remain remarkably stable." On Bernanke's show of hands, ten participants voted to keep "stable" in the statement. They are anchored but drifting up.

### Discussion-specific

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 47 +-7 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Williams: "the current very accommodative stance in monetary policy remains entirely appropriate." Hoenig: "I prefer alternative C ... We are just inviting trouble by staying too low too long." Chairman: "My modal forecast at this point is that we will go on hold for a short period."
- Center of gravity: The bulk favors alternative B (finish QE2, hold at zero), with a vocal hawkish wing pressing for earlier exit signals, so the net lean is roughly balanced with a slight tilt toward accommodation.
- Direction confirmation: Policy lean is near neutral to slightly dovish because the action is a hold with QE2 completed, offset by an active minority for earlier tightening and detailed exit planning.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Fisher: "I was tempted to embrace alternative C and to dissent at this meeting; I will not do so." Chairman on exit: "there we got quite a bit of divergence, particularly when weighted by enthusiasm." The roll call shows ten "Yes" votes and no dissent.
- Center of gravity: There was no formal dissent. Real reservations came from Fisher, Hoenig (a non-voter), Bullard, Lacker and Plosser, plus sharp exchanges on how to define inflation and on "monetizing the debt", and wide divergence on exit sequencing.
- Direction confirmation: Disagreement is moderate: unanimous formal outcome, but visible camps on inflation risk and exit design.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Fisher: "Continued accommodation is encouraging a debasement of prudent credit practices and an enhancement of speculative impulses." Chairman: "Risks to financial stability exist, including greater leverage and risk-taking in areas such as leveraged loans and land acquisition."
- Center of gravity: Several participants (Fisher, Hoenig, Dudley, Duke, Yellen) address leverage, sovereign/banking risk and credit conditions, but it is a secondary topic next to inflation and exit strategy.
- Direction confirmation: This is a moderate-attention score. Several voices, but not the focus of the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 +-10 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Yellen: "I, therefore, support the continuation of our forward guidance that the federal funds rate is likely to remain exceptionally low for an extended period." Plosser: "I think this is a good time to begin changing the language."
- Center of gravity: "Extended period" stays in the statement and the Chairman expects a pause, but participants discuss opening exit communication and the first rate rise is seen within a year or so, so the signal is easing-leaning with data dependence.
- Direction confirmation: The forward signal is below neutral (easing lean) because the guidance stays "extended period", partly offset by preparation for exit.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate confidence)**
- Evidence: Williams: "there is still a great deal of uncertainty around these effects [of LSAPs]." Yellen: "My modal forecast is benign, but the risks surrounding it keep me awake at night."
- Center of gravity: Many participants cite uncertainty about asset-sale effects, models, oil, Europe, Japan, the debt ceiling and fiscal drag, though the modal outlook is stated confidently.
- Direction confirmation: Uncertainty is elevated, not extreme.
