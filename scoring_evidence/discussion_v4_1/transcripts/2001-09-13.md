# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting date 2001-09-13 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript of a short emergency "information" meeting held in the days after a major terrorist attack on New York. The Chairman opens with a long assessment, asks staff questions (the staff answers are removed, so several Chairman questions appear with no reply), the Vice Chairman reports on New York market and payment-system infrastructure, then a quick go-around on the economy and on whether and how much to cut the funds rate. The Chairman closes by saying the Committee will watch data tomorrow. There is no vote and no formal policy proposal. The text is about 3,500 words, so it is short, but not under 500. Because it is an emergency meeting, I use N/A for topics that are not discussed and flag an anomaly.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 2001, an economy that was already weak before the shock (manufacturing and capital spending weak, inventory liquidation), with rates already lowered during the year. I use this only as background. All scores come from what participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chairman Greenspan: pre-shock economy in a "very precarious balance"; the shock is "clearly a negative one"; capital spending weak; consumption holding up before the event; supply-chain disruption; no policy preference stated.
- Vice Chairman McDonough: New York infrastructure, exchange reopening, clearing banks (BONY), discount window; "devastating weakness" expected in his District; declines to give a size or timing view.
- Poole: be ready to move; liquidity first; a cut "not necessary or required at this point" but would not rule out 25 or 50.
- Parry: nothing positive; wants a move soon, "not a 25 basis point move".
- Moskow: 50 bp, "sooner rather than later"; consumer spending soft.
- Stern: move sooner; psychology; can reverse later.
- Hoenig: 50 bp if acting; no need to rush today or tomorrow.
- Santomero: move before the markets open, 50 bp.
- Broaddus: 50 bp fairly soon, not more than 50.
- Minehan: marginally favors 25, can accept 50; moving now and again in October.
- Guynn: measured move now, keep room to respond.
- McTeer: 50 bp at about 9:00 on Monday morning.
- Jordan: liquidity is abundant, about $100 billion added; rate action would be mainly a message to Main Street, "doubt very much that it's needed for Wall Street".
- Poole asked a question about euro prices vs currency effect (staff answer removed).

**4. Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: no participant mentions inflation or prices. N/A, a truly zero-discussion case.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output as below potential, by implication. Pre-shock the economy was in "precarious balance" with weak manufacturing and capital goods and accelerating inventory liquidation; post-shock "demand had just dried up" (Minehan). No one says "below potential" literally, so the score rests on weakness language. Below 50, around 38.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: no discussion of employment, unemployment or wages. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Weak, below trend. Capital goods orders weak and no sign of business fixed investment improvement (Hoenig); consumer confidence fell; consumption had held up before the shock but post-shock Sears sales down 50% on Tuesday, Lands End calls off 60-75% (Moskow). Net about 30.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as somewhat tight/disrupted but with ample liquidity. Greenspan: the shock "increases the real risk premium for long-term capital investment"; flight to quality; exchanges closed; payment-system problems at BONY. Offsetting: Jordan says liquidity availability is not an issue and the liquidity injection has had a calming effect. No "accommodative" or "easy" wording, so no bright-line conflict. Somewhat tight, about 58, low confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants do not specifically discuss the Treasury market (only a Chairman question about the flight to quality, whose answer is absent). No dysfunction is described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The only evidence is the Chairman's inference that "corporate yields may have eased slightly but nowhere near the extent to which government yields have", which implies widening spreads. This is direction only, with no level stated. Per the v4.1 rule: 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence. Score 54.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity markets appear only as an operational matter (reopening the exchanges) and as a pro forma Dow question whose answer is removed. No valuation view from any participant. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: The banking system is discussed only as payments/clearing: a "small problem with Chase" that worked itself out and BONY "a considerable problem... improving from a low base." This is operational stress in clearing, not capital. No "sound"/"well-capitalized" wording. Mild stress, slightly above neutral, about 55, low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no discussion of the dollar. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: nothing said about goods, services, housing or expectations. All N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: higher = stronger; evidence says weak, so below 50, matches. OUTPUT_GAP: higher = more above potential; evidence says weak, so below 50, matches. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter; evidence says risk premium up, markets disrupted, but liquidity ample, so slightly above 50, matches. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; implied widening of spreads relative to Treasuries, so slightly above 50 on direction only, matches. BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress; operational problems at one clearing bank, so slightly above 50, matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Strongly dovish. Most speakers want a cut soon, mostly 50 bp (Moskow, Hoenig, Santomero, Broaddus, McTeer; Parry wants more than 25). Poole, Minehan and Guynn lean to a smaller or more measured move; Jordan doubts a cut is needed for Wall Street; Greenspan and McDonough state no size. This is a majority for substantial easing, but not a QE-style or "whatever it takes" posture.
- Disagreement: No vote and no formal dissents. Differences are tactical: size (25 vs 50), timing (before the open, Monday 9:00, wait for data) and whether a move is needed at all today (Poole, Jordan). No disagreement about the economic assessment, which everyone sees as negative. Mild to moderate, about 28.
- Financial stability emphasis: Very high. Liquidity provision, clearing banks, discount-window use, exchange reopening, flight to quality, market-maker capacity, and the confidence message dominate. They rival the economic assessment.
- Forward guidance: Easing bias. Several speakers expect to move again at the October meeting or reverse later if conditions allow; none signals tightening. No formal guidance language, so a mild easing bias with some two-way flexibility, about 22.
- Uncertainty: Very high. "Exceptionally difficult to judge" (Greenspan), "unprecedented situation" (Moskow), "I don't know how all this is going to play out" (Stern), "events that we can't predict" (Poole), "very surreal environment" (McDonough).

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 (< 50, weakness language; no "above" wording), OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 58 (> 50; no "accommodative/easy" wording used for markets), OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 54 (direction only, within 45-55, on the side of the move, Low confidence), OK. BANKING_STRESS 55 (no "sound"/"healthy" language, so no forced < 50), OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS N/A, and no dysfunction is described.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (N/A): cannot compare.
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (32): within 15 points, coherent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (54): co-move, both slightly above neutral, coherent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A, nothing to reconcile.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-components: all N/A.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_PRESSURE and sub-components: members never mention prices or inflation. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: no labor market discussion. PRODUCTIVITY: not raised. TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market not discussed by members (only a Chairman question without a visible answer). EQUITY_VALUATION: only operational reopening of exchanges, no valuation view. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not raised.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: N/A
- Evidence: none; no participant discusses inflation or prices.
- Summary: topic not raised.
- Direction confirmation: not applicable.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 ±8 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "we were still in that very precarious balance with regard to which way the economy was going to go"; Minehan: "demand had just dried up."
- Summary: Weak manufacturing, weak capital spending and a negative shock imply output below potential, though no one uses "below potential" literally.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because participants describe weakness and a further negative shock.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A
- Evidence: none.
- Summary: labor market not discussed.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 32 ±8 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Moskow: Sears sales "down 50 percent on Tuesday" and Lands End calls "off 75 percent"; Hoenig: "no signs whatsoever of any improvement in business fixed investment."
- Summary: Weak business investment and a sudden consumer pullback outweigh earlier resilient consumption.
- Direction confirmation: spending is below trend because investment was already weak and consumer demand fell sharply after the shock.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
- Evidence: none.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 ±10 (Low confidence)
- Evidence: Greenspan: the shock "increases the real risk premium for long-term capital investment"; Jordan: "it would be hard to imagine the availability of liquidity as an issue."
- Summary: Market disruption, closed exchanges and flight to quality make conditions somewhat tighter, partly offset by abundant central bank liquidity.
- Direction confirmation: conditions are slightly above neutral (tighter) because risk premia rose and markets were disrupted, even with ample liquidity.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
- Evidence: none from participants; the Chairman's question on flight to quality is unanswered in the text.
- Summary: no market dysfunction described; not discussed.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 ±8 (Low confidence)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "corporate yields may have eased slightly but nowhere near the extent to which government yields have."
- Summary: Implies some spread widening (direction only; no level given), so a score just above neutral under the direction-only rule.
- Direction confirmation: slightly above neutral on the side of the widening move; the level is not stated.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A
- Evidence: none; equities arise only as the operational question of reopening the exchanges.

### BANKING_STRESS: 55 ±10 (Low confidence)
- Evidence: McDonough: "The Bank of New York has been a considerable problem," with the situation "improving from a low base"; Chase "quite certain it can function well."
- Summary: Operational stress in clearing at one bank, with the other major clearing bank functioning; no capital concerns raised.
- Direction confirmation: slightly above neutral because of the clearing problem, but this is operational rather than solvency stress.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
- Evidence: none.

### INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING / EXPECTATIONS: N/A
- Evidence: none.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 24 ±6 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Moskow: "my instincts would be to move sooner rather than later. I also think that 50 basis points is the appropriate magnitude"; Santomero: "I think we should move... before the markets open."
- Summary: A clear majority wants a substantial funds rate cut, mostly 50 bp, with a few preferring 25 or a measured move, and one voice saying a cut is not needed for Wall Street.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is dovish (below 50) because most speakers advocate immediate or near-term easing.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 ±8 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Minehan: "I would be marginally in favor of the smaller 25 basis point move rather than 50"; Parry: "I would not favor a 25 basis point move"; Poole: such an adjustment "is not necessary or required at this point."
- Summary: No vote and no formal dissent; differences over size and timing but common agreement that the shock is negative and that readiness to ease is needed.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is limited and tactical, so low-to-moderate.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 80 ±8 (High confidence)
- Evidence: McDonough on exchange reopening, clearing banks and the discount window; Jordan: "we've added about $100 billion worth of liquidity."
- Summary: Market functioning, liquidity and confidence take up roughly half the meeting and rival the economic assessment.
- Direction confirmation: attention to financial stability is very high.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 ±8 (Moderate confidence)
- Evidence: Minehan: "moving at this time and then having the October meeting available to move again"; Stern: "we can always reverse it."
- Summary: Participants signal willingness to ease now and possibly again in October while retaining the flexibility to reverse, an easing bias without formal guidance.
- Direction confirmation: the forward signal is below neutral (easing direction).

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 84 ±6 (High confidence)
- Evidence: Greenspan: "exceptionally difficult to judge"; Moskow: "obviously this is an unprecedented situation."
- Summary: Nearly every speaker stresses unprecedented circumstances and an unpredictable outlook.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is very high, in the 76-90 band.
