# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2018-01-31 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, two sessions. It opens with organizational items, a ceremonial farewell to the outgoing Chair, member questions to staff, a go-round on inflation dynamics (Phillips curve, expectations), a go-round on financial stability, the economic go-round, and the policy go-round with a short debate on statement wording ("further" and the inflation-compensation sentence). Staff turns are removed. It ends with a roll-call read-out in which all nine voters vote yes.

**2. Era context.** The text puts this at the start of 2018. Unemployment is 4.1 percent, the funds rate is at 1.25-1.50, the tax act has just passed, global growth is synchronized, the dollar has fallen and equities are high. Core PCE inflation has been about 1.5 percent. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Almost everyone reports very strong momentum, optimism among contacts, and above-trend growth (Mester, Williams, Brainard, Harker, Evans, Kaplan, Quarles, Powell, Dudley).
- Almost everyone also says the labor market is tight and tightening, with unemployment heading below 4 percent. Several note wage growth is still modest (Brainard, Bostic, Kashkari).
- On inflation, nearly everyone says it is still below 2 percent but should rise. Evans, Kashkari, Brainard, Harker and Bostic stress low inflation and low expectations. Rosengren, Kaplan, Dudley, Williams, Mester and Powell stress upside and overheating risks.
- A large share of the financial stability round (Rosengren, Kaplan, Brainard, Quarles, George, Dudley) is about stretched valuations, narrow spreads, low volatility and leverage.
- Policy: Alternative B is adopted with "further" in paragraphs 2 and 4, in effect a hold with a hawkish signal. A March hike is widely expected, and Dudley, Kaplan and Powell float 3-4 hikes in 2018. Evans wants a pause until June. Kashkari prefers alternative A. Bullard, Bostic and Evans dislike "further". Harker and Brainard have reservations about the pace.

**4. Directions in words, before numbers.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as somewhat below target but firming (Bullard: core 1.5, headline 1.7; Brainard: 1.5 percent in 2017). Below 50 but not deep.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy above potential (Williams: "further above potential by a significant degree"; Evans: growth "1 percentage point above potential"; many say "above-trend growth"). Above 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment is 4.1 percent and heading under 4. Participants describe it as already tight and tightening, with the unemployment rate below most natural-rate estimates (Mester, Powell). Above 55. Wage growth is only modest, which tempers the score a little.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as strong (Williams: "especially robust"; Brainard: private final purchases at 4.6 percent; Quarles: investment at the fastest pace since the crisis). Above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Mentioned as low over the expansion (Mester, Quarles) but possibly turning up with investment (Quarles, Kaplan). Slightly below trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants call them accommodative or supportive (Mester: "accommodative"; Powell: "highly supportive"; Brainard: "exceptionally supportive"; Dudley: easing even as policy tightens). Below 50 by the bright-line rule.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Participants say spreads are narrow (Brainard: "risk premiums and spreads remain narrow by historical standards"; Powell: "narrow risk spreads"). Below 50 by the bright-line rule.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: On the rubric's table, stretched valuations sit in 16-30. Brainard: price-to-earnings "highest levels outside the dot-com era"; George: "asset valuations look quite stretched"; Williams: "reminiscent of the late '90s".
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants say the banking system is strong (Quarles: "very strong by historical standards"). Below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar depreciated about 4 percent in the intermeeting period (Rosengren, Brainard, Quarles). Below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yields and the curve are discussed, but only as rate moves and term premiums, with no mention of functioning problems. Normal.

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter, my evidence says looser, so the score is low. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress, evidence says compressed, so low. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: evidence is depreciation, so low. BANKING_STRESS: evidence is "strong", so low. EQUITY_VALUATION follows the rubric table, where stretched means low. OUTPUT_GAP: evidence is above potential, so high.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: hold now, with an explicit tightening signal ("further" added) and a March hike strongly expected. Several want faster hikes (Rosengren, Kaplan, Dudley, Mester, Powell). Evans, Kashkari, Harker and Bostic want to be more patient. That is a moderately hawkish lean.
- Disagreement: no dissents, and all nine voters say yes. There are real reservations, but they are mostly about wording and pace: Bullard, Evans and Bostic on "further"; Kashkari prefers alternative A; Brainard on the inflation-compensation sentence; Harker on the forward guidance. Disagreement about the Phillips curve is sharp but intellectual. That puts it in the 30s.
- Financial stability attention: a dedicated round with six or more speakers, plus Powell, Kaplan and Williams raising it in the economic and policy rounds. Prominent.
- Forward guidance: "further gradual increases", an explicit wish to drop the "below longer-run levels" phrase, and Yellen's statement that the minutes will signal greater concern with upside risks. A clear tightening signal that stops short of aggressive.
- Uncertainty: heavy talk of humility on inflation dynamics and r* ("murky", "wet cement" on tax effects). Most participants are fairly confident about 2018 growth. Moderate-low overall.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 with "above potential": satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 with "accommodative/supportive": satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 with "narrow": satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 with "strong": satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 with depreciation: satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS in 36-50 with no dysfunction: satisfied.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63) are within 15 points.
- OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (62) are within 15 points.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) and CREDIT_SPREADS (25) are both low and co-move.
- TREASURY_STRESS (40) is normal, consistent with easy financial conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (42) is consistent with sub-components of roughly 45-58: housing is a little firm, services and expectations are below or near target.

**9. N/A justification.** None of the four financial market dimensions is N/A. Treasury is scored low-confidence because it is only discussed as yield and term-premium moves. All four inflation sub-components are scored because members touched on each. Productivity is scored because Mester, Quarles and Kaplan address it.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bullard, "core PCE inflation remains at 1.5 percent year over year, and headline is 1.7"; Powell, "inflation is still falling short of our 2 percent inflation objective". Center of gravity: below target but edging up, with upside risks flagged by a minority. Inflation is below neutral because members describe a persistent shortfall.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "push the economy further above potential by a significant degree"; Evans, "growth in 2018 coming in 1 percentage point above potential". Center of gravity: clear overshoot, with some dissent from Kashkari and Bostic on slack. The output gap is above neutral because members describe above-potential and above-trend growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Mester, "the unemployment rate remains at its lowest level since December 2000"; Powell, "our already-tight labor market". Center of gravity: unemployment at 4.1 percent and heading under 4 percent, with modest wage growth. The labor market is tighter than neutral because the unemployment rate is below the natural rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "Consumer and business spending have been especially robust"; Quarles, "Investment in capital equipment increased last year at the fastest pace since the post-crisis recovery". Center of gravity: strong and above trend, with Bostic cautioning that the tax effect is unclear. Spending is above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Mester, "We have seen a low level of productivity growth over this expansion"; Quarles, "the slowdown in productivity growth ... has eluded a compelling explanation". Center of gravity: low productivity now, with hopes for improvement. Productivity is slightly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "financial conditions have remained highly supportive ... narrow risk spreads"; Brainard, "financial conditions are exceptionally supportive". Center of gravity: loose and easing despite hikes (Dudley, Yellen). Conditions are looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Quarles, "Treasury yields have climbed"; Bullard, "Bonds are selling off as we speak". Center of gravity: rates and term premium moves, orderly, with no dysfunction mentioned. Normal functioning, so slightly below neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Brainard, "risk premiums and spreads remain narrow by historical standards"; Powell, "narrow risk spreads". Center of gravity: compressed spreads, with George and Kaplan noting weak covenants. Spreads are below normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 22 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Brainard, "price-to-earning ratios have reached their highest levels outside the dot-com era"; George, "asset valuations look quite stretched". Center of gravity: widespread view of elevated valuations. On this dimension's table, that sits in the elevated-valuation band.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Quarles, "a financial system that is very strong by historical standards"; Bullard, "District bankers report improving conditions". Center of gravity: healthy banks, with George warning about leverage at the largest banks. Stress is below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 24 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Brainard, "the dollar has depreciated about 4 percent across a broad basket"; Rosengren, "The exchange rate has depreciated 4 percent". Center of gravity: notable weakness. The dollar is weaker, so below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52.** Evidence: Dudley, "tariffs ... will tend to push up goods prices"; Evans, Ford saw no meaningful retail price effect and used-car prices were falling. Mixed, near stable.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 45.** Evidence: George, healthcare services inflation of 2.5 percent annualized; Mester, cell phone plan prices dropped sharply. Mixed, slightly soft.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Evidence: George, "I also expect inflation for housing services to continue putting upward pressure"; Bostic, "other than in shelter, price pressures ... are still a bit soft". Firm relative to the rest.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Evidence: Evans, "inflation expectations remain uncomfortably low"; Mester, "Longer-run inflation expectations remain well anchored". Center of gravity: anchored, at or slightly below target, with breakevens rising.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 64 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kaplan, "Three quarter-point increases over the next 12 months is my base case"; Evans, "I'm in favor of holding the funds rate at its current target range today". Center of gravity: the current action is a hold, but most favor continued gradual tightening beginning in March, with some wanting faster hikes and a few wanting patience. This is moderate hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-6 (High).** Evidence: the roll call shows nine yes votes; Kashkari, "I support alternative A at this meeting"; Bullard, "I would oppose adding the word 'further'". Center of gravity: no formal dissent, but several reservations on pace and wording and a sharp theoretical debate on the Phillips curve. Zero dissents with several reservations places this in the moderate-differences band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kaplan, "2018 and 2019 are going to be years when we need to be on our toes"; Rosengren, "at this point we have very little capacity to offset a significant financial instability shock". Center of gravity: a dedicated round with six or more speakers plus recurrent mentions in later rounds. It is a prominent topic but not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "I actually favor the insertion of the word 'further' ... because I think it reinforces the message that more monetary policy tightening lies ahead"; Yellen, "there's going to be a pretty strong inclination in the Committee to increase the funds rate in March". Center of gravity: a clear but measured tightening signal, with the "below longer-run" phrase marked for removal later.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 36 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell, "I see the natural rate as subject to great uncertainty"; Bostic, "We're still working with wet cement". Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty on inflation dynamics, r* and tax effects, but members are confident on 2018 growth.
