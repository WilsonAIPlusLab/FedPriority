# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 2020-01-29 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript (staff turns removed). It covers: organizational items; a framework-review go-round (financial stability and inflation target ranges); a Desk/balance-sheet Q&A; members' questions and a go-round on financial stability; the economic go-round; a short Chair summary; the policy go-round (alternative B, hold at 1.5-1.75, plus a 5 bp technical adjustment to IOER/ON RRP); the vote read-out (10 Yes, no No votes).

**Era context.** Written from the text alone: unemployment at a 50-year low (3.5%), core PCE about 1.6% and running below the 2% target for years, three rate cuts in 2019 (75 bp), a China phase-one trade deal, an emerging coronavirus outbreak, a low-r* world, and asset valuations described as high. None of this is used to override the text.

**Speakers.** Powell (Chair), Williams, Clarida, Brainard, Bowman, Quarles (Board/NY), and Presidents Rosengren, Barkin, Mester, Bostic, Bullard, Kaplan, Harker, Evans, George, Daly, Kashkari. Nearly all speak in each round.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (target). Evidence: core PCE 1.6% (Kashkari, Brainard), "somewhat below our objective" (Clarida), "little evidence of cost-push", margins compressing (George, Kaplan), contacts report limited pass-through. Counterpoint: Bostic reads alternative measures as already at target, and Barkin and Bowman expect a pickup. Modestly below target, so about 40.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Mixed, but growth is described as "above trend" (Barkin), "above most estimates of trend" (Clarida), "growing faster than potential" and "beyond full employment" (Rosengren), "growth running above its potential rate" (Brainard), "a touch above its trend rate" (Daly). Kashkari disputes any positive gap, and Mester sees trend growth. The bright-line rule (above trend or potential means above 50) applies, with a small net margin: 53.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment at 3.5% is a 50-year low, and "very tight labor markets" (Rosengren) and "contacts view the labor market as tight" (Mester, Bostic, Barkin). Daly, Kashkari and Williams argue that tepid wage growth means the market is less tight than the headline suggests and that maximum employment has not been reached. The level rule (UE below 4% implies above 55) governs. Score 64 with wide bands because of the disagreement over how tight the market is.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: A strong consumer and housing rebound against weak business investment, manufacturing and exports. Q4 consumption stepped down. Net is at trend, about 50.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing mentions (George, "persistent weakness in productivity growth"; Clarida, a pickup over three years; Barkin, trend lower than in 1999). Mixed, Low confidence, about 47.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants call conditions accommodative or loose, so below 50 by the bright-line rule: "financial conditions that are notably accommodative" and "in the very low end of its historical range" (Brainard), "highly accommodative" (Rosengren), "looser financial conditions" (Kaplan). Score 25.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only the September repo spike and Desk calming of it, with comments that functioning is now smooth ("Money markets are operating very smoothly", Williams). Bullard notes low yields. Yield moves are not dysfunction. Score about 36, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level language is low: "Corporate bond spreads have continued to narrow and are in the lower part of their historical range ... post-crisis lows" (Brainard), "credit spreads ... have narrowed" (Rosengren), "tightened credit spreads" (Kaplan). Below 50, score 25.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants express valuation concern: "an all-time high in 50 years" (Kaplan), "sky-high asset prices" (Williams), "elevated price-to-forward-earnings" (Brainard), "does give me pause" (Mester). Bullard argues for context. Per the scale (elevated or stretched, 16-30), score 26.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks are described as well capitalized with "historically-high levels of capital" (Quarles) and low financial-sector leverage. There is concern about declining buffers and high payouts (Rosengren, Mester, George, Brainard, Williams). Healthy with minor concerns, so below 50, score 34.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed by members. N/A.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Unanimous hold ("alternative B as written"). Dovish elements: willingness to overshoot 2%, Kashkari preferring alternative A, Bullard saying further easing may be needed, Williams and Daly stressing downside risks and the inflation shortfall. Hawkish elements: Rosengren ("more insurance than we need"), Barkin (wants a live alternative C), Kaplan ("how accommodative, and for how long"), and the 5 bp technical increase, which is explicitly not a stance change. Lean dovish-to-balanced, about 45.
- Dissent: Zero dissents and a unanimous vote. Substantive disagreement existed on the framework topics (target ranges, the role of financial stability), and one member (Kashkari) preferred a different alternative and a different guidance. Broad agreement on the current stance: about 22.
- Financial stability attention: Very high. A full framework round on financial stability and a full financial-stability go-round (banks, leverage, valuations, CCyB), plus recurring references in the economic and policy rounds. Dominant alongside the economy: about 80.
- Forward guidance: On hold, data dependent ("material change in circumstances"), accommodation to persist, overshoot tolerated, Kashkari wanting a no-hike-until-sustained-2% commitment, Bullard flagging an easing contingency. Lean toward easing or lower-for-longer with no tightening signal: about 38.
- Uncertainty: The overall tone is "good place" with standard caveats; trade, coronavirus, geopolitics and u*/r* are flagged but not described as unusual ("fear fatigue", "risks more in balance"). Normal to somewhat elevated: about 35.

**Polarity verification.** FC: members say conditions are accommodative, higher means tighter, so score below 50, which matches. Credit spreads: narrow means low stress, so below 50, which matches. Equity valuation: the scale treats stretched valuations as the low end (16-30) and I follow that convention, 26. Banking stress: healthy means below 50, which matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (>50, "above trend"); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 25 (<50); CREDIT_SPREADS 25 (<50, level stated as low); BANKING_STRESS 34 (<50); DOLLAR N/A; TREASURY 36 (no dysfunction, within 36-50 band edge; set to 38 to sit inside the normal range).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 64: gap of 11, within 15; members call labor very tight but are unsure the economy runs above potential. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 51: consistent. FC 25 vs spreads 25: co-move. FC vs Treasury stress: normal times, Treasury about 38. INFLATION_PRESSURE 40 vs sub-components (goods 42, services 52, expectations 38): consistent with a modestly below-target reading.

**N/A justification.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no member discussed the exchange rate. INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussed shelter or rent inflation (housing comments were about activity and valuations).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±5 [High].** Evidence: "PCE inflation is running somewhat below our objective" (Clarida); "core PCE inflation still running at 1.6 percent" (Kashkari); "I anticipate that consumer price inflation will remain largely muted" (George). Summary: below target, with a gradual return expected. Direction: below neutral because inflation is below the 2% target and wage and cost pass-through is limited.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: "real GDP growth at a pace of 2.3 percent is above most estimates of trend" (Clarida); "I don't see any evidence that the economy is operating above potential" (Kashkari). Summary: growth modestly above trend per most, contested by a minority. Direction: slightly above neutral, per the bright-line rule on "above trend" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 64 ±6 [High].** Evidence: "very tight labor markets" (Rosengren); "District contacts view the labor market as tight" (Mester); "the labor market isn't as tight as we think" (Daly). Summary: 3.5% unemployment is a 50-year low, with a debate about whether wages show true tightness. Direction: above neutral on the level of unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 51 ±5 [High].** Evidence: "strong consumer spending and weak investment" (Mester); "domestic demand looks pretty weak" (Kashkari). Summary: strong household spending and housing offset weak business investment and manufacturing. Direction: at neutral because the components net out.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 47 ±10 [Low].** Evidence: "persistent weakness in productivity growth" (George); "the pickup in productivity that we've seen in the past three years" (Clarida). Summary: brief and conflicting remarks. Direction: about at trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 25 ±5 [High].** Evidence: "financial conditions have eased further and are now notably accommodative by historical standards" (Brainard); "highly accommodative monetary policy" (Rosengren). Summary: broad agreement that conditions are loose. Direction: below neutral (looser than normal).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Low].** Evidence: "Desk operations since September have been effective in calming repo market turbulence" (Quarles); "Money markets are operating very smoothly" (Williams). Summary: past repo strains resolved; low Treasury yields reflect growth concerns, not dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 25 ±6 [High].** Evidence: "Corporate bond spreads have continued to narrow and are in the lower part of their historical range" (Brainard); "Credit spreads ... have narrowed" (Rosengren). Summary: low spread levels and strong credit availability. Direction: below neutral because the level is low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 26 ±6 [High].** Evidence: "we are at an all-time high in 50 years" (Kaplan); "sky-high asset prices" (Williams). Summary: several members flag stretched valuations, with Bullard offering a more benign reading. Direction: valuations elevated (low end of this scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 34 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: "Banks have historically-high levels of capital" (Quarles); "large bank capital positions are eroding amid high payouts" (George). Summary: sound system with a worry about shrinking buffers. Direction: below neutral because banks are well capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:** N/A. Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42.** "ongoing deflation in nonpetroleum import prices" (George); "softness in import prices" (Bostic). **INFLATION_SERVICES: 52.** "modest increases in health-care prices" (Evans); health insurance premium anecdote (Harker). **INFLATION_HOUSING:** N/A. **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38.** "inflation expectations are at the low end of the range that I consider consistent with our objective" (Clarida); "Survey measures of inflation expectations remain near record lows" (Kashkari); Bostic and Mester see them stable and anchored.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 45 ±5 [High].** Evidence: "I support alternative B as written" (nearly all); "willing to tolerate an overshoot for a time" (Kaplan); "I prefer alternative A ... but will support alternative B" (Kashkari). Summary: unanimous hold with an accommodative stance and tolerance for overshooting, with a few hawkish cautions. Direction: lean dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±6 [High].** Evidence: roll call, 10 Yes; "I prefer alternative A in this meeting but will support alternative B" (Kashkari). Summary: unanimous on policy; framework debates (ranges, financial stability) show differences of emphasis but not on the decision.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 80 ±6 [High].** Evidence: "Valuations have increased notably ... elevated leverage among nonfinancial corporates and high payout rates at the largest banks" (Brainard); "when unemployment is low, asset prices are high ... it is exactly the time when we should be most concerned" (Rosengren). Summary: two full rounds plus recurring references, rivaling the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 ±6 [Moderate].** Evidence: "rates are on hold until and unless PCE inflation not only reaches 2 percent, but for some time also modestly exceeds" (Clarida); "it will take a material change in circumstances to generate a policy response" (Harker). Summary: patient, accommodative, no tightening signal, some members open to further easing. Direction: easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 ±8 [Moderate].** Evidence: "Several contacts referred to this as 'fear fatigue'" (Mester); "there is considerable uncertainty associated with estimates of [the natural] rate" (Mester); the coronavirus described as "a big wildcard" (Kashkari). Summary: ordinary caveats plus identified risks, with a generally confident baseline.
