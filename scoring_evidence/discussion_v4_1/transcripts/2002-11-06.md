# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2002-11-06

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). It holds the staff Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around (Parry through McDonough, then the Governors), the Chair's policy recommendation and the policy go-around, then a second agenda item on alternatives to Treasury securities (Ginnie Maes, an auction credit facility, a contingency ACF), and a unanimous roll-call read-out. The second item is not an economic assessment and is not scored; it is noted in the anomaly flag. Staff answers are removed, so some member remarks (e.g. "Four point zero percent?") are fragments.

**2. Era context.** The text describes a sluggish recovery with a weak labor market, weak business investment, tapering consumption, strong housing, very low inflation with talk of a "latent deflationary" economy, geopolitical (Iraq) uncertainty, and a 50bp funds-rate cut proposal with a move to a balanced risk statement. Scored from the text only.

**3. Speakers.** The Chair (Greenspan) proposes 50bp and a balanced statement. McDonough strongly backs "insurance." Dovish voices in the economic round: Parry, Minehan, Santomero, McTeer, Kohn, Bies, Ferguson, Gramlich, Bernanke, McDonough. Patience voices: Broaddus ("lone voice in the wilderness"), Guynn, Hoenig (cautionary), Stern (mild). Poole queries whether policy is truly expansionary. Kohn and Gramlich (citing a Goldman index) stress that financial conditions have tightened. Jordan and Moskow report firmer anecdotes but still back a cut.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Parry: core PCE 1.5 then 1.25 percent, "little inflation threat". Kohn: inflation "already as low as I would like to see it go". McTeer: prices "flat to down slightly". Greenspan: "latent deflationary type of economy". Not an acute deflation spiral, so the 26-40 band, toward the low end: about 27.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential and widening. Broaddus: gap "growing rather than shrinking". Bernanke: gap "widening rather than diminishing", capacity utilization near record lows. Parry: "considerable excess capacity" at end of 2003. Bright line (<50) satisfied. About 29.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Slack. Minimal job growth (Moskow, Santomero 36,000 a month), McTeer "negative job growth", Minehan "another year of rising unemployment". Texas UE 6.1 percent, Third District 5.3 percent. Score about 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend on net. Investment weak (Gramlich, Ferguson, Kohn); consumption "tapering off" (Kohn, Gramlich, Olson); housing strong and a bright spot everywhere. Net about 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend and stressed repeatedly. Kohn "astonishing productivity numbers", Moskow "productivity looks very good", Stern "strong and favorable trend", Broaddus "continuing strong growth of productivity". About 68.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed but leaning tighter than the policy stance suggests. Kohn: "in several key dimensions, financial conditions are tighter and markets less receptive"; BBB rate up 20bp, stocks off 15 percent. Gramlich: Goldman index says conditions tighter than at start of easing. Ferguson: cost of capital for many corporations higher. Offsetting: household borrowing costs at record lows (Bies), Broaddus calls policy "still quite stimulative", Santomero "accommodative monetary policy", Kohn notes recent equity rise and falling risk premiums. Policy accommodative but conditions described as tighter on the business side. About 55, wide band.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield levels and later a Treasury-supply study; no market dysfunction described. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan: "Credit spreads suggest that financing is harder to come by for more industries"; Kohn: defaults/downgrades mounting, BBB rate up while Treasury rates fell (implies wider spreads), though "decline in some risk premiums over the intermeeting period"; Jordan: "risk premiums in markets" elevated; Olson: auto finance risk premiums increased. No explicit "wide/elevated" label but the level story is somewhat stressed. Level reading: somewhat wide, about 59, moderate-low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stocks down about 15 percent since 9/11 and "depressed equity markets" (Jordan), with a recovery in October; Greenspan on conservative accounting lowering earnings estimates; Gramlich: forward earnings-price ratios high relative to real rates. Valuations depressed but orderly, about 58.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy. Moskow "banking system is in better shape than after the last recession"; Guynn "credit quality still appears to be good... past the low point"; Olson "no significant deterioration in asset quality"; McDonough "very stable delinquency rates" though lending standards tightening. Below 50: about 33.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Barely discussed. Kohn "broad exchange rate index is about unchanged"; Gramlich "even the mighty dollar could finally fall". Roughly stable, high level. About 51, low confidence. Bright line: no appreciation or depreciation stated, so near 50 is acceptable.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says little pressure, so score is low. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: evidence slack, score low. BANKING_STRESS: evidence healthy, so low stress, score below 50. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress; evidence of somewhat elevated risk premiums, so above 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: majority of the economic round favors easing, Chair recommends 50bp, all 12 voters approve a 50bp cut. Strong dovish with a balanced risk statement tempering it: about 23.
- Dissent: no dissents and no dissenting statement. Differences: a number of members (Broaddus, Poole, McTeer, Gramlich, Guynn, Santomero, Minehan) prefer to keep the weakness tilt or were uncomfortable with pairing 50bp with a balanced statement; Broaddus, Guynn and Hoenig urged patience or caution before the policy round; Minehan "a little at odds with the logic" but goes along. Tactical rather than economic disagreement. About 22.
- Financial stability emphasis: Several participants (Kohn, Gramlich, Minehan, Ferguson, Jordan) discuss financial conditions, credit spreads, risk premiums; Hoenig and Santomero discuss household debt/housing imbalances. Moderate, not dominant: about 38.
- Forward guidance: Statement moves from weakness tilt to balanced risks; members argue it does not tie hands (McDonough, Moskow), Moskow reminds the Committee to be ready to raise rates; Poole warns market will read balanced as "finished reducing". Near neutral, slightly easing-leaning: about 45.
- Uncertainty: McDonough "uncertain, to put it mildly"; Stern "very uncertain"; Hoenig "high degree of uncertainty"; Gramlich "policy under uncertainty"; Greenspan "go/no-go", Iraq war and geopolitical uncertainty repeatedly cited as the main drag. Many emphasize it, but forecast broad shape is accepted. About 66.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 29 < 50 (below potential): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 55 > 50: the text says conditions are tighter in several dimensions (Kohn, Gramlich); policy is described as accommodative but that is policy, not conditions. CREDIT_SPREADS 59 > 50: elevated risk premiums, with the stated levels not explicit; kept modest. BANKING_STRESS 33 < 50: ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH ~50: no directional statement, ok. TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 29, LABOR 34, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38: all below 50 and within 15 of each other. FC 55 and CREDIT_SPREADS 59 co-move. Treasury stress N/A in normal times. INFLATION_PRESSURE 27 versus sub-components: goods about 30, expectations about 40; services and housing not discussed; parent consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no discussion of Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_SERVICES: not discussed (only health insurance costs). INFLATION_HOUSING: house-price strength noted but no shelter-inflation discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 27 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "inflation is already as low as I would like to see it go"; Parry, core PCE "1½ percent in 2003 and 1¼ percent in 2004". Center of gravity: low and falling inflation with latent deflation worries. Inflation pressure is below neutral because members describe disinflation, not pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP 29 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "the gap between output and potential output is, if anything, widening rather than diminishing"; Broaddus, output gap "growing rather than shrinking". Center: sizable and widening slack. Below neutral because of explicit below-potential language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 34 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "Labor markets remain slack"; McTeer, "negative job growth so far during this past year". Center: weak hiring, flat unemployment around 5.5-6 percent. Below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "weakness in investment persists, and now consumption growth is tapering off"; Gramlich, "until investment comes back, there seems to be nothing to drive the economy". Center: weak investment and slowing consumption partly offset by strong housing. Below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 68 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "astonishing productivity numbers"; Stern, "productivity is on a strong and favorable trend". Center: broad agreement that productivity growth is strong. Above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "in several key dimensions, financial conditions are tighter and markets less receptive than they were before"; Broaddus, policy "still quite stimulative". Center: business financing conditions tighter, household conditions easy, policy accommodative. Slightly above neutral because the tightening evidence is specific and the easy evidence is mostly about policy.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not discussed as market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 59 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "Credit spreads suggest that financing is harder to come by for more industries"; Kohn, BBB rate "actually risen 20 basis points" while the ten-year fell 80bp. Center: spreads somewhat wide with credit quality worries. Above neutral by level reading (elevated risk premiums, rising defaults), but no explicit level word, hence low confidence.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 58 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "Stock prices are off 15 percent"; Jordan, "depressed equity markets". Center: depressed but recovering and orderly. Above neutral on the stress scale, no bubble talk.

**BANKING_STRESS 33 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "banking system is in better shape than it was after the last recession"; Guynn, "banks... think they're past the low point in the current credit quality cycle". Center: healthy system with some tighter lending standards. Below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 51 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Kohn, "broad exchange rate index is about unchanged"; Gramlich, "even the mighty dollar could finally fall". Center: stable, high level. At neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS 30.** Greenspan on "sharp price declines" in motor vehicles; McTeer, prices "flat to down slightly"; Moskow, retailers will sell at lower prices; Jordan, one-dollar stores. Below neutral.
**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** Not discussed as shelter inflation.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 40.** Stern, "inflationary expectations are low and likely to stay low"; Kohn, "low inflation and inflation expectations". Anchored slightly low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 23 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "my suggestion would be to lower the funds rate 50 basis points"; Bernanke, "A significant rate cut at this point... would help". Center: strongly dovish, a large cut with unanimous backing. Dovish because the whole Committee votes to ease; moderated by balanced-risk wording.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 22 +-6 (High).** Evidence: all twelve vote Yes; Broaddus, "if I had my druthers, I personally would continue to be patient a little while longer"; Gramlich, "modest preference to retain the asymmetry". Center: unanimous vote, disagreement over wording and size caveats only. Broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn and Gramlich on tighter financial conditions; Hoenig, consumers "taking on added debt... increasing their fragility". Center: several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 45 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "going to balanced risks seems to average out close to the Committee's consensus"; Moskow, "we have to be willing to raise rates at the appropriate time". Center: balanced statement, no extended easing commitment, slightly easing-leaning after the cut. Slightly below neutral because several members still wanted the weakness tilt and the Chair flagged cutting as reversible.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 66 +-7 (High).** Evidence: McDonough, "the economic outlook remains uncertain, to put it mildly"; Stern, "I'm very uncertain about whether growth in real GDP will pick up". Center: many emphasize geopolitical and outlook uncertainty and the possibility of a deflationary hole. High.
