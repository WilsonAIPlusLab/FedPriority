# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion transcript, 2010-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Participant-only transcript of a one-day FOMC meeting. It has a long opening block of questions on exit and balance-sheet issues (reserve collateral accounts, Treasury redemptions, MBS sales), then the economic go-around, then the policy go-around on statement alternatives A, B, C and C-prime, then a vote read-out. Staff turns are removed. I scored only what participants say about conditions and policy.

**Era context.** The text describes a slow recovery from a deep recession. The funds rate is near zero, the "extended period" language is in the statement, the liquidity facilities are being closed, and the LSAP program is ending. I let the text set every score.

**Speakers.**
- Dovish or accommodative-hold: Yellen, Dudley, Kohn, Rosengren, Evans, Pianalto, Tarullo, Duke, Lockhart, Bernanke (chooses B), and Warsh (supports B).
- Hawkish tilt:
  - Plosser wants to drop "extended period".
  - Hoenig wants to raise rates late this summer and casts the lone "No" vote.
  - Lacker supports B plus the exit-strategy paragraph from C-prime.
  - Kocherlakota supports B plus the C-prime exit paragraph, with an explicit sales schedule.
  - Fisher supports C-prime or a blend, and worries about the balance sheet.
  - Bullard wants more state-contingent wording.

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Core inflation is about 1 percent. Rosengren says "the trend of lower core prices continues". Pianalto says "more than 40 percent of the consumer's market basket has been exhibiting outright price decreases". Kohn expects "very slow inflation below Committee members' objectives". Some medium-term upside risk is mentioned (Plosser, Kocherlakota, Bernanke), but the current reading is low. Score about 26.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Kohn: "a considerable output gap". Yellen: "extended period of substantial underutilization of resources" and "far below maximum sustainable employment". Evans: "sizable resource gap". Tarullo: "lots of slack". Score about 28.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well below the midpoint. Unemployment is 9.7 percent (Kocherlakota), with long-term unemployment and "very weak" labor conditions (Bernanke). Hiring is reluctant everywhere. Score about 20.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Modestly below trend on net. Retail sales and equipment and software spending are encouraging, but housing has stalled, the saving rate was revised down, income growth is absent, and business investment is mostly maintenance. Pent-up demand has "not materialized" (Evans). Score about 40.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend, from a few mentions. Rosengren cites "rapid productivity growth". Bernanke notes productivity growth "accelerated" and recalls 6 percent in 2009. Warsh sees a "cyclical upsurge in productivity". Score about 62, with wide uncertainty.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Somewhat loose. Fisher: "Liquidity is ample, and money is cheap". Kocherlakota: spreads and volatility are at normal or below-normal levels and markets function much better. Bernanke: markets are "supportive of recovery". Offsetting this, Kohn and Bernanke note that bank credit is constrained and a "headwind". Net score is just below neutral, about 44.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only Kocherlakota addresses it directly (Treasury volatility near or below normal, markets functioning well). Warsh raises heavy sovereign issuance and "sloppy auctions" only as a hypothetical risk. This is normal functioning, so I score it at the low end with moderate confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Kocherlakota: "Corporate bond risk spreads and interbank lending spreads are at normal or below-normal levels". Kohn: bond issuance proceeds "without any upward pressure on spreads", but loan spreads over benchmarks "have risen further". Greek and peripheral spreads are elevated (Warsh), but that is a foreign sovereign matter. The net level is slightly below normal, so I score 42.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Kohn says "equity prices ... are still in line with fundamentals". Tarullo sees "little evidence of incipient asset bubbles". Roughly fair valued, score 47.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Credit losses are plateauing and REO is moving (Duke), but bank loans are declining rapidly (Kohn). Mortgage quality is a risk (Yellen, Bernanke), CRE is weak, and European banks are fragile (Warsh, Rosengren). No one calls US banks healthy or stressed outright. Neutral, slightly above, score 52.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Tarullo: "The strengthening of the U.S. dollar". Bernanke: "The dollar has also strengthened". Appreciation means above 50, so I score 57.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS and SERVICES: not discussed in a way that supports a side, so N/A. There are only scattered anecdotes (Chinese prices up, AT&T pricing, used cars).
- HOUSING: Extensive shelter discussion. Bullard: shelter reflects "excess supply conditions". Dudley and Yellen: shelter and lodging decelerating. Bernanke on OER. Low and decelerating, score 30.
- EXPECTATIONS: Described as stable and anchored by most. Evans: "pretty well anchored". Yellen: "reasonably well anchored". Bernanke: "moderated slightly". Hoenig and Kocherlakota raise a long-run unanchoring risk only as a risk. Score 45.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee holds at near-zero rates with "extended period" retained. There is no new easing and no tightening. A majority is firmly in hold-accommodation mode (Yellen "no case now for tightening", Kohn, Dudley, Rosengren, Tarullo). A visible minority wants normalization or an exit road map sooner (Hoenig, Plosser, Kocherlakota, Lacker, Fisher). Score is lean dovish to balanced, 42.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal dissent (Hoenig). Several reservations on statement language and on the sequencing of MBS sales versus reserve draining. Disagreement mostly concerns exit tactics and the guidance language, with some economic disagreement about financial imbalances. Moderate differences, score 42.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Prominent but not dominant. Hoenig makes it his central argument. Tarullo, Bernanke, Yellen, Rosengren, Kohn, Dudley, Warsh, Lacker and Plosser all address financial imbalances or bubbles. Europe's sovereign risk, housing finance, and bank credit also come up. Meanwhile much of the meeting is balance-sheet mechanics. Score 55.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The decision retains "exceptionally low ... for an extended period" and it is stressed as conditional. Bernanke: changing it "would be tightening". Exit strategy is deferred to April. This is a low-for-long signal with some preparation for exit, so 27.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Rosengren cites "a high degree of uncertainty". Bernanke: "uncertainty about the forecast remains quite high". Duke: "so much uncertainty gripping markets, business people, and consumers". Fisher and Pianalto stress policy uncertainty among CEOs. Others (Hoenig, Dudley) are fairly confident in a modest recovery. Elevated but not extreme, 50.

**Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 28 (below potential, so < 50).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 ("supportive", "cheap money", so < 50).
- CREDIT_SPREADS 42 (stated normal or below-normal level, so < 50).
- BANKING_STRESS 52 (no "sound" or "stressed" language, so neutral is allowed).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 (appreciation, so > 50).
- TREASURY_STRESS 30 (no dysfunction described).

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 28 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 20 differ by 8 points, which is consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP 28 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40 differ by 12 points. Spending is growing modestly while slack is large, which is plausible.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 and CREDIT_SPREADS 42 co-move.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS 30 are consistent in a normal market.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 26 is consistent with housing 30 and expectations 45 (expectations are stable and anchored, while current inflation is low).

**N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS and INFLATION_SERVICES were not addressed beyond anecdote.

**Text quality.** The text reads cleanly. Staff turns are removed, so the opening Q&A shows only member questions. A few page-marker artefacts ("3" before Dudley, a footnote line) are ignorable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 26 +-6 (High).** Rosengren: "the trend of lower core prices continues". Pianalto: "more than 40 percent of the consumer's market basket has been exhibiting outright price decreases". Center of gravity: broad disinflation, core near 1 percent. Inflation pressure is below neutral because members see underlying inflation falling below target.

**OUTPUT_GAP 28 +-6 (High).** Kohn: "a considerable output gap". Yellen: "an extended period of substantial underutilization of resources". The output gap is below neutral because members describe a large and slowly closing gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 20 +-5 (High).** Kocherlakota: unemployment "remained elevated at 9.7 percent". Bernanke: "labor market conditions overall remain very weak". Labor is much looser than neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 40 +-7 (Moderate).** Kohn: retail sales and equipment spending "encouraging", but saving rate drop and weak income. Evans: pent-up demand "has not materialized". Net is modestly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY 62 +-10 (Low).** Bernanke: "productivity growth accelerated". Rosengren: "rapid productivity growth". Productivity is above trend per the few who mention it.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 44 +-8 (Moderate).** Fisher: "Liquidity is ample, and money is cheap". Kohn: "high cost of bank credit" and loans declining. Conditions are slightly loose (below neutral), with a credit-quantity headwind.

**TREASURY_STRESS 30 +-8 (Low).** Kocherlakota: "Treasury and equity volatility are at near normal or below-normal levels". Normal functioning, below the middle.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 42 +-7 (Moderate).** Kocherlakota: corporate and interbank spreads "at normal or below-normal levels". Kohn: bond issuance with no "upward pressure on spreads". Level is slightly below normal, hence below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 47 +-6 (Low).** Kohn: "equity prices, I think, are still in line with fundamentals". Roughly fair valued, so at neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Duke: "credit losses reaching a plateau and loan demand weak". Kohn: "Bank loans continue to decline rapidly". Mixed signals, near neutral, so a roughly neutral score.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 +-6 (Moderate).** Tarullo: "The strengthening of the U.S. dollar". Bernanke: "The dollar has also strengthened". Modest appreciation, so above neutral.

**INFLATION_HOUSING 30 +-8.** Bullard: shelter "more reflective of excess supply conditions in housing". Yellen: "deceleration in shelter costs". Housing inflation is below stable.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 45 +-... (value only).** Evans: "inflation expectations are pretty well anchored". Bernanke: "moderated slightly". Slightly below the anchored-at-target midpoint.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 42 +-7 (High).** Yellen: "I see no case now for tightening monetary policy". Hoenig: "a case for modestly raising the interest rate on reserves ... sooner than the Greenbook or the markets". Majority hold with accommodation, a vocal hawkish minority. Lean is dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 42 +-7 (High).** Vote read-out: "President Hoenig No". Several members disagree about exit sequencing and language. One dissent plus multiple reservations puts this in the moderate-differences band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Hoenig: "the longer-run risk to financial and economic stability". Bernanke: "financial imbalances ... need our attention". Several members address it meaningfully, though it is not the main topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 27 +-8 (Moderate).** Bernanke: "I would propose that we take alternative B, including the 'extended period' language". Low-for-long retained and described as conditional. An exit road map is deferred to April.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 50 +-8 (Moderate).** Rosengren: "a high degree of uncertainty around the forecast". Duke: "so much uncertainty gripping markets, business people, and consumers". Elevated but mostly standard recovery and policy-uncertainty language.
