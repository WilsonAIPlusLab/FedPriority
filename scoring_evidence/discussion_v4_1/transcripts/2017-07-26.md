## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- INFLATION_PRESSURE 30 +-5 High. Mester: core PCE "1.4 percent in May", inflation "underrunning our 2 percent goal for quite some time". Brainard: "well short of our objective", "five straight years of undershooting". Fischer: inflation "stubbornly low". Kashkari: "significant shortfall below our 2 percent target". Below target, expected to be partly transitory but not scored on forecast.
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 +-6 Moderate. Williams: growth "exceeds, by a sizable margin, its sustainable trend", labor market "has exceeded full employment". Powell: "moderate but above-trend pace". Rosengren: unemployment "well below the natural rate". Counter: Evans "basically are at full employment"; Kashkari "still slack in the labor market". Net modestly above potential (bright line > 50).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 62 +-5 High. Fischer/Mester: unemployment "4.4 percent, below most estimates of the natural rate". Mullinix: "labor market is tight and continues to tighten". Yellen: firms perceive the market "quite tight and becoming yet tighter"; Bostic/Kashkari note wages not accelerating, and Kashkari sees some residual slack (minority).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 54 +-6 Moderate. Mester: "fundamentals supporting consumer spending remain sound, and business investment has accelerated". Powell: "solid gains in private domestic final purchases", "resurgence in business investment". Offsets: retail sales "soft/weak", auto sales softening (Mester, Evans, Harker, Bostic), Evans: customers "slow to sign off" on borrowing. Net at to slightly above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY 33 +-8 Moderate. Mullinix: "very poor labor productivity growth". Williams: "recent weak productivity gains". Mester: "low productivity growth we've seen over the expansion". Fischer: "lower rate of productivity growth".
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 27 +-5 High. Brainard: "loose financial conditions", "remain quite loose". Powell: "financial conditions are now looser than in December 2015". George: "increasingly accommodative". Dudley: "financial conditions continue to ease". Bright line < 50 satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS 40 +-8 Low. Dudley: worries about "valuation in the market for Treasury securities", 10-year yield "quite surprising", "depressed term premiums are another name for overvaluation". Yields and term premia discussed; no dysfunction, liquidity or auction problem described, so normal-functioning range (36-50).
- CREDIT_SPREADS 22 +-6 High. Dudley: "credit spreads are narrow", "credit spreads are narrower". George: "corporate bond spreads reached post-recession lows". Yellen: "corporate bond yields unusually low". Fischer: leveraged-loan practices "worrisome" but "less frothy" than before 2013 guidance; riskiest-bond issuance "subdued". Stated level is tight, so < 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION 30 +-7 Moderate. Dudley: "valuations are high", "equity risk premiums are somewhat low... but not unusually so", "not particularly worried". Yellen: P/E "in the top quartile", valuation pressures red zone "quite reasonable". Powell: asset prices "high, but not alarmingly so"; Brainard: "valuation pressures are greatest in the equity markets". Elevated valuation view but explicitly not alarmed.
- BANKING_STRESS 25 +-5 High. Yellen: "the banking system is well capitalized and has ample liquidity". Fischer: "financial system is well placed to absorb large drops in asset prices"; banks not increasing risk-taking materially. Brainard: "robust capital buffers". George worries capital may be eroded by deregulation (forward risk), Rosengren notes medium-sized banks' CRE exposure (pocket of concern). Net healthy, < 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 +-5 Moderate. Brainard: "the dollar has depreciated a bit on a trade-weighted basis". Powell: "the dollar is weaker" since December 2015. Dudley: "the dollar is weaker". Depreciation, so < 50, modest.
- INFLATION_GOODS 35. George: "12-month inflation rate of imported consumer goods remains near zero". Evans: "further downward pressure on vehicle prices".
- INFLATION_SERVICES 38. Mester: "drops in the prices of cell phone service plans in March and the prices of prescription drugs in April" (special factors). Thin evidence.
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A. Rents and shelter prices not discussed by members.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 38. Mester: "Longer-run inflation expectations remain reasonably stable". Kashkari: "no evidence that inflation expectations are rising, and there is some evidence that they may be falling". Brainard: "imperative to raise longer-term inflation expectations". Harker: "possible decline in inflation expectations". Below target, mostly anchored.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 50 +-7 Moderate. Policy unchanged (alternative B), unanimous. Tightening elements: announcement of balance sheet runoff signalled for September ("relatively soon"); Rosengren: "continue gradually and steadily removing accommodation... taking out insurance" against overheating; Williams: "additional rate hike by year-end". Offsets: Bullard: "likely be on pause through the end of the year and into 2018"; Evans: "further rate hikes are going to be a tough sell"; Kashkari/Bostic favour alternative A inflation language; Yellen: "prudent to hold off on making further changes" to the target range. Roughly balanced.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 +-6 High. Vote read-out: all nine voters "Yes", no dissents. Differences were on the "For the time being" wording (Yellen: "very slim majority"), whether to adopt alternative A paragraph 3 (Bostic, Evans, Bullard, Kashkari for; Kaplan, Dudley not yet), and the cyclical-versus-secular inflation debate. Mostly tactical; some disagreement about the economics of inflation.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 68 +-7 High. A dedicated financial-stability round with Dudley, Rosengren, Fischer, Kaplan, George, Kashkari and Brainard; Fischer: asset prices "for the first time... flashing red"; Kashkari: "I... don't have great clarity on the Committee's reaction function as it relates to financial stability". Continued in the policy round (Powell on the inflation versus financial-stability tradeoff, Dudley, Mester) and in Yellen's closing remarks. Prominent but secondary to the inflation debate.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 60 +-8 Moderate. Statement language change: balance sheet reinvestment change "relatively soon" plus "For the time being" (Yellen: signal for September, Fischer: "definitely ought to be there"). Rates: Yellen "hold off on making further changes to the target range until we've seen more inflation data", "wait until December". Balance sheet reduction signal is tightening; the rate path is patient.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 52 +-8 Moderate. Bostic: "puzzled and perhaps a bit troubled", "lack of clarity regarding the inflation situation". Kashkari: "we don't really understand inflation dynamics very well". Powell: "Inflation is the main source of uncertainty", "high uncertainty regarding the level of the natural rate". Mester/Harker: political and fiscal policy uncertainty weighing on firms. Evans: "quite uneasy about the outlook for inflation". Elevated about inflation specifically, with many members still fairly confident in the broad outlook.
