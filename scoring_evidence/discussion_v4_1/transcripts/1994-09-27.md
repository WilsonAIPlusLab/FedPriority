# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 1994-09-27

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It holds the members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic go-around, the Chairman's opening policy remarks, the policy go-around and the roll call. Staff content is ignored. Some lines are missing: the staff answers were removed, so a few questions appear without context. Nothing is garbled enough to change the reading. Page markers and page-break fragments such as "9/27/94 -10MR. PARRY." are extraction artifacts.

**2. Era context.** Autumn 1994, after the tightening that began in February and the 50bp move in August. The funds rate is 4-3/4 percent. The expansion is in its fourth year. The discussion is about whether to tighten again now or hold with an asymmetric directive. Scores below rest on what participants say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chairman Greenspan: sees industrial momentum with a "head of steam," rising margins and price pressure, and expects to need another move, probably 50bp, but proposes no move today ("B" asymmetric).
- Hawks: Broaddus (dissents, wants "D"), Stern, Melzer (wants D plus a discount rate increase now), Parry, Hoenig, Lindsey and Minehan ("torn," risks to the upside).
- Dovish or patient: Yellen (inflation increase is temporary), Blinder (activity is concentrated, restraint is still in the pipeline), Jordan (Main Street does not see inflation, money and reserves are fine), LaWare ("sensitive foot on the brakes"), Forrestal ("a blip is a blip"), Phillips and Kelley.
- McDonough: hold, with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening. He thinks policy is now close to neutral.

**4. Directional signals (core dimensions).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Melzer cites CPI at 4.1% annualized and PPI above 6%. Broaddus expects 3-3/4% CPI in early 1995 and rising expectations. McDonough sees CPI going from 2-3/4% to 3-1/4%. Offsetting views are Jordan's Main Street survey, several districts saying costs cannot be passed through, and Yellen's "temporary" argument. The "transitory" gotcha says to score the current level. About 3% and rising, with clear upside pressure, gives the low 60s. The Chairman's remark that there was "surprisingly little talk of more price pressures" pulls it slightly down.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as above potential in flow terms and at or just beyond capacity in level terms. Phillips says "third year with above-potential GDP growth" and "most of the slack... is used up." Moskow says "little slack remains." Parry says growth is above potential and "labor and product markets... tight." Jordan says autos are at capacity. Yellen says "close to potential" and "roughly the natural rate." Above-potential language means the score is above 50 (bright line). The result is modestly above 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as tight. Melzer reports shortages of unskilled labor, Jackson TN pausing recruitment and a district unemployment rate near its lowest since 1974. Jordan reports a "shortage of teenagers." Stern says "jobs are certainly readily available." Moskow reports temp wages up 5-10% and bonuses. Yellen puts unemployment "roughly the natural rate" and says wages are well-behaved (0.2% AHE). The national unemployment rate is never stated. The era's natural rate is about 6%, so I score the participants' "tight, near or at natural rate, with shortages in places" description in the low 60s rather than applying the rubric's UE table.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Spending is above trend. Business equipment and capital goods are strong. Durables and autos are strong, though supply-constrained. Retail sales are strong, with a possible Christmas spree. Housing is slowing, and Phillips and McDonough mention some consumer slowing. Net: above trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed lightly. Greenspan says unit labor costs are contained "by productivity gains." Phillips says the strength is "dependent on productivity gains" and asks whether the 1990s trajectory continues. Forrestal says potential growth may have risen. This reads as modestly above trend, with low evidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Phillips says the capital markets give "a cost of capital that is clearly supportive of investment and expansion." Stern says credit is "a good deal more readily available" to small businesses and that rate rises have had "no restraining effect." Phillips says "Bank credit markets are showing significant strength." Against that, bond yields are at a four-year high and Boehne reports concern about rising rates. "Supportive" means the score must be below 50 (bright line).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants discuss yields, the 1-to-2-year curve oddity (Jordan) and "nervous" bond markets (Phillips, "not the wild roller coaster"). There is no dysfunction. Score 44, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Not discussed as spreads. Stern says credit is readily available. McTeer says small banks complain about aggressive large-bank competition "more a matter of price and spreads than standards." Boehne and Minehan report slippage in underwriting standards. These show ample availability and a below-normal level (below 50, bright line). Low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only Phillips: stocks show "amazing resilience," with prices "sustained by earnings," giving a "healthier" price-earnings relationship. No valuation concern. Neutral, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Jordan says banks are in "very good shape; this is the strongest year in decades." Slippage in underwriting standards and Phillips's "balance sheet strains could reemerge" are mild flags. Healthy with minor concerns (below 50, bright line).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen refers to a "significant depreciation of the dollar" raising import prices. Lindsey refers to prior "dollar devaluation" and a possible short-term dollar appreciation in a downside scenario. The direction is depreciation, so the score is below 50 (bright line). Moderate to Low confidence.

**5. Polarity check.** Higher means more of the named construct. Financial conditions are "supportive," which is looser, so below 50. Credit spreads are narrow or ample, which is low stress, so below 50. Banking is healthy, so low stress, below 50. The dollar has depreciated, so below 50. Inflation, output, labor and spending are all above 50 because the evidence points to more of each.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the Committee holds at 4-3/4% with an asymmetric directive toward tightening ("B" asymmetric). Many expect a move, probably 50bp, before or at the November meeting (Lindsey, Minehan "likely that we'll move during the intermeeting period," Phillips, Hoenig, Parry). Three participants want to tighten now (Broaddus, Stern, Melzer), and Broaddus dissents. No one argues for easing, and McDonough and Blinder note easing before November is "zero" probability. It is a hold with a tightening bias and a hawkish tail: low 60s.
- Disagreement: one formal dissent (Broaddus, the hawkish side). Jordan, McTeer and Phillips voice reservations about asymmetry but vote yes. Melzer and Stern also wanted to move now, though neither votes (not voting members). The economics are contested (a blip versus a durable inflation increase; Main Street versus Wall Street). The camps are hold-with-tilt versus move-now versus skeptical of asymmetry. This is moderate-to-notable disagreement, not a deep split.
- Financial stability emphasis: this is 1994, with a monetary-policy focus. Credit availability, bond yields, underwriting standards, the Japan trade deadline and German elections in the market sense, monetary aggregates and the credibility of the "for the time being" language appear as context, not as a financial stability concern. Limited.
- Forward guidance: the asymmetric directive tilts toward tightening. The Chairman says that if the Committee moves in November it would likely be 50bp plus the discount rate, and that the Committee should be prepared to move before then, by telephone. The statement "for the time being" from August was interpreted as an on-hold signal. The signal is a clear tilt toward tightening, but with no move now and some restraint on timing: about 70.
- Uncertainty: many caveats. Is the price rise a blip or durable? How large are the policy lags? Will the data surprise? Greenspan says the cycle "may be mushy where the end result will be somewhere in between." Forrestal says "too early to determine." Boehne says "the major uncertainty." Yet most members state firm views on momentum, and McDonough says risks are "fairly well balanced." Somewhat-to-moderately elevated, normal for a turning point.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP above 50 with above-potential language: satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS below 50 on "supportive": satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS below 50 on ample availability: satisfied. BANKING_STRESS below 50 on healthy: satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH below 50 on depreciation: satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS in the 45-55 range or below with no dysfunction: satisfied (44, no dysfunction described, normal yield movement).

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 56 versus LABOR 62: a 6-point gap, within tolerance.
- OUTPUT_GAP 56 versus CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62: a 6-point gap, within tolerance.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 versus CREDIT_SPREADS 33: both below 50, consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS versus TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, near 44 with no dysfunction, consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 61 versus sub-components: goods 60 (raw materials and PPI up, little pass-through) and expectations 62 (bond rate, credibility gap). Services and housing are N/A.

**9. N/A justification.**
- INFLATION_SERVICES: no member comments on services prices.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: only passing references to house prices rising in the Cleveland and Atlanta districts (Jordan, Forrestal), nothing on rents or shelter inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 61 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Melzer: "In July and August, consumer prices accelerated to an annual rate of 4.1 percent and producer prices to an annual rate of more than 6 percent." Broaddus: "a fair number of private forecasts of inflation rates of 4 percent or higher toward the end of 1995."
- Summary: Pressure is clearly above the comfort zone and rising, but the incoming core is modest and several members call it temporary.
- Direction: above neutral because most members report accelerating prices and expect further increases.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Phillips: "the third year with above-potential GDP growth," "most of the slack in labor and product markets is used up." Moskow: "little slack remains in the economy."
- Summary: The economy is at or modestly beyond capacity, with growth above potential.
- Direction: above neutral because of explicit above-potential and little-slack language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 +-7 [High].**
- Evidence: Melzer: "shortages even of unskilled labor," a "six-month hiatus in recruiting" in Jackson, and unemployment near its lowest since 1974 in his district. Stern: "Labor markets have tightened further and jobs are certainly readily available."
- Summary: The labor market is tight in many districts, with isolated wage pressure. Yellen sees unemployment near the natural rate, and wages are still well behaved.
- Direction: above neutral because tightness is widely reported, although wage pressure is limited.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "The strong recent growth in investment activity, both fixed and inventory investment, is certainly not suggestive of a business expansion that is about to run out of steam." Phillips: strength in "consumer durables and in business investment, especially equipment spending," though housing has slowed.
- Summary: Spending and investment are well above trend, with housing and some consumer categories cooling.
- Direction: above neutral because strength is broad across investment and durables.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 57 +-9 [Low].**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "unit labor costs apparently have been so well contained by productivity gains." Phillips: "Much of the strength that we have seen... is dependent on productivity gains."
- Summary: Productivity is seen as contributing and perhaps above trend, discussed by only a couple of participants.
- Direction: above neutral because productivity gains are credited with containing costs.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Phillips: "the capital markets are producing a cost of capital that is clearly supportive of investment and expansion." Stern: credit is "a good deal more readily available today to small and medium-size businesses."
- Summary: Conditions are supportive and credit is ample despite higher rates and bond yields.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) because credit availability and the cost of capital are described as supportive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 44 +-8 [Low].**
- Evidence: Phillips: "Bond markets are certainly nervous... but it's not the wild roller coaster that we saw in the first part of this year." Broaddus: the bond rate is persistently "over 7-3/4 percent."
- Summary: The bond market is nervous and yields are high, but there is no dysfunction.
- Direction: near neutral to slightly below because yield moves reflect inflation expectations, not market breakdown.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-9 [Low].**
- Evidence: Stern: credit is "a good deal more readily available." McTeer: aggressive bank competition is "more a matter of price and spreads than standards."
- Summary: Credit is ample and competitive, with no spread stress.
- Direction: below neutral because availability is ample and underwriting is loosening. No numeric level is given, so confidence is Low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-7 [Low].**
- Evidence: Phillips: "The stock markets... have shown quite amazing resilience... Stock prices are being sustained... by earnings," "a healthier relationship between prices and earnings."
- Summary: One member comments, with no valuation concern.
- Direction: near neutral because there is no overvaluation or distress commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Jordan: "all of the banks in the District are reporting that they are in very good shape; this is the strongest year in decades." Boehne: "a fairly general slippage in underwriting standards."
- Summary: Banks are healthy, with some loosening of standards as a minor concern.
- Direction: below neutral because banks are described as healthy and profitable.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Yellen: "we have had a significant depreciation of the dollar, which is causing... a rise in the level of import prices." Lindsey: "dollar devaluation."
- Summary: The dollar has depreciated notably, though the staff and Yellen expect the fall to stop. Lindsey notes a possible short-term appreciation under a German-election scenario.
- Direction: below neutral because the dollar has weakened.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 60 [no UNC/CONF].**
- Evidence: Melzer: steel "two price increases so far this year totaling 10 to 12 percent." Moskow: container board, chemicals and aluminum prices "particularly pronounced."
- Summary: Raw material and producer prices are rising sharply, with limited pass-through to final goods.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Only passing references to house prices.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62 [no UNC/CONF].**
- Evidence: Broaddus: "inflation expectations are rising at least in the financial markets," "we still have a credibility gap." Jordan: Main Street contacts report inflation concern "the same or down."
- Summary: Market-based expectations are drifting up while household and business surveys are calm.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "My own inclination at this stage is to go with 'B' but be asymmetric." Broaddus: "I think we are going to have to move further... My choice would be alternative D." Melzer: "I'd do the 50 basis points now."
- Summary: The Committee holds but tilts toward tightening, expecting a move soon, and three members want it now. No one argues for easing.
- Direction: above neutral (hawkish) because the directive is asymmetric toward tightening and a further 50bp is widely expected.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +-6 [High].**
- Evidence: Roll call: "President Broaddus No." Jordan: "I don't prefer asymmetric directives. I would not dissent." Stern, Melzer, Minehan wanted to act sooner.
- Summary: One formal dissent plus reservations on both sides (move-now versus no asymmetry), and some disagreement on whether the price rise is a blip.
- Direction: moderate differences, above the unanimous floor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 14 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Boehne: "a fairly general slippage in underwriting standards." Phillips: "Balance sheet strains could reemerge following the strong credit growth and the merger and acquisition activity."
- Summary: Brief mentions of credit standards, bond yields and credibility. The focus is on monetary policy and inflation.
- Direction: low attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-7 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Greenspan: "If we do move then, I think that would probably require us to move another 50 basis points and probably move the discount rate as well." McDonough: "an asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening is appropriate."
- Summary: The signal is a tightening tilt with a move likely in November or before, with no move today.
- Direction: hawkish because of the asymmetric directive and the expected 50bp.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +-8 [Moderate].**
- Evidence: Forrestal: "it's simply too early to determine whether we should be worried or not." Greenspan: this cycle "may be mushy where the end result will be somewhere in between, so we won't learn very much."
- Summary: Policy lags, the blip-or-trend question and the incoming data leave several members unsure, though most hold firm views on momentum.
- Direction: somewhat to moderately elevated.
