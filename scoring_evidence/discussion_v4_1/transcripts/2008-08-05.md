# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 2008-08-05

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: members' questions during the staff Q&A (staff answers removed), the economic go-around, a Chair summary, the policy go-around over statement alternatives B and C, and a roll-call read-out. The text is clean; some consecutive turns by one speaker are follow-ups to removed staff answers, but nothing is garbled.

**2. Era context (not used to override text).** Roughly a year into financial turmoil. Policy funds rate is 2 percent. Participants discuss IndyMac, the GSEs, bank capital, oil/commodity prices that just fell off a peak, and a weak labor market.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Chair Bernanke: slow second half, significant downside risk, credit availability is tight, funds rate does not describe the stance fully; inflation is a concern but commodity declines are good news; recommends hold with slightly hawkish language (alt B).
- Hawkish voices: Fisher (dissents for alt C, inflation expectations "unmoored"), Evans (alt C, +50 to 75bp soon), Hoenig (raise modestly now), Plosser (prepare markets, "accommodative"), Lacker (hold now, tighten early), Bullard (hold, prepare September hike), Lockhart/Pianalto/Stern/Warsh (alt B, inflation-tilted).
- Dovish/patient voices: Yellen (balanced wording, next move could be down), Rosengren (tightening would sap strength), Kohn (can be patient), Mishkin (risks balanced, keep options open), Kroszner, Duke, Geithner (no move before early next year).
- Financial-stability voices: Warsh, Kroszner, Duke, Mishkin, Rosengren, Yellen, Kohn; Bullard/Plosser/Lacker argue systemic risk has receded or is overused.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as clearly above target. Headline CPI near 5 percent, June CPI annualized 13.4 percent (Bullard), core above 2 percent and creeping up, broad pass-through (Fisher, Pianalto, Plosser, Evans). Offsets: oil down, wages quiet, expected to moderate (Kohn, Yellen, Rosengren). Score what is reported now: above midpoint, in the "clearly above target, broadening" band, 72.
- OUTPUT_GAP: below potential. Kohn: economy "growing appreciably below the growth rate of its potential"; Mishkin: "subpotential growth for quite a period"; Yellen "significant slack"; Hoenig/Evans note only "modest slack". Score 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: unemployment 5.7 percent and rising, payrolls falling (Rosengren, Kohn, Bernanke). Below full employment: 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: below trend. Plunge in July car sales, June consumption decline (Kohn, Yellen), business fixed investment marked down (Pianalto), nonresidential structures weak; offset by Q2 GDP 1.9 percent with final sales 3.9 percent (Bullard) and resilient consumers earlier. Net 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: touched lightly. Kohn "relatively robust productivity growth", Warsh cites recent productivity, Lockhart contacts cite higher average productivity. Mildly above trend, low evidence: 58.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: tight. Kohn: conditions "tightened since the last FOMC meeting", Yellen "credit crunch has intensified", Bernanke: rates faced by borrowers are higher than a year ago. Not a freeze (Bullard, Plosser, Lacker minimize, Bernanke does not call it panic). 75.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not discussed (only Treasury yields falling and MBS spread to Treasuries as a measure). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: stated high levels. Kroszner: OIS spreads "50 basis points rather than 10"; Bernanke: MBS spread about 260bp vs 120bp a year ago, jumbo-conforming 120bp vs normal 25-50bp; Bullard "many interest rate spreads remain elevated". Level is wide: 76.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: price moves only (stock market down 7 percent since June, bank stocks driven by short selling per Duke, "all being painted with the same brush"). No broad valuation commentary; the bank-equity discussion implies depressed prices not tied to fundamentals. Modestly depressed, low confidence: 57.
- BANKING_STRESS: significant. IndyMac failure, "list of troubled depository institutions growing longer" (Yellen), "pretty grim" bank balance sheets (Mishkin), $228bn projected losses vs $68bn provisions, capital ratios to fall 30-50 percent at big banks (Bernanke), Duke: confidence lowest since the S&L crisis. Mitigants: Hoenig/Plosser local banks are well capitalized or in good shape, Lacker sees reallocation, Duke says credit numbers "don't look all that bad". 74.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Kohn says the dollar "hasn't changed much on balance for four or five months"; Pianalto mentions earlier depreciation as a background factor; Warsh/Bernanke mention dollar weakness as a conditional risk. Stable, 45.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher inflation pressure = more pressure; evidence is high; match. Higher credit spreads = more stress; evidence is wide; match. Higher banking stress = more stress; evidence is deteriorating; match. Financial conditions higher = tighter; evidence is tighter; match. Labor higher = tighter; evidence is loosening; low score matches.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: strong goods and input-cost pass-through (Fisher, Pianalto, Evans, Plosser surveys), commodity/energy driven: 72.
- SERVICES: not discussed: N/A.
- HOUSING (shelter inflation): housing prices and activity discussed, not shelter/rent inflation: N/A.
- EXPECTATIONS: Fisher "becoming unmoored", Hoenig "erosion", Kohn "less well anchored", Plosser risk premiums; Yellen, Mishkin, Kroszner, Rosengren say contained or not deteriorating, Michigan 1-year 5.25 percent. Mixed, leaning elevated: 62.

**7. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: hold at 2 percent with a statement that is "slightly hawkish" (Bernanke); one voting dissent for a hike; four non-voting or voting members want a hike now or soon (Evans, Hoenig; Bullard, Lacker, Plosser want a September or early tightening signal); Yellen, Rosengren, Kohn, Mishkin, Kroszner, Geithner patient. Hold with tightening bias: 57.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Fisher), plus Evans and Hoenig advocating hikes (non-voting this year), statement-wording fights ("also"), a head-on disagreement about systemic risk (Bullard/Lacker/Plosser vs Bernanke/Mishkin) and about whether policy is accommodative (Lacker/Plosser vs Bernanke/Kohn/Yellen). Disagreement extends to assessment: 50.
- Financial stability emphasis: dominant for Q&A, Warsh, Kroszner, Duke, Mishkin, Rosengren; rivals the economic assessment: 78.
- Forward guidance: statement signals slight upward tilt, "state-dependent" exit, Bernanke says accommodation must be removed "relatively soon", Geithner no move before early next year: 62.
- Uncertainty: Lockhart "considerable uncertainty", Hoenig "considerable", Kohn "uncertainties remain quite elevated", Bernanke "very uncertain", Mishkin and Kroszner stress tail scenarios and "keep options open": 66.

**8. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 (<50, "below potential"): ok. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 75 (>50, tightened): ok. CREDIT_SPREADS 76 (>50, stated wide levels): ok. BANKING_STRESS 74 (>50 stressed): ok. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 45 (stable, no appreciation language): ok. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**9. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs LABOR 34: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36: aligned. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 75 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 76: co-move. Treasury stress N/A in a non-Treasury-focused discussion. INFLATION_PRESSURE 72 vs goods 72 and expectations 62: consistent (parent weighted to goods/headline).

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS (Treasury market function not raised), INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING (not raised by participants).

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 72 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bullard, "headline CPI inflation rate is running close to 5 percent"; Plosser, "Year-over-year core PCE inflation has exceeded 2 percent every month but one since April 2004". Center of gravity: clearly above target with broad pass-through, tempered by a recent oil decline. Direction: above neutral because headline and core are above target and pass-through is widespread.

**OUTPUT_GAP 33 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "growing appreciably below the growth rate of its potential"; Mishkin, "subpotential growth for quite a period of time". Center: economy below potential with slack widening. Direction: below neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 34 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Rosengren, unemployment "has already reached 5.7 percent"; Kohn, "persistence of a soft labor market". Center: unemployment 5.7 percent and rising, payrolls falling. Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "decline in consumption in June" and "further sharp drop in auto sales in July"; Pianalto, "mark down the prospects for business fixed investment this year and next". Center: below-trend spending with weakening housing and structures; Q2 GDP only partly offsets. Direction: below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 58 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Kohn, "relatively robust productivity growth is holding down unit labor costs"; Warsh, "owing in part to the productivity we've seen in recent months". Center: mildly above trend, few mentions. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 75 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "financial conditions for households and businesses have tightened since the last FOMC meeting"; Yellen, "The credit crunch appears to have intensified since we last met". Center: tight and tightening credit, but short of a freeze. Direction: above neutral (tighter).

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Not discussed as a market-function topic.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 76 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kroszner, OIS spreads "at 50 basis points rather than at 10 basis points"; Bernanke, MBS-Treasury spread "about 260 basis points compared with 120 basis points last summer". Center: spreads at stated high levels, widely cited. Direction: above neutral on level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 57 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Rosengren, "the stock market has fallen 7 percent"; Duke, short selling "really driven down the prices... painted with the same brush". Center: depressed bank-equity prices, little valuation commentary. Direction: modestly above neutral (depressed).

**BANKING_STRESS 74 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Yellen, "The list of troubled depository institutions is growing longer"; Mishkin, "Bank balance sheets do not look very good... they look pretty grim". Center: material and rising stress with failures, offset by regional pockets of health. Direction: above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 45 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "The dollar hasn't changed much on balance for four or five months now". Center: stable dollar. Direction: just below neutral, no appreciation language.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS 72 (Fisher/Pianalto pass-through anecdotes, Plosser surveys). SERVICES N/A. HOUSING N/A. EXPECTATIONS 62 (Fisher "unmoored" and Kohn "less well anchored" vs Yellen/Mishkin "well contained").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 57 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "my intention is for it to be slightly hawkish—to indicate a slight upward tilt"; Evans, "increasing the funds rate in the range of 50, maybe 75, basis points". Center: hold with a tightening bias, a vocal hawkish minority, a patient majority. Direction: above neutral but modest.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 50 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Fisher, "I'm going to vote against alternative B and for alternative C"; Bernanke, "I know we don't have agreement around the table". Center: one formal dissent, several non-voting advocates for hikes, open clashes on systemic risk and the policy stance. Direction: notable disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 78 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Warsh and Kroszner on bank capital and GSE turmoil; Bernanke, "I do believe that the financial stress and its implications for credit availability are important". Center: dominant topic alongside the macro assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 62 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Pianalto, "puts the markets on notice"; Bernanke, "the speed at which we remove the accommodation... we do have to do that relatively soon". Center: mild tightening bias, state-dependent; Geithner no move before early next year. Direction: above neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 66 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "uncertainties remain quite elevated"; Hoenig, "the uncertainty regarding the economic outlook is considerable". Center: high uncertainty, tail scenarios taken seriously by many. Direction: elevated.
