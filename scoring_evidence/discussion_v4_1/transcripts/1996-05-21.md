# v4.1 Scoring: member discussion, transcript, meeting 1996-05-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It holds the Chair's opening, members' questions during the staff Q&A, the regional go-around, the Chairman's policy summary, the policy go-around on alternative B and symmetry, a closing discussion of an inflation-goal exercise, and a roll-call read-out (ten "Yes" votes). Staff turns are absent. A few passages are fragmentary (staff answers removed), but the members' substantive remarks read cleanly. One gap: the line "a remarkable sense of denial or willingness to deny the problems" (Lindsey, on Europe) appears truncated. It does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1996, mature expansion. The Fed cut 25bp in January (funds rate now 5-1/4). Members describe a rebound in growth in Q1, a rise in long rates of about 80bp since January, higher oil and grain prices, and a possible minimum wage increase. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan, McDonough, Governors Phillips, Kelley, Yellen, Lindsey, and Presidents Parry, Moskow, McTeer, Minehan, Guynn, Hoenig, Boehne, Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Jordan. Views on conditions: solid-to-strong growth, tight labor markets with little wage evidence, energy/food-driven inflation, and rising upside inflation risk. Views on policy: Melzer, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig and Minehan want to tighten 25bp now. The Chair, McDonough, Boehne, Stern, McTeer, Yellen, Phillips, Kelley, Guynn and Jordan favor holding (B). Hoenig, Minehan, Lindsey and Moskow want an asymmetric directive toward tightening.

**4. Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. The CPI is running about 3% and rising on food and energy, inflation expectations are drifting up (Michigan survey), and upside risks have grown (Moskow, Melzer, Minehan, Stern). Core is flat and firms cannot raise prices (Guynn, McDonough, Greenspan), which holds the score to the modest range. About 60.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as at to modestly above potential. Hoenig: unemployment and capacity utilization "around potential". Kelley: "GDP is at or near potential". Moskow: risks "clearly on the up side for output relative to potential". Broaddus: growth "well above a sustainable noninflationary pace" this quarter. The staff view (growth near or above potential) is not scored. About 54, bright line satisfied (>50 with above-potential language, not below).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see labor markets as tight. Moskow: "labor markets remain tight throughout the District"; Broaddus: shortages of skilled labor; Minehan: "severe labor shortages" for software people; Kelley: UE about 5-1/2%, "at or below the NAIRU". But the UE rate is 5.4% (Phillips) and wage evidence is mixed. The rubric table's UE band argues for the mid-40s; participants' tight language argues higher. About 55.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Broaddus: Q1 GDP "remarkable strength across the board". Phillips: housing "amazingly strong", capital spending a major factor. Yellen: "surprised by the strength of both consumption and investment". Greenspan: a wealth effect is driving the consumer. Some moderation is expected in H2, but current spending is above trend. About 63.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched on only indirectly: Greenspan (services show only a small productivity increase, with measurement anomalies), Jordan (anecdotes say productivity gains are higher than in the statistics), Moskow (a steel CEO can offset wage gains with productivity). Mixed, near trend, low confidence. About 48.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Long rates are up about 80bp and the dollar is higher (Yellen calls it a "large swing in financial conditions", McTeer says "we already have had some tightening"). Offsetting this: Phillips says financial markets "continue to support" investment, the term structure is "reasonably low", the stock market is strong, and "bank credit is still available"; Minehan says credit and funding are "available and not overly costly"; lenders are competing fiercely (Minehan, Melzer). Net is slightly loose-to-neutral. About 46. Bright line: the "supportive/available" language is placed below 50, and no one calls conditions tight or restrictive.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The Treasury market is discussed only as yield-curve and term-structure signals of policy expectations (Broaddus, Yellen, Jordan, Greenspan). That is yield movement, not dysfunction. No functioning issues raised. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Only Melzer gives a level: the card-backed securities spread over Treasuries "has remained very low". Banks are relaxing terms (Minehan). Spreads are below normal, so the score is below 50. About 36, Low confidence, since it is a single comment.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants see stocks as richly valued. Yellen: "dangerously lofty level of stock prices". Lindsey: OTC "a bubble might very well be forming", "the silly season". Phillips: "a bit on the rich side". Greenspan: "the stock market ... is high; it's not that there is a bubble". Jordan: the software bubble. Lower score means more overvaluation concern. About 24.
- BANKING_STRESS: Lightly discussed. Phillips: "balance sheets are healthier"; Melzer: consumer delinquencies up somewhat, spreads low; McTeer: ag bankers report rising delinquencies and debt loads; Jordan: ag lenders are worried; Minehan: loosening terms. Healthy overall with pockets of concern. Bright line: "healthier" is below 50. About 38, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar has appreciated. Yellen: "appreciated about 4 percent just this year", a stabilizer. Kelley and McTeer: the dollar is up. Appreciation means above 50, modest, and viewed as helpful rather than a concern. About 57.

**6. Inflation sub-components.** GOODS: grain, oil and food price shocks pass through (Moskow, Melzer, Stern) but manufacturers cannot make price increases stick (Guynn, Phillips, McDonough); modest upward net, about 57. SERVICES: N/A (only a medical-CPI aside from Greenspan on measurement). HOUSING: N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Michigan survey expectations rising (Melzer), Stern on "deterioration in inflationary expectations", Melzer on volatile expectations and credibility; Yellen says most of the long-rate rise is real, and Guynn thinks embedding is unlikely without accommodation. Drifting up from a roughly anchored level, about 60.

**7. Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: The Committee voted unanimously for no change (B, symmetric). But five participants argued for an immediate 25bp hike (Melzer, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Minehan), four asked for an asymmetric directive (Hoenig, Minehan, Lindsey, Moskow), and many said the risks have shifted up. The Chair said he would tighten sooner if he fully believed the Greenbook, and expects to judge by July. The center of gravity is a hold with a tightening bias. About 58.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: No formal dissents (ten Yes votes). Disagreement among all participants is about the timing of tightening and about symmetry: a camp of five wants to hike now, versus a hold majority, with Jordan voting yes but with "considerable concern" and saying he would regret not acting. Disagreement is about tactics and the inflation-translation mechanism rather than the economic assessment. The goal-exercise discussion at the end is about framing, not dispute. About 38.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Stock market valuation and bubble talk (Yellen, Lindsey, Greenspan, Jordan), wealth effect, ag lending stress, and bank lending terms are raised but mostly as context for the growth/inflation debate. 1990s anchor is 1-10; stock-valuation attention lifts it. About 17.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Directive symmetric, but the Chair says a judgment is likely by July, McDonough says the next meeting is "a new ballgame", Yellen says the market perceives some chance of a rise "this summer and next fall, and so do I", Guynn "ready to move at our next meeting or before". Signal leans toward eventual tightening with data dependence. About 57.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Repeated acknowledgement that the inflation/real-economy link is not understood. Greenspan: "something different is happening that we do not fully understand"; Stern: "I do not have the sense that I know enough"; Yellen: "imponderables"; Boehne: "I don't know whether current policy is a little restrictive or a little stimulative"; Moskow: "something obviously different is going on". Elevated but not extreme, and policy views are reasonably firm. About 52.

**8. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 (>50, consistent with above-trend language and "at or near potential" mix; near the 48-52 band for "at potential" statements, slightly above because of the "above sustainable" growth remarks). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 46 (<50 with supportive language, no "tight" language). CREDIT_SPREADS 36 (<50, "very low"). BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50, "healthier"). DOLLAR 57 (>50, appreciation). TREASURY N/A.

**9. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs LABOR 55 vs CONSUMPTION 63: within 15 of each other (consumption 9 above the gap; explained by spending being strong while participants expect slowing and keep the gap near potential). FC 46 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 36: both below 50, consistent. FC vs Treasury: Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 60 vs sub-components goods 57 and expectations 60: consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only policy-expectation yield curve talk, no market functioning comment. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: not discussed as price pressures.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Moskow, "the risks of higher inflation are greater today than at the time of our last meeting"; Guynn, "We now expect the CPI to average about 3 percent for the year." Summary: CPI near 3% and rising with upside risks, but core is quiescent and firms cannot raise prices. Direction: above neutral because inflation is perceived to be creeping up.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Hoenig, "the unemployment rate is around its potential and capacity utilization around its potential"; Moskow, "the risks are clearly on the up side for output relative to potential." Summary: economy at or slightly above potential with current-quarter growth above trend. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "Labor markets remain tight throughout the District"; Kelley, unemployment "around 5-1/2 percent, which of course is seen as being at or below the NAIRU". Summary: tight by participants' account with shortages in pockets, but wage pressure mostly absent and UE is 5.4%. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, Q1 GDP "showed remarkable strength across the board"; Phillips, "Housing has remained amazingly strong." Summary: strong consumer, housing and capital spending, expected to moderate later. Direction: above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 48 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Greenspan, "a fairly small increase in productivity" in services; Jordan, anecdotes say productivity improvements are "substantially higher than those in the national statistics". Summary: mixed, roughly trend. Direction: near neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 46 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "Bank credit is still available"; Yellen, "the large swing in financial conditions" from higher long rates. Summary: higher long rates and dollar offset by strong stocks and available credit. Direction: slightly below neutral (somewhat supportive).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Melzer, the spread between card-backed securities and Treasuries "has remained very low"; Minehan, banks "relaxing terms". Summary: spreads and terms favorable. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 24 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "dangerously lofty level of stock prices"; Lindsey, "a bubble might very well be forming". Summary: valuations seen as rich, with several participants voicing bubble concern, though Greenspan says no bubble. Direction: strongly toward overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Phillips, "Balance sheets are healthier"; McTeer, ag bankers "reporting rising debt loads and delinquent payments". Summary: broadly healthy with pockets in agriculture and consumer credit. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +-5 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, "a dollar that has appreciated about 4 percent just this year"; Kelley, "we have seen the dollar rise". Summary: modest appreciation, seen as a stabilizer. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 57. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 60.** Evidence: Melzer, Michigan survey households' 12-month inflation expectations "from 4 percent in December to ... 5 percent in May"; Moskow, food prices "seep into the overall price level".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "it would be wise to tighten policy at this time"; Greenspan, "my general view ... is alternative 'B,' that is, to do nothing at this stage." Summary: hold with a clear tightening bias and a vocal minority wanting a hike now. Direction: hawkish side of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: roll call, ten Yes votes; Jordan, "I support a no-change, symmetric directive, though with considerable concern". Summary: no formal dissents but distinct camps on timing and symmetry. Direction: moderate differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 17 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Yellen, stock prices "dangerously lofty"; Greenspan, "It's hard to believe that ... the market would not come down rather substantially". Summary: stock-market valuation is a recurring side theme, not the focus. Direction: low attention, above the 1996 floor.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "We probably will have to make a judgment by July"; McDonough, "The next meeting will be a new ballgame." Summary: symmetric directive but a tilt toward eventual tightening if data confirm. Direction: mildly hawkish.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "something different is happening that we do not fully understand"; Yellen, "They are all imponderables". Summary: multiple participants stress an unusual, poorly understood inflation-growth relationship. Direction: elevated.
