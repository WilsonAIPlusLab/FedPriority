# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 -- Meeting 2014-06-18 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) staff Q&A on market operations (members' questions only), (b) a long policy-normalization session (ON RRP facility, IOER, fed funds definition, reinvestment, caps), (c) the economic go-around (Rosengren through Williams, then Dudley through Powell, then the Chair's summary), and (d) the policy go-around on "alternative B" (continue a $10 billion taper, plus the question of finishing with $15 billion in October vs. $5 billion in December), ending in a vote read-out: all 10 voting members Yes, no dissents. This is a standard meeting, not a special-topic meeting. Normalization mechanics carry no economic-condition content, so I scored from the go-around, the policy round and the sections that touch financial stability. The text is clean; I noticed no garbled passages. Many staff answers are absent, so some member questions are one-sided, which does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context (not allowed to override the text).** Mid-recovery, asset purchases being tapered, policy rate at the floor. The text itself supplies the backdrop: Q1 GDP was sharply negative, unemployment is 6.3 percent, core PCE inflation is below 2 percent but firming.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish tilt on policy/inflation: Plosser (liftoff possibly Q4 2014, rules say earlier and steeper), Fisher (wage pressure in Texas, "behind the curve" risk, wants to taper reinvestments), Bullard ("everyone should add 25 basis points to the staff forecast for inflation", inflation above target by 2015), Lacker (inflation has bottomed), George (rules suggest preparing markets for earlier liftoff), Williams (upside inflation risk from short-term unemployment gap).
- Dovish tilt: Kocherlakota (inflation expectations may unanchor downward, wants deadlines and more accommodation if inflation is low), Evans (inflation and pressures low, liftoff delayed until high confidence), Rosengren (weak growth, slack, liftoff early 2016), Brainard, Dudley, Powell (slack, no wage or price tightness), Yellen (caution, "substantial remaining slack").
- Financial-stability voices: Powell (long section on leveraged loans, covenant-lite, CLOs, low volatility, reach for yield), Dudley (low volatility, a tradeoff accepted at the ZLB), Fischer, Fisher (CLO, VIX), Yellen's summary, Williams (global divergence, carry trade), Lockhart (Herstatt note), Tarullo/Kocherlakota/Evans/Plosser on ON RRP run risks.

**4. Directions in words, before numbers.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent target but firming (Plosser: "three-month core PCE increasing 1.8 percent"; George: "1.6 percent year-over-year"; Evans: "slow progress back toward our inflation target"; Yellen: many see "positive evidence that inflation is in fact moving back up"). Below midpoint, in the 26-40 / 41-49 border region, with a hawkish minority pulling toward target.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential: Williams "substantial labor market slack persists", Powell "a good bit of slack in the economy", Yellen "substantial remaining slack", Rosengren on slack. Growth is expected to run above trend to close it. Below 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 6.3 percent, "the unemployment rate, though lower, remains elevated" (alt B, endorsed by Fisher); Rosengren, Lockhart, Dudley, Yellen see more slack than U-3 shows; Fisher reports Texas shortages and wage pressure; national wages "very slowly" rising. Below 50, rapid improvement noted but level governs. 31-45 band.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Q1 collapse, rebound expected; consumer spending "momentum ... may not be quite as strong" (Evans), "ho-hum retail sales"; housing soft (Dudley "slow slog", Lacker, Plosser, Lockhart, George); business capex outlook improving (Tarullo, Evans, Lockhart). Net: roughly at or slightly below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched only briefly. Fischer: "productivity must be growing less fast than we thought"; Brainard on real wages lagging productivity; Mester on trend growth debate. Modestly below trend, thin evidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose / supportive (Dudley "supportive financial market conditions"; Powell "leveraged finance markets ... extremely loose"; George "financing conditions remaining supportive"; Lockhart small-business credit improving). Counterpoint: Evans on housing credit "still appear to be restrictive" and tight mortgage standards. Net loose, below 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Not discussed as dysfunction. Volatility in fixed income "at historically low levels" (Powell), question about absorbing 2016 Treasury volumes "in ordinary times", worries about future dealer liquidity. Calm market; low score, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Powell: "Rates and spreads are also at or near historic lows"; Yellen: "some compression in credit spreads, reach for yield". Level compressed, so below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members mention rising stock prices and low volatility/complacency (Dudley, Powell, Fisher) but give no explicit valuation call. Mild concern, near the default.
- BANKING_STRESS: Powell: "the core of the system, which is much stronger and better protected than before the crisis"; Lacker: bankers say "credit quality is getting better and better". Healthy, below 50. Few comments.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Not discussed as a dollar-level or direction topic. N/A.
- INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: not discussed by members as sub-components. N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Heavily discussed. Most see them as anchored near 2 percent (Bullard, Williams, Mester "well anchored", Kocherlakota "we continue to be credible"), but a vocal group (Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Evans, Brainard) fear drift down; Fisher/Plosser the reverse. Close to anchored, slight downside tilt.

**5. Polarity checks.**
Higher = more inflation pressure; members say below target, so score under 50: matches. Higher = tighter labor; members say slack, so under 50: matches. Higher = tighter financial conditions; members say loose/supportive, so under 50: matches. Higher = more credit stress; spreads at historic lows means a low score: matches. Higher = more banking stress; banks sound means low: matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: majority supports alternative B unchanged, continuing a measured $10 billion taper to an end in October or December, with forward guidance of "considerable time". Asset purchases (QE) still in progress is a dovish signal, but the debate is about winding down. A hawkish group (Plosser, Fisher, Bullard, George, Lacker) argues for earlier liftoff, ending reinvestment, or tightening sooner; a dovish group (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren, Brainard) argues for more accommodation or later liftoff. Net lean dovish-to-balanced, low 40s.
- Disagreement: vote is 10-0, no dissents. Reservations: Plosser has "reservations about paragraph 6", Kocherlakota proposes extra wording and pushes for more aggressive response to low inflation, Bullard "I do not think we should add the sentence". Real disagreement exists on the inflation outlook (overshoot vs. undershoot), on reinvestment timing, and on ON RRP design, but it is expressed as preference differences, not dissent. Moderate-low.
- Financial stability attention: prominent. Powell's extended speech, Dudley's volatility section, Fischer, Fisher, Williams, Yellen's summary and the ON RRP run-risk theme throughout the normalization session. Moderate-to-prominent, not dominant.
- Forward guidance: statement keeps "considerable time" and low-rate-for-longer language; Yellen and others stress data dependence and that accommodation could be removed sooner than expected; Plosser, Mester and George want the guidance changed toward state-contingent / earlier liftoff; Kocherlakota wants a stronger easing commitment. Net mild easing bias with data dependence.
- Uncertainty: Fischer "incredible uncertainty"; Dudley "considerable uncertainty about what the growth rate is going to be"; Rosengren "somewhat experimental"; Yellen "quite perplexing" Q1 data; Tarullo on lingering Q1 concern. But others (Lockhart, Mester, Williams, Yellen) call risks "balanced"/"reasonably balanced". Somewhat-to-moderately elevated.

**7. Bright-line check.**
OUTPUT_GAP below potential and score < 50: satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS "supportive"/"extremely loose" and score < 50: satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS "historic lows" and score < 50: satisfied. BANKING_STRESS "much stronger" and score < 50: satisfied. TREASURY_STRESS: no dysfunction described, low score. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

### Economic conditions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Plosser: "the three-month core PCE increasing 1.8 percent ... trending upward at this point toward the Committee's target." Evans: "it still appears that we're making only slow progress back toward our inflation target." George: "The current 1.6 percent year-over-year rate of inflation."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is seen as below 2 percent but firming, with a hawkish minority (Bullard, Fisher, Lacker) expecting it to reach or pass target.
- Direction: Below neutral because most members describe inflation as running under target, and the mean member view is slow progress.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "substantial labor market slack persists, and inflation remains below our longer-run target." Powell: "I continue to see a good bit of slack in the economy."
- Center of gravity: Slack remains, growth expected above trend will gradually close it.
- Direction: Below neutral because members repeatedly cite remaining slack; no one says the economy is above potential (growth "above trend" refers to the pace, not the level).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 37 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "Wage pressures are rising in every single sector" in Texas (unemployment 5.2 percent) but "the unemployment rate ... remains elevated" (Fisher endorsing alt B text). Yellen: "overall movements we have seen in compensation still show little evidence of acceleration, which is consistent with substantial remaining slack." Rosengren: "I also remain concerned about slack in the labor market." National unemployment 6.3 percent.
- Center of gravity: The national labor market is improving steadily but is still viewed as slack, with the Texas pocket the exception.
- Direction: Below neutral because unemployment is 6.3 percent with broad underemployment and participation concerns.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: "the ho-hum retail sales report for May suggest that the momentum in household spending may not be quite as strong as we've assumed." Lockhart: "Spending on autos ... particularly robust"; Tarullo: "cap-ex almost has to jump some this year."
- Center of gravity: Weak Q1 followed by an expected return to moderate spending growth, with a soft housing sector offsetting improving capex.
- Direction: Slightly below to at trend, because rebound hopes are balanced by acknowledged housing weakness and the Q1 collapse.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fischer: "That means that productivity must be growing less fast than we thought, except for the participation rate declining." Brainard: "the continued failure of real wages to grow in line with productivity."
- Center of gravity: Brief, indirect comments pointing to lower trend growth.
- Direction: Modestly below trend, thin evidence.

### Financial conditions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "supportive financial market conditions"; "keeping financial conditions accommodative is what we're trying to achieve." Powell: "Conditions in the leveraged finance markets in the United States and Europe are extremely loose."
- Center of gravity: Markets and credit are viewed as loose and supportive, worries being about complacency, with housing credit the lone tight pocket.
- Direction: Below neutral (looser than normal) because multiple members describe accommodative and easy conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 28 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Powell: "implied volatility for equities, currencies, and long- and short-term fixed income are at historically low levels around the globe." Powell (Q&A): asks whether Treasury volumes in 2016 are ones the market can absorb "in ordinary times."
- Center of gravity: Treasury market is described as calm; no dysfunction described.
- Direction: Below neutral because there is no stress, only prospective liquidity concerns.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 18 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "Rates and spreads are also at or near historic lows." Yellen: "some compression in credit spreads, reach for yield."
- Center of gravity: Spreads are compressed and issuance strong with weak covenants.
- Direction: Below neutral because members state a compressed level, not just a direction.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Dudley: "low volatility also contributes to rising stock prices and lower term premiums." Fisher: "The market is so complacent."
- Center of gravity: Rising prices and complacency noted, but no explicit stretched-valuation call.
- Direction: Slightly on the elevated-valuation side of neutral because of repeated complacency language; confidence low.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Powell: "the core of the system, which is much stronger and better protected than before the crisis." Lacker: "In North Carolina, bankers said that credit quality is getting better and better."
- Center of gravity: Banks viewed as sound; stress concerns pertain to markets and liquidity, not bank capital.
- Direction: Below neutral because the system is described as stronger and credit quality improving.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed as a dollar-level topic by members.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS: N/A (not discussed).
- INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A (not discussed).
- INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A (not discussed).
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45.** Evidence: Mester: "inflation expectations well anchored"; Kocherlakota: "the public continues to trust our willingness and ability to deliver 2 percent inflation" while warning "inflation expectations could drift down." Anchored near target with a downside tilt.

### Discussion-specific

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 43 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: "these circumstances call for maintaining the current course of policy ... a further measured reduction in the pace of asset purchases, combined with forward guidance that signals a highly accommodative future path." Plosser: "most of the policy rules ... indicate that we should have an earlier liftoff and a steeper path."
- Center of gravity: Majority keeps the current accommodative course with the taper winding down; a hawkish minority wants earlier liftoff, a dovish minority wants more accommodation.
- Direction: Slightly dovish of balanced because asset purchases continue and almost all support unchanged accommodative language.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Vote 10-0, "congratulations on your highest majority yet" (Fischer). Plosser: "I have, since we instituted it in March, expressed reservations about paragraph 6." Kocherlakota proposes alternative A language; Bullard: "I do not think we should add the sentence."
- Center of gravity: No formal dissents, but distinct views on the inflation outlook, liftoff timing, reinvestment and ON RRP design.
- Direction: Above the unanimous floor because of real reservations and substantive disagreement on economics, but well below dissent-driven levels.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "After almost six years of highly-accommodative policy, the risks are out there and continue to build." Dudley: "we're essentially incurring some increased risk on financial stability ... in order to generate easier financial conditions." Tarullo: concern about the ON RRP facility facilitating "a run on assets."
- Center of gravity: Several members address it at length, and ON RRP run risk shapes the normalization debate, though the economic assessment still leads.
- Direction: Moderate-to-prominent attention (direction irrelevant).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 25 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Williams: statement "signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate." Mester: "it will very soon be time to change the guidance indicating that liftoff will be a considerable time after the asset purchases end." Yellen: "policy is truly data-dependent ... accommodation would be removed sooner than markets might anticipate."
- Center of gravity: Guidance stays easing-leaning (considerable time, low for longer), but several members want it moved toward state-contingent language and the Chair stresses data dependence.
- Direction: Below neutral (easing bias with data dependence).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fischer: "we have this incredible uncertainty about these things, and we don't really know how to deal with it." Dudley: "I still have considerable uncertainty about what the growth rate is going to be during the second half of the year."
- Center of gravity: Notable uncertainty about growth after Q1, trend growth and normalization, offset by many members calling risks balanced.
- Direction: Somewhat above normal because several members emphasize unusual uncertainty, not most.
