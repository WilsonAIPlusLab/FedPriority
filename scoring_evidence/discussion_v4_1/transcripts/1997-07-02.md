# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting of 1997-07-02 (transcript, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant transcript discussion with staff turns removed. It holds the members' staff Q&A, the Chart Show questions, the round-table economic go-around, a long discussion of a staff paper on a working definition of price stability, the morning session on long-run monetary ranges and communications, a discussion of rewording the directive, and then the policy go-around ending in a unanimous vote read-out (10 Yes; the vote appears twice, once for the ranges and once for the directive). Some staff answers are missing so a few member questions stand without context. A few quoted names are blanked out ("told us that his company"), which does not affect scoring.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1997. The text shows a long expansion, very low unemployment, very low measured inflation, a strong dollar, a booming stock market, and a funds rate of 5-1/2 percent. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hoenig, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Guynn, Stern, Melzer and Meyer stress upside inflation risks. Parry sees excess demand, Moskow a positive output gap, and Meyer fears a sharper rise in inflation than the Greenbook shows.
- Boehne, Rivlin, McDonough, McTeer, Phillips and Kelley are more patient and cite the favorable inflation news.
- Greenspan says the data "do not yet make a credible case for tightening," that risks remain "on the upside," and that there is "no urgency for a particularly aggressive move."
- Policy go-around: all support B-asymmetric (no change, tightening bias) and the vote is unanimous. Melzer would favor C (tighten). Broaddus would "prefer to take out some insurance now." Hoenig sees some tightening as wise. Meyer plans 50bp by year end. Parry says tightening is coming "soon." Stern, Minehan and Moskow stress risks to the upside and the need to act promptly later.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation pressure as only slightly above the midpoint. Measured inflation is benign (CPI up 1.4 percent annualized, PPI down five months, "little, if any, evidence of mounting inflationary pressures," Greenspan), but the broad price indices are 2 to 2.5 percent, "we clearly are not at price stability" (Parry), and nearly everyone forecasts an upward drift. Score 52.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above potential. Moskow: "we currently have a positive output gap"; Parry: "noticeably higher level... GDP gap"; Moskow in the policy round: "the economy is operating beyond its potential." Meyer qualifies that capacity utilization is only "a shade above" its natural rate. Bright-line requires above 50. Score 58.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Stern: "the degree of tightness in our labor market cannot be overstated"; Broaddus: "exceptionally tight"; unemployment 3.8-4.8 percent; Greenspan: "digging into the bottom of the barrel for workers." Meyer: unemployment below NAIRU. Score 70.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly above trend. Q2 growth slowed to about 2 percent and retail sales slumped, but the fundamentals (confidence at highs, an "investment boom," Phillips on business fixed investment) point to a rebound. Net 58.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length. Circumstantial evidence points to faster productivity growth (Greenspan, Rivlin, McDonough, Boehne, Guynn), but it is unproven (Stern, Rivlin: "we really do not know"). Mildly above trend. Score 60.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Loose. Meyer: "financial conditions are generally favorable"; Phillips: "no capital shortage and certainly no credit crunch"; Minehan: "booming capital markets, enormous liquidity" and policy "a bit too accommodative for those kinds of markets." Bright-line requires below 50. Score 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only yield levels were mentioned (Melzer on the 30-year at 6.7-6.8 percent; McTeer on the yield curve). That is no market functioning commentary. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Minehan: banks note "narrowing of spreads and weakening of loan covenants," and nontraditional lenders supply "a tremendous amount of liquidity." Melzer: "more generous lending terms." Level is below normal (narrow). Score 28. Bright-line requires below 50.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Rivlin: stock market "even more emphatically... seriously overvalued." The Greenbook correction is discussed; Minehan wonders about wealth effects. Valuation concern is elevated, which is the low-score side. Score 25.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Jordan: bankers' "margins are getting squeezed, and the quality of their loan portfolios is deteriorating," with aggressive real estate bidding. Broaddus: charge-offs up at some banks but no constraint on credit. Minehan: banks maintain loan quality, and financial services are "booming." Overall banks are earning well with emerging credit-quality worries, so I put it slightly below neutral. Score 42.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: McTeer: "the dollar remains strong"; Rivlin: import-price restraint "from the strong dollar"; Meyer: "appreciation of the dollar." Appreciation and strength require above 50. Rivlin adds that the effect is coming to an end. Score 60.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- Goods: Import prices and PPI have been falling and energy and food are benign, so I score below 50. Score 38. There is some note of a rebound in oil and import prices to come.
- Services: Only Parry's regional remark about Bay Area service prices and wage talk, with no national services price assessment. N/A.
- Housing: Only commercial rents (Dallas, Boston), which is not housing or shelter inflation. N/A.
- Expectations: Broaddus: "inflation expectations basically stable"; Rivlin: "more confident that expected inflation will be low"; Melzer: 3 percent "entrenched in budgets and long-term interest rates." Anchored at roughly current levels. Score 50.

**7. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: no change now, with an asymmetric directive toward tightening. A minority wants to tighten now (Melzer C, Broaddus, Hoenig leaning), and the Chair expects the case may emerge by August. Lean hawkish, short of an actual move. 56.
- Dissent: formal vote unanimous, 0 dissents. Notable differences on timing (Melzer, Broaddus preferring tightening now vs. Boehne, Rivlin, McDonough waiting), and a split on directive wording, but the tone is collegial. 28.
- Financial stability emphasis: Minehan's asset-bubble and boom/bust warning, Jordan's speculative activity in farm land and real estate, Greenspan's "financial stability" aside in the price-stability debate, and the stock market concern. Several members address it meaningfully but it is not dominant. 38.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric bias, "if we begin to see signs of a pickup in demand... no choice but to start to move," "case will emerge by... August." Mild tightening bias. 63.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan: "levels of confusion that I sense," models "do not account for what has been happening," "something different." Rivlin "agnostic," McDonough "knowing what we do not know," Boehne, Kelley "lack of understanding." High and widely shared. 68.

**8. Polarity checks.** Labor: higher = tighter, evidence says tight, score high. Financial conditions: higher = tighter, evidence says loose, score low. Credit spreads: narrow, low. Equity: overvalued concern maps to low in this scale. Dollar: strong, above 50. All consistent.

**9. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 58 vs LABOR 70 differ by 12, within 15, and Meyer explains the labor-versus-product-market disparity. OUTPUT_GAP vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 58 vs 58, fine. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 and CREDIT_SPREADS 28 co-move. Treasury N/A. Parent INFLATION_PRESSURE 52 is consistent with goods 38 (benign) and expectations 50, plus upside forecast concerns.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no discussion of Treasury market functioning. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no national assessment from members.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "hard pressed to find any evidence of rising inflationary pressures in the data"; Parry, "we clearly are not at price stability." Summary: benign current readings offset by general agreement on upside drift in broad indices of 2 to 2.5 percent. Direction: slightly above neutral because the level is above price stability and forecasts rise, though realized data are benign.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +-7 [High].** Evidence: Moskow, "we currently have a positive output gap"; Parry, "noticeably higher level... GDP gap." Summary: economy above potential with a positive gap. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Stern, "the degree of tightness in our labor market cannot be overstated"; Broaddus, "exceptionally tight." Summary: nearly every participant reports severe shortages, bonuses, 4 percent unemployment in the Midwest. Direction: well above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Meyer, "consumer confidence have soared to new highs"; Broaddus, "approaching an investment boom." Summary: Q2 pause with strong fundamentals and rebound expected. Direction: modestly above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +-10 [Moderate].** Evidence: Rivlin, "circumstantial evidence still points to an acceleration in productivity growth"; Stern, "not much evidence that that is the case." Summary: evidence leans to above-trend gains but is disputed. Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Phillips, "no capital shortage and certainly no credit crunch"; Minehan, "booming capital markets, enormous liquidity." Summary: easy, accommodative conditions. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan, "narrowing of spreads and weakening of loan covenants"; Melzer, "more generous lending terms." Summary: spreads narrow and terms loosening. Direction: below neutral (narrow level).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 25 +-10 [Moderate].** Evidence: Rivlin, "seriously overvalued"; Minehan, "asset bubbles" worry. Summary: members see rich valuations and correction risk. Direction: below 50 on this scale (overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-10 [Low].** Evidence: Jordan, "quality of their loan portfolios is deteriorating"; Minehan, banks' "commitment to loan quality remains firm." Summary: healthy earnings with early signs of credit-quality slippage. Direction: slightly below neutral (low stress with minor concerns).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: McTeer, "the dollar remains strong"; Rivlin, "strong dollar." Summary: strong, appreciated dollar restraining import prices. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Evidence: McTeer on five months of falling PPI; Meyer on declining import prices. Benign goods prices.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50.** Evidence: Broaddus, "inflation expectations basically stable." Anchored near current levels.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan, "adopt 'B' asymmetric"; Melzer, "I would favor alternative C"; Meyer, "raise the funds rate by 50 basis points over the remainder of the year." Summary: hold with tightening bias, a vocal minority favors acting now. Direction: modestly hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +-7 [High].** Evidence: unanimous roll call; Broaddus, "I can support your recommendation"; Melzer prefers C but yields. Summary: no formal dissent, differences on timing and on directive wording. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan, "boom/bust scenario... asset bubbles"; Jordan, "speculative activity in farm land, residential real estate." Summary: several members address it meaningfully, not dominant. Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan, "no choice but to start to move" if demand exceeds labor supply; "case will emerge by... August." Summary: asymmetric bias toward tightening. Direction: mild tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-8 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan, "levels of confusion that I sense"; Boehne, "we do not know if this divergence between our models and actual experience is temporary or permanent." Summary: deep, widely shared uncertainty about models and structure. Direction: high.
