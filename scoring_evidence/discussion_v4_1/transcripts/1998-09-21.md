# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting dated 1998-09-21 (transcript, SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** A member/participant discussion transcript, and a short one (about 3,300 words). It is a telephone conference call, not a regular meeting with an economic go-around. The Chairman opens with a case for easing and asks whether he may signal an easing move. The members then respond one by one. A Secretary's note says the line was interrupted just as the Chairman began "My basic concern...."; he then repeats the point about spreads and equities after being asked to. No staff turns are present. There is no formal vote, so any dissent has to be inferred from expressed reservations.

**2. Era context.** The text points to a period of international financial turmoil and a flight to safety, with Japan, emerging markets and Latin America mentioned. Domestically the economy is described as still strong. I score only what the speakers say.

**3. Speakers.**
- Greenspan (Chair) argues that the economy is deteriorating and that private debt spreads have widened sharply. He also cites falling equity prices, a pervasive global "disengagement, fear, uncertainty", and a need to signal an easing move on Sept 29. He prefers 25bp.
- McDonough (Vice Chair) is strongly for easing and says markets are "no longer functioning very well".
- Rivlin, Minehan, Parry, Boehne, Hoenig, Gramlich and Poole all support the signal and the move. Poole asks about the size of the move.
- Broaddus, Stern, Guynn and Kelley support it with hesitation about the domestic case.
- Pianalto (substituting for Jordan) reports that Jordan would probably object to revealing an action before the Committee has deliberated.
- McTeer may argue for 50bp.

**4. Directional signals for the core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: no participant mentions inflation, prices or wages, so there is nothing to score. This is the "truly zero discussion" case and I mark it N/A.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as "holding up" and "operating at a relatively high level" (Boehne), "relatively strong" (Hoenig), with some softening and growth of 3% or less ahead (Greenspan). Nobody says "above" or "below" potential. I place it just above 50 on the "high level of activity" language, with low confidence.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: The only datum is Hoenig's "unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent", described as unusual to be easing at. That is the border of "near NAIRU" and "tight", so about 53.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Current activity is "holding up", but there are "anecdotal indications of some softening", "pervasive" weakness that is "by no means dangerous", and "pessimism". Greenspan expects capital investment and inventory decisions to restrain growth. The net is slightly below trend and weakening, so about 45.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as tight. Greenspan says there is a "significant tightening in private debt markets", "the cost of capital has moved up", and the junk market is "virtually shutting down". McDonough says markets are "no longer functioning very well". That is well above 50.
- TREASURY_STRESS: McDonough describes a "tremendous widening of spreads even in the Treasury market between the on-the-run Treasury securities and the other Treasury securities", counterparty lines being cut, and markets feeding on themselves. That is described functioning impairment rather than just yield moves, so it is above 60 but short of severe dysfunction.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level language is clear. The corporate spread opening is "the largest ... in at least a decade", "BAAs are showing their highest spreads since 1991", and junk yields are rising sharply against Treasuries. Greenspan also says AAA yields are about unchanged. The level is wide, so the score is above 50, with the junk market near shut and a middle-market squeeze.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: The only remark is "the decline in the stock market has had an equivalent effect" and "equity prices have gone down". This is price direction without valuation commentary, so I use the default region, nudged up slightly for the decline, with low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: No participant characterizes bank health. Counterparty-line cutting, a squeeze in middle-market lending, and "movement of money out of Latin America" (Guynn) are credit-market and market-function issues, not statements about bank capital or solvency. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: not discussed. N/A. PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: not discussed. All N/A.

- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress, and the evidence says the widest in about a decade. Matches.
- TREASURY_STRESS: higher means more dysfunction, and the evidence is a spread between on-the-run and off-the-run issues with markets "not functioning very well". Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: higher means more hawkish, and the evidence is easing. The score is low. Matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: nearly everyone supports signaling, and then delivering, a rate cut on Sept 29 (25bp preferred by the Chair and Poole, possibly 50bp per McTeer). The lean is clearly dovish and close to unanimous, but it is a modest 25bp move rather than an emergency action. That puts it in the "moderate dovish" band.
- Disagreement: there is no vote and no formal dissent. There are reservations on the domestic case from Broaddus ("a little less strongly"), Stern (domestic policy "may be the only available response"), Guynn and Kelley ("slightly hesitant"). Pianalto flags that Jordan would probably object to revealing an action before full deliberation, and McTeer may argue for 50bp. These are differences of emphasis and tactics. The call is in broad agreement.
- Financial stability emphasis: market functioning, spreads, liquidity, counterparty exposure and global financial turmoil are the central rationale for acting. They rival the economic assessment, and the domestic real economy is explicitly described as still strong.
- Forward guidance: the whole call is about signaling an imminent easing, though the Chairman limits it to "possibilities, not commitments" and Broaddus wants an "open mind". This is a clear easing signal, but only for one meeting ahead.
- Uncertainty: "fear, uncertainty" is described as pervasive, the "international financial maelstrom", a risk of being "well behind the curve", and "Whether we move" is data-dependent ("remains to be seen"). On the other hand, the Chairman is fairly confident in the direction of policy. This is elevated rather than very high.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: N/A.** Evidence: none. Inflation is not discussed by any participant. Direction confirmation: no direction can be stated.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-8 Low.**
- Evidence: Boehne, "the economy is still operating at a relatively high level"; Hoenig, "the domestic economy more generally ... remains relatively strong". Greenspan, "very hard ... to believe that growth above 3 percent is possible".
- Center of gravity: activity is high but beginning to soften, with no explicit gap language.
- Direction: just above neutral, on the "relatively high level" language, with weak textual support.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +-7 Low.**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "it is unusual to be thinking about easing when we have an unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent".
- Center of gravity: a single data point at roughly full employment to tight.
- Direction: at or slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-8 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "anecdotal indications of some softening ... becoming more pervasive but by no means dangerous"; Boehne, "a notable change in sentiment ... spilled out into broader sectors".
- Center of gravity: spending has been holding up, but sentiment and capital-investment and inventory decisions are weakening.
- Direction: slightly below trend and weakening.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 74 +-7 High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "the sharp widening in yield spreads is suggesting significant tightening in private debt markets ... the cost of capital has moved up"; McDonough, "markets are no longer functioning very well".
- Center of gravity: credit and equity conditions have tightened a lot and the junk market has nearly shut.
- Direction: well above neutral (tight).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 66 +-8 Moderate.**
- Evidence: McDonough, "a tremendous widening of spreads even in the Treasury market between the on-the-run Treasury securities and the other Treasury securities"; "people cutting back on counterparty exposure lines".
- Center of gravity: described liquidity impairment in Treasuries, in a flight to safety (Treasury yields have gone down), but not an auction failure or breakdown.
- Direction: above neutral on actual functioning concerns, not on yield moves.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 79 +-7 High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "the opening of spreads in the corporate area is the largest that we have seen in such a short period of time in at least a decade. BAAs are showing their highest spreads since 1991"; "the junk market is virtually shutting down".
- Center of gravity: spreads are at a wide level, with reduced access in the junk and middle-market segments, though AAA yields are about unchanged.
- Direction: well above neutral (wide).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 +-7 Low.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "the decline in the stock market has had an equivalent effect on the equity side"; "equity prices have gone down".
- Center of gravity: a decline is noted with no valuation view.
- Direction: slightly above neutral (modest decline), which is the default region.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A.** The system's health is not characterized (only counterparty-line cutbacks and a middle-market lending squeeze, which are credit-market remarks).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Not discussed.

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 27 +-6 High.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we will be easing our policy next Tuesday"; Parry, "a fairly riskless insurance policy"; McTeer, "it is very likely that I will argue for 1/2 point".
- Center of gravity: near-unanimous support for a 25bp cut at the next meeting, with a hint of 50bp from one president and a few domestic reservations.
- Direction: well on the dovish side of neutral. It is a moderate-dovish consensus, not an emergency or QE-type stance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +-6 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Broaddus, "maybe a little less strongly than some of the others"; Pianalto, "Jerry would raise some objection to revealing an action ... without a full deliberation"; Kelley, "slightly hesitant thoughts".
- Center of gravity: broad agreement, with minor differences in emphasis (global versus domestic rationale, 25bp versus 50bp, and the Jordan reservation about process). There is no vote, no formal dissent, and the exchanges are courteous.
- Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 78 +-8 High.**
- Evidence: McDonough, "evidence that people are reducing their involvement in financial markets ... the market is in one of those periods where it is starting to feed on itself in a rather dangerous way"; Greenspan, "The international financial maelstrom is beginning to have some obvious impacts directly in our markets".
- Center of gravity: market functioning, liquidity and spreads are the main reason for acting, since the domestic real economy is described as relatively strong.
- Direction: attention is high, dominant and close to rivaling the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 15 +-7 Moderate.**
- Evidence: McDonough, "we should not only take action next week but ... a signal ... be given this week"; Greenspan, "I will endeavor to suggest possibilities, not commitments".
- Center of gravity: a deliberate signal of an easing at the next meeting, though hedged as a possibility, with Broaddus wanting an "open mind" and no commitment beyond the next meeting.
- Direction: a clear easing signal, so well below 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +-8 Moderate.**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "this element of disengagement, fear, uncertainty, and the like is really pervasive around the world"; "the probability of such deterioration over the next week is quite small, but its consequences are ... extraordinarily important".
- Center of gravity: the outlook is clouded by global turmoil and market self-reinforcement, but members are fairly confident about the policy direction and see the domestic economy as still strong.
- Direction: elevated, not very high.
