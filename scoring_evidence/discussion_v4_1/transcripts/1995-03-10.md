# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting date 1995-03-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript of a short intermeeting conference call. Staff turns are removed, so the staff briefings (Fisher on foreign exchange, Prell, Kohn, Truman's reply on Argentina) are not in the text, apart from the end of one Truman turn. There is no economic go-around and no policy go-around. The call covers four things: (1) questions on Mexico and Argentina, (2) a possible Treasury/Fed foreign-exchange intervention the following week, (3) the Bankers Trust first-quarter loss, and (4) the Chairman's account of his Capitol Hill testimony. There is no vote and no rate decision.

**Era context.** Early March 1995, after the Mexican peso crisis and during a spell of dollar weakness. The Committee had raised rates several times in recent months. I do not let this override the text. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), McDonough (Vice Chair), Minehan, Hoenig, Melzer, LaWare, Kelley, Blinder, Phillips, McTeer, and Truman (staff, one turn that is mostly filtered). Views on the economy are essentially absent. The substance is views on the dollar, Argentine and Mexican financial stress, and the tactics and merits of intervention.

**Special-topic check.** This is close to the "special-topic / operations" edge case: a conference call on FX intervention with no standard assessment of the US economy or monetary policy. The edge-case rule would make every dimension N/A. Members do discuss the dollar, banking-system strain and financial-market turmoil directly, though. I therefore apply N/A to every dimension that is not discussed at all, and I score the dimensions that members do address (dollar, banking, and the five discussion-specific ones) with Low confidence. This is a judgment call, flagged in ANOMALY_NOTE. A strict "all N/A" reading is also defensible.

**Core economic dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: no member comments on US inflation. N/A.
- OUTPUT_GAP: not discussed. N/A. (Truman's remark that Argentine growth will be slow is staff and about Argentina.)
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: not discussed. N/A.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: not discussed. N/A.
- PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.

**Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Greenspan mentions "chaos in the exchange market", but there is no assessment of US credit availability, spreads or the general tightness of conditions. N/A. The bright-line rule is not triggered because no accommodative/tight language is used.
- TREASURY_STRESS: no Treasury market discussion. N/A. (The "Treasury" in the text is the Treasury Department.)
- CREDIT_SPREADS: not discussed. N/A. Minehan relays a client's nervousness about the Argentine interbank credit market, but this is not about spreads or the US.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: not discussed. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: discussed in two ways.
  - Argentine banking system strain: deposits have fallen $3-4 billion, a roughly $4 billion lender-of-last-resort fund is being assembled, and McDonough relays the authorities' view that the fund is needed to support the banking system. Minehan passes on a client's concern about Argentine banks and US banks in that interbank market.
  - Bankers Trust reports an operating loss of about $125 million. McDonough: "the markets probably will be somewhat concerned about a major money center bank reporting a loss", but "that is not true of any other money center banks located in this city."
  - The Bankers Trust quote about "strong capital reserve position" is the company's own statement, relayed by a member.
  - Direction: participants see banking stress as mildly elevated (an emerging stress pocket, mostly foreign) but the US system as not impaired. That puts the score at roughly the midpoint, slightly above. The bright-line rule is not triggered, since no "sound/well-capitalized" language comes from members themselves. Confidence is Low because the evidence is thin and mixed.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: discussed directly and repeatedly. Participants see the dollar as having weakened or declined and as under pressure. Evidence:
  - Greenspan: "a concern ... to anticipate the need to intervene again."
  - McDonough: "dollar weakness adversely affects the European crosses".
  - Minehan: "a floor to this dollar decline".
  - LaWare and Blinder discuss the previous week's intervention, which had no lasting effect.
  - Kelley: "a strong currency is in our national interest."
  The dollar is below the midpoint (depreciation), so the bright-line rule requires < 50. "Notable weakness; declined; depreciation" maps to 16-30. Some members think the decline has quieted ("things have quieted" - Hoenig; "bouncing back" - Minehan), so I take it toward the upper end of that band.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: no policy action is on the table. Melzer: "I don't mean to suggest that I think any policy actions are appropriate right now." Phillips: going in with intervention would need to be backed by monetary policy, "I don't see us doing that, at least in the next week or so." Greenspan notes that "in the last few months when we have moved, we have usually subsequently moved the rate up as well", which is a historical comment, not a forward lean. Balanced/no stance, so 50. Confidence Low.
- Disagreement: there is no vote and no formal dissent. The differences are tactical, about whether and how to intervene. McDonough explicitly takes "a minority position" against large intervention. Blinder, Hoenig, Kelley and Phillips are skeptical of modest "flag-showing", and Greenspan himself says he is "not enthusiastic". The tone is cordial and there are no sharp exchanges. Disagreement about tactics counts for less than disagreement about economics. Broad agreement with minor differences, 20.
- Financial stability attention: a large share of the call is about Mexican and Argentine financial turmoil, the exchange market, a lender-of-last-resort fund for the Argentine banking system, and the Bankers Trust loss. This is international financial-market stress, not US financial stability, and the format is a call. Moderate attention, around 50. Low confidence, because the era anchor for typical 1993-96 meetings is 1-10 but this call is atypical.
- Forward guidance: none. 50. Low confidence.
- Uncertainty: members state limits on what they know about market reactions and intervention effects. Examples: Greenspan, "hypothetical interventions very difficult to outline"; Melzer, "I'm not sure either ... it is risky business"; Truman, "I would not bet a lot of money"; Greenspan, "Foreign exchange forecasting is notorious". This is uncertainty about market responses and Argentina, not about the macro outlook. Somewhat elevated, 45, with Low confidence.

**Polarity checks.**
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher means more dollar strength. The evidence says the dollar is weak. A low score matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. The evidence is mild stress (Bankers Trust loss, Argentine strain) with no US system impairment. A score slightly above 50 matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS: both N/A, so no divergence.
2. OUTPUT_GAP and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: both N/A.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and CREDIT_SPREADS: both N/A.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: both N/A.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE and the sub-components: all N/A.

**N/A justification.** The macroeconomic dimensions, FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS, TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION and all four inflation sub-components were not raised by participants. The call was confined to FX intervention, Mexico and Argentina, the Bankers Trust loss, and testimony.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** No participant comments on the US economy.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS, TREASURY_STRESS, CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** No assessment of US financial conditions, Treasury markets, spreads or equities. Greenspan's "chaos in the exchange market" is covered under the dollar.

**BANKING_STRESS: 52 ±12 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: McDonough, "the markets probably will be somewhat concerned about a major money center bank reporting a loss ... that is not true of any other money center banks"; Minehan, clients are "very nervous particularly about the interbank credit market" and "concerns ... about Argentine banks but U.S. banks that might be involved".
- Center of gravity: stress is concentrated in Argentine banks and one US bank, and the US system as a whole is not described as impaired.
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is slightly above neutral because of an emerging stress pocket (the Bankers Trust loss and the Argentine strain), partly offset by the absence of any broad US concern.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 27 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: McDonough, "dollar weakness adversely affects the European crosses"; Minehan, "a floor to this dollar decline ... we are bouncing back up from it."
- Center of gravity: members treat the dollar as having declined and as the reason for possible intervention, with some sense that the decline has paused.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because participants describe depreciation and weakness.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 50 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "I don't mean to suggest that I think any policy actions are appropriate right now"; Phillips, backing intervention with monetary policy is something "I don't see us doing ... in the next week or so."
- Center of gravity: no policy action is on the table or advocated, and no tightening or easing bias is expressed.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is at neutral because there is no stated direction for policy.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: McDonough, "I would like to put in a minority position here"; Greenspan, "That's the dilemma that we have with this whole operation."
- Center of gravity: there is no vote and no formal dissent, and the differences are about the tactics of intervention in a cordial exchange.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is low because the differences concern tactics only and no member dissents on policy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 ±15 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: McDonough's extended account of the Argentine lender-of-last-resort fund and the Bankers Trust loss; Greenspan, "through all this chaos in the exchange market".
- Center of gravity: much of the call concerns financial turmoil abroad, the exchange market and bank strain, but it is an intervention call and not a US financial-stability discussion.
- Direction confirmation: attention is moderate because several members address market stress, with the focus on FX and foreign banks.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I haven't come to a conclusion myself as to how we ought to behave next week"; no policy signal is given.
- Center of gravity: no forward signal on rates.
- Direction confirmation: the tone is neutral because no direction is signaled.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 ±12 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "hypothetical interventions very difficult to outline"; Melzer, "I'm not sure either ... it is risky business."
- Center of gravity: members are unsure how markets will react to intervention and how Argentina will hold up, but there is no expressed uncertainty about the US macro outlook.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is somewhat elevated because of repeated admissions that market responses cannot be predicted.

**INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: N/A.** Not discussed.
