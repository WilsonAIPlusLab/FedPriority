# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1994-02-28 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant turns from a short intermeeting telephone conference, not a regular meeting. Staff turns are removed. What remains is the Chairman's opening remarks, a few member questions to the staff, a brief round of comments from some presidents, a G-7 report and a regulatory-consolidation briefing from the Chairman. The member views on conditions and policy total about 1,500 words. There is no formal go-around, no policy vote and no directive. The Chairman says a full go-around will happen at the next meeting. The session is also well under the full-meeting length, so confidence is Low to Moderate throughout.

**2. Era context.** The meeting falls in the early-1994 tightening phase. The text shows a recent 25 bp tightening ("the policy tightening", "even with the 25 basis points"). It also shows a sharp bond-market sell-off, speculation in equities that has been "pricked", and Q4 1993 GDP revised far higher. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Greenspan: the 25 bp move had a bigger effect than expected. It "partially broke the back of an emerging speculation in equities" and pricked an overshoot in bonds. Markets "have been shocked". The expected growth distribution has shifted up. "We're going to have to move again, obviously", but he would let markets settle first. He thinks the rise in long rates is more real than inflation-expectations driven, but says "I don't think we can know".
- Broaddus: policy is still too accommodative, with real short rates "close to zero". He is happy to wait until March but favors another notch ("the risk of not acting and falling behind the curve is greater").
- Jordan: wants the other 1/4 point now. He says the 1/2 point should have been done last time, and that policy is de facto more expansionary given upward forecast revisions.
- McDonough: wait. Markets overreacted, are nervous and oversold. A move now would signal that the Fed fears being behind the curve. "Watchful waiting."
- Parry: an additional move is justifiable, and timing is "not particularly important". He worries about inflation expectations in long rates.
- Minehan: wait. Data are distorted by weather and the earthquake. Locally there are "very weak retail numbers with no pressure on prices", with some manufacturing strength. "In the next few months there has to be some tightening."
- Melzer: the funds rate is "significantly out of position", and the sooner it gets to neutral the better. No compelling need to move on this call. He opposes "talking the market down".
- Hoenig: asks whether the markets will self-correct. He is bothered by the "other shoe" uncertainty.
- Phillips and L. Lindsey: only trivial questions. Dave Lindsey (staff) on monetary aggregates and bank credit is ignored as staff content.

**4. Directional statements for core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation pressure as around or slightly below the midpoint. Minehan sees "no pressure on prices", and nobody reports actual price acceleration. The concern is forward-looking (policy too accommodative, expectations possibly rising). Slightly below 50 on current pressure, with the forward worry holding it near the midpoint: 46. Low confidence because there is little direct inflation discussion.
- OUTPUT_GAP: There is no "above/below potential" language. Growth is expected to be stronger, and policy is said to be "out of position" and too accommodative, which hints at no large slack. Weather distortion blurs the data. 50, wide band, Low.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Only Greenspan's passing remark that "measured labor input" is soft, with weather effects. Little evidence. 47, Low.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Greenspan and Jordan say growth expectations are being revised up. Minehan reports very weak retail locally and some manufacturing strength. Net slightly above trend at the national level: 53, Low.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Greenspan infers "productivity growth probably is going to register a reasonably strong number in the first quarter" from strong GDP and soft labor input. This is a brief member remark. 58, Low.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants describe markets as "shocked", "nervous", and not yet settled. Long-term rates have risen sharply. Greenspan says the long end is imparting "more restraint to this recovery than I think we would have anticipated". Real short rates are described as near zero, but that is policy, not conditions. Conditions are somewhat tighter than neutral: 57.
- TREASURY_STRESS: The long bond market had rapid declines and overshoots, and is described as nervous and oversold. There is no mention of liquidity problems, auction failures or settlement problems. Normal functioning with volatility: 48.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: not discussed. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Greenspan says emerging equity speculation was partly broken. McDonough notes equity losses and oversold markets. This is a mild valuation-concern view that has been partly corrected, so I stay near fair value with a slight tilt toward prior overvaluation now deflated: 46.
- BANKING_STRESS: Regulatory-consolidation discussion is institutional/political, not about bank stress. Staff bank-credit data are ignored. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only the Desk (staff) reports on FX. Members do not discuss the dollar. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** Financial conditions: higher means tighter. The evidence is "shocked markets" and rising long rates imparting restraint, so somewhat tight and a score above 50 is consistent. Inflation expectations: Greenspan thinks the rise is real, not inflation expectations. Parry worries. Net near anchored: 52, Low.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: all who speak accept that more tightening is coming. Greenspan: "we're going to have to move again, obviously." Broaddus, Jordan and Melzer say policy is behind or out of position. The only disagreement is timing (now versus the 3/22 meeting). No one argues for easing. Moderate hawkish, with current action held on the call: 67.
- Disagreement: no vote and no formal dissent. Jordan wants action now and Broaddus would accept it, while McDonough, Minehan and Greenspan prefer to wait. The disagreement is tactical, not about the economics. Broad agreement: 27.
- Financial stability: the pricking of equity speculation, bond market overshoot, shocked and nervous markets, Soros losses, and rumors/leaks are discussed by several participants, but there is no stability-crisis framing. The regulatory consolidation topic is institutional. Limited to moderate: 35.
- Forward guidance: Greenspan signals another move after markets settle, with a fuller review next meeting. Melzer wants "less said and more done". The signal is a clear tightening bias without a date: 70.
- Uncertainty: Greenspan says "I don't think we can know" whether the rise in rates is real or expectations. Data are distorted by weather and the earthquake. Markets are shocked. The tone is elevated but not extreme: 50.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 (no above/below language, within 48-52). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 (> 50; participants describe markets as shocked, with added restraint from the long end, not accommodative). CREDIT_SPREADS N/A. BANKING_STRESS N/A. DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS 48 (no dysfunction described; yield moves are not dysfunction).

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 50 versus LABOR 47: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 50 versus CONSUMPTION 53: within 15. FC and CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads N/A, so no check. FC 57 and TREASURY 48: normal-times pairing, which is consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 46 with expectations 52 and no scored goods/services/housing: consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** CREDIT_SPREADS, BANKING_STRESS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH were not raised by members (only staff). INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES and HOUSING were not discussed by members. Minehan's "no pressure on prices" is a general statement, not a component.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 46 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Minehan, "very weak retail numbers with no pressure on prices... no pressure on prices whatsoever." Broaddus, "falling behind the curve".
- Summary: Current price pressure is not reported as high, and the concern is forward-looking.
- Direction: slightly below neutral because the only direct price report is of no pressure, offset by forward concerns.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Melzer, "we are probably significantly out of position" (policy); Jordan, "overall they'll see 1994 as stronger".
- Summary: no explicit potential language, and growth expectations are revised up from a modest base.
- Direction: at neutral because no above or below potential claim is made.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "at least the measured labor input soft".
- Summary: one passing remark, weather-affected.
- Direction: slightly below neutral on the single soft-labor remark.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "markets are expecting stronger growth than, say, back in November"; Minehan, "very weak retail numbers... some strength in our manufacturing base".
- Summary: national growth expectations are revised up while one District reports weak retail.
- Direction: slightly above neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "productivity growth probably is going to register a reasonably strong number in the first quarter."
- Summary: a single member inference of strong first-quarter productivity.
- Direction: above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 57 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "markets, which clearly have been shocked"; "imparting, through the long end of the market, more restraint to this recovery".
- Summary: rising long-term rates and nervous markets mean somewhat tighter conditions than before.
- Direction: above neutral (tighter) because members describe additional restraint from the long end.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 48 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: McDonough, "very nervous markets... they are oversold"; Greenspan, "tremendously rapid declines in long-term rates... significant overshoot".
- Summary: volatile, but there is no description of impaired functioning.
- Direction: normal functioning with volatility, so near the middle.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: N/A**

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "partially broke the back of an emerging speculation in equities".
- Summary: speculation was emerging and has been partly corrected.
- Direction: roughly fair value to slightly stretched, with no strong concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** (members did not discuss bank health; regulatory topic only).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A** (only Desk staff discussed FX).

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52** (value only)
- Evidence: Parry, "inflationary prospects longer term are not as positive"; Greenspan, "hard to argue that inflation expectations are really beginning to pick up in Europe... more real than inflation expectations".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we're going to have to move again, obviously"; Broaddus, "a case can be made for an immediate action to increase rates another notch"; Jordan, "do the other 1/4 point now".
- Summary: everyone expects more tightening, with disagreement only about whether to go now or at the next meeting.
- Direction: hawkish because no one favors easing and several say policy is behind the curve.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Jordan, "I wish we had gone ahead and done the full 1/2 point"; McDonough, "watchful waiting".
- Summary: tactical timing differences only, with no vote and no disagreement on the outlook.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "We pricked that bubble as well"; McDonough, "very significant losses... nervous markets".
- Summary: market conditions and speculation get real but limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we're going to have to move again"; Minehan, "in the next few months there has to be some tightening".
- Summary: a clear tightening direction without a date, with a review at the next meeting.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +-10 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "I don't think we can know at this particular point"; Minehan, "data as a little suspect given that they are affected so much by weather and the earthquake".
- Summary: data distortion and unsettled markets, with real versus inflation-expectation drivers unknown.
