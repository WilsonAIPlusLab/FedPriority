# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 (V4.1.1 clarifications) — 2006-12-12 transcript

Part 1: omitted in this run

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 62 +-5 (High)**
- Yellen: "underlying trend in core consumer price inflation remains above my comfort zone"; Moskow: "inflation running too high for my taste ... core PCE inflation flat at 2.4 percent"; Lacker: "news on inflation has been disappointing yet again"; Geithner: "underlying inflation still at uncomfortably high levels"; Poole: inflation "most, if not all, FOMC participants believe is too high"; Moskow: "clear consensus that core inflation has been too high". Offsetting: Yellen "news has been pretty good on balance", Pianalto/Mishkin expect gradual drift down.
- Clearly above target and persistent, with some signs of easing: above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Level signals above potential: Yellen "the output gap is more positive than assumed in the Greenbook"; unemployment "½ percentage point below our estimate of the NAIRU"; Kohn "capacity utilization in manufacturing continues to be above its long-term average, suggesting continued pressures in resource markets"; Moskow "could be facing increasing resource pressure at GDP growth rates that were previously viewed as below potential".
- Below-potential language refers mostly to the growth rate: Moskow "growing moderately below potential in '07"; Hoenig "growth to be below potential"; Mishkin "slightly below potential GDP growth"; Bies "running below capacity and below potential growth rates".
- Level language (tight resources, positive gap) governs; growth-rate "below potential" language pulls toward 50. Slightly above neutral; the conflicting bright-line phrases widen the band.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +-5 (High)**
- Yellen: "Labor markets are tight, and jobs are hard to fill"; Mishkin: "the labor markets are very tight"; Fisher: "significant shortage of skilled and semi-skilled labor"; Plosser, Hoenig, Minehan: difficulty finding skilled workers; Barron: "relatively tight labor markets that we've all talked about"; UE 4½ percent.
- Some softening signs (Yellen: Conference Board index "suggesting that labor markets are roughly in balance"; Poole: UPS "not having labor availability issues"; trucker layoffs). Tight, above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +-6 (High)**
- Strong consumption: Warsh "the consumer appears to be quite strong"; Kroszner "amazing persistence of consumer spending"; Lacker "consumer spending keeps chugging along at about 3 percent".
- Weak housing/business investment: Yellen "correction in the housing sector has continued, even sharpening"; "nonresidential investment ... below expectations"; Kohn downward revision "was in business fixed investment"; Fisher/Poole retail softness. Lacker: "Business investment continues to be a source of strength."
- Net roughly at trend, slightly below given the residential contraction.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 47 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: nonfarm output per hour "only about 1½ percent over the past year, well below its trend" but "may overstate the slowing"; Geithner: "productivity growth in the range of 2½ percent for the nonfinancial corporate sector"; Kroszner: "productivity is likely to continue to go forward".
- Measured data somewhat below trend, members doubtful of the slowdown: slightly below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 22 +-6 (High)**
- Moskow: "hard to see current financial conditions as being that restrictive"; Plosser: CEOs said "money was almost free ... plenty of liquidity"; Fisher: capital "readily available"; Warsh: debt markets "incredibly robust", "awash in liquidity"; Bernanke summary: "supportive financial conditions"; Minehan: "more-accommodative financial markets".
- Bright line: accommodative/supportive, so below 50; loose.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Discussion of yield-curve inversion and falling term premiums (Plosser, Stern, Geithner, Kroszner, Mishkin "term premium is extremely low"); Warsh: "Capital markets ... continue to function well". No dysfunction mentioned.
- Normal, quiet functioning: below neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 20 +-6 (High)**
- Moskow: "risk spreads remain narrow"; Minehan: liquidity "keeping spreads tight"; Plosser: "credit spreads show less stress on businesses"; Warsh: "high-yield corporate issuances were at a record", leveraged loans "at a record"; Geithner: "credit spreads are consistent with the view of sustained expansion".
- Warsh's "widening somewhat" is attributed to Board staff and refers to forward spreads (direction only). Stated level is narrow: below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Warsh: "the price-earnings multiples don't look out of whack ... don't make these companies look all that expensive"; LBO "floor valuation" raising prices; Poole/Stern/Lacker/Minehan: equities strong and "rising".
- Valuation view is roughly fair with prices rising: just below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 32 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Bies: "Bank earnings are really, really strong"; "Delinquency rates are really, really low by historical standards"; loan-loss provision "the best in many, many years"; but "Net interest margins ... under significant pressure" and subprime ARM delinquencies jumping, "still not at alarming levels". Barron: consumer "asset quality remained good"; real estate lending pipeline "all but dried up".
- Healthy with minor concerns: below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 32 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Lacker: "odds seem to favor a further depreciation of the dollar"; Barron: "the decline of the dollar"; Kohn: "the weaker dollar"; Stern: "the lower dollar"; Minehan: "a dollar that's declining"; Hoenig: "if that continues to go down".
- Depreciation: below 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 52**
- Poole: Wal-Mart basket prices "up about 1.1 percent", "does not see much inflation pressure"; Plosser: prices paid and received "continued to climb"; Moskow: further cost pass-throughs from petroleum inputs; Kohn: commodity prices "continued to climb". Roughly stable with mild cost pressure.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 57**
- Fisher: "accelerating medical care prices"; Bernanke: medical costs "more than 7 percent on an annualized basis" in the latest month, a risk. Modest upward pressure.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 47**
- Kroszner: owners' equivalent rent "is now coming down"; Pianalto: "declines in shelter costs"; Bernanke: "the slowing of rents". Fisher notes a slight OER pickup. Slight easing.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52**
- Lacker: "anchored between 2 and 2¼ percent"; Plosser: "inflation expectations have remained stable"; Kohn: long-term expectations "remain within their ranges"; Geithner: TIPS expectations "have eased a bit"; Bernanke: five-by-five "has come down about 20 basis points". Anchored, slightly above target level.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +-4 (High)**
- Unanimous-except-one support to hold at 5¼ percent with the firming bias retained ("the Committee judges that some inflation risks remain ... additional firming"); Kohn: "interest rates are more likely to rise than to fall"; Hoenig: "prepared to support additional tightening"; Lacker: "economy could theoretically handle a marginally higher fed funds rate" (dissent). Dovish pull: Poole "strongly favor a symmetrical balance-of-risk statement"; Yellen: downside risk "intensified".
- Hold with tightening bias: lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-5 (High)**
- Vote read-out: 10 Yes, 1 No (President Lacker, favoring alternative C / firmer stance). Poole voted Yes but asked "that the minutes explain that at least one member strongly favored a balanced risk assessment." Other differences are about statement wording (alternatives B/C, "than anticipated").
- One dissent plus one strong reservation, otherwise broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Liquidity/risk-taking raised by several: Fisher ("a potential trip wire"), Bies (MBS/CDO risk, "fraud rate on mortgages has tripled", "tremendous amount of liquidity"), Warsh (extended liquidity discussion, PIK notes), Plosser, Moskow, Minehan (CRE "may be a sign of real overheating"), Chairman's question on liquidity; Pianalto: households "pretty fragile", subprime resets.
- Moderate discussion by several participants, secondary to the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +-5 (High)**
- Statement retains "the extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed"; Kohn, Yellen, Mishkin, Geithner, Bernanke keep section 4 unchanged; Bernanke: "our willingness to raise rates if inflation doesn't moderate". Poole and Pianalto argue the firming signal is stale; section 2 softened to "recent indicators have been mixed".
- Mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Yellen: "pretty challenging to read the tea leaves", "sizable risks on both sides", "a great deal of uncertainty about inflation"; Geithner: "considerable uncertainty about the outlook for growth"; Moskow: "Our uncertainty about the outlook has increased somewhat"; Kroszner: "much data and relatively little information"; Bernanke: "significant uncertainties in the forecast". Mishkin: "a bit greater uncertainty, not a whole lot"; Stern and Lacker fairly confident.
- Somewhat elevated, not unusual.
