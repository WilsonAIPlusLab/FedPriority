# v4.1 Scoring: Member Discussion Transcript, Meeting 2002-05-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening questions to staff (members' own questions only), the District-by-District economic go-around (Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Jordan, Santomero, Guynn, Broaddus, Stern, McTeer, Hoenig, Poole, McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Bies, Olson), questions on the policy presentation, Chairman Greenspan's summary and recommendation, the round of "agree" statements, and a vote read-out (all listed members Yes). Staff turns are removed. Procedural and banter passages (minutes approval, currency arrangements, forward-index exchange, jokes) are ignored. The staff-quoted wording of the directive is procedure, not a member view. The text reads cleanly; no garbled passages affect scoring.

**Era context.** Spring 2002: recovery from a mild recession, funds rate 1.75 percent, first-quarter GDP boosted by inventories, strong measured productivity, 6 percent unemployment. Scoring rests on what members say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Moskow: recovery with mixed messages; growth near potential; "we begin moving soon" on policy but "do not have to move today"; the first hike will be easy to explain.
- Parry: recovery taking hold; core PCE 1.5 percent or lower, "little evidence of inflation pressures outside the energy sector"; productivity an upside risk; equities "overvalued" by almost every model; breathing room on timing of hikes.
- Minehan: stabilizing; "zero real interest rates are too low for an expanding economy", but low inflation, so "waiting and reflecting"; nervous markets offset policy stimulus; event risk.
- Jordan: natural rate of interest rising; 1.75 percent "not consistent with a nonaccelerating rate of inflation"; the current stance is "reinflationary"; businesses expect tightening. Does not explicitly demand a move today.
- Santomero: recovery under way; "first and inevitable step" toward neutrality; weak labor market and benign inflation give time; dollar depreciation noted.
- Guynn: rates "unsustainably low", but preemptive tightening now "risky"; time to wait.
- Broaddus: unemployment 6 percent, payroll +43,000, disinflation in core PCE, unit labor costs falling; "substantial downside risks"; no move toward tighter policy today, but not arguing for easing bias.
- Stern: anecdotes improved; inflation outlook satisfactory; growth satisfactory.
- McTeer: Greenbook credible; risks balanced; bias dilemma lies in the future.
- Hoenig: the most hawkish voice; real funds rate near zero, risks of inflation and "financial excess" later; still goes along with holding.
- Poole: business tone softer than the GDP number; no real improvement until later in the year.
- McDonough: gloom in New York business circles; grim Wall Street mood; liquidity and market-making concerns; tighter bank credit standards, steady to lower delinquencies.
- Ferguson ("sit tight"), Gramlich (scared off a positive bias; neither raise nor lower), Bies (no change needed; excess capacity, inflation well under 2 percent; longer-term worry that productivity will slow and rates will have to rise), Olson (fiscal discipline eroding).
- Greenspan: "virtually everybody" wants to stay at 1.75 percent with balanced risks; outlook close to the Greenbook; terrorist risk the biggest risk; "a lot of uncertainty"; "I'm still worried".

**Core dimensions (direction in words first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Evidence: Parry "little evidence of inflation pressures outside the energy sector", core PCE 1.5 percent; Broaddus sees disinflation; Bies "well under 2 percent"; Stern "little anticipation of an acceleration"; Hoenig "stable or even slightly lower core". Offsets: Santomero sees early firming of industrial prices, and Jordan and Hoenig flag later risks. "Contained/modest/below 2 percent" fits 26-40. Score 36.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Evidence: Minehan "unemployment remaining above our estimate of the natural rate"; Bies "level of excess capacity"; Hoenig "small output gap"; Parry "capacity to spare"; Moskow sees growth close to potential from here. "Slack remains" fits 31-40. Score 38. Bright line (below potential means under 50) is satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Evidence: Broaddus "unemployment rate up to 6 percent", payroll +43,000, softening compensation; Hoenig "labor markets are generally slack"; Guynn "IT wages more flexible downward". Offsets: temp-help demand and some hiring (Moskow, Jordan, Hoenig). UE of 6 percent falls in the 31-45 band. Score 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Mixed, net slightly below trend. Consumers and housing are described as holding up or strong (Stern, Parry, Bies "mainstay", Broaddus PCE 3.5 percent), while business capital spending is weak (Moskow "weak", Bies "lack of business investment for over eighteen months", Greenspan "uncertain outlook for capital goods"). Net 44.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend. Evidence: Gramlich "eye-popping productivity number... 8.6 percent"; Broaddus underlying trend "at or above the 2½ to 3 percent"; Hoenig "remarkable strength in productivity". Qualifiers: Parry says a good deal is temporary, and Bies fears a later slowdown. Score 70.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Near neutral. Mentions of "accommodative" refer to monetary policy, not market conditions (the rubric says to score conditions). Market-side comments lean cautious: Minehan "negative tone of markets acts to offset the stimulus"; McDonough "skittish, nervous feel about financial markets" and tighter bank credit standards; Parry says markets reacted negatively. Offsets: policy is stimulative and Hoenig sees "financial excess". Score 52, with a wide band. Bright line: no member describes conditions themselves as accommodative or tight, so no constraint is triggered.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Greenspan's questions on swap spreads and Treasury supply concern yield levels, not dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No member characterises the level of credit spreads. Greenspan's AAA/swap query is a question, not a view, and McDonough's "tighter credit standards" concerns bank lending. N/A.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Parry says "almost every valuation model suggests that equity markets are overvalued" and calls the stock market a downside risk. McDonough and Minehan describe a grim mood rather than a valuation view. Overvaluation concern points to modestly above fair value, so below 50 on this polarity. Score 42, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Only McDonough, who reports "tighter credit standards, and steady to lower delinquency rates", plus a liquidity worry about brokerages. Healthy with minor concerns. Score 38, Low confidence. Bright line (healthy means under 50) is satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar seen as depreciating. Evidence: Greenspan "coming into a period of dollar erosion"; Santomero "If the dollar continues to depreciate"; Parry on the 3 percent per year depreciation assumption. Score 35. Bright line (depreciation means under 50) is satisfied.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS 45: industrial prices firming slightly (Santomero), but "no pricing power" (Hoenig, Bies, Greenspan "pricing power... eroding"), with energy up.
- SERVICES 52: insurance and medical costs rising about 20 percent (Poole, Stern, Santomero), offset by soft labor compensation.
- HOUSING 62: strong demand, "multiple bids" and prices above asking (Parry, Minehan, Santomero "pushed up house prices"), with some softening in segments (Guynn, McDonough).
- EXPECTATIONS 47: credibility for low inflation intact (Broaddus, Hoenig); no unanchoring signal.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: all recommend holding at 1.75 percent with a balanced-risks statement. Many say tightening will be needed ("inevitable," "unsustainably low," "begin moving soon"), but none wants it today and none wants an easing bias (Broaddus explicitly not). A balanced hold with an eventual tightening expectation gives about 52.
- Dissent: unanimous vote, zero dissents, no reservations. Differences are of emphasis (Hoenig and Jordan on inflation risk versus Broaddus and Gramlich on downside risk). Score 14.
- Financial stability: limited. McDonough on Wall Street and liquidity, Minehan on market tone, Parry on equities, Hoenig on "financial excess", with brief mentions elsewhere. Score 28.
- Forward guidance: the statement stays balanced, but the shared expectation is that the next move is up and a bias dilemma lies ahead (McTeer, Moskow, Santomero). Gramlich dropped the idea of a positive bias for now. Mild tightening lean, 55.
- Uncertainty: widespread talk of "mixed signals", the "puzzle", an "extraordinary dilemma", and Greenspan's "a lot of uncertainty" and "I'm still worried". Several note scenario ranges (Ferguson's three scenarios). The language is elevated but not extreme. Score 52.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE means more pressure; evidence says contained, so under 50, matches. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter; evidence says slack, so under 50, matches. EQUITY_VALUATION: overvaluation concern is low-score by rubric table (1-45); 42 matches. DOLLAR: depreciation, so under 50, matches. HAWKISH_DOVISH: a hold with a tightening-eventually tone sits slightly above 50.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs LABOR 34: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 38 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 52 with CREDIT_SPREADS N/A: no contradiction. TREASURY_STRESS N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 vs sub-components (45, 52, 62, 47): the parent is lower than the sub-average, because sub-component scoring reflects selective upward items (housing, insurance) while the members' headline view is core inflation contained and falling; difference noted, headline retained.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS and CREDIT_SPREADS: not characterised by any member (only chair questions on swap spreads).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "We see little evidence of inflation pressures outside the energy sector, with the core PCE price index expected to come in at 1½ percent or slightly lower"; Broaddus, "there is already evidence of disinflation in the works". Center of gravity: core inflation low and edging down, with a few early price firming signs. Below neutral because inflation is below target and contained.

**OUTPUT_GAP 38 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "unemployment remaining above our estimate of the natural rate"; Bies, "Given the level of excess capacity and the lack of pricing power". Center of gravity: modest slack that is expected to narrow. Below neutral because members describe excess capacity and unemployment above the natural rate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 34 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Broaddus, "driving the unemployment rate up to 6 percent... softening labor compensation"; Hoenig, "The labor markets are generally slack". Center of gravity: elevated unemployment and weak hiring despite temp-help pickup. Below neutral because UE is 6 percent with slack described.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Bies, "the consumer is the mainstay"; Moskow, "capital spending plans by firms in the District are best described as weak". Center of gravity: solid consumers and housing offset by weak investment. Slightly below neutral because investment weakness has more weight in the discussion.

**PRODUCTIVITY 70 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Gramlich, "eye-popping productivity number for the first quarter, now pegged at 8.6 percent"; Broaddus, the underlying trend "may be at or above the 2½ to 3 percent". Center of gravity: strong productivity widely recognised, partly cyclical. Above neutral because members see productivity well above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 52 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Minehan, "the negative tone of markets acts to offset the stimulus of monetary and fiscal policy"; McDonough, "a skittish, nervous feel about financial markets". Center of gravity: market-side caution roughly offsets accommodative policy. Slightly above neutral because of the nervous market tone and tighter bank standards.

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** Treasury market functioning not discussed by members (yield and supply questions only).

**CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.** No member states a spread level or move.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 42 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Parry, "almost every valuation model suggests that equity markets are overvalued"; McDonough, "The mood on Wall Street is very grim". Center of gravity: overvaluation concern from one member with a gloomy sentiment backdrop. Below neutral on this polarity because the stated valuation view is overvaluation.

**BANKING_STRESS 38 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: McDonough, bankers report "tighter credit standards, and steady to lower delinquency rates". Center of gravity: system sound, with a liquidity worry about brokerage market-making. Below neutral because the one direct bank report is healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 35 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Santomero, "If the dollar continues to depreciate, import growth could be weaker"; Greenspan, "If we're coming into a period of dollar erosion". Center of gravity: modest dollar depreciation. Below neutral because depreciation is described.

**INFLATION_GOODS 45; INFLATION_SERVICES 52; INFLATION_HOUSING 62; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 47.** Evidence: Hoenig, manufacturers "don't have the pricing power"; Poole, health and casualty insurance premiums up about 20 percent; Santomero, "strong demand has pushed up house prices"; Broaddus, "our current credibility for low inflation".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 52 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "stay at 1¾ percent on the funds rate and retain the statement that we view the risks as balanced"; Guynn, "rates currently are unsustainably low and... will soon need to begin to rise. The question is whether now is the time". Center of gravity: a unanimous hold, with tightening expected later and no easing appetite. Slightly above neutral for the tightening-eventually tone.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 14 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "I sense from virtually everybody a desire to stay where we are"; all roll-call members Yes. Center of gravity: unanimous with emphasis differences only. Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 28 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: McDonough, "if the brokerage firms get into the bomb shelters... that would adversely affect liquidity"; Hoenig, "some financial excess, which in my view we need to keep in mind". Center of gravity: a few members raise markets and liquidity as context, not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 55 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "the well-known lags of monetary policy require that we begin moving soon"; McTeer, "we could well come to the point of needing to tighten policy a bit before we reach the stage where an inflationary bias statement is justified". Center of gravity: balanced statement now, next move expected up. Mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 52 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "there's a lot of uncertainty, understandably"; Ferguson, "at today's meeting we aren't really sure". Center of gravity: repeated emphasis on mixed signals and scenario ranges without extreme language. Elevated.
