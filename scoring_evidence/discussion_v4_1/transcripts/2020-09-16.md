# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 2020-09-16 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It holds the opening tributes, members' questions in the staff Q&A sessions, the economic go-around (day 1), the policy go-around (day 2), Chair Powell's closing remarks, and a short roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed, so the staff answers are missing and some member questions refer to answers I cannot see. I score only what members say.

**2. Era context.** The document comes from the pandemic recovery in September 2020. It is the first meeting after the new framework statement (flexible average inflation targeting). The policy choice is among three statement alternatives (A, B, C) on forward guidance and asset-purchase language. The funds rate stays at 0-1/4 percent. I let the text drive the scores, not what I know about the period.

**3. Speakers.**
- Clarida, Bullard, Daly, Brainard, Bowman, Quarles, Williams, Powell, George, Harker and Barkin describe a strong rebound that still leaves a deep hole.
- Rosengren and Bostic stress headwinds, structural change and dispersion.
- Rosengren, Mester, Kaplan and Barkin raise financial stability costs of prolonged near-zero rates.
- Policy preferences:
  - Alt B: Clarida, Bullard, Harker (with reservations), Daly, Mester (reluctantly), George, Bowman, Brainard, Quarles, Williams and Powell.
  - Alt C: Rosengren, Bostic, Barkin and Kaplan.
  - Alt A: Evans, who is not a voter, and Kashkari, who wants even stronger guidance.
- Roll call: eight yes, two no (Kaplan, Kashkari).

**4. Core dimensions (direction first).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (below target). Powell says "overall inflation is running well below our 2 percent objective". Kashkari cites core PCE at about 1.3 percent. Barkin says inflation is "still subdued, has rebounded". Several note upside surprises, used cars, lumber and durables, so it is not deeply low. Score about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below the midpoint. Powell says the economy is "in a deep hole" and GDP is about 4 percent below pre-pandemic levels. Daly says "a very deep hole". Recovery is under way, so it is not catastrophic. Score about 30.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Below the midpoint. Unemployment is 8.4 percent, payrolls are about 11.5-12 million below pre-pandemic, and Powell says U-3 understates the problem by about 3 points. Some firms report trouble hiring, but that is a minor offset. Score about 24 (UE > 7 percent anchor).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Slightly below the midpoint on net. Goods spending is above pre-pandemic levels, housing and autos are strong, and equipment investment is rebounding. Services are depressed, structures are weak, and members flag fiscal-cliff risk. Level is below trend with very strong momentum. Score about 43.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Touched only briefly. Quarles says supply frictions "hinder productivity". Barkin and Mester mention childcare costs to productivity. George links business formation to productivity. Modestly below trend, thin evidence. Score about 42, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (accommodative). Brainard says "overall financing conditions are accommodative". Kaplan says they are "highly accommodative". Powell says "highly accommodative financial conditions". Offsets are tight bank lending and a SLOOS index at crisis-level restrictiveness, plus small-business strain. Score about 34.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Low. Bostic says "U.S. Treasury securities market functioning is back to pre-COVID levels". Williams says market functioning indicators "continue to be very positive". Yield moves alone are not dysfunction. Score about 28.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spread levels are not discussed directly. Credit availability is: Brainard says capital market conditions are "very supportive" with very strong debt issuance, and Quarles says issuance is robust even for speculative grade. Against that, bank lending is tight, small-business credit quality is deteriorating, and the spec-grade default rate is above 12 percent. No stated level of spreads. Score slightly below normal, 40, low confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Valuation concern is elevated, so below 50. Kaplan says contacts use "bubble," "distorted," "artificial". Rosengren fears asset prices "gotten way too frothy". Mester notes recent tech-led equity volatility. Brainard calls markets "buoyant". Score about 32.
- BANKING_STRESS: Slightly below the midpoint. Banks are currently healthy:
  - Bowman reports bankers see "record low delinquencies" on consumer loans.
  - Mester reports delinquencies "lower than ever".
  - Bowman says banks have "ample resources".
  
  Forward concerns offset this:
  - Rosengren on CRE and small and medium banks, and capital buffers.
  - Brainard on stress in hotel and retail CRE.
  - Quarles on rising business-loan delinquencies.
  
  Score about 44.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only Brainard states it: "the broad value of the dollar has declined modestly and is now close to its pre-pandemic level". Modest weakness, so below 50. Score about 38, low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION means more pressure, and the evidence says below target, so the score is below 50. Higher OUTPUT_GAP means more above potential, and the evidence says below, so below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS means tighter, and the evidence says slack, so below 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS means tighter, and the evidence says accommodative, so below 50. Higher EQUITY_VALUATION means more stress or concern, and here the concern is overvaluation, which the table places at 16-30, so the score is below 50. Higher BANKING_STRESS means more stress, and banks are described as currently healthy with forward concerns, so just below 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: dovish. The funds rate stays at the lower bound, the Committee adopts strong outcome-based guidance, and asset purchases continue at least at the current pace. The majority support maximum accommodation. Rosengren, Bostic, Barkin and Kaplan prefer a weaker statement, but on tactics and costs. They still want accommodation, and Kaplan and Rosengren expect to be at the lower bound for years. Score about 22.
- Disagreement: two formal dissents (Kaplan, Kashkari) in opposite directions. Several others prefer alternatives C or A. Harker and Mester are reluctant yes votes. Disagreement is about guidance design and financial stability, not the economic assessment. The tone is collegial. Score about 55.
- Financial stability attention: prominent. The knockout clause, search for yield, CRE and leverage are discussed by Rosengren, Mester, Kaplan, Barkin, Brainard, Bullard, Kashkari, Quarles and Powell. Financial conditions also appear in the economic go-around. Score about 62.
- Forward guidance: the adopted guidance holds the range until maximum employment is reached, inflation reaches 2 percent and is on track to moderately exceed 2 percent. This is threshold-based and the strongest form of easing commitment, partly tempered by the paragraph 5 escape clause. Score about 8.
- Uncertainty: very high. Bullard says "exceptionally high". Powell says "extraordinarily elevated". Mester says "very high". Kashkari says "continued high uncertainty". The baseline outlook improved and tail risks receded, which keeps this below the extreme band. Score about 72.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 (below potential, below 50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 34 (accommodative, below 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 40 (no stated high level, so not above 50). BANKING_STRESS 44 (healthy, below 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 38 (modest decline, below 50). TREASURY_STRESS 28 (no dysfunction). All satisfied.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs LABOR 24 differ by 6 (fine). OUTPUT_GAP 30 vs CONSUMPTION 43 differ by 13 (under 15; consumption level is closer to trend while the gap reflects the whole economy). FC 34 and CREDIT_SPREADS 40 co-move loosely (bank credit tight offsets). FC 34 and TREASURY 28 are consistent. INFLATION_PRESSURE 32 vs sub-components (goods 62, services 35, housing 40, expectations 40) is consistent given that Powell and others say broad inflation is well below target and the goods strength is narrow.

**9. N/A.** None of the financial dimensions is N/A. The scores for PRODUCTIVITY, CREDIT_SPREADS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH rest on thin evidence and carry low confidence.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Powell: "overall inflation is running well below our 2 percent objective." Kashkari: "core PCE inflation running at around 1.3 percent."
- Summary: inflation is below target, with some rebound and narrow upside surprises.
- Direction: below neutral because members describe inflation as subdued and well short of 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +-7 [High]**
- Powell: "the economy remains fragile... and remains in a deep hole." Daly: "we remain in a very deep hole."
- Summary: GDP and employment are well below pre-pandemic levels despite a fast rebound.
- Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 +-6 [High]**
- Powell: "Unemployment is far too high... the U-3 numbers understate the problem by about 3 percentage points." Quarles: unemployment "dropped... to 8.4 percent."
- Summary: large slack, millions still out of work, with pockets of hiring difficulty.
- Direction: below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +-8 [High]**
- Powell: "consumer spending has retraced 75 percent of its pandemic-related decline. Goods spending is now actually above pre-pandemic levels." Brainard: "Interest-sensitive sectors are rebounding strongly... investment on nonresidential structures remains depressed."
- Summary: strong rebound in goods, housing and equipment, offset by depressed services and structures and fiscal-cliff risk.
- Direction: just below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 +-10 [Low]**
- Quarles: "supply-side frictions linger and hinder productivity through the projection horizon." Mester: childcare disruption could "constrain labor supply and productivity."
- Summary: brief mentions of productivity drags.
- Direction: modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 34 +-7 [High]**
- Brainard: "Overall financing conditions are accommodative." Kaplan: "financial conditions are highly accommodative."
- Summary: broadly accommodative, with tight bank lending and small-business strain as offsets.
- Direction: below neutral (accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 28 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Bostic: "U.S. Treasury securities market functioning is back to pre-COVID levels." Williams: "Indicators of market functioning continue to be very positive."
- Summary: market functioning is normal.
- Direction: below neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +-10 [Low]**
- Brainard: "For large businesses, capital market conditions are very supportive, and their debt issuance has been very strong. But bank lending to businesses remains relatively tight." Quarles: "Net issuance of corporate bonds has been robust... even for speculative-grade firms."
- Summary: ample capital-market credit for large firms, offset by tight bank and small-business credit. No explicit spread level.
- Direction: somewhat below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 32 +-10 [Moderate]**
- Kaplan: contacts "using adjectives like 'bubble,' 'distorted,' 'artificial,' and 'manipulated'." Rosengren: "a world in which asset prices have gotten way too frothy."
- Summary: valuation and froth concern, with some sell-off volatility.
- Direction: elevated valuations (below 50 on this scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 44 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Bowman: "record low delinquencies on their consumer loan portfolios." Rosengren: "I am particularly worried about a 'second shoe' dropping that will particularly affect small and medium-sized banks."
- Summary: banks are healthy now, with forward CRE and small-bank concerns.
- Direction: just below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 +-8 [Low]**
- Brainard: "the broad value of the dollar has declined modestly and is now close to its pre-pandemic level."
- Summary: one explicit mention of a modest decline.
- Direction: below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 62.** Brainard: "recent strong increases in durable goods prices appear to reflect strong demand." George: lumber and used-car prices up sharply.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 35.** Powell: "weaker demand, especially in sectors that have been most affected by the pandemic, has held down consumer prices."

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 40.** Rosengren: CoStar data show "fairly sharp declines in rents in several major cities."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40.** Brainard: market-based measures "rose notably... although they remain at the lower end of their typical ranges." Bullard: TIPS breakevens "are still too low."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 22 +-6 [High]**
- Clarida: "I support alternative B as written." Quarles: "This is a highly accommodative policy change that's appropriate for an economy that's still in a deep recession."
- Summary: the funds rate stays at the lower bound with strong guidance and continued purchases. Alternative C advocates (Rosengren, Bostic, Barkin, Kaplan) differ on tactics, not on the need for accommodation.
- Direction: strongly dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 55 +-7 [High]**
- Roll call: Kaplan No, Kashkari No. Mester: "I'll support the Chair and vote for alternative B." Rosengren: "I would much prefer option C."
- Summary: two opposite-direction dissents and several reluctant supporters or non-voters preferring other options, in a collegial tone.
- Direction: notable disagreement, mainly over guidance design.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [High]**
- Rosengren: "There is not a serious financial stability 'knockout' clause in the statement." Kashkari asks for "some kind of a rule or guidepost" on financial stability decisions.
- Summary: a prominent theme in the policy go-around, with attention to search for yield, CRE and leverage.
- Direction: attention is high, not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 +-4 [High]**
- Clarida: alternative B provides "threshold guidance... until labor market conditions have returned to levels consistent with our assessment of maximum employment and until PCE inflation has returned to 2 percent and is also on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time."
- Summary: threshold-based commitment to the lower bound, tempered only by a paragraph 5 escape clause.
- Direction: strongly easing signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 +-6 [High]**
- Bullard: "macroeconomic uncertainty remains exceptionally high." Powell: "Uncertainty remains extraordinarily elevated."
- Summary: nearly every participant stresses uncertainty about the virus, fiscal policy and the elections, though the baseline outlook improved.
- Direction: high.
