# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - transcript discussion, meeting of 2009-12-16

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion), two-day meeting. It has four parts: (a) members' questions during the staff briefing on balance-sheet exit tools and facilities (staff answers removed); (b) the economic go-around; (c) the Chair's summary of the go-around plus the policy go-around on statement alternatives B and C; (d) a special-topic session on inflation dynamics and output gaps, which is mostly methodological, and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed, so many Chair lines read as one half of an exchange. I found no garbled passages that change meaning.

**2. Era context.** Participants speak of a recovery from a deep financial-crisis recession. The November payroll report lost only 11,000 jobs and unemployment is about 10 percent. Policy is the zero bound with "extended period" guidance, and the large-scale asset purchase program is winding down at end Q1 2010. I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Doves: Yellen and Kohn (further easing would be warranted if feasible), Rosengren, Evans, Pianalto, Lockhart, Dudley, Tarullo (support B and the extended-period language).
- Hawkish tilt: Plosser, Hoenig, Kocherlakota, Lacker, and Fisher on the "for some time" wording. They favor preparing the market to reduce accommodation or shrinking the balance sheet.
- Bullard: an active, data-responsive balance-sheet strategy that can run both ways.
- Bernanke: alternative B, no tightening and no strong tightening signal, but signal normalization through the facility wind-down.
- Warsh and Duke: support B. Warsh stresses froth and sovereign risk; Duke gives a detailed account of banks and small-business credit.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the 2 percent objective and subdued. Rosengren: "little evidence of inflationary pressures". Yellen: "inflation is undesirably low". Evans: core is "below my guideline of 2 percent". Kohn says market-based core PCE is about 1¼ percent. Offsetting this, Plosser sees "greater upside risk in the medium term", and Kocherlakota and Lacker note tail risk or a weakening disinflation case. The Chair calls inflation "reasonably controlled". This is below 50, about 32.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see output well below potential. Evans: "resource slack remains large". Kocherlakota: output "well below its trend level". Yellen: "far below maximum sustainable employment". Several (Lacker, Plosser, Bullard) dispute how large or how relevant the gap is, but nobody puts it at or above potential. This is below 50, about 25.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see a very slack labor market with unemployment near 10 percent. Evans: "unemployment rate is at 10 percent". Rosengren: "double-digit unemployment". Kocherlakota: employment-to-population ratio "at a 25-year low". Job losses are slowing, but the level is the anchor. This is below 50, about 17.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as improving but below trend. Consumer spending "has picked up more than expected" (Plosser) and capital equipment spending is edging up (Kohn). Business investment and hiring are held back by caution and policy uncertainty. Small-firm capex is at "record low levels" (Fisher). Net: below trend but recovering, about 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by several. Yellen mentions "productivity figures so spectacularly" boosted, Warsh calls the productivity surge "more impressive than I thought", and Kohn and Bernanke note strong gains and falling unit labor costs. The Chair expects the gains to fade. Above trend, about 66.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as eased and improving for markets and large firms (Evans: "eased further"; Warsh: "melting up"). For bank-dependent small firms credit remains tight, bank lending is contracting, and securitization such as CMBS is "still not functioning well". Net: somewhat loose to neutral, below 50, about 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning is not described as a problem. Treasuries appear only in balance-sheet and rate-level remarks. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: The mix is TED spreads "returned to normal" and MBS spreads "near historical lows" (Kocherlakota) or "well below historic norms" (Lacker). Fisher says "key spreads have continued to narrow". Kohn says securities markets are open "at still somewhat elevated risk premiums" and bank-loan spreads "widened further". Level reading: near normal overall, agency spreads below normal, bank-loan spreads wide. About 46, with moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Equity prices have risen (Yellen, Kohn). Evans reports a "chorus of concerns regarding frothiness" but calls the warnings "premature". Warsh worries about "old money chasing old products". The Chair says "asset values do not seem out of line with fundamentals". Roughly fair with mild froth concern, about 44.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see continuing stress. Rosengren: bank assets in low-rated institutions "continues to grow". Bullard: CRE "remains a huge concern". Duke: small banks' problems "are still building". The Chair expects "many are still likely to fail". Large banks are repaying TARP. Emerging-to-significant stress, above 50, about 62.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: The dollar fell through much of the period, and Plosser notes "a more rapid decline in the dollar". In the last ten days it reversed. Warsh: "A stronger dollar, commodities a bit weaker". Bernanke says good US news is now good for the dollar. Mixed with a recent rebound. About 47, with low confidence.
- INFLATION sub-components: GOODS is partly discussed (Fisher on soft-goods price concessions and possible reversal; oil and commodity moves; import prices). SERVICES is N/A. HOUSING (shelter) is N/A. EXPECTATIONS is stable and anchored, with risk premiums and dispersion up, about 47.

**5. Polarity verification.**
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means tighter. Evidence is 10 percent unemployment, so low. Matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher means more stress. Evidence is CRE problems and expected failures, so above 50. Matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. Evidence on level is mixed and roughly normal, so near 46. Matches.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: higher means stronger dollar. Longer-term weakening and recent strengthening net slightly under 50.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The vote covers alternative B, which holds at 0 to ¼ percent, keeps "extended period", completes the purchase program, and winds down liquidity facilities. Most want to keep accommodation. Yellen and Kohn say more easing is warranted in principle. Lockhart: "premature to reduce stimulus". A hawkish minority (Plosser, Hoenig, Kocherlakota, Lacker) wants earlier signaling of exit. The Chair: "we should not, at this point, either tighten or strongly signal a tightening". Because this is a late-QE meeting and the majority favors maintained maximum accommodation, I score moderately dovish.
- Dissent: The roll call is 10 yes, 0 no. Open reservations exist: Plosser, Hoenig, Kocherlakota, Lacker and Fisher on wording and balance-sheet language. Several (Fisher, Lockhart, Lacker, Pianalto) were also uneasy about the primary-credit-rate proposal. Yellen and Plosser differ on where real rates are heading. That is differences in emphasis and tactics, not formal dissents.
- Financial stability: Bank credit, CRE, small banks, exit from facilities, CMBS/TALF, sovereign risk (Dubai, Greece), froth and complacency all get meaningful attention from several participants, but economic assessment and balance-sheet exit mechanics dominate. Moderate.
- Forward guidance: "Extended period" is kept. The Chair wants no tightening signal but a signal of "normalization" through the facility wind-down. Plosser and Hoenig push for "some time". Overall a low-for-long signal with exit preparation, so easing side.
- Uncertainty: Frequently cited. Bullard: "Uncertainty is high" and current policy is "completely unprecedented". Tarullo: "uncharted waters". Pianalto: businesses find the environment "so alien". The Chair: "walking on eggshells". Yellen says it is "high". The central growth forecast is still moderate and several are fairly confident. Elevated but not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 25: below potential, below 50. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42: eased/"melting up" for markets, below 50. The tight small-bank credit keeps it from going lower. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 46: no "elevated/wide" level is stated overall (Kohn notes "somewhat elevated risk premiums" and wider bank loan spreads); other speakers state normal or low levels. Just under 50 is defensible, with moderate confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS 62: stressed/troubled, above 50. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH 47: mixed; the recent move is appreciation and the longer move depreciation. The score sits close to neutral, so no bright line is clearly broken. Low confidence.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 25 vs LABOR 17: gap of 8, consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP 25 vs CONSUMPTION 38: gap of 13, under the 15-point threshold. Spending is recovering faster than the gap closes.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 46: close, consistent.
- FC vs TREASURY: Treasury stress is N/A, and FC is not crisis-level, so fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 32 vs sub-components: expectations 47 and goods 42 sit above the parent because actual inflation is below target while expectations are anchored. Parent weighted toward the actual low inflation prints.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not discussed by participants. INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: no member discussion of services or shelter price components (only passing remarks on nonmarket prices).

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-7 [Moderate-High, stated as Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "inflation is undesirably low". Rosengren: "little evidence of inflationary pressures". Plosser: "a greater upside risk in the medium term".
- Summary: Inflation is below target with disinflation risk, and a minority sees medium-term upside risk.
- Direction: below neutral because core is around 1.25 to 1.75 percent and slack is large.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 25 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "resource slack remains large". Kocherlakota: "output is well below its trend level".
- Summary: Large slack is accepted widely, though Lacker, Plosser and Bullard question the measurement.
- Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 +-4 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "The unemployment rate is at 10 percent". Rosengren: "double-digit unemployment".
- Summary: Unemployment near 10 percent with firms reluctant to hire.
- Direction: far below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Plosser: consumer spending "has picked up more than expected". Fisher: small-business capital spending "on hold".
- Summary: Consumption is recovering, business investment and hiring remain cautious.
- Direction: below trend, improving.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 66 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "productivity surge has been more impressive than I thought it would be". Yellen: productivity figures boosted "so spectacularly".
- Summary: Strong recent gains, with the Chair expecting some mean reversion.
- Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: "Financial conditions seem to have eased further in a number of markets". Pianalto: smaller firms "are complaining about the availability of credit".
- Summary: Markets and large firms enjoy easy conditions; bank-dependent borrowers face tight credit.
- Direction: below neutral (looser) on balance.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 46 +-8 [Low-Moderate, stated as Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: "TED spreads have returned to normal". Kohn: "Spreads on business loans from banks widened further".
- Summary: Spreads are normal to low in capital markets and wide in bank lending.
- Direction: at or slightly below neutral by level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: warnings about frothiness "seem premature". Warsh: "old money chasing old products".
- Summary: Valuations are viewed as broadly fair, with some froth worry.
- Direction: near neutral, slightly toward valuation concern on the rich side.

**BANKING_STRESS: 62 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: bank assets with low supervisory ratings "continues to grow". Duke: small banks' "problems... are still building".
- Summary: CRE losses and expected small-bank failures outweigh large-bank recovery.
- Direction: above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 47 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Warsh: "A stronger dollar, commodities a bit weaker in the last ten days". Plosser: "a more rapid decline in the dollar".
- Summary: The dollar weakened earlier and rebounded recently.
- Direction: roughly neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 42 [value only].** Evidence: Fisher's contacts report soft-goods price concessions with a possible reversal; pricing power "eludes producers"; oil fell about $10 (Bernanke).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.**
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 [value only].** Evidence: Yellen: expectations "reasonably stable"; Plosser: "dispersion... has widened".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 35 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "If we had a positive nominal federal funds rate, I'd probably be arguing for some easing". Plosser: "The time to change policy is not now, but I do believe the time is getting closer."
- Summary: A majority backs holding at zero with the extended-period language, with a minority leaning toward earlier exit.
- Direction: dovish side of neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Roll call 10 yes, 0 no. Kocherlakota: "I do not see the utility of our buying the extra $150 billion", but "willing to support".
- Summary: Unanimous vote, with open disagreement on wording, balance-sheet direction and real-rate views.
- Direction: low-to-moderate disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Warsh's three themes (sovereign risk, asset chasing, dollar/commodities); Duke's extended account of banks and credit.
- Summary: Several participants give meaningful time to bank and market conditions, alongside macro and exit mechanics.
- Direction: moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 24 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: keep "extended period" and avoid "premature signals, either of tightening or ease". Lockhart: the language "has become a fixation".
- Summary: Low-for-long guidance retained, with normalization signaled only via facility wind-down.
- Direction: easing side of neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bullard: "Uncertainty is high, both around this table and in financial markets". Tarullo: "navigating in somewhat uncharted waters".
- Summary: Many participants stress unusual uncertainty, but the central outlook is a moderate recovery.
- Direction: elevated.
