# FOMC member-discussion scoring (v4.1) - meeting dated 2011-06-22 - SOURCE_TYPE: discussion

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It covers a two-day meeting (the "June 22 Session" marker splits the days). Staff turns are removed, so the file holds members' questions, procedural exchanges and the vote read-out. Besides the economic go-round and the policy go-round, it contains a special DSGE-model discussion, an exit-strategy-principles discussion, a financial-stability discussion, Maiden Lane II / swap-line / debt-ceiling contingency Q&A, a statement-wording discussion and an external-communications policy debate. Some staff-answer turns are missing, so a few member questions appear without answers. The text is readable and I found no garbled passages.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2011: slow recovery after the financial crisis, a second "soft patch", QE2 ending, the funds rate at 0-1/4 percent, unemployment about 9.1 percent. Other themes are an oil and commodity spike that is now reversing, the Japan earthquake supply disruption, Greece, and the debt ceiling. I scored what members say, not this backdrop.

**3. Speakers.**
- Chair Bernanke: soft patch partly temporary; recovery weak; "financial conditions ... remain accommodative"; "watch and wait".
- Dudley: monetary policy may be less stimulative than thought; wants contingency planning for easing; worried about the debt ceiling and Greece.
- Governors Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin: soft patch, downside risks, a weak housing market, and bank credit conditions (Duke).
- Hawkish-leaning presidents: Plosser (may need to withdraw accommodation before year-end), Kocherlakota (reduce accommodation in H2), Fisher (close to alternative C), Hoenig (excessive accommodation sows financial imbalances), Lacker (further stimulus futile, potential output lower).
- Dovish-contingent presidents: Rosengren (stimulus options if there is no self-sustaining recovery), Evans (A-like guidance, 2 percent is not a ceiling), Williams (worried 2011 replays 2010), Pianalto, Lockhart, Bullard (patience).

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Headline and core rose sharply (Yellen: core PCE 1.9 percent and core CPI 2.5 percent over three months; Lacker: core PCE about 2.3 percent over three months). But forecasts for 2012-13 are mostly 1.5-2.25 percent, and Plosser alone is above 2. Pass-through is real (Lockhart, Fisher), and Williams, Evans and Yellen see inflation falling below 2 percent. Net: slightly above target on current readings and rising, but not clearly above. Score 54. This is below the 61+ "clearly above target" band and consistent with 51-60 "somewhat elevated".
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the output gap as below potential. Rosengren: "significant slack"; Williams: "sizable slack"; Bernanke: output growth "below what we think to be potential". Plosser, Lacker and Kocherlakota question the size of the slack, but none say the economy is above potential. Score 36. The bright-line rule (< 50) is satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Participants see the labor market as slack. Unemployment is 9.1 percent (Kocherlakota), "exceptionally high" (Yellen), and Rosengren expects it above 8 percent at end-2012. Score 20, in the 16-30 band (UE > 7 percent).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Net below trend. Consumer spending is "subdued" and "disturbingly weak" (Bernanke, Yellen, Raskin), against strong equipment and software spending and strong exports (Bernanke, Lacker). Score 38.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only passing remarks: firms "emphasize productivity gains" instead of hiring (Evans, Fisher, Bernanke), and Yellen cites "moderate productivity gains". No clear read on a trend, so 50 with low confidence and a wide band.

**5. Financial dimensions.**
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Bernanke says "Financial conditions, of course, remain accommodative" (low volatility, low P/Es). But he also says conditions "weakened during the intermeeting period", with wider spreads, lower equities and a pullback in leverage (Yellen). Credit is "tight in many markets" (residential mortgages, CRE, consumer) yet ample for large firms (Duke, Bernanke), and Fisher says "liquidity is not an issue". Net slightly loose, 45. The bright line (accommodative means < 50) is satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries are discussed only as the debt-ceiling risk and contingency planning (Lockhart, Kocherlakota, Lacker, Dudley, Bernanke). Bernanke says markets are "relaxed" so far. No current dysfunction is described, only prospective risk, so 42 with a wide band.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Direction only. Williams: "rise in high-yield bond spreads". Bernanke: "larger spreads". No level is given, so per the v4.1 rule I score 53 (clear widening, 52-55) with Low confidence. Mixed with that, large-firm credit access is good and lending competition is "ferocious" (Duke), which argues against a wide-level reading.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Williams says Silicon Valley valuations "seem reasonable", with no dot-com excess. Bernanke cites "low P/E ratios", and the market is "faltering" (Williams). Fisher raises a possible 20 percent selloff only as a hypothetical risk. Roughly fair, 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: Duke and Bernanke report "slow but steady" improvement in credit quality, healthy deposits and M&A. Offsetting this: concern about A1/P1 downgrades of Bank of America, Citigroup and Morgan Stanley (Rosengren, Tarullo, Dudley), repurchase reserves seen as insufficient (Raskin), Dexia and money funds (Rosengren), and CRE guidance (Duke). Sound but with pockets of concern, 42 (< 50 as required).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. The dollar is mentioned only as the reserve-currency role in a default scenario (Dudley). No movement is discussed.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: The Committee unanimously holds the range at 0-1/4 percent under alternative B and keeps the "extended period" language. QE2 is ending. Hawkish voices (Plosser, Kocherlakota, Fisher, Hoenig, Lacker on QE) want less accommodation later this year. Dovish contingency voices (Rosengren, Evans, Williams, Dudley, Yellen, Raskin) want planning for more stimulus. Current action is neutral and the lean is evenly split, so 50.
- Dissent: There are no formal dissents (the roll call is unanimous Yes). But there are clear camps on the exit strategy (Bullard "I am opposed"; Lacker conditionally unable to support; Kocherlakota and Rosengren dissatisfied on timing), on an inflation target (Tarullo drifting toward opposition against Plosser, Pianalto and Lacker), and on whether to ease or tighten next. Several "support B but lean to C or A" statements. Score 42.
- Financial stability: A dedicated financial-stability session (money market funds, Europe/Greece, debt ceiling, bank downgrades, Farm Credit System, leverage in agriculture and energy), plus Raskin, Hoenig, Plosser and Fisher comments. It is prominent but shares the meeting with the economy, the exit strategy and communications. Score 62.
- Forward guidance: "Extended period" is retained and Yellen has pushed out her expected liftoff date. Against that, exit principles are agreed, Plosser and Kocherlakota signal tightening steps in H2, and Yellen says the next move is "toward less" accommodation. Rosengren and Evans signal readiness to add stimulus. Roughly balanced and data-dependent, slightly easing through the retained language, 48.
- Uncertainty: Pianalto says uncertainty is "extraordinarily high" and elevated beyond April. Yellen documents "abnormally high" uncertainty (the Tealbook box, Bloom). Lockhart is "tentative". Evans says "uncertainty is substantial", and Kocherlakota is "highly uncertain" about labor-force dynamics. Many participants emphasize this, though there is no Knightian language. Score 62.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 ±8 [High]** Evidence: Yellen: core PCE and core CPI of "1.9 percent and 2.5 percent" over three months vs 0.8 and 0.6 a year earlier; Plosser: "Both total and core inflation have accelerated significantly since the beginning of this year." Center of gravity: inflation has risen toward and slightly above target on current readings but most forecasts sit near or below 2 percent. Direction: above neutral because current core and headline readings are up sharply, but only modestly.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 ±7 [High]** Evidence: Rosengren: "the significant slack in the economy"; Williams: "Given the sizable slack ..." Center of gravity: most see substantial slack, though a minority question its size. Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±5 [High]** Evidence: Kocherlakota: "Unemployment has fallen from 9.8 percent ... to 9.1 percent"; Yellen: "exceptionally high unemployment". Center of gravity: unemployment above 9 percent and weak hiring. Direction: far below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±7 [High]** Evidence: Bernanke: "surprisingly weak consumption, even ex autos, is ... disturbing"; Raskin: "slow consumption and slow growth". Offset by "Real equipment and software spending is still strong" (Bernanke). Center of gravity: net below trend because consumers dominate. Direction: below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 ±10 [Low]** Evidence: Evans: "Businesses will continue to emphasize productivity gains"; Yellen: "moderate productivity gains". Center of gravity: only passing, trend-like references. Direction: roughly at neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 ±8 [Moderate]** Evidence: Bernanke: "Financial conditions, of course, remain accommodative"; but "Financial conditions weakened during the intermeeting period". Center of gravity: loosish overall with some intermeeting tightening and tight pockets of credit. Direction: slightly below neutral (looser).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Low]** Evidence: Bernanke: no immediate default but "Armageddon" avoidance planning; Dudley: markets "so relaxed" about the debt limit. Center of gravity: orderly markets, with prospective rather than present stress. Direction: normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 53 ±6 [Low]** Evidence: Williams: "the rise in high-yield bond spreads since our last meeting"; Bernanke: "larger spreads, more risk aversion". Center of gravity: direction only (widening), no level given, so 52-55 per the v4.1 rule. Direction: slightly above neutral because of the move.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±7 [Moderate]** Evidence: Williams: "valuations that seem reasonable"; Bernanke: "low P/E ratios". Center of gravity: fairly valued, with a hypothetical selloff risk (Fisher). Direction: about neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 ±8 [Moderate]** Evidence: Duke: "slow but steady progress on credit quality"; Tarullo: a downgrade would be "a significant challenge for B of A". Center of gravity: sound system with identifiable stress pockets. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS: 66.** Lockhart and Fisher report pass-through; Fisher: big-box retailers raising 40-50 percent of products by 5-8 percent; Yellen: motor vehicles drive core.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 55.** Tarullo: persistent services inflation, "the lion's share ... in rents"; Yellen: restaurant services and transportation up.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 52.** Rosengren: owners' equivalent rent rising; Bernanke: shelter "still ... rising more slowly than the rest of core" but poised to rise.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52.** Bernanke: "inflation expectations remain anchored" and TIPS "close to the middle of their historical range"; Plosser notes TIPS moved up to 100 bp; Pianalto: risk if expectations "become unanchored".

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 50 ±6 [Moderate]** Evidence: Plosser: "increasingly uncomfortable with the funds rate sitting at zero"; Rosengren: "our focus should be on what additional stimulus should be considered". Center of gravity: a unanimous hold, with a roughly even split between tighten-later and ease-if-needed voices. Direction: balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 ±8 [Moderate]** Evidence: Bullard on the exit principles: "I am opposed"; Lacker: "I couldn't support this" without the 4-5 year figure. Tarullo: "drifting toward opposition to any inflation target." Center of gravity: no formal dissents, but real camps on the exit strategy, an inflation target and the next direction of policy. Direction: above the unanimous floor, below split-committee levels.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 ±8 [High]** Evidence: Rosengren on money market funds: "I fear they could play a similar role were European problems to evolve"; Fisher: "this financial stability aspect of it is very, very important". Center of gravity: dedicated session plus repeated interventions across the meeting. Direction: n/a (attention measure).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 48 ±8 [Moderate]** Evidence: Yellen: "I remain very comfortable with ... the 'extended period' language" and "our next move will be toward less ... accommodation"; Kocherlakota: "alternative closer to C in the latter part of the year". Center of gravity: language retained, data-dependent, with competing signals. Direction: near neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±8 [High]** Evidence: Pianalto: "The uncertainty surrounding this assessment is extraordinarily high"; Yellen: uncertainty "is now abnormally high". Center of gravity: many participants stress heightened uncertainty. Direction: above normal.
