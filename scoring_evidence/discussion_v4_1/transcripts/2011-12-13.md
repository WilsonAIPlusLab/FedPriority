# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 -- Meeting 2011-12-13 (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Transcript of member/participant turns only (staff turns removed, so many member questions to staff appear without answers). It contains: member questions during the financial and economic briefings, the economic go-around, the Chair's summary and remarks, the policy go-round on "Alternative B" versus "Alternative A", a short follow-up on statement paragraph 5, an afternoon session on communications (policy projections in the SEP and a statement of longer-run goals), and a vote read-out. Roughly the last third is communications/framework talk; I score only what participants say about conditions and policy, and use the communications session for forward guidance, dissent and uncertainty evidence.

**2. Era context.** Funds rate at zero for three years, mid-2013 calendar guidance in the statement, maturity extension program under way, European sovereign/banking crisis dominant, US data recently a bit better than expected.

**3. Speakers.** Chairman Bernanke; Vice Chairman Dudley; Governors Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin; Presidents Lockhart, Plosser, Williams, Rosengren, Bullard, Evans, Pianalto, Fisher, George, Kocherlakota, Lacker. Views: moderate growth with downside risk from Europe (almost all); inflation easing and likely below 2 percent (almost all; Lacker, Plosser, George see medium-term upside risks); large slack (Evans, Yellen, Tarullo, Raskin, Bernanke) versus smaller slack/lower potential (Bullard, partly Kocherlakota, Lacker); policy: most back B, Evans wants A, Yellen/Tarullo/Rosengren/Dudley/Raskin lean to more easing in January, Fisher/Plosser/Lacker/Lockhart/Pianalto/George/Bullard oppose further easing now.

**4. Direction statements (core dimensions).**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as slightly below the midpoint/target and moderating: "price pressures continue to decline," six-month PCE below 2 percent, forecasts 1.2-1.5 percent (Williams, Evans, Yellen, Rosengren). Offsets: Lacker notes 12-month still near 3 percent, Pianalto notes headline still above 2 percent, Plosser/George see medium-term upside risk. Net just below target, about 42.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. "Massive output gap" (Evans), "considerable slack" (Yellen), Chair "still quite substantial." Bullard argues potential is lower, so the gap is smaller; Bernanke notes slack may be smaller than the Tealbook. Still clearly below 50: about 33.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment 8.6 percent, long-term unemployment "unprecedented," 24 million underemployed (Raskin), "labor market dysfunction." Well below 50: about 26.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Moderate growth, Q4 GDP growth 3.25-3.5 percent with stronger consumer spending, but participants doubt sustainability (income weak, saving rate drop, $7 trillion wealth loss), business investment held back by uncertainty, housing flat. Net somewhat below trend: about 42.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only one passing remark (Pianalto: labor productivity growth "has recovered"), plus potential-growth debate (Bullard) that is not about productivity as such. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed. Tightening forces: Europe, wider spreads, stressed ABCP, strained European bank funding, tight small-business credit (Williams, Pianalto). Easing forces: "capital is widely available and attractively priced" (Fisher), most firms have easy access to capital (Bernanke summary), lower Treasury yields, low mortgage rates, banks substituting for European lenders (Duke). Slightly tighter than neutral on net: about 54, wide uncertainty.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Treasuries discussed only as safe haven with lower yields (Bernanke, Plosser, Williams); no dysfunction described. About 40, low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level never stated. Direction only: "wider spreads" (Bernanke), ABCP rates up (Rosengren), mortgage-Treasury spread widened (Yellen), corporate spreads rise with uncertainty (Williams model), elevated CDS. Per the level rule, direction only gives 52-55 with Low confidence: 54.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Fisher fears a major correction after a bubble pattern (S&P to 800); Bernanke notes "a correction in the stock market is also a risk"; bank shares fell. A valuation-concern-on-the-rich-side view from one speaker, otherwise price moves only. About 40, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed. Duke and Bernanke: balance sheets strengthening; Plosser: US institutions have mostly normal funding; Dudley: funding held up "surprisingly well." Against: European banks stressed, broker-dealer funding model at risk, elevated CDS (Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Jefferies), weak community banks (Williams), S&P downgrades. About 48.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Appreciation noted: "strengthening of the Texas dollar" (Fisher), "stronger dollar" drag (Yellen), further appreciation as a risk, dollar-euro higher than last meeting (Rosengren). Modest strengthening: 60.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure; evidence says easing, below target, so below 50. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter; evidence says high unemployment, so well below 50. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress; evidence says widening, so slightly above 50. Higher EQUITY_VALUATION = more stress/concern (rubric table); Fisher's concern is overvaluation-then-correction, which the table puts on the low side but with a risk of correction. Kept in the low 40s with Low confidence. Higher DOLLAR = stronger; evidence says stronger, so above 50.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: the decision is hold (Alternative B) with the existing mid-2013 guidance, existing MBS/maturity extension programs. Several (Evans, Yellen, Tarullo, Rosengren, Dudley, Raskin, Duke in effect) favor more easing, MBS purchases, extending guidance to end-2014; hawkish side (Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, Pianalto, George) opposes additional accommodation and worries about inflation expectations, but nobody advocates tightening now (Plosser says tightening "well before mid-2013" in his go-around remarks). Dovish tilt, QE-era: 32.
- Dissent: one formal dissent (Evans, wanted A). Several reservations: Yellen, Tarullo, Rosengren, Dudley say more easing is warranted in January; Kocherlakota and Lacker wanted old paragraph 5; Fisher said he would vote against extending the date. In the communications session the straw vote on policy projections was 11 for, 4 against, 1 abstain, with Duke's change to "present"; sharp disagreement on framework (Tarullo skeptical, Fisher, Bullard opposed). Chair says "pretty broad consensus today for Alternative B." One dissent plus multiple reservations and real disagreement on the prescription: 42.
- Financial stability attention: Europe/Lehman-style risk, broker-dealer and money-fund funding, ABCP, swap lines, stock-correction risk, farmland prices, and a whole debate on whether statement paragraph 5 and the longer-run statement should mention financial stability (Rosengren, George for; Kocherlakota, Lacker against). Prominent but sharing space with the economic assessment: 62.
- Forward guidance: existing date-based guidance (mid-2013) retained; many argue it should be pushed to late 2014 or tied to thresholds, and MBS purchases and open-ended programs are tabled for January; policy paths to be published in the SEP, which the participants note show liftoff mostly no later than 2014. Strong easing bias: 12.
- Uncertainty: "enormous amount of uncertainty tied to the European situation" (Bernanke), uncertainty elevated by almost any metric (Williams), many contacts and members stress uncertainty, difficulty measuring potential/slack, income/consumption mismeasurement, mixed labor signals. Many participants emphasize it: 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 (<50, "slack"/"massive output gap"): OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 54 (>50): both "easy access to capital/attractively priced" and "tight credit"/"strained" appear; net mildly tighter because the dominant message is that conditions are hostage to Europe, so I stay near neutral, just above 50. CREDIT_SPREADS 54: direction only, within 45-55, Low confidence: OK. BANKING_STRESS 48: no explicit "well-capitalized/healthy" overall verdict, mixed signals: OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 60 (>50, appreciation): OK. TREASURY_STRESS 40: no dysfunction: OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs LABOR 26: diff 7, fine. OUTPUT_GAP 33 vs CONSUMPTION 42: diff 9, fine (consumption is above the gap because of recent surprise strength). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 54 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 54: co-move. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 54 vs TREASURY_STRESS 40: acceptable, Treasury functioning normally with safe-haven rally. INFLATION 42 vs sub-components (goods 36, housing 58, expectations 47): consistent with a below-target headline.

**9. N/A justification.** PRODUCTIVITY: one passing mention only. INFLATION_SERVICES: no participant discusses services prices as such (only wages/compensation and rents, which I score under housing).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: "price pressures continue to decline pretty much as most forecasts anticipated"; Williams: "Six-month annualized changes in overall, core, and trimmed mean PCE prices are now all below 2 percent." Offset: Lacker, "on a 12-month basis, we are still much closer to 3 percent than to 2 percent."
- Summary: Inflation seen as easing and likely to run below target, with a minority noting elevated 12-month readings and medium-term upside risk.
- Direction: Below neutral because most participants expect inflation under 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans: "there still appears to be a massive output gap"; Yellen: "considerable slack in the labor market."
- Summary: Most see substantial slack; Bullard's lower-potential view is a minority and the Chair sides with substantial slack.
- Direction: Below potential, so below 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 26 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota: unemployment "8.6 percent," EPOP "58 1/2 percent"; Raskin: "still 24 million underemployed people."
- Summary: Very high unemployment and long-term unemployment, only slow improvement.
- Direction: Well below neutral because of the level of unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: card sales "up 7.4 percent year over year... highest... in five years"; Raskin: "I expect household spending to be pretty unimpressive going forward." Business investment held back: Williams, uncertainty prompts firms "to postpone large capital investments."
- Summary: Recent consumer strength offset by doubts about sustainability and weak investment/housing.
- Direction: Slightly below trend because strength is seen as temporary and investment is cautious.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Only a passing remark (Pianalto: "labor productivity growth has recovered").

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 54 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "Financial conditions remain hostage to European developments... Funding conditions for European banks and even a few U.S. institutions remain stressed." Fisher: "Capital is widely available and attractively priced."
- Summary: Europe-driven stress and tight small-business credit are roughly offset by easy corporate access to funding and low yields, leaving a slight tightening.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because stress is the dominant theme, tempered by easy corporate funding.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "stress in Europe lowers U.S. Treasury yields"; Williams: massive safe-haven flows could "drive the entire Treasury yield curve down."
- Summary: Treasury market discussed only as a safe haven, with no dysfunction described.
- Direction: Below the stress threshold; normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "the wider spreads, lower stock prices, and the like"; Rosengren: ABCP "maturities have declined and rates have risen."
- Summary: Spreads described as widening with no stated level, so scored just above 50 under the direction-only rule.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral by the direction-only rule; no level statement.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "I remain deeply concerned that we could have a significant correction in stock markets... we could easily go back to 800 on the S&P"; Bernanke: "A correction in the stock market is also a risk."
- Summary: One member voices valuation concern and downside risk of correction; others mention only lower bank shares.
- Direction: Modestly toward the rich-valuation side, below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 48 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Duke: "balance sheets of households, businesses, and financial institutions continue to strengthen"; Rosengren: "I am concerned that the broker-dealer model of financing might not survive a severe European shock."
- Summary: US banks viewed as sound with funding intact, but European bank stress, broker-dealer fragility and weak community banks keep it near neutral.
- Direction: Roughly neutral, slightly below 50 because US funding has held up.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Fisher: "a strengthening of what we calculate as the 'Texas dollar'"; Yellen: "a weakening global economy and stronger dollar."
- Summary: Dollar has appreciated modestly and may appreciate further under European stress.
- Direction: Above neutral because of appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 36.** Evidence: Pianalto, "softer incoming inflation data for a broad range of goods, commodity prices that are no longer trending higher"; Bernanke, "import prices have softened."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** No services price discussion.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 58.** Evidence: Bernanke, "tenants' rents have actually increased considerably... about a 4 percent rate of increase," expected to moderate.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47.** Evidence: Williams, "inflation expectations are well anchored"; Pianalto, "remain below 2 percent for more than a decade"; Plosser worries about anchoring if accommodation continues.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Evans: "I favor more accommodation. I cannot support Alternative B"; Yellen: "I see a compelling case for further policy accommodation... I'd also be supportive in January of a program of further asset purchases." Opposite pole: Plosser: "I am not confident that inflation expectations will remain anchored... if we keep trying to increase the degree of accommodation"; Fisher: "I would not support additional monetary accommodation."
- Summary: Hold with an easing tilt; a sizable group wants more MBS purchases and longer guidance, no one wants tightening now, and the hawkish group mainly resists further easing.
- Direction: Dovish because the active debate is over more easing and the hold is explicitly framed as a pause.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out: "President Evans No"; Chairman: "pretty broad consensus today for Alternative B, although, obviously, people have different nuances"; communications straw vote with four against and Duke abstaining.
- Summary: One formal dissent, many reservations in both directions, and real disagreement on paragraph 5, forward guidance and publishing the rate paths.
- Direction: Moderate-to-notable disagreement; economic views broadly shared but prescriptions differ.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 62 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "financial stability does deserve to get attention, and it is separate from just looking at the mean path"; Bernanke: "the risk of a Lehman-style catastrophic financial event has not been eliminated."
- Summary: European financial spillovers, broker-dealer funding, ABCP, swap lines and whether the Committee statement should mention financial stability occupy a large part of the discussion.
- Direction: Prominent topic, though not the sole focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-5 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "I'd be inclined to shift mid-2013 guidance out to late 2014"; Dudley: "Alternative A, paragraph 4, talks about extending the date to the end of 2014—that makes sense to me."
- Summary: Existing date-based low-rate promise kept, and the dominant forward signal is later liftoff and possible further easing in January.
- Direction: Strongly easing, well below 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Bernanke: "We clearly have an enormous amount of uncertainty tied to the European situation"; Williams: "By almost any metric, uncertainty is elevated."
- Summary: Many participants stress Europe, mismeasurement of income and consumption, and uncertainty about potential output and slack.
- Direction: Elevated-to-high uncertainty.
