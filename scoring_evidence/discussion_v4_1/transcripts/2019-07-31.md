# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 -- meeting 2019-07-31 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, staff turns removed. It has four blocks: (a) a long strategic framework-review go-round (r*, effective lower bound, makeup strategies, symmetric vs balanced approach, ranges for inflation); (b) the financial-stability go-round; (c) the economic-conditions go-round, including the Chair's summary; (d) the policy go-round on Alternatives A/B/C, the statement-wording exchange, and a vote read-out. The framework block is not an assessment of current conditions. I scored current conditions and policy from blocks b to d and used block a only where members described conditions, risk views or policy philosophy.

**2. Era context.** Mid-2019: long expansion, very low unemployment, core inflation persistently below 2 percent, trade tension and weak global growth, inverted 3-month/10-year curve, Committee considering its first cut in the cycle. I took the scores from what members said, not from this backdrop.

**3. Speakers.** Powell, Williams, Clarida, Bowman, Brainard, Quarles (Board/NY). Presidents Rosengren, Mester, Evans, Bullard, Daly, Barkin, Harker, George, Bostic, Kashkari, Kaplan.
- Doves on policy: Evans (preferred 50bp), Kashkari (50bp plus an explicit no-hike pledge), Daly, Williams, Clarida, Bullard (a cut to "ratify"), Powell.
- Reluctant supporters of the 25bp cut: Brainard, Quarles, Bowman, Kaplan, Harker, Barkin.
- Preferred no change: Rosengren, George, Mester, Bostic. Rosengren and George cast the two "No" votes.

**4. Core dimensions: direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation as below the midpoint (target). Core PCE is 1.5-1.6 percent. Words used are "muted," "persistently below," "mired below target," and "uncomfortably subdued." A minority (Rosengren, Bostic, Barkin, Mester) point to trimmed-mean, median and recent monthly readings near 2 percent. Net: clearly below target, with some firming. About 35.
- OUTPUT_GAP: participants see the economy as modestly above potential or at trend and slowing toward trend. Rosengren says "growing faster than potential." Kaplan says "growth at or above potential." Mester says "above-trend pace." George says unemployment is "below most estimates of its longer-run level." Clarida and Daly argue the gap is smaller than the staff thinks, but still not negative. Bright line: "above trend" means more than 50. About 54.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: participants see the market as tight. Unemployment is 3.7 percent, a "50-year low," and "the best in generations" (Quarles). Contacts report hiring trouble and wage pressure at the low end. Overall wage growth is muted and Kashkari says "slack likely remains" (outlier). Using the UE anchor: 70.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: consumer spending is "robust," with PCE up 4.3 percent in Q2. Business fixed investment is flat or negative, housing and manufacturing are weak, and exports are down. Weighted by discussion emphasis: roughly at trend, 49.
- PRODUCTIVITY: touched only briefly (Evans: "moderate productivity trends"; Harker: declining dynamism "contributing to declining productivity"). Modestly below trend, Low confidence, 42.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: described as "accommodative" (Mester), "very accommodative" (Brainard) and "growth friendly" (Powell). Bright line: accommodative means below 50. 30.
- TREASURY_STRESS: yields, inversion, negative-yielding sovereigns and the term premium are discussed. No dysfunction (liquidity, auctions) is raised. Normal functioning, 42, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level reading. Rosengren says spreads are "not elevated." Mester says "relatively low levels of corporate debt spreads." Brainard says spreads are "down 20 to 30 basis points," and describes loose leveraged-loan terms and low CRE cap rates. A low level means below 50. 30.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: the table gives high valuation concern low scores. "Near historic peaks" (Mester, Brainard), "exuberant" (Rosengren), "a little frothy" (Quarles). Offsetting views: Powell says the equity spread is at its median and equities are not overvalued if low rates are the new normal. Kaplan is not worried about stock valuations. Net: elevated, with a moderate concern. 28.
- BANKING_STRESS: Quarles ("extremely resilient"), Powell (leverage "low") and Bowman say the system is resilient. Brainard, Kashkari and George warn that capital ratios are falling, payouts exceed earnings, and the banks are "south of green." Farm-loan non-performing loans are edging up. Sound with concerns, so below 50. 35.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Brainard says the dollar depreciated about 0.5 percent over the intermeeting period, with some risk of reversal. George mentions the earlier large appreciation as a disinflationary force. Small recent depreciation, brief discussion. 45, Low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components:
  - Goods: George says goods prices "continue to show deflation." 28.
  - Services: George says services prices rise at a stable rate "over 2 percent." 54.
  - Housing: shelter inflation is not discussed. House-price and housing-activity remarks are not shelter inflation. N/A.
  - Expectations: some members call them "well anchored" (Mester, Bostic, Barkin). Others say they are too low and drifting (Clarida, Williams, Daly, Evans, Kashkari). Net: below anchored-at-target, 36.

**5. Polarity checks.**
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher means tighter. Evidence is a tight market. Score 70 matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress. Evidence is low spreads. Score 30 matches.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: higher means more equity stress per the rubric table. Evidence is high-valuation concern, which the table puts in 16-30. Score 28 matches the table. I did not read it as "high prices = high score."
- BANKING_STRESS: evidence is "resilient," so below 50. Score 35 matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: a 25bp cut, with a majority supporting some easing. About 8 of the 10 voters, plus several non-voters, supported a cut; two wanted 50bp; four non-voters and two voters preferred no change. Many supporters were reluctant and wanted "optionality," and Powell called it a midcycle adjustment, not the start of an extended loosening cycle. The framework go-round was strongly dovish in philosophy (err toward accommodation, avoid the ELB). Moderate dovish, about 35.
- Dissent: two formal dissents (George, Rosengren), both on the hawkish side. Others voiced strong reservations (Mester, Bostic, Barkin, Harker "cannot support B as written" yet "reluctantly" going along). Others wanted 50bp (Evans, Kashkari). Open disagreement on the framework ("flag vs map" camps) and on the inflation shortfall. Notable disagreement, distinct camps, about 58.
- Financial stability: a dedicated block of about 14 interventions, plus its use as a rationale (Rosengren, Brainard, Kaplan, George). Prominent topic, about 72.
- Forward guidance: Powell wants data dependence and "optionality," no commitment to September, and a "midcycle adjustment, not an extended loosening cycle." The statement keeps "act as appropriate." Markets expect more easing. Kashkari asks for a no-hike pledge, and the hawks want to pull easing expectations back. Net: easing lean softened by data dependence, about 42.
- Uncertainty: trade, Brexit, global weakness, and r*/u*/Phillips-curve model uncertainty are stressed by many (Quarles: "as uncertain as at any time during my tenure"; Mester; Bowman; Brainard). Others say uncertainty is not elevated or is falling (Rosengren, Barkin, Bostic). Elevated but contested, about 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 54 (above-trend language, greater than 50). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 (accommodative, less than 50). CREDIT_SPREADS 30 (low level, less than 50). BANKING_STRESS 35 (resilient, less than 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 45 (slight depreciation, less than 50). TREASURY_STRESS 42 (no dysfunction, within 36-50). All satisfied.

**8. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs LABOR 70: a 16-point gap. Explanation: labor is tight by level (UE under 4), but members stress muted wages, a flat Phillips curve, a lower u*, and growth slowing to trend, so they see little excess demand. I moved the gap only a little above 50.
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 vs CONSUMPTION 49: within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 30 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 30: consistent (loose, low spreads).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: normal times, so Treasury stress is near 40. Fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 35 vs sub-components (goods 28, services 54, expectations 36): consistent.

**9. N/A.** INFLATION_HOUSING is N/A because shelter or rent inflation is not discussed by participants.

**Text note.** No garbled passages affected scoring. Some speaker labels are mis-tagged (for example "MR. WILLIAMS" and unlabeled continuation turns), and staff-answer turns are missing, so questions sometimes appear without replies. The material mixes July 30 and July 31 sessions under the 2019-07-31 label.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Clarida: "core PCE is running at 1.6 percent... U.S. price pressures remain muted." Evans: "there simply isn't any meaningful evidence of building inflation pressures."
- Center of gravity: core inflation is persistently below target, with a minority pointing to firming trimmed-mean measures.
- Direction: below neutral because most members describe inflation as muted and persistently under 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "the economy so far continues to grow faster than potential." Kaplan: "growth at or above potential, cyclical inflationary forces continue to firm."
- Center of gravity: modestly above potential, but slowing to trend, with the gap smaller than the staff's.
- Direction: above neutral because of explicit above-trend and above-potential language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "The labor market continues to be the best in generations." Mester: "unemployment rate is 3.7 percent, near a 50-year low... trouble finding workers."
- Center of gravity: very tight by level, with muted overall wage growth.
- Direction: above neutral because unemployment is under 4 percent and contacts report shortages. Kashkari's "slack likely remains" is a single outlier.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 49 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "PCE surged at an annual rate of 4.3 percent. Business fixed investment, however, was down 0.6 percent." Williams: "Robust consumer spending is balanced by weakness in housing, business fixed investment, and exports."
- Center of gravity: strong consumers offset weak investment, housing and exports.
- Direction: at neutral on a net basis.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 42 +-10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Evans: "moderate productivity trends"; Harker: "declining dynamism... contributing factors to declining productivity."
- Center of gravity: brief mentions of modestly below-trend productivity and dynamism.
- Direction: below neutral because of the "declining" remarks, with few speakers.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "Financial conditions are very accommodative, with borrowing rates low and the stock market at all-time highs." Mester: "Financial conditions remain accommodative."
- Center of gravity: loose, partly because easing is already priced in.
- Direction: below neutral (accommodative) per the bright line.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Clarida: the 3-month/10-year inversion "has now persisted for around two months." Williams: "is the term premium unusually low?... could very well be the new normal."
- Center of gravity: yields and curve slope are debated, but there is no dysfunction.
- Direction: normal functioning, within 36-50 (yield moves are not stress).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren: "Credit spreads... are also not elevated and remain below the levels reached at the end of last year." Brainard: "Spreads of corporate bond yields over Treasury security yields were down 20 to 30 basis points."
- Center of gravity: spreads are low and credit is available, with generous leveraged-loan terms.
- Direction: below neutral because the stated level is low (not elevated).

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 28 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Mester: "Equity and corporate bond prices are near their historical peaks, and overall valuation risks appear to be rising." Quarles: "maybe a little 'frothy' but not really out of line with this stage of the business cycle."
- Center of gravity: valuations are seen as elevated, with moderate concern, partly justified by low rates.
- Direction: in the table's "elevated valuations" band (16-30), below neutral on this scale.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "our largest banks are extremely, extremely resilient." Kashkari: "I think the banks are something south of green."
- Center of gravity: sound system, with a vocal minority worried about falling capital and farm and private-credit exposures.
- Direction: below neutral because the system is called resilient and well capitalized.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 45 +-6 [Low]**
- Evidence: Brainard: "the dollar had depreciated about 1/2 percent." George: "the large appreciation of the dollar and the resulting drop in import prices that began early last year."
- Center of gravity: slight recent depreciation after earlier strength.
- Direction: just below neutral because the intermeeting move was depreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 28**
- Evidence: George: "Goods prices in the PCE price index continue to show deflation, owing to the large appreciation of the dollar."
- Direction: below neutral (falling goods prices).

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 54**
- Evidence: George: "services prices continue to rise at a stable rate over 2 percent."
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Shelter inflation is not discussed.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 36**
- Evidence: Williams: "inflation expectations are quite low relative to history." Mester: "Long-run inflation expectations have remained broadly stable."
- Direction: below the anchored-at-target level because the majority see them as too low, despite the anchored-view minority.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 35 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "I see the case for providing more accommodation at this meeting as persuasive." Evans: "I prefer alt-A... the risk is not doing too much... it's being timid and doing too little."
- Center of gravity: a majority backs a 25bp cut, several reluctantly or as a one-off, while two voters and several others wanted no change.
- Direction: dovish because the Committee is easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 58 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Votes: George "No," Rosengren "No." Bostic: "I support the policy action as described in alternative C." Harker: "I cannot support alternative B as written." Evans and Kashkari wanted 50bp.
- Center of gravity: two formal hawkish dissents, dovish and hawkish camps, and open disagreement on the inflation shortfall and the framework.
- Direction: above the moderate-difference level because there are distinct camps and a split of the disagreement beyond tactics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: a dedicated go-round (Mester, Rosengren on WeWork, Bowman on private credit, Quarles, George, Brainard, Kaplan on BDCs, Kashkari). Rosengren on policy: "I am worried about the financial stability implications of easing."
- Center of gravity: a prominent topic that most participants addressed at length.
- Direction: attention-based and above the "moderate" band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Powell: "I see the Committee as adjusting policy midcycle to a somewhat more accommodative stance, not beginning an extended loosening cycle." Powell: "I do want to have more optionality coming out of this meeting."
- Center of gravity: easing bias softened to data dependence, with many wanting to pull back September expectations.
- Direction: below 50 (mild easing lean), but not an easing commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Quarles: "The macroeconomic environment seems as uncertain as at any time during my tenure." Rosengren: "Many measures of uncertainty are not elevated."
- Center of gravity: trade, global and structural uncertainty are emphasized by many, but contested by several.
- Direction: elevated, mid-range.
