# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, transcript, meeting 1993-12-21

## Part 1: Thinking process

**Document type.** Participant-only transcript (v2 input, staff turns removed). It opens with a price-stability and productivity research discussion, then members' questions to the staff, the economic go-around (Broaddus through Phillips), a short Q&A on directive asymmetry, the policy go-around (Greenspan proposes "B symmetric", unchanged funds rate), and a vote read-out. A long final section covers a non-monetary item: the Gonzalez request about the October 15 conference-call tape. That section is procedural/legal and carries no economic content, so I ignored it for scoring. Staff answers to member questions are missing, so some member questions lack context. Page markers and a few "[unintelligible]" gaps are present but do not obscure the substance.

**Era context (not used to override the text).** Early 1993-94 expansion. The funds rate has been 3 percent for about 17 months. Members debate whether to begin removing accommodation.

**Speakers.**
- Broaddus, Angell, Lindsey, Mullins, Melzer, Hoenig and Phillips lean toward tightening now or soon.
- Greenspan, McDonough, Boehne, Keehn, Syron, McTeer, Stern, Kelley, LaWare, Forrestal and Parry accept waiting with a symmetric directive.
- Most of the waiting group say a tightening is coming "sooner rather than later".
- LaWare, Kelley, Stern and Boehne are the most cautious.
- Most Reserve Bank presidents report strong, broad momentum but difficulty raising prices.

**Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- *Inflation pressure.* Participants see inflation as about at or modestly above an acceptable level, with upside risk but little current pass-through.
  - Melzer says price stability "has not been achieved", with core CPI at 3 percent and 3.6 percent recently.
  - Phillips sees "some very worrisome signs on the horizon".
  - Keehn, Forrestal, Syron, Boehne, Stern and McDonough stress that firms cannot make price increases stick.
  - The net is slightly above the midpoint, 52.
- *Output gap.* Participants see the economy as below potential but the gap closing.
  - Unemployment is about 6.5 percent. Mullins says capacity utilization is 83 against a prior peak of 84.8.
  - Kelley refers to "how fast the gap in the GDP gets closed". Parry sees the gap "diminishing at a faster rate".
  - Melzer says growth "at or above potential next year". That is a forecast, not a present reading.
  - I score 44. No "above potential" language describes the present, so the bright-line rule is satisfied.
- *Labor tightness.* Unemployment of 6.4-6.5 percent maps to the 31-45 band. There are tight spots (Keehn's truck drivers, Mullins' record factory overtime and workweek), but employment growth is called "marginal" by Jordan and Boehne. I score 38.
- *Consumption and investment.* Participants see spending as above trend. The fourth quarter is about 5 percent and described as technically unsustainable. Interest-sensitive sectors are strong (autos, housing, equipment). Several expect a pullback, so I net to 62.
- *Productivity.* It comes up only in passing.
  - Jordan reports "very substantial increases in productivity" in manufacturing.
  - McDonough says large firms are concentrating on improving productivity.
  - The opening discussion is also about productivity and low inflation.
  - It is modestly above trend, low confidence, 58.
- *Financial conditions.* Participants describe policy as "very accommodative" with real short rates near zero or negative (Mullins, Lindsey, Angell).
  - Headwinds from balance-sheet adjustment have faded (Greenspan), and long rates have backed up about 1/2 point.
  - Credit demand is "finally beginning to move" (Greenspan).
  - Conditions are on the loose side of neutral but not extreme: 38. The "accommodative" bright line requires below 50.
- *Treasury stress.* Only yield levels and a thin-market remark by Mullins appear. No dysfunction is described, so N/A.
- *Credit spreads.* Spreads are not discussed, so N/A.
- *Equity valuation.* Mullins says the market went "essentially sideways for half a year" and the economy has "caught up with the market". Lindsey notes speculative OTC and new issues. There is no valuation alarm: 47, low confidence.
- *Banking stress.* McDonough says commercial and investment banks are "doing very well". Phillips says the financial sector is "well positioned to support growth". Jordan reports rising loan demand. Angell mentions altered bank lending behavior after asset deflation. This is healthy with minor residual caution: 30. The bright line requires below 50.
- *Dollar.* Lindsey wishes for "some appreciation in the dollar" as a way to restrain prices, and Angell mentions the dollar in 1987. No one describes the current direction of the dollar, so N/A.
- *Inflation sub-components.*
  - Goods: commodity, scrap steel and lumber prices are up, oil is down and manufacturers cannot pass prices through. Mixed, 54.
  - Services and housing shelter inflation are not discussed, so N/A. Land and real-estate price talk is about asset prices, not shelter inflation.
  - Expectations: Phillips sees the long-rate backup as "showing the deterioration in inflation expectations". Broaddus and Melzer fear expectations deteriorating. Modestly above anchored, 56.

**Tier 3 check.**
- *Lean.* The action is unchanged policy with a symmetric directive. Greenspan says "the days of accommodation have got to be [about] over" and expects "a whole succession" of increases toward a funds rate near 4 percent, starting "fairly early in the year".
  - Broaddus (25bp now), Angell (50bp now), Lindsey (25bp now), Melzer, Hoenig and Phillips favor moving now or soon.
  - Mullins argues for moving but accepts waiting.
  - The cautious group (LaWare, Kelley, Stern, Boehne) still concede tightening is coming.
  - Current action is a hold, but the forward lean is clearly hawkish. I score 62.
- *Dissent.* The vote read-out shows Angell "A soft no" and Lindsey "No" after an initial "Pass". That is two dissents, both in the hawkish direction (Angell wanted 50bp, Lindsey 25bp now). Several others, including non-voters Broaddus and Melzer, argued for moving now but not against the proposal.
  - Disagreement is mostly about timing and size, with some difference in the economic read (Broaddus versus Kelley and LaWare).
  - I score 45.
- *Financial stability emphasis.* There are scattered references to balance-sheet adjustment, household credit, asset redeployment, bond-market credibility and the long-rate backup. No one frames it as financial stability. I score 18.
- *Forward guidance.* The signal is that accommodation will be removed in stages, early in 1994, with the first move to be taken at a meeting and consulted on beforehand if conditions accelerate. The directive itself stays symmetric. I score 70.
- *Uncertainty.* Members call it a "close call" (Kelley, Stern) and say "we just don't know" (Boehne, Syron). Key unknowns are the Q1 slowdown, the saving rate and the tax effect. Most still speak with conviction about direction. That is normal to somewhat elevated: 38.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 44: below potential, closing. Consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38: accommodative means below 50. Consistent.
- BANKING_STRESS 30: healthy means below 50. Consistent.
- CREDIT_SPREADS, TREASURY_STRESS and DOLLAR_STRENGTH are N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 44 versus LABOR 38 (6 points apart) and versus CONSUMPTION 62 (18 points apart). The divergence from consumption is explained: spending is surging from a still-slack base, and the gap is described as closing, not closed.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and CREDIT_SPREADS: spreads are N/A, so no conflict.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury is N/A.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 52 versus sub-components 54 (goods) and 56 (expectations): consistent.

**N/A justification.** Treasury, credit spreads, services inflation, housing inflation and the dollar's current direction are not raised by participants in a way that can be scored.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

### Economic conditions
- **INFLATION_PRESSURE 52 ±8 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Melzer: "our goal of price stability has not been achieved... 3.6 percent rate during the past two months." Syron: firms "don't seem to feel they have the ability" to raise prices.
  - Summary: moderate inflation with upside risk but little pass-through now.
  - Direction: slightly above neutral because several see 3 percent core and rising commodity and food prices, offset by many reports that prices will not stick.
- **OUTPUT_GAP 44 ±6 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Parry: "the gap between potential and actual GDP may be diminishing". Kelley: "how fast the gap in the GDP gets closed."
  - Summary: slack remains at 6.5 percent unemployment and about 83 percent capacity utilization, but is closing.
  - Direction: below neutral because a gap is acknowledged. Melzer's "at or above potential next year" is forward-looking only.
- **LABOR_TIGHTNESS 38 ±6 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Mullins: unemployment "down to 6.4 percent... factory workweek and factory overtime both stand at post-World War II highs." Jordan: "only... marginal employment growth."
  - Summary: unemployment is elevated, but hiring is improving and pockets are tight.
  - Direction: below neutral by the level of unemployment.
- **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 62 ±7 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Broaddus: "the current acceleration in activity, which is quite strong". Greenspan: the fourth quarter is "technically unsustainable".
  - Summary: very strong now, with a likely partial pullback.
  - Direction: above trend because of the 5 percent fourth quarter and durable and equipment strength.
- **PRODUCTIVITY 58 ±10 Low.**
  - Evidence: Jordan: manufacturers report "very substantial increases in productivity". McDonough: firms are "concentrating on reducing staff and improving productivity."
  - Summary: only anecdotal remarks; productivity is perceived as solid.
  - Direction: above neutral on the anecdotes.

### Financial conditions
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 ±8 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Mullins: "the very accommodative monetary stance in place now for a year and a half." Lindsey: policy is "keeping real after-tax rates decidedly negative."
  - Summary: policy-driven ease with lower headwinds, partly offset by the long-rate backup.
  - Direction: below neutral because conditions are described as accommodative.
- **TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** No dysfunction discussed.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS N/A.** Spreads not discussed.
- **EQUITY_VALUATION 47 ±8 Low.**
  - Evidence: Mullins: the market "has gone essentially sideways for half a year. The economic outlook has caught up with the market."
  - Summary: no valuation concern was voiced.
  - Direction: about neutral.
- **BANKING_STRESS 30 ±8 Low.**
  - Evidence: McDonough: "commercial banks and investment banks are doing very well." Phillips: "the financial sector is certainly well positioned to support growth."
  - Summary: the banking system is viewed as healthy.
  - Direction: below neutral by the bright-line rule.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A.** No statement about the current direction of the dollar.

### Inflation sub-components
- INFLATION_GOODS 54. Phillips: "construction and industrial materials prices--scrap steel and lumber--are up." Offset by Syron and Keehn: manufacturers cannot raise prices, and oil is down.
- INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.
- INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 56. Phillips: the long-rate backup is "showing the deterioration in inflation expectations." Melzer: expectations "almost certain to intensify."

### Discussion-specific
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 62 ±6 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Greenspan: "the days of accommodation have got to be [about] over... a whole succession of them." Broaddus: "I would favor a 1/4 point increase in the funds rate now."
  - Summary: the vote is for no change, but a majority wants tightening soon and several want it now.
  - Direction: hawkish because the lean favors moving toward neutral.
- **DISSENT_INTENSITY 45 ±8 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: vote read-out: "Governor Angell A soft no" and "Governor Lindsey No". Angell: "do 50 and do it now."
  - Summary: two hawkish dissents and a split on timing and size, with agreement that tightening is coming.
  - Direction: above the unanimous range because there are two formal dissents, but the dispute is mostly tactical.
- **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 18 ±6 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Greenspan: "the credit numbers are finally beginning to move." Phillips: the long-rate backup shows deteriorating inflation expectations.
  - Summary: only brief, scattered references to credit and financial conditions.
  - Direction: low attention.
- **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 70 ±7 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Greenspan: "we would consider moving to that path fairly early in the year." Boehne: "the days of this degree of accommodation are numbered."
  - Summary: the signal is staged tightening early in 1994 with a symmetric directive for now.
  - Direction: hawkish because further increases are signaled.
- **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 38 ±8 Moderate.**
  - Evidence: Kelley: "a terribly close call... we just don't know." Syron: "we simply don't know what's going to happen."
  - Summary: standard to somewhat elevated caveats on the first-quarter pace, with firm conviction about direction.
  - Direction: moderate.
