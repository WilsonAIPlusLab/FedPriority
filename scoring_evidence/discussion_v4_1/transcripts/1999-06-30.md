# v4.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 1999-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. It contains the staff-Q&A questions from members (the staff answers are removed), the two-day economic go-around (Parry through Gramlich), the money-range discussion, questions to the staff on the policy options, the policy go-around, and a two-block roll-call read-out. The vote read-out blocks are unlabeled. The first block is all Yes and follows the single vote on the monetary ranges. The second block has President McTeer voting No and follows the policy decision.

**2. Era context.** Mid-1999. Strong expansion, a very tight labor market (national unemployment about 4.2-4.3 percent), low measured inflation, the fall 1998 financial turmoil largely past. The Committee had adopted an asymmetric (tightening-bias) directive in May. The question at this meeting is whether to take back part of the 75bp of easing from last fall, and with which directive tilt. The text itself, not this backdrop, sets the scores below.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish or tightening-now voices: Hoenig (stance accommodative, upside inflation risk, initially wanted a 50bp discussion), Moskow ("time to begin the process of tightening"), Minehan (core inflation near 3 percent "clearly unacceptable", 50-75bp needed, financial imbalances), Broaddus ("classic case of excessive growth in domestic demand", "excessively accommodative", opposes symmetry), Guynn (policy below equilibrium, one move not enough), Kelley ("danger zone"), Meyer (50bp prudent, Taylor rule), Gramlich (initially 50, talked down to 25), Poole, Jordan (stronger action is appropriate), Parry.
- Cautious or dovish voices: Boehne ("family dog, not wolf"), McTeer ("cry dog", deflationary forces, would not tighten; voted No), McDonough (productivity boom, "tactical symmetry", moves reluctantly), Stern (low inflation, mild).
- Greenspan: a productivity-acceleration, once-in-a-century structural-shift argument, no clear evidence of overheating in unit costs, but labor markets out of equilibrium. He recommends +25bp to 5 percent and a symmetric directive, and says the next move is "almost surely up".
- Staff content (Greenbook, Bluebook, Kohn, Prell, Fisher) is ignored except as members characterize it.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see measured inflation as modest and still low ("inflation numbers are still coming in at a modest level", Hoenig; "wolf vs. family dog", Boehne; Greenspan: no sign in unit costs). They also report prices that stick, rising wages, rising commodity prices, and forecasts of acceleration. Slightly above midpoint on pressures building, so about 54.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Many speak of "above trend" (Ferguson), "excessive growth in domestic demand" (Broaddus), "imports unsustainable ... excess aggregate demand" (Poole), and Greenspan says demand exceeds potential growth by about ½ point. Others (McTeer, Greenspan on product markets) see no overheating. Above 50 by the bright-line rule, modestly: 59.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "extremely tight" (Hoenig, Moskow), unemployment 3½ percent in the Kansas City District, 1.6 percent in the Twin Cities, 4.2-4.3 percent nationally, widespread shortages, large signing and retention bonuses. Very tight, so 75. The wage data are only beginning to move, which keeps it short of the 80s.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: "robust consumer spending", "hefty household spending", autos above 17 million, "housing sales absurdly high", capital investment strong, and booming real estate. Well above trend, but members expect some slowing: 67.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Productivity gains are central. McDonough puts productivity growth at 3 percent and possibly 4 percent or more in Q2. Greenspan describes "perhaps a once-in-a-century structural shift". Moskow reports the optimistic and the skeptical views. Significantly above trend, though the persistence is debated: 74.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: The financial strain has eased ("the financial turmoil ... has largely subsided, risk spreads are in fact lower", Hoenig). "Creditworthy borrowers are finding financing" (Moskow). Equity wealth is high. Hoenig's "accommodative" refers to the policy stance, not to conditions. Conditions are on the loose side of neutral: 42.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only long-rate movement and the TIPS premium are mentioned, with no dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Moskow says spreads are "still somewhat elevated, back to more reasonable levels". Hoenig says "risk spreads are in fact lower". The one explicit level statement is "somewhat elevated", which puts the score just above 50, with narrowing direction and low confidence: 52.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Minehan: "equity assets are so overvalued", "high stock market PE ratios". Greenspan: "we simultaneously argue that stock market prices are overvalued". Kelley: the market is "very precarious ... fragile". Valuation-concern view, so the stretched/rich range: 28.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mild pockets only. Bankers worry about consumer bankruptcies and delinquencies (Broaddus, Jordan). Examiners see "a little concern" about loan administration (Poole). Farm-area bank repercussions (Hoenig). McDonough reports declining delinquency rates in the Second District. Healthy system with minor concerns: 40.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Sparse. McTeer: "the dollar is still pretty strong". Kelley: the dollar "could begin to weaken". Stern notes a baseline of mild real depreciation. Roughly stable to modestly strong, with low confidence: 53.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more of the named construct. LABOR high (tight), OUTPUT_GAP above 50 (demand over potential), EQUITY 28 (valuation concern is the low end of the scale), BANKING 40 (healthy with minor concerns, so below 50). All consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: +25bp to 5 percent, effectively unanimous on moving. Several members wanted 50bp or more, or explicitly said one 25bp move is not enough (Guynn, Minehan, Meyer, Broaddus). Greenspan says the next move is "almost surely up". The directive goes symmetric, and the Chairman reads the Committee as "strongly in support of a 25 basis point increase and modestly in support of symmetry". Moderate hawkish: 62.
- Dissent: one formal dissent on the policy vote (McTeer No; his remarks show he preferred no tightening and an unbiased directive). Notable disagreement on tilt (symmetric vs. asymmetric, roughly evenly argued) and size (25 vs. 50). A real split on the economics (new economy/productivity vs. inflation risk), but the policy outcome was shared. Moderate: 42.
- Financial stability attention: moderate. Members discuss financial imbalances and equity valuation (Minehan, Kelley, Greenspan), consumer credit stress (Broaddus, Jordan, Poole), the Y2K liquidity period, and the markets "psychoanalyzing" the Fed (McDonough, Boehne, Kelley). Much of the policy go-around is about market reaction to the tilt, not about stability itself. Moderate: 38.
- Forward guidance: the directive goes symmetric ("no predilection about near-term policy action"), but the announcement says the Committee "must be especially alert to ... inflationary forces". Members expect more tightening, and a number defer on tilt only reluctantly. Mild hawkish: 58.
- Uncertainty: prominent and genuine. Productivity trend, NAIRU and output-gap measurement, Y2K, forecasting record, "something profoundly important ... we don't know where it is going to lead" (Greenspan), "unusual degree of uncertainty on all counts" (Meyer). Elevated to high: 62.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 59 (above trend language present, so > 50, satisfied). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 (no "tight" language; supportive/eased sense, < 50, satisfied). CREDIT_SPREADS 52 (stated level "somewhat elevated", so just > 50, satisfied; direction only is lower). BANKING_STRESS 40 (healthy/sound, < 50, satisfied). DOLLAR 53 ("still pretty strong", > 50, satisfied). TREASURY N/A.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 59 vs LABOR 75: 16 apart, a hair over 15. Explained: members say the labor market is tight but also say product markets show no tightness and that productivity growth lets output run above the earlier trend without a large gap (Greenspan, McTeer, McDonough). OUTPUT_GAP 59 vs CONSUMPTION 67: within 15. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 42 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 52: 10 apart, not contradictory. Spreads are described as back near "reasonable levels", while conditions overall are easy because of high wealth and eased strain. TREASURY N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 54 vs sub-components (goods 56, services 57, housing 62, expectations 60): parent a little lower because headline/core inflation is repeatedly described as still low.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning discussion by members. Inflation sub-components are scored because members cite goods, services (health care), housing and expectations (TIPS, surveys) explicitly, but only lightly, and carry no UNC/CONF.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "I continue to be impressed by the fact that the inflation numbers are still coming in at a modest level" (Hoenig). "We increasingly hear reports of price increases that stick" (Moskow).
- Center of gravity: inflation is still low, but pressures are visibly building and the forecasts show acceleration.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because members see low realized inflation with rising price, wage and commodity pressures. Higher means more pressure; evidence points to modestly more.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 59 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "vigorous growth, which may well be above trend" (Ferguson). "a classic case of excessive growth in domestic demand" (Broaddus). Greenspan: demand running in excess of potential by about ½ point, "a situation that is out of equilibrium".
- Center of gravity: most see activity above sustainable capacity, with dissenting views (McTeer on capacity utilization under 80 percent, Greenspan on no product-market tightness).
- Direction: above potential, so above 50 by rule.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 75 ±5 [High]**
- Evidence: "the labor market in our District remains extremely tight ... unemployment rate edged down to about 3½ percent" (Hoenig). Twin Cities rate 1.6 percent (Stern). "continued exceptionally tight labor markets pretty much across the board" (Broaddus).
- Center of gravity: unanimous reports of shortages, with wages only beginning to respond.
- Direction: well above neutral because unemployment is below 4.5 percent nationally and far lower in many districts.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 67 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: "robust household demand for goods, services, and housing" (Broaddus). "Housing sales are absurdly high. Capital investment is strong" (Greenspan).
- Center of gravity: consumption and investment are running well above trend, and members expect only gradual slowing.
- Direction: above trend because of repeated strong-momentum language from many participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 74 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: "productivity growth ... may have grown at an annual rate of 4 percent or even more" (McDonough). "something profound is happening ... a once-in-a-century structural shift" (Greenspan).
- Center of gravity: productivity growth is well above the old trend, with disagreement only on persistence.
- Direction: well above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "the financial turmoil that occurred last fall has largely subsided, risk spreads are in fact lower" (Hoenig). "creditworthy borrowers are finding financing" (Moskow).
- Center of gravity: conditions are easy to neutral, with strain eased and high wealth.
- Direction: below neutral because conditions are described as supportive rather than tightening.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** (no participant describes Treasury-market functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 52 ±6 [Low]**
- Evidence: "risk spreads, while still somewhat elevated, are back to more reasonable levels" (Moskow). "risk spreads are in fact lower" (Hoenig).
- Center of gravity: the level is near normal, slightly elevated, and narrowing.
- Direction: just above neutral because a stated "somewhat elevated" level is above 50, and the narrowing direction does not move the side of 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 28 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "equity assets are so overvalued" (Minehan). "stock market prices are overvalued" (Greenspan, as the argument being made by others and himself).
- Center of gravity: several members consider equities rich, with Kelley calling the market "fragile".
- Direction: toward the overvaluation end (low numbers) because the concern is valuation, not a decline.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "delinquencies are rising ... more concerned about consumer bankruptcies" (Broaddus). "a small degree of concern about loan administration" (Poole). "ongoing declines in delinquency rates" (McDonough).
- Center of gravity: banks are healthy, with small pockets of consumer-credit and farm worries.
- Direction: below neutral because of the healthy system with minor concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 53 ±8 [Low]**
- Evidence: "the dollar is still pretty strong" (McTeer). "the dollar's foreign exchange value could begin to weaken" (Kelley, a risk).
- Center of gravity: the dollar is stable to moderately strong, with an expected softening, and little direct discussion.
- Direction: slightly above neutral because the only level statement is "still pretty strong".

**INFLATION_GOODS: 56.** Evidence: price increases that stick for goods (Moskow), PPI intermediates (Ferguson), commodities firming and oil up (Kelley, Ferguson).
**INFLATION_SERVICES: 57.** Evidence: health care and hospital price adjustments (Guynn), medical costs (Jordan), restaurants raising prices (Moskow).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: 62.** Evidence: rents above New York and San Francisco and renovation quotes up 30 percent (Minehan, Broaddus), upscale housing prices rising (Boehne).
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 60.** Evidence: "signs that inflation expectations are rising ... TIPS inflation premium rising" (Hoenig), "both perceptions of current inflation and expectations about future inflation have moved up sharply" (Jordan). Counter: Greenspan on analysts' "no pricing power" long-term forecasts.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: "I think we have to move at this meeting" (Greenspan). "the next move we are likely to make is almost surely up" (Greenspan). "one 25 basis point tightening is probably not enough" (Guynn).
- Center of gravity: the Committee tightens 25bp with a majority expecting more, tempered by a symmetric directive and a cautious dissent (McTeer).
- Direction: hawkish because the Committee is raising rates and several favor a larger or further move. The symmetric directive and Boehne/McTeer/McDonough caution hold it below the 70s.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: the policy roll call shows "President McTeer No". McTeer: "I'd hate to see us lose our nerve ... risk bringing the experiment to a premature end." The sizing and tilt disputes (Broaddus, Guynn, Parry for asymmetry; Jordan, Boehne, Hoenig, Stern for symmetry).
- Center of gravity: one formal dissent plus clear, civil camps on the tilt and on 25 vs. 50.
- Direction: moderate disagreement because one dissent and notable differences in assessment and tactics, but a shared decision.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "financial imbalances now evidenced in high stock market PE ratios and in the sizable growth in corporate and personal debt" (Minehan). "a stock market that is in a very precarious position" (Kelley).
- Center of gravity: several participants address it, but the main thread is policy tilt and market reaction.
- Direction: moderate attention (not primary).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 58 ±7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: the announcement says the directive "includes no predilection about near-term policy action" but the Committee "must be especially alert to the emergence ... of inflationary forces". Poole: "more likely than not, we are going to need some increases later in the year".
- Center of gravity: formal neutral language with a hawkish verbal tilt.
- Direction: mild tightening bias because the signal is neutral in form but expected tightening is widely voiced.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: "an unusual degree of uncertainty on all counts" (Meyer). "something profoundly important, and we don't know where it is going to lead" (Greenspan). "We don't know and can't know what is over the horizon" (McTeer).
- Center of gravity: many emphasize the productivity, NAIRU and Y2K uncertainty and the poor forecast record.
- Direction: elevated to high because multiple speakers question the standard models.
