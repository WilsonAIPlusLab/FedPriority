# v4.1 scoring: member discussion transcript, meeting 2000-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion): staff-Q&A questions from members, the economic go-around, the policy go-around, the roll call. Staff turns are removed. A few passages are PDF-extraction artifacts (a few lost names/words, e.g. "with a long career in steel said", "customers such as and others"); none changes the meaning of any scored item.

**Era context.** Late 2000. Members describe a sharp slowdown from very rapid growth earlier in the year, with funds rate at 6.5 percent. The text itself supplies the picture: growth "unsustainable" earlier, now below trend, unemployment about 4 percent, energy prices and a fallen equity market. I score what participants say.

**Speakers.** Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chair McDonough, Governors Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, and Presidents McTeer, Minehan, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig, Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Stern, Santomero, Poole.

**Dimension by dimension (direction first).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as roughly at/just modestly above the midpoint, with the risk fading. "Quiescent," "in check," "generally good" (Guynn, Hoenig, Gramlich, McDonough), Greenspan sees a "very significantly subdued rate of inflation" with falling corporate pricing power, but also "slightly disturbing" rental movements; Broaddus says inflation is "drifting upward," Kelley "off its lows and slowly rising," Parry core PCE up to about 2 percent. Net about 50, wide band because views are mixed. Not scored from forecasts.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Growth is now below trend (Santomero, Moskow, McDonough), but the level of activity is judged by several to still be above sustainable capacity: Meyer "strong probability that the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity"; Moskow, slowing "needed to restore balance between aggregate demand and potential supply"; Guynn "sky-high levels...weren't sustainable". Gramlich and Stern/Minehan question the NAIRU, and Hoenig/Gramlich see slack coming. So level slightly above potential: 53, bright-line (above => >50) satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: "Still tight" is the dominant phrase (Moskow, Stern, Santomero "unemployment rate...4 percent or less," Broaddus "quite tight," Minehan 2.5 percent regional unemployment, "labor being extremely hard to find"). Counter: loosening at margin (Poole, Jordan "eased considerably," Broaddus "a little less tight"). UE about 4 percent, so above 55: 62.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Participants see spending as below trend and weakening: retail "anemic," autos weak, consumer confidence drop, capex plans cut, though some strength (Santomero capital goods orders, construction in places). Net 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed as strong structural productivity and high potential growth (potential cited at 4 to 4.5 percent; Kelley "basic momentum in productivity growth remains strong"; Minehan "strong underlying productivity"; Gramlich "productivity shock"). Above trend: 68.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Many cite tightening: Hoenig "tighter financial conditions...higher interest rate spreads, lower equity prices, tighter lending terms," Parry "tightening financial conditions on a broad range of fronts," Stern "flight to quality in the debt markets," Ferguson "ongoing tightening," McTeer "tighter credit conditions for all but the most exemplary credit risks." Counter-weights: Minehan "bankable credits are getting banked," Kelley "many interest rates have fallen." Somewhat tight: 62. Bright line (tight => >50) satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No participant describes Treasury-market dysfunction (Greenspan's swap-spread and CP questions are market-structure puzzles; McDonough's "markets...extraordinarily thin" is generic). N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Poole "much wider credit spreads that are reducing credit availability to more marginal borrowers"; Hoenig "higher interest rate spreads...junk bond default rates"; McTeer tighter credit for all but exemplary; Guynn shrinking syndicated loan market. Minehan says spreads are reflecting loans made "when spreads were really narrow in 1997," i.e. a widening from a compressed base, and she does not view it as a problem. The level language ("reducing credit availability," "less favorable") points to somewhat wide rather than extreme: 60. Level rule: reduced access / less favorable conditions are the stated level, so above 50 is supported; confidence moderate because the level is partly inferred from the widening.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stock decline widely noted (Hoenig "down significantly," Ferguson earnings revisions "fast and furious," Greenspan NASDAQ declines hurting high-tech investment) so there is concern about the downside; but Kelley says prices "still very high by historical standards." Valuation view mixed, concern about declines moderate: 56.
- BANKING_STRESS: Kelley "banks remain very healthy," Minehan "generally healthy banking conditions," Guynn nonperforming assets "modest and manageable." Jordan: higher charge-offs budgeted, voluntary repossessions; Hoenig "higher though still modest" delinquencies. Healthy with minor concerns: 38. Bright line (healthy => <50) satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Santomero cites "import competition stemming from the strong dollar" as a drag on steel/metals; Gramlich "rise in the dollar" as special factor; Ferguson expects a "slightly weaker dollar." Sparse. Strong dollar => >50: 57, low confidence.
- Inflation sub-components: Goods: energy spike vs falling construction materials, steel, software prices, little pricing power: 45. Services: Stern "price pressures...confined mostly to the services sector," health benefit costs rising (Santomero), medical costs (Greenspan): 58. Housing: Greenspan "movement in the underlying rental components," Hoenig house prices rising faster than general level: 57. Expectations: Gramlich "dropped to historical lows," Greenspan TIPS down about 0.3 point, Santomero "in check": 42.

**Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Committee held at 6.5 percent with risks weighted to weakness. Several (Poole, Hoenig, Gramlich, McTeer, Jordan) wanted a 25bp cut now; Guynn, Broaddus, Minehan would have preferred a balanced statement; Greenspan, McDonough, Ferguson, Moskow and others favored hold with a downside bias and possible intermeeting move. Lean dovish: 31.
- Dissent: unanimous roll call, no formal dissent. But at least five members said cut now was their first choice (reluctantly accepting hold) and three others preferred a balanced statement, with open debate on the economic assessment (Meyer vs Gramlich on NAIRU/sustainable capacity). Moderate: 36.
- Financial stability: Credit spreads, defaults (Moody's), equity decline, flight to quality, PG&E/Edison downgrades, thin markets, discussed by several but as one input among many: 45.
- Forward guidance: asymmetric risk statement toward weakness; Greenspan expects a likely January conference call move; many "stand by our telephones." Strong easing bias without a commitment: 14.
- Uncertainty: High: Greenspan "we really do not know," "go/no go" over the coming weeks, nonlinear dynamics, anecdotes vs models, Ferguson "great uncertainty." 68.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure: evidence says contained/slightly rising, score about 50 is consistent. Higher LABOR_TIGHTNESS = tighter: evidence "still tight," 62 matches. Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN = more hawkish: evidence says ease/hold with easing bias, 31 matches. Higher FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE = more hawkish: downside bias, 14 matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: healthy banks, 38 matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter: tightening, 62 matches.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 (above sustainable capacity language => >50, not "below potential" at the level); FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 62 (>50 for tight); CREDIT_SPREADS 60 (reduced availability / wider level => >50); BANKING_STRESS 38 (<50); DOLLAR_STRENGTH 57 (>50); TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs LABOR 62: gap 9, within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 53 vs CONSUMPTION 36: gap 17, slightly above 15; explanation: members describe the level of activity as still above sustainable capacity after a long boom while the flow of spending is now below trend and decelerating; I do not raise consumption since participants clearly describe soft spending. FC 62 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 60: co-move. FC vs TREASURY: Treasury N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 50 vs sub-components (45, 58, 57, 42): average about 50, consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury market functioning not raised by members as a problem.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 ±6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "core inflation remains modest"; Kelley, "inflation is off its lows and is slowly rising"; Broaddus, "inflation is drifting upward."
- Summary: Contained but not falling, with mixed signals.
- Direction: At neutral because "quiescent" and "slightly disturbing" patterns offset.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 53 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Meyer, "strong probability that the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity before this loss of momentum"; Moskow, slowing "needed to restore balance between aggregate demand and potential supply."
- Summary: Level modestly above potential though growth is now below trend.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral because several see excess demand being unwound; below-trend growth is flow, not level.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Santomero, "unemployment rate in our region has remained at 4 percent or less all year...conditions remain tight"; Broaddus, "labor market situation that is tight by historical standards."
- Summary: Tight at about 4 percent unemployment, easing only at the margin.
- Direction: Above neutral because the level is tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: McTeer, "anemic retail sales and the enormous discounting of merchandise"; Hoenig, "Consumers are reining in their spending...businesses more generally are retrenching."
- Summary: Spending and investment broadly weak and decelerating.
- Direction: Below trend because most describe weakening retail, autos, capex.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 68 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kelley, "the basic momentum in productivity growth remains strong"; Minehan, "environment of strong underlying productivity."
- Summary: Structural productivity seen as high, potential growth elevated.
- Direction: Above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Parry, "tightening financial conditions on a broad range of fronts"; Hoenig, "higher interest rate spreads, lower equity prices, tighter lending terms and standards."
- Summary: Conditions have tightened noticeably.
- Direction: Above neutral (tighter).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Poole, "much wider credit spreads that are reducing credit availability to more marginal borrowers"; McTeer, "tighter credit conditions for all but the most exemplary credit risks."
- Summary: Spreads wide enough to reduce access at the margin; Minehan notes the base was very narrow.
- Direction: Above neutral on the stated reduced access / less favorable level.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Hoenig, "stock market is down significantly"; Kelley, "market prices are still very high by historical standards."
- Summary: Declines are a concern, but valuations are not seen as depressed.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral for downside concern.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kelley, "banks remain very healthy"; Guynn, nonperforming assets "still at modest and manageable levels."
- Summary: Healthy banks with edging-up charge-offs.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±10 [Low]**
- Evidence: Santomero, "increased import competition stemming from the strong dollar"; Ferguson, "slightly weaker dollar in the forecast."
- Summary: Dollar seen as strong, a modest drag, only briefly mentioned.
- Direction: Above neutral (strength).

**INFLATION_GOODS 45; INFLATION_SERVICES 58; INFLATION_HOUSING 57; INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 42.**
- Evidence: Stern, "Price pressures...confined mostly to the services sector"; Greenspan, "movement in the underlying rental components"; Gramlich, long-term expectations "dropped to historical lows."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 31 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Gramlich, "I think monetary policy has become too tight"; Greenspan, "no change in the funds rate, but with a bias toward the down side."
- Summary: Hold with strong easing bias, with a sizable group wanting to cut now.
- Direction: Below neutral (dovish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 36 ±7 [High]**
- Evidence: Poole, "I would have preferred to reduce the federal funds rate at this meeting"; Broaddus, "My preference would have been to have a balanced risk statement."
- Summary: Unanimous vote but a spread of preferences from cut-now to balanced.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement; no formal dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Ferguson, Moody's expects defaults "more than triple"; Hoenig, "combination of tighter financial conditions and large financial imbalances increases the risks."
- Summary: Several address credit and equity strains meaningfully.
- Direction: Moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 ±6 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "if the erosion continues, we very likely will have to move before the next meeting"; Gramlich, "stand by our telephones."
- Summary: Downside-weighted statement and expected possible intermeeting easing.
- Direction: Below neutral (easing signal).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 ±8 [High]**
- Evidence: Greenspan, "we really do not know the answer for the intermediate period"; Ferguson, "We are in a period...of great uncertainty."
- Summary: Many emphasize difficulty of reading whether slowing will stabilize.
- Direction: High uncertainty.
