# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2003-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (staff turns removed). It holds members' questions in the staff Q&A, the economic go-around (Broaddus through Bernanke, with a coffee break), the Chairman's summing-up and policy recommendation, the policy go-around (each member "supports the recommendation", with some reservations on communication), a discussion of zero-bound contingency planning, and a vote read-out (all listed voters Yes; the read-out is unanimous, no dissent).

**2. Era context.** March 2003, on the eve of the Iraq war, funds rate 1 1/4 percent, a soft recovery, with energy prices elevated. The text itself supplies this: rising gasoline and oil prices, February labor-market weakness, war uncertainty, a sizable output gap, low and declining inflation, and talk of deflation risk and the zero bound. I score only what members say.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (summing-up: "Knightian uncertainty", economy weak, next move more likely a cut but no conviction, hold, no balance-of-risks statement, "heightened surveillance"); McDonough ("a prudent central banker thinks a lot, does nothing"); Broaddus (Greenbook plausible, downside risks "considerable", deflation preparedness); Hoenig (sluggish, wait and watch); Guynn (not the optimum time for another easing); Parry (significant easing seems appropriate as insurance, but supports the recommendation); Moskow, Poole, Stern, Santomero, Pianalto (patient, wait); Minehan (downside risks, Greenbook too optimistic, wants "downside" language); Kohn (next action more likely easing, no conviction); McTeer (downside risks prevail, not now); Gramlich (be ready to act under Knightian uncertainty, think about unconventional measures); Ferguson, Olson, Bies (financial markets and banks); Bernanke (slack is substantial, hope to consider easing next meeting if no improvement).

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Hoenig: "wage and price pressures obviously remain pretty well subdued"; Kohn: "low and declining inflation"; Parry: core PCE forecast 1 1/4 percent in 2004; Broaddus sees possible disinflation, deflation not a strong current threat. Offsets: higher energy prices and input costs (Moskow, Guynn) with "little or no pricing power". Level: well below the midpoint, but not deflationary: 33.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Kohn: "sizable output gap"; Minehan: "output about 3/4 percentage point below potential" through 2004 even in the Greenbook baseline; Bernanke: "a lot of accumulated slack"; Parry: "excess capacity rises further"; Guynn/Pianalto: low capacity utilization. Bright-line: below potential implies below 50. Score 36.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Loose. "No employment gains for about two years" (Bernanke), "hiring paralysis" (Moskow), February report with "sizable and widespread job losses" (Santomero), unemployment 5 1/2 percent in 2004 baseline (Minehan), long-term unemployment up (Minehan). A few scattered shortages (Guynn's small firms). Score 34.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Below trend. Consumer spending slowing and auto sales soft (Moskow, Santomero, Gramlich), business investment flat/weak with "no growth" in capex (Poole, Pianalto), housing the strong spot. Net: 36.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Above trend and repeatedly cited as a strength: Broaddus "robust trend productivity growth", Stern "productivity numbers", Pianalto "healthy rate of multifactor productivity growth", Greenspan "staff forecast for this quarter is a 3 percent rate", Moskow "strong productivity growth". Score 66.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Mixed but on balance supportive/loose. Monetary policy "stimulative", "accommodative" (Hoenig, Moskow, Santomero, Ferguson "by many measures still supportive"), banks "very liquid and anxious to lend" (Pianalto), long rates down. Offsets: equity risk premium "highest in more than a decade" (Ferguson, Kohn), equity prices down until the recent rally, debt spreads still wide by historical standards. Bright-line: accommodative/supportive implies below 50. Score 43.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Participants mention rates being marked down across the curve (Kohn) and bond prices falling on the rally (Ferguson), and Greenspan's discussion of not pegging longer rates; these are yield movements, with no dysfunction or functioning commentary. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Bies: "debt spreads are wider than historically, the spreads have moved in" and excessive spreads "continued to improve"; Ferguson: corporate bond purchases "drove yield spreads on those assets down further"; Olson: banks "reaching for yield". Level reading: a stated level above historical norms implies above 50; direction is narrowing. Score 54, Low-to-Moderate confidence.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Participants discuss equity weakness and the equity risk premium at its highest in over a decade (Ferguson, Kohn), then the sharp rally in the last few days (Poole, Ferguson, Greenspan), with Bies saying equity markets "have shifted to neutral". Valuation/concern view: modestly depressed, easing. Score 57.
- BANKING_STRESS: Healthy. Bies: record earnings two years running, risk management strong, "banks deleveraged"; Olson: commercial credit quality "has leveled out and maybe even improved"; Pianalto: bankers "very liquid". Weak loan demand and CRE (Guynn, Minehan) are mild offsets, and Olson notes reach-for-yield. Bright-line: healthy implies below 50. Score 27.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Mixed. Poole: "a big increase in the value of the dollar" in a one-day move; Ferguson: "the dollar rallied" in the last days but "the dollar has weakened a bit on net". Net: near neutral with slight weakness over the longer window, recent rally. Score 48, Low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION means more pressure: evidence says subdued, so below 50, correct. Higher LABOR means tighter: evidence says loose, so below 50, correct. CREDIT_SPREADS higher means more stress: level stated wider than historical but tightening, so a modest above-50 score. EQUITY_VALUATION higher means more stress/depressed valuation: premium high, so above 50. BANKING higher means more stress: healthy, so low.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: unanimous hold at 1 1/4 percent. Several members explicitly lean toward easing (Parry: "significant easing... seems appropriate"; Minehan: risks on the downside, concerned how long to stay put; McTeer drawn toward lower-rate simulations; Kohn: "next action is more likely to be an easing"; Greenspan: "next move is more likely to be a reduction than an increase"; Bernanke and Gramlich: do not wait indefinitely, consider easing). No one argues for tightening. Others (Guynn, Santomero, Poole, Stern) simply argue for patience. Hold with a mild-to-moderate easing bias: 38.
- Disagreement: no dissent, but reservations. Parry and Minehan see an economic case for easing/downside risk language; Poole and Broaddus worry about communication of "heightened surveillance" and dropping the balance-of-risks statement; Minehan prefers stating downside risks; Guynn/Santomero see easing as premature; Gramlich/Bernanke push against waiting indefinitely. Differences are mostly emphasis and tactics, with some on the economics (Broaddus sanguine vs Minehan/Parry). Broad agreement with some reservations: 24.
- Financial stability attention: Bies's extended account of banks, credit spreads, household and corporate balance sheets; Olson on banks and reaching for yield; Ferguson on equity risk premium and asset pools; Kohn on equity premium and credit markets; Greenspan on money funds and not pegging rates. Several members, mostly as part of the conditions discussion, not the focus: 36.
- Forward guidance: no balance-of-risks statement, "heightened surveillance", weekly conference calls, Greenspan's stated instinct that the next move is a cut, and a possible call to act in a few weeks. Easing lean with data dependence, but deliberately avoiding a directional signal: 38.
- Uncertainty: dominant theme. "Maximum uncertainty" (Bernanke), "extraordinary uncertainty" (Guynn), "unusually high" (Moskow), risk vs uncertainty distinction (Santomero, Ferguson), Greenspan: "a true Knightian uncertainty issue... nobody can know" and a refusal to give a balance of risks. Very high, near extreme: 91.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 (<50, below potential): satisfied. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 43 (<50, accommodative/supportive): satisfied. CREDIT_SPREADS 54 (>50, stated level wider than historical; narrowing noted): satisfied. BANKING_STRESS 27 (<50, healthy): satisfied. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 48 (mixed; recent rally vs net weakness): no clean direction, kept near neutral with Low confidence. TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 36 / LABOR 34 / CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 36: all within 15 points, consistent with a soft recovery with slack. FC 43 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 54: FC below 50 comes from accommodative policy and liquid banks, spreads are above 50 because members describe the level as wider than historical even as it narrows; the gap is about 11 points and is explained, and the equity premium pushes FC toward neutral. FC vs Treasury: Treasury is N/A. INFLATION_PRESSURE 33 vs expectations 44: expectations described as anchored/credible at a low level, consistent with the parent.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: only yield moves mentioned, no market-functioning discussion. INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES, HOUSING: members mention energy and input costs and weak pricing power but no goods/services/shelter breakdown. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is scored because Broaddus ("credibility for low inflation means neither rising actual inflation nor rising inflation expectations pose much of a risk") and Minehan ("inflation credibility") address anchoring.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Hoenig: "wage and price pressures obviously remain pretty well subdued." Kohn: "a sizable output gap and low and declining inflation."
- Summary: pressures are low and falling with deflation risk discussed, partly offset by energy and cost increases without pricing power.
- Direction: below neutral because members see low and declining inflation.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-6 (High)**
- Minehan: "Excess capacity persists with the unemployment rate at 5 1/2 percent and output about 3/4 percentage point below potential." Bernanke: "a lot of accumulated slack in the economy."
- Summary: broad agreement on slack and excess capacity persisting into 2004.
- Direction: below potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 34 +-6 (High)**
- Moskow: "The phrase we keep hearing is 'hiring paralysis.'" Bernanke: "essentially no employment gains for about two years."
- Summary: weak hiring, job losses, long-term unemployment rising.
- Direction: below full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +-6 (High)**
- Poole: "capital spending is flat." Santomero: "indications that the growth of consumer spending is beginning to slow."
- Summary: soft consumption (except housing) and flat investment, with a rebound expected but not yet evident.
- Direction: below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 66 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Broaddus: "the prospect of continued robust trend productivity growth." Pianalto: "healthy rate of multifactor productivity growth."
- Summary: productivity cited as a strength by many members.
- Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Hoenig: "financial market conditions and a banking industry that are generally able to be supportive of an expansion." Ferguson: equity risk premium "highest level in more than a decade."
- Summary: accommodative policy and liquid banks outweigh a high equity premium and still-wide spreads.
- Direction: below neutral (looser than neutral).

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 +-7 (Low)**
- Bies: "while debt spreads are wider than historically, the spreads have moved in." Ferguson: "purchases of corporate bonds drove yield spreads on those assets down further."
- Summary: level somewhat above normal but narrowing, so only slightly above neutral.
- Direction: slightly above neutral by the level reading.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Kohn: "the equity premium... has risen sharply." Bies: "equity markets we know at least have shifted to neutral."
- Summary: depressed valuations and high premium earlier, easing with the recent rally.
- Direction: modestly above neutral (depressed).

**BANKING_STRESS: 27 +-6 (High)**
- Bies: "commercial banks in the United States had record earnings in 2002. And they had record earnings in 2001." Olson: credit quality "has leveled out and maybe even improved."
- Summary: banks healthy and liquid; weak loan demand is the issue, not stress.
- Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 +-8 (Low)**
- Poole: "a big increase in the value of the dollar—a large one-day move yesterday." Ferguson: "the dollar has weakened a bit on net."
- Summary: recent sharp rally against a mild net weakening.
- Direction: roughly neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS / SERVICES / HOUSING: N/A.** **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44.** Broaddus: "our credibility for low inflation means that neither rising actual inflation nor rising inflation expectations pose much of a risk."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +-6 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "the probability that our next move is more likely to be a reduction than an increase." Parry: "a significant easing of policy seems appropriate."
- Summary: unanimous hold with an easing bias held by several and patience by the rest; nobody argues for tightening.
- Direction: dovish side of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 24 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Parry: "although I think an economic case can be made for easing policy, I support your recommendation." Poole/Broaddus voice communication reservations; Minehan prefers stating downside risk.
- Summary: no dissents, a handful of reservations on tactics and communication, little disagreement on the assessment.
- Direction: broad agreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 36 +-8 (Moderate)**
- Bies's extended discussion of bank earnings, credit spreads and balance sheets; Olson: "banks reaching for yield."
- Summary: several members address financial conditions in some depth but it is not the focus of the meeting.
- Direction: limited-to-moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 +-7 (Moderate)**
- Greenspan: "we probably ought to stay where we are, indicate that we do not have the capability of truly making a judgment about the balance of risks"; "heightened surveillance"; possible telephone conference and easing if no improvement.
- Summary: no explicit signal, but the underlying lean and contingency planning point toward easing.
- Direction: below neutral (easing lean with data dependence).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 91 +-4 (High)**
- Greenspan: "a true Knightian uncertainty issue... nobody can know." Bernanke: "a period of maximum uncertainty."
- Summary: nearly every member stresses extraordinary uncertainty, and the Chair declines to give a balance of risks.
- Direction: far above normal.
