# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - Meeting 1997-08-19 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant discussion transcript. It holds the questions members put to staff, the regional go-around and national outlook, the directive-wording exchange, the Chairman's policy summary, the policy go-around, and the vote read-out. Staff turns have been removed, so some answers are missing and Greenspan's follow-up remarks sometimes appear without the question that prompted them. A few speaker or contact names are blank. None of these gaps hides a passage whose meaning I cannot tell, so I use no garbled-text penalty beyond confidence on the thinnest items.

**Era context.** Summer 1997. Members describe a long expansion with a 4.8 percent unemployment rate (a 24-year low), very low and falling inflation (core CPI +2.4 percent, PPI down seven months, the smallest gross-domestic-purchases deflator rise since 1961), a strengthening dollar, and a booming stock market. The debate is "old paradigm" versus "new paradigm" and whether tight labor markets will eventually raise inflation. I score what members say, not what I recall of the period.

**Speakers.** Regional presidents McTeer, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Hoenig, Guynn, Broaddus, Stern, Boehne, Melzer, Jordan. Vice Chair McDonough. Governors Kelley, Rivlin, Meyer, Phillips. Chairman Greenspan. Most describe a strong, balanced, tight-labor-market economy with benign current inflation and upside risk to inflation. Boehne and Kelley lean toward waiting. Guynn, Melzer, Broaddus and Stern are more concerned about future inflation.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see current inflation pressure as below the midpoint. Greenspan says "we still may be experiencing disinflation". McTeer cites the seven-month PPI decline and the lowest core CPI since 1965. Hotels, commercial real estate, rents and health costs are exceptions. The risk is described as upside and building ("running out the string"), but members score the present as benign. Melzer disputes the level, saying CPI is back near 2-3/4 percent and median CPI near 3 percent, which pulls the score up somewhat. Score about 43.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Above the midpoint. McDonough: the economy is "about 2 percentage points above" potential GDP. Meyer: "operating beyond its sustainable capacity". Hoenig: "a bit over potential". Parry: "relatively high levels of resource utilization". Kelley notes capacity utilization is "down a little". Bright line requires more than 50. Score 59.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Above the midpoint, and strongly worded. Melzer: "extremely tight". Parry: "extremely tight". Moskow: highest Q4 hiring intentions since 1978. Greenspan: the pool of people who want to work is shrinking fast and "when we run out of a product--in this case labor--its price goes up". The rubric's unemployment anchor (4.8 percent) points toward about 52, while the language points to 65 or more. Meyer assumes the economy is below NAIRU. I blend to 63.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Modestly above trend. Consumer spending rebounded in Q3 (Melzer: "above trend", with a "recent surge"). Capital spending is "booming" (Jordan), and Phillips sees no reason for business fixed investment to fade. Second-quarter weakness was a pause and Jordan thinks the quarterly mix was unsustainable. Score 57.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed, but members say the official data show no acceleration. Moskow: "the most recent official statistics do not provide much evidence". Rivlin: "a major mystery". Greenspan: nonfinancial corporate productivity about 2-1/4 percent, but total nonfarm productivity modest. Jordan argues gains are unmeasured. Net: roughly at trend as measured. Score 50 with wide band.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Below the midpoint (supportive). Phillips: "The capital market is supportive of further spending". Long rates eased and the yield curve is flatter and lower (Kelley). Asset prices are booming (Minehan, Jordan). Bright line requires less than 50. Score 38.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Members discuss the new inflation-indexed note only as an inflation-expectations indicator, and McDonough notes the issue is still too new and illiquid to read much from. No dysfunction is described. Treated as N/A because no one raised market functioning.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: No member talks about spreads. The only relevant evidence is credit availability: Jordan reports that "regionals and super regionals continue to lower their credit standards", and Hoenig reports bank lending picked up. This implies ample credit, below normal stress. Score 35 with Low confidence and a wide band. The bright-line rule is satisfied (below 50, no wide-spread language).
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Moderate concern about elevated asset prices. Stern asks about the staff's assumed equity decline. Phillips lists "the stock market could take a dive off its recent roller coaster ride" as a risk. Minehan asks what to do when "asset prices are booming". Greenspan refers to "irrational exuberance". No one calls it a bubble. Score 32.
- BANKING_STRESS: Little direct discussion. Jordan reports lower credit standards at larger banks, rising retail vacancies, and developers "later and later in their payments". Hoenig: loan activity is fair. Boehne: loan volumes are up slightly. Banks are described as functioning normally, with some commercial real estate concentration risk. Mild stress pockets, system not described as impaired. Score 40, Low confidence.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Above the midpoint. Kelley: "The dollar has continued to strengthen on balance". Meyer: "the further appreciation of the dollar". Rivlin: "the high dollar". McDonough and Phillips treat it as a restraint on inflation. Minehan alone mentions dollar depreciation, as a forecast difference. Bright line satisfied. Score 63.

**Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: McDonough expects core goods prices to "remain subdued". McTeer reports chip prices down and failed petrochemical increases. Meyer says the dollar's pass-through is less transitory. Score 35.
- SERVICES: McDonough is "somewhat more concerned about increases in the prices of services". Greenspan expects the services CPI to "look a little stronger". Retail services pressure is subdued. Score 50.
- HOUSING: Greenspan: the rent-to-property-value ratio is stabilizing and the "little pop" in the last CPI. Minehan: "an uptick in housing-related components of the CPI". McTeer: local house price inflation is a third of the national rate. Score 56.
- EXPECTATIONS: Broaddus reads the TIPS gap as 2-3/4 percent CPI, "volatile", and credibility "not fully consistent with price stability". Melzer wants to lock in lower inflation. Score 50.

Coherence: parent INFLATION_PRESSURE (43) is close to the sub-component mix of 35, 50, 56, 50.

**Tier 3 in words.**
- Policy lean: Every participant accepts the Chairman's "B" recommendation: hold the funds rate and keep the asymmetric tightening bias. The Chairman says "very little reason to move today". Melzer would like to act preemptively but cannot see a rationale. Guynn expects an "anticipatory anti-inflationary policy move in the future". Boehne favors "wait and watch". Hold with a tightening bias is lean hawkish.
- Disagreement: no dissents, a unanimous 10-0 vote read-out, and a unanimous show of hands on the directive structure. There is a short exchange over whether asymmetry language can be changed (Broaddus raised a wording point, Kelley wanted no change) and a split between "wait" (Boehne, Kelley) and "preempt" (Melzer, Guynn). These are tactical, not about the economic assessment. Near consensus.
- Financial stability attention: Limited. Real estate and farmland valuations (Jordan, McTeer, Minehan), asset prices versus consumer prices (Minehan and Greenspan), Asian currency questions (members' questions to staff). It is context, not a main subject.
- Forward guidance: Retaining the asymmetric directive and Greenspan's "we will have no choice but to tighten" if demand and inventories stay strong give a mild tightening signal, offset by "very little reason to move today". Mild tightening bias.
- Uncertainty: Several members stress an unusual lack of clarity. Rivlin, Phillips and Kelley say data do not help decide between paradigms ("straws in the wind"). Meyer sees "crosscurrents". Moskow says it will take "quite some time" to know. Others state their forecasts with normal confidence. Elevated but not extreme.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 59 (greater than 50, "above potential" language present). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 (less than 50, "supportive"). CREDIT_SPREADS 35 (less than 50, no widening-only reading). BANKING_STRESS 40 (less than 50, no stress language). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 63 (greater than 50, appreciation). TREASURY_STRESS N/A.

**Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 59, LABOR 63 and CONSUMPTION 57 are within 6 points of each other. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 and CREDIT_SPREADS 35 move together. Treasury stress is N/A in normal times. Inflation parent consistent with sub-components.

**Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE is more pressure; members say low now, so under 50. Higher LABOR is tighter; members say extremely tight, so above 50. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is tighter; members say supportive, so under 50. Higher DOLLAR is more strength; members say strengthened, so above 50. Higher EQUITY score is more downside or stress; members voice mild overvaluation worry, so under 50.

**N/A.** TREASURY_STRESS only. All other financial dimensions have at least some participant evidence.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 43 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we still may be experiencing disinflation"; McTeer, core CPI rise "the lowest since 1965". Center of gravity: current inflation is low and falling, with upside risk later. Direction: below neutral because actual price readings are benign.

**OUTPUT_GAP 59 +-6 (High).** Evidence: McDonough, "operating about 2 percentage points above what would appear to be potential GDP"; Meyer, "an economy that is operating beyond its sustainable capacity". Center of gravity: resource use is clearly above sustainable levels, though growth is near trend. Direction: above neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 63 +-7 (High).** Evidence: Melzer, "labor markets have become extremely tight"; Greenspan, "when we run out of a product--in this case labor--its price goes up". Center of gravity: very tight labor markets with only modest measured wage growth. Direction: above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 57 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Melzer, "a recent surge in consumer expenditures"; Jordan, "booming capital spending" in the second quarter. Center of gravity: demand is solid and a little above trend. Direction: above neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY 50 +-9 (Moderate).** Evidence: Moskow, "the most recent official statistics do not provide much evidence of this"; Greenspan, nonfinancial corporate productivity "something like 2-1/4 percent". Center of gravity: measured productivity near trend with a debated, unmeasured upside. Direction: at neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "The capital market is supportive of further spending"; Kelley, "long-term interest rates have eased on balance". Center of gravity: easy conditions with booming asset prices. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS N/A.** No participant discussed Treasury market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 35 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Jordan, "the regionals and super regionals continue to lower their credit standards". Center of gravity: ample credit availability, no mention of spreads or stress. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION 32 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Phillips, "The stock market could take a dive off its recent roller coaster ride"; Minehan, "asset prices are booming". Center of gravity: moderate concern about elevated asset prices. Direction: below neutral (elevated valuation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS 40 +-10 (Low).** Evidence: Jordan, developers "later and later in their payments"; Hoenig, "Bank lending picked up in July". Center of gravity: banks functioning, with some commercial real estate concentration notes. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 63 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kelley, "The dollar has continued to strengthen on balance"; Meyer, "the further appreciation of the dollar". Center of gravity: dollar up and restraining inflation. Direction: above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS 35.** McDonough, core goods prices "will remain subdued". **INFLATION_SERVICES 50.** McDonough, "more concerned about increases in the prices of services". **INFLATION_HOUSING 56.** Greenspan, the CPI rent ratio "stabilizing or moving back to a somewhat higher path". **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 50.** Broaddus, credibility "not fully consistent with price stability" given volatile indexed-note spread.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 56 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Greenspan, "little reason to discard the asymmetric directive toward tightening"; Boehne, "a strong case for us to wait and watch". Center of gravity: hold with tightening bias. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 10 +-4 (High).** Evidence: all participants accepted the Chairman's recommendation; the vote read-out is all Yes. Center of gravity: near consensus. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 25 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "What do we do with monetary policy when there is no inflation but asset prices are booming?"; Jordan on farmland prices. Center of gravity: context only. Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 60 +-6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Greenspan, "we will have no choice but to tighten" if demand stays strong; Guynn, "an anticipatory anti-inflationary policy move in the future is still quite likely". Center of gravity: mild tightening bias retained. Direction: above neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 48 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Rivlin, "a major mystery about what is going on, especially about productivity"; Meyer, "crosscurrents on both output growth and inflation". Center of gravity: elevated paradigm uncertainty, but members still forecast with normal confidence. Direction: moderately elevated.
