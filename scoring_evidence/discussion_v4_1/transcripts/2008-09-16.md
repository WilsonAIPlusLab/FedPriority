## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

INFLATION_PRESSURE 62 +-6 Moderate: Yellen "core inflation will remain uncomfortably high for a while longer"; Hoenig "Our core inflation is still above where it should be"; Lacker "still in the midst of an upsurge in core inflation ... near 3 percent"; Bullard headline CPI "about 6 1/4 percent year-to-date"; Kroszner "uncomfortably elevated levels now". Offset by many members saying commodity prices have fallen and pressures are easing (Stern, Evans, Bernanke "less worrisome now"). Level clearly above target, direction improving.

OUTPUT_GAP 36 +-6 Moderate: Kohn "Resource utilization was falling appreciably ... higher output gaps"; Bernanke "slack is increasing", "we are in a period that will be designated as an official NBER recession"; Lacker "sluggish". Below potential, not deep.

LABOR_TIGHTNESS 34 +-6 Moderate: Rosengren "labor market is weak and getting weaker. The unemployment rate has risen 1.1 percentage points since April"; Kohn "decline in employment shows no signs of abating"; Yellen "labor markets are weakening across the board". No level given by members; slack and rising unemployment.

CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 30 +-6 Moderate: Kohn "consumption has weakened substantially ... three months in a row of declines in the retail control component"; Yellen "cutbacks in spending are widespread"; Rosengren "reluctance to consume or to invest"; Pianalto "new projects are all but impossible to finance"; Kohn "Activity is more likely to stagnate than to decline"; Plosser notes survey improvement (minor offset).

PRODUCTIVITY 60 +-8 Moderate: Kohn "surprisingly good growth of productivity is holding down unit labor costs"; Bernanke "productivity has been strong". Above trend, two speakers only.

FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 87 +-5 High: Bernanke "Conditions clearly have worsened ... Almost all major financial institutions are facing significant stress ... securitization markets are moribund. Credit terms and conditions are quite tight"; Duke "The markets are fragile to dead"; Rosengren "Many securitization vehicles are frozen"; Stern "most severe financial crisis ... in my career"; Warsh "prospect of some meaningful discontinuity and of some systemic risk remains real". Tight/crisis, above 50.

TREASURY_STRESS N/A: no participant discusses Treasury market functioning (Bullard's "safe interest rates have fallen dramatically" is a yield move only; Warsh on tri-party repo plumbing is not Treasury).

CREDIT_SPREADS 80 +-7 High: Rosengren "credit spreads are rising, and the cost and availability of financing is becoming more difficult"; Kohn "a high cost of capital for borrowers relative to benchmark interest rates"; Duke "breakeven spread for a loan was 400 basis points over LIBOR", "secondary market for jumbos ... nonexistent"; Pianalto loan repriced from 7 percent to 12 percent. Stated high level plus availability rationing, above 50.

EQUITY_VALUATION 55 +-10 Low: only price moves and share-price pressure reported, no valuation view: Lacker "at least some of changes in equity prices"; Warsh "equity moves on the other broker-dealers"; Duke "they started to short the stock". Default rule, price move on the down side, 45-55 band upper end.

BANKING_STRESS 80 +-6 High: Bernanke "Almost all major financial institutions are facing significant stress, particularly difficulties in raising capital"; Duke "There is no more capital available in external markets ... contract the balance sheet and not lend"; Kroszner "very large regional banks and a very large thrift ... facing a lot of challenges"; Pianalto "banks ... find it very, very difficult to attract new capital". Partial offset: Duke "credit metrics are just nowhere near what they were in the S&L crisis" (nonperformers 1.2 percent); Warsh notes Fed actions "cushion the blow".

DOLLAR_STRENGTH 62 +-6 Moderate: Yellen "now that the dollar has begun to rise"; Warsh "strength in the exchange value of the dollar"; Kohn "Because of the stronger dollar, we will be able to rely less on exports"; Bernanke "The dollar has also strengthened". Appreciation, noted as notable but not extreme.

INFLATION_GOODS 57: Energy and commodity prices falling (Stern, Bernanke "Steel scrap is down 40 percent", Lacker, Yellen "Import inflation has already begun to ease") against sticky retail pricing (Fisher "average price increase of 10 percent" at Wal-Mart; Pianalto "suppliers are trying to maintain their prices"); Kohn "rise in import prices ... higher than anticipated".
INFLATION_SERVICES N/A: services prices not discussed.
INFLATION_HOUSING N/A: shelter or rent inflation not discussed (house prices and housing activity are discussed, not housing inflation).
INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 55: Yellen "remarkable decline in inflation compensation for the next five years"; Lockhart "upward drift in some inflation expectation measures appears to have been reversed"; Lacker "five-year, five-year-forward inflation compensation seems more consistent with a trend above 2 1/2 percent"; Bullard "unmoored expectations" as a risk; Plosser "I do not perceive an immediate threat to the unanchoring of expectations". Contained, slightly above anchored.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 47 +-6 Moderate: hold at 2 percent, endorsed by essentially all (Bernanke "keep the funds rate at its current level"; "inappropriate to increase rates ... It is simply premature"). Dovish side: Rosengren "I support alternative A to reduce the fed funds rate 25 basis points"; Kohn "open at some point in the future to a lowering of interest rates"; Stern, Evans, Cumming would do 50 bp if acting. Hawkish side: Hoenig "resist the impulse to ease"; Plosser "rates ultimately will have to rise"; Lacker "raise rates sooner rather than later if core inflation doesn't moderate"; Bullard hold, inflation "brewing". Balanced hold with a slight net dovish tilt from financial strain.

DISSENT_INTENSITY 30 +-6 Moderate: unanimous vote of all 10 voters (Cumming through Warsh all "Yes"), no formal dissent. Rosengren (not a voter in the vote record) preferred a 25 bp cut in the go-around; Hoenig and Bullard argued against any easing signal; Plosser and Lacker flagged eventual hikes; Fisher "important for us to be unanimous". Extended exchange on the words "closely"/"market" (Lacker, Evans, Kroszner). Differences are about next move and wording, not the current action.

FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 90 +-4 High: dominant topic; Bernanke "Financial markets received a lot of attention around the table"; Stern "most severe financial crisis ... in my career"; Warsh on CDS spreads, AIG, systemic risk; Duke on bank capital; Rosengren on money market funds and tri-party repo; opening session on dollar swap lines; statement wording argument centred on financial strains.

FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 46 +-7 Moderate: statement "monitor economic and financial developments carefully and will act as needed"; Warsh "watchful waiting. We are not indifferent ... but we are not predisposed"; Kroszner "leave our options open ... a move that wouldn't be a shock to the markets but ... wouldn't be something the markets could bank on"; Bernanke "when the time comes, we do need to be prompt at removing accommodation"; Hoenig "not to encourage the expectation of further rate decreases"; Kohn open to lowering if conditions tighten sustainably. Neutral, data dependent, slight easing option.

UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 78 +-6 High: Evans "enormous uncertainty ... downside risks to output are almost too dispersed to characterize ... the mother of all credit crunches"; Lacker "uncertainty is especially high now"; Bullard "difficult and probably unwise to try to assess growth and inflation prospects"; Bernanke "decidedly confused and very muddled", "situation right now is very uncertain"; Kroszner oil "$100, plus or minus $50". Many members, genuine rather than rhetorical.
