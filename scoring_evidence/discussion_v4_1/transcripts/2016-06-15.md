# v4.1 Scoring: Member Discussion (transcript), meeting 2016-06-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member and participant turns only: briefing Q&A, a two-day economic go-around (Day 1, ending with the Chair's summary), the policy go-around (Day 2, discussion of statement alternatives A/B/C), and a vote read-out (10 Yes, no No votes). Staff turns are removed. There is some PDF line-break noise but nothing garbled enough to hide meaning. Procedural parts (retirement tribute, calendar, framework project, ratification of Desk operations) are ignored.

**Era context.** Mid-2016. Unemployment is 4.7 percent, core PCE inflation is about 1.6 percent, the funds rate has been raised once (December 2015), and a Brexit referendum is a week away. The May payroll report was very weak. I score what the text says, not this backdrop.

**Speakers and main views.**
- Hawkish side: Lacker, Mester, George, Harker, Rosengren, Williams, Lockhart (soft lean to a July move), Powell (patient but baseline gradual tightening), Fischer (July open).
- Dovish or patient side: Dudley, Brainard, Tarullo, Kashkari, Evans (prefers alt A forward guidance but supports B), Kaplan (shallower path), Bullard (the single further hike is only on good news).
- Chair Yellen: leave policy unchanged until the labor market picture is clearer; expects further increases over time if the labor market is on a solid path.

**Dimension directions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE. Core is 1.6 percent and "firming" (George, Powell, Lacker cite 2.1 percent annualized in Q1). Several see it returning to 2 percent. Evans, Kashkari, Brainard and Dudley stress downside risk and low expectations. Participants see inflation as below target but rising, so below 50, near the "edging toward target" band. About 42.
- OUTPUT_GAP. Williams: "Output and unemployment gaps have essentially closed." Brainard cites the staff view that the gap is closed. Others (Powell, Rosengren, George) say growth is slightly above potential. Dudley and Evans see a bit of slack. Net is at potential, so 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS. The unemployment rate is 4.7 percent, described as full employment or near it. Wages are rising, and JOLTS openings and quits are strong. The May payrolls were weak and there is debate over remaining slack (Tarullo, Dudley, Evans, Brainard). Net is just at or slightly above neutral, so 52.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT. Consumer spending is strong or robust (retail sales, autos, Michigan sentiment at a decade high). Business fixed investment has fallen for two quarters, with inventories a drag and an election-year pause. The net is roughly at trend or slightly below, so 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY. "Anemic" (Powell), "very low" (Fischer, Brainard), Williams at about 1 percent trend, Bullard's "low productivity growth regime". Well below historical trend, so about 27.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Eased since December: Powell "financial conditions have actually eased somewhat on net"; Rosengren "more accommodative financial environment"; Mester "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions"; Dudley "buoyant financial market conditions". Brexit volatility is a risk, not the current state. Bright line: accommodative means below 50. Score 36.
- TREASURY_STRESS. Only yield levels and policy expectations appear. No market-functioning discussion. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS. Only Brainard says "credit in many markets is widely available" and Mester raises the possibility of a future tightening. Energy-sector credit stress is regional (George). There is no direct discussion of spreads. Level reading: credit availability is good, so below 50, Low confidence. Score 42.
- EQUITY_VALUATION. Only price moves ("equity prices up", "stock market has recovered"), with no valuation commentary. Default 45-55, Low confidence. Score 48.
- BANKING_STRESS. Not discussed for the system. Only George notes rising past-due loans at banks with energy exposure in her District, and Harker notes accelerating bank lending. A small pocket of stress, with the system not described as stressed. Score 40, Low.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH. Mixed but mostly "dollar down" or "steadied" since the January peak (Powell, Brainard "receded a bit", Dudley "relatively stable"; Harker "fairly stable"). Brainard also calls dollar strength a continuing business headwind. Bright line: depreciation or stable means below 50 or 46-54. Score 46.
- INFLATION sub-components. Expectations are discussed at length: Michigan at a record low, low TIPS compensation, with Mester and Dudley taking comfort in other measures. Below target and softening, so 38. Goods, services and housing prices are not discussed as components (only passing oil and import-price effects), so N/A.

**Tier 3 check.**
- Lean: The action is a hold (alt B). About half the participants are inclined to move in July if the data cooperate; the other half wants a shallower or indefinite hold. Several are reluctant. Balanced to slightly dovish on the near-term action, with the median path still gradually upward. About 48.
- Dissent: There are no dissents (unanimous vote). But Mester prefers alt C, Evans prefers alt A and supports B only because reversing course is costly, Lacker, Harker and George want more hawkish language, and Tarullo and Mester say more dissent may come. Visible disagreement over economics (supply constraints versus demand weakness; Bullard versus Williams on frameworks) but the discussion is civil. About 38.
- Financial stability: Brexit market risk, China debt and exchange rate risk, and Mester's financial stability risks from low rates are mentioned. Limited discussion and not the focus. About 30.
- Forward guidance: The Chair and Williams say the gradual path is intact but data dependent. Many (Tarullo, Mester, Bullard, Evans, Chair) discuss de-emphasizing the rate path. A neutral to slightly dovish signal. About 47.
- Uncertainty: Frequent, specific uncertainty: "considerable uncertainty" (Brainard), "materially increased uncertainty" (Powell), "acute uncertainties" (Williams), "truly data dependent" (Fischer), Brexit, the May report and the election. About 60.

**Polarity and bright-line checks.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: "closed / at potential" gives 50, inside 48-52. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: accommodative gives 36, below 50. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: availability good gives 42, below 50. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: weakened or stable gives 46, below 50. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. BANKING_STRESS: 40, no "stressed" language. OK.

**Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 50 and LABOR 52 are within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 50 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47 are within 15.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 36 and CREDIT_SPREADS 42 co-move. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A as expected in normal times.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 42 and INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 38 are consistent.

**N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no market-functioning discussion. INFLATION_GOODS, SERVICES and HOUSING: not discussed as components.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "Core inflation is running at 1.6 percent over the past year"; Evans, "I cannot express a similar confidence about reaching our inflation target." Summary: below target but firming, with downside concern about expectations. Direction: below neutral because core is under 2 percent.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 ±5 (High).** Evidence: Williams, "Output and unemployment gaps have essentially closed already"; Brainard, "the staff estimating that the output gap is closed". Summary: at potential, with a few seeing slightly above or slightly below. Direction: at neutral.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Rosengren, "We are now at my estimate of full employment"; Lacker, firms "having difficulty hiring". Counter: Dudley, "premature to conclude from the wage compensation data that we've reached full employment." Summary: at or just past full employment at 4.7 percent, despite weak May payrolls. Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "Consumption increased robustly in April... Investment has been weak, however"; Brainard, "Business fixed investment has declined in the past two quarters". Summary: strong consumers offset by weak investment. Direction: slightly below trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 27 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Powell, "Productivity growth has been anemic, to be generous about it"; Williams, "not much above 1 percent". Summary: well below historical trend. Direction: below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 ±7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell, "financial conditions have actually eased somewhat on net since then"; Mester, "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions". Summary: supportive, with Brexit volatility as a risk. Direction: looser than neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as market functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: Brainard, "credit in many markets is widely available"; George on regional energy credit stress. Summary: little direct discussion of spreads; availability is good. Direction: below neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8 (Low).** Evidence: Dudley, "The stock market has recovered"; Powell, "equity prices up". Summary: price moves only, no valuation view. Direction: neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±10 (Low).** Evidence: George, "past-due loans at our banks with energy exposures have been increasing"; Harker, "the growth rate of bank lending has accelerated". Summary: a pocket of energy-related stress, no system concern. Direction: below neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 46 ±6 (Moderate).** Evidence: Powell, "the dollar down"; Brainard, "the dollar has receded a bit, on net, from its peak in January". Summary: edged down or stable since the peak. Direction: slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kashkari, "The Michigan survey... has just fallen to a record low"; Evans, "household measures of inflation expectations have fallen to new lows". Offset: Mester, "reasonably well anchored". Summary: soft and below target in several measures. Goods, services, housing: N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 48 ±6 (High).** Evidence: Dudley, "I don't see a good case for tightening monetary policy at this meeting"; Mester, "I would be advocating increasing the rate at this meeting" absent Brexit. Summary: a hold, with roughly half leaning to a July hike and half to an extended hold. Direction: balanced, slightly dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Evans, "I'm far from confident that this signal is appropriate now"; Tarullo and Mester on "some more dissent". Summary: unanimous vote but several reluctant supporters and real disagreement over language and the economics of the labor market. Direction: moderate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Brainard, "global markets are likely to remain fragile"; Mester, "Risks to financial stability associated with very low interest rates appear so far to be contained." Summary: Brexit, China and a few passing references; not a focus. Direction: limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 47 ±8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Chair Yellen, "our expectation still is, over time, that some further increases will be appropriate if we do see the labor market on a solid path"; Williams on alt B being "more dovish". Summary: gradual path retained but data dependent, with much talk of de-emphasizing the path. Direction: roughly neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±7 (High).** Evidence: Brainard, "considerable uncertainty about what to infer"; Powell, "has materially increased uncertainty about the underlying state and momentum of the economy". Summary: many emphasize unusual uncertainty from the May report and Brexit. Direction: elevated.
