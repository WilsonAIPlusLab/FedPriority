# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion, meeting of 1999-05-18 (transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript (SOURCE_TYPE: discussion). It holds the Chairman's questions to staff, members' questions in the staff Q&A (staff answers removed), the economic go-around, the policy go-around, the draft press statement and a vote read-out. The opening segment is a Q&A on Y2K discount-window readiness for banks. Staff replies are absent, so some exchanges are one-sided fragments. One passage (Vice Chairman McDonough on China) has a missing phrase after "what I called"; it does not affect any score.

**2. Era context.** Late-1990s expansion. The text shows low unemployment (about 4.2 percent, heading to 4 or lower), strong productivity, a jump in April CPI, and a rebound in financial markets after the 1998 turmoil and the three easings. I score only what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** Hawkish voices:
- Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan and Hoenig worry about inflation and policy ease. Broaddus, Minehan and Poole prefer a 25bp rise now; Jordan says policy is "more expansionary than desirable."
- Moskow, Guynn, Meyer, Gramlich and Ferguson see upside risk and favor a tilt.
- Stern is also concerned about inflation and supports the tilt.

Cautious voices: Rivlin would wait. Boehne sees benign inflation but a "heightened state of alert." McTeer hopes the tilt will not need to be followed through. Kelley wants flexibility. Greenspan and McDonough want an announced tilt toward tightening and no rate move.

**4. Core dimensions (direction in words first).**

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. Measured inflation is still low (Greenspan: core PCE about 1.1 percent over three months; Kelley: "remarkably quiescent"; Boehne: "inflation remains quite benign"). But the April CPI jolt, price-paid surveys, commodity and construction-materials price rises and building pricing-power anecdotes point up (Broaddus, Moskow, Hoenig, Minehan). Net: slightly above midpoint, about 54, since much of the concern is about risk and trend rather than present levels.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as above potential. Evidence: private domestic final purchases up 7.5 percent, demand exceeding productivity gains (Greenspan), "excessive utilization" (Meyer), "very high level of utilization" (Guynn), "growth above trend" (Meyer). Greenspan notes no capacity-constraint symptoms (zero items in short supply, utilization about 80 percent). Bright line satisfied (> 50). Modestly to clearly above: 59.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Very tight. Unemployment is below recent natural-rate estimates and heading to 4 percent or lower (Broaddus, Kelley, Guynn); Hoenig cites 3.4 percent locally; Poole reports "tightest the company has ever seen" in Louisville; Greenspan cites the pool of non-employed at a series low. Wage growth is subdued (the "puzzle"), which holds the score below the 75+ range. Score 72.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Well above trend. "Consumer spending is very strong" (Broaddus), "strength in domestic demand continues unabated" (Boehne), 7.5 percent final purchases, investment "very strong" (Poole). Score 71. Housing and construction are hot; some moderation in a few regions is minor.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed at length. Participants see an exceptional acceleration ("magnificent," Kelley; "exceptional growth," Meyer; "on a roll," Rivlin). Greenspan sees no leveling off yet. Meyer and others doubt the staff's 2.25 percent trend as aggressive, but the level of gains is strong. Score 74.

**5. Financial dimensions.**

- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Poole: "no question that financial conditions are accommodative"; Meyer: "continued favorable financial conditions," "set of financial conditions that have so dramatically improved"; Minehan: credit "reminiscent of the ease we had last summer"; Broaddus: "plenty of money around." Easy, so < 50. Score 29.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Boehne says fixed-income markets "look increasingly normal following their seizing up last summer and fall." Long yields rose on CPI, which is not dysfunction. Minimal direct discussion. Score 38, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Minehan: "credit conditions are now coming back to the narrower spreads"; Broaddus: credit readily available, "competition for loans very, very strong--probably excessive"; Ferguson: "a great deal of availability of credit." Narrow and ample, so < 50. Score 33.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Concern about valuation is on the elevated side, but only moderate. Minehan: "stock markets, real estate markets, corporate and personal debt... are getting there" (extended), bubble discussion at the Academic Advisory Council; Meyer: equity prices up about 40 percent from the trough. Others call it wealth creation (Kelley). Score 36 (modestly overvalued side), Moderate.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed but mostly healthy. Minehan: banks "better capitalized"; McDonough: delinquencies down, tightening standards; Broaddus: no significant decline in credit quality; Guynn: some examiner evidence of deteriorating credit quality in hotels and health care. McDonough also notes large banks might become "less than well capitalized" from Y2K inflows, and Greenspan probes small-bank liquidity under Y2K runs (hypothetical). Net: healthy with minor concerns. Score 37.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only passing reference to the "strong dollar" as a past disinflationary force (Rivlin) and a stronger peso (McTeer). No discussion of current dollar direction. N/A.

**6. Inflation sub-components.**
- GOODS: Commodity prices turning up, building materials, oil, raw materials (Hoenig, Minehan, Moskow); offset by commodity-like price falls in electronics (McTeer) and zero pricing power (Boehne). Score 57.
- SERVICES: Cost of service inputs rising (McDonough); CPI drivers were owners' equivalent rent, lodging, airfares, "other services" (Ferguson). Score 56.
- HOUSING: Minehan: "rents rising precipitously, house prices up"; McDonough: home prices up sharply but Manhattan rent inflation "slowed markedly"; McTeer: used homes selling above list. Score 60.
- EXPECTATIONS: Jordan: people will "expect higher rather than lower inflation"; Poole: expectations have "trumped the gap" but could turn. Greenspan: "very little evidence to date of a pickup in inflation expectations." Anchored, with concern about drift. Score 54.

**7. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Consensus among voters to hold the funds rate and adopt an announced asymmetric directive toward tightening. A sizable group (Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan, Gramlich four-fifths, Hoenig as an unwinding rationale) would raise rates now; Parry "would not take much more evidence." Greenspan: "not ready to move rates," yet the tilt arguments are "strongly on your side." Hold with a tightening bias, plus a vocal group wanting a hike. Score about 60.
- Dissent: Unanimous vote (11 yes, no dissents). Reservations: Broaddus and Minehan expressed a preference for a 25bp hike (explicitly "alternative C" for Broaddus); Jordan objects to announcing the tilt ("cocked the gun"); Rivlin would wait; Kelley and McTeer want flexibility. Disagreements are mostly on tactics and timing, not on the economic assessment. Score 32.
- Financial stability emphasis: The meeting opens with a lengthy bank-liquidity and discount-window exchange (Y2K), then Minehan on asset-market excesses and margin requirements, Broaddus on excessive loan competition, Guynn on credit quality, Poole and Meyer on financial conditions, and the policy case of "unwinding" the crisis easings. Moderate attention. Score 42.
- Forward guidance: A tilt toward firming is to be announced publicly, with a press statement referencing "developments over coming months." Greenspan: could move at the next meeting or the one after; Poole expects "a tightening trend"; Kelley and McTeer caution against a lock-in. Mild-to-clear tightening signal. Score 69.
- Uncertainty: Parry stresses "considerable uncertainty" from productivity and the supply shock; Rivlin "mystified"; Greenspan: "I don't see how we will know which is which until after the fact" and "I do not know for certain." But most express conviction that risks tilt up. Elevated but not extreme. Score 48.

**8. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 59 > 50: above trend/potential language. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 29 < 50: accommodative/favorable language. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS 33 < 50: "narrower spreads," ample credit (level reading). OK.
- BANKING_STRESS 37 < 50: banks better capitalized, healthy with minor concerns. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A (no direction discussed).
- TREASURY_STRESS 38, below 60: no dysfunction; rising yields are not stress. OK.

**9. Coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 59 versus LABOR 72: 13-point divergence, within 15. Labor is anchored on the unemployment-rate level, while members note demand is not yet generating capacity-restraint symptoms (Greenspan) and productivity expands potential (Meyer: higher trend), so the output gap is smaller than labor tightness.
- OUTPUT_GAP 59 versus CONSUMPTION 71: 12 points, within 15. Spending is described as "above trend" with productivity absorbing part of it.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 29 and CREDIT_SPREADS 33: both loose, consistent.
- TREASURY_STRESS 38: normal, consistent with easy financial conditions in a non-crisis setting.
- INFLATION 54 and sub-components (57, 56, 60, 54): average about 57; parent slightly lower because headline and PCE measures remain low, consistent.

**10. N/A.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no discussion of current dollar movement beyond a passing past-tense mention.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 54 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Broaddus: the April CPI "offers the first direct evidence in some time that inflation pressures may be building"; Kelley: "Inflation remains remarkably quiescent." Center of gravity: low measured inflation with building upside pressure. Direction: slightly above neutral because the CPI, commodity and price-survey signals add to a low base.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 59 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Meyer: "it is more likely that growth will be above trend than below trend"; Greenspan: "growth in aggregate demand still exceeds the rate of increase in productivity." Center of gravity: demand running above sustainable growth. Direction: above neutral because of "above trend" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 +-5 [High].** Evidence: Kelley: unemployment "appears to be headed to 4 percent or lower"; Guynn: "unemployment is below even recent estimates of the natural rate." Center of gravity: very tight labor market, wages lagging. Direction: above neutral on the unemployment level.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 71 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Broaddus: "Consumer spending is very strong... exceeded even those expectations"; Boehne: "strength in domestic demand continues unabated." Center of gravity: private domestic final purchases at 7.5 percent. Direction: well above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 74 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Kelley: "magnificent productivity gains of recent quarters"; Meyer: "recent exceptional growth in productivity." Center of gravity: exceptional acceleration, with disagreement over how durable. Direction: above trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 29 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Poole: "financial conditions are accommodative as measured by money growth, the ease of financing in markets, and the behavior of the equity markets"; Meyer: "continued favorable financial conditions." Center of gravity: easy conditions after the fall 1998 recovery. Direction: below neutral (loose).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +-8 [Low].** Evidence: Boehne: fixed-income markets "look increasingly normal following their seizing up last summer and fall." Center of gravity: normal functioning; a yield rise on CPI is not dysfunction. Direction: normal, below the stress threshold.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan: "credit conditions are now coming back to the narrower spreads"; Ferguson: "a great deal of availability of credit." Center of gravity: narrow spreads and ample credit. Direction: below neutral by the level reading.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 36 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan: "Stock markets, real estate markets, and corporate and personal debt may not all be similarly extended, but they are getting there"; Meyer: equity prices "rebounded by about 40 percent from their trough." Center of gravity: modest overvaluation concern. Direction: on the elevated-valuation side (below 50 on this scale).

**BANKING_STRESS: 37 +-7 [Moderate].** Evidence: Minehan: banks "are supplying less of the credit and are better capitalized"; Guynn: examiners "see some evidence of a deterioration in credit quality." Center of gravity: healthy system, minor credit-quality and Y2K concerns. Direction: below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Only a passing past reference to the strong dollar (Rivlin).

**INFLATION_GOODS: 57. INFLATION_SERVICES: 56. INFLATION_HOUSING: 60. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 54.** Evidence: Hoenig on construction materials ("sheet rock... up about 50 percent"); McDonough on "persistent increases in the cost of service inputs"; Minehan "Rents are rising precipitously"; Jordan "people are going to expect higher rather than lower inflation" versus Greenspan "very little evidence to date of a pickup in inflation expectations."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +-5 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan: "it is by no means premature to move toward a tilt and to announce it"; Broaddus: "my preference would be to go ahead and move the funds rate up 1/4 percentage point today." Center of gravity: hold with a tightening bias, with a sizable minority wanting a hike now. Direction: hawkish side of balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Gramlich: "Four-fifths is not 100 percent so I won't dissent"; Jordan: "I am bothered by the idea of saying we have cocked the gun." Center of gravity: unanimous vote, several reservations on tactics. Direction: low to moderate.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Greenspan on small-bank liquidity and the discount window; Minehan: academic council recommended action "that might take the wind out of asset markets." Center of gravity: moderate attention via Y2K bank liquidity and asset-market excess.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 69 +-6 [High].** Evidence: Greenspan's draft statement: "the Committee recognizes the need to be alert to developments over coming months"; Poole: "I anticipate a tightening trend." Center of gravity: announced tightening tilt, no commitment to move.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 +-8 [Moderate].** Evidence: Parry: "there is considerable uncertainty associated with this outlook"; Greenspan: "I don't see how we will know which is which until after the fact." Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty about productivity and the supply shock, but firm views on the direction of risks.
