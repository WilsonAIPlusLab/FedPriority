# v4.1 Discussion Scoring: FOMC meeting 2013-01-30 (transcript discussion)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript with staff turns removed. It has (a) the organizational items (officer elections, authorizations, a straw vote on the principles statement), (b) members' questions during the staff briefings, (c) the economic go-round of Jan 29, (d) the Jan 30 policy go-round on alternatives A/B/C and the statement wording, (e) a long closing discussion of Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) enhancements (medians, voter dots, fan charts, balance-sheet questions), and (f) a vote read-out. The organizational and SEP-communications parts are procedural and were not scored, except that the SEP talk shows disagreement and attitudes to uncertainty. The vote read-out shows 11 Yes and 1 No (President George).

**2. Era context.** Early 2013, with the open-ended asset purchase program (QE3) and state-contingent forward guidance (6.5 percent unemployment and 2.5 percent inflation thresholds) in place since the previous meeting. The stated backdrop is high unemployment (about 7.7-7.8 percent), inflation below 2 percent, fiscal-cliff and Europe tail risks fading, and housing and autos improving. I scored what participants say.

**3. Speakers.** All 19 participants speak. Doves and accommodation advocates: Rosengren, Williams, Evans, Kocherlakota (wants the unemployment threshold lowered to 5.5 percent), Yellen, Raskin, Tarullo, Dudley. Participants with cost and risk concerns: George (dissents), Fisher (non-voter, opposes policy), Lacker, Plosser, Pianalto, Lockhart (wants efficacy and costs emphasized), Powell and Stein (want to taper purchases soon), Duke (limits, political risk).

**4. Direction statements.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint (target). Bernanke's summary: "inflation has been relatively soft and should remain near or below target." Kocherlakota expects 1.6 percent in 2013; Williams expects about 1.5 percent; Lockhart hears of a "very soft pricing environment"; Evans says "identifiable and concrete inflation risks continue to be remarkably low." Offsetting this, Lacker, George and Fisher see upside medium-term risk, and Pianalto sees median CPI at 2.2 percent. Net: modestly below target, so mid-30s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential. Slack is still large (Plosser cites an output gap of about 3.5 percent; Tarullo says "an aggregate demand shortfall"; Rosengren says "insufficient aggregate demand"). Potential has been revised down, which narrows the gap, but all agree the economy is still below it. Bright line: below 50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Unemployment about 7.8 percent, 4 million fewer jobs than in 2008 (Tarullo), improving slowly. Level is low tightness, so below 30.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Mixed. Housing, autos and durables are strong (Rosengren: motor vehicles +9.6 percent, residential investment +13.6 percent), but nondurables and services consumption is weak (Evans), the payroll tax hits spending (Dudley, Raskin), and capital spending is cautious (Kocherlakota, Lockhart). Net: a little below trend.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed briefly. Potential and productivity growth are seen as lower after the crisis (Yellen: Laubach-Williams trend growth down 1 point; Bernanke: potential growth lower than thought; Plosser, Lacker, Bullard on lower potential). Modestly below trend.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Looser and improving. Dudley: "financial conditions are considerably more buoyant. Risk appetite has increased considerably." Duke: "headwinds from tight credit conditions are rapidly dissipating" (except mortgages). Powell: "financial conditions are a net positive." Evans: "better financial conditions." Accommodative language, so below 50 (bright line).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Discussed (Stein on the risk of a disorderly rate spike, Pianalto and Powell on fixed-income valuation), but only as a prospective risk. Current functioning is described as fine (Evans: "Market functioning is not an issue"; Yellen: costs "do not seem to be impeding the functioning of financial markets"). The 10-year yield near 2 percent moved on optimism. This is normal functioning, so 36-45.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Mixed on level. Fisher: "Triple-C credits are trading at less than 8 percent... junk in the 5 percent range... overbought." Dudley: high-yield yields low but "the spreads don't seem to be particularly narrow." Powell: "spreads are not that low yet, but they're definitely tightening; terms are deteriorating." Pianalto: a wide primary-secondary mortgage spread. The level is near to somewhat below normal and tightening, so below 50 but not far.
- EQUITY_VALUATION (higher means more stress or depressed pricing): Opposing views. Fisher sees overbought markets and a correction risk (low-score view). Dudley and Kocherlakota say equities are cheap against bonds ("extremely wide equity risk premium"; "equities remain unusually cheap relative to risk-free bonds") (higher-score view). Bernanke sees the rise as justified by earnings. These roughly offset, so near fair value.
- BANKING_STRESS: Duke: banks' "plans are all about lending," credit quality improving, credit costs declining. Pianalto and George raise rate-risk exposure and duration extension as risks, but nobody describes stress today. Healthy, so below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only touched on. Raskin: the dollar declined in September-October partly due to policy. Yellen: it appreciated 0.3-0.4 percent on the minutes reaction. Bernanke's summary: "A weaker dollar may help the United States." Net: modest weakness or stable, slightly below 50, low confidence.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Anchored near 2 percent (Pianalto, Bernanke), with some drift up in the 5y5y breakeven (George: about 30 bp above its pre-September level; Fisher: 5-year TIPS breakeven at 2.46). Anchored at target to slightly higher.
- Inflation goods, services and housing: no participant discusses price pressure in those categories (house prices are discussed, but not shelter or rent inflation), so N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher means tighter; the evidence says looser, so a score below 50 is correct. CREDIT_SPREADS: higher means more stress; the evidence says spreads are tightening and below normal, so below 50 is correct. BANKING_STRESS: the evidence says healthy and lending, so low. EQUITY_VALUATION: higher means more depressed or stressed; the "cheap" views push up and the "overbought" views push down, netting near the middle.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: The Committee holds alternative B, with open-ended QE3 purchases at $85 billion a month and zero rates. A majority voices support for continued accommodation. Some want more (Kocherlakota, Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen, who expect purchases to run toward the alt-A path), and a growing group wants to plan a taper or end in 2013 (Powell, Stein, Pianalto, Lockhart, Plosser, Lacker, George, Fisher). Bernanke: "Most people agree that the case for continued accommodation is strong." This is the QE era with active "how much more" debate, with a growing cost-and-exit tilt, so about 32.
- Dissent: One formal dissent (George, "too accommodative"). Fisher (a non-voter) states he opposes the policy. Kocherlakota argues for more easing via a lower threshold. There are many reservations about efficacy and costs, and sharp but collegial exchanges on exit and tapering, but no hostile exchanges. Camps differ on policy direction (more vs. less), though most agree on the economic assessment. Net: moderate to notable, about 40.
- Financial stability attention: Large. Stein devotes his turn to disorderly bond-market correction risk and leverage; Powell, George, Pianalto, Yellen, Duke, Fisher (froth, junk bonds, "manic optimism"), Raskin and Dudley discuss it, and the Chair names fiscal costs and financial stability as the two focus areas for March. It does not dominate the economic assessment, so about 60.
- Forward guidance: Threshold-based guidance retained, with calendar date dropped. Bernanke and most favor staying accommodative "for a considerable period," with the thresholds having been "well received". There is no tightening signal in the statement, although the discussion openly contemplates tapering or ending purchases later in 2013 (Powell, Stein, Bullard's tapering rule, Bernanke's "tapered as the economy improves"). Strongly easing commitment with a modest taper-talk offset, so about 14.
- Uncertainty: Evans: "Forecast uncertainty remains high... a single-point forecast would probably overstate my confidence." Dudley has a "very broad range of potential outcomes" for Q1 consumption. Lockhart and Kocherlakota cite uncertainty as a drag. Yellen: "the jury still out." Plosser and Lacker stress imprecision of potential and the gap, and Bullard says "uncertainties abound." Others (Fisher, Williams, Tarullo) speak with more conviction. Elevated but not extreme, 55.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP below potential and score below 50 (35). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative/buoyant and score below 50 (33). CREDIT_SPREADS stated level: junk/CCC yields low, "tightening," "not particularly narrow," so near to below normal, score 40, below 50. BANKING_STRESS healthy and score below 50 (30). DOLLAR_STRENGTH weakening or stable so below 50 (43). TREASURY_STRESS no dysfunction described, score 38, below 60.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP 35 vs LABOR 28: 7 apart, consistent (both below 50).
- OUTPUT_GAP 35 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 44: 9 apart, consistent (housing and autos strong, consumption soft).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 40: both below 50, co-move; spreads somewhat closer to normal than overall conditions because of the mortgage-spread and CRE caveats.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 33 vs TREASURY_STRESS 38: normal times, fine.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 36 vs INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 52: parent reflects current soft price data, expectations anchored near target with some upward drift in breakevens. Divergence is explained by current inflation being measured and expectations being anchored near 2 percent. No other subcomponents scored.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES and INFLATION_HOUSING: participants do not discuss price pressure in these categories (house prices and home equity are discussed as activity and wealth drivers, not shelter inflation). Everything else had at least some participant commentary.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Kocherlakota, "inflation pressures seem likely to remain muted. I expect inflation to be around 1.6 percent in 2013"; Evans, "Identifiable and concrete inflation risks continue to be remarkably low."
- Summary: Most participants see inflation soft and below 2 percent, with only a minority flagging medium-term upside risk.
- Direction: Below neutral because forecasts and contacts show inflation under the 2 percent target.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Tarullo, "the better explanation for most of the elevated unemployment rate continues to be an aggregate demand shortfall... still considerable room for noninflationary stimulus"; Rosengren, "Our economy continues to suffer from insufficient aggregate demand."
- Summary: The gap is smaller than previously thought because potential was marked down, but the economy is still clearly below potential.
- Direction: Below neutral because of persistent slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Tarullo, "this economy still has 4 million fewer jobs than it did in January 2008... steady but... painfully slow"; Raskin, the unemployment rate "moved down to 7.8 percent" with widespread underemployment.
- Summary: Improving but still deeply slack labor market, unemployment near 7.7-7.8 percent.
- Direction: Below neutral; level of tightness is low despite improvement.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "motor vehicle spending has grown by 9.6 percent, and residential investment has grown by 13.6 percent"; Evans, nondurables and services consumption growth "well under 2 percent"; Kocherlakota, businesses "had a strong sense of caution about doing more cap-ex."
- Summary: Strong in housing and autos but weak in other consumption and capital spending, with the payroll tax adding a drag.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 ±10 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "a key question is whether the pace of structural productivity growth has fallen persistently in the wake of the recession... Laubach and Williams's estimates of trend GDP growth, which have declined by 1 full percentage point"; Bernanke, "potential output growth has been much less than we thought."
- Summary: Participants see weaker-than-trend structural productivity and potential growth, though it is a side topic and contested.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "financial conditions are considerably more buoyant. Risk appetite has increased considerably, which is what we were seeking"; Duke, "the headwinds from tight credit conditions are rapidly dissipating."
- Summary: Conditions are easier and improving, with mortgage credit still restrained.
- Direction: Below neutral (accommodative), consistent with the bright-line rule.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Stein on the "risk of an abrupt and disorderly correction in the Treasury market" (prospective, "I'm extremely hazy"); Yellen, costs "do not seem to be impeding the functioning of financial markets."
- Summary: Participants worry about a future rate spike but describe current Treasury functioning as normal.
- Direction: Below the stress threshold; normal functioning with some forward concern.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Powell, "Rates are low; spreads are not that low yet, but they're definitely tightening; and terms are deteriorating rapidly"; Dudley, "the spreads don't seem to be particularly narrow"; Fisher, "Triple-C credits are trading at less than 8 percent."
- Summary: Spreads are described as tightening toward the low side, from near-normal to somewhat tight, with some froth in leveraged finance.
- Direction: Below neutral on level (tightening, favorable credit), with Dudley's "not particularly narrow" keeping it near normal.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Dudley, "the equity risk premium... seems several hundred basis points higher than normal... P/E ratio for the S&P 500 is only about 14"; Fisher, "we are seeing signs that the markets are overbought."
- Summary: Valuation views conflict (cheap against bonds vs. overbought), netting to roughly fair value.
- Direction: Near neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Duke, "As the banks look forward to 2013, their plans are all about lending. Most expect credit costs to decline a bit more as credit quality improves"; George, bank examiners see banks "beginning to extend the duration of their portfolios" (a forward risk).
- Summary: Banks are described as healthy and competing to lend, with rate-risk concerns about the future.
- Direction: Below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 43 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Raskin, "the decline in the dollar over September and October of last year was driven, at least in part, by the new monetary policy accommodation"; Yellen, the dollar "appreciated three-tenths of 1 percent versus the yen" on the minutes release.
- Summary: Brief mentions describe an earlier decline and small recent moves.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral (modest weakness to stable).

**INFLATION_GOODS: N/A. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed as inflation categories.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52**
- Evidence: Pianalto, "inflation expectations have remained well anchored despite the growth in our balance sheet"; George, the 5y5y breakeven "remains about 30 basis points above its level prior to the September FOMC meeting, and at the upper end of its historical range."
- Summary: Anchored at about 2 percent, with some participants noting a drift higher in market measures.
- Direction: At neutral, slightly above.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Bernanke, "Most people agree that the case for continued accommodation is strong"; Powell, "I believe we should adjust our purchases downward, with a view to ending them before year-end"; Kocherlakota, the Committee "should begin to move... toward lowering the unemployment threshold to 5½ percent."
- Summary: Open-ended QE3 is retained by a large majority, with some wanting more and a growing group wanting to plan a taper and exit.
- Direction: Dovish (below neutral) with a tilt toward cost concerns that lifts it out of the 15-25 range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Vote read-out, "President George No"; George, "a policy that I believe is too accommodative and poses more risks than benefit"; Fisher, "I don't support our current policy."
- Summary: One formal dissent, an opposing non-voter, and camps on both sides (more easing vs. earlier exit), but with agreement on the economic assessment and a collegial tone.
- Direction: Moderate disagreement, above the typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Stein devotes his entire go-round turn to Treasury-market correction, leverage and complacency; Powell, "growing market distortions... every reason to expect a sharp and painful correction"; Bernanke, "the financial stability issues" are one of two focus areas for March.
- Summary: A prominent topic raised by many participants, with a full staff analysis scheduled for March, but secondary to the economic and policy assessment.
- Direction: Substantial attention (direction-neutral).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 14 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "I anticipate that we will be able to leave the forward guidance as is for quite some time"; Bernanke, an abrupt end to purchases "should be avoided... it should be tapered as the economy improves."
- Summary: Threshold guidance with strong accommodation commitment is kept, with open discussion of tapering purchases later in 2013 raising it above the 3-8 floor.
- Direction: Strongly easing signal, slightly eased by taper talk.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Evans, "Forecast uncertainty remains high... a single-point forecast would probably overstate my confidence"; Dudley, "I have a very broad range of potential outcomes."
- Summary: Several members stress elevated uncertainty (fiscal, Europe, consumer response, potential output), while others speak with more conviction.
- Direction: Elevated.
