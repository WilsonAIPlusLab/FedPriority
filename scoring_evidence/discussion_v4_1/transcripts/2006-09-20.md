# v4.1 Scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting 2006-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript. Staff turns are removed, so the opening staff Q&A shows only members' questions (answers missing). It contains the economic go-around, the Chairman's summary, the policy go-around (hold vs. a tightening-bias variant "B+" of the statement, plus wording edits such as deleting "on balance"), and a roll-call read-out with one "No".

**2. Era context.** A meeting at which the Committee has just paused (after a long run of rate increases, funds rate 5 1/4 percent). The text shows a housing correction under way, energy prices falling back, and core inflation described as above the Committee's comfort zone. I score what participants say, not what I recall of the period.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Hawkish-leaning on inflation: Moskow (would have raised in August; wants B+; policy "will need to be tightened further"), Fisher (B+; board voted 7 to 1 for a discount-rate increase), Plosser (favored a hike last time; B+), Lacker (wants 5 1/2 percent; votes No), Hoenig (hold, but B+ and ready to tighten if inflation fails to moderate), Poole (inflation focus but wants a symmetric statement), Kroszner/Warsh (upside inflation risk, but B).
- Comfortable with the pause and the Greenbook path: Kohn, Yellen, Pianalto, Stern, Guynn, Minehan, Bies, Mishkin, Geithner (while stressing inflation risk and the need for flexibility to do more), and the Chairman (hold; "bias is toward resisting inflation").
- Growth: most are more optimistic than the Greenbook outside housing. Housing is a clear correction (Yellen: "ghost towns of the West"; Guynn: cancellation rates above 50 percent). Spillovers are doubted by most; the Chairman is less sure and shows a figure on recession nonlinearity.

**4. Directional statements for core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: participants see inflation pressure as above the midpoint. Core is "uncomfortably high" (Geithner), "well above my comfort zone" (Yellen), above 2 percent for two and a half years (Plosser), with some intermeeting relief (momentum "broken", Bernanke). Clearly above target but easing in recent readings: low-to-mid 60s.
- OUTPUT_GAP: mixed. Kohn says growth is "below potential"; Plosser "somewhat below potential"; Moskow "no meaningful resource gaps"; Yellen "economy still likely operating beyond potential"; the Chairman calls it a "full employment economy". The level of the gap is roughly at potential, with the near-term growth pace below trend. Slightly below 50, in the "at/near potential" band (48-52).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: above the midpoint. "Full employment economy" (Bernanke), widespread shortages of skilled workers (Fisher, Minehan, Lacker), wages and compensation rising, unemployment "below 5 percent" (Plosser). About 60.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: net near trend. Consumption "strong and resilient" (Warsh), business investment and profits "firm", offset by the sharp housing contraction and softer capex plans (Business Roundtable, Duke CFO survey). Net a little below the middle.
- PRODUCTIVITY: touched only lightly (Geithner: fundamentals "supporting relatively strong productivity growth seem to be intact"; Kroszner: compensation might be revised away with "higher productivity growth"). Slightly above trend, low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: loose / supportive. Kohn: "Financial conditions remain quite supportive"; Minehan: "financial conditions remain pretty accommodative"; Warsh: capital-market liquidity "incredibly robust", "euphoria". Must be below 50; set in the 20s.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A. Only yields, the yield-curve shape (Poole) and a coming issuance "liquidity test" (Warsh, about credit issuance, not Treasury dysfunction).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: level is narrow (Kohn: "Risk spreads are narrow"; Geithner: credit spreads suggest confidence; Warsh: investors have "very little leverage" on pricing and covenants). Below 50, in the 20s.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: equity gains are cited as support for wealth and confidence (Stern, Kohn, Warsh), with no explicit valuation concern except Warsh's "one-way bet" remark about markets. Default band, 46.
- BANKING_STRESS: banks "have not pulled back on business credit" (Kohn); mortgage delinquencies "still look good" but ARM resets and subprime are a concern (Bies); small-bank commercial real estate NPLs have picked up (Plosser). Healthy with minor concerns, below 50.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A. One passing assumption in Geithner's forecast ("the dollar declines only modestly"), no discussion of dollar movement.
- Inflation sub-components: HOUSING scored (owners' equivalent rent and rent of shelter discussed by Kohn and Bernanke: decelerating but still elevated). EXPECTATIONS scored (anchored but at a level some find high). GOODS and SERVICES are only mentioned in passing (energy, commodities, airfares, used cars, "not much movement in services") with mixed direction, so N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: higher = more above potential. Evidence is mixed and centered on "near potential", so a score just under 50 matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: higher = tighter. Evidence says supportive/accommodative, so low. Matches.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: higher = more stress. Evidence says narrow, so low. Matches.
- BANKING_STRESS: higher = more stress. Evidence says sound with minor pockets, so below 50. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: higher = tighter. Evidence says shortages and "full employment", so above 50. Matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: a hold at 5 1/4 percent. Several want a tightening bias (B+: Moskow, Fisher, Hoenig, Plosser; Lacker wants a hike). Many are content with B (Stern, Kohn, Pianalto, Guynn, Bies, Kroszner, Warsh, Mishkin, Geithner, Yellen). Poole wants a symmetric statement and no tightening tilt. The Chairman: the statement's "bias is toward resisting inflation". Hold with a tightening bias: lean hawkish, mid-50s.
- Disagreement: one formal dissent (Lacker, hawkish, preferring 5 1/2 percent). Several reservations on statement wording (B vs B+, "on balance", housing reference), different views on how persistent inflation is and how worrying growth risks are. Broad agreement on holding. Moderate-low range.
- Financial-stability attention: housing correction and speculative inventory, mortgage and subprime ARM risk (Bies), Warsh's long passage on capital-market liquidity, and discussion of whether markets are overconfident about the policy path. Several participants address it, but economics and inflation dominate. Moderate-low.
- Forward guidance: the statement keeps "any additional firming that may be needed"; participants and the Chairman describe the bias as toward resisting inflation, though the sentence "policy is more likely to firm than ease" was left out of the statement and Yellen/Kroszner/Geithner want flexibility. Mild tightening bias.
- Uncertainty: "uncertainties are quite high... 'higher than usual'" (Kohn), "no reduction in uncertainty" (Bernanke), nonlinearity in housing and growth (Bernanke), inflation dynamics poorly understood (Guynn, Plosser, Geithner). Elevated, but not extreme.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 48 (within 48-52 for "near potential"; no "above/below" side is dominant). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS < 50 (accommodative/supportive). CREDIT_SPREADS < 50 (narrow). BANKING_STRESS < 50 (sound/healthy). DOLLAR_STRENGTH and TREASURY_STRESS N/A. All satisfied.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
1. OUTPUT_GAP 48 vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS 60: 12 points apart, within the 15 allowed. Participants describe labor as tight and "full employment" while the gap is near potential with growth below trend, a normal mild divergence.
2. OUTPUT_GAP 48 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47: aligned.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 27 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 27: aligned (loose and narrow).
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS vs TREASURY_STRESS: Treasury N/A in normal times; fine.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE 63 vs sub-components: HOUSING 62 and EXPECTATIONS 56 (goods and services N/A). Consistent: expectations anchored pulls the parent a little below housing.

**9. N/A justification.** TREASURY_STRESS: no member describes Treasury market functioning. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only an assumption in a forecast, no discussion of dollar movement. INFLATION_GOODS and INFLATION_SERVICES: only passing mentions with mixed signs and no clear separate assessment.

## Part 2: Evidence and scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 63 ±5 (High).**
Evidence: Geithner: "core PCE and a range of alternative measures continue to grow at levels that are uncomfortably high." Bernanke: "definite unhappiness with the level of core inflation and with the amount of time that is projected to return it to a level of less than 2 percent." Offsetting: Bernanke, "the momentum has been broken."
Center of gravity: core inflation clearly above the comfort zone and persistent in the forecast, with some recent easing.
Direction: above neutral because most participants call core inflation too high for too long.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±5 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Kohn: "weakness in housing... is more definitively leading to growth of output below potential." Yellen: "the economy still likely operating beyond potential." Moskow: "we do not think that any meaningful resource gaps will emerge."
Center of gravity: near potential, growth below trend, views split on which side of potential.
Direction: at (slightly below) neutral because below-trend growth is offset by "full employment" descriptions.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±5 (High).**
Evidence: Fisher: "the economy feels like a full employment economy"; "a shortage of truck drivers, of oil field hands." Bernanke: "people characterized it as a full employment economy... attracting more highly skilled workers remains difficult."
Center of gravity: tight labor market with skill shortages and rising wages, unemployment below 5 percent.
Direction: above neutral because of widespread shortage reports.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Warsh: "consumer spending appears to be strong and resilient." Bies (Duke CFO survey): CFOs trimming capital spending plans "to 5.1 percent, compared with 7.5 percent last quarter"; Yellen on housing: "permits and starts will continue to fall."
Center of gravity: solid consumption and firm business investment, offset by a sharp residential contraction and softer capex plans.
Direction: slightly below neutral on net because housing drag and capex caution outweigh solid consumption.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 54 ±8 (Low).**
Evidence: Geithner: "fundamentals supporting relatively strong productivity growth seem to be intact." Kroszner: compensation revisions might show "actually higher productivity growth."
Center of gravity: brief, mildly positive.
Direction: slightly above neutral.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 27 ±6 (High).**
Evidence: Kohn: "Financial conditions remain quite supportive of both business and household spending." Minehan: "financial conditions remain pretty accommodative."
Center of gravity: supportive, liquid markets, long rates down; Warsh worries markets are too confident.
Direction: below neutral (looser) because participants repeatedly call conditions supportive and liquidity ample.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Not discussed as a functioning issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 27 ±6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Kohn: "Risk spreads are narrow, banks have not pulled back on business credit." Warsh: investors "have very little leverage in terms of the pricing of these deals."
Center of gravity: narrow spreads, abundant credit.
Direction: below neutral because the stated level is narrow.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 ±7 (Low).**
Evidence: Kohn: "equity prices have risen on balance in recent months." Warsh: "robust gains in the equity markets."
Center of gravity: price gains cited as support, with no explicit valuation view; default band.
Direction: at neutral (slightly under) because no valuation concern is expressed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±8 (Low).**
Evidence: Bies: "delinquency rates in mortgages still look good," but concern about adjustable-rate resets and subprime. Plosser: "recent pickup in nonperforming loans, which is concentrated in their commercial real estate portfolios" at some small banks.
Center of gravity: sound system with minor pockets of concern.
Direction: below neutral because the system is described as healthy with limited concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.**

**INFLATION_GOODS: N/A. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.**

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 62.** Bernanke: owners' equivalent rent "still at a three-month rate of 4.4 percent, relative to... 2.66 percent in 2005," though decelerating; Kohn: "A portion of the pickup is in the rent-of-shelter category." Elevated but easing.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 56.** Mishkin: expectations "well anchored" but "anchored somewhere around 2 1/2 percent on the CPI... somewhat on the high side"; Bernanke: "expectations are, in fact, really quite well contained." Anchored, at a level some find high.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 ±5 (High).**
Evidence: Moskow: "policy will need to be tightened further"; Plosser: "remain on hold for this meeting but to have strong enough language... to initiate rate increases in the near future"; Bernanke: "our bias is toward resisting inflation." Counter: Poole wants "language in our statement" that does not "tilt us toward tightening."
Center of gravity: hold with a tightening bias, a hawkish minority wanting more, a majority content with B.
Direction: above neutral because the hold carries a bias to further firming.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 ±6 (High).**
Evidence: Lacker: "I still think that the fed funds rate should be 5 1/2 percent." Roll call: "President Lacker No." Wording disputes: B vs B+, "on balance" struck by agreement.
Center of gravity: one hawkish formal dissent, several reservations on wording, near-consensus on holding.
Direction: moderate-low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 33 ±8 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Warsh: "we are at the beginning of a major test of market liquidity that's happening in real time in the fixed-income markets." Bies: subprime and ARM repricing "could have spillover effects on consumer spending."
Center of gravity: a few participants address market liquidity and mortgage risk meaningfully; the meeting is mainly about inflation and housing activity.
Direction: limited to moderate attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±6 (Moderate).**
Evidence: Bernanke: "It's very clear that our bias is toward resisting inflation"; the statement retains "any additional firming that may be needed." Moskow: the sense that policy is more likely to firm than ease "should be reflected in the minutes."
Center of gravity: mild tightening bias retained, with explicit flexibility and several participants resisting a stronger signal.
Direction: above neutral because the forward signal points toward firming.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 56 ±6 (High).**
Evidence: Kohn: "uncertainties are quite high. They might even justify the 'higher than usual' description." Bernanke: "no reduction in uncertainty."
Center of gravity: multiple participants stress housing and inflation-dynamics uncertainty, with nonlinearity concerns, not extreme.
Direction: elevated.
