# FOMC Member Discussion Scoring v4.1 - 2007-09-18 (transcript discussion)

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: the Q&A with the Desk and staff (members' questions only), the economic go-around, the policy go-around, a vote read-out, a short communications item, and a closed-session discussion of a proposed auction credit facility (ACF) and dollar swap lines. Staff content is ignored. Procedural remarks are ignored. The text is clean; no garbled passages affected scoring.

**2. Era context (not allowed to override the text).** Mid-2007. Participants describe a financial shock since mid-July/August (subprime, ABCP, term interbank funding) landing on an already weak housing sector, after a long hold at a 5.25% funds rate.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Chairman Bernanke: financial conditions tighter, housing/labor the key channel, downside tail risk, inflation moderated, favors 50 bp as insurance and willing to take it back.
- Geithner, Kohn, Mishkin, Yellen, Warsh, Kroszner, Rosengren, Stern, Poole, Lockhart, Pianalto, Hoenig: favor 50 bp. Mishkin and Yellen stress nonlinear downside and a restrictive real funds rate; Hoenig and Pianalto say the real rate is above neutral.
- Plosser: supports a cut and prefers 50 bp only if the statement signals balanced risks and no series of cuts.
- Evans: slight preference for 25 now plus 25 later, accepts 50.
- Fisher: argues firmly for 25 bp and no more; stresses global price pressures, labor shortages, moral hazard.
- Lacker: mild preference for 25 bp, can accept 50, wants the tilt removed; strongly opposes the ACF.

**4. Directional signals, core dimensions.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation as modestly below the level of concern. Core readings have been "encouraging" (Yellen, Pianalto, Lacker: core PCE under 1.4% over five months, Evans 1.8%), though headline stays elevated (Plosser), Kohn lists upside risks, and Fisher cites global cost pressure. Slightly below the midpoint, about 45.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Resource utilization is described as still high (Kohn), the Chair says labor markets remain tight, growth is slowing toward and below trend, and the Greenbook gap widens later (Kohn). Currently near potential, with the gap expected to open. About 49-50.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Many shortage reports (Fisher, Stern, Evans, Plosser, Chair "labor markets do remain tight"), but payrolls are weak (-4k August, Plosser, Yellen, Rosengren). Unemployment is not quoted, though Mishkin puts NAIRU a "smidgeon under 5 percent." Near-full employment, softening. About 53.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumer spending "continues to grow" (Chair, Stern, Warsh "fine, not great"), business spending "fairly solid" (Plosser), but housing and residential investment are very weak and confidence is falling. Net slightly below trend, about 45.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Only Evans mentions "slower growth of structural productivity," and Kroszner mentions 1998 productivity. Thin evidence, modestly below trend, about 43, Low confidence.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Clearly tighter. Evans: "overall financial conditions have tightened and in some markets have turned very restrictive." Yellen: rate moves add a "modest tightening," plus credit availability sharply cut. Geithner and Kohn agree. Above the midpoint, about 67.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Only flight-to-quality remarks: Rosengren "extremely low rates for short-term Treasury securities" as an abating anomaly, Kroszner comparing the on-the-run/off-the-run spread with 1998 (and saying it was larger then), Kohn "fled toward... Treasury bills." This is safe-haven demand with some bill-market anomalies, not auction or settlement failure. About 50, Low confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: A stated high level in several markets: LIBOR term spreads "very elevated" (Rosengren), "way out of line" (Mishkin), jumbo spreads up (Kohn), structured credit not clearing (Lockhart), nonprime unavailable. Offsets: Plosser says risk premiums rose "from abnormally low levels" and Lockhart's contact says spreads are not at the historical extremes. Level reading: above normal but not extreme, about 66.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Only price-move remarks (Plosser: market "still up roughly 5 percent for the year"; Yellen: small net change in equity indexes; homebuilder stocks down). No valuation view. Default neutral, 50, Low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed but sound on balance. Kroszner: "banking system... is in a good state," capital above minimums, but 23% of examined credits had weak underwriting, and balance-sheet growth and ABCP/SIV exposure are a concern (Rosengren, Kohn). Hoenig: banking conditions good. Fisher: well capitalized. Healthy with concerns, about 42.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Yellen notes only small net changes in the dollar. Kohn says finished-goods pressure could rise "if the dollar declines further," implying mild prior weakness. Brief, about 44, Low confidence.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher INFLATION_PRESSURE = more pressure: members say pressure is easing, so below 50 matches. Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter: members say tighter, above 50 matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress: members say spreads rose to a high level in funding and mortgage markets, above 50 matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: "good state" with caveats gives below 50, matches.

**6. Tier 3.**
- Policy lean: Large majority (about 15 of 19 participants) favor a 50 bp cut. Most attach a "not a series of cuts" caveat and see the funds rate moving back toward neutral. Moderate dovish, about 28. Heavy "insurance against tail risk" framing adds dovish weight.
- Disagreement: All ten voters voted Yes, so zero formal dissents. Fisher (firm) and Lacker (mild) preferred 25 bp as non-voters. Evans leaned 25 initially. Real, but tactical, disagreement on statement wording (Plosser, Pianalto, Lacker, Fisher want less on financial markets and a more balanced risk sentence; Kohn, Yellen, Stern, Warsh oppose). Moral hazard is debated, and Lacker sharply opposes the ACF while others welcome it. Moderate differences, about 33.
- Financial stability attention: Dominant. Financial market conditions were the Chair's "key theme" and a whole closed-session item (ACF, swaps) followed. Warsh, Kohn, Rosengren, Kroszner and Geithner devoted long passages to it. About 85.
- Forward guidance: Statement says uncertainty has increased and the Committee will "act as needed." Members split on whether this reads as a downside tilt, and many avoid signaling more cuts; Chair and others say the cut can be reversed. Easing lean with data dependence, about 37.
- Uncertainty: Strong. Kohn: uncertainty "larger than usual" and "very little central tendency"; Chair: "uncertainty has increased"; Mishkin: nonlinear, tail risk; Evans and Plosser: considerable uncertainty. About 70.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: "near potential" language gives 48-52. I use 50. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "tightened," "restrictive" gives above 50. 67. OK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: stated "elevated"/"way out of line" gives above 50. 66. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: "good state"/"well capitalized" gives below 50. 42. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: implied weakness gives below 50. 44. OK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: flight-to-quality and yield drops are not dysfunction, so score 45-55. 50. OK.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.**
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): within 15. OK.
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45): within 15. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (67) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (66): co-move. OK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (67) vs TREASURY_STRESS (50): treasury is only mildly discussed, so near-normal is consistent with the guidance for non-crisis dysfunction.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (45) vs sub-components: goods slightly above 50 (global/commodity costs) while core/expectations are benign, so a parent below 50 is consistent.

**9. N/A justification.** INFLATION_SERVICES: no member discussion of services prices (Kohn's unit labor costs and Fisher's lawyer salaries are too thin to score). INFLATION_HOUSING: only the Chair's passing uncertainty about owners' equivalent rent, no assessment. These are N/A.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "Readings on core inflation... have continued to be encouraging." Plosser, "Headline inflation remains quite elevated. I don't think that we can afford to be sanguine."
- Summary: Core inflation easing and risks seen as nearer to balance, with headline and global-cost caveats, leaves a slightly-below-target reading.
- Direction: below neutral because most members report disinflation progress.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "Resource utilization remains high by historical standards," alongside "the output gap is noticeably wider at the end of '08." Evans, expects "growth to return to potential by the second half of 2008."
- Summary: Economy near potential now, with slack expected to open.
- Direction: at neutral, near-potential language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +-6 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Chair, "Labor markets do remain tight." Fisher, "everybody is complaining that there's a massive shortage of labor." Counter: payrolls fell 4,000 in August (Plosser).
- Summary: Near full employment with shortage anecdotes, softening on the margin.
- Direction: slightly above neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Chair, "Consumer spending continues to grow along with incomes." Pianalto, "the combination of weaker consumer and business spending has led me to mark down my GDP projection."
- Summary: Solid current spending offset by collapsing housing investment and weakening confidence.
- Direction: slightly below trend on net.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 43 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Evans, "Taking into account the slower growth of structural productivity."
- Summary: Single passing reference to slower productivity.
- Direction: modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 67 +-7 [High]**
- Evidence: Evans, "overall financial conditions have tightened and in some markets have turned very restrictive." Geithner, "Financial conditions have tightened since the August meeting."
- Summary: Broad agreement that credit availability and terms tightened sharply.
- Direction: above neutral (tighter).

**TREASURY_STRESS: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "some of the financial anomalies have abated, such as the extremely low rates for short-term Treasury securities." Kohn, "people have fled toward liquidity and safety in Treasury bills."
- Summary: Safe-haven demand without described auction or settlement dysfunction.
- Direction: at neutral; yield moves are not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 66 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Rosengren, "The one-month and three-month LIBOR rates have remained very elevated." Mishkin, LIBOR "clearly has spreads that are way out of line." Counter: Lockhart's contact, risk spreads have not reached "the extremes of historical bands."
- Summary: Spreads moved up from abnormally low levels to clearly wide in funding, mortgage and structured credit markets, not to crisis extremes.
- Direction: above neutral, stated high level in key markets.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Plosser, "even though the market is down heavily in August, it is still up roughly 5 percent for the year."
- Summary: Price remarks only, no valuation view, so the default.
- Direction: at neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42 +-8 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Kroszner, "The banking system, as has been mentioned, is in a good state, but I don't want to overstate that"; examiners found "about 23 percent" of sampled credits with weak underwriting. Rosengren worries bank balance sheets will "crowd out other investment opportunities."
- Summary: Healthy capital with concerns about balance-sheet absorption and pockets of weakness.
- Direction: below neutral (sound with concerns).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 44 +-8 [Low]**
- Evidence: Yellen, "only small net changes in broad equity indexes and the dollar." Kohn, "especially if the dollar declines further."
- Summary: Brief references imply stable-to-slightly-weaker dollar.
- Direction: below neutral, weakly.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 56** - Fisher on Panamax rates, China retail cost increases of 2 to 4 percent, bread prices; Kohn on import prices and finished goods; offset by Chair's favorable PPI.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** - not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** - only a passing note by the Chair on owners' equivalent rent.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50** - Mishkin, "inflation expectations are grounded around 2 percent"; Chair, "Inflation expectations remain stable"; Plosser worries about keeping them stable.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 28 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Chair, "So I do advocate 50 basis points... some insurance is worthwhile." Yellen, "I think the stance of policy is unnecessarily restrictive." Fisher, "I would strongly recommend a rate cut of only 25 basis points."
- Summary: Large majority backs a 50 bp cut as insurance and a move toward neutral, with a minority preferring 25 and many wary of promising more.
- Direction: dovish side of 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-8 [High]**
- Evidence: Unanimous roll call (ten Yes votes). Fisher, "I'm still there" at 25 bp; Lacker, "I would describe that preference as a mild one"; Lacker on the ACF, "the balance of considerations weighs heavily against this proposal."
- Summary: No formal dissent, but non-voters preferred a smaller cut and members clashed on statement wording, moral hazard and the facility.
- Direction: moderate differences about pace and communication, not economics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 85 +-5 [High]**
- Evidence: Chair, "Financial market conditions were a key theme of our discussion today." Warsh's phased description of liquidity and valuation; the closed session on the ACF and swaps, with Kohn, "The piling-up of financing in very short term vehicles is an issue for financial stability."
- Summary: Financial market stress frames the economic assessment, the policy choice and an entire additional agenda item.
- Direction: very high attention (rivals economic assessment).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 37 +-7 [Moderate]**
- Evidence: Vote text, "will act as needed to foster price stability and sustainable economic growth." Lacker, language "going to be read as the equivalent of a downward tilt." Chair, "we should be prepared to take this back."
- Summary: Data-dependent, easing-leaning but deliberately not promising further cuts and keeping reversibility explicit.
- Direction: below neutral (easing lean).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 70 +-6 [High]**
- Evidence: Kohn, "the uncertainties around the output forecast were indeed larger than usual," and "The range of outcomes is just too wide, and there's very little central tendency in it." Chair, "uncertainty has increased."
- Summary: Many members stress unusual uncertainty, nonlinear tail risks and an unsettled financial structure.
- Direction: high.
