# v4.1 Score: FOMC member discussion, 1994-05-17 (transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type.** Member/participant transcript: opening questions to the Foreign Desk, Open Market Desk and staff (members' questions only), the District-by-District economic go-around, the Chairman's policy opening, the policy go-around and a roll-call read-out. The Chair proposes a 50 bp rise in the funds rate and the discount rate and a symmetric directive. Staff answers are removed, so some Q&A fragments are one-sided. Speaker labels are slightly irregular (e.g., "GOVERNOR KELLEY", one name blanked in McTeer's remark). I read this as a clean document and ignore the page markers.

**Era context.** Spring 1994, a year or so into a recovery after the early-1990s recession, and in the middle of a tightening cycle. The text says three 25 bp moves have been made already and that long rates and bond markets have been volatile since February. I score only what participants say.

**Speakers and views.**
- Most Reserve Bank presidents (Hoenig, Keehn, Parry, Broaddus, Forrestal, Stern, Jordan, Melzer) report very strong activity. Keehn reports capacity limits in autos, steel and trucks. Jordan reports plants running above sustainable capacity. Broaddus says "I can't remember a time when the anecdotal information... was any stronger."
- Hawkish-leaning inflation worriers: Jordan ("monetary policy is well behind the curve"), Melzer, Broaddus, Parry, Keehn (risks "on the up side"), Stern (risks of inflation on the up side).
- Calmer on inflation: Forrestal ("price pressures are practically nonexistent"), Boehne ("difficult to make those increases stick"), Minehan, Kelley (unit labor costs falling, productivity ~2%), Phillips, LaWare (fragile psychology).
- Lindsey: demand is moderate, the supply side is unknown, and he expects to "be coming back."
- Greenspan: the economy is "probably stronger than we suspect"; the move is needed to deflate a financial "bubble"; he wants to leave later moves open.

**Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see pressure as modestly above a comfortable level and rising. Core CPI is 2.7% and total CPI 2.4% (Parry), the median CPI is running 2.9% (Jordan), and seasonal revisions suggest higher numbers ahead. Price increases are appearing in steel, aluminum, rail cars and building materials (Keehn, Jordan). Wages are quiet. Several presidents (Forrestal, Boehne, Minehan) report none. Greenspan notes actual inflation and wages are "lower than one would ordinarily expect." Above midpoint, modestly: 57.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Participants see the economy as at or slightly above potential. Parry: "small amount of unused capacity." McDonough: "potential GDP will be fully used up" by year-end. LaWare: "reduction in slack." Jordan: motor vehicle plants run "above what would be sustainable long-run capacity" and growth of 5-6% would overshoot. Kelley and Phillips doubt overheating. Bright line is satisfied by "above sustainable" language; modest, so 55.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Skilled-labor shortages are widespread (Keehn, Stern, Broaddus, McTeer), but there is no broad wage pressure and job growth is sluggish in some Districts (Boehne). Lindsey says the help-wanted index is at 1982-recession-depth levels. Minehan cites "tightening in labor markets." Near NAIRU by year-end, no UE level stated for the nation. Near neutral, slightly tight: 52.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Strong. Housing very strong, autos and capital goods "booming" (Jordan), retail sales surpass expectations, appliances +25%, trucks capacity-constrained. Some caveats (April retail soft, Kelley's consumer-debt worry, LaWare's fragility). Above trend: 63.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Discussed by Kelley (2% recently, staff trend 1.5%, private-sector contacts think gains may persist), Lindsey, Phillips (re-engineering and cost savings), Boehne/Minehan on potential growth. Modestly above trend: 57.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Policy tightening and the bond sell-off have raised long rates and volatility ("turmoil is not over," Phillips; "bubble," "implied volatility," Greenspan). Phillips: price declines "increase the cost of capital." But credit availability is fine, loan demand is up and spreads barely moved. Somewhat tighter than a normal state, no stress: 57. No "accommodative" or "easy" wording is applied to conditions. (Kelley and Melzer call policy accommodative; that is policy, not conditions.)
- TREASURY_STRESS: Volatile long end, hedging flows from mortgage-backed securities (McDonough), "some market liquidity constraints as near substitutes have been sought" (Phillips). This is yield and volatility movement plus some liquidity strain but not dysfunction. Score 50, low-moderate confidence.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Greenspan states "a very modest increase in the yield spreads," small rise in CP over bills, BAA small, "junk bond yield spreads are not moving up," and that spreads in 1993 "had all been coming down dramatically." The level is low and compressed by recent history; the move is small. Level reading puts it below 50: 38.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Stock and bond price declines and a "bubble" of speculative positions are mentioned (Phillips, Greenspan), but there is no real equity valuation commentary. Default near neutral: 50, low confidence.
- BANKING_STRESS: Hoenig: "banking industry has not been this strong in our District in three decades," classified assets lowest on record, capital over 8.5%. Forrestal: banking quite strong. McDonough notes malaise in the financial services industry (Kidder Peabody, municipal finance) but that is securities firms, not banks. Healthy, so below 50: 27.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Broaddus, Lindsey, Jordan and Forrestal frame the dollar as under pressure ("run on the dollar," intervention May 4, "dollar bashing"). Melzer: "declines in... the foreign exchange value of the dollar since our last meeting." Weakening, so below 50: 33.

**Inflation sub-components.** GOODS: rising input and finished-goods prices in steel, aluminum, rail cars, building materials, autos (Keehn, Jordan, Forrestal on construction/textiles), against Forrestal's "nonexistent" and Boehne's "hard to make stick" elsewhere: 62. SERVICES: not discussed, N/A. HOUSING: house prices and rising construction costs discussed (McTeer, Forrestal) but shelter-cost inflation is not discussed; N/A. EXPECTATIONS: Broaddus's poll ("vast majority expect some worsening"), Melzer ("inflationary expectations have increased"), Greenspan's risk premium; rising modestly: 62.

**Tier 3.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Unanimous support for a 50 bp funds-rate move, a larger step than the earlier 25 bp moves, on the grounds that policy is behind the curve (Jordan wanted to go further; Melzer, Broaddus, Parry want no suggestion that the series is over). Greenspan: "unlikely we are near the end of this pattern" but "more data dependent"; symmetric directive favored by most. Moderate hawkish, with some forward balance: 68.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Zero dissents (10 yes votes on the read-out). Differences exist (Jordan vs. Forrestal on inflation; LaWare/Kelley/Phillips vs. the rest; Broaddus/Melzer prefer asymmetric language and object to "neutrality"; Melzer's wording suggestion; Jordan wanting more), but all minor and tactical. Broad agreement: 20.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Substantial. Foreign-currency and bond-market discussion; Greenspan's long exposition on the bubble, risk premia, option volatility and "tensile strength" of the financial system; Phillips on fragile markets and unwinding; McDonough on mortgage hedging; Jordan on yield-curve behavior; Kidder/Lazard remarks. Not the whole meeting; the economy dominates the go-around. Moderate-to-prominent: 55.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: The statement is explicitly non-committal ("continue to monitor"), directive symmetric, Greenspan says he doubts a move is needed before the next meeting but is "not near the end of this pattern." Members debate not signalling a pause (Melzer, Broaddus, Parry, Stern, Hoenig). Mild tightening bias, mostly data dependent: 58.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: McDonough: "question marks are rather larger than usual." Phillips: "uncertainty," "turmoil not over," wide range of forecasts. Stern: broken relations between labor markets and wages "raises uncertainty." Greenspan: financial "uncertainty and a lot of nervousness," "two credible scenarios." Lindsey: uncertainty about interest-rate objective. Several emphasize it but the policy action itself is stated with conviction: 50.

**Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 (> 50, matches "above sustainable"). FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 (> 50, no accommodative conditions language). CREDIT_SPREADS 38 (< 50: low level stated, small widening). BANKING_STRESS 27 (< 50). DOLLAR_STRENGTH 33 (< 50, weakening). TREASURY_STRESS 50 (no dysfunction; within 36-50 range plus a nudge for liquidity remarks; < 60).

**Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs LABOR 52: within 15. OUTPUT_GAP 55 vs CONSUMPTION 63: gap 8, fine. FC 57 vs CREDIT_SPREADS 38: gap reflects that tightening is in rates and volatility, not in credit risk premia; Greenspan explicitly notes the divergence. FC vs TREASURY: normal-ish times, near 50. INFLATION 57 vs sub-components (goods 62, expectations 62): consistent with a modestly above-neutral reading given calm wages.

**N/A.** INFLATION_SERVICES (no service-price discussion), INFLATION_HOUSING (no shelter-cost discussion). All other dimensions have some basis.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE 57 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Keehn, "some worrisome signs on the pricing front"; Forrestal, "price pressures are practically nonexistent." Center of gravity: pressure is building from commodities and expectations, but CPI and wages are still tame. Inflation is above neutral because the weight of presidents' remarks point to rising price pressure and upside risk.

**OUTPUT_GAP 55 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Parry, "small amount of unused capacity"; Jordan, motor vehicle plants "running above what would be sustainable long-run capacity." Center of gravity: near full capacity, edging above it. The gap is above neutral because several members describe capacity use as unsustainable and McDonough expects full use of potential by year-end.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS 52 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Stern, "labor shortages, particularly of skilled labor"; Keehn, "so far at least it has not resulted in higher wage rates." Center of gravity: tightening with quiet wages. Slightly above neutral because shortages are widely noted, but not much above because wages and job growth are subdued in places.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 63 +-5 High.** Evidence: Jordan, "Capital goods overall are booming"; Broaddus, "can't remember a time when the anecdotal information... was any stronger." Center of gravity: broad strength in housing, autos and capital spending. Above trend because nearly every District reports strong spending.

**PRODUCTIVITY 57 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Kelley, "recently been looking at a 2 percent increase... we might continue to do quite well"; Phillips, "emphasis on cost savings and productivity." Center of gravity: gains above the staff's trend assumption, with modest participant support. Above neutral because of Kelley's case and the supportive remarks; discussion is brief.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 57 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Phillips, "capital market volatility and stock and bond price declines do increase the cost of capital"; Greenspan, "this bubble is a bigger one than we had anticipated." Center of gravity: conditions have tightened through long rates and volatility without credit stress. Above neutral because long rates, volatility and the cost of capital rose, but loan demand and spreads show no impairment.

**TREASURY_STRESS 50 +-8 Low.** Evidence: McDonough, "the hedging requirement moves out the yield curve"; Phillips, "some market liquidity constraints." Center of gravity: volatile, but functioning. Neutral because yield moves and hedging flows are not dysfunction, with a small nudge for liquidity remarks.

**CREDIT_SPREADS 38 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "Junk bond yield spreads are not moving up"; "yield spreads were very marginal, and indeed they had all been coming down dramatically." Center of gravity: spreads remain low, with at most a small rise. Below neutral because the stated level is compressed and the widening is "very small."

**EQUITY_VALUATION 50 +-8 Low.** Evidence: Phillips, "stock and bond price declines"; Greenspan, "financial bubble" and "huge increases in mutual funds, both stock and bond funds." Center of gravity: price moves and bubble talk without valuation judgement on equities specifically. Neutral by the default for price-move-only commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS 27 +-6 Moderate.** Evidence: Hoenig, "banking industry has not been this strong in our District in three decades"; Forrestal, "banking in our District is quite strong; loan demand is up." Center of gravity: healthy banks. Below neutral because members describe strong earnings and capital; McDonough's financial-services malaise concerns securities firms.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH 33 +-7 Moderate.** Evidence: Melzer, "declines in both bond prices and the foreign exchange value of the dollar since our last meeting"; Lindsey, "we may have another run on the dollar." Center of gravity: dollar weak and under pressure despite intervention. Below neutral because the dollar is weakening.

**INFLATION_GOODS 62.** Evidence: Keehn, steel +$10/ton and ~2% more via discounts, aluminum, rail cars, truck prices; Jordan, "cost and availability of building materials." Goods prices are rising in industrial inputs; counterweight from Forrestal and Boehne.
**INFLATION_SERVICES N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING N/A.** House prices and construction costs mentioned, shelter inflation not.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS 62.** Evidence: Melzer, "inflationary expectations have increased as well"; Broaddus, "vast majority expect some worsening." Modestly rising expectations.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 68 +-6 High.** Evidence: Greenspan, "indicate that we are taking out a substantial part of the degree of accommodation"; Jordan, "I'd rather do more than less." Center of gravity: all back a 50 bp hike, with a symmetric directive and an open path. Moderately hawkish because a larger-than-before hike is unanimous and several see policy as still behind the curve, tempered by the symmetric directive and the "more data dependent" posture.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY 20 +-6 High.** Evidence: roll call, ten yes votes, no dissents; Melzer and Broaddus raise wording and asymmetry preferences. Center of gravity: unanimous on action, minor tactical differences. Low because the vote is unanimous and the differences concern language and pace.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS 55 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "we need to be very careful about this very large risk premium that exists in the financial markets"; Phillips, "I don't think the turmoil is over." Center of gravity: several members and the Chair address market fragility at length, while the economy still dominates. Moderate because multiple participants engage substantively but it does not rival the economic go-around.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 58 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: Greenspan, "I find it unlikely that we are near the end of this pattern, but... we are now becoming more data dependent"; draft statement, "continue to monitor developments." Center of gravity: not a pause promise, not a pre-commitment. Mildly hawkish because further tightening is considered likely, while the statement itself and the symmetric directive remain non-committal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED 50 +-8 Moderate.** Evidence: McDonough, "the question marks are rather larger than usual"; Stern, "it raises uncertainty in my mind... about the price outlook." Center of gravity: elevated uncertainty on transmission, supply side and markets, with firm conviction on the action. Elevated, not extreme.
