# v4.1 / V4.1.1 scoring: FOMC member discussion (transcript), meeting dated 2003-08-12

## PART 1: THINKING PROCESS

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript: opening procedural items, member questions to staff (staff answers removed), a full economic go-around by all participants, Chairman's policy recommendation, a policy go-around (every participant states support), a long exchange on the wording of the post-meeting statement, and a vote read-out at the end. It contains a policy vote and a stance discussion, so under V4.1.1 section 1 it is NOT special-topic. The file was read to the last line; the roll call at the end shows 12 voting names, all "Yes".

**2. Era context.** Mid-2003: the funds rate is 1 percent; a recovery is judged to be beginning after a weak, "jobless" period; inflation is very low and the Committee's main worry is unwelcome disinflation; long-term interest rates rose sharply during the intermeeting period; communication is a central theme. The scoring below rests on what participants say, not on this background.

**3. Speakers.** Greenspan (Chair), Governors Kohn, Ferguson, Olson, Gramlich, Bernanke, Bies; Presidents Moskow, Parry, Guynn, Santomero, Pianalto, Minehan, Broaddus, Stern, McTeer, Poole, Hoenig; First VP Stewart. Broad view: recovery is strengthening ("growth is in the process of strengthening", Kohn), but slack is large, employment is weak, productivity is astonishingly high, inflation is very low and the risk of further disinflation is the main concern (Parry, Bernanke, Ferguson, Gramlich, Bies, Broaddus, Kohn). Stern and Hoenig are less worried about disinflation; Guynn doubts the output gap is large enough to compel disinflation. Policy go-around: all support holding at 1 percent and the same risk assessment. Much of the debate is about the final "considerable period" sentence in the statement.

**4. Core dimensions, direction in words first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Participants see inflation pressure as well below the midpoint. Parry: core PCE about 1 percent "about as low as I would like to see it go"; Gramlich: core PCE "flirting dangerously" with the bottom of an acceptable range; Greenspan: "no evidence of any improvement in business pricing power". Table band 11-25 ("subdued", "very low"). Some think market expectations of inflation are higher, and Hoenig sees balanced 2004 inflation risk, which keeps it from the bottom of the band. Score about 23.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below midpoint. "Substantial gap between actual and potential output will remain" (Stewart); "the current gap remains substantial" (Bernanke); "slack considerable" (Kohn). Guynn doubts the gap is large enough for long enough but does not say there is none. Bright-line: below potential, must be less than 50. Score about 32.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Well below midpoint. "Labor market continues to disappoint" (Ferguson); "slowest expansion in post-World War II history, as measured by employment", jobs down 2.6 million (McTeer); unemployment 6.2 percent (Broaddus) with the downside impulse perhaps understated. Table: UE 5.5-7 gives 31-45, but the weak job creation and jobless character push to the low end. Score about 30.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Near but slightly below trend, net. Consumers holding up, housing strong, tax cuts arriving, Q2 final domestic demand "jumped sharply" (Parry), equipment spending "showing more life" (Moskow), but cautious firms, "no widespread commitment ... for a full-bodied capital expansion" (Stewart), and consumers worried about jobs (Santomero). Net about 47.
- PRODUCTIVITY: Far above trend. "Astounding pace of productivity growth" (Kohn); "huge positive productivity shock ... slightly greater than the one that occurred in the late 1940s" (Gramlich); "second strongest recession-expansion phase" (McTeer). Score about 86.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants say conditions tightened since June as long rates rose (Parry, Kohn, Bernanke), but the level is still described as supportive/accommodative: "financial market conditions remain very favorable" (Guynn), "rates are still quite low", corporate risk spreads narrow, equities resilient (Kohn), healthy credit (Hoenig). Bright-line: "supportive/favorable/accommodative" means below 50. The recent tightening keeps it from being very loose. Score about 40.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yield rises are discussed at length but they are policy-expectation moves, which are not dysfunction. The only market-functioning item is Bies's question on Treasury fails ("fails always worry me when they reach that magnitude ... day-to-day functioning of the dealers"), a concern, not described dysfunction. Score 45-55 band, Low confidence: 48.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Kohn gives a level view: "risk spreads have come in so far this year", "persistence of narrow corporate risk spreads", "real corporate bond rates especially low". Olson: "corporate bond market is still strong", high-risk markets improved, leveraged transactions up. Level is narrow: below 50 under the bright-line rule. Minehan notes reduced willingness to lend on shared national credits but willing lending for smaller C&I loans, a minor offset. Score about 34.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: Members report price moves and profit outlook ("Equities have turned in a very respectable performance", Ferguson; "resilience of equity prices", Kohn; Poole: good profits would make the stock market perform better). Bies is skeptical about S&P earnings forecasts, a mild valuation-type remark but not a stated overvaluation view. V4.1.1 section 2: price move only, so 45-55, Low confidence. Score 48.
- BANKING_STRESS: Hoenig "healthy financial system"; Olson "loan quality is no longer mentioned as an issue of concern ... continued improvement in asset quality"; Minehan "shift of loans into problem status continues to decrease". Healthy: below 50. Score about 28.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Only China's peg and the trade deficit are mentioned (Pianalto, Stewart); no member describes the dollar's movement. N/A.

**5. Polarity verification.** Higher = more inflation pressure / more slack-free economy / tighter labor / tighter financial conditions / more credit stress. Evidence of low inflation, large slack, weak labor each means lower scores; evidence of a favorable financial backdrop with narrow spreads means below 50 on FC and credit spreads. All consistent.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- Policy lean: Hold at 1 percent with the risk assessment weighted toward unwelcome disinflation. No one argues for tightening. Bernanke says "premature to conclude that we should not consider further rate cuts"; Gramlich says if any of his three questions turns "yes" "we ought to be lowering rates"; Kohn wants "highly accommodative stance of policy for some time". Poole, Stern and Hoenig are more relaxed about disinflation, but none argues for a hike. Lean dovish: about 40.
- Dissent: Unanimous vote (all 12 voting names "Yes"). No formal dissent. Disagreement exists on emphasis (how worried to be about disinflation: Stern, Hoenig, Guynn versus Bernanke, Ferguson, Gramlich) and sharply on whether to keep the last statement sentence; a show of hands on dropping it gave 7 of 18 (the Chairman called it "right on the margin" and kept the truncated sentence). Minehan, Guynn, Hoenig, Poole, Broaddus pushed back against Ferguson, Bernanke, Kohn. This is mostly tactical wording disagreement, no formal dissent. About 33.
- Financial stability: Rise in long rates, fed funds futures, mortgage refinancings, market-versus-Fed divergence, Treasury fails, bank lending and credit quality are discussed by several participants; but it is market conditions rather than stability risk, and not the main focus. About 42.
- Forward guidance: Committee language "policy accommodation can be maintained for a considerable period" kept in the statement; Greenspan says "we can hold tight ... for a considerable period"; Poole wants to say the Fed will be "on hold at a 1 percent funds rate for the foreseeable future". Several resist pre-commitment, but the signal itself is low-for-long. About 22.
- Uncertainty: Substantial: timing and extent of recovery uncertain ("plenty of room for uncertainty", Parry; "the outlook has been so uncertain", Minehan), productivity model questions, Greenspan's "humility" and "different economic world", Ferguson's "new world of greater uncertainty", inventory-swing upside risk. Not Knightian; many pair it with growing confidence in recovery. About 52.

**7. Bright-line check.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 (<50, slack) OK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 40 (<50, supportive) OK. CREDIT_SPREADS 34 (<50, narrow level) OK. BANKING_STRESS 28 (<50, healthy) OK. DOLLAR_STRENGTH N/A. TREASURY_STRESS 48 (no dysfunction described, 45-55) OK.

**8. Coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs LABOR 30: aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 32 vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 47: diverge by 15, justified because spending is near trend and improving while the gap level is still large (the gap is a level, spending a rate; recovery has only just begun). FC 40 vs credit spreads 34: both loose, co-move. FC vs Treasury stress: normal times, Treasury near the middle. Parent inflation 23 vs sub-components: goods about 38 and expectations 45 are above parent, but members emphasize disinflation in the underlying rate; the parent is anchored to core PCE near 1 percent, so small mismatch is explained.

**9. N/A justification.** DOLLAR_STRENGTH: no participant describes dollar movement. INFLATION_SERVICES: no participant discusses services prices. INFLATION_HOUSING: house prices/sales mentioned only as activity (Stern), not shelter inflation; N/A.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE AND SCORES

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 23 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Parry, "core PCE inflation to average about 1 percent in 2004, which is about as low as I would like to see it go"; Gramlich, core PCE "flirting dangerously with ... the bottom of an acceptable range". Center of gravity: very low, with concern about further drift down. Direction: below neutral because inflation is below target and the stated worry is disinflation.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "the most reasonable guess is that the current gap remains substantial"; Stewart, "a substantial gap between actual and potential output will remain". Center: slack persists even with a pickup. Below neutral (below potential).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Ferguson, "The labor market continues to disappoint"; McTeer, "jobs are still down 2.6 million from the level in February 2001". Center: weak, jobless. Below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Parry, "real final demand by domestic purchasers jumped sharply in the second quarter"; Stewart, "there isn't any widespread commitment in the business community for a full-bodied capital expansion". Center: consumers and housing near or above trend, investment just reviving, cautious firms. Slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 86 +-5 (High).** Evidence: Kohn, "the astounding pace of productivity growth"; Gramlich, "a huge positive productivity shock". Center: many participants call it exceptional. Above neutral (well above trend).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Guynn, "financial market conditions remain very favorable, and fiscal policy and monetary policy remain accommodative"; Parry, "the tightening of financial conditions since we met in June". Center: recent tightening from higher long rates, but level still supportive. Below neutral (supportive); tightening direction stops it from being lower.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Bies, "fails always worry me when they reach that magnitude. I worry what that does to the day-to-day functioning of the dealers." Center: one concern about fails, large yield moves treated as expectations-driven. Near neutral; no dysfunction described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 34 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn, "risk spreads have come in so far this year" and "the persistence of narrow corporate risk spreads despite huge increases in bond yields"; Olson, "The corporate bond market is still strong". Center: spreads narrow, credit available for large borrowers. Below neutral (level narrow). Note: Minehan reports reduced willingness to lend on shared national credits, a small offset.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 (Low).** Evidence: Ferguson, "Equities have turned in a very respectable performance since the last meeting"; Kohn, "the resilience of equity prices". Center: price moves reported without valuation commentary (Bies's skepticism on earnings forecasts is mild). At the rubric default (V4.1.1 section 2), slightly below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: Hoenig, "a healthy financial system that can support the credit needs of businesses"; Olson, "continued improvement in asset quality in both the consumer and the business sectors". Center: healthy banks. Below neutral (low stress).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** No participant describes dollar movement.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38.** Evidence: Hoenig, "prices of materials have continued to rise while prices of finished goods have continued to encounter downward pressure"; Pianalto, relative prices of manufactured goods "lower" due to China. Center: weak goods pricing, some input cost pressure.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A.** Not discussed.
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A.** Not discussed as price inflation.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45.** Evidence: Parry, market forecasts of inflation "quite a bit higher" than Committee forecasts; Hoenig, "the inflation premium has in fact increased"; Kohn, a "reduction in fears of deflation". Center: expectations seen as anchored, with markets expecting somewhat more inflation than members.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 +-6 (High).** Evidence: Bernanke, "premature to conclude that we should not consider further rate cuts"; Gramlich, "there doesn't seem to be enough present evidence of the need to lower them either ... sit tight". Center: hold at 1 percent with an easing bias on risks (downside inflation risk). Lean dovish to balanced; nobody calls for a hike.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +-8 (High).** Evidence: Vote read-out, all 12 "Yes"; Minehan on the last sentence, "I think that's hitting it too hard", against Bernanke, "I would appeal to the Committee to retain the sentence"; show of hands: "seven of nineteen" (18 present) wanted it dropped. Center: unanimous vote, real but tactical disagreement on wording, moderate difference on disinflation concern. Moderate differences level.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Kohn's extended analysis of financial conditions and the term structure; Bies on Treasury fails; Hoenig and Ferguson on yield moves. Center: several participants address market conditions meaningfully, but not as the main focus and with little about stability risks.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +-7 (Moderate).** Evidence: statement text read by Greenspan, "policy accommodation can be maintained for a considerable period"; Poole, "we're going to be on hold at a 1 percent funds rate for the foreseeable future". Center: accommodation to persist; some resist being explicit. Mild easing bias / low-for-long.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +-8 (Moderate).** Evidence: Minehan, "the outlook has been so uncertain"; Parry, "there still is plenty of room for uncertainty about the next few quarters". Center: many stress uncertainty about the timing and strength of recovery and the economy's workings, but mostly with growing confidence that recovery is under way. Elevated.
