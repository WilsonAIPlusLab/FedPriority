# v4.1 scoring: member discussion, transcript, meeting 2007-12-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**1. Document type.** Member/participant discussion transcript, a joint FOMC-Board videoconference called on short notice. It is not a regular economic go-around. The topic is two liquidity measures: a Term Auction Facility (TAF) and a dollar swap line with the ECB. The input holds the Chairman's opening remarks, one go-round on the proposals, a few exchanges (Poole, Plosser, Stern), the closing procedure and a roll-call read-out. Staff turns are removed, so some answers are missing and a few member follow-ups ("And on the callable issue?") hang without a reply. Nothing is garbled enough to hinder scoring.

**2. Era context (kept separate from the scoring).** Members place the meeting in a period of strained term funding markets, a year-end funding concern, and a policy meeting scheduled "next week." I score only what the participants say.

**3. Speakers and views.**
- Bernanke: the problem is "not primarily a liquidity issue... a credit and valuation issue," but liquidity providers "have withdrawn significantly" and term funding "has become quite difficult"; fire-sale risk is rising.
- Supporters of the TAF and the swap: Lockhart, Fisher, Rosengren, Pianalto (reluctantly, she leans toward a discount-rate spread cut), Evans, Yellen (with doubts about benefits), Geithner, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Hoenig (asks only questions and votes yes on the swap).
- Opponents: Poole ("I do not support the TAF"), Lacker ("I oppose this facility at this time"), Stern ("serious misgivings... wouldn't proceed with it at this time"). Plosser has no problem with the swap but is "more troubled" about the TAF and would first cut the discount rate.
- Geithner: "what's happening in markets now is very serious and really potentially very dangerous," and monetary policy "is going to have to bear most of the burden."
- Kohn: institutions facing "true Knightian uncertainty."
- Warsh: deterioration "quite remarkable and quite profound."

**4. Core dimensions, direction first.**
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: no participant says anything about the level of inflation. N/A.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Geithner mentions "fear about the likely bout ahead for housing and the U.S. economy" and Warsh says the deterioration "is likely to have some impact on the real economy." Neither gives a level relative to potential. N/A.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, PRODUCTIVITY: not discussed. N/A.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Participants see conditions as tight, well above the midpoint. Evidence: term funding "very hard to get" (Bernanke); "very difficult and disturbed situation in money markets" (Yellen); "extreme" pressures and a system "operating with a thinner margin" (Geithner); "dysfunctional markets" (Warsh); "hair-raisingly scary out there" (Kroszner). It is not a freeze: the Chairman says markets still function at the margin and Lacker argues this may be normal coping. Estimate: tight, in the 66-80 band, near the lower middle of it.
- TREASURY_STRESS: the only reference is Bernanke noting the one-month T-bill rate "has been very erratic" because of demand ebbs and flows. That is not a description of Treasury dysfunction. N/A.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Level reading. Participants state high levels: LIBOR "50 basis points over the discount rate" (Lacker), "wide spreads we're observing" (Stern), "spreads have widened quite significantly" (Kroszner), euro unsecured term spreads "quite elevated" and ours 25 to 30 bp above them (Geithner), "elevated rates like LIBOR" (Bernanke). Stern adds that quality spreads were "very narrow" months ago, so his remark confirms that today's level is wide, not just that spreads moved. Geithner calls part of this "a normalization of risk premiums" but says it "really is extreme." The level is stated high, so the score must be above 50. These are interbank term funding spreads and risk premiums rather than corporate bond spreads, which I note as a limit on the evidence. Estimate: wide, 66-80 band, lower half.
- EQUITY_VALUATION: not discussed. N/A.
- BANKING_STRESS: Participants see stress as elevated and above the midpoint. Evidence: "Some banks obviously need more capital" (Poole); "capital, balance sheet, credit loss problem" (Stern); "liquidity problems... will turn into capital insolvency problems" (Kroszner); "questions about the financial wherewithal of some large commercial banks are now more apparent" (Warsh); the Dallas bank-failure story (Fisher) as a warning. No one says banks are sound; Lacker says only that balance sheet pressure "is at the heart of what's going on." Estimate 55-80 band, in the middle (stress emerging to significant, short of failures).
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: the only dollar remark is a dollar funding shortage in Europe. That is not the value of the dollar. N/A.
- Inflation sub-components: not discussed. N/A.

**5. Polarity checks.** Higher FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS = tighter: evidence shows tight, so the score is above 50, which matches. Higher CREDIT_SPREADS = more stress: wide spreads, so above 50, which matches. Higher BANKING_STRESS = more stress: capital and solvency worries, so above 50, which matches.

**6. Tier 3 check.**
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: No one argues about the funds rate directly because the policy meeting is "next week." The tilt is dovish by implication: Geithner says monetary policy "is going to have to bear most of the burden" in responding to risks to the economy; Kohn speaks of avoiding "cutting the federal funds rate even more than we otherwise would"; Poole expects the confidence effect to come from "a further reduction in the federal funds rate"; Yellen worries the TAF will be seen as a substitute for "standard monetary policy action." Several members (Pianalto, Plosser, Rosengren, Kroszner earlier) favored cutting the discount rate. The evidence on rate policy is indirect, so confidence is Low. Lean: dovish, about 36.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: One formal "No" on the swap roll call (Poole). Three members oppose the TAF outright (Poole, Lacker, Stern; Lacker and Stern do not vote in the roll call as printed), with Plosser troubled, Pianalto and Yellen reluctant, and a split over whether the problem is liquidity or capital. The TAF itself gets no formal vote in this document. Exchanges are civil, with no heated debate. This falls in the "moderate differences" band, upper part (31-45).
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: the whole meeting is about funding market strain, fire-sale risk, bank capital and liquidity facilities. Dominant, near the top of the scale.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: No explicit guidance. Implicit easing expectation from Geithner and Poole, and Stern, Yellen and Kohn each refer to next week's decision without saying which way. Mild easing lean with very low confidence.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Strong. "Unknowables" (Bernanke); "true Knightian uncertainty" (Kohn); "uncertainty about what's responsible for the run-up in term funding spreads" (Yellen); "great uncertainty banks face" (Geithner); repeated "it may not work" from nearly everyone. Much of it concerns whether the TAF will work, which is a different thing from uncertainty about the economic outlook, so I stay in the high band, not the very high one.

**7. Bright-line check.**
- OUTPUT_GAP: N/A, no rule triggered.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: described as strained and dysfunctional, none says accommodative. Above 50, satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: stated high level (wide, elevated), above 50, satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: no "sound/healthy" language, above 50, satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.
- TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.

**8. Cross-dimension coherence.** OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT are all N/A, so those pairs do not apply. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (tight) and CREDIT_SPREADS (wide) co-move, consistent. TREASURY_STRESS is N/A, which is allowed. Inflation parent is N/A with N/A sub-components, so that check does not apply.

**9. N/A justification.** All macro dimensions: no participant gave an assessment of the real economy or inflation, and the meeting was called for the funding-market measures alone. TREASURY_STRESS: no member described Treasury market functioning. EQUITY_VALUATION: equities never come up. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: only a dollar funding shortage abroad is mentioned.

**Special-topic judgment (stated plainly).** The rubric's edge case says special-topic operations meetings with no standard economic assessment should be all N/A. This meeting is an operations meeting, and I applied that rule to every macro and market-price dimension. I did not apply it to the financial-stress dimensions and Tier 3, because members here give extended, explicit descriptions of funding conditions, spreads, bank capital, uncertainty and policy stance, so there is real text to score. A reader who applies the rubric rule literally would set everything to N/A; the financial and Tier 3 values below should be read as lower in reliability than the usual go-around-based scores and are flagged in ANOMALY_NOTE.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: N/A.** Not discussed by any participant.

**OUTPUT_GAP: N/A.** Only passing references to risks to housing and "the real economy," with no level.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: N/A.** Not discussed.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: N/A.** Not discussed.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.** Not discussed.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 72 +-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Yellen: "a very difficult and disturbed situation in money markets." Geithner: "what's happening in markets now is very serious and really potentially very dangerous." Warsh: "the deterioration of the financial markets since we last met has been quite remarkable and quite profound."
- Center of gravity: nearly every participant describes term funding and risk-taking in markets as strained and worsening, though not frozen.
- Direction confirmation: financial conditions are above neutral (tighter) because funding is hard to get and risk premiums are extreme.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A.** Only a remark that the one-month T-bill rate has been erratic (Bernanke); no description of Treasury market dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 72 +-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Lacker: "LIBOR is 50 basis points over the discount rate." Kroszner: "these spreads have widened quite significantly." Stern: "uncomfortable with reacting to the wide spreads we're observing."
- Center of gravity: members describe term funding spreads as clearly wide and elevated, with Geithner saying the move "really is extreme" even if partly a normalization of risk premiums.
- Direction confirmation: spreads are above normal (more stress) because members state a high level, not only a widening. Confidence is Moderate because the spreads discussed are interbank term funding spreads, not corporate credit spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A.** Not discussed.

**BANKING_STRESS: 68 +-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Kroszner: "it's exactly these liquidity problems that will turn into capital insolvency problems." Warsh: "questions about the financial wherewithal of some large commercial banks are now more apparent in the markets."
- Center of gravity: capital shortfalls, balance sheet pressure and doubts about large banks are raised by several participants, with no one calling the system sound.
- Direction confirmation: banking stress is above neutral because capital and solvency concerns are explicit, but there are no failures, so it stays short of severe.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A.** Only a dollar funding shortage abroad is mentioned.

**Inflation sub-components (goods, services, housing, expectations): N/A.** Not discussed.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 36 +-10 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Geithner: "monetary policy is going to have to bear most of the burden." Poole: if it works it will be because of the expectation of "a further reduction in the federal funds rate." Kohn: addressing liquidity directly "rather than, say, cutting the federal funds rate even more than we otherwise would."
- Center of gravity: the rate decision is deferred to next week, but the surrounding language leans toward easing and the liquidity measures are accommodative in kind.
- Direction confirmation: the lean is dovish (below 50), because several members expect or assume more easing and none argues for tightening. Confidence is Low because there is no direct debate of the funds rate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 44 +-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Poole voted "No" on the swap and said "I do not support the TAF." Lacker: "I oppose this facility at this time." Stern: "I have serious misgivings about this proposal and wouldn't proceed with it at this time."
- Center of gravity: one formal No and three explicit opponents of the TAF, with several reluctant or conditional supporters, and a split over liquidity versus capital as the diagnosis, but a civil tone and a large majority in favor.
- Direction confirmation: disagreement is moderate to notable, above the near-consensus range because opposition extends to the diagnosis, and below the significant-division range because there is one formal dissent and no heated exchange.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 90 +-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Chairman: the imperative of "trying to help markets function more normally"; Geithner: the system "is operating with a thinner margin."
- Center of gravity: the entire meeting is devoted to funding-market strain, fire-sale risk and lender-of-last-resort tools.
- Direction confirmation: attention is at the top of the scale because financial stability is the sole agenda item.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +-12 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Geithner: monetary policy will have to respond to the risks; Yellen and Stern defer the policy question to "next week."
- Center of gravity: no explicit signal, with only an implied easing lean from a few members.
- Direction confirmation: the signal is slightly below neutral (easing side) because of the implied expectation of more rate cuts; confidence Low because the guidance is inferred.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Kohn: "a true Knightian uncertainty that they can't price." Yellen: "the uncertainty about what's responsible for the run-up in term funding spreads."
- Center of gravity: many participants stress that outcomes of the market stress and of the facility itself are unknowable, but much of it is about efficacy of the tools more than the macro outlook.
- Direction confirmation: uncertainty is high because several members use strong language ("Knightian," "unknowables") and almost all say the measure "may not work."
