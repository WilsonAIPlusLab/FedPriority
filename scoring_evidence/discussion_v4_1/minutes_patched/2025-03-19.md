## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 55 +-10, Low).** The old quote about "increased investment in technology or in business process improvements" does not appear in the participants' views in the new input. The one productivity reference is that several participants said their contacts expressed optimism about profitability from "expected productivity gains from artificial intelligence and related technologies". That is a forward-looking, secondhand and brief remark, so productivity is modestly above trend with Low confidence. The score is unchanged and only the evidence is replaced.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old score of 40 relied on an assumed backdrop of a sound banking system. Participants did not discuss bank health, capital or stress. The closest passages are Waller's dissent on reserve balances and ample reserves (a reserves-management point) and the remark that borrowers "with higher credit scores continued to be able to obtain financing" (credit availability). Under the N/A rule the topic is not discussed, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50).** Policy is balanced. All participants favored holding the rate. "Well positioned to wait for more clarity" and a majority seeing inflationary effects "more persistent than they projected" lean cautious. The runoff slowing, which "almost all" supported, is described as having "no implications for the stance of monetary policy". Participants also said policy "could be eased if labor market conditions were to deteriorate". Hawkish and dovish signals offset.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 30).** Broad agreement on the economy and the rate ("all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain"). There is one formal dissent, Governor Waller, who agreed with the rate but opposed the slower balance-sheet runoff. "Several participants did not see a compelling case for slowing the pace of runoff." The disagreement is about tactics, not the economic assessment, which matches the one-dissent anchor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 30).** "Some participants" noted tighter financial conditions, and "most of them" said risk premiums remained low. "A few participants cautioned that an abrupt repricing of risk in financial markets could exacerbate the effects of any negative shocks." Balance-sheet and reserve-scarcity remarks are market-functioning context. This is limited attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50).** The signal is neutral and data-dependent. Participants said "policy decisions were not on a preset course", and the statement language is "extent and timing of additional adjustments". The Committee "would be prepared to adjust the stance... if risks emerged". Restrictive policy "could be maintained for longer" if inflation stays elevated and "eased" if the labor market weakens, so there is no directional tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 72).** "Participants generally noted the high degree of uncertainty facing the economy." "Significant uncertainty surrounded the magnitude and persistence" of tariff effects, and "uncertainty about the net effect of an array of government policies... was high". The vote record says uncertainty "had increased". The language is widespread and genuine (policy and tariff paths unknown, a few noting the difficulty of separating persistent from temporary inflation), but not "uncharted territory".
