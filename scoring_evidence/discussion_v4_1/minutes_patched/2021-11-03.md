## Evidence for changed dimensions

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 33 +-6, Moderate).** Participants see the banking system as healthy, so stress sits below 50, but the evidence is thin and mixed. A couple of participants noted "the solid capital and liquidity conditions of banks"; a few stressed "the importance of maintaining strong bank capital positions, particularly at the largest banks", and a few cited "the growing exposure of banks to nonbank financial firms" as a vulnerability. The old unqualified "well-capitalized" reading overstated the comfort, so the score moves from 28 to 33. The bright-line (healthy implies below 50) is still met.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss goods demand, energy, wages and rents as price forces. No participant states a view on services price inflation. The only services reference is that Delta "slowed the shift of consumer demand toward purchases of services", which is about demand composition. Set to N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58 +-6, High).** Lean hawkish: the policy rate stays at 0 to 1/4 percent, but the Committee begins tapering. "All participants judged that ... it would be appropriate to announce at this meeting a reduction in the pace of net asset purchases", and the vote record has similar $15 billion monthly cuts through mid-2022. "Some participants preferred a somewhat faster pace", and "various participants" would raise the target range sooner if inflation stayed high. A number stressed "a patient attitude toward incoming data", and the target range is unchanged with "more stringent criteria for raising the target range". This is a hold with a tightening bias, so 58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 24 +-6, High).** Broad agreement. The vote record shows "Voting against this action: None", and "all participants" backed starting the taper. Differences are about pace and timing: "some" wanted faster reductions, "various" would move on rates sooner, and "a number" preferred patience. There is no disagreement about the assessment itself and no reservation recorded in the vote, so 24.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42 +-8, Moderate).** Moderate attention. "A number of participants commented on issues related to financial stability": a couple on resilience, a few on bank capital, a few on elevated valuations, nonbank exposure and CCP collateral liquidity, a few on cyber, several on stablecoins, a few on climate risk. The topic is broad but is a minority, single-paragraph item beside the economic assessment, and accommodative financial conditions come up as a supportive backdrop. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 55 +-8, Moderate).** Mildly hawkish forward signal. Purchases will keep falling by similar amounts each month, "implying that increases in securities holdings would cease by the middle of next year", and the statement adds that the Committee "is prepared to adjust the pace of purchases". This is balance-sheet tightening ahead. On rates the guidance is conditional and unchanged: hold the range until maximum employment is reached and inflation is on track to moderately exceed 2 percent, with participants saying the taper "was not intended to convey any direct signal" on rates, and "patient" language. Flexibility runs both ways ("would not hesitate to take appropriate actions to address inflation pressures"). Score 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 66 +-7, High).** High, genuine uncertainty. "Participants observed that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained high"; they "particularly stressed" labor force participation and the time to resolve supply chains. Their "uncertainty regarding this assessment [inflation easing in 2022] had increased", policy is conducted "in the current highly uncertain environment", and "continuing considerable uncertainty" supports a patient attitude. Score 66 (considerable uncertainty range).
