## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 52).** Participants see the economy as operating near potential with high resource use. "The most likely outcome of the current policy stance was growth near potential" and the current tightness is "consistent with the economy growing at a pace near its potential"; only "some members" saw "growth somewhat above potential" as "a distinct possibility", and inflation persisted "despite high levels of resource use". Near-potential wording keeps the score in the 48-52 band, at the top because of the high resource use.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 60).** Participants describe a tight labor market: "similarly tight labor market conditions", construction "limited only by shortages of qualified labor", "the current degree of tightness in labor markets", with the directive recording unemployment of 4.8 percent. Members worry that further tightening could put "substantial upward pressures on wages". "Tight", not "very tight", and a 4.8% rate point to the upper-moderate range rather than 64.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' text contains no statement about credit spreads, credit availability or issuance. The only financing remark is the "accommodative financial climate" that supports business investment, which is scored under FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS. Topic not addressed, so N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48).** The "large gains that had occurred in financial wealth" and "very large increases that had occurred in stock market wealth" are cited as supports for consumer spending and housing. No participant comments on valuation or risk of a correction, so the score sits near neutral at low confidence.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, 35).** Members attribute subdued inflation to dollar appreciation lowering import prices, "a significant decline in world oil prices", large grain harvests, and businesses citing "highly competitive conditions" that made it "very difficult to raise prices". These point to soft goods prices, though no explicit goods price figures come from participants, so low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss wage, benefit and cost pressures and the inflation outlook, but nowhere characterize inflation expectations or their anchoring. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** All members endorsed an unchanged stance, judged policy "appropriately positioned" with real rates "relatively high by historical standards", yet "a number of them expressed the view that an anticipatory policy move ... likely would be needed at some point" and the members "remained concerned about the outlook for inflation". Hold with a tightening bias: lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" and "all the members" supported the directive and the tightening bias; vote record shows ten votes for and none against. Near-consensus; only a mild difference in emphasis ("some members" on growth above potential).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12).** Financial matters appear only as brief supports to spending ("an accommodative financial climate", large stock-market wealth gains, housing affordability). No participant discusses financial stability risks. Brief mentions only.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 63).** Members "agreed that the retention of an asymmetric directive toward tightening was consistent with their view that the risks remained biased toward a rise in inflation" and viewed the next move as "more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing", while not attaching "a high probability" to a move in the intermeeting period. Mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 32).** Participants note that "the underlying reasons for the favorable price trends were not entirely clear" and that labor costs rose less than past experience implied, but otherwise express standard caveats and a clear view of the policy stance. Somewhat elevated at most.
