## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 58).** Participants see activity above a sustainable pace: "a number questioned how long these conditions might last", members worried foreign-trade restraint might "not exert enough restraint on aggregate demand to slow the expansion to a sustainable pace in line with the growth of the economy's potential", and the economy might "continue to accommodate relatively robust economic growth and a high level of resource use for an extended period". Above-sustainable language puts the score above 50 (bright-line), but no explicit "beyond potential" statement exists, so it is modestly above neutral: 58.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 58).** Productivity is viewed as a positive but limited offset: "unit labor costs had been contained by large capital investments and other initiatives that had raised the productivity of labor", yet "additional improvements in productivity growth could not be counted on to offset" faster compensation, which would "increasingly outpace productivity". Modestly above trend, thin evidence: 58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 32).** Several members noted "further increases in equity prices from already high levels"; the rise was "somewhat puzzling" given slowing profit growth, and several cited a possible downward stock-market adjustment. Elevated but not bubble language: 32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** Nearly all preferred or accepted an unchanged funds rate (5-1/2 percent) with a tightening-tilted directive; "most saw little urgency to tighten", while one member (Jordan, dissent) wanted immediate tightening and several saw tightening needed soon. Hold with a clear tightening bias: 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 30).** Eleven votes for, one against (Mr. Jordan, who thought money and credit growth were inconsistent with reducing inflation). Members otherwise agreed on direction and differed on timing and urgency. One hawkish dissent, broad agreement: 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** Equity prices "played an important role in the assessments of several members"; a number cited ample liquidity and accommodative financial conditions; several cited a possible stock-market correction and some a possible "outsized and undesirable response in financial markets" to tightening. Moderate attention, mostly as input to the macro assessment: 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 70).** The Committee adopted an asymmetric directive; members agreed "policy tightening likely would be needed at some point" if strength persisted, the information made "some tightening a likely prospect in the not too distant future", and "several suggested" it could come "in the near future". A clear tightening bias without commitment to timing, tempered by "most saw little urgency": 70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 45).** Asian "outcomes for economic growth and exchange rates were still very much in doubt"; the factors behind favorable inflation "were not all well understood, and consequently expectations of greater price pressures had to be tentative"; the Committee cited "uncertainties in the outlook". Several explicit caveats but a mostly confident core view: 45.
