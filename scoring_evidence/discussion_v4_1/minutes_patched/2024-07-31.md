## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38.** Participants lean dovish: a hold now, with easing expected soon. All participants supported keeping the range at 5 1/4 to 5 1/2 percent, "although several observed that the recent progress on inflation and increases in the unemployment rate had provided a plausible case for reducing the target range 25 basis points at this meeting." "The vast majority observed that, if the data continued to come in about as expected, it would likely be appropriate to ease policy at the next meeting." "Many" warned that easing "too late or too little" risked weakening activity, while "several" warned that easing too soon could reverse the progress on inflation. Majority tilt toward some easing, but no cut yet, so 38 (moderate dovish, not strong).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18.** The vote record shows twelve votes in favor and "Voting against this action: None." Participants were broadly aligned on the assessment. The differences were of emphasis and timing: several could have supported a cut now, and "almost all" wanted more information first; "several" stressed the risk of easing too soon, while "many" stressed the risk of easing too late. No dissent and no sharp disagreement, but some visible tactical divergence, so 18 (broad agreement, above the unanimous floor).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50.** Participants spent a distinct passage on financial stability: "participants who commented noted vulnerabilities to the financial system that they assessed warranted monitoring." Banking soundness with unrealized losses and uninsured deposits ("some"), CRE exposures ("participants generally"), the discount window ("several"), cyber risk ("many"), Treasury market leverage and the standing repo facility ("several"), and household delinquencies ("a couple") were all raised. It is a meaningful, multi-topic section but secondary to the macro and policy assessment, so 50 (moderate discussion, several participants addressing it).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 35.** The signal points toward easing, conditioned on data. "Almost all participants" said more information was needed before lowering the range, yet "the vast majority" said easing at the next meeting would likely be appropriate if data came in as expected. "Most participants" emphasized that decisions are "conditional on the evolution of the economy rather than being on a preset path." The agreed statement keeps "does not expect it will be appropriate to reduce the target range until it has gained greater confidence." Easing lean with data dependence, 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 36.** Somewhat elevated but largely standard. Participants "highlighted uncertainties affecting the outlook, such as those regarding the amount of restraint currently provided by monetary policy, the lags ... and the degree of normalization of the economy"; the vote record states "members viewed the economic outlook as uncertain." Against that, the participants saw risks "continuing to move into better balance," the moderation in growth as "largely in line with what they had anticipated," and a couple viewed risks as "more or less balanced." Standard caveats plus a few named sources of uncertainty, so 36.
