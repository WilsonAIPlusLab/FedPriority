## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 42 +-7, Moderate).** Participants see conditions as mixed but on net supportive. "Most participants" attributed "the increase in longer-term interest rates, the strengthening of the dollar, the rise in equity prices, and the narrowing of credit spreads" to fiscal and tax expectations (tightening and loosening forces together), and the vote record says the securities holdings "should help maintain accommodative financial conditions." Accommodative wording requires a score below 50. The old quoted sentences were not in the participants' views, so the value is retained on new evidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 46 +-7, Low).** The only mention is "the narrowing of credit spreads" listed among post-election market moves. That is direction with no level information. The V4.1 rule puts direction-only cases at 45-55 on the side of the move; narrowing goes at 46. The old 32 treated narrowing as a compressed level, which the text does not state.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48 +-8, Low).** Participants mention the "rise in equity prices" (attributed by "most" to fiscal and corporate-tax expectations) and that household wealth was "boosted by ongoing gains in housing and equity prices." There is no valuation or overvaluation commentary beyond "many" urging caution about market developments, so the price-move-only default of 45-55 applies.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' views never address the banking system, bank capital or lending standards. The old low-stress score rested on sentences not found in the input, so it becomes N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 62 +-6, Moderate).** Moderately hawkish. All members voted for a 25bp hike and judged the stance would "remain accommodative." "Most participants" favored a "gradual pace," but "many participants" judged the Committee "might need to raise the federal funds rate more quickly," while "some" saw only modest overshoot risk. This sits near the Dec 2015 liftoff anchor with a slight forward tilt.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20 +-6, High).** Broad agreement. "Voting against this action: None," and participants "agreed" that evidence warranted the increase. Differences are about emphasis: "many" wanting readiness to move faster versus "some" seeing little overshoot risk, and "a couple" worried the gradual-pace message could be misread. No distinct camps on the economy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28 +-8, Moderate).** Limited attention. One paragraph on financial-market changes, with "many" cautioning about implications, and "financial vulnerabilities in some foreign economies" as one listed downside risk. The Committee's "readings on financial... developments" appear as an input to policy. Financial stability itself is context, not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 60 +-7, Moderate).** Mild tightening bias. Members expected conditions to "warrant only gradual increases," with the funds rate staying "below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run" for some time, but stressed the path "would depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming data." "Many" flagged a faster path as possible and "a couple" worried that guidance could be read as only one or two hikes a year.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 60 +-8, High).** Elevated, concentrated on policy. Participants "emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the timing, size, and composition" of fiscal and other policies, "uncertainty regarding fiscal and other economic policies had increased," and "many" said it made communication "more challenging." Data being "broadly in line" and near-term risks "roughly balanced" keep it below the high band.
