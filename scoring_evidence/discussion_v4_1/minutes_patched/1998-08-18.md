**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 70).** Participants see the labor market as above neutral and clearly tight, but the old "extremely tight" wording came from outside the participants' discussion. The new input has members citing "very tight labor markets", "widespread tightness in labor markets", "an upward tilt in the rise of labor compensation", and other members saying the economy "could well be producing beyond its sustainable potential". The 4.5 percent unemployment rate (directive only) caps the score within the "very tight" band. Score 70.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 60).** Direction is appreciation (above 50). Members cite "the appreciation of the dollar" as a factor holding down inflation, and the directive says the dollar "rose somewhat further" against other major currencies. The "strongly against the yen" detail is not in the new input. "Somewhat" implies modest strength. Score 60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50).** Policy held steady ("all but one" agreed). A majority saw risks as "now more balanced" and favored symmetry, while other members still saw risks "tilted to some degree toward rising inflation"; "a few members" worried about monetary-aggregate growth and one called for "a prompt firming". Downside risks "had increased appreciably further". Evenly balanced, with hawkish minority and dovish drift offsetting. Score 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** One formal dissent (Mr. Jordan, preferring firmer policy given strong demand and money growth), plus several members preferring asymmetry who "could accept symmetry". Differences are about risk balance and directive wording, not the near-term action. Moderate differences with one dissent. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** Several participants address financial conditions: spreading foreign "financial turmoil", "equity prices and risk spreads ... beginning to be adversely affected", the paragraph on financial conditions less stimulative, and one member's "rising speculative imbalances and excesses". Discussion is meaningful but secondary to the economic assessment. Score 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 47).** The forward signal is neutral to slightly less hawkish than before: a majority moved to a symmetric directive so that "the Committee's next policy move could be in either direction", with a "cautious wait-and-see approach" and no change expected in the intermeeting period. The remaining tightening-bias members only accepted symmetry. Score 47.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 65).** Members "viewed the external sector as a major source of uncertainty"; "the members agreed" the duration and extent of disruptions "could not be anticipated with any degree of confidence"; "the uncertainties ... remained substantial"; the effects of foreign fragility "were unknown", and worse outcomes "could not be ruled out". Many members emphasize genuine difficulty, not rhetorical hedging. Score 65.
