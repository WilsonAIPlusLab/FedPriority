## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants' text gives only the direction: "several participants also cited wider credit spreads as a factor that was likely to restrain growth in demand," and early in the period spreads on riskier corporate bonds were "widening" before conditions "eased on net." No participant states a level (neither "elevated" nor "compressed"). Under the V4.1 level rule this is direction-only, so a modest widening score on the stressed side with Low confidence: 54.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** The old score rested on a staff passage about "recent depreciation of the dollar," which is not in the participants' views. The participants' own references point to strength: "many participants" expect net exports to be weighed on by "the appreciation of the dollar since mid-2014," "a number" say the "earlier appreciation of the dollar" weighs on net exports and manufacturing, and "it also was noted that weak growth abroad could lead to further appreciation of the dollar." The dollar is described as an established drag but with no movement this period, so a moderately strong reading: 60.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED).** The old evidence on a non-appreciating dollar and bottoming oil was staff text. Participants say inflation is expected to stay low "in part because of earlier declines in energy prices" and in "prices of non-energy imports," with these "transitory effects" expected to dissipate; "a couple" cite the "apparent leveling-off of oil prices." Goods price pressure is judged below neutral but there is no direct goods-price discussion, hence Low confidence: 40.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** "Most participants" judged it appropriate to hold at 1/4 to 1/2 percent; "many" favored "a somewhat lower path for the federal funds rate than ... in December"; "several" argued for "proceeding cautiously." Against this, "a couple" favored a hike now, "some other participants" saw April as possible, and George dissented for a hike. Hold with a mild easing bias and a hawkish minority: 42.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote record shows nine in favor and one dissent (Ms. George, for a 25bp hike, citing near full employment and "financial distortions" from delay). "A couple" of participants shared that view and participants split on whether labor markets were at maximum employment and on the April outlook. One dissent plus assessment differences on labor slack and inflation persistence: 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Several themes involve financial conditions: the early-period "sharp reduction in risk-taking," "recent financial market turbulence," "wider credit spreads," and the statement that relevant data include "changes in financial conditions." The dissent mentions "financial distortions." It is a recurring context item rather than the focus: 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The Committee "expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases," with the funds rate "likely to remain, for some time, below" longer-run levels, and "several members" now project a lower path than in December. Participants agreed "data ... rather than calendar dates" will decide timing, with "a range of views" on April. Data-dependent with an easing-leaning revision: 38.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** "Many participants" saw "appreciable downside risks," participants "offered different characterizations of the risks," "several" raised two-sided risks (stronger-than-expected activity, inflation expectations), and the Committee cited "risks and uncertainties surrounding the outlook." Elevated but with confidence that data are "mostly consistent with their expectations": 52.
