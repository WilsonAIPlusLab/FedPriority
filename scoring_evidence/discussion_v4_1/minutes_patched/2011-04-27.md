## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 45).** Participants lean slightly dovish to balanced. The Committee made no changes and kept the $600 billion purchase program to completion by end of June with the funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent (dovish current action). Against that, "some participants" said the Committee "would need to be prepared to begin taking steps toward less-accommodative policy", and "a few of these participants" thought action to raise rates or sell assets might be warranted later in the year. "Some participants" countered that "an early exit could unnecessarily damp the ongoing economic recovery", and "some members" said a new asset-purchase program would need a significant change in outlook. The camps roughly offset, with the stance remaining accommodative. Score 45.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 25).** Broad agreement. The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None"), so there were no formal dissents. Participants "all agreed" on monitoring inflation closely. Differences were of emphasis and timing: "some" favored preparing for less-accommodative policy, "some" cautioned against an early exit, and "a few members remained uncertain about the benefits of the asset purchase program" but judged changes inappropriate with the program nearly complete. Disagreement stays on tactics rather than economics. Score 25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Limited attention. One paragraph reviews improving financial conditions and bank credit quality. "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions was creating incentives for increased leverage and risk-taking", which "could pose a risk to financial stability" if widespread, and the downside risks mention European fiscal strains and "financial disruptions" from a failure to raise the debt limit. These are context rather than focus. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 33).** Easing lean with data dependence. The vote record keeps the "exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period" language and says the Committee "will regularly review the size and composition of its securities holdings", "prepared to adjust those holdings as needed". "A few members" saw inflation risks as possibly warranting "steps toward less-accommodative policy sooner than currently anticipated", which tempers the low-for-long signal. Score 33.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Somewhat elevated. Participants noted an "unexpected slowing" in first-quarter growth, said "a number now judged" growth risks "tilted to the downside" (energy prices, Europe, Japan supply disruptions, fiscal adjustment, debt limit), and "many" became "more concerned about the upside risks" to inflation; "sentiment can change quickly" and one participant's contacts turned more pessimistic. Most still saw growth risks as "broadly balanced" and viewed the weakness as "largely transitory". Score 40.
