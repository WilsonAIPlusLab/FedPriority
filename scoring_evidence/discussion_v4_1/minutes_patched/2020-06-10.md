## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 47 +-8, Low).** Participants say only that "risk spreads across a range of fixed-income markets had narrowed as the intense flight to safety ... ebbed further." This is direction only. The old "still above pre-COVID" reading came from staff-style level information, not participants' views. Under the V4.1 level-governs rule, direction-only text scores 45-55 on the side of the move. The move is narrowing, so 47, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old evidence ("Banks had entered the crisis well capitalized", "could come under significant stress") does not appear in the new input. Participants discuss Fed actions supporting "the flow of credit" and lending facilities, but say nothing about the health of the banking system. Topic not raised, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 16).** Strongly dovish. "All participants judged" maintaining 0 to 1/4 percent; "a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy would likely be needed for some time"; Treasury, MBS and CMBS purchases to continue "at least at the current pace". The Committee holds maximum accommodation but does not launch or expand easing, so the score sits in the upper part of the 11-25 band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows ten votes for and "Voting against this action: None." Participants agreed the "current stance of monetary policy remained appropriate"; differences are limited to how much further to clarify guidance ("many", "most", "a number of") and to facility details ("several").

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** Moderate attention. Participants gave a dedicated paragraph to market functioning, receding funding strains and narrower risk spreads. They returned to market functioning and credit flow in the policy paragraphs, and "several participants" said the Fed should "remain ready to adjust" the emergency facilities. There is little on financial-stability risks, so the score stays in the middle of the 40-60 band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 10).** Strong easing bias, not yet an explicit threshold. Participants would hold the range "until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track" to meet its goals. "Most participants" said the Committee should "communicate a more explicit form of forward guidance", and "a number" wanted guidance structured to supply "the accommodation necessary to support rapid economic recovery".

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 80).** Very high. "There remained an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to the economic outlook"; "a great deal of uncertainty" about reopening and infections; "a number of participants" saw "a substantial likelihood of additional waves of outbreaks." Participants also said it was "too early to draw any firm conclusions" about the trough. Not at the March 2020 emergency extreme, since recovery signs were noted.
