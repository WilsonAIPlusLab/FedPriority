## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 60).** Participants see the labor market as tight and tightening, but not at an extreme. Evidence: unemployment "declining further and payroll gains well above a pace consistent with maintaining a stable unemployment rate"; in several Districts "some difficulty in finding qualified workers", with labor shortages in some cases; a few said the labor market "was increasingly tight". Offsetting: wage increases "generally ... modest and in line with inflation and productivity growth", and one participant saw the absence of wage pressure as leaving "scope for further improvement". Score 60, above neutral, band widened to +-6.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants did not assess the banking system's health. Banks appear only in a conditional: several worried that "a protracted yield curve inversion could adversely affect the financial condition of banks and other financial institutions". The old claim of post-crisis reforms is not in the input. Set to N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 62).** Policy lean is moderately hawkish. "Almost all participants" favored a 25bp hike, and "most participants" supported a gradual approach. Two members preferred to hold, a few were uncomfortable with the projected 2018 path, and a few favored a somewhat faster pace. The two sides roughly offset, and the stance is described as "accommodative". Score 62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 47).** Two formal dissents (Evans, Kashkari), both preferring to hold the 1 to 1 1/4 percent range because inflation stayed "substantially" or "notably below" 2 percent. Disagreement reaches the inflation and expectations assessment (a couple of members worried about the SEP pace). Most others agreed on the economic outlook and differed on timing and pace, so this sits in the lower part of the 46-60 band. Score 47.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** Moderate attention. "Some" described accommodative financial conditions, "a couple" worried that persistent accommodation "could, over time, pose risks to financial stability", and "some" raised yield-curve inversion as a risk to "financial stability". A few advocating faster tightening cited "continued low interest rates risked financial instability". It is more than passing but not a dominant topic. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** Mild tightening bias. Almost all members "reaffirmed their expectation that economic conditions would ... warrant gradual increases in the federal funds rate", with the rate "likely to remain, for some time, below levels ... expected to prevail in the longer run"; "Nonetheless" the path "would depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming data". Gradual and data-dependent, so mild rather than clear tightening. Score 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 30).** Standard-to-slightly-elevated uncertainty. "A number of participants expressed uncertainty about the magnitude of the effects of tax reform on consumer spending"; business-tax effects "uncertain", labor-supply effects "quite uncertain". Risks were seen as "roughly balanced", and no one questioned models or described unusual uncertainty. Score 30.
