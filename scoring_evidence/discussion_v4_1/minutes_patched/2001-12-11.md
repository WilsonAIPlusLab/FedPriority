## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 25 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see output as well below potential: members said "the economy clearly was continuing to contract", and expected "aggregate demand would remain well below the economy's potential output over the next several quarters", with "slack in labor and product markets". Offsetting this, the decline was moderating and the incoming data no longer called for downward forecast revisions. A deep-recession reading from the old input's labor and industrial data is not supported by the participants' text; 25 sits in the 16-30 band, below 50 as the bright line requires.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 32 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see slack and further weakness: "slack in labor and product markets" and "substantial further deterioration in labor market conditions, which was widely anticipated". No level (unemployment rate) appears in the participants' views, so the score is a direction-plus-slack reading, 32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 38 +-8, Moderate).** Mixed, net below trend. Consumers: "consumer expenditures appeared to have been relatively well sustained thus far"; members "generally anticipated that solid gains in consumer spending would underpin the economic recovery". Housing showed "considerable resilience". Business: capital spending "continuing to decline at a rapid pace", nonresidential construction "declined sharply", firms "very cautious". Weighted toward the investment collapse and anticipated labor-market-driven consumer downside, net 38.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 43 +-10, Low).** Participants say little about market conditions. What they do say is supportive: "relatively attractive mortgage interest rates", "low interest rates ... helping to overcome" consumer caution, and the risk of policy-reversal market unsettlement judged "limited". Offsets are the "recent rise in mortgage interest rates" and stock market vulnerability. Policy accommodation is a policy, not a conditions, statement. Slightly loose, 43, low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant statement about spreads, credit availability or credit pricing; the old basis was staff intermeeting data.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 40 +-10, Low).** Members cited among risks "the vulnerability of current stock market valuations should forecasts of a robust rebound in earnings fail to materialize" and "the risk of a downturn in the stock market". This is a modest concern that valuations are stretched relative to earnings prospects, not a view that equities are depressed; 40, low confidence given two brief mentions.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Banking system is not discussed in the participants' views.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The exchange value of the dollar is not discussed by participants.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only related phrase is "rapid increases in the cost of healthcare and other worker benefits", which describes labor compensation costs, not services price inflation; no services price commentary by participants.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 37 +-6, High).** Moderately dovish: "all but one member" supported "a 25 basis point reduction in the federal funds rate to a level of 1-3/4 percent", and "several members viewed an easing action as a measure of insurance". Tempered by the view that the stance "was already quite accommodative" and that "the Committee's easing cycle was likely to be approaching its completion", plus one hawkish dissent.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35 +-6, High).** One formal dissent: "Hoenig dissented because he preferred to leave the federal funds rate unchanged", judging policy already stimulative and fearing greater rate volatility. "Several saw a decision to ease as a close call." Disagreement concerned policy tactics and risk weighting more than the economic assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22 +-8, Moderate).** Limited: members mentioned stock market valuation vulnerability, consumer debt burdens, further terrorist incidents, and the risk that a policy reversal "would foster significant market unsettlement" (judged limited). No sustained discussion of financial stability; a few passing references, 22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 42 +-7, Moderate).** Mild easing lean moving toward neutral. The press statement retained that "risks continued to be weighted mainly toward conditions that could foster economic weakness", but members said this "was not intended to convey the impression that the Committee necessarily contemplated further easing", and "several suggested the desirability of signaling" that the easing cycle was nearing completion; a "policy turnaround next year" was widely expected by markets.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 68 +-6, High).** "The members agreed" that stabilization evidence "remained quite tentative" and that recovery timing and strength were "surrounded by a high degree of uncertainty"; "a number of members saw substantial risks" that activity could fall short of the baseline, and "it was unclear how the various factors affecting consumers would interact". High, shared by all members, hence 68.
