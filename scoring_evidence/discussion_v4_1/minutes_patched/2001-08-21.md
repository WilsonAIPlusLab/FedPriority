## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 40).** Participants see output below potential but not in a deep slump: "many" said the economy had "deteriorated marginally further," "substantial excess capacity in many industries," capacity utilization "declined substantially," and policy aims for growth "near that of the economy's potential" eventually. Modest-to-moderate slack, 40. The 14-point gap to LABOR_TIGHTNESS is explained by members describing labor markets as still generally tight.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 54).** Members reported "widespread indications of some slackening in what were still generally tight labor markets" and that "workers available to fill a variety of skilled job openings continued to be in short supply," with compensation pressures "easing somewhat." Level is tight but easing, with payrolls declining; 54.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 40).** Members "again emphasized the ongoing strength in household spending" and "homebuilding generally had remained robust," but business capital expenditures were "slowing sharply further," inventory liquidation persisted, and members saw "little likelihood of a marked turnaround." Business investment is described as "the major source of weakness," so the net is below trend; 40.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 45).** Members cited "accommodative financial conditions" as underpinning consumer spending and noted the funds rate "had been lowered substantially" and the aggregates "growing rapidly." Only "some members" said conditions "did not indicate as much oncoming stimulus" (long rates not lower, equities lower, dollar higher). Bright-line (accommodative gives < 50) is respected; somewhat loose to neutral, 45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The new input contains no participant comment on corporate or risk spreads; the old evidence came from text outside the participants' views. N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 55).** Some members noted "prices in equity markets had fallen substantially further," and members cited "negative wealth effects from falling stock market prices" and "downward revisions to expected earnings." No valuation judgement is given, so near the neutral default with a mild lean toward concern; 55, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant discusses banks, capital or lending standards. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 57).** Some members noted "the dollar had appreciated in foreign exchange markets" since the start of the year, as one reason financial conditions provided less stimulus. Appreciation, mentioned briefly, so modestly above 50; 57, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 35).** Dovish: "all the members endorsed" a 25bp cut to 3-1/2 percent, with "no member" preferring unchanged or a larger move; the weakness was "a distinct threat." Tempered by "some members" expecting the easing to end "relatively soon" and by envisaged preemptive reversal later. Moderate dovish; 35.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 10).** Vote record: "Votes against this action: None." All members endorsed the cut and the press-statement sentence; only emphasis differences (timing of the end of easing, "some" longer-term inflation concern). Near-consensus; 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Financial conditions get one paragraph from "some members," plus wealth effects and "perceptions of greater risk in financial markets" from foreign weakness mentioned as context. Limited attention; 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 30).** "All the members supported the retention of the sentence" that risks are weighted toward "further weakness in the foreseeable future," an easing bias. But "the easing process probably would have to be terminated" before clear strengthening, "this point might come relatively soon" (some), and members envisaged "moving preemptively to offset" stimulus later. Easing lean with a nearing pause; 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** "The strength and timing of the pickup remained uncertain," "considerable uncertainty surrounded the timing of the eventual upturn" in business investment, productivity an "important uncertainty," and foreign weakness added "unease." The uncertainty concerns mainly timing rather than the whole outlook, so elevated but not extreme; 50.
