## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 62 to 58).** Participants see spending as strong but moderating, not surging. Several attributed views: members expected "appreciable further expansion in consumer expenditures" backed by employment and income growth, but saw the absence of further large stock gains as likely to "induce some moderation"; for business investment, "weak trends in orders" and "a considerable deceleration in total business investment" with "still robust if reduced increases"; residential activity "expected to moderate a bit" though currently strong. The old evidence (brisk consumer pace, investment accelerating "to a surprising extent") was not participant text. Net: above trend ("robust") but decelerating, 58.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60).** All but one member favored "a slight tightening" (25bp to about 5-1/4%) to "foster continued subdued inflation", and many saw "a possible increase in inflation pressures as the main threat". Offsetting this, supporters "did not anticipate that further tightening would be needed in the near term", and the action was a limited move. Majority favors tightening, but with a pause lean afterward: 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 32).** The vote record shows a single dissent, McTeer, "for essentially the same reasons he did at the June 30 meeting: low inflation and, except for energy, minimal inflation in the pipeline"; one member in the discussion judged tightening "not warranted". Supporters were unified on a symmetric directive ("The members all agreed"). A typical one-dissent meeting, with the disagreement on economics but narrow: 32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Financial conditions are mentioned as a damper on demand ("less accommodative financial conditions"), stock-market uncertainty gets a few sentences, and "some members" noted that the action reduces the stimulus given "to counter the global financial turmoil", whose vestiges had not all disappeared. These are context mentions by some members rather than a focus: 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50).** The Committee kept a symmetric directive; members agreed to "keep its options open" with the next move dependent on "evolving economic and financial conditions", and the directive says developments are "equally likely to warrant an increase or a decrease". No directional commitment, with a near-term pause expectation roughly balanced by the stated inflation risk: 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 45).** Several participants flagged uncertainty explicitly: "the outlook for price inflation remained subject to considerable uncertainty", "substantial uncertainty surrounded the outlook for stock market prices", the productivity path was "the major uncertainty", and inventory forecasts carried "substantial risks in both directions". This language is partly standard minutes hedging, and the Committee was broadly confident of a rebound in a stable expansion, so moderate-elevated rather than high: 45.
