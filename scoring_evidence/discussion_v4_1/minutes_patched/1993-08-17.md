## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** The new input has no participant statement on productivity trends; the old quotes about productivity-enhancing equipment spending cannot be found in it. Not discussed, so N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quote about concern that equity prices were "high relative to earnings and dividends" is not in the new input. Participants mention only shifts of funds into "stock and bond mutual funds" because of low short-term rates, which is not a valuation view. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quote on banks having "bolstered their capital positions" is not in the new input. The text refers only to a "greater willingness to supply credit by banks", which is about credit supply, not bank health. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50).** Members agreed on "a steady policy course" and endorsed returning to an unbiased directive, since a tightening bias was "less likely" to be needed (a dovish tilt relative to the prior stance). Offsetting this, "many members" saw policy as accommodative and "monetary policy probably would have to move in the direction of restraint at some point", and "one member" thought policy "overly stimulative for some time" and wanted restraint "at the first favorable opportunity". Net: balanced, 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 16).** The vote record shows 12 in favor and "None" against; "all the members expressed a preference" for the directive. One member voiced a more hawkish view but agreed tightening was not appropriate now. Near-consensus with a minor emphasis difference: 16.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12).** Attention is limited to one paragraph: "no compelling evidence" that policy was fostering credit flows associated with "speculative excesses", damped debt growth and shifts into mutual funds. A brief mention, in line with the 1993 era: 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 52).** The Committee moved to an "unbiased intermeeting instruction", said the probability of an intermeeting change was "relatively remote" and that a tightening bias was now less needed, yet "many members" thought restraint would be needed "at some point". Close to neutral with a faint tightening tilt: 52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 38).** Members said it was "too early to determine" whether the price data meant renewed disinflation, to "withhold judgment" on car demand, that the timing of the tax effect "was uncertain", and that the NAFTA outcome was "uncertain". Standard-to-somewhat-elevated caveats: 38.
