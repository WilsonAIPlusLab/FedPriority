## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 47 rested on a quoted bank-lending phrase that is not in the new input. In the new text participants discuss household and business balance-sheet improvement and note that some housing developers had trouble securing construction finance, but no one describes spread levels or credit pricing. With no level information and no clear direction, the topic is not discussed in a scoreable way, so the dimension is N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old evidence ("major indexes of stock prices rose") is absent from the new input. Stock-related text is limited to mutual fund inflows in the staff analysis of M2, which is excluded. No participant offers a valuation view, so N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score used the pattern of appreciation against European currencies and the yen, which is not in the new input. The one remaining dollar sentence ("trade-weighted value of the dollar ... increased on balance") is part of the directive's background summary, not a view voiced by participants, so it is not scored. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** Most members "saw little or no reason to change monetary policy in either direction", and "nearly all" favored unchanged pressure on reserves. A majority favored retaining the tightening bias; some expected the next move to be firming; several said steady reserve conditions had become "more accommodative" in real terms; one member urged "a prompt move toward restraint". The range reductions were stated to be "solely technical adjustments" with no tightening implied. A hold with a tightening bias gives 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** One formal dissent, Mr. Angell, who wanted a prompt tightening and argued policy was "overly expansive". Both range votes were unanimous. Other members who preferred a symmetric directive "could support an asymmetric directive", and "several members" preferred to retain the old ranges but accepted the reductions. One dissent plus mild reservations about tactics gives 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12).** Financial matters appear mainly as context: the "much improved financial condition of many households and business firms", the balance-sheet adjustment process, a few developers' difficulties securing construction finance, and Angell's "excessive liquidity in financial markets". There is no sustained discussion of financial stability, which fits the 1993 anchor. Moderate confidence because the aggregate and balance-sheet talk could be read as slightly more.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 60).** A majority retained the "bias toward possible tightening"; some said "the next policy move was more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing", although any adjustment seemed "somewhat remote". The directive text allows "slightly greater reserve restraint" as acceptable. This is a mild tightening bias with no commitment, so 60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** The Committee's policy discussion cites "considerable uncertainty about the outlook" from mixed data and "questions about the course of fiscal policy"; "a number of members" cautioned about downside risks from the budget, tax and health-care-reform outlook, and "some members" said uncertainty about M2 and M3 might persist. Offsetting this, members "generally agreed" the expansion was firmly established and the forecast central tendencies were narrow. That puts it in the elevated band, below the 65-80 range for "considerable uncertainty" used rhetorically by all.
