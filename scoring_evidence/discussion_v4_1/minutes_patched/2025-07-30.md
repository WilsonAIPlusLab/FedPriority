## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 42).** Participants see spending and investment as below trend. Participants observed growth slowed in the first half "driven in large part by slower consumption growth and a decline in residential investment"; several expect growth to "remain low in the second half"; several cited slower real income growth weighing on consumers and policy uncertainty slowing business investment. Offsets: elevated household net worth (some), improved business sentiment (several). Score 42.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old AI-adoption support did not come from participants. Participants mention AI only as a possible employment risk. Not discussed as productivity.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 45).** Several participants said broader financial conditions were "either neutral or supportive of stronger economic activity", and some cited elevated household net worth. A possible tightening via higher risk premiums appears only as a downside risk. Neutral-to-supportive is below 50 under the bright-line rule. Score 45.

**TREASURY_STRESS (FILLED, 50).** A few participants raised vulnerabilities in the Treasury market: dealer intermediation capacity, hedge fund presence and "fragility associated with low market depth". These are structural concerns to monitor, with no described dysfunction. Score 50, Low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old values (42, 38, 38) were taken from the Desk manager's market report and staff review. Participants do not describe spread levels, equity valuations or dollar movement. The only related phrases are "elevated asset valuation pressures" (several participants, not equity-specific) and FX swaps as dollar funding sources (a couple), which are financial-stability items. All three are N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (FILLED, 38).** A couple of participants said capital levels "remained strong" while some banks were vulnerable to higher long-term yields and unrealized losses. Healthy with minor concerns; Low confidence because only a couple spoke. Score 38.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, 62).** Participants noted tariff effects "more apparent in the data, as indicated by recent increases in goods price inflation", with inflation expected to rise near term. Score 62.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED, 47).** Participants said "services price inflation had continued to slow". Slightly below stable. Score 47.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED, N/A).** A few participants noted weakening housing demand and falling house prices, but nobody discussed shelter or rent inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 55).** Participants noted longer-term expectations "continued to be well anchored", but several stressed that inflation above 2 percent for an extended period raised the risk of unanchoring, and various stressed monetary policy's role in preventing it. Anchored with a modest upside concern. Score 55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 47).** Almost all participants favored holding at 4.25-4.50 percent. A majority saw upside inflation risk as greater than downside employment risk, yet several saw the policy rate as possibly "not far above its neutral level" and a couple judged employment risk more salient. Two members (Bowman and Waller) preferred a 25bp cut. No participant argued for a hike. Hold with a slightly dovish tilt from the dissents. Score 47.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 50).** Two formal dissents, both for a 25bp cut, with explanations (inflation close to target excluding tariffs, rising downside employment risk). Participants also split on which risk is greater (a majority upside inflation, several balanced, a couple employment). Distinct camps with disagreement reaching the assessment, but a common direction among dissenters and an otherwise unified hold. Score 50.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 48).** A distinct financial-stability passage: several on elevated asset valuation pressures, a couple on bank interest-rate vulnerability, a few on Treasury market fragility, a couple on FX swaps, and many on stablecoins and the GENIUS Act. Several participants addressed it meaningfully, though it is not the dominant topic. Score 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 46).** Almost all participants said the Committee was "well positioned to respond in a timely way"; policy would follow incoming data and the balance of risks. Two-sided contingencies: a more restrictive stance if tariff inflation proves larger or more persistent, a less restrictive stance if the labor market weakens materially. The statement language on "additional adjustments" leans slightly toward easing. Score 46.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** Participants judged that "considerable uncertainty remained" about the timing, magnitude and persistence of tariff effects, and that outlook uncertainty "remained elevated", though several noted some reduction in policy uncertainty. Score 62.
