## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants do not discuss credit spreads or their level. The only related phrase is that "access to financing" would bolster consumer spending, which is not spread or credit-condition language. The quote the old analysis relied on is not in the participants' text, so the dimension is N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to 45).** Some members saw "an upside risk from the wealth effects of the large rise that had occurred in the value of stock market holdings". This is a spending channel, not a view that valuations are stretched, so it scores in the rubric's default band for price-move language without valuation commentary. Score 45, +-8, Low.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to 55).** Members cite "the prospective effects of the rise in interest rates and the dollar that had occurred since earlier in the year" as a restraint on second-half growth, so participants see a stronger dollar. The directive's "depreciated slightly over the intermeeting period" is the information summary, not a member view, and is not used. It is a single passing mention, so the score is modest. Score 55, +-8, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58).** Direction is lean hawkish: "all but one" of the members supported unchanged policy, they "preferred or could accept an asymmetric directive that was biased toward restraint", and the risks were "tilted toward higher inflation". A "differing view" (Stern) wanted immediate tightening. Others stressed the Committee "could afford to wait", which keeps it at a hold with a tightening bias. Score 58, +-5, High.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** There was one dissent on the policy vote (Stern, wanting a "modestly more restrictive policy") and two dissents on the money and debt ranges (Lindsey and Yellen, wanting "somewhat higher ranges"). A "differing view" on timing of tightening is stated, but the assessment of the economy is largely shared and the debate is about tactics. That fits moderate differences with a formal dissent. Score 40, +-6, High.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 8).** Participants make only passing references to financing access, higher interest rates and stock-market wealth, all as spending channels. There is no discussion of financial stability. Score 8, +-4, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** Members "observed that an asymmetric directive would represent a shift from the symmetric directives", implying "special vigilance" with a firming bias. Several stressed it "did not imply a commitment to tighten", and the Committee "would consult in some way before any policy tightening". That is a mild tightening bias with data dependence. Score 62, +-6, High.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** "Members viewed the outlook for inflation as a source of substantial uncertainty", whether wage pressures would emerge was "a critical issue", and M2 velocity remained "subject to considerable uncertainty". Against that, the modal forecast of slowing to potential was stated with fair confidence. Score 48, +-8, Moderate.
