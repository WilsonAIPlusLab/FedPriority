## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 28 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see credit as readily available and cheap, which is below normal stress. They gave no explicit statement on spread levels or direction. Key phrases: members cited "the ample availability of loans" and "the ready availability of equity and debt financing on favorable terms"; some emphasized "ample availability of financing to most borrowers on very attractive terms"; Jordan reported bankers saying "credit was cheap and plentiful". The level reading is below 50 (bright line met). The score sits closer to 30 than to the old 22 because no wording about spreads themselves is in the input, and confidence stays Moderate.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, 30).** Goods and commodity price pressure is seen as subdued by import, dollar and energy influences, not by any direct goods-price discussion. Key phrases: "the appreciation of the dollar, reduced commodity prices" would hold down price pressures; "the drop in world oil prices"; "reducing the prices of oil and other imported commodities". Some members warned these effects were "unlikely to be repeated". Score 30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only labor-cost and price remarks are general ("faster increases in compensation costs", "pressures on prices"). No participant refers to services prices, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** Participants lean hawkish but hold steady for now. "A majority of the members" preferred or could accept unchanged policy, retained the asymmetric directive, and judged "the risks clearly seemed to be tilted in the direction that action would become necessary at some point". "Many" thought the costs of delay were "small", and "a number" called it a close call. "Two members" (Jordan and Poole) strongly preferred tightening now, and "several" worried policy was "not positioned to restrain ebullient domestic demand". This fits hold-with-tightening-bias (51-60), at 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 48).** The vote was 10 to 2, with Jordan and Poole dissenting for immediate tightening. Their reasoning differs somewhat (Poole on money growth and demand exceeding potential; Jordan on asset prices and "too much cash chasing too few deals") but points the same way. The majority did not dispute the inflation risk, only the timing. "A number of members" found the decision a "close call", and "some" took the view that conditions were "very accommodative". Two formal dissents place it at the low end of notable disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 55).** Attention is moderate to substantial and direction-neutral. "Members took particular note of the effect of accommodative financial conditions" and "some members expressed concern that the widespread perceptions of reduced risk or complacency" were spreading to property, business ventures and land. "Even a modest tightening action could have outsized effects on the already very sensitive financial markets in Asia" was a stated reason for not acting. Jordan cited asset-price increases. Several participants address it, so the score is mid-range, not higher, because it is one theme among several in an economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68).** The signal is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias. Those voting for unchanged policy "supported the retention of a directive that was biased toward restraint". They felt "a policy tightening move, possibly in the near future, was a likely though not an inevitable prospect", and that if action was delayed it might need to be "prompt and perhaps vigorous". The rate stays unchanged with the policy bias unchanged, so this is not in the 76-90 band.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 63).** Uncertainty is high and is the stated basis for the wait-and-see majority. Key phrases: the net-export outlook "especially uncertain" and "subject to substantial uncertainty"; the "exact timing and extent" of the slowdown "were unknown"; "considerable questions remained about the relationship of labor market pressures to inflation"; earlier-pattern analyses "had proved consistently wrong". "A good deal of uncertainty" surrounded inventories. This is a genuine "don't know" posture by many participants, but there is no language of unprecedented or Knightian uncertainty, hence the low-to-mid 60s.
