## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 52).** Most participants expected output growth "close to growth of potential GDP", which the bright line puts at 48-52. Participants also cite "the continuing high level of resource utilization", while several suggest "sustainable rates of resource utilization could be slightly higher than typically estimated". Net: at to marginally above potential, 52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 55, Low).** The input has no direct statement on labor-market tightness. Several participants "noted significant increases in wages in their Districts, particularly in the service sector", offset by "overall gains in labor compensation had remained moderate", with "solid gains in employment". Slightly tight, 55, with Low confidence because it is inferred.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 57).** Participants see conditions as somewhat tighter, with "credit conditions had become tighter" and "tighter credit conditions for some households and businesses". The tightening is concentrated in riskier borrowers and subprime mortgages. For investment-grade businesses "little net change in the cost of credit", and mortgages "remained readily available to most potential borrowers". Somewhat tight, 57.

**TREASURY_STRESS, EQUITY_VALUATION, BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** None of these markets or institutions is discussed in the participants' views in the new input. The old evidence came from market-development text that is not part of this input. All three are now N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 54, Low).** Participants say "funding had become more costly and difficult to obtain for riskier corporate borrowers" and "lenders ... less willing to extend credit for financial restructuring". No spread level is given, so under the v4.1 rule this is direction only: widening, 54, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 52).** Members agreed that maintaining 5-1/4 percent was appropriate and that the risk that inflation "would fail to moderate" remained the predominant concern, a mild tightening bias. This is offset by "downside risks to growth had increased" and a "might require a policy response" if financial conditions worsen. Balanced to slightly hawkish, 52.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** All participants agreed on the policy stance, and the vote was ten for with no votes against. Near-consensus, 8.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** Several passages address mortgage-market illiquidity, "recent strains in financial markets", "volatile financial markets" and tighter credit. Members said "a further deterioration in financial conditions could not be ruled out". Financial conditions are a meaningful secondary theme alongside the economic assessment, not dominant, 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 54).** The statement keeps inflation as the "predominant policy concern", but says "future policy adjustments will depend on the outlook for both inflation and economic growth" and adds the increased downside risks. This is data-dependent with a slight tightening-bias tilt, 54.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** Housing "remained a key source of uncertainty about the outlook". Participants noted that "investors had become much more uncertain" about mortgage-backed securities and that "heightened asset market volatility and the associated increase in uncertainty" could lead businesses to pare capital spending. Conditions in corporate credit markets "could change rapidly". Elevated but not extreme, 50.
