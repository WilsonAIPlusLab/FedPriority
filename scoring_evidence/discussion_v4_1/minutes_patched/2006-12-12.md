## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 32 rested on spread language that is not part of the participants' views. Participants speak only of "favorable financial conditions" and the "ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions". They do not discuss spreads, so the dimension is N/A and the credit-availability signal sits in FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old evidence was a market-developments sentence, not a participant view. Participants mention "strong corporate balance sheets and profits" but not equity valuations.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 38 +-10 Low).** Participants "noted that the strength of global demand and the recent decline in the foreign exchange value of the dollar should help to support increases in U.S. exports". Direction is weakening (below 50, per the bright line). The size of the move is not stated, so the score is a modest-weakness reading with Low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 52 +-8 Low).** Participants "expressed concern that a failure of inflation to moderate as expected could entail significant costs if an upward drift in inflation expectations ensued". This is a risk statement. Nothing says expectations are currently contained or rising, so the score is slightly above neutral with Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55).** Nearly all members favored holding at 5-1/4 percent. All agreed that inflation failing to moderate "remained the predominant concern", and members agreed that "additional policy firming was possible". Several saw downside growth risks increase a little. One member wanted language allowing adjustment "in either direction", and Lacker dissented in favor of further tightening. This is a hold with a modest tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 30).** One formal dissent (Lacker, for tightening) and one member who objected to one-sided statement language. Views on the economy were broadly shared, and all members agreed inflation was the main concern. This fits the one-dissent, broad-agreement range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 20).** Several participants cited "accommodative financial conditions" for commercial real estate, and others cited favorable conditions supporting spending. These are brief supporting references. There is no stability or risk discussion beyond housing.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 58).** The approved statement text says "some inflation risks remain" and that "the extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed" depends on both inflation and growth. This leaves a tightening option open while conditioning it on data, and one member wanted the two-way wording. Mild tightening bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** "Considerable uncertainty regarding the ultimate extent of the housing market correction" and "considerable uncertainty as to the probable pace and extent of the moderation in core inflation". Indicators were "mixed", and productivity was an open question. The caveats are repeated but partly conditional or routine, so the score is mid-elevated.
