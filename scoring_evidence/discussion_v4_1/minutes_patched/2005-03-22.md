## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 44).** Participants see the economy as somewhat below full resource use but closing the gap. Key phrases: members judged that "a degree of economic slack apparently remained"; "meeting participants perceived that slack in labor markets was gradually diminishing"; the wording was said to presume "a gradual return to high levels of resource utilization". Capacity utilization is rising, but nothing in the participants' text says output is above potential. The old "above potential this year and next" line is not in the input. Slack language puts the score under 50: 44 +-6, Moderate.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old score of 20 rested on a statement about corporate bond risk spreads that does not appear in the participants' views. Participants mention only "unusually supportive financial conditions" and a rise in longer-term rates, nothing on spread levels, so the dimension is N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 35).** Weakening, not strengthening. Participant evidence: "Several participants noted that past declines in the foreign exchange value of the dollar were contributing to U.S. economic activity" (tourism, exports), and some executives cited "the lower foreign exchange value of the dollar putting upward pressure on import prices". Depreciation rule gives < 50; modest weakness, 35 +-8, Moderate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 63).** Moderately hawkish. All members favored a 25bp hike to 2-3/4 percent, judged policy "still quite accommodative", and saw pressures on inflation as having risen, but said an accelerated pace "did not appear necessary at this time"; "measured removal of policy accommodation was appropriate for now." Clear action plus a measured forward lean: 63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20).** Near-consensus with minor emphasis differences. Vote record: twelve votes for, "None" against; "All the members ultimately approved" the conditional risk-assessment language. A number of members thought formulaic language too rigid and some wanted the risk assessment dropped, and some expressed discomfort with the measured-pace wording, but these are wording reservations, not dissent: 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 15).** Brief mentions only. "unusually supportive financial conditions" is cited as context for investment, and "a few participants" cited speculative housing activity; nothing is developed into a financial-stability discussion: 15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 65).** Mild-to-moderate tightening bias. The approved statement says "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured" and the Committee "will respond to changes in economic prospects", while "some" worried the measured-pace language could constrain policy as "the odds that the Committee might need to step up the pace of policy firming were thought to have increased". Further hikes are signaled, conditionally and at a gradual pace: 65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** Elevated, centered on inflation. "many participants indicated that their uncertainty about the intensity of inflation pressures had risen", the distribution of inflation outcomes "tilted a little to the upside", and "considerable uncertainty persisted" about longer-run productivity. Other parts of the outlook are described with ordinary confidence (investment momentum, "nearly all participants thought that core and total inflation ... would be relatively low"): 52.
