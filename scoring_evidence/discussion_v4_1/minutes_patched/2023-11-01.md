## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55).** Participants lean mildly hawkish while holding. All participants judged it appropriate to maintain the 5 1/4-5 1/2 percent range. They said "further tightening of monetary policy would be appropriate if incoming information indicated that progress toward the Committee's inflation objective was insufficient." All participants judged that policy should "remain at a restrictive stance for some time." Most participants still saw upside inflation risks. Offsetting this, participants generally judged that risks had become "more two sided", and "proceed carefully" language was used. The result is a hold with a modest tightening bias, so 51-60 and about the middle of that band. No cut is discussed beyond a few participants noting that balance sheet runoff could continue "even after the Committee begins to reduce the target range."

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 10).** The vote record reads "Voting against this action: None", and the Board votes were also unanimous. "All participants" judged it appropriate to hold and to continue balance sheet reduction, and "all participants" agreed on a restrictive stance. Differences were limited to emphasis (some participants said District contacts reported weaker consumer demand, while several saw momentum persisting; most saw upside inflation risks and many stressed downside activity risks). That is near-consensus, so 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 62).** There is a dedicated financial stability paragraph. "Many participants" commented on unrealized losses, reliance on uninsured deposits and increased funding costs at banks, and "many" on a possible sharp decline in CRE valuations. "Several" raised cyber risks and the need for readiness to use Federal Reserve liquidity facilities, and "a few" raised Treasury market functioning and hedge fund leverage. Separately, participants spent a long passage on the "significant tightening in financial conditions" and the term-premium rise, and the statement repeats that tighter financial and credit conditions would weigh on activity. This is a prominent but not dominant topic that several to many participants address, so 62.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 58).** The signal is a mild, conditional tightening bias within a data-dependent posture. "All participants" agreed the Committee "was in a position to proceed carefully" with decisions based on "the totality of incoming information." Participants noted that "further tightening ... would be appropriate" if progress stalled, and all judged policy should stay restrictive "for some time until inflation is clearly moving down sustainably." The statement language, "in determining the extent of additional policy firming", is retained, while "would be prepared to adjust the stance ... if risks emerge" is symmetric. No explicit commitment to further hikes, so below 60-75 mid-band and above neutral: 58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** "Participants generally noted a high degree of uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook", with two-way risks listed (resilient spending, student loan repayments, a government shutdown, a Middle East conflict broadening). Participants called the persistence and source of the rise in long-term yields "uncertain", and the "extent of these effects remained uncertain." This is genuine rather than purely rhetorical, though not at crisis-level language ("uncharted" or "cannot bound"), and risks were described as "more two sided" with fairly clear policy reasoning. Score 62, lower end of the "High" band.
