## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Participants lean dovish on balance: the Committee kept purchases at $40 billion per month of MBS and $45 billion of Treasuries, and "all but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted." Offsetting this, "a number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting" and one preferred to begin decreasing purchases immediately, while another preferred more accommodation and a couple would respond further if inflation fell. "Most participants emphasized" being ready to adjust purchases up or down. Since the debate is about whether and when to slow QE and not about raising rates, the score sits in the moderate-dovish band, nudged up from the QE-era anchor by the taper-leaning group.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** One formal dissent: Esther George, who "continued to view monetary policy as overly accommodative" and preferred "to signal a near-term tapering." Several participants differed on timing of any slowing and on market expectations of the program's size, but the vote was 11 to 1 and the policy and statement language were agreed. This fits the typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 35).** Limited but real attention. "A few participants" expressed concern that conditions in certain markets were "becoming too buoyant," citing lower-credit-quality and covenant-lite issuance. One participant warned that imbalances could be hard for regulators to identify and that it "would be appropriate to adjust monetary policy to help guard against risks to financial stability." The one dissent was grounded in "imbalances and excessive risk-taking." Most participants did not take this up, so it stays in the limited-discussion band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 12).** The signal is strongly easing. The statement kept the rate threshold guidance (rates low "at least as long as the unemployment rate remains above 6 1/2 percent") and said a highly accommodative stance "will remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends." The new language that the Committee is "prepared to increase or reduce the pace" of purchases, and a number of participants' willingness to slow as early as June, add some hawkish tilt on the purchase pace, so the score is above the lowest threshold-guidance anchors.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Somewhat elevated. Participants said data were "mixed, raising some concern that the recovery might be slowing"; views on fiscal drag ranged "from an estimate of substantial fiscal drag to one of less restraint than previously expected"; one participant described the labor-force-participation trend as "subject to considerable uncertainty"; another saw fiscal and regulatory uncertainty as blocking transmission of asset purchases; and "views differed about what evidence would be necessary" to slow purchases. These are specific but come mostly from a few participants, so not in the high band.
