## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 32).** Participants see output below potential. Members spoke of "high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets" that would "diminish only gradually" and "a considerable amount of unused resources", with "subpar economic growth" as a risk. The old specifics (twenty-year-low capacity utilization) are not in the participants' text, so confidence drops to Moderate and the score moves slightly toward neutral. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 35).** Participants see a slack labor market. Reports on employment were "generally disappointing", labor-market excess capacity is high, and the business community shows "a persistently high degree of caution ... with regard to investment and hiring decisions". No unemployment rate or payroll figure appears in the participants' text, so the earlier 27 was over-precise; 35 with Moderate confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The earlier score rested entirely on a sentence about narrowing risk spreads that is not part of the participants' views. Members only mention "steps taken to address corporate governance and credit problems", which carries no spread or level information. Not discussed, so N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The earlier score rested on bank-lending text absent from the participants' views. Members mention financial institutions only hypothetically (disinflation's effect on "the functioning of financial institutions and markets"). Not specifically discussed, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 38).** Hold with a clear easing bias. All members could support unchanged policy, and they "acknowledged that a case could be made for easing policy immediately", but concluded easing was "not desirable at this time"; some members said an easing move "might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting". Not a majority favoring easing, so upper end of the dovish range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20).** Broad agreement. The vote record shows twelve votes for and "None" against. Reservations were minor: "some members cautioned" against prolonged inaction, "some concern" over the summary risk sentence, and "two members saw merit" in a balanced-risks wording.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Limited. Members cite the "marked strengthening of domestic financial markets" and "substantial gains in financial markets" as supports for growth, and note disinflation could harm "the functioning of financial institutions and markets". These are context rather than a focus of deliberation.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 28).** Easing-leaning, data dependent. Members adopted separate risk assessments: growth risks "about balanced" but "the probability of some disinflation ... exceeded that of a pickup in inflation", with the overall balance "tilted toward the downside over the foreseeable future". Easing was tied to the absence of "convincing indications of an appreciable pickup", so it is a lean rather than a commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 42).** Somewhat elevated. Members noted "the timing and vigor of a pickup ... remained uncertain" and that state and local budget outcomes were "not clear", and some cited "persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends". Offsetting this, the end of the Iraq war removed "key uncertainties" and the data ahead were expected to give "a clearer basis" for assessment.
