## Evidence for changed dimensions

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** The old score of 63 rested on text that is not participants' views (the Desk-style statement that the dollar "appreciated considerably against the yen and the euro"). That sentence is absent from the new input. The only participant content is a forward-looking risk: as global portfolios become "increasingly weighted toward dollar assets," returns would need to rise, with "much of the adjustment potentially occurring through a decline in the exchange rate," and, in the policy discussion, investors' willingness to keep acquiring dollar assets was "in question." No current appreciation or depreciation is described, so no bright-line side applies. The topic is raised, with an implied tilt toward possible weakening, so score 45 with Low confidence rather than N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Moderately to strongly hawkish. "All the members supported" a 25 bp rise to 5-3/4 percent; "a few members" preferred 50 bp; a "majority" preferred a "limited policy move" but the members "generally agreed that further tightening actions might well be needed." The gradual preference and the lack of a 50 bp majority hold it below the strong-hawkish band. Score 70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Broad agreement on policy. The policy vote had no dissents. Meyer's single dissent concerned the monetary-aggregate ranges ("All but one of the members favored" keeping the ranges), a secondary matter. The disagreement over 25 versus 50 bp was a tactical difference, and the 50 bp advocates "could accept" 25 bp. Score 30, at the upper end of broad agreement because of the formal dissent and the pace disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Limited attention. Participants discuss financial conditions mainly as a macro channel: stock-market wealth "lifted the growth of purchasing power and spending," asset values, long-term rates, and whether "financial conditions had adjusted sufficiently." Stability proper appears only as "recently somewhat unsettled conditions in financial markets." Score 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Clear tightening bias. The members agreed the statement should say that "even after their firming today the risks remained weighted mainly in the direction of rising inflation pressures," and that the Committee "saw inflation risks as persisting even after today's action." The words "might well be needed" are short of a commitment, and some members saw "considerable advantage" in a calibrated approach, so the score sits at the top of the mild band. Score 75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Elevated but not extreme. "The outlook for inflation remained subject to a marked degree of uncertainty"; stock prices "highly uncertain"; energy prices "very difficult to predict"; members cited "uncertainties" about demand versus supply and the response to earlier actions, and whether rates sufficed was "an open question." Against this, projections were concentrated (real GDP 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent) and the direction of risk was agreed. Score 55.
