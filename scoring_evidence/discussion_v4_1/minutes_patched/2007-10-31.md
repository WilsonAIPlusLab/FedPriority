## Evidence for changed dimensions

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The only Treasury-specific evidence in the old analysis was staff reporting on Treasury bill liquidity, which is not in the participants' views. Participants mention only "unusual pressures in funding markets persisted", which is not about the Treasury market. N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to 56 +-8, Low).** Level reading: "a number of participants" saw downside risk to nonresidential building from "elevated spreads on commercial-mortgage-backed securities" (level high, above 50), and nonconforming mortgage markets "remained disrupted". Against that, participants said "most businesses were apparently having little difficulty raising external funds" with strong investment-grade issuance, and credit availability "appeared to be ample, albeit on somewhat tighter terms" (near normal). Spread levels are stated for only one segment, so the score sits modestly above 50 with Low confidence. Level governs; no direction-only reading was used.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quotes on stock price moves and financial stocks come from staff text outside the participants' views. Participants do not address equity markets or valuations. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to 56 +-8, Low).** Participants "were somewhat less concerned that banks would not have sufficient balance-sheet capacity to absorb large volumes of assets" after improvement in commercial paper and leveraged loan markets, which implies earlier concern now easing. "A number of participants" cited "a further tightening of banks' lending standards for commercial real estate loans", and tighter lending standards across credit products were noted. Net: emerging but easing stress, no solvency or capital talk. Score slightly above 50; evidence thin and indirect, so Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 36).** The policy action is a 25bp cut, supported by "nearly all members" as "valuable additional insurance" given "substantial downside risks" (most members), with many members saying easing would support market functioning. Offsets: "many members" called it "a close call", Hoenig preferred no change and "slightly firm" policy, and the Committee judged risks "roughly balanced" after the action with some noting reversibility. Moderate dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 36).** Nine votes for, one against (Hoenig, who saw inflation risks as elevated and the funds rate as "close to neutral"). Many members called the decision a close call, but the discussion otherwise shows broad agreement on the outlook. One dissent with some disagreement on risk weighting fits the moderate-differences band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 66).** A full passage on market functioning ("strained", "fragile", "relapse in financial conditions late in the intermeeting period", "unusual pressures in funding markets persisted"), concern about adverse shocks denting confidence, and "many members" tying the cut to "support improvements in market functioning". Prominent topic but sharing space with housing, consumer and inflation discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 48).** The Committee's language is symmetric: "upside risks to inflation roughly balanced the downside risks to growth" and "will act as needed". "A number of members" noted the moves "could readily be reversed", and Hoenig said the Committee "could" ease further if data warranted. No explicit signal in either direction, slightly below neutral because the action was an easing with continued downside-risk language.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** "While the Committee saw uncertainty regarding the economic outlook as still elevated", markets "still fragile", downside risks "still seen as significant", and "heightened economic and financial uncertainty" for business sentiment. Elevated but not extreme, with participants projecting a gradual return to trend by 2009.
