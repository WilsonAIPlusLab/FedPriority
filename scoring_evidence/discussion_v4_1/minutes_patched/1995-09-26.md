## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 40 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see conditions as mildly supportive, below neutral. Members note housing demand responding to "more attractive mortgage rates", "increases ... in the value of financial assets" as a favorable factor for consumers, and the directive says market interest rates "have fallen somewhat". No participant describes financing availability or terms, so the earlier "very accommodative" 33 with High confidence is not supported. Mild easing across a few indicators gives 40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant mentions spreads, credit terms or availability. The only financing remarks are about mortgage rates and possible "adverse repercussions in financial markets" if deficit reduction falls short, neither giving a level or direction for spreads. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The banking system is not discussed by participants anywhere in the input. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 42 +-9, Low).** The dollar is seen as modestly weaker. Evidence: the directive states the trade-weighted dollar "has declined over the intermeeting period", and members cite "lagged effects of earlier declines in the value of the dollar" as supporting trade improvement. Little direct member commentary, so Low confidence; below 50 by the bright-line rule.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Members discuss consumer and producer prices in aggregate and labor-cost/pass-through issues; no goods-specific price discussion appears. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Concern is that "significant further progress toward achieving stable prices might not be made", which is about realized inflation, not expectations. No member discusses the anchoring of expectations. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 44).** All members supported an unchanged policy stance. "Several members" thought policy "might have to be eased eventually" given downside risks and a slightly restrictive stance, while "other members" stressed little progress on inflation and thought easing premature. Hold with a mild easing tilt among a minority, offset by caution: lean dovish to balanced, 44.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20).** Vote record: "Votes against this action: None"; "all the members" endorsed the policy and directive. Differences are in emphasis (eventual easing vs. premature easing; "some members" unsure of direction). A few members voiced strong reservations on one provision of the Senate bill, a side topic. Broad agreement, 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 8).** Financial markets appear only in passing: asset-value gains supporting spending and "adverse repercussions in financial markets" if fiscal measures fall short. No stability discussion; typical 1990s level, 8.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 46).** Members unanimously chose a directive "that did not include a presumption about the likely direction of any adjustments"; "no specific reason to anticipate" a change in the weeks ahead. "Several" saw eventual easing, but "some" said "the direction of the next policy move was not clear". Essentially neutral, slight easing lean from the eventual-easing remark, 46.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** "Many members" saw downside risks to growth; the outlook is "subject to a variety of uncertainties, including the still unsettled course of the federal budget"; "a number of members" called fiscal policy "a major uncertainty"; the outlook "remained clouded". Housing response and inventory projections flagged as highly uncertain. Elevated but specific rather than extreme, 48.
