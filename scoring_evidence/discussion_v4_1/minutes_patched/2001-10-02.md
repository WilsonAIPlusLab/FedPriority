## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED).** Participants see activity below potential in a downturn, but characterize the baseline forecast as "a relatively mild and short contraction followed by a gradual recovery", with "currently high levels of excess capacity in many industries" and "greater and longer-lasting slack in labor and product markets". Below 50 by bright line, but not a deep-recession reading. Score 35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED).** Members cite "a persisting stream of worker layoffs and rising unemployment", "prospective weakness in employment", and slack in labor markets. No unemployment level appears in the participants' text. Below neutral; score kept at 37 with wider uncertainty and Moderate confidence.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** The only related text is "ongoing technological improvements" as a reason for a later capital-spending upturn. Members do not assess productivity growth, so N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED).** Several members (in the policy discussion) said "the decline in stock market prices and the widening of risk spreads had damped the stimulative financial effects of the Committee's earlier easing actions"; the statement on the real funds rate is policy, not conditions. Somewhat tight, offsetting part of the easing. Score 60.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** No participant discusses Treasury market functioning in the new input; the old settlement and liquidity-infrastructure evidence came from text that is not part of the participants' views.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The only participant reference is "the widening of risk spreads", direction only with no level. Under the v4.1 rule: 45-55 on the side of the move, Low confidence. Score 54.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** Members cite "a sharp drop, at least initially, in stock prices", "cumulative declines in stock market prices" with adverse wealth effects, and a risk that "prices in equity markets might continue to decline and perhaps even overadjust to lower earnings expectations". Declined and a concern, but no extreme undervaluation or fire-sale language. Score 60.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** Members refer to "continued firmness in the dollar" which, with weaker foreign economies, was "depressing the outlook for U.S. exports". Strong-dollar reading (> 50), brief and indirect. Score 58, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** All members endorsed a 50bp cut; "decidedly downside risks" and "further vigorous easing action" to support confidence. Tempering factors: the funds rate would "not reflect an unusually accommodative policy stance" and policy "could be reversed in a timely manner". Strong dovish. Score 22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Unanimity: "all the members endorsed" the 50bp cut and "all the members supported" retaining the weakness-risk sentence; vote record shows ten for and none against. Near-consensus. Score 7.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Several mentions of equity declines, wealth effects, wider risk spreads, weak business-loan demand, and "a sound banking system" appear as outlook context rather than a focus of deliberation. Limited discussion. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** All members supported keeping the sentence that "risks continue to be weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness", an easing-bias signal; the flexibility to reverse policy later tempers it. Mild-to-moderate easing bias. Score 22.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Members saw their forecast as "subject to an unusually wide range of uncertainty, notably in the direction of a potentially much weaker outcome"; "there were no historical precedents" for the effects on confidence; "heightened anxiety and uncertainty". High uncertainty, genuine rather than rhetorical. Score 75.
