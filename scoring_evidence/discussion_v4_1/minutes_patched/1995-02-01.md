## Evidence for changed dimensions

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 38).** Participants see the dollar as weaker than before but not collapsing. The new text has members citing "the lower value of the dollar" as a support for the external balance, and a "potential further decline in the dollar" and a dollar that "already appeared to be vulnerable to further weakness" (a number of members). The old claims that the dollar was near post-WWII lows and sharply depreciating do not appear in the input; the sharp depreciation in the directive refers to the Mexican peso, and the directive says the dollar "declined somewhat". Under the magnitude rule, "somewhat" weakness plus vulnerability is 5-15 points below 50. Score 38 (below 50, bright line met).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 65).** Participants lean toward tightening. "all the members indicated that they could support some firming"; "most of the members were convinced" that policy should be "adjusted promptly to a more clearly restrictive stance" because the economy was at or beyond sustainable capacity. A few preferred a short delay but said they would vote to tighten; the action paired with a 1/2 point discount rate rise. The forward lean is symmetric, which holds the score below the 70s. Score 65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** Disagreement is moderate. The policy vote was unanimous with no votes against, though "a few" preferred delay and "opinions differed to some degree" on the extent of slowing and the inflation outlook. Messrs. Lindsey and Melzer formally dissented, but on the Mexico swap line and ESF warehousing, not on the policy stance or the economic assessment. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Limited attention. Members commented on financial conditions, stock prices, narrow quality spreads and "accommodative lending policies", and "some members expressed concern" that banks had eased standards unduly; the Mexico rescue was justified partly on financial stability grounds, though two dissenters said the Committee lacked information to judge whether Mexico threatened U.S. financial stability. This is context rather than the focus. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 48).** Roughly neutral. The members were "unanimously in favor of adopting a symmetric directive" and "did not believe that there should be a presumption toward possible further tightening"; "a number of members" thought further restraint might not be needed if the expansion moderated. Offsetting this, the Committee's "most difficult decision" would be when further tightening was no longer desirable, and most saw inflation risk as principal. Score 48.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** Moderately elevated. Members said projections were "subject to substantial risks"; "a key uncertainty" was whether slowing would relieve resource pressures; the fiscal outcome was "very uncertain" with "a wide range of error"; inventory timing was "impossible to predict" and consumer slowing was "uncertain" in timing and extent. Most of this is conventional caveating around a firm view that inflation risk was rising. Score 48.
