## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 56).** One participant said credit risk spreads were "still above pre-recession norms" but "may have been boosted by safe-haven demands for Treasury securities". The stated level is above normal, so the score must be above 50; the safe-haven attribution, and participants' general view that domestic credit markets were "more favorable than a year ago", keep the elevation modest. Only a single participant spoke to spreads, so confidence stays Low. The old 50 gave no weight to the stated above-normal level.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 27).** Participants discussed tools for additional accommodation, including a new large-scale asset purchase program that many expected "could provide additional support". The vote record says "many members judged that additional monetary accommodation would likely be warranted fairly soon", and several preferred to await more information. The hawkish side is small: one member said more accommodation "would likely not be effective" and its costs "unacceptably high", and a few worried about inflation expectations and the forward-guidance wording. Many but not a majority favoring easing soon, in a QE-debate context, gives a moderate dovish score of 27.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** Lacker formally dissented, saying exceptionally low rates were not likely warranted for the stated length of time. One further member opposed additional accommodation, and there was disagreement over the extent of slack ("a number" saw substantial slack, "several others" saw a lower potential path) and over a reserve-rate cut ("a couple" in favor, "several" opposed). The statement was otherwise agreed, so this is a typical one-dissent meeting with moderate differences on assessment and tools.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** Participants gave a distinct paragraph to euro-area sovereign and banking strains as "a significant downside risk", to credit-market conditions and to the resilience from stronger institutions. "A few" raised direct financial-stability risks from prolonged accommodation or new purchases, and "some" were concerned about market functioning. That is moderate attention, with several participants addressing it, but it is not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 8).** "Many members expressed support for extending the Committee's forward guidance", with the decision deferred to September. The statement keeps rates exceptionally low "at least through late 2014" and says it "will provide additional accommodation as needed". A few participants asked for conditional or removed calendar guidance, and Lacker dissented on the horizon. These pull toward a less dovish figure, but the overall forward signal is strongly easing, date-based guidance with extension favored.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 65).** The vote record says "members generally attached an unusually high level of uncertainty to their assessments of the economic outlook", and "many participants" said a high level of uncertainty about euro-area spillovers and U.S. fiscal and regulatory policy was holding back spending. Lacker added that "significant uncertainty" made the rate path difficult to forecast. Broad and explicit uncertainty without Knightian language gives a high score of 65.
