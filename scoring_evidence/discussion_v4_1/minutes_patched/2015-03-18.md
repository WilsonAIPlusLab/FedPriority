## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (54)**: Participants lean modestly hawkish on the forward path while holding the current stance. "Almost all participants favored removing" the "patient" language, "several participants judged" the data likely warranted beginning normalization at the June meeting, and the vote record has members seeing the new language as flexibility "to begin raising the target range ... in June or at a subsequent meeting". Offsetting this, "others" expected liftoff only later in the year, "a couple" thought 2016, "some" stressed policy would stay "highly accommodative", and the current action was a hold at 0 to 1/4 percent. Hold with a mild tightening bias gives 54.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (25)**: Broad agreement. The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None"). One participant "did not favor the change to the forward guidance" because it "could lead to a tightening of financial conditions", and participants "expressed a range of views" on liftoff timing (several June, others later, a couple 2016) and on slack ("many" vs "several"), but these are differences of emphasis and timing rather than camps with different prescriptions. Zero dissents and a single stated reservation gives 25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (22)**: Brief, context-only references. "Almost all participants noted potential risks ... resulting from foreign economic and financial developments", Greece's "fiscal and financial problems", explanations of inflation compensation via "risk or liquidity premiums", and one participant's concern about tightening of financial conditions. No sustained discussion of financial stability; the vote record's "accommodative financial conditions" is a policy-mechanism remark. Limited discussion gives 22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (56)**: Mild tightening bias. The guidance drops "patient" and says the Committee "anticipates that it will be appropriate to raise the target range ... when it has seen further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will move back to its 2 percent objective", and an April increase "remained unlikely". Tempering this, the change "does not indicate that the Committee has decided on the timing", the approach is explicitly meeting-by-meeting and data dependent, rates may stay "below levels the Committee views as normal" for some time, and "several" expected a "fairly gradual pace of normalization". A shift to normalization-ready but non-committal guidance gives 56.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (33)**: Somewhat elevated but mostly standard caveats. "Almost all participants noted potential risks" from abroad, and participants offered "various explanations" for the oil/inflation-compensation correlation, with one cautioning it was "important not to dismiss" a pessimistic interpretation. Views differed on liftoff timing and slack ("no simple criteria"), and the vote record cites "uncertainties attending the outlook for inflation". Yet "most saw the risks ... as nearly balanced" and projections were framed as likely outcomes. Somewhat elevated gives 33.
