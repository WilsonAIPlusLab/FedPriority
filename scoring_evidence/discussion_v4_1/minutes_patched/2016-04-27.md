## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The only statement is "credit spreads declined somewhat" over the intermeeting period. This is direction only. Participants give no information on the level of spreads, and the old analysis' "elevated post-January levels" is not in the participants' text. Under the V4.1 level-governs rule, the score stays within 45-55 on the side of the move (narrowing, so just below 50), with Low confidence. "A number" also cited "favorable credit conditions for household and business borrowers", which is consistent with a slightly sub-50 reading. Score 47 +-8, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Policy was held at 1/4 to 1/2 percent with a modest tightening bias. "Most participants" judged that a June increase would likely be appropriate if growth picked up, labor markets strengthened and inflation progressed. "A few participants" judged an increase appropriate now, and "two" cited policy rules implying much higher rates. Against that, "a number" wanted to "proceed cautiously" given inflation below 2 percent, and "some" saw limited costs to patience. The vote record says "members generally agreed" it would be "prudent to wait". Net: hold with a tightening bias, 54.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote was 9-1; Ms. George dissented because a 25bp increase was appropriate, citing diminished downside risks and policy-rule prescriptions. Beyond the formal dissent, the differences are mainly on timing and tactics (a few for a hike now, some wanting to see data first, a couple worried about credibility) and on whether risks were "roughly balanced" ("several") or still tilted down ("many"). "Participants expressed a range of views" on June. That is a moderate-difference, one-dissent meeting, 33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants gave a full paragraph to the significant improvement in financial conditions and its role in the outlook. A further paragraph covered vulnerabilities: "several noted the ongoing need to remain alert", "a few cited concerns about rapidly rising prices of CRE" and mutual fund illiquidity, and Puerto Rico. "Some participants" noted "potential risks to financial stability" of waiting too long, and two warned that overly accommodative policy "could also induce imprudent risk-taking". Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not dominant, 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Participants "generally" saw a hold "as leaving open the possibility of an increase" in June, and "most" said a June hike would "likely" be appropriate if data cooperated. "Some" were concerned that market pricing of a June increase "might be unduly low" and stressed communication. Against this, the Committee text expects "only gradual increases", rates below longer-run levels "for some time", and a path dependent on incoming data. Mild tightening bias with data dependence, 57.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants acknowledged "uncertainty about the reasons for the slowdown in productivity growth", a range of outcomes under weak productivity, and a trend that is "quite difficult to identify in only a few quarters". "Several" worried the data might not give clear signals by mid-June. "Many" kept downside risks in view (Brexit referendum, China's exchange rate). "Most" nevertheless held a stable medium-term outlook, so the uncertainty is elevated but not unusual, 44.

**Note on unchanged judgment calls.** EQUITY_VALUATION (44) sits one point under the rubric's 45-55 default for price moves without valuation commentary, and its evidence leans on CRE concerns rather than equities. BANKING_STRESS (40, Low) rests on credit-availability and resilience language rather than direct bank commentary. No new text bears on either, so both were left unchanged.
