## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 52).** The old evidence cited participants who said conditions "had tightened significantly" and others who pointed to equity gains and "easing in bank lending standards"; those phrases are not in the participants' views. What the text does say is mixed. Market interest rates and volatility rose, and "several participants worried that higher mortgage rates and bond yields could slow the recovery in the housing market and restrain business expansion." Against that, "corporate credit spreads had not widened substantially," the stock market "had posted further gains," "some others" thought adverse effects on financial conditions "appeared to be limited," and "a broad index of financial stress remained below average." The balance is slightly on the tighter side of neutral because of the rate rise, with no tight or accommodative wording to trigger a bright-line rule. Score 52, Low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 48).** The only statement is that "corporate credit spreads had not widened substantially." This gives direction (little or no widening) and no level, so the v4.1 level rule applies: score 45-55 with Low confidence. The old reading of spreads as "relatively compressed" is a level claim the text does not make. A broad financial-stress index "below average" tilts slightly under 50. Score 48.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 32).** Moderate dovish. "All members but one" judged that the outlook "warranted the continuation of the Committee's current highly accommodative stance" with purchases continuing at $85 billion a month, and "many members" said further labor-market improvement was needed before slowing purchases. A sizeable minority leaned hawkish: "several members judged that a reduction in asset purchases would likely soon be warranted," and "most participants" wanted the Chairman to describe a conditional path for purchases. The one dovish-side voice, a member worried that inflation downside risks had increased (Bullard dissented on this ground), offsets part of that. Because the debate is about the pace of QE, the score stays in the moderate-dovish band, nudged up from the QE-era 15-25 range by the taper signal. Score 32.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 55).** Two formal dissents, in opposite directions and with different reasoning: Bullard wanted a stronger commitment to defend 2 percent inflation, and George wanted a deliberate statement that purchases would be cut "in the very near future" and cited "financial imbalances." Beyond the votes, views split on timing and communication of tapering ("some" vs "many" vs "several"), but most economic assessments were shared. This fits the "notable disagreement, 1-2 formal dissents" band. Score 55.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** "A number of participants offered views on risks to financial stability": "a couple" on institutions' exposure to rising rates, "two others" on money market fund resilience, "a few" on excessive risk-taking from prolonged low rates, and "one participant" on calibrating these risks for policy. A further paragraph deals with the rise in rates, volatility and credit spreads, and George's dissent cites "financial imbalances"; "two" of the members wanting earlier tapering raised the program's "potential negative consequences." This is a moderate-discussion case, not a dominant theme. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 15).** The forward signal is overwhelmingly easing on the funds rate: the 6.5 percent unemployment threshold was retained, and "in general, members continued to anticipate that maintaining the current exceptionally low level of the federal funds rate was likely to remain appropriate for a considerable period after asset purchases are concluded." Offsetting that, "most participants" wanted the Chairman to lay out a likely conditional path for slowing purchases, which is the taper signal. The score sits at the top of the strong-easing band, near the taper anchors. Score 15.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 32).** Mostly ordinary caveats. "Some participants noted that they remained uncertain about the projected pickup in growth," because earlier forecasts of a sustained pickup "had not been realized"; "the magnitude and timing" of fiscal effects "remained unclear"; and businesses cited regulatory and fiscal uncertainty. Many also saw downside risks as having diminished, and the communication discussion aimed at reducing market uncertainty. Somewhat above normal, not unusual. Score 32.
