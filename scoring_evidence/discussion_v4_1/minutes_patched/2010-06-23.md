## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants described spreads as moving wider: "risk spreads for many corporate borrowers had widened noticeably" (participants' views, Financial markets paragraph). This describes direction only. The text gives no level of spreads, and it adds that "more-creditworthy business borrowers were still able to obtain funding in the open markets on fairly attractive terms", which argues against reading it as a high level. The old 58 with High confidence treated the widening as an elevated level, so it is re-scored under the v4.1 direction-only rule to 54, Low confidence. This is a clear widening, so it sits at the top of the 45-55 band.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Policy lean is to hold at the zero bound with an easing bias. All members agreed to maintain 0 to 1/4 percent, and "nearly all" judged it appropriate to reiterate the extended-period expectation. The vote record adds that "the Committee would need to consider whether further policy stimulus might become appropriate if the outlook were to worsen appreciably", while members also noted work on exit instruments. One member (Hoenig) wanted that language dropped and an earlier modest start to raising rates, a minority hawkish pull. No QE debate occurs. Score 41, Moderate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** There was a single formal dissent. Mr. Hoenig objected to "exceptionally low levels ... for an extended period" because it would limit flexibility to "begin raising rates modestly in a timely fashion". The disagreement concerns guidance wording and risk management rather than the economic assessment, and the other nine voted in favor. This fits the typical one-dissent anchor. Score 30, High.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants gave a full paragraph to financial conditions: European strains, wider risk spreads, falling equities, a stronger dollar, "ongoing difficulties in financing commercial real estate", and the state of bank credit. Several participants addressed these, and the vote record calls for a rewording of the statement for "the shift to less accommodative financial conditions". Hoenig raised "future financial imbalances" as well. This is a moderate rather than dominant share of the discussion. The appended May 9 conference-call text on swap lines was treated only as marginal context. Score 50, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Guidance is a firm low-for-long signal. "Nearly all members judged that it was appropriate to reiterate the expectation" of "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period", with a stated readiness to consider more stimulus if the outlook worsened. It is not date or threshold based, so it is not in the strongest band. The one member who would drop the language is a minority. Score 20, High.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not extreme. The minutes cite "several uncertainties, including those related to legislative changes and to developments in global financial markets" generating "a heightened level of caution". About half judged growth risks tilted to the downside, a number saw inflation risks tilted down, and "a few" cited deflation risk. Most still saw inflation risks as balanced and the recovery as continuing. Score 45, Moderate.
