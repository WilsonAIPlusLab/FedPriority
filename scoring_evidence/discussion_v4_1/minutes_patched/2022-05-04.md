## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants did not address rents, shelter or OER. The only housing remarks are rising mortgage rates and, from a couple of participants, "robust housing demand and elevated home prices despite higher mortgage interest rates". That is housing demand and house prices, not housing inflation. The topic was not discussed, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 76).** The direction is clearly hawkish. All participants agreed on a 50bp hike and anticipated that "ongoing increases" were warranted. Most judged that 50bp increases "would likely be appropriate at the next couple of meetings". All supported balance sheet reduction from June 1, and many favored "expediting the removal of policy accommodation". The Committee is aggressive and near-unanimous, but the language stops short of "act forcefully", and some stressed risk management and data dependence. That puts it in the strong hawkish band, below the 80+ range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** The direction is near-consensus. The vote record lists nine votes in favor and no dissents. The text says "all participants" concurred on the economic assessment, the 50bp move and the runoff plans. Only minor nuances appear: a number noted price pressures may "no longer be worsening", and several noted the dual-mandate challenge. Score 8.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 30).** Attention is limited. "Several participants who commented on issues related to financial stability" tied tightening to Treasury market liquidity and private intermediation capacity. "A couple" flagged commodity-market and CCP margin risks, and "several" noted potential unanticipated effects of runoff on financial market conditions. These are context items, not a focus of the discussion. Score 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 82).** The forward signal is clearly tightening. The policy discussion and the statement both anticipate "ongoing increases in the target range". Participants agreed to "expeditiously move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture", and noted that "a restrictive stance of policy may well become appropriate". Data dependence is stated, but the direction is unambiguous. Score 82.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 65).** Uncertainty is high. "Participants agreed that the economic outlook was highly uncertain". The war's implications were "highly uncertain", the timing and magnitude of supply easing were "uncertain", and "uncertainty about real activity was also seen as elevated". Risk-management considerations were said to be important. The language is genuine rather than rhetorical, but participants still voiced conviction about the direction of policy and inflation. Score 65.
