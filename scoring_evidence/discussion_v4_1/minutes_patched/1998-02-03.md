## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 46 +-8, Low).** Participants mention equities only as a source of wealth and confidence: consumer sentiment reflects "the very large cumulative increase in stock market wealth" and a "more ebullient consumer sector" was not ruled out given "the high level of stock market prices"; the policy discussion lists "rising equity prices" as a sign of stimulative conditions. No participant is reported as calling prices rich, stretched or a bubble, or as warning of a correction. Because this is price and wealth commentary without valuation commentary, the rubric default of 45-55 applies; score 46 (slight nod to the "high level" wording), Low confidence. The prior 38 relied on a "puzzling rise" remark that is not in the input.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 53 +-5, Moderate).** Participants unanimously favored an unchanged stance with a symmetric directive, so the current action is neutral. The forward lean is slightly toward tightening: "a number of members expressed the view that the next policy move was likely to be a tightening action," against "one member" who "saw a greater probability of an easing action." The Committee judged "the greater risk would be to make a preemptive policy move." Mild hawkish tilt in a hold: 53.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12 +-5, High).** Near-consensus. All twelve voting members supported the directive, "Votes against this action: None," and "all the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" and agreed on a symmetric directive. The only differences were that members "differed to some extent in their forecasts of major trends in the economy and in the risks of alternative outcomes," plus the lone member leaning toward easing. Score 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 27 +-8, Moderate).** Limited attention, as context rather than a focus. Items: financial conditions "quite stimulative" (lower rates, rising equities, ready credit, rapid money and credit growth), consumer debt burdens "large, [but] manageable," "emerging signs of speculative overbuilding in some areas, especially of commercial structures," and difficulties Asian importers had "in securing financing." No participant-level stability or systemic-risk discussion. Score 27.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 52 +-6, Moderate).** The signal is essentially neutral and data-dependent: all members favored a symmetric directive because "the uncertainties were sufficiently great to warrant remaining sensitive to the need for a policy change in either direction," and needed adjustments "could be made on a timely basis once the balance of underlying forces became more evident." The slight tightening tilt comes from "a number of members" expecting the next move to be a tightening, offset by one member expecting an easing. Score 52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 72 +-6, High).** Uncertainty is stressed repeatedly and by the members collectively. "The members acknowledged that their forecasts were subject to a great deal of uncertainty since there was little precedent" for the Asian turmoil; "the risks of a considerable deviation on the upside or the downside ... were unusually high"; in the policy discussion "the range of possible outcomes was unusually wide," the direction policy would need to move "was unclear," and the Asian drag was "a source of major uncertainty." This is genuine, not rhetorical, hedging. Not pushed into the 76+ range because the near-term outlook was described as very good and policy was clearly a hold. Score 72.
