## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 50 +-8, Low).** Participants speak of the "still-restrictive monetary policy stance", which is policy, not conditions, so it cannot carry a tight reading. The only conditions language is two-sided risk language: an upside inflation risk from "a larger-than-anticipated easing in financial conditions" and a downside risk from "sharply worsening financing conditions". No participant calls conditions tight or accommodative, so neutral at 50 with Low confidence. The old claim that "supportive financial conditions" were noted is not in the text.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED, 45 +-8, Low).** "Several participants noted that leverage in the market for Treasury securities remained a risk" and that the market's resilience should be monitored. This is a monitoring concern, not dysfunction (no liquidity, auction or settlement problems), so normal functioning with a watch item: 45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The minutes contain no statement about the level or the direction of credit spreads. Private credit growth, CRE office risks, card and auto delinquencies and "sharply worsening financing conditions" (a hypothetical downside risk) are not spread readings. The old 47 inferred a level from absent stress language, so it is withdrawn.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 38).** Direction is moderately dovish. "All participants viewed it as appropriate to lower the target range ... by 25 basis points", and "many participants" thought uncertainty about the neutral rate made it "appropriate to reduce policy restraint gradually". Offsetting: "some participants" would pause if inflation stayed elevated, and risks were "roughly in balance". Easing action with a gradual, two-sided path gives 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows "Voting against this action: None", and all participants supported the cut. The only differences are conditional: "some" would pause if inflation stayed elevated and "some" would accelerate if the labor market turned down. No camps, no reservations about the decision. Score 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** A dedicated section, but one of moderate weight. "Many participants" discussed CRE office exposures, "several" Treasury market leverage, "some" cyber and third-party risks, "a few" private credit, and "a couple" the banking system, asset valuations, household strains and MMF rules. It is addressed meaningfully by several participants but is not the focus of the discussion: 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 35).** Easing lean with data dependence. If data come in as expected "it would likely be appropriate to move gradually toward a more neutral stance of policy", but decisions "were not on a preset course", and the Committee dropped its September confidence language. Some also raised a possible pause. Score 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Somewhat elevated, mostly standard. The vote record says "The economic outlook is uncertain", with risks "roughly in balance" and "remained confident" on inflation. Added: "many" cite neutral-rate uncertainty and volatile data, "some" describe labor-market assessment as subject to "considerable uncertainty", and "a couple" the durability of productivity. Score 40.
