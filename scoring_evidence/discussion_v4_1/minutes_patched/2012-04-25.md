## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN — 30.** Participants and members lean dovish: the Committee agreed "to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance," with a date-based low-rate path to late 2014 and the maturity extension program continuing. "Several members indicated that additional monetary policy accommodation could be necessary" if the recovery lost momentum, and "a few members" cited compensating for the zero-bound period as a reason for a longer low-rate stretch. A hawkish minority ("some participants" worried accommodation "could erode the stability of inflation expectations"; one participant flagged costs of additional balance sheet actions; Lacker dissented) keeps it from the strong-dovish band. No new easing was advocated, so it sits at the top of the maintain-maximum-accommodation range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY — 35.** One formal dissent: Mr. Lacker "did not believe that economic conditions were likely to warrant exceptionally low levels... through late 2014" and saw a hike as likely necessary "by mid-2013." Otherwise "almost all of the members" agreed on keeping the guidance; "another member" wanted guidance more responsive to data. Disagreement on the economics was present but mild (some participants skeptical of slack-based inflation models; "some participants" saw upside inflation risk; a range of views on structural unemployment), and the rules discussion showed "a few" skeptics. This is a typical one-dissent meeting with modest economic disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS — 40.** Participants gave financial conditions a full paragraph: domestic credit markets "continued to improve," "many" institutions bolstering capital and reducing European exposures, "a few" saw very low rates inducing "imprudent risks," and euro-area sovereign and banking concerns "intensified." Global financial strains were repeatedly named a significant downside risk. Moderate attention (several participants address it), but the meeting's focus remained the economy, communications and policy rules.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE — 8.** The forward signal is strongly easing: "exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014" was retained by "almost all of the members," the guidance was described as conditional, and "several members" said more accommodation could be needed if the outlook worsened. Only a minority (Lacker, and a discussion of a possible threshold-based revision by "another member") pointed toward earlier tightening or a changed guidance. Consistent with the date-based commitment anchor for 2012-13.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED — 42.** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated rather than unusual: the guidance was kept "particularly given the uncertainty surrounding economic forecasts"; "some members" said the mild winter made the underlying trend "harder to discern"; "several participants" said fiscal-policy uncertainty could lead businesses to defer hiring; Europe and fiscal-cliff risks were "significant"/"sizable." Offsetting this, "most participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlook as being roughly balanced" and the outlook was "little changed."
