## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 46).** Participants address equities as a demand channel, not as a valuation concern: the faster rise in productivity had bolstered demand "through its effects on equity prices", "wealth effects from sharp earlier increases in the value of stock market holdings would wane in the absence of a new upsurge", and a "less buoyant stock market" would weigh on sentiment "should the latter persist". Several members make these points but without any statement of over- or undervaluation, so the rubric default of 45-55 applies; 46 with Low confidence (a mild lean to elevated earlier gains, no explicit valuation language).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 36).** Members (a clear group) cite "the dollar's recent depreciation" and "the dollar's recent depreciation and the strengthening of foreign economies" as drivers of a "significant further pickup in exports". Participants thus see the dollar as having weakened; bright-line rule requires < 50. The magnitude is not quantified and the directive says the trade-weighted dollar "changed little", so a modest-weakness score of 36 with Moderate confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only expectations language in the participants' views concerns growth ("expectations of slower growth") and forecasts of inflation that "had failed to materialize". Nothing in the new input has participants characterising the public's or markets' inflation expectations, and the old quotation is not in the new input. Topic not discussed, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 63).** Direction: moderately hawkish. "All the members supported raising the Committee's target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points", citing "tightening resource constraints indicating unsustainable growth" and the "risks of waiting ... unacceptably high". Forward lean is mixed: "a number of members" were "quite uncertain" about further tightening, while "other members" thought "additional firming ... probably would be necessary". A unanimous hike with a split forward view and a symmetric directive sits in the low 60s.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18).** Unanimous: "Votes against this action: None." The minutes say "views differed to an extent on the outlook for inflation and policy going forward" and "to some degree regarding the subsequent outlook for policy", with two groups (a number of members uncertain on further action; other members expecting more firming), but no dissent and agreement on the action. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences: 18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Financial topics appear as context rather than focus: a December action "could add to the potential financial uncertainties and unsettlement surrounding the century date change", a retained tightening bias "could well ... have an unsettling effect on financial markets", and "the special situation in financial markets over the year-end". Stock-market wealth effects also recur. Limited discussion, used mainly to explain timing: 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 48).** The Committee moved to a symmetric directive, "consistent with the Committee's current expectation that no further policy move was likely to be considered before the Committee's meeting in February"; keeping the tightening bias "could well send a misleading signal". Some members stressed that further action "should depend on incoming information", others that "additional firming ... probably would be necessary". Net neutral, data dependent, slightly below 50 for dropping the tightening bias: 48.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** "The members acknowledged that their forecasts were subject to a substantial degree of uncertainty"; housing outlook "subject to considerable uncertainty"; members "recognized that forecasts of rising inflation had failed to materialize, raising questions about their understanding" of the inflation process; "a number of members" were "quite uncertain" about further tightening. Several explicit statements, but partly standard caveats and the baseline view (robust growth, tight labor markets) is held with conviction: 52, elevated but not high.
