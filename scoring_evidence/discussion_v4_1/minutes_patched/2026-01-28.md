## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 42).** Participants see financial conditions as somewhat loose. "Most participants expected growth to be supported by continued favorable financial conditions", and "several participants commented on high asset valuations and historically low credit spreads". The old 50 was anchored on the policy rate being "within the range of estimates of the neutral level", which is a statement about policy, not conditions. Favorable or supportive conditions require < 50, and a score of 42 fits "generally supportive" (31-45) with modest magnitude. It is Moderate confidence because "favorable" is stated indirectly, as a growth driver. This also removes the inconsistency with CREDIT_SPREADS (20).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 53).** The policy is a hold with a roughly balanced lean. "Almost all participants supported maintaining" the range, "a couple" preferred a cut, and "several" expected further cuts if inflation declines. On the other side, "some" favored holding "for some time", a "number of these" saw no easing until disinflation is "firmly back on track", and "several" would have supported a two-sided description. The "vast majority" saw downside employment risks moderating while the persistence risk "remained". The slight hawkish tilt comes from the risk balance and the two-sided-guidance wish. Score 53, Moderate because the camps pull in opposite directions.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 45).** The vote record shows two formal dissents, "Stephen I. Miran and Christopher J. Waller preferred to lower the target range", against ten votes for the hold. The dissenters judged policy "still meaningfully restrictive" and weighted labor risk above inflation persistence, while "several" cautioned that easing would signal reduced commitment. The disagreement is mostly about risk weighting and extends only partly to the assessment, since most participants agree on the data. Two dissents with the rest broadly aligned gives 45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** A full paragraph is devoted to the topic: "several participants commented on high asset valuations and historically low credit spreads", "some" on AI-sector vulnerabilities, "several" on private credit and "several" on hedge-fund leverage, "a few" on opaque AI financing and global bond/FX spillovers, and "a couple" on household credit. It is a moderate, multi-topic discussion rather than a dominant theme beside the economic assessment, so 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 46).** The signal is data-dependent and close to neutral, with a mild easing lean. "All participants agreed that monetary policy was not on a preset course", and the statement keeps "extent and timing of additional adjustments" language. "Several" said further downward adjustments "would likely be appropriate if inflation were to decline", "some" favored holding for some time, and "several" would have supported two-sided wording (a tightening possibility). Net is slightly below 50.
