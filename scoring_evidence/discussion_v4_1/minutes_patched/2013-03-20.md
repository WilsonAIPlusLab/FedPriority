## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED).** Participants see conditions as accommodative, below the midpoint. Most participants saw asset purchases as "having a meaningful effect in easing financial conditions"; several said monetary policy "reduced the cost of financing purchases and improved credit availability to some degree"; many noted investors "taking on additional risk" to boost returns. The old quotes about C&I terms and "eased further" are not in the new input, so confidence drops to Moderate. Score 40, below 50 (bright-line satisfied).

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** No participant states a spread level. The indirect level signal is that many saw investors taking "credit risk or interest rate risk" to boost returns, and one dissenter (George) cited "a mispricing of risk". This suggests somewhat below-normal spreads, but only inferentially. The old covenant-lite and junk-issuance evidence is not in the new input. Score 42 with Low confidence, below 50 as the level reading requires.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Clearly dovish. "All but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted", and purchases of $40B MBS and $45B Treasuries per month continued. The hawkish pull is limited to how soon to taper: one member would slow immediately, a few would reduce around midyear, several would slow later in the year, and two would continue through year-end. Score 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (Ms. George, who wanted purchases wound down), and the distribution of views on the end date was wide ("a few" wanted a close soon, "a few" saw continuation to late in the year). Disagreement was mainly over the costs, benefits and timing of purchases rather than the economic assessment. Score 36.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Prominent. "Participants discussed their assessments of risks to financial stability"; "many participants" noted reach for risk; "a number of participants remained concerned about the potential for financial stability risks to build"; participants "generally agreed" on an ongoing need to evaluate interactions with monetary policy. A large share of the asset-purchase section is about these risks, but the economic assessment still carries the document. Score 63.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The committee retained "the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates", and the statement expects a highly accommodative stance "for a considerable time" after purchases end (a strong easing commitment). Against that, "many participants" expected a slowing of purchases "beginning at some point over the next several meetings", and some noted purchases could instead be increased. Net strong easing bias with a taper signal: score 12.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Moderately elevated. Participants "recognized that estimates of the economic effects were necessarily imprecise and covered a wide range"; they were "not yet confident" that labor progress would be sustained; one called the housing outlook "quite uncertain". Offsetting this, "a few participants" said contacts saw uncertainty as having declined. Score 38.
