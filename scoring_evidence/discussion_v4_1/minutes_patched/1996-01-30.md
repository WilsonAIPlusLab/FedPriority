## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED).** Participants see goods-price pressure as roughly balanced. Some upward pressure is cited in food ("increased pressures on food prices stemming from disappointing harvests ... and relatively low grain supplies"), offset by strong competition that was "preventing many businesses from passing on rising costs". The old support (quiescent food prices, subdued finished-goods PPI) is not in the participants' text. Score 50, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Moderately dovish. The members "supported a proposal calling for some slight easing in reserve conditions", judged the real funds rate "still a bit to the firm side" (a number of members), and saw "only a very limited risk" of fueling inflation; all members backed the slight reduction in reserve pressure. A few wanted a more pronounced move, while some noted that postponing could be defended. Easing is slight and cautious, so 36.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The policy directive passed unanimously ("all the members supported"), but Mr. Lindsey and Ms. Yellen formally dissented on the M2/M3 range vote (preferring "somewhat higher ranges"), and members divided over explicit inflation goals ("Some members" in favor, "Other members" with reservations, "many" skeptical). Differences are mostly about tactics and communication rather than the economic assessment. Score 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Financial conditions appear only as context: "favorable conditions in financial markets", "still readily available credit", "increased consumer debt burdens", and lenders "no longer easing their terms". No participant treats financial stability as a topic. Score 10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Neutral. Members "favored an unbiased directive that did not incorporate a presumption about the likely direction of any adjustments", and "did not believe that a further policy move was likely to be needed during the intermeeting period"; the Committee did not rule out an intermeeting change. Score 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Somewhat elevated but mostly standard caveats. Many observed that risks "did not seem balanced"; "the outlook for fiscal policy was uncertain"; "substantial uncertainty still surrounded any projections of monetary expansion"; a few cited "uncertainties that surrounded their forecasts". Arguments for waiting cite "this uncertain economic climate". Score 40.
