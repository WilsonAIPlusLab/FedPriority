## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants lean mildly hawkish: hold steady with a tightening bias. "All the members" endorsed an unchanged degree of reserve pressure and said it would be "prudent to pause and assess developments". "Most members" preferred an asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint, citing "the recent rise in inflation and the risk of an unexpected impulse". "A few members" thought tightening "might well be needed sooner rather than later". "A few members" preferred a symmetric directive, but they "could accept" the asymmetric one. The current action is a hold and the tilt is hawkish, so I score 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Disagreement is minimal. The vote record lists 11 votes for and "None" against. "All the members indicated that they preferred or could support" the directive. Differences were limited to the directive's tilt (a few preferred symmetric) and "opinions differed somewhat" on the extent of the slowdown and the prognosis for inflation. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences, so I score 18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Attention to financial stability is brief. Passing references cover "favorable recent developments in financial markets", accommodative bank lending policies, and the rise in bond and stock prices that "strengthened household balance sheets". Nothing is framed as a stability concern, which fits the 1993-96 anchor of 1-10 and brief mentions. I score 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The forward signal is a mild tightening bias. "Most members" favored an asymmetric directive that "would provide a clear signal of the Committee's intention to resist higher inflation". "Additional monetary tightening" was seen as "needed at some point to contain inflation". The signal is tempered because the current stance is a pause and "a few members" would accept only a symmetric tilt, so I score 64.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Uncertainty is elevated. The members agreed that "the extent of the slowdown was not yet clear". It was "difficult to determine whether" weak consumer spending was "a temporary pause or a more prolonged pullback". The timing and extent of the inventory correction "could not be anticipated with any precision". "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for the external sector". The pause was justified "given the substantial uncertainties that were involved". These are several explicit statements, but the economy is still described as keeping "considerable forward momentum", so I score 52.
