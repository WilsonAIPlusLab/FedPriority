## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 34 +-6, Moderate).** Participants see activity below potential with slack persisting. Members cited "persisting slack in resource utilization" and "continued low levels of capital and labor utilization"; some members saw "continued growth below potential as the primary risk"; the expansion "had displayed signs of faltering". Many nonetheless thought "conditions were in place for a strengthening expansion", so this is ordinary slack rather than deep contraction. Bright-line (< 50) satisfied.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 32 +-8, Low).** The labor market is described as weak but only in passing: consumer spending was held back by "weak labor markets", and labor utilization was "low". No unemployment rate or payroll figure appears in the participants' text, so confidence is Low and the score sits in the below-full-employment band.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 42 +-8, Low).** No spread level or direction is given. The only relevant language is that "accommodative financial and bank lending markets" were among the supports for a strengthening expansion; read as favorable credit availability, slightly below normal. Low confidence.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 54 +-8, Low).** Several members tied war uncertainty to "lower equity prices", and many expected a favorable outcome "should generate a positive response in equity markets". This is price-move commentary without valuation commentary, so the 45-55 default applies, set at the upper end.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants do not mention the dollar or exchange rates in the NEW input.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 42).** High oil prices were "likely to have more of an effect in damping demand than in raising inflation, given well-entrenched expectations of low inflation"; expectations are anchored, with low inflation, hence a little below 50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 45).** The Committee unanimously held at 1-1/4 percent. Policy was "positioned to accommodate a strengthening economic performance", and some members saw growth below potential as the primary near-term risk; some also cautioned the Committee "might well need to adjust its policy" (direction unspecified). Hold with a mild easing tilt.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** "The members unanimously supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"; vote record shows 12 in favor and "None" against. Differences are only of emphasis (many upbeat on the rebound, some focused on below-potential growth).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 15).** Financial conditions appear only as brief context (accommodative financial and bank lending markets; lower equity prices from war worries; possible equity rally). No stability-focused discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 46).** Members agreed on "a steady policy stance, a high degree of vigilance, and a readiness to respond promptly"; the Committee omitted its usual balance-of-risks statement and agreed "heightened surveillance" would be useful. Neutral and data-dependent, with slight accommodative tilt from the stance "positioned to accommodate" a pickup.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 82).** Members opened with "an unusually high degree of uncertainty" making assessment "very difficult"; most members held that it was "impossible to assign reasonable probabilities to plausible alternative economic outcomes" and called these "considerable uncertainties".
