## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 50 relied on a staff sentence about "relatively strong productivity performance". That sentence is not in the new input, and participants do not discuss productivity. N/A for the value, UNC and CONF.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to 55, Low).** The old "already elevated" level came from staff text. Participants say "increasing concerns about financial institutions had contributed to a widening of some risk spreads" and note "a recent rise in corporate bond yields across most risk categories". This describes direction only, with no stated level, so the score goes to the top of the direction-only band: 55, Low confidence. Coherence with FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (72) is looser than before, but the level-governs rule prevents inferring a high level from a widening.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to 54, Low).** Participants mention only that "broad equity price indexes had declined" and that "lower equity prices" reduced household wealth. The latter is a wealth-effect point, not a valuation or concern view, so the score defaults to the middle of the 45-55 band, slightly above 50, with Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old 56 rested on a staff description of the trade-weighted dollar. Participants do not mention the dollar or exchange rates in the new input.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 56).** Nearly all members judged that holding the federal funds rate at 2 percent was appropriate. Members "generally anticipated that the next policy move would likely be a tightening", and "a number of participants worried" core inflation might fail to moderate unless policy tightened sooner than markets expected. Fisher voted for a hike. Nearly all participants also saw continuing downside risks to growth. That is a hold with a tightening bias, so the lean lands in the lean-hawkish band at 56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 36).** The vote record shows one dissent: Fisher "favored an increase in the target federal funds rate". Ten members voted for the action. The minutes show differences on whether financial-system risks had receded ("many participants" versus "several other participants") and on whether inflation risks had diminished ("some" versus "others"). The one dissent plus these differences fit the moderate-differences band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 65).** "Many participants referred to the adverse financial sector developments" such as the GSEs, tighter bank standards and capital pressures. They "noted that the financial system remained fragile", and some expressed concern about an adverse feedback loop with housing. Financial conditions are the "primary source of continuing downside risks to growth". Prominent but not the sole focus, as inflation takes equal space, so 65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** "Members generally anticipated that the next policy move would likely be a tightening", although "the timing and extent... would depend on evolving economic and financial developments". The statement says the Committee "will act as needed" and calls the upside inflation risks "of significant concern". This is a mild tightening bias with data dependence and no commitment, so 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 64).** The statement says "the inflation outlook remains highly uncertain". Participants disagree on whether financial-system risks had receded and on whether upside inflation risks had grown or diminished. They stress possible adverse feedback loops. Uncertainty is clearly elevated but mostly expressed as risk balance and disagreement, not as unprecedented uncertainty, so 64 with Moderate confidence.
