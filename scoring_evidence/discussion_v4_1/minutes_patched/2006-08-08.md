## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 54 +-10, Low).** The old analysis rested on a 4.8% unemployment rate and business contacts reporting shortages of labor; neither is in the participants' views. The only labor language is indirect: federal receipts reflecting "continued strong growth in labor and non-labor income" and revisions that marked up "labor compensation". This points to a labor market at or slightly above balance, inferred from limited language, hence Low confidence.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 45 +-10, Low).** Several participants noted the NIPA revision suggested potential growth "likely was lower than previously believed", and unit labor costs were marked up on "downward revisions to labor productivity". Direction: modestly below the prior view of trend. The old offsetting evidence that some saw potential growth as "solid" is not in the input.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 50 +-8, Low).** The old "still-supportive financial conditions" quote is not in the input, so the supportive bright-line no longer applies. Remaining evidence is mixed: "continued increases in ... borrowing costs appeared to have restrained consumer spending" and lagged effects of past rate increases (somewhat tight), against Lacker's note that real short-term rates "were still low relative to rates typically associated with sustained expansions" and "strong business balance sheets" (supportive). Net neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The sole old evidence (spreads "about unchanged") is not in the input and participants do not discuss spreads. N/A with no level or direction information (the level rule cannot be applied).

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old evidence ("stock price indexes rose modestly") is not in the input; participants do not discuss equities.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED, 62).** Direction: shelter inflation is seen as elevated and a substantial contributor to core inflation this year. Key phrase: "the recent acceleration in shelter costs, which contributed substantially to the increase in core inflation this year, could prove short-lived." Score reflects the clearly above-normal current reading, tempered by the view it may fade.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 56).** "Nearly all members" favored holding at 5-1/4 percent, but "many members" called it "a close call" and said "additional firming could well be needed"; "all members" wanted the statement to say a pause did not necessarily end tightening; Lacker dissented for a hike. Offsetting this, most expected inflation pressures to ease and a pause was seen as limiting the risk of over-tightening. Lean hawkish hold.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 32).** One formal dissent (Lacker, wanting further tightening because core inflation "had persisted and appeared to be broad-based") against ten votes in favor. Many saw a close call, but there was broad agreement on the assessment and the statement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 10).** Only brief context references: household "financial condition ... appeared to remain solid", "strong business balance sheets". No participants discuss financial stability or market conditions as a topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** Mild tightening bias: the Committee judged "some inflation risks remain" and that "the extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed" depends on incoming information; all members agreed the pause "did not necessarily mark the end of the tightening cycle". Data-dependent, so not higher.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** Participants expressed "considerable uncertainty regarding prospects for the housing sector", said by how much housing wealth would restrain consumption "was uncertain", and that whether the rise in core inflation was transitory or persistent "remained unclear"; upside inflation risks "significant". Otherwise standard caveats; uncertainty is concentrated in specific areas, so elevated-moderate.
