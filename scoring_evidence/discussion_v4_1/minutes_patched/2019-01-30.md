## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 54 +-7, Low).** Participants noted "wider corporate risk spreads" (as part of materially tighter conditions) and a couple said "the rise in credit spreads over recent months, if it were to persist, could restrain future economic activity." These describe direction (widening) only, with no statement of whether spread levels are high or low versus normal, so the score sits just above 50 on the side of the move, with Low confidence, rather than the old 58 from a "widened therefore >50" reading.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 45 +-6, High).** Participants are balanced to slightly dovish. "All participants" favored holding at 2 1/4 to 2 1/2 percent; they supported replacing "some further gradual increases" with being "patient"; holding "posed few risks"; "several participants" judged that risks of higher-than-expected inflation had diminished and "some risks to the downside had increased." Offsetting, "several other participants" would view a hike later this year as appropriate if the economy evolves as expected, and "many" said it was not yet clear which adjustment would be appropriate. Score 45: hold with a removed tightening bias, but not an easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 17 +-5, High).** Vote record: "Voting against this action: None." Participants unanimously favored the hold ("all participants expressed the view"). Differences are limited to emphasis: whether hikes later this year hinge on inflation (several of "many") versus the economy evolving as expected (several others), and "a few" concerned that SEP projections mislead versus "some other participants" valuing them. Broad agreement, minor emphasis differences: 17.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45 +-8, Moderate).** A distinct paragraph: "among those participants who commented on financial stability, a number expressed concerns about the elevated financial market volatility and the apparent decline in investors' willingness to bear risk," with "a couple" on year-end liquidity and "a few" on bank capital. Tighter financial conditions and the flat yield curve ("several participants") are also cited as reasons for patience. Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the dominant topic: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 42 +-6, High).** Participants "supported a proposed change in the forward guidance language" replacing "some further gradual increases" with the Committee "would be patient as it determines what future adjustments ... may be appropriate," and supported removing the "roughly balanced" risk characterization. "Many participants" said that if uncertainty abated the Committee might reassess "patient" language. A patient, data-dependent posture, shifted away from tightening bias, but with no easing commitment and "several" still expecting a later hike: 42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58 +-6, High).** "Participants observed that since then, the economic outlook had become more uncertain"; concerns tied to global growth, trade policy, Brexit and the shutdown; the Committee, "in light of the range of uncertainties," dropped the balance-of-risks judgment; "a few" said that in "the current environment of increased uncertainty" SEP rate projections do not convey the policy outlook. Multiple participants stress elevated uncertainty, but no deep or Knightian language: 58.
