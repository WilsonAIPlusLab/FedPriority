## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED).** Participants see labor markets as slack, but the new input offers only the general statement that "the economy probably would continue to operate with significant margins of slack in both labor and product markets" (members). The payroll and unemployment-rate evidence was staff text. Score stays 30, with a wider band (+-7) and Low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Members noted "some decline in risk spreads from very high levels" alongside sizable new bond issuance. Spreads have come down somewhat but are still described as coming from a very high level, so the level reading puts the score modestly above 50. Score 56, with a Moderate rating from a single explicit passage.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** Members say only that "stock prices, though recently declining somewhat, were still well above the lows of early October" and that equity issuance had edged up. This is price direction without a valuation view, so the score defaults to the neutral band: 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** The new input contains no participant discussion of the dollar or exchange rates. The old score rested on a staff statement. Now N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** "All the members endorsed a proposal to retain the current stance of policy," judging policy "well positioned to support a strengthening economic expansion," while seeing "little risk of any significant increase in inflationary pressures." A hold at an accommodative setting with balanced risks, no explicit easing bias. Score 48.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus: all members supported the hold and the balanced-risks statement; the vote record shows twelve votes for and "Votes against this action: None." Only minor emphasis differences (one member's benign-deflation view; some on fiscal discipline). Score 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** The improvement in financial markets is called "an additional positive element to the economic outlook," with discussion of risk spreads, bond and equity issuance, corporate governance concerns and household credit quality. Meaningful attention but subordinate to the real-economy discussion. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** All members supported retaining the balanced-risks sentence, "with some commenting that recent developments had established a firmer basis" for it; the Committee would "continue to assess emerging economic and financial developments, retaining the flexibility to adjust." No directional signal. Score 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Members said "uncertainties about the outlook remained substantial" with geopolitical downside risks, and "some uncertainty surrounded the prospects for consumer spending", fiscal initiatives were "subject to substantial uncertainty." Tempered by balanced risks and a firmer expected strengthening. Elevated but not extreme: 54.
