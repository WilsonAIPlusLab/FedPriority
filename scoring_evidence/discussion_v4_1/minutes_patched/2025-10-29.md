## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 53).** Participants see spending near to slightly above trend. Participants generally noted consumer spending "had shown signs of firming"; many highlighted "strong investment in technology, particularly spending related to AI and data centers". Offsets: many noted a divergence with lower-income households showing "increased price sensitivity", a couple cited continued housing weakness, and a few cited agricultural headwinds. Net 53, Moderate confidence.

**PRODUCTIVITY (FILLED, 57).** Participants see productivity prospects as modestly above trend, mostly prospective. Some suggested AI/technology investments "could boost productivity and thus aggregate supply"; a few said "potential recent productivity gains achieved through automation and AI" may support margins; some tied weak hiring to AI-driven productivity growth. Score 57, Moderate confidence (hedged wording, few-to-some participants).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 43).** Some participants "remarked that financial conditions were supportive of economic activity", and some pointed to "supportive financial conditions" as a sign policy was not clearly restrictive. "Supportive" requires a score below 50; the old 53 contradicted this. Offset: some participants thought the policy stance was still restrictive, and that concerns policy rather than conditions. Score 43 because only some participants say it.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants never describe credit spread levels or moves. Concerns about private-credit loan quality and "stretched asset valuations" are not statements about spreads. N/A (topic not discussed by participants).

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 50).** The only banking-related participant content is a number of participants citing nonbank credit failures and "banks' exposure to the sector" with possible transmission to the real economy. No participant calls the banking system sound or stressed. Mixed signals, 50, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 42).** Lean dovish to balanced. Many favored a cut, some could support either, several opposed; the Committee cut 25bp "toward a more neutral setting", with one voter wanting 50bp. Most judged further downward adjustments "would likely be appropriate" over time, but many preferred holding through year-end and most warned further cuts could risk entrenching inflation. Score 42.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 58).** Vote record: ten for, two against, Miran (50bp) and Schmid (no change), opposed from opposite sides. Participants had "strongly differing views" about December and differed on whether policy was still restrictive and on inflation (several say near target ex-tariffs; many say little sign of sustainable return). Two formal dissents plus assessment-level disagreement: 58.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 40).** One discrete financial-stability paragraph: a number of participants on nonbank/private-credit failures and its transmission channels, some on stretched valuations and a possible disorderly equity fall, a few on regulatory perimeter, a couple on corporate borrowing. Moderate attention, not the focus of the meeting: 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 43).** Participants "agreed that monetary policy was not on a preset course" and the statement says the Committee will "carefully assess incoming data". Most expect further cuts over time, several say December could well be appropriate, but many see holding for the rest of the year. Slight easing lean with data dependence: 43.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 57).** "Participants generally judged that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated"; various participants stressed the shutdown's effect on data availability; several expressed uncertainty about the timing and magnitude of tariff price effects; many called the growth/jobs divergence a "particularly challenging environment". Elevated but not extreme: 57.
