## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 87 to 76).** Participants describe a high level of spreads: "rising risk spreads kept the cost of issuing corporate bonds at a high level—especially for lower-rated firms", with securitization markets "largely dysfunctional" and banks cutting credit lines. Under the level rule this is above 50. The old 87 relied on the CDS-spread sentence, which is not part of the participants' views, and participants also note improvement in commercial paper and interbank markets and sharply lower Treasury yields. No participant describes credit rationing or a lending freeze. Score 76, wide band, Moderate confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Strongly dovish. All participants and members endorsed a target range of 0 to 1/4 percent, a large asset-purchase program (up to $100 billion GSE debt and $500 billion agency MBS), "all available tools", and readiness to expand purchases and evaluate Treasury purchases. Members "agreed" that "maintaining that level ... implied a substantial further reduction in the target". Emergency easing with QE launch. Score 4.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows "Votes against this action: None." Differences were tactical: "several members" favored no explicit funds-rate target, "other members" feared confusion, "some" backed reserve or base targets while "several other participants" doubted their effect, and "most participants" preferred coordination by cooperation. No disagreement on the economic assessment or the direction of policy. Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants devote much of the discussion to financial conditions. "financial strains continued to exert a powerful drag" and the "adverse feedback loop" had intensified; "financial markets generally remained under severe stress"; securitization "largely dysfunctional". The policy discussion centers on liquidity facilities, market functioning, and costs of very low rates for "certain financial markets and some financial institutions". It rivals the economic assessment in emphasis but does not exceed it. Score 76.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Strong easing bias. "Participants judged that communicating the Committee's expectation that short-term interest rates were likely to stay exceptionally low for some time could be useful"; members agreed the statement should say weak conditions "were likely to warrant exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for some time", conditioned on the outlook, with large purchases and balance-sheet expansion signaled. There is no date or threshold, so it is not at the strongest-commitment end. Score 8.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** High. "Meeting participants generally agreed that the uncertainty surrounding the outlook was considerable"; downside risks were "a serious concern"; some saw "the distinct possibility of a prolonged contraction"; participants "were uncertain about the extent to which inflation would fall" and about the size of asset-purchase effects. Matches the fall-2008 crisis anchor, tempered because a slow recovery in late 2009 was still the modal view. Score 74.
