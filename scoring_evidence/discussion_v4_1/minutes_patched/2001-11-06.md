## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 30).** Participants see output below potential and contracting, but describe the downturn as "a fairly brief and limited decrease in economic activity", with "considerable slack in labor markets" and "diminished pressures on labor and other resources". Bright-line (<50) holds. The old extreme 16 depended on staff data not in the participants' views. Score 30, Moderate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 35).** Mixed. Most business-side language is very weak: investment "declining at an even faster rate", capital goods orders down sharply, firms cutting "excess capacity". Consumers are described as having "held up reasonably well", with a motor vehicle surge that was temporary, services spending weak, and housing "still at a relatively elevated level" with slippage. Net well below trend but not collapsing. Score 35, High.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 56).** Participants stress that easing had been only partly passed through ("a variety of factors had limited the passthrough ... into long-term rates, equity prices, bank lending rates, and the foreign exchange value of the dollar") and that "risk aversion" was weighing on spending, but low mortgage rates were an "important support". Modestly tight relative to policy easing; the stronger old reading is not supported. Score 56, Moderate. (The three members' remark that "policy was already accommodative" concerns the policy stance, not market conditions.)

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' text contains no statement on credit spreads or their level, so there is nothing to score under the level rule; the earlier 58 came from non-participant text.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 55).** Only passing references to the "decline in stock market wealth" as a drag on consumers and to limited equity-price passthrough; no valuation view. Placed at the top of the 45-55 band, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant comment on bank health appears; "bank lending rates" is only a passthrough channel.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The dollar appears only as a channel through which easing passthrough was limited; no direction of movement is stated by participants.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss the inflation outlook ("benign and possibly to drift lower") but not inflation expectations as such.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20).** Strongly dovish: "all the members" supported a 50 basis point cut to 2 percent, "most members" favored it citing "absence of evidence that the economy was beginning to stabilize", and the risks were judged "clearly tilted toward further weakness". Three favored 25bp but accepted 50bp, which tempers the score slightly. Score 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 28).** Unanimous vote with "None" against. A number of members called 25 vs 50bp a "close call" and three favored a smaller move but accepted the larger, a mild expressed reservation. Broad agreement. Score 28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 20).** Financial conditions appear only as brief context (passthrough to long rates, equities and bank lending rates; low mortgage rates; stock wealth; risk aversion; markets possibly building in expectations of more easing). No focus on stability. Score 20.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 30).** An easing-bias signal that is explicitly data dependent: "all the members" could vote to retain the statement that risks were tilted to weakness, yet "several stressed" this was no commitment to ease at the next meeting and further easing "would depend entirely on the nature of future economic and financial developments". A tightening need "would be a highly welcome development". Score 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 68).** Many cite considerable uncertainty: "marked degree of uncertainty" about fiscal legislation, the war on terrorism and foreign weakness; risks "strongly tilted to the downside"; "substantial uncertainty about the course of the economy and the appropriate stance of policy"; recovery timing "remained subject to question". Participants still hold a base case of a brief downturn and 2002 upturn, so not extreme. Score 68.
