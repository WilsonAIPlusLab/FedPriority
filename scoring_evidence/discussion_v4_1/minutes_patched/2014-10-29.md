## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (46).** Participants lean slightly dovish to balanced, consistent with a pre-liftoff hold. The Committee agreed to conclude asset purchases and to keep the 0 to 1/4 percent range, with "all but one member" agreeing to keep the "considerable time" expectation. Pulling in different directions, "some participants preferred to eliminate" the "considerable time" language (a hawkish-leaning, data-dependence concern), while "a couple of others" thought guidance implied too early a liftoff, and the lone dissenter wanted continued purchases and stronger guidance. Ending QE is the completion of an already-announced taper and not a tightening signal in itself. Score 46: balanced with a mild easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (33).** One formal dissent, by Mr. Kocherlakota, who "should commit to maintaining the current target range ... at least until projected inflation one to two years ahead has returned to 2 percent and should continue the asset purchase program." Beyond that, "some participants" vs. "other participants" and "a couple" on each side differed on the "considerable time" wording and the "below normal" language, and "a few" worried inflation might stay low for quite some time. These are tactical and communications differences within broad agreement on the assessment, and the vote was 9-1. Score 33, at the one-dissent anchor, with High confidence because the vote record is explicit.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (46).** "Many participants commented on the turbulence in financial markets that occurred in mid-October"; "some" stressed that conditions "remained highly accommodative" and more turbulence was likely; "a couple" proposed collecting wholesale-funding data. The vote record shows members weighing whether to reference financial developments in the statement (decided against). The strategy-statement section adds that "a number of participants noted that financial stability is a necessary condition" for the goals. It is a distinct paragraph with several participants engaged, but not a dominant topic. Score 46.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30).** The signal remains easing-leaning: "all but one member" agreed to reiterate that the range would likely be maintained "for a considerable time" after asset purchases end, and to keep the language that policy may stay "below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run." Added statement wording makes timing of liftoff two-sided and data dependent (sooner if progress is faster, later if slower), and "some" wanted "considerable time" dropped, which tempers the easing bias. No date or threshold commitment. Score 30, easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (32).** "Most viewed the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced," so the baseline is normal caveats. Somewhat elevated by "a number of participants" noting slower growth if foreign conditions deteriorated, the mid-October turbulence, participants expressing "different views about how to interpret" the fall in market-based inflation compensation, and the "inherent uncertainty of the economic and financial outlook" for post-liftoff guidance. Proposals to state heightened uncertainty were judged too pessimistic. Score 32, just above the normal band.
