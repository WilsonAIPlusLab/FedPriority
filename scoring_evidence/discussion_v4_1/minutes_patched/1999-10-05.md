## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 62).** Participants see the labor market as tight, though the new text carries less direct language than the old analysis quoted. Members cited "tight labor markets and rising labor costs" (said to have confounded earlier inflation forecasts), expected "further anticipated growth in employment and incomes", and saw "upward pressures on labor costs" as a clear risk. The directive records unemployment at 4.2 percent. A level of 4.2% falls in the 53-65 band, so the old 70 is too high. Score 62.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 50).** No participant gives a valuation view. The only remarks are the "steadying of stock market prices" and that "the pause in the stock market, should it persist" would damp consumer spending with a lag. Price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55. Score 50, Low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 48).** Members do not describe the level of expectations directly. The only on-point language is that "inflation and inflation expectations were not likely to worsen substantially in the near term", alongside most members seeing some rise in core inflation as a "definite possibility". This points to roughly anchored expectations. Score 48, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** All members favored or could accept unchanged policy; a majority wanted a directive "biased toward restraint" and expected to need "a less accommodative policy stance in the relatively near future, possibly at the November meeting". Other members were "quite uncertain about the timing" and preferred to stay open but accepted the tilt. Hold with a tightening bias: lean hawkish. Score 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18).** The vote was ten to none with no dissents. The differences were minor: a majority favored the tightening tilt while others preferred more openness, and "most of the members" accepted a "not fully satisfactory" announcement practice. Broad agreement. Score 18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 18).** Financial conditions are mentioned mainly as context: "somewhat less accommodative conditions in financial markets", wealth effects of the stock market pause, and the view that announcements "seemed to have added to volatility in financial markets". No discussion of financial stability risks as a focus. Score 18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 65).** The majority viewed an asymmetric directive as conveying that "policy adjustments might not yet be completed for the balance of this year"; members agreed Y2K would not preclude tightening in November "should such an action seem warranted". The directive states that developments are "more likely to warrant an increase than a decrease". A moderate tightening bias, with some members keeping the path open. Score 65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** Many members stressed uncertainty across several areas: "a great deal of uncertainty surrounded the behavior of productivity growth", "considerable uncertainty surrounded expectations of somewhat higher core inflation", "substantial uncertainty surrounded the outlook for aggregate supply and aggregate demand", and Y2K inventory effects "of uncertain magnitude". Offset by the shared view that the risks of waiting were "small". Score 62.
