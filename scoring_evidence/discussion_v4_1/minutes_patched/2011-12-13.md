## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Participants lean dovish, though the decision was to hold. Almost all members supported maintaining the existing stance (continuing the maturity extension program, 0 to 1/4 percent, mid-2013 guidance). "A number of members indicated that current and prospective economic conditions could well warrant additional policy accommodation," and one member (Evans) dissented to undertake it, suggesting additional asset purchases. Only "a few others" judged that maintaining the current accommodation beyond the near term would be inappropriate, and some participants worried about erosion of inflation expectations. The ongoing balance sheet program is in the QE-era band. Score 30, between the strong-dovish and balanced hold bands.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** The vote was 9 to 1, with Evans dissenting for more accommodation. Beyond the formal dissent, "a number of members" held that more accommodation could be warranted but were willing to wait for better communication, and "a few" were on the opposite side. Participants also showed "a range of views on the current extent of slack" and "some" did not favor publishing policy projections. These are differences of emphasis and tactics rather than a split in the economic assessment, which fits 31-45. The November 28 swap-line vote (Lacker's dissent) is a separate videoconference decision and is not counted.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** Several participants addressed financial conditions meaningfully. "Financial markets remained volatile... in large part because of developments in Europe"; "many anticipated that further efforts... would be necessary"; dollar funding for European institutions "still strained"; "several participants commented on strains affecting some community banks". The outlook discussion also lists unsettled financial markets and euro-area banking issues as a restraint, and the policy section calls global financial strains a "significant downside risk". This is a moderate-attention meeting, not a dominant theme; the main focus remained the real economy and communications.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 8).** The forward signal is strongly easing. The Committee reiterated its anticipation of "exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013", which is date-based guidance. "Several members noted that the reference to mid-2013 might need to be adjusted before long", which implies an extension, and "a number of members" favored more accommodation after better communication. The 2012 SEP federal funds rate projections are a communications change, not a tightening signal. Score 8, in the strong easing / date-based band, slightly above the 3-5 of explicit threshold commitments.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** Uncertainty is elevated and widely cited. "High levels of uncertainty among households and businesses", "a high level of uncertainty about the economic outlook and the political environment" among business contacts, financial markets expected to "remain unsettled" until the euro-area issues are addressed, and "significant downside risks". Almost all members emphasized "carefully monitoring economic developments given the uncertainties and risks attending the outlook". This is elevated and focused on Europe and fiscal policy, not unprecedented, so 58 rather than 70+.
