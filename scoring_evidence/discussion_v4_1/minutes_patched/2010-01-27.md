## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 50 to 44).** Participants see conditions as supportive and little changed since December: "Policymakers judged that financial conditions were, on balance, about as supportive of growth as when the Committee met in December," and "financial markets generally continued to function significantly better than early last year." The bright-line rule sends any "supportive" reading below 50. The offset is that "All categories of bank loans ... continued to contract sharply," so the score is kept close to neutral at 44 rather than lower.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 45 with Low confidence).** The old quote about corporate risk spreads being "broadly consistent with historical norms" does not appear in the input. The only credit-spread statement is "private credit spreads narrowed somewhat," which gives a direction (narrowing) and no level. Under the v4.1 level rule that means 45-55 on the side of the move, with Low confidence. Score 45, +-10, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Members agreed "no changes to the Committee's large-scale asset purchase programs or to its target range ... were warranted," and the Committee reiterated that conditions "were likely to warrant exceptionally low rates for an extended period." The purchases are being completed on schedule and slowed to "promote a smooth transition," so there is no push for more easing. That puts the lean in the maintaining-maximum-accommodation range, not strong dovish. Hoenig's dissent for a more hawkish stance is a lone minority, and one member's "holdings" wording suggestion (keeping open both sales and purchases) was set aside as premature by other members. Score 30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** There is one formal dissent in a 9-1 vote: "Voting against this action: Thomas M. Hoenig," who wanted "low for some time" rather than "exceptionally low ... for an extended period" so as to "begin raising rates modestly." The discussion also shows some difference on inflation risks (some saw downside, "others ... saw inflation risks as tilted toward the upside") and on the degree of slack. These are emphasis differences, not camps. Score 35, typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** Several participants address financial conditions meaningfully: credit spreads, bank lending contraction, bank standards, and the commercial real estate exception. The policy section adds that "the functioning of most financial markets, including interbank markets, no longer showed significant impairment," with the liquidity facilities closing, and that members "were prepared to modify these plans if necessary to support financial stability." Hoenig's dissent also cites "future financial imbalances." It is a moderate topic, not a dominant one. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 18).** The Committee "decided to reiterate its anticipation" of "exceptionally low rates for an extended period" while "members agreed that the path of short-term rates going forward would depend on the evolution of the economic outlook." That is an easing-leaning, low-for-long signal with data dependence and no date or threshold commitment. Hoenig's request to soften the language shows some pressure toward a more hawkish signal, which the Committee did not adopt. Score 18.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Participants described "the prospects for job growth" as "an important source of uncertainty," said "their judgments about the degree of slack varied" (the unemployment insurance effect and the sustainability of productivity gains), and saw growth risks as "roughly balanced." Most noted a "rather slow" recovery without describing extraordinary uncertainty. That is somewhat elevated, concentrated on labor and slack. Score 40.
