## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 25).** Participants lean strongly dovish. "Committee members agreed that the stance of monetary policy should not be changed," keeping the range at 0 to 1/4 percent given "weak economic activity, substantial resource slack, and subdued inflation." The Committee judged an expansion of asset purchases "might provide additional support" but was not warranted, and the programs "should proceed for now on the schedule announced." Tapering was discussed without decision, and "a few participants" worried about inflation expectations, which keeps the score above the emergency/strong-easing end. Maximum accommodation held with no further easing: 25.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12).** Near-consensus. The vote record reads "Voting against this action: None," with all 11 voters in favor, and the discussion shows broad agreement ("participants generally agreed", "Committee members agreed"). Differences are minor: "some" saw a possible asset-purchase plan that "most" rejected as too difficult, and "a few" raised inflation-expectation concerns. No dissents and no stated reluctance: 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 66).** Financial conditions are a prominent topic though not dominant over the economic assessment. One full paragraph covers market developments ("broadly positive", SCAP, "underlying financial conditions remained fragile", "banks could face substantial losses", "many participants" on CRE). A further long passage has "meeting participants" judging that "market conditions remained fragile" and that early termination of liquidity facilities "might put renewed pressure on some financial institutions and markets"; the year-end period was "seen as posing heightened risks." Most participants engage at length: 66.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 14).** Strong easing bias without a threshold or date commitment. "Members thought it most likely that the federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period," and the directive anticipates a significantly expanding balance sheet. The remark that facilities may not need extending past February 2010 and the tapering discussion are exit-side hints but carry no tightening signal: 14.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** Elevated, though easing from the crisis. Participants cited "significant uncertainties in the economic outlook," "considerable uncertainty surrounding estimates of the output and unemployment gaps," business contacts "remained quite uncertain about the timing and extent of the recovery," and "elevated uncertainty was said to be inhibiting capital spending." Effects of further purchases "were uncertain." Offsetting this, "most believed that downside risks ... had diminished somewhat" and activity seemed to be "leveling out." Mix of multiple explicit uncertainty statements and reduced tail risk: 58 (Moderate confidence because the language is spread across topics rather than a single emphasis).
