## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 38 to 45, Low).** Participants said "credit spreads tightened" over the intermeeting period, a direction with no statement of the level of spreads. The old text asserted a level ("back toward pre-European-crisis levels") that the minutes do not give. Credit was "readily available" for larger corporations and only beginning to improve for bank-dependent smaller firms, which does not set the level of spreads. Under the v4.1 rule, direction alone scores 45-55 on the side of the move, so 45 with Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED evidence, score unchanged at 45).** The old quote about "banking and fiscal strains in peripheral Europe" is not in the input. Actual evidence: "Banking institutions reported signs of improving credit quality" and better consumer-loan performance (pulling below 50), against "elevated losses on commercial real estate loans" and the "risk of losses at financial institutions stemming from investors putting mortgages back to sellers" (pulling toward 50). Net: slightly below neutral, Moderate confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20).** Dovish, QE-era. "Most members judged it appropriate to take action to promote a stronger pace of economic recovery"; "most saw the benefits as exceeding the costs." Several participants argued purchases would guard against disinflation. Offsets: "some" expected only limited effect, "several" saw inflation risk, and Hoenig dissented on the hawkish side. The popular lean is clearly easing, so 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 48).** One formal dissent: Hoenig judged "the risks of additional purchases of Treasury securities outweighed the benefits" and objected to the extended-period language and the reinvestment policy. Beyond the vote, participants "differed in their assessments of the likely benefits and costs", "some" thought the effect would be limited, and participants differed on structural versus cyclical unemployment. Most still supported the action and the statement language ("nearly all"), so notable but not deep disagreement; 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 38).** A full paragraph on financial conditions, credit availability, bank credit quality, CRE losses and put-back risk, but no sustained financial-stability debate. Hoenig's dissent flags "future financial imbalances that could destabilize the economy." Several participants address the topic, mostly as conditions, so 38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 14).** Strong easing bias. "Nearly all members agreed that the statement should reiterate the expectation" of "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period", plus an open-ended securities program the Committee "will adjust ... as needed." There are no thresholds or dates, so it is not in the strongest 1-5 band; 14.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 42).** Participants cited "continuing uncertainty about the future tax and regulatory environment" and "elevated uncertainty about job prospects", and "some" saw the recovery as "vulnerable to negative shocks". Offsetting this, "quite unlikely that the economy would slide back into recession", "most saw the risks to growth as broadly balanced" (though "many" saw downside tilt), and a majority saw inflation risks as balanced. Somewhat elevated, not unusually high; 42.
