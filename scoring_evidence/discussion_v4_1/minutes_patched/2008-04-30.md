## Evidence for changed dimensions

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED, 46).** Participants mention only that "equity prices and yields on Treasury securities had increased" alongside easing volatility; no Treasury-specific dysfunction. A yield move alone is normal functioning, so the score sits in the 45-55 band, Low confidence. Old support was staff text not in the new input.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 58).** Participants say "a range of credit risk premiums had moved down" and note "a narrowing of spreads on corporate bonds", but add that "credit conditions were seen as remaining tight" and markets "remained under considerable stress". Direction is narrowing; no spread level is stated, so the score rests on the tight-credit level reading, somewhat above neutral, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 64).** Participants cite "continuing pressures on the liquidity positions of financial institutions" and banks that "had tightened lending standards and pricing terms", offset by the "willingness and ability of financial institutions to raise new capital" and a risk of severe deterioration judged to have "receded somewhat". This is emerging-to-significant stress rather than capital-impairment language; score 64, Moderate.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 35).** Participants note exports "supported ... by declines in the foreign exchange value of the dollar", and in the dissent record Mr. Fisher is concerned that "lowering the funds rate had been pushing down the exchange value of the dollar". Direction is depreciation, modest and briefly discussed, so below 50 at 35, Moderate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 40).** "Most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points", yet "most members viewed the decision ... as a close call" and members were "concerned about the upside risks to the inflation outlook". Two members (Fisher, Plosser) preferred no change, and "several members" said further easing was unlikely absent a significant weakening. A modest, reluctant easing with a pause signal: moderate dovish edging toward balance, 40.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 50).** Two formal dissents, both for no change, with the "close call" language from most members. The dissenters agreed the economy "evolved roughly as expected", so disagreement is about inflation risk weighting and tactics rather than the economic assessment; 50.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 66).** Much of the discussion is devoted to financial conditions: "markets remained under considerable stress", "strains in short-term funding markets had intensified", liquidity facilities, loan officer survey, and "financial headwinds ... through much of next year". Prominent, but alongside a full real-economy and inflation discussion; 66.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 55).** The Committee agreed "it was no longer appropriate for the statement to emphasize the downside risks to growth", risks were "more closely balanced", and "several members" thought easing further unlikely unless the outlook weakened significantly. This is a pause signal with data dependence, a slight tilt away from easing; 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** Participants "emphasized the considerable uncertainty attending the likely path of commodity prices", "stressed the difficulty of gauging the appropriate stance of policy", and several said uncertainty led firms to defer projects. The adopted statement says "uncertainty about the inflation outlook remains high". Tempered by lower odds of severe financial disruption and data "broadly consistent with expectations"; 58.
