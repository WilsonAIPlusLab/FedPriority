## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED).** Participants treat productivity only in passing. Many firms are absorbing "rising labor costs to the extent that the latter were not offset by improvements in productivity", which implies some gains but no assessment against trend. This is neutral to slightly positive, so the score is 55. Confidence is Low and the band wide, since the old "robust productivity growth" quote is not in the input.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** Members discuss realized price inflation and wage and price pass-through, not expectations. The old quotes are absent from the input, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The Committee eased 25bp to 4-3/4 percent. "Nearly all" members could accept it and "many" believed prompt easing would insure against financial strains. "Some" members preferred to wait, "all but one" of them endorsed the move, and Jordan dissented against it. The directive's removal of the easing bias shows the adjustment was seen as complete. This is a moderate dovish current action with a balanced forward lean, so the score is 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** There is one formal dissent (Jordan, citing "excessively rapid" money and credit growth). The decision was "a close call" for "several members" and "some members" would have waited. Disagreement is mostly about tactics and timing, and the Committee agrees on the economic assessment. This is the 31-45 band, so the score is 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Members "gave considerable attention" to financial developments, covering risk spreads, new-issue markets, year-end money-market pressures, bank lending terms and equity prices. "Many" members believed the easing would help "ensure against a resurgence of severe financial strains", and "many" saw a risk of an equity run-up. It is a prominent topic across the discussion but does not dominate the economic assessment, so the score is 68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The Committee moved from an easing bias to a symmetric directive, expecting "further easing was not likely to be needed" unless activity or inflation weakened more than anticipated. The easing was said to be readily reversible. The forward signal is neutral and data dependent, so the score is 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants repeatedly stress "considerable uncertainty", about the extent of foreign weakness, the consumer deceleration and the financial-market repercussions. They also call markets "atypically volatile" and the inventory adjustment "largely unpredictable". This is elevated, not extreme, uncertainty, so the score is 60.
