## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Several participants commented that leveraged-finance markets "had been correcting since midyear", especially the riskiest assets such as energy firms, and that "the widening of credit spreads in corporate bond markets appeared to be largely due to the repricing of riskier assets." This describes a direction of move with no stated level, and some participants framed it as repricing rather than stress. Under the v4.1 level rule the score is placed just above 50 on the side of the widening, with Low confidence: 54.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Almost all participants judged the labor-market progress and confidence in returning inflation to 2 percent to satisfy the criteria for beginning normalization, and the vote was unanimous for a 25 bp increase. The lean is held back by several things: a number of participants said the path should be gradual because inflation was "well below the Committee's objective", some members called the decision "a close call", and the stance stays "accommodative". This fits the liftoff anchor of 60-65, shaded to 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote record reads "Voting against this action: None." There was no sharp disagreement: a majority saw inflation risks as balanced while "many" were concerned about the downside, and "some others" wanted further labor-market progress. These are differences of emphasis and tactics, in line with the Dec 2015 liftoff anchor of 25-35: 25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants discussed the junk-bond fund closure and the leveraged-finance correction ("several participants commented"), and "a few participants also indicated that significant risks to financial stability, should they emerge, could alter their view of the appropriate policy path." This is a limited discussion, a context for the policy decision but not its focus: 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Participants and members expected conditions to "warrant only gradual increases" with the rate "likely to remain, for some time, below levels" expected in the longer run. They also stressed the need "to avoid appearing to commit to any specific pace" and said the path could become shallower or steeper. The signal is of further, gradual tightening with data dependence and an emphasis on patience. That gives a mild tightening bias: 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** A number of participants said the inflation outlook "was subject to considerable uncertainty", and many saw persistent weakness in energy and commodity prices as "adding uncertainty or posing important downside risks." Against this, most judged risks to activity and the labor market to be balanced and global downside risks had diminished. The uncertainty is concentrated in inflation, with otherwise standard caveats: 42.
