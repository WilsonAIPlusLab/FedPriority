## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22).** Participants lean dovish in the sense of maintaining maximum accommodation: they judged "the current stance of policy and policy guidance remained appropriate", that guidance was "serving the economy well", and that purchases "would continue at least at the current pace" until substantial further progress. The vote record has all members keeping 0 to 1/4 percent and continuing asset purchases. A number of participants wanted clear advance communication before any change in the pace of purchases, which keeps this from the QE-launch extreme but does not alter the lean. Score 22 in the strong-dovish / QE-era band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (10).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows "Voting against this action: None" and "All members agreed to maintain the target range". Differences are minor in emphasis: a couple of participants worried about risk-taking and financial imbalances, a few about CRE stress and forbearance unwinding, and most saw inflation risks as balanced while several flagged either upside or downside risk. No reluctant supporters are described. Score 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (38).** Participants spent one paragraph on financial conditions: the notable rise in longer-term Treasury yields (seen as reflecting the improved outlook), "disorderly conditions in Treasury markets or a persistent rise in yields" as a cause for concern, a number noting small-business financing remained challenging, and "a couple of participants" worried that accommodative conditions could lead to "excessive risk-taking and the buildup of financial imbalances". A few also cited CRE stress. Several participants address it, but as context for the economic outlook rather than a central theme. Score 38, Moderate confidence.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8).** The signal is strongly easing and threshold-based. Participants stressed the "outcome-based guidance" and that policy changes "should be based primarily on observed outcomes rather than forecasts", and that "it would likely be some time until substantial further progress" is realized. The statement in the vote record keeps the rate at 0 to 1/4 percent until maximum employment is reached and inflation is "on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time". A number of participants wanted advance communication before any taper, a mild offset. Score 8.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (58).** Participants "continued to see the uncertainty surrounding that outlook as elevated", agreed "the path ahead remained highly uncertain", and most said the pandemic "continued to pose considerable risks" (new strains, vaccine uptake, social-distancing fatigue), with some upside risks to spending named by "some". The outlook had improved, which tempers the level, but the language is explicit and widely shared rather than rhetorical hedging. Score 58.
