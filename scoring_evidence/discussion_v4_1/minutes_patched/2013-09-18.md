## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 28).** Participants lean dovish on net: the Committee kept purchases at $40 billion (MBS) and $45 billion (Treasuries) per month, "all members but one" judged it appropriate to "await more evidence", and "a number of participants thought that risk-management considerations called for a cautious approach". A sizable camp ("the participants who spoke in favor of moderating the pace") favored an immediate, "relatively small reduction", and the decision was "a relatively close call" for several, which holds the score above the strong-dovish band. The ongoing QE debate is itself the dovish signal; score 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** One formal dissent: Ms. George wanted a reduction in the pace of purchases, saying waiting "discounted the cumulative improvement in the economy as well as the potential costs". Two distinct camps debated taper-now versus wait, but both shared a broadly similar economic assessment and differed mainly on timing, tactics and communications. Moderate differences with one dissent; score 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 55).** Several participants judged financial conditions had "tightened notably" (mortgage rates) and a few others disagreed, a debate that ran through the discussion; the Committee decided to "underline its concern about the tightening of financial conditions". A separate paragraph covers financial-stability implications of higher rates (securities held by banks and nonbanks, "unwinding of leveraged trades", fixed-income market liquidity, community-bank risk-taking, municipal bonds, EMEs). Moderate-to-substantial attention; score 55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 12).** The 6 1/2 percent unemployment threshold was retained, with the policy rate expected to stay at 0 to 1/4 percent, and the statement says a highly accommodative stance "will remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends". Participants discussed "clarifying or strengthening" guidance, for example not raising rates if inflation is expected below a given level, and a couple saw low 2016 rate projections as "a commitment ... to support the economy by maintaining a more accommodative policy for longer". The hawkish offset is the signaled eventual moderation of purchases ("in judging when to moderate the pace ... at its coming meetings"), so the score is slightly above the Evans-rule anchor; score 12.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** A number of participants pointed to "heightened uncertainty about the course of federal fiscal policy" (shutdown, debt ceiling); the housing effect of higher mortgage rates "remained unclear"; the data had an "ambiguous cast"; business uncertainty about fiscal and regulatory policy weighed on decisions; structural damage was "quite difficult" to judge; and leadership-transition uncertainty bore on guidance timing. The caveats are specific and repeated but not Knightian; elevated; score 50.
