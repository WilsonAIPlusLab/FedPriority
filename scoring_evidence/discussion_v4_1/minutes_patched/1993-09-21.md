## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (50).** Participants are evenly balanced with an unchanged stance. All members agreed on no change and a symmetric directive. "Some members" were concerned the stance was "quite accommodative and probably would need to be firmed at some point", while "other members" gave weight to a sluggish expansion and "could not rule out" the next move being "toward greater monetary stimulus". The two camps offset, so 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (20).** The vote record lists twelve votes for and "None" against, and "all the members" endorsed the symmetric directive. The only difference is a disagreement among "some members" and "other members" about the likely direction of a future move, a tactical difference, not a dispute over current policy or the economic assessment. Broad agreement with minor differences of emphasis, so 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (30).** One paragraph covers financial developments: improved balance sheets, banks with stronger capital, and "a few members" worried that equity prices were "high relative to earnings and dividends", with a possible "cumulative selling" by mutual funds and efforts to raise investor awareness. It is context for the outlook rather than the focus of the meeting, so limited discussion, 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50).** The symmetric directive carries no presumption about direction. The text says that "both the timing and, in the view of at least some members, the direction of the next policy change could not be foreseen", and nothing in the next intermeeting period was expected to call for an adjustment. Some members lean to eventual firming and others to possible easing, with no directional signal overall, so 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (58).** Members said the outlook "clearly remained subject to a variety of uncertainties, including potential developments abroad that were especially difficult to predict", that interpreting the data raised "some unusual problems" (employment versus output and productivity), and that the inflation outlook was subject to "considerable uncertainty" and that current uncertainties "were mirrored in uncertainties about the future course of monetary policy". Several recurring statements, but the central outlook of moderate expansion was held with reasonable confidence, so 58.
