## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE (32).** Participants see inflation as below target and subdued. "The members viewed the outlook for core price inflation as still quite benign" and "expected price pressures to remain relatively contained", with "quiescent inflation pressures" cited in the policy discussion. Some members flagged potential wage, steel, energy and insurance cost pressures, which keeps the score from going lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP (35).** Participants see output below potential, with the gap closing only gradually. Most anticipated "a relatively subdued rate of expansion that would only gradually erode current margins of underutilized productive resources"; "low capacity utilization rates in numerous industries" and "excess capacity in labor and product markets" were cited. Below-potential language satisfies the bright-line rule; fairly vigorous near-term growth keeps the score above the deep-slack range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (36).** Participants see slack in labor markets. Cited were "ample availability of labor", a "somewhat elevated rate of unemployment" and a "relatively high unemployment rate", with employers "cautious in their hiring policies", though some manufacturing firms anticipated payroll increases.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45).** Net slightly below trend. Consumer spending "displayed surprising strength" and housing showed "renewed vigor", but further consumer durable gains were seen as limited, business confidence "remained at a low level", and "no evidence at this point of any broad-based improvement" in capital spending was reported. Members saw only "moderate further growth in consumer spending".

**PRODUCTIVITY (62).** Participants see productivity growth as above trend. "Robust underlying growth in labor productivity" and "favorable prospects for further robust growth in productivity" were repeated in several passages.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42, Low).** Only indirect evidence: "relatively low mortgage interest rates" sustaining housing (supportive, below 50) offset by a possible "negative stock market wealth effect". Participants did not otherwise discuss financial conditions, so the score is Low confidence with a wide band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (52, Low).** A single reference to the "negative stock market wealth effect stemming from large earlier declines" with no valuation judgment, so the score takes the default 45-55 range.

**INFLATION_GOODS (54).** A passing mention of "increasing steel, energy, and insurance costs" and oil prices having "risen appreciably since the start of the year"; a modest upward pressure in goods prices against an otherwise benign picture. Thin evidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52).** Hold with a mild tightening bias. All members supported an unchanged 1-3/4 percent funds rate and judged the "currently accommodative stance" appropriate "for now"; members also said policy would "need to be adjusted at some point to provide less stimulus", with timing to be assessed at the May meeting.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (8).** Near-consensus. All members supported the unchanged policy, nearly all agreed on the balanced-risks statement, and the vote record shows no dissents ("Votes against this action: None"). Members' only stated differences concerned the timing of eventual tightening, not the current action.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (8).** The minutes of participants' views carry no discussion of financial stability; only passing mentions of the stock-market wealth effect and mortgage rates.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62).** A mild tightening bias. The balance-of-risks language moved from weakness to neutral, and members said a reference to the "currently accommodative policy stance" in the press announcement "would alert the public to the need to firm policy at some point in the future"; they also noted that a neutral statement "did not preclude a tightening policy move". Timing was left to May, which keeps the signal mild.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (62).** Many members stressed difficulty in projecting the recovery. The recovery's strength "remained subject to substantial uncertainty", "considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for final demand", the economy was "undergoing significant structural changes", and "a wide range of possible outcomes could not be ruled out" for capital spending.
