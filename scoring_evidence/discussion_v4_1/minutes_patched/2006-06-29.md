## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 60 to 57).** Participants see the labor market as tight, somewhat above balance. The old evidence of a 4.6% May unemployment rate is staff text and was dropped. Remaining participant evidence: "resource utilization was currently tight", "relatively taut resource markets", and "some participants noted that businesses in their Districts were experiencing difficulty hiring certain types of skilled workers." Participants' unemployment forecasts (central tendency 4-3/4 to 5 percent) are in the near-NAIRU range, which holds the score below the old 60. Score 57 +-7, Moderate.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 48 to 47).** Participants see conditions as roughly neutral, a little on the supportive side. "Several participants observed that the continued ready availability of credit would support business expenditures" (supportive); "others, however, noted that the pullback from risk-taking ... could intensify, raising the cost of funds" (a possible tightening). The bond-spread widening used before is staff text. Few participants speak to it, so confidence is Low. Score 47 +-9, Low. Bright line (supportive means <50) is met.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 30 to 42).** The only participant evidence is on credit availability, not spread levels: "continued ready availability of credit" (several), set against a possible "pullback from risk-taking" (others). Availability is a mildly favorable reading of the level, so the score sits below 50 but not at the old 30, which relied on staff text and an unstated "extremely compressed" level. Score 42 +-10, Low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 50 to N/A).** The old evidence ("Major stock price indexes fell sharply") is staff text. No participant comments on equity valuation, so N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 55 to N/A).** The old evidence about the dollar's foreign exchange value is staff text. Participants discuss foreign central bank tightening and trading partner growth, not the dollar, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60).** The Committee tightened by 25bp with all members agreeing ("All Committee members agreed that raising the target ... was appropriate"), which is a majority-tightening reading, but the forward lean is balanced: "members recognized the value of accumulating more information for determining what, if any, additional policy action would be needed", and one member called the decision "a close call". Stance was seen as ranging "from modestly restrictive to somewhat accommodative". Score 60 +-7, Moderate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18).** The vote was 10 for, none against. Differences were minor: one member saw the hike as a close call, "members expressed a wide range of views" on statement wording (some favored a shorter statement), and most found the stance hard to characterize. Near-consensus with small emphasis differences. Score 18 +-6, High.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 20).** Only brief references: several participants on "ready availability of credit", others on a "pullback from risk-taking" that "could intensify", and "a reassessment of risks by investors globally". These are context for the outlook, not a focus. Score 20 +-7, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 57).** Mild tightening bias with explicit data dependence. The statement paragraph says "some inflation risks remain" and "the extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed ... will depend on the evolution of the outlook"; minutes add that action "at future meetings was not foreordained". Score 57 +-7, Moderate.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Elevated but concentrated on policy setting and the inflation path. "Many members noted that significant uncertainty accompanied the appropriate setting of policy going forward"; "greater uncertainty about inflation pressures and the needed policy response"; a key issue was whether the spring inflation increase was transitory or persistent. Not described as unusual across the whole outlook. Score 55 +-8, Moderate.
