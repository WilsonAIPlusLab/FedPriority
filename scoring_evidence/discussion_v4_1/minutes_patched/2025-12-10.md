## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 54).** Participants see spending as at or modestly above trend, with a split by income group. "Many participants viewed aggregate consumption spending as solid, although several pointed to signs of some recent slowing"; "some participants" cited "robust business fixed investment", with several pointing to the technology sector. A majority noted lower-income households were adjusting spending to price increases. Net: solid, upper end of the at-trend band. Score 54.

**PRODUCTIVITY (FILLED, 57).** Direction: modestly above trend, but hedged. "A number of participants noted that structural factors such as technological progress and higher productivity growth, possibly reflecting increasing use of AI, could boost economic growth without generating price pressures." Some participants also said "business productivity gains might enable some firms to manage" cost pressures. These participants added it was hard to separate structural from cyclical factors. Hedged language gives a modest score and Low confidence. Score 57.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 46).** Direction: somewhat loose to neutral. "Many participants expected growth to be supported by ... somewhat favorable financial market conditions"; a couple noted "recent declines in mortgage rates would provide support" to housing. Participants also "expressed a range of views about the restrictiveness of the Committee's policy stance", but that concerns policy rather than conditions. The old reasoning from the policy rate and the "neutral level" quote is not supported. The brief and indirect evidence gives Low confidence. Score 46.

**CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, now N/A).** The old values rested on "context" from a different meeting and on financial-stability themes that do not appear anywhere in the participants' views or the vote record. No participant is reported as commenting on credit spread levels, equity valuations or bank conditions. Under the N/A rule these become N/A. This also avoids a CREDIT_SPREADS value with no level information.

**INFLATION_GOODS (FILLED, 60).** Goods prices are seen as pushed up. "Most participants remarked that core inflation had been pushed up by higher tariffs that boosted goods prices"; "many participants" expect the tariff effect on core goods inflation to wane, though "some expressed uncertainty" about timing and pass-through. Score 60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 40).** Moderately dovish, tempered. "Most participants supported lowering the target range" (nine members voted for a 25bp cut) and "most participants judged that further downward adjustments ... would likely be appropriate if inflation declined over time as expected". Against this, "some preferred to keep the target range unchanged", "some participants suggested ... keep the target range unchanged for some time", and "several participants pointed to the risk of higher inflation becoming entrenched". A few supporters called the decision "finely balanced". One dissent was for a larger cut and two for no change. Score 40.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 68).** The vote record shows three formal dissents in opposite directions: Miran preferred a 50bp cut; Goolsbee and Schmid preferred no change. The disagreement extends to the assessment: hold advocates said "progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective had stalled", while cut advocates said upside inflation risks "had diminished". A few said that cutting "was not justified". No sharp exchanges are reported, which holds the score below the top of the band. Score 68.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 10).** Only passing references. The participants' views mention "somewhat favorable financial market conditions" as a support for growth, and the boilerplate lists "financial and international developments" among inputs. There is no discussion of valuations, leverage or stability risks. Score 10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 42).** Mildly easing but data-dependent. "All participants agreed that monetary policy was not on a preset course". The statement conditions further moves on "the extent and timing of additional adjustments". Most see further cuts as likely appropriate if inflation falls, while some and a few favor holding "for some time". The signal is close to neutral with a small easing lean. Score 42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** Elevated, with several specific sources. "Participants judged that uncertainty about their forecast of real GDP growth remained high"; the labor outlook "was still quite uncertain, especially amid the delays in the release of government data". Some expressed uncertainty about tariff pass-through, and a number of participants said it was hard to tell structural from cyclical forces. The statement says "Uncertainty about the economic outlook remains elevated." This is elevated rather than extreme. Score 58.
