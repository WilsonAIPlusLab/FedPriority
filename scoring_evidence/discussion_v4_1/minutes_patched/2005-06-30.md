## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 48).** Participants see the economy as near but not yet at potential: slack is "limited" and "remaining" in labor markets, the Committee wants to "bring output in line with potential", and "margins of slack resources" are "narrowing". The old "just above potential" support was staff forecast text. No participant says output is above potential, so the bright-line rule does not push the score above 50. The score is slightly below midpoint.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 22).** The level is low. Participants cite "thin risk spreads in corporate debt markets" while noting higher stock prices. This is a single passing reference, so confidence is Moderate with a wider band. The score stays below 50 under the level rule and agrees with the accommodative FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The new input has no participant comments on bank capital or loan performance. Those came from the first-day housing-valuation passage, which is not part of the rebuilt input.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only support was a staff-presented sentence. Participants in the new input do not discuss the dollar.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 64).** All members agreed on a 25bp increase to 3-1/4 percent, and the rate "remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary in the long run". "additional tightening would probably be necessary", though "views differed on the amount". Gradual, measured tightening with a vigilant inflation stance puts the score in the moderate-hawkish range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 22).** The vote was unanimous (no votes against, per the vote record) and "all members agreed" on the action. Participants' inflation views were "mixed" (some concerned about a step-up, others emphasizing restrained pricing power) and "views differed on the amount of tightening", which are minor emphasis differences in a broad-agreement meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** Participants' asset-market comments "focused on two related issues: the low level of long-term interest rates and the continued run-up in home prices", and a separate paragraph considers whether policy should respond to "possible imbalances in housing or bond markets", concluding it should not respond directly. A moderate share of the discussion, with no stress framing.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68).** Members "agreed that the statement language indicating that 'policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured' correctly characterized the outlook", and "additional tightening would probably be necessary". Guidance is conditional on data ("not seen as limiting the Committee's flexibility"), so the tightening bias is mild-to-clear rather than strong.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** It was "agreed that considerable uncertainty attended the outlook for both long-term interest rates and home prices"; the degree of labor slack "was very uncertain"; uncertainty about correcting imbalances "complicated the assessment". Confidence about the near-term expansion ("remained firm") limits the score to the elevated range.
