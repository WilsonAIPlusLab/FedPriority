## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20 +-5, High).** Participants lean clearly dovish. "All members but one judged that continued provision of monetary accommodation was warranted," and the Committee added Treasury purchases ($45 billion per month) to continuing MBS purchases ($40 billion). A few members saw purchases as warranted until about the end of 2013. Several others thought it appropriate to slow or stop purchases well before then, and one viewed any additional purchases as unwarranted. That minority pulls the score off the deepest QE-era values, but the majority is expanding accommodation. Score 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38 +-6, High).** There is one formal dissent: Mr. Lacker opposed the asset purchases and the numerical threshold language. Beyond the dissent, "several" favored slowing or stopping purchases well before end-2013 and "a number of participants" worried about complicating the eventual exit, but they supported the action. "Almost all" thought the program effective while "generally" seeing the benefits as uncertain. The disagreement is mainly about tactics, with some economic disagreement (Lacker on inflation risk). Moderate differences with one dissent places this in the 31-45 band. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42 +-8, Moderate).** Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the dominant theme. "A few participants" said low rates "could lead to financial imbalances and imprudent risk-taking"; one pointed to historical episodes of financial strains from very low long-term rates; one cited money market mutual fund reform; participants said the Committee must assess whether large purchases have "adverse effects on market functioning and financial stability"; several wanting to slow purchases cited "concerns about financial stability." Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 6 +-3, High).** The signal is a strong easing commitment. "Most participants favored replacing the calendar-date forward guidance with economic thresholds," and the statement keeps the rate at 0 to 1/4 percent at least while unemployment remains above 6½ percent, and expects a "highly accommodative stance" to remain appropriate "for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends." This is the threshold-based guidance anchor (Evans Rule). Mild offsets, namely that thresholds are not triggers and other information will be considered, and a few preferring qualitative guidance, do not change the easing direction. Score 6.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55 +-8, Moderate).** Uncertainty is elevated and specific. "Almost all indicated that heightened uncertainty about fiscal policy probably was affecting economic activity adversely"; business contacts were "highly uncertain"; there was "continued uncertainty regarding the European fiscal and banking situation"; "many" said it was "difficult to judge when" deleveraging would be complete; the benefits of purchases were "uncertain"; and one participant noted "considerable uncertainty" about the unemployment-inflation relationship. Most participants saw inflation risks as "broadly balanced," and the staff uncertainty text in the earlier input was not used. Score 55.
