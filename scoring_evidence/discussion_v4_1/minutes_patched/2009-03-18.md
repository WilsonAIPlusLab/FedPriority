## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 74 +-10, Low).** The old score leaned on a staff statement that corporate bond spreads were extremely high; participants' own text gives no spread levels. Participants' language implies a high level of credit stress: credit conditions "remained very tight", businesses reported bank financing "becoming more expensive and more difficult to obtain", and "CDS spreads of financial institutions" suggested worsening strains. The "large volume of issuance of investment-grade corporate bonds" was called "a notable bright spot", so the reading is high but not at the extreme. Level governs and is above 50; score 74, Low confidence because spreads are only indirectly referenced.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only support was the staff outlook sentence on oil and raw materials declines. Participants discuss overall inflation, slack and expectations but not goods prices; set to N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 4).** Strongly dovish. Committee members agreed "substantial additional purchases of longer-term assets" were appropriate; "several members felt that the significant deterioration in the economic outlook merited a very substantial increase", and the Committee approved up to $750 billion more of agency MBS, $100 billion more of agency debt and $300 billion of Treasuries, with the funds rate held at 0 to 1/4 percent. This is the emergency-easing, QE-launch end of the scale. Score 4.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 24).** Near-unanimity: the vote record says "Voting against this action: None" and the swap-line resolution was approved unanimously. Differences were over tactics: "One member preferred to focus additional purchases on longer-term Treasury securities, whereas another ... agency MBS", "a range of views as to the preferred size", "some participants" worried about older, lower-quality TALF collateral and about credibility from Treasury purchases (which "most participants" saw as low risk), and varied views on the recovery's strength and timing. Broad agreement with tactical and outlook-timing differences; score 24.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 68).** Most participants' remarks touch financial conditions: "Credit conditions remained very tight, and financial markets remained fragile and unsettled", "extraordinarily stressed", concern about bank financing, financial-industry contacts on government programs, "Many viewed the strengthening of the banking system as essential", and substantial attention to the TALF and credit-market purchases. It is prominent but shares the meeting with the real-economy assessment and balance-sheet mechanics. Score 68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 9).** Strong easing bias: members decided "to communicate to the public the Committee's view that the federal funds rate was likely to remain exceptionally low for an extended period", and the statement adds that the balance sheet would expand further. There is no explicit date or threshold, so it sits in the 6-15 band rather than 1-5. Score 9.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** Several elements of the participants' text point to high but not extreme uncertainty: business spending weakening "in an environment of uncertainty and low business confidence", "concern about downside risks to an outlook ... already weak", "a variety of views about the strength and timing of the recovery", "the greater uncertainty over the value of such assets", and "several members noted ... the effects of any one tactic were uncertain". Policymakers still expressed a clear central view on direction, so this is below the extreme band. Score 62.
