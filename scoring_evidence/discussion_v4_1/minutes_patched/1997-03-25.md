## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 43 +-10, Low).** The old analysis rested on text about automated equipment and productivity gains that does not appear in the input. The only relevant passage is that, as labor force participation depleted the pool of workers, "less productive workers would tend to be hired, with adverse effects on productivity and costs" (members, cyclical composition effect). Participants therefore see productivity as modestly below trend at the margin; a single passing remark, so Low confidence and wide band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48, +-8, Low).** Participants discuss the stock market only as a driver of consumer spending ("run-up in household net worth stemming from the earlier buoyant performance of the stock market"; "large gains in stock market wealth") and note the effect was "difficult to isolate" and restrained by saving motives. No participant calls valuations stretched or fair; price and wealth talk without valuation commentary scores near neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss broad consumer/GDP price measures ("consistent with a slightly declining trend") and compensation, but nothing on goods prices specifically. The old goods score used quotes that are not in the input, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 64, High).** Participants favor a tightening now: members "supported or could accept" a slightly less accommodative stance, seeing a "clear need for a preemptive policy action"; "a few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed", while others preferred caution. The vote was 10-0 for the increase. Forward lean is balanced (majority symmetric directive), which holds the score below the 70s: moderate hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 22, High).** Zero formal dissents ("Votes against this action: None"; "all the members indicated that they supported or could accept"). Differences are over magnitude ("a few members" wanted more) and directive symmetry ("a majority" symmetric, "other members" wanted an asymmetric bias but "could accept" symmetry). Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22, Moderate).** Attention to financial conditions is limited and contextual: household wealth effects, availability of financing for construction, concern that a policy shift "often can have exaggerated effects in financial markets", and inflation expectations becoming "embedded in financial markets". No discussion of financial stability risks.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 52, Moderate).** "A majority of the members favored a symmetric directive"; "additional policy tightening might be needed at some point" but further action "did not appear very likely" in the intermeeting period; the directive "should not establish a presumption" of further tightening. Other members stressed "vigilance" and wanted tightening "sooner rather than later" but could accept symmetry. Essentially neutral with a slight tightening tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50, Moderate).** Members said "substantial uncertainty surrounded the timing and extent" of the slowing, that it was "not possible to forecast with confidence when the period of favorable price behavior would end", and that the outlook was "subject to substantial uncertainties" (also the uncertain fiscal outcome and hard-to-isolate wealth effects). Repeated and explicit but paired with firm conviction about the policy step, so elevated rather than high.
