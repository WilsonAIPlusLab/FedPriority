## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55).** Participants lean mildly hawkish in a hold. All members supported "an unchanged policy stance while also retaining a bias toward restraint"; "anticipatory tightening was not yet called for" and "watchful waiting remained the prudent course", while some members wanted prompt action against rising inflation or embedded expectations. Hold with a tightening bias: 55.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (10).** Near-consensus. "All the members" supported the hold and the directive, the vote record shows "Votes against this action: None", and the views described are uniform (risks tilted up, patience warranted). Only some members added emphasis on acting promptly. Zero dissents, no reservations: 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (25).** Limited. Members cited "generally supportive financial conditions" and one paragraph noted stock-market valuations at "fairly high levels" and "the need to monitor with special care price movements in the stock market and asset markets more generally", but this is framed around consumer-spending implications and is not a focus of the meeting. Score 25, Moderate confidence.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62).** Mild tightening bias. Members saw "the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing", with the asymmetric directive retained, but "were not suggesting that policy should be especially quick to react" and the stance remained watchful waiting. Mild tightening bias without commitment: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (55).** Elevated but concentrated on inflation. Members said an inflation pickup "remained subject to a great deal of uncertainty", "there was a good deal of uncertainty regarding the outlook for inflation", the stock market "injected an additional note of uncertainty", and the unchanged policy reflected "the uncertainties surrounding the outlook". Other parts of the assessment were fairly confident ("quite favorably", "few imbalances"). Score 55.
