## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 43 +-7, Moderate).** Participants see the labor market as improving but still below full employment. Members cite "notable improvement in labor market conditions", "persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor and other productive resources", and projected unemployment of "5-1/4 to 5-1/2 percent" in the fourth quarter of 2004. The old payroll and participation quotes were removed because they are not part of the participants' views. The score is unchanged.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants say nothing about credit spreads or corporate bond risk premia. The old analysis scored 30 from a sentence about corporate yields and risk spreads that is not in the participants' views, so the dimension becomes N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 35 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see a weaker dollar. "Some limited inflationary impetus as well from the depreciation of the dollar", and "expanding foreign economies and the depreciation of the dollar were expected to foster appreciable growth in U.S. exports". Depreciation puts the score below 50 (bright line). With no magnitude stated and only passing mentions, 35 replaces 30.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 50 +-8, Moderate).** Members point to "some increase in near-term inflation expectations", while those expecting deceleration cite "steady long-term inflation expectations". Others stress "changes in business attitudes and expectations". Near-term drift up with long-term anchoring nets to about 50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60 +-6, High).** The lean is modestly hawkish. "All of the members" supported raising the target from 1 to 1-1/4 percent as "a first step in the process of removing policy accommodation", and the vote was 12-0. The forward pace is expected to be "gradual" or "measured". Some said "a more aggressive pace ... might be called for", and "a number" saw upside inflation risks without further tightening. A few preferred dropping forward characterizations. Score 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20 +-5, High).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows no votes against the action. Differences were over inflation-outlook emphasis (those expecting deceleration versus those stressing momentum) and the wording of forward guidance ("A few" wanted it removed). Score 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12 +-5, Moderate).** Only passing mentions: "accommodative financial conditions" as a support for growth, and "financial market conditions had tightened in recent months". There is no discussion of stability risks. Score 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68 +-6, High).** The signal is mildly to moderately hawkish: further removal of accommodation ahead, at a gradual pace. The statement paragraph says "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured". Members "could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves", while "a number" would see risks tilted to the upside absent more tightening. "A few" wanted no forward characterization. Score 68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55 +-7, High).** Elevated but not extreme. "Considerable uncertainty still surrounded" cost pass-through, the extent of inflation slowing was "difficult to forecast", "several noted that they now had less confidence in those forecasts", and fiscal policy carried "a marked degree of uncertainty". The outlook also assumed that "major terrorist disruptions would be averted". The statement still rated risks as roughly balanced. Score 55.
