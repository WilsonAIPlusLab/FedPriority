## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58).** Participants lean hawkish but held. "Participants generally agreed that the case for increasing the target range... had continued to strengthen" and "Most participants expressed a view that it could well become appropriate to raise the target range... relatively soon," with "a few" advocating an increase at this meeting and "some" saying it should come at the next one for credibility. The vote record shows two members (George, Mester) wanting 25 bp now, but "a majority of members" preferred to "await some further evidence," and "a few members" stressed caution given the lower bound. Hold with a tightening bias and a near-term hike expected: 58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (48).** Two formal dissents, both on the hawkish side ("George and Mester dissented because they preferred to increase the target range... by 25 basis points"). The disagreement is mainly about timing, but it extends partly to the assessment: "some participants" saw the economy "close to or at full employment" while "several others judged that appreciable slack could remain," and "some others" favored letting unemployment undershoot while "many" worried about overheating. Two dissents plus distinct camps on slack but a shared view that the case for a hike was strengthening: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (45).** The minutes open a paragraph with "Participants discussed a range of issues related to recent developments in financial markets and financial stability" (MMF reform, term premiums), and "many judged that risks to economic and financial stability could increase over time if the labor market overheated" or worried about "reach for yield... mispricing of risk and misallocation of capital." "A few" also cited financial fragilities abroad. Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the focus: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62).** The signal points toward tightening: the Committee "judges that the case for an increase... has continued to strengthen," "most" see a raise "relatively soon" if data cooperate, and the path is "only gradual increases." This is tempered by "likely to remain, for some time, below levels... in the longer run" and "the actual path... will depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming data." Mild tightening bias with data dependence: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (33).** "A substantial majority viewed the near-term risks... as roughly balanced" and "almost all" in the vote record agreed. Elevated items are limited: "a few participants judged that significant downside risks remained" (low neutral rate, foreign growth, Brexit, financial fragilities), and "participants expressed uncertainty about how long the participation rate could be expected to continue rising." Standard caveats with a few specific areas of uncertainty: 33.
