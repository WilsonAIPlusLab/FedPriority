## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The only credit-spread content is that participants commented on "the widening of credit spreads on corporate bonds." This describes direction; no participant gives a level (neither "elevated" nor "compressed"). Under the v4.1 level rule the old 57 (which treated "widening" as putting spreads clearly above normal) is not supported. Direction only, so 53 on the side of the move (a mild widening), Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants lean slightly hawkish on the forward path but held policy now. "Some participants thought that the conditions for beginning the policy normalization process had already been met," and "most participants anticipated" those conditions "could well be met by the time of the next meeting." Against that, "almost all members agreed it was appropriate to wait," "some others" judged a December move unlikely, "several participants" favored caution and wanted options for more accommodation if the outlook weakened, and one member (Lacker) preferred an immediate 25bp hike. Hold steady with a tightening bias: 54.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote was 9-1; "Voting against this action: Jeffrey M. Lacker," who wanted a 25bp hike because "persistently strong consumption growth and tightening labor markets" made low real rates inappropriate. Beyond that, "some" and "some others" differed on whether December liftoff was warranted, "a couple of members" worried the new wording signaled a hike too strongly, and "members differed in their assessment of the likelihood" of an increase in December. These are differences of timing and risk weighting rather than of assessment, with one formal dissent: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants "discussed a range of topics related to financial market developments and financial stability" (macroprudential tools, valuation risks in leveraged loans and real estate, corporate spreads, risks from prolonged low rates, Puerto Rico). In the policy debate "a number" cited "the increasing risk of a buildup of financial imbalances" as a reason not to delay, and "several" worried liftoff "might be associated with an unwarranted tightening of financial conditions." Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the dominant topic: 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The statement drops the "how long" framing and says the Committee will assess progress "in determining whether it will be appropriate to raise the target range at its next meeting"; "members emphasized" it "may well become appropriate to initiate the normalization process at the next meeting." This is a clear nearing-liftoff signal, tempered by "no decision had been made," "a couple of members" worrying it was too strong, participants "generally agreed" removal would be "gradual," and rates likely to stay "below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run" for some time. Mild tightening bias: 60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** "Several participants observed that the recent employment reports had increased the uncertainty about the outlook for the labor market," and several "noted uncertainty about whether economic growth was robust enough to withstand potential adverse shocks." Most saw downside risks from abroad as diminished, risks "nearly balanced," and the baseline outlook was held with reasonable confidence. Somewhat elevated, not unusual: 42.
