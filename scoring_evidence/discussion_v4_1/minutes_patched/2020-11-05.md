## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 42 +-10, Low).** The old support was a staff statement that corporate spreads narrowed; participants never mention spread levels. They describe credit availability as mixed: financing conditions "eased further" for mortgage borrowers and large corporations with market access, "bank lending conditions tightened further", and "a few participants" called small-business financing "especially worrisome", all within "generally accommodative" overall conditions. The rule gives direction without level, so the score sits slightly below 50 (net accommodative) with Low confidence.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** Every phrase the old analysis cited on equity prices, IPOs and valuations came from staff text. Participants raise no equity-market view; "a couple of participants" warn that prolonged low rates and highly accommodative financial market conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking, which is not a view on equity valuations. Set to N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The cited "pickup in market-based measures of inflation compensation" and the premature-liftoff remark are not in the participants' text. The new input only has "several participants" encouraged that market expectations about liftoff conditions matched the guidance, and the vote record's goal that "longer-term inflation expectations remain well anchored at 2 percent". Neither describes the level of expectations. Set to N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20).** Dovish: "all participants judged that maintaining an accommodative stance of monetary policy was essential"; "most concurred" much of the accommodation comes from guidance and securities holdings; purchases to continue "at least at the current pace"; "many participants" want to enhance asset-purchase guidance fairly soon; "a few" hesitant. Unanimous hold at 0 to 1/4 percent. Maintaining maximum accommodation fits the 11-25 band, not an easing move.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12).** Unanimous vote ("Voting against this action: None"), "all participants" agree on stance, SEP changes "unanimously supported". The only difference is tactical, "a few participants were hesitant" about changing asset-purchase guidance soon. Near-consensus band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** A dedicated paragraph where "a number of participants" discuss risks: "a few" on banking resilience, "several" on bank capital and the March Treasury selloff, "a couple" on excessive risk-taking, "a few" on climate, "several" on 13(3) facilities as a backstop, plus financial conditions in the asset-purchase text. Several participants address it meaningfully but it does not rival the economic assessment, so mid-range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 6).** Participants called the September outcome-based guidance "an important step"; the vote record keeps the range at 0 to 1/4 percent until labor market conditions reach maximum employment and inflation is "on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time"; purchases continue "at least at the current pace"; "most participants favored" outcome-based asset-purchase guidance. Readiness to "adjust the stance ... as appropriate" is standard risk-management wording. Strong threshold-based easing commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 72).** "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as quite elevated"; stance justified by uncertainty that "continued to be very elevated"; "considerable risks" medium term; several downside risks (virus resurgence, fiscal support, Europe) and "considerable uncertainty" on relative price movements. Many emphasize it with a wide range of outcomes, but not Knightian or emergency-level.
