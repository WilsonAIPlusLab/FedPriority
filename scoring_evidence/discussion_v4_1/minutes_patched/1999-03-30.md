## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 62).** Participants see spending as well above trend but expected to moderate. Members anticipated consumer spending "would retain considerable upward momentum" though "unlikely... sustained at its recent exceptional pace"; the "extraordinary expansion in business fixed investment" showed "some signs of deceleration... though from extremely rapid rates"; residential activity "very strong in many parts of the country". The old surge/deceleration quotes were not in the input. Score unchanged at 62.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 40).** Conditions seen as mildly supportive, not clearly accommodative. Members cited "ready access to consumer credit", "the rise in financial wealth", and "recovery in financial markets" (enough that a number saw a case for "unwinding part of the Committee's easing"), against "the rise in mortgage rates since last fall". Below 50 under the bright-line rule; moved from 37 to 40 because the strong "ample financing" quote is absent.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' text contains no statement on credit spreads, risk premiums or borrower creditworthiness, so neither a level nor a direction can be read. The old 35 rested on text not in the input.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 48).** Participants say little about current expectations. Some members warned that the "dissipation or reversal of favorable supply factors... could contribute to higher inflation expectations", while most saw inflation "contained for a time". Near-neutral, slightly below 50, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55).** Unanimous for an unchanged stance with a tightening tilt in expectations: "In the view of some... the next policy action was more likely to be a firming than an easing"; "many believed that the next policy move likely would be in the direction of some tightening"; a number noted a case for unwinding part of the fall easing but argued data did not support it; many said the Committee "might need to act promptly". Hold with a tightening bias: 55.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15).** Vote record shows eleven votes for, none against. All members favored unchanged policy and "all the members endorsed" retaining symmetry. Differences were of emphasis (some worried about stretched labor markets, some others pointed to productivity). Near-consensus: 15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Financial topics appear only as backdrop: emerging-market markets "more settled", Brazil contagion "reduced threat", stock-market wealth effects, and the concern that announcing a tightening bias would have an "undesired effect on financial markets". No participant treats financial stability as a focus. Limited discussion: 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 54).** Formal signal is neutral: the directive is symmetric ("equally likely to warrant an increase or a decrease"), and the members chose to keep symmetry rather than signal a tightening bias, noting markets "might well build in higher odds of a policy tightening move at the May or June meetings". Many nonetheless expect the next move to be tightening, though "not a foregone conclusion". Slightly hawkish neutral: 54.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Several participants stress uncertainty: "the timing and extent of such moderation were subject to a wide range of uncertainty"; "a great deal of uncertainty surrounded the extent to which productivity gains and other factors, some unspecified, might continue to hold down inflation"; policy forecasts "subject to a substantial degree of uncertainty"; foreign forecasts "subject to considerable downside risk". Elevated but with a stable expansion backdrop: 55.
