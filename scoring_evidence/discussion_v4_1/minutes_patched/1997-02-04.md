## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 45 +-10, Low).** Participants raise the stock market only as a risk to consumer spending ("the possibility of a setback in the stock market"). There is no valuation commentary such as "stretched" or "frothy" in the participants' views, and the old 22 relied on directive text about index gains. Under the rubric's rule for price moves without valuation commentary, the score is near neutral, at the low end given the implied correction risk.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 60, +-8, Moderate).** Participants see the dollar as having appreciated: net exports reflect "the earlier appreciation of the dollar", and "some members reported that business contacts had already communicated concerns about increased competitive pressures from imports because of the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Appreciation is clear (> 50) but the participant language is modest, so 60 instead of the staff-text-driven 67.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss overall inflation, food and energy prices, and labor costs, but not goods prices as a category; the old evidence came from non-participant text.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58, +-5, High).** The Committee held policy unchanged but "strongly supported" a bias toward restraint, and the members agreed it "would be desirable to tighten before any sign of actual higher inflation." A few members saw output above potential and noted that an immediate tightening "would help," but all accepted "wait and see"; several worried a move was "not generally anticipated in financial markets." This is a hold with a tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15, +-5, High).** The vote was unanimous ("Votes against this action: None") and "all the members" backed the directive. The only differences were emphasis: "a few members" stressed the above-potential risk, while "most members" thought inflation would stay contained.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12, +-5, Moderate).** Financial conditions appear only in passing: the stock-market setback possibility, high consumer debt and "rising repayment problems", and the financial-market reaction to a tightening move. No participant makes it a topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68, +-6, High).** Members "recognized that an asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening was consistent with their general view that the risks were now more clearly in the direction of an upward trend in inflation" and called for "prompt action." The signal is a tightening bias, but no action is promised now.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 60, +-8, Moderate).** "The outlook was subject to considerable uncertainty," the "extent of the slowdown ... was unclear," and "considerable uncertainty about future inflationary developments" justified the wait-and-see stance. Members still reached a central forecast and expected inflation to remain contained for a time, so the score sits below the rubric's 65-80 band for "considerable uncertainty."
