## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants say nothing about the level or direction of credit spreads. The only credit-supply language is that a few participants thought "financing and credit conditions were particularly challenging for small businesses", alongside rising consumer delinquencies and low farm-loan delinquencies. This is mixed and not about spreads, so the V4.1 no-level rule applies: score near the midpoint with Low confidence. Score 52 +-8, Low. The 52 itself is retained, within 45-55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants were unanimous on holding the rate at 5 1/4-5 1/2 and on continued runoff. They judged the rate "likely at its peak", which removes the earlier tightening bias, and many wanted in-depth balance-sheet discussions "to guide an eventual decision to slow the pace of runoff." Against that, "most participants noted the risks of moving too quickly to ease" and the Committee stayed "highly attentive to inflation risks"; only a couple stressed the downside of overly restrictive policy. Net: hold, balanced to a slight dovish lean. Score 47.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote record shows 12 votes for and "Voting against this action: None." The text says "all participants judged it appropriate" to hold and to continue runoff. Differences were of emphasis only (most on easing too soon, a couple on over-restriction). Score 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** There is a dedicated paragraph. Participants judged bank risks "had receded notably," but "some banks" warranted monitoring (funding costs, uninsured deposits, unrealized losses, CRE). Liquidity was "more than ample." Several raised discount-window readiness, some raised wholesale funding, and a few raised Treasury resilience, cyber risk and household delinquencies. Risks to financial conditions also appear in the outlook risks. This is moderate attention, not dominant. Score 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Participants judged the policy rate "likely at its peak for this tightening cycle." Most did "not expect it would be appropriate to reduce the target range" until "greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent", and the path would "depend on incoming data." The vote record shows the additional-firming language removed. The signal is patient and data-dependent, with an easing direction but no near-term cut. Score 42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants "noted that the economic outlook was uncertain" and "highlighted the uncertainty associated with how long a restrictive monetary policy stance would need to be maintained." Two-sided upside and downside risks were listed. Much of the language is standard statement wording, and the risks were seen as "moving into better balance." That is somewhat elevated, not high. Score 38.
