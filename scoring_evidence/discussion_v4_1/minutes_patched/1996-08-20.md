## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 52 +-10, Low).** Participants do not claim productivity is running above trend. They say productivity improvements "were likely to offset part" of rising labor costs, but "how much remained an open question"; one differing view (Stern) worried about the absence of "a substantial and sustained improvement in productivity". Roughly neutral with a slight tilt to improvement; thin evidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant describes spread levels, credit availability or direction; the old score came from absence of comment. Scored N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The text has no equity-market comment at all; the "record highs" evidence in the old analysis is not in the input.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only dollar reference is the directive's background paragraph ("depreciated slightly"), which describes information reviewed and is not a participants' view. Scored N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55).** Most members (all but one) supported keeping the stance unchanged, citing slowing growth, subdued broad price measures, and the risk that an unexpected tightening could cause an "excessive reaction in financial markets"; but they also kept a bias toward tightening, with inflation risks "clearly ... to the upside". One member argued a "prompt policy action was needed". Hold with tightening bias, lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 32).** One formal dissent: Mr. Stern wanted policy "modestly more restrictive". The disagreement is about timing and the weight on inflation risk, not the economic assessment. Typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12).** Only brief mentions: "generally supportive financial conditions" as a growth factor, and a few members' concern about market reaction to a tightening. No financial-stability discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 63).** The directive keeps a bias toward "possible firming"; members said tightening "should be implemented promptly if developments were perceived as pointing to rising inflation" and the Committee should "remain particularly vigilant". Mild tightening bias, tempered by the decision to await more information.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Members said the extent of slowing "remained uncertain", inventory behavior was "a key uncertainty", and the pass-through of labor costs and the narrowing of profit margins were uncertain or "difficult to predict". Several caveats, but framed as ordinary forecasting uncertainty.
