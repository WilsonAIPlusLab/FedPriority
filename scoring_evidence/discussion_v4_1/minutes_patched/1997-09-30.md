## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 44 rested on a sentence about share-price volatility that does not appear in the new input. The only equity-price text is in the directive's market-background paragraph, which is not a participants' view. Participants raise no valuation or equity-concern view (wealth appears only as support for consumer spending). N/A for value, UNC and CONF.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED).** The prior "producer prices down slightly" evidence is absent. Participants said the dollar's appreciation and sluggish foreign economies were "holding down prices in world commodity markets and prices of imported goods", and firms found it "very difficult" to raise prices. That points to goods prices below normal pressure but without direct goods-deflation language. Score 35 +-9, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Hold with a tightening bias. All members backed an unchanged stance; "several" said developments "could well require a tightening of policy in the relatively near future", and several urged preemptive action; risks were "strongly tilted toward rising inflation". Reasons for delay (benign inflation, unintended market effects) held the lean short of a call to tighten now. Score 58 (lean hawkish, 51-60).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Unanimous vote ("Votes against this action: None"), "all the members" favored retaining the asymmetric directive, and "a consensus emerged" despite "diverging considerations" on timing. Difference is limited to emphasis on timing, not the assessment. Score 17 (broad agreement, minor emphasis differences).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Financial conditions get limited, context-level attention: "several" noted credit "amply available ... perhaps overly so", "some" noted broad money strength meant policy "was not restraining liquidity or financial conditions", and members worried a surprise tightening might have "adverse effects on financial markets". No sustained focus. Score 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The directive's asymmetry toward restraint was retained; members "continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing", with "a heightened degree of vigilance", though the chance of a move in the intermeeting period "might not be high". Mild tightening bias. Score 64.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** It was "an open question" whether demand would slow; inflation's benign behavior was "not fully understood", making forecasts of an upturn "subject to a considerable degree of uncertainty"; it was "unclear" whether demands would moderate without action. Confident language on current strength and tightness offsets this, and the uncertainty centers on one issue. Score 52 (elevated, 46-60).
