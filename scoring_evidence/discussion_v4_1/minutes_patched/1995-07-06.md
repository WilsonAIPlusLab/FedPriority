## Evidence for changed dimensions

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, score unchanged at 28).** Participants see the banking system as healthy and improving. The M3 discussion says "the financial health of depository institutions had improved markedly over the last few years", and financing was readily available from "banking institutions". The old quote about banks easing terms and standards does not appear in the input. Healthy means below 50, and the single mention keeps confidence Moderate: 28 +-7.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 32 to 40).** The only participant references are to "earlier declines in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" and "the recent stability of the dollar" (easing import-price pressure), plus a note that easing could affect foreign exchange markets. This reads as weak earlier, now stable. The "declined considerably" wording sits only in the directive's descriptive paragraph, which is not participants' views. Score 40 +-9, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 36).** "Nearly all the members" favored or could support slightly easing, because policy was seen as "somewhat restrictive". "A few members" preferred more easing, and some leaned unchanged (all but one of them would not dissent). The easing was framed as cautious ("Several members" noted a change in direction could have pronounced market effects). Hoenig dissented in favor of no change. A moderate dovish lean, tempered by the small size of the move and the unchanged-leaners: 36.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** Formal dissents: Hoenig on the policy action (hawkish, no change). Blinder and Yellen dissented on the M2 range, as "a technical judgment, not a policy difference". Three camps (slight easing, more easing, unchanged), with unchanged-leaners reluctantly going along. Disagreement is mostly tactical, and views on the economy are broadly shared. Moderate differences: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22).** Members "gave particular emphasis to the favorable financial climate", and a few noted that falling long rates partly "rested on market expectations of significant monetary policy easing". The health of depository institutions is mentioned once. These are brief to limited mentions with no stability concern, in a mid-1990s meeting dominated by the economy and the monetary ranges: 22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 34).** The Committee adopted a directive with "a bias toward possible further easing", preferred by "most of the members who favored some easing". The few preferring more easing expected policy "would need to be eased by more than a slight amount over time". However, "no member expressed a strong presumption about the likely need to ease policy during the weeks ahead", and the Committee would "monitor and respond as needed". An easing lean with data dependence: 34.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** "While the timing remained uncertain", the "evidence ... remained mixed", "substantial uncertainty remained about projections of money growth", and members cited "current uncertainties surrounding the economic outlook"; risks to the disinflation outcome "remained substantial", and most saw downside risks. This is elevated but conventional language, with a clear base case of a resumption of moderate growth: 50.
