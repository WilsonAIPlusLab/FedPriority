## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score rested on a sentence about equity prices responding to lower long-term rates that does not appear in the participants' discussion or the vote record. The only "equity" in the new input is business equipment. Participants offer no valuation or equity-market view, so the dimension is N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to 37, +-8, Low).** Direction is weak dollar, below 50. The directive says the trade-weighted dollar "declined on balance over the intermeeting period", and the two dissenters (Angell and Lindsey) call it "relatively depressed" given U.S. strength; Angell links a firm price-stability stance to stabilizing "the exchange value of the dollar". The old "substantially... all-time low against the yen" language is absent. A modest decline raised by only two members and the directive supports a milder score than 30, with Low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED to 45, +-8, Low).** The direction is slightly below anchored-at-target. The members noted that "the recent behavior of long-term debt markets suggested expectations of more subdued inflation", and most concluded the evidence "did not confirm a resurgence in inflationary pressures". The dissenters' concern is about the inflation outlook and "incipient price pressures" in commodity prices, not about expectations as such. The old "inflationary psychology" and "higher inflation expectations" quotes are not in the new input.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 52).** Most members endorsed an unchanged degree of reserve pressure as "appropriately balanced" against the opposing risks. Several acknowledged that near-zero real rates "could have inflationary consequences" if the expansion strengthened, and some observed that any intermeeting move might well be a tightening. Against that, most saw "no compelling case" for a tightening move and one member said a restraint tilt "would give a misleading indication". Two members wanted an immediate tightening. Balanced with a slight hawkish pull gives 52.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 46).** There were two formal dissents, Angell and Lindsey, both for immediate tightening on the same inflation-risk reasoning, and Angell added a separate rationale. The disagreement is mainly about risk weighting and timing, though the dissenters judged policy "overly accommodative" where the majority judged it "appropriately balanced". There were no other noted reservations, which puts this at the low end of the 46-60 band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 15).** Attention to financial stability is brief. The material is the caution of financial institutions in lending, "credit markets did not provide confirming evidence" of inflation, and the effect of deficit reduction on financial markets. These are context for the growth and inflation discussion, not a focus, which fits the 1993 typical range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 52).** The majority wanted the symmetric directive retained; they gave no presumption about the likely direction of policy. "Some observed that a policy change during the intermeeting period, if any, might well be in the direction of a tightening move", and the Committee "would need to remain alert" to a stronger expansion. A neutral signal with a slight tightening-side tilt in the commentary gives 52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** Several strands of uncertainty appear. "Some members cautioned" that the expansion differed from earlier recoveries and that "a considerably different result--in either direction--could not be ruled out". The members described the deficit-reduction outlook as "subject to considerable uncertainty". Price developments were "notably difficult to predict", and business contacts were uncertain about demand. The members also agreed that "considerable uncertainty" surrounded broad money relative to spending. Most of these are topic-specific caveats and the central outlook is a moderate expansion, so the score is moderately elevated.
