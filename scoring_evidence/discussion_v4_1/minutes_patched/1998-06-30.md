## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** Participants discuss equities only as a source of household wealth ("extended uptrend in stock market prices") and a financing channel ("debt and equity financing on relatively attractive terms"), and note that "equity price gains of recent years" were "unlikely to be repeated". There is no explicit overvaluation or concern language, so the rubric default of 45-55 applies, nudged slightly toward the elevated side: 45.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, value kept).** Participants say "reduced prices of oil, other commodities, and imports more generally could well keep inflation in check", and cite the "decline in energy prices in recent quarters" as a special factor that may unwind. Goods prices are seen as soft to stable with some pressure from those factors fading: 38.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old support was core CPI, tobacco and shelter detail, which is not in the new input. Participants talk about labor compensation and unit labor costs, not services prices, so N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old support was shelter cost detail, which is not in the new input. Home prices appear only as a wealth effect and office rents only as nonresidential construction context, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The Committee held the funds rate at about 5-1/2 percent and kept the bias toward firming. "Many members" saw no urgency to tighten for domestic reasons and gave "important weight" to reactions abroad, while "a number of these members" saw a "high probability" of later tightening and "one member" wanted action at this meeting; Mr. Jordan dissented for "modest monetary restraint". Hold with a tightening bias, tempered by wait-and-see: 56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (Mr. Jordan, hawkish), with a stated rationale about "unsustainably rapid growth of domestic demand". All others accepted the directive, and the range vote was "None" against. Differences were over timing, not the economic assessment: 33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** A tightening move "would involve the risk of outsized reactions and consequent destabilizing effects on financial markets" abroad, called "an important reason" for deferring; Asian turmoil might have "a more major effect on U.S. financial markets". Domestic financing conditions are noted as accommodative. Several participants address it, but it is not the dominant topic: 36.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The Committee agreed "to retain the tilt toward restraint", which "would continue to underscore ... its view of the likely direction of the next policy move"; "a number of" members would "take advantage of any early opportunity to tighten". Mild tightening bias, softened by the "wait and see" posture: 65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** "Many of the members" said the "already substantial risks" had "increased on both sides"; members said it was "too soon to judge" the Asian turmoil and that it was "adding significantly to the uncertainties"; "substantial uncertainties" favored a "wait and see" stance. Elevated, short of extreme: 60.
