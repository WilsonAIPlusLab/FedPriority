## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 35 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see the labor market as slack but stabilizing. Members said "labor demand had begun to show signs of stabilizing after an extended period of weakness" and that most firms were "hesitant to expand their facilities or hire permanent workers"; the Committee referred to "currently wide margins of idle labor". The 6.1% unemployment rate cited earlier is not part of the participants' views. Below 50, slack remains: 35.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 28 +-7, Moderate).** Conditions are seen as loose. Members listed "accommodative financial conditions" among factors supporting growth and noted "still low mortgage interest rates"; this is a single list mention plus a housing remark, so confidence is Moderate rather than High. Bright-line (accommodative, so below 50) satisfied: 28.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants do not comment on credit spreads or credit availability; the earlier spread-decline evidence came from the manager's report. Score N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 30 +-8, Moderate).** The dollar is seen as weaker. Members referred to "a weaker dollar" fostering some improvement in exports and cited "the weakness in the dollar" as a factor tending to reduce domestic disinflation; no size of the decline is given by participants. Bright-line (depreciation, so below 50) satisfied: 30.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 36 +-6, High).** Dovish hold. All members favored an unchanged 1 percent funds rate, saying "an accommodative monetary policy might remain desirable for a considerable period of time" and that "the risk of undesirably low inflation" would remain the predominant concern. No easing was sought and some members saw risk-assessment changes eventually approaching, so not lower: 36.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 14 +-5, High).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows twelve votes for and "None" against, and "all the members agreed" on the policy stance. Views on the vigor of the expansion "differed to some extent" and "some members" saw risk-assessment changes possibly approaching; no consensus on communication approaches, referred to a working group. Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 10 +-4, High).** Almost no attention. Only a passing reference to "accommodative financial conditions" as a growth support and to low mortgage rates; no discussion of financial stability or markets. Score 10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 22 +-6, High).** Easing-leaning, low-for-long signal. The Committee said accommodation "might remain desirable for a considerable period of time", it "could wait for more definitive signs" of inflationary pressure before any significant adjustment, and the vote record keeps inflation risks and the balance of risks weighted to the downside. Some members' remark that risk-assessment changes "might be approaching" keeps it from going lower. Score 22.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 45 +-8, Moderate).** Somewhat elevated. Members acknowledged "the economy was emerging from an atypical period that limited the guidance that historical experience provided", some emphasized "a high degree of uncertainty about the underlying trend in productivity", and the timing of inventory rebuilding and the duration of the tax-cut effect were called uncertain. The factors for vigorous expansion were said to predominate, so not higher. Score 45.
