## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 47).** Participants gave direction without a clear level: "some risk spreads had narrowed" as European concerns eased. Level hints cut both ways: large firms "able to borrow readily and at relatively low rates" and a sizable fraction of banks easing C&I terms, against "small businesses continued to find credit hard to obtain" (some) and CRE and mortgage standards "very tight". Narrowing direction with no firm level reading puts the score just below 50, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** The Committee held the rate at 0 to 1/4 percent and chose to reinvest paydowns in Treasuries because "most members" thought a runoff-driven tightening of financial conditions "would be inappropriate". "Several members" emphasized readiness to "provide additional policy stimulus" if the outlook weakened further. Only one member (Hoenig) argued for starting to remove accommodation. A moderate dovish majority with a hold and an easing bias gives 30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** One formal dissent, Hoenig, objecting to the "extended period" language and to reinvestment, and citing future financial imbalances. Beyond that, "a few" worried the reinvestment could signal renewed asset purchases, and "another member" feared it would complicate exit. Views on the economy were broadly shared; differences were mainly about policy tactics and the dissent. Score 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** "Many participants" noted that European fiscal efforts and the bank stress tests reduced concern about financial dislocation; a lengthy passage covered credit availability for large firms, small businesses, CRE and consumers. "Most members" focused on the financial-conditions effect of balance sheet runoff. Hoenig raised "buildup of future financial imbalances". Moderate attention, not dominant. Score 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 18).** "All members but one" judged it appropriate to reiterate that conditions were "likely to warrant exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period", and "several members" said the Committee would need to consider more stimulus if the outlook weakened. Qualitative low-for-long plus an easing-ready stance, but no threshold or date. Score 18.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** Participants "recognized that the implications of these new data for the outlook were unclear" (consumer saving revisions), "a number" cited business uncertainty about fiscal and regulatory policy, "some" worried "further adverse shocks could have disproportionate effects", and members noted the size of the runoff tightening "was uncertain". "A few" thought the data were just noisy and the outlook unchanged. Elevated but not extreme. Score 48.
