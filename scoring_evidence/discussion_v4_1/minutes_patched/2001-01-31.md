## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 55).** Participants see conditions as somewhat tight in places but not broadly restrictive. The new input cites "tighter supply conditions in segments of the credit markets", "deterioration in the financial balance sheets of some business firms" and "investors in financial markets remaining skittish". Against that, "Reduced mortgage interest rates", long-term rates "appreciably below their peaks of the past year" (a few) and "sound condition of the banking system" (several) are supportive. This sits slightly above neutral, 55, with Low confidence because the text is mixed and brief. The old 62 rested on staff-style text about a stronger dollar and smaller financial stimulus that is not in the participants' views.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 54).** There is no statement of spread levels. The only credit evidence is direction: "tighter supply conditions in segments of the credit markets" and a risk of "tighter standards and terms on credit". Under the v4.1 direction-only rule this scores 45-55 on the side of the move. Score 54, Low confidence. The old "risk spreads ... fell substantially" evidence is not in the input.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 55).** Participants (some, in the consumer discussion) cite "the adverse wealth effects of the decrease in stock market valuations" and list "lower equity prices" among downside risks. There is no valuation judgment, and falling prices are not treated as undervaluation, so the score is a mild-concern default of 55 with Low confidence. The old 62 relied on equity moves not found in the input.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 45).** The only dollar text is that "the large current account deficit was seen as a factor pointing to potential depreciation of the dollar over time". This is a prospective weakness view, not an observed move, so it scores modestly below 50 (45) with Low confidence. The old "changed little" evidence is not in the input.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 17).** "All the members endorsed a proposal calling for a further easing ... 50 basis point decrease ... to a level of 5-1/2 percent", a "relatively aggressive policy adjustment" after the early-January cut. Several saw the need for "front-loaded" easing, and members saw "little inflation risk" in it. This is strong dovish with unanimous support, 17. Some upside risks "could not be totally dismissed" keeps it from the extreme.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 11).** Vote record shows ten votes for and none against. The members "agreed on its desirability". The only visible differences were over the foreign outlook ("some emphasized ... Other members were concerned") and whether upside risks matter ("some members"). Near-consensus, 11.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 27).** Several mention the sound banking system as a supportive factor, and a few passages note tighter credit supply, balance-sheet deterioration and skittish investors. These are context for the growth outlook rather than a focus of the meeting. Limited discussion, 27.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 24).** All members agreed the balance-of-risks sentence "should continue to indicate that the risks would remain tilted toward economic weakness", and the vote text says risks are "weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness in the foreseeable future". This is a mild easing bias without a date or threshold commitment, 24.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** "The eventual degree and duration of the softening ... were difficult to predict", "it was unclear whether the pause ... would be largely limited to a relatively short inventory correction or would involve a more extensive cyclical adjustment", and "the extent and duration of the current economic correction remained uncertain". Members also saw "considerable downside risks" yet "substantial underlying strength and resilience" and were optimistic beyond the near term. Elevated but not extreme, 55.
