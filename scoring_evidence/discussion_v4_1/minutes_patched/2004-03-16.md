## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants see credit conditions as modestly less favorable than at the last meeting, because "financial markets also seemed a little less positive... with stock prices lower and some risk spreads wider." This gives direction only (widening) and no statement of level, so the v4.1 rule puts the score just above 50 on the side of the move, with Low confidence. Score 53. (The gap from FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS at 26 reflects that the spread passage is directional only; it is not a level reading.)

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** Participants referred to the dollar only as having declined: "members cited the decline in the value of the dollar on foreign exchange markets since the middle of last year" as a factor boosting export demand. Depreciation puts the score below 50. It is a brief, background mention, so Low confidence. Score 33.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** The old score rested on a contained-expectations statement that is not in the participants' views. The participant text discusses prices, unit labor costs and pricing power but never inflation expectations, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The stance is hold-with-patience at 1%, with a mild easing bias remaining. "All the members favored the retention of the current target rate"; "the current accommodative stance of monetary policy remained appropriate." Many members saw a balanced inflation risk assessment as a possible move and "a number" had a marginal preference for it, and "some" stressed watching for inflationary pressures, but all agreed to retain the "slight tilt toward ... further disinflation." Score 42.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows twelve votes for and "None" against. There were mild differences on the inflation outlook ("other members were less certain that inflation had leveled out") and on the risk wording ("many viewed it as a close call"), but "all members indicated that they could support" retaining the language. Score 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** A limited topic. "Some members were concerned that keeping monetary policy stimulative for so long might be encouraging increased leverage and excessive risk-taking," plus reports that speculative forces might be boosting housing demand in some regions. Mentioned as a side concern in the policy discussion rather than a focus. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The approved statement paragraph says the Committee "can be patient in removing its policy accommodation," and in discussion the members kept the slight tilt toward further disinflation (though that probability had "diminished"). This signals eventual removal of accommodation but not soon, an easing-leaning, data-dependent patience. Score 33.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Somewhat elevated, mostly ordinary. "The extent and duration of the resulting restraint on hiring were difficult to assess"; firms in some regions "somewhat more uncertain"; "reasons for the reduced optimism were not entirely clear"; members split on whether the downtrend in inflation had ended. Overall outlook seen as supported by the breadth of the expansion ("considerable assurance of its sustainability"). Score 38.
