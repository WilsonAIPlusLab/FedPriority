## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 32).** Participants lean dovish but did not act at this meeting. "Many participants" said that if growth stayed too slow or inflation too low "it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation," and "several members" would "consider it appropriate to take action soon"; the discussion centred on further longer-term Treasury purchases and ways to affect inflation expectations (a QE-type debate, the dovish signal). Others would ease only if the outlook worsened, "some members saw merit in accumulating further information," nearly all voted to hold, and Hoenig's dissent was hawkish. Net: moderate dovish lean with a hold, 32.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** One formal dissent (Hoenig: remove "extended period," raise rates toward 1 percent, stop reinvesting), based on a different view of the economy and risks ("new imbalances"). The "one member" opposing the "extended period" language is most likely the same person. Participants were also divided between "many" favoring conditional easing and "others" wanting a worse outlook first. That is a notable difference in timing and risk with one dissent, 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 35).** Limited-to-moderate attention: one paragraph on "Financial developments" (bank caution, international capital standards, CRE losses, European stresses that "bore watching") and the vote record's concern that low rates "could lead to macroeconomic and financial imbalances." Financial conditions appear as context, not the main topic, 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 14).** A strong easing bias: "Nearly all members agreed" to reiterate "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period," "members generally thought" the statement should say the Committee "was prepared to provide additional accommodation," and such accommodation "may be appropriate before long." The guidance is neither date- nor threshold-based and remains data-dependent, so it sits in the strong-easing-bias band rather than the lowest one, 14.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Elevated but not extreme. Members cited "considerable uncertainty about the current trajectory for the economy" (vote record, "some members" waiting for information), "elevated uncertainty about employment prospects," and business contacts holding back over fiscal and regulatory uncertainty. Participants still "considered it unlikely that the economy would reenter a recession" and judged the recovery to be continuing, so the level is elevated rather than very high, 55.
