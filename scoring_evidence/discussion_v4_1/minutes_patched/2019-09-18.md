## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 35).** Participants lean moderately dovish: "most participants believed that a reduction of 25 basis points ... would be appropriate", citing the outlook, risk management and inflation. A couple wanted 50 bp; several wanted to hold and judged policy "already adequately accommodative". The decision was a 25 bp cut (7 for, 3 against), and "for most participants" the outlook relied on "a somewhat more accommodative path for policy". Majority favoring some easing, with hold and larger-cut camps on either side, gives 35.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 68).** The vote record lists three dissents: Bullard wanted 50 bp ("insurance against further declines in expected inflation"), George wanted no change, and Rosengren judged policy "already accommodative" with risk of "inflating the prices of risky assets". The dissents point in opposite directions with different reasoning, and participants split into three camps (25 bp, hold, 50 bp). The minutes describe no sharp exchanges, so the score stays in the significant-division band rather than the deep-split band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** "Several participants cited considerations that led them to be concerned about financial stability, including low risk spreads and a buildup of corporate debt, corporate stock buybacks ... and the pace of lending in the CRE market", while "several others pointed to signs that the financial system remained resilient". A few hold-camp participants worried easing "could exacerbate financial imbalances", and Rosengren's dissent cited leverage and risky-asset prices. A moderate, multi-participant topic that is not dominant.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 42).** Participants "agreed that policy was not on a preset course" and depended on incoming information; the statement says the Committee "will act as appropriate to sustain the expansion". A few judged that market pricing implied "greater provision of accommodation at coming meetings than they saw as appropriate", which pushes toward neutral, while several wanted clarity on when the recalibration "would likely come to an end" and a couple of 50 bp supporters wanted enhanced forward guidance. Data-dependent with a mild easing lean overall.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** "Participants generally had become more concerned about risks associated with trade tensions and adverse developments in the geopolitical and global economic spheres"; downside risks "had increased somewhat". Several noted recession-probability models were hard to read ("a couple ... stressed the difficulty of extracting the right signal"), and the hold camp said key uncertainties "were unlikely to be resolved soon". Elevated, in the trade-tension anchor range, but outlook described as positive and little changed, so not high.
