## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20).** Participants lean strongly dovish: all members agreed to hold the range at 0 to 1/4 percent, "almost all" backed outcome-based guidance that keeps it there until inflation is "on track to run moderately in excess of 2 percent for some time", and participants judged asset purchases should continue "at least at the current pace". The only hawkish-leaning voices are "several participants" who preferred to retain the older, more flexible guidance, and Kaplan (flexibility beyond the threshold); a couple of participants and Kashkari wanted stronger, even more dovish guidance. Net: strong dovish, maximum accommodation, score 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** Two formal dissents (Kaplan wanted greater policy rate flexibility; Kashkari wanted a stronger commitment tied to core inflation reaching 2 percent), pulling in opposite directions. Several participants also voiced reservations about the new guidance, and a couple wanted stronger guidance. But "all members agreed" to hold rates and "almost all" agreed on the guidance, and the dispute concerned wording rather than the economic assessment. Above the one-dissent anchor, below distinct-camps territory: 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 42).** Participants devoted a paragraph to financial conditions: differences in credit quality, rising business-loan delinquencies, tight bank lending, and "several participants" noting stress that small and medium-sized banks could face; "a couple of participants" warned that accommodative conditions could lead to "excessive risk-taking", and several cautioned that guidance could "contribute to a buildup of financial imbalances". Several participants engaged meaningfully but it was not a dominant topic: 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 6).** The forward signal is a strong easing commitment: outcome-based, threshold-style guidance that rates stay at the ELB until labor market conditions reach maximum employment and inflation has risen to 2 percent and is on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time, plus asset purchases "at least at the current pace". "Most participants" supported explicit guidance and the vote record shows "almost all" members agreed. Caveat that it is "not an unconditional commitment" and the stated willingness to adjust if risks emerge keeps it slightly above the Evans-rule floor: 6.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 70).** "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as very elevated", the inflation outlook was "highly uncertain", and the path of the economy depended on the virus, with "considerable risks" to the medium-term outlook and several downside scenarios cited. Language is strong and broad but not "uncharted territory" or unbounded, and the shutdown risk was seen as having "receded": 70.
