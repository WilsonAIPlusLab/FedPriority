## Evidence for changed dimensions

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** Participants see the dollar as having weakened modestly, not as unchanged. The directive's "changed little" is the Committee's text and not a member view, whereas members cite "the dollar's depreciation" raising import prices, "the slippage of the foreign exchange value of the dollar," and several are concerned the current account deficit could produce "a considerable weakening of the dollar." Modest weakness to stable: 42, with Moderate confidence because the move is described as slight and the language is indirect.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Hold with a tightening bias. All members endorsed unchanged policy, motivated mainly by Y2K market stability; "they saw little cost in deferring" tightening, but "concerns about rising inflation" could be addressed in February and "a preemptive tightening move might be warranted in the not-too-distant future." Most preferred a symmetric directive, a few an asymmetric (tightening) one. Score 56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows no votes against; "all the members" endorsed the hold. Minor differences: a few "marginal preference" for an asymmetric directive but "could readily accept" symmetry; one member had reservations about the intermeeting-move amendment (a procedural matter). Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Moderate-to-limited attention. The Committee's "primary near-term objective was to foster steady conditions in financial markets" and to preserve confidence that liquidity would be provided during the century date change, and members flagged consumer spending vulnerability to stock-market declines; but the discussion remained mostly about the real economy and inflation. Score 38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Mild tightening bias, muted near term. Most members wanted a symmetric directive to convey that "no tightening action was contemplated for the weeks immediately ahead," while longer-run inflation concerns would go in the press statement; a few wanted an asymmetric directive consistent with "the consensus... regarding the most likely course of monetary policy over the next few meetings." The disclosure changes move toward risk-based language. Score 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Somewhat elevated to elevated, but largely standard. Members described the extent of demand moderation as "subject to a wide range of uncertainty," and said uncertainties about potential output and inflation dynamics "made it difficult to relate with confidence" demand projections to inflation; elsewhere the assessments are firm (strong expansion, clear momentum). Score 52.
