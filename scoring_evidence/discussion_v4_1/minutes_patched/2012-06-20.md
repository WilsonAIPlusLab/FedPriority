## Evidence for changed dimensions

**TREASURY_STRESS (FILLED, 35 +-8, Low).** The rebuilt policy discussion shows participants addressing Treasury market functioning: "some members noted the risk" that continued purchases "could, at some point, lead to deterioration in the functioning of the Treasury securities market", but "members generally agreed that such risks seemed low at present". "A few members" wanted to understand how large purchases would have to be to impair functioning. This is a hypothetical risk, not observed dysfunction, so normal functioning (below 50, above the very quiet band). Low confidence because it is a forward risk only. The earlier "marked drop in yields" remark is safe-haven yield movement and is not dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 54 +-6, Low).** Many participants described investors' euro-area concerns "leading to higher risk spreads and lower prices for riskier assets". This describes a widening move with no statement of the level of spreads (not "elevated" or "wide"). "Credit standards remained tight" concerns availability, not spread levels. Under the level rule, direction only gives 45-55 on the side of the widening, with Low confidence. Score 54. The old 60 treated the widening as a high level.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 26 +-6, High).** The Committee kept the funds rate at 0-1/4 percent and almost all members agreed to extend the maturity extension program. "A few members" said further stimulus "likely would be necessary", and "several others" said additional action "could be warranted" if recovery lost momentum or inflation ran persistently low. The Committee said it was "prepared to take further action". The only hawkish counterweights were one member (Lacker) who saw little effect and "a few" who saw upside inflation risk. This is a strong dovish stance in a QE-era setting, with some hawkish dilution. Score 26.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38 +-7, High).** There was one formal dissent (Lacker, who opposed the maturity extension program and felt stimulus would raise inflation without helping growth). Beyond the dissent, participants split on the amount of labor slack ("some" structural, "some other" persistent slack), and "a few" saw upside inflation risks while "some" saw downside risks. Some noted Treasury market risks. Moderate differences with a single dissent. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50 +-8, Moderate).** The discussion devotes a full paragraph to financial conditions: euro-area sovereign and bank strains, the health of US banks, "a few participants" noting imprudent risk-taking from low rates, "participants discussed the risk" of spillover and "some noted the importance of ... adequate preparations". The policy section adds concern about Treasury market functioning. Several participants address it meaningfully but it does not rival the economic assessment. Score 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 6 +-3, High).** "Almost all members again agreed" that conditions "are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014", with the date described as conditional. The Committee also said it was "prepared to take further action". This is date-based easing guidance with a further easing bias, matching the strongest easing-commitment band. Score 6.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58 +-8, Moderate).** The vote-record summary says strains in global financial markets "continued to pose significant downside risks ... making the outlook quite uncertain". Participants also cited a possible sharper fiscal tightening, and "some participants" cited business contacts putting projects on hold because of tax and regulatory uncertainty. Inflation risks were seen as "roughly balanced" by most, and "some" thought recent weakness would prove transitory, which limits the score. Elevated but not extreme. Score 58.
