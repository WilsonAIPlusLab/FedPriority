## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 55 cited "ongoing gains in productivity" and contained unit labor cost reasoning that is not in the new input. The only nearby text says many firms were upgrading equipment "to reduce costs and improve their product offerings", which describes investment motives, not perceived productivity growth. Participants do not discuss productivity, so N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old evidence ("major indexes of stock prices were narrowly mixed") is not in the new input and no participant comments on equity markets or valuations. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 28 to 36).** Direction is weaker dollar. The Committee's directive says the dollar "declined somewhat on balance", and Mr. Angell cites a "surprisingly weak exchange value of the dollar" as a reason to tighten. The "historical low against the yen" wording that justified 28 is not in the new input, and "somewhat" implies roughly 5-10 points below 50 for the general decline, pulled lower by Angell's emphasis. Score 36, Moderate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** Direction is lean hawkish: hold steady with a tightening bias. "In the view of a majority of the members" developments were "sufficiently worrisome to warrant positioning policy for a move toward restraint", and these members supported a directive with "a greater predilection to tighten than to ease". "Some members" preferred a symmetric directive and "all but one" of them accepted the asymmetric one; "in one view" (Angell) a "prompt move toward restraint" was needed. No change in the degree of reserve pressure. Score 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 55).** Two formal dissents pulling in opposite directions: Mr. Angell wanted an immediate tightening, Mr. Boehne rejected any tightening bias and favored a "balanced policy directive". Disagreement reaches the inflation assessment ("a number of members" stressed that faster inflation "might well tend to be sustained", "others" stressed slack and restrained money and credit). The vote was 10-2 and no sharp exchanges are recorded. Distinct camps and two dissents place it in the notable-disagreement band. Score 55.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 10).** Attention is minimal: low rates, "receptive financial markets" and "more aggressive lending policies of some depository institutions" appear as context for equipment spending, and bonds and foreign exchange appear as inflation-psychology gauges. Nothing on financial stability proper. Score 10, consistent with the era's typical low attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** The signal is a mild tightening bias: the directive allows "slightly greater reserve restraint", and a tightening action "could help to moderate inflationary expectations". It is hedged by the Chairman's proposal for an intermeeting consultation before any tightening, and by the view of "many" that inflation might prove temporary. Score 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** "Several emphasized that the outlook was subject to substantial uncertainty" tied to deficit-reduction legislation; members said it "remained difficult to find a satisfactory explanation" for the price increases, and "a considerable degree of uncertainty surrounded the outlook for inflation". Monetary aggregates were described as unreliable guides. Several rather than most stress it, and the committee also sees the growth outlook as "reasonable", so 62 rather than higher.
