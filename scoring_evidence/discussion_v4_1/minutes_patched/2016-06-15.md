## Evidence for changed dimensions

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 35 to 50).** The old Part 2 evidence relied on a statement that U.S. banks' capital and liquidity "remained strong", which does not appear in this meeting's participants' views. The only banking-relevant passage is that "a couple of participants" observed energy-related "financial strains ... for firms or financial institutions in their Districts", likely to persist absent higher energy prices. That is a localized stress pocket with no system-wide assessment either way, so it is scored neutral (50), Low confidence, wide band.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50).** Policy held at 1/4 to 1/2 percent. "Most participants judged" that a hike "would be appropriate if incoming information confirmed" the pickup, job gains and inflation path, and "some" said another step "should not be delayed too long". Offsetting this, "most" wanted more labor-market data and to wait for the U.K. vote, "some" noted a shallower path than in March, "some other participants were uncertain" a hike was warranted, and "several" said policy may need to stay accommodative. Conditional tightening bias and a wait-and-see hold balance out at 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 27).** The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None"). Differences were about timing and interpretation of the May payroll shock (transitory noise vs. slowdown vs. supply constraints) and about downside inflation risks ("several others continued to see downside risks"), with "a couple of members" wanting more evidence. These are minor emphasis differences, not camps with different prescriptions, so 27.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 40).** "Most participants noted" that the U.K. referendum "could generate financial market turbulence"; "some" flagged China's FX policy and debt as "appreciable risks to global financial stability"; "several" of the hawkish participants worried that delay "would increase the risks to financial stability". Several participants address it meaningfully but it is framed as risk context beside the economic assessment, so 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50).** The statement expects "only gradual increases" with the funds rate "likely to remain, for some time, below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run", a mild normalization signal. It is explicitly tied to incoming data ("not ... a preset plan", "leave their policy options open"), and the near-term signal is to wait, so the net forward signal is neutral, 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** "Almost all participants judged that the surprisingly weak May employment report increased their uncertainty"; "Members noted the considerable uncertainty about the outcome" of the Brexit vote; "the longer-run neutral rate was highly uncertain"; "several" cited "considerable uncertainty about the neutral federal funds rate"; data dependence stressed "given the uncertainties". Multiple, specific, near-universal sources, but participants also stated that fundamentals remained solid and they were reluctant to change outlooks, so elevated rather than very high: 58.
