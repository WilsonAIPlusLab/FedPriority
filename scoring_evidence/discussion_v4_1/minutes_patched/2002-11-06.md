**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 33 +-7 Moderate).** Participants see labor markets as slack: members cited "slack in labor and product markets at elevated levels" and expected "pressures on labor and other resources" to be "limited" over coming quarters. The old payroll and unemployment-rate evidence is not in the participants' text. Below neutral, mildly so given only brief, indirect treatment.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 64 +-8 Moderate).** Members expected "the continuation of a strong uptrend in productivity" to underpin recovery and cited "continued robust growth in structural labor productivity." This is above trend ("encouraging gains" / strong) but the old "largest since 1973" quantification is not in the input, so the score is lowered from 70 to 64.

Price moves without a valuation judgment default to 45-55; slight tilt to the depressed side, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old "household credit quality stable" evidence is absent. The only banking reference ("bank lending terms... more restrictive," cited by some members) is a financial-conditions statement, not an assessment of bank soundness or stress; N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old "appreciated slightly" evidence is absent. Participants discuss only that little stimulus could be expected from exports given weak trading partners; no dollar or FX view; N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Dovish: all members favored a 50 basis point cut to 1-1/4 percent, a "relatively aggressive easing action," with "potential costs" of an unneeded easing "quite limited." Tempered by the shift of the risk assessment to balanced and the remark that easing "could readily be unwound." Moderate dovish, not emergency.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18).** The vote record shows "Votes against this action: None." Disagreement was confined to wording of the risk assessment: "some voiced reservations" and some had "at least a marginal preference for retaining the current tilt toward weakness" but were "willing to accept a balanced statement." Near-consensus with minor reluctance.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Limited: "some members" cited that "overall financial conditions, including bank lending terms, had become more restrictive," and "many members" worried about an "overreaction in financial markets" to the statement. Context rather than focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50).** The approved press-statement sentence says the Committee "believes that the risks are balanced" for "the foreseeable future," removing the earlier weakness tilt. Members worried a weakness tilt would be read as signalling "further easing later." No directional signal either way; neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Elevated, standard-plus: "a high degree of uncertainty and related caution" holding back investment, "High and persisting uncertainty" including geopolitical risks, and "uncertainties that surrounded prospective market reactions."
