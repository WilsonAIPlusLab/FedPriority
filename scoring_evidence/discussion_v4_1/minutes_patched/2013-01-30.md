## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants see credit conditions as improving, but no one states a level of spreads. Several participants said domestic credit conditions "appeared to have improved", with auto lending expanding rapidly and "robust" competition in C&I lending; mortgage credit stayed tight. This is direction/availability evidence with no level information, so under the v4.1 rule it scores slightly below 50 on the side of the move: 45, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED).** The old score rested on a sentence about banks having expanded capital and being in sound condition. That sentence is not in the rebuilt participants' input. Participants do not assess the health of the banking system, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Policy lean is dovish-to-moderate. Most participants said asset purchases had been effective, and "all but one member" supported maintaining the highly accommodative stance with purchases at $40bn MBS and $45bn Treasuries per month. A number of participants thought ongoing evaluation might lead to tapering or ending purchases before substantial labor-market improvement; several others wanted purchases to continue until then; a few raised inflation risks; one member (Ms. George) dissented as policy was "too accommodative". QE-era debate over the pace of purchases places this at 30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (Ms. George, citing financial imbalances and inflation expectations), plus "many participants" voicing concerns about costs of further purchases, and divided views (a number vs. several others) on tapering before labor-market improvement. Views on the economy itself were largely shared. Moderate disagreement about pace and risk with one dissent: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** "Several others expressed concern about the potential for excessive risk-taking and adverse consequences for financial stability"; "some participants mentioned the potential for a sharp increase in longer-term interest rates to adversely affect financial stability" and wanted further work; "some noted that further asset purchases could foster market behavior that could undermine financial stability"; the dissent cited financial stability risks. Several participants address it meaningfully but it is not the dominant theme: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The Committee retained the threshold-based guidance (unemployment above 6.5 percent, inflation projected no more than half a point above 2 percent) and expects a highly accommodative stance to remain appropriate "for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends". Participants "generally judged" that markets had adapted to threshold guidance; one preferred lowering the unemployment threshold. A number of participants raising possible tapering ahead of the labor-market goal and a few communications concerns add a slight upward tilt: 10.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants "generally agreed that fiscal negotiations could develop in a way that would result in significantly greater drag" and anecdotes said uncertainty about the economy and government policy "continued to restrain firms' hiring and capital spending"; a couple said uncertainty about the level and sources of potential output made real-time output gap measures hard to use; costs and benefits of asset purchases were debated. Offsetting this, most saw downside risks reduced. Somewhat elevated but not unusual: 38.
