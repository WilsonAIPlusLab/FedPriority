## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants describe private credit spreads as "substantially lower" and capital-market conditions as having eased significantly, but give no statement of whether the level is wide or narrow. Under the V4.1 level rule this is direction only, so the score stays slightly below neutral at 48 with Low confidence (was Moderate).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The policy lean is dovish. The Committee held the range at 0 to 1/4 percent, kept "exceptionally low rates for an extended period" and, per "all members", reiterated the $1.25 trillion MBS and about $175 billion agency debt purchases; this is a QE-era "how much to buy" posture. Small hawkish offsets are the plan to "gradually slow the pace" of purchases and that "most" or "many" participants see some upside inflation risk longer term. Score 22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote was unanimous (ten voting for, "None" against), and "all" members backed the asset-purchase plan and the rate-path wording. Differences were only emphasis: "some participants" worried about a self-sustaining recovery, and inflation risks were tilted down by "some" and up by "others". Score 11.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants devoted a substantial paragraph to financial conditions, tight bank credit, CRE exposure and small-firm financing, and members flagged possible "excessive risk-taking in financial markets" from prolonged low rates while seeing the likelihood as "relatively low". This is moderate, multi-participant attention. Score 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The forward signal is easing: rates exceptionally low "for an extended period", tied to low resource utilization, subdued inflation and stable expectations, with "all" agreeing the path depends on the outlook. The signal is low-for-long without thresholds, tempered by "all participants" stressing communication about withdrawing accommodation "at the appropriate time". Score 18.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** The text says uncertainty about the forecasts "was still viewed as quite elevated", calls households' saving behavior "an important source of uncertainty", and notes participants "could not completely discount" a further substantial rise in saving. Offsetting this, "most participants" now see growth risks as roughly balanced. Score 55.
