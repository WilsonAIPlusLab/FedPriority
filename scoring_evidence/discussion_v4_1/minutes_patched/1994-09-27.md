## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58).** Participants lean hawkish but do not call for immediate tightening. Most members favored "maintaining a steady policy course, at least for the near term", and they "generally continued to feel that the recent evidence did not warrant an immediate further tightening". Against that, for many members "additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time", and the members "generally supported a shift from the symmetry in the August directive to asymmetry toward restraint". One member "favored an immediate move to somewhat greater reserve restraint" and Mr. Broaddus dissented for that reason. Some members, and a number of them on the monetary aggregates, argued for caution. This is a hold with a tightening bias, so 58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** The vote record shows one formal dissent, from Mr. Broaddus, who thought the stance "overly accommodative" and wanted a prompt move to greater restraint. Some members "expressed misgivings" about using an asymmetric directive, and "views differed with regard to the likely need for some policy tightening over the weeks immediately ahead". The differences are mainly over timing and tactics, not over the economic assessment. That fits one dissent plus a few reservations, so 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Financial conditions get attention in two places. "Many members stressed the ready availability of financing from increasingly aggressive bank lenders", and a closing paragraph says "ready availability of bank credit" gives borrowers "ample financing". Both are used as context for spending strength and for reading policy restraint. No participant raises financial stability or fragility as a concern, so this is limited discussion, 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68).** The signal is a mild-to-clear tightening bias. The Committee moved to an asymmetric directive toward restraint ("somewhat greater reserve restraint would or slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable"), and members said it "would need to act promptly and forcefully" if price pressures intensified. The near-term stance is steady and data-dependent ("information that would become available during the intermeeting period should provide a firmer basis"), which keeps the score below the clear-signal range, so 68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Several passages stress unusual difficulty: how large an inflation rise might be or when it might be reversed "was very difficult to predict"; it was "extremely difficult to evaluate whether the earlier tightening moves were exerting a lesser effect than usual or it simply was more delayed, or whether the members might have misjudged the underlying strength of the expansion"; inventory investment was "a key uncertainty". The tone is elevated but not extreme, since members still describe a baseline of slowing growth. Score 55, Moderate confidence.
