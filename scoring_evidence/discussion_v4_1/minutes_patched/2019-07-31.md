## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 40 +-10, Low).** The V4 analysis rested on a quoted phrase, "low risk spreads", attributed to several participants; that wording does not appear anywhere in the participants' views or the vote record. The new text gives no direct statement on the level of spreads. The only level evidence is indirect: several participants observed that "valuations in equity and corporate bond markets were near all-time highs", most of those commenting on financial stability "highlighted recent credit market developments", and borrowing rates were "low". Rich corporate bond valuations imply spreads below normal, so the score stays below 50 under the level rule, but with thinner evidence than before: modestly compressed, Low confidence, wider band.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 35).** Easing lean. The Committee cut 25bp (to 2 to 2 1/4 percent) with "all but two members" agreeing; most participants "viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing ... as part of a recalibration of the stance of policy, or mid-cycle adjustment". A couple preferred "a 50 basis point cut" (more dovish), while several favored holding and two voted against the cut (more hawkish), so the majority position is moderate easing with a limited, risk-management framing. Score 35.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 52).** Two formal dissents: George ("an unchanged setting of policy was appropriate") and Rosengren ("did not see a clear and compelling case for additional accommodation"), both on the hold side. In addition, a couple of participants wanted 50bp and a few worried a cut "could be misinterpreted as a negative signal". Disagreement extends to the assessment of risks (several saw risks as "diminished"; others as "elevated") and to financial stability. Distinct camps with two dissents place it in the notable-disagreement band: 52.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** A full paragraph: among those who commented, most highlighted credit market developments, elevated valuations and high corporate indebtedness; several flagged corporate debt and leveraged lending and near-record equity and corporate bond valuations; a few raised private credit and falling bank capital ratios; a few worried further accommodation "presented a risk to financial stability"; and Rosengren's dissent cites "financial stability concerns were elevated". Several participants engage meaningfully but it is not the dominant topic: 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 44).** Participants "generally favored an approach in which policy would be guided by incoming information ... and that avoided any appearance of following a preset course"; members stressed "optionality"; the statement says the Committee "will act as appropriate to sustain the expansion". The cut is framed as a mid-cycle recalibration, not a start of a long easing path, so the signal is data-dependent with a mild easing lean: 44, Moderate (little explicit forward signal).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 48).** Elevated but not extreme. Participants "generally judged that the risks associated with trade uncertainty would remain a persistent headwind", "many of the risks and uncertainties ... had remained elevated", and the vote record says "significant risks and uncertainties attending the outlook remained". Offsetting this, they called the overall outlook "favorable" and said downside risks "had diminished somewhat", and the economy had been "resilient". Score 48.
