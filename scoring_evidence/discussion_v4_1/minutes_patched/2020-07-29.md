## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants never describe spread levels or direction. The only related statements are that borrowing at Fed liquidity facilities "had stayed low, reflecting improved availability of funding from market sources", that market-functioning improvements since March were "sustained", and a conditional risk that "banks and other lenders could tighten conditions in credit markets appreciably" in adverse virus scenarios. This points to improvement from the March stress with no level information, so the score stays at 45 (side of the narrowing move, within 45-55) with wider uncertainty (+-10) and Low confidence. The old "bank lending was tight" evidence is not in the participants' text.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Strongly dovish. All participants judged it appropriate to hold the range at 0 to 1/4 percent and keep buying assets "at least at the current pace"; several participants suggested "additional accommodation could be required"; many saw "yield caps and targets" as not warranted now, a couple favored them. No participant argued for tightening. Not an emergency-easing meeting, so the score is 20, in the QE-era band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows "Voting against this action: None." Differences are minor: a couple wanted bank payout restrictions extended and another called that premature; a couple favored yield caps against most who did not. Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** A moderate share of the discussion. "A number of participants commented on various potential risks to financial stability" (bank stress in adverse scenarios, corporate indebtedness, Treasury debt and market functioning), "several participants" noted bank-resilience steps, and the financial-conditions and market-functioning theme runs through the policy section. It is a distinct paragraph rather than the dominant topic, so the score is 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Strong easing bias. The range is to be held "until they were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track" to reach the goals; "a number of participants" said greater clarity on the rate path would be appropriate at some point and discussed outcome-based thresholds (inflation, unemployment) and calendar-based guidance. This is not yet adopted, hence Moderate confidence. Score 10, in the 2020 emergency-easing band (2-10) to strong easing bias (6-15).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Very high. Participants "agreed that the path of the economy would depend on the course of the virus, which was seen as highly uncertain"; uncertainty "remained very elevated"; business contacts reported "extraordinarily high levels of uncertainty and risks"; several noted "the increase in uncertainty about the economic outlook over the intermeeting period". The language is shared by essentially all participants, but this is not the March 2020 extreme. Score 78.
