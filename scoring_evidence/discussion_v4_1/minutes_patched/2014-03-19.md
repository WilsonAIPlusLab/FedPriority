**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 30 +-8, Low).** Participants see spreads as below normal in level. A couple of participants "pointed to the decline in credit spreads to relatively low levels by historical standards", and one of them flagged the risk of a sharp rise or of a further decline that "could undermine financial stability". The level language puts the score below 50. Only a couple of participants raised it, so the score is mild (30) with Low confidence. The old quotes about leveraged loans and risk appetite are not in the input and were dropped.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 33).** Participants lean dovish. Asset purchases continue at a reduced pace, and the Committee "reaffirmed its view that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy remains appropriate". Most participants wanted qualitative guidance and nobody argued for tightening. A couple of members wanted a commitment to keep rates low if inflation stayed persistently below 2 percent, and the lone dissenter wanted more accommodation. A number of participants did worry that the higher dot-plot path could look less accommodative. The score sits above the QE-debate range because the Committee is tapering.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** There is one formal dissent: Kocherlakota dissented because the guidance "would weaken the credibility of the Committee's commitment to its inflation goal". Beyond that, one participant favored keeping the existing thresholds, another proposed new thresholds (5½ percent unemployment and 2¼ percent inflation), a few proposed inflation-floor language, and a few suggested delaying the post-liftoff language. The differences are mostly about tactics and wording, not the economic assessment, so the score is in the one-dissent band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 32).** The topic was limited and not a focus. "Several participants mentioned trends that, if continued, could become a concern from the perspective of financial stability": a couple on credit spreads, one on margin debt and equity valuations and commodity investments, another on consumer credit growth to less creditworthy households.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 22).** The signal is a mild easing bias, "low for long". The Committee anticipates keeping the target range "for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends, especially if projected inflation continues to run below" 2 percent. It also added that rates may stay "below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run". Most participants wanted an explicit statement that the guidance change implies no change in policy intentions. The numerical thresholds were dropped, which softens it from the strongest-commitment range.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 30).** Uncertainty is at the normal-to-somewhat-elevated level. Participants noted uncertainty over how much of the Q1 slowdown was weather, "a range of views regarding the amount of slack", and that "uncertainty about the trend rate of productivity growth" complicates reading GDP. A couple flagged a China slowdown, and Ukraine was seen as having "little direct effect". The Committee judged risks "nearly balanced". It is not described as unusual or deep.
