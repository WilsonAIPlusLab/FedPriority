## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 45 +-8, Low).** Participants gave no level or direction for credit spreads. The only related text is that "almost all" cited a "larger-than-anticipated easing in financial conditions" as a reason upside inflation risks had increased. That is direction only and points to looser conditions, so under the V4.1 rule the score stays on the low side of 50 (45) with Low confidence rather than Moderate.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants did not discuss bank capital, lending standards or CRE. A couple of them cited rising card and auto-loan delinquencies, and some mentioned "financial vulnerabilities stemming from overvaluation of risky assets". Neither is a view on the banking system, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 45 +-6, Moderate).** The action was a 25bp cut to 4.25-4.50%, supported by "the vast majority", which is mildly dovish. The forward lean is cautious: participants said the Committee was "at or near the point" to "slow the pace of policy easing". "A majority" called the decision "finely balanced", "some" saw "merit in keeping the target range... unchanged", and "many" said the Committee could "hold the policy rate at a restrictive level". Against that, "several" said easing could be faster if conditions deteriorated. Hammack dissented in favor of a hold. Net: an easing action with a hawkish tilt in the forward lean, so lean dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38 +-6, High).** One formal dissent (Hammack, preferring to hold at 4.50-4.75%, citing "uneven progress" on inflation and "the state of financial conditions"). Beyond that, "a majority" found the decision "finely balanced" and "some" saw merit in holding. Participants agreed on the assessment ("members concurred" on solid growth and somewhat elevated inflation), so the differences are mostly about pace and risk. That fits the 31-45 band (one dissent plus several reservations).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 30 +-8, Moderate).** Financial conditions and vulnerabilities come up as context, not as a focus. "Almost all" cited easing in financial conditions as an upside inflation risk. "A majority" noted positive equity sentiment. Participants listed "overvaluation of risky assets" as a downside risk. "A couple" cited delinquencies, and "several" of those favoring a hold stressed fostering suitably tight financial conditions. This is limited discussion, with several participants touching on it.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 45 +-8, Moderate).** The signal is a shift to a slower pace. The statement language is "carefully assess incoming data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks", and participants said decisions "were not on a preset course". "Most" said the Committee could take a "careful approach"; "many" said it could hold or ease more slowly if inflation stayed elevated; "several" said it could ease faster if the labor market or activity weakened. The direction is still gradual movement toward neutral, with two-sided conditionality, so it sits below 50 but close to it.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55 +-7, High).** "All participants" judged uncertainty about trade and immigration policy "was elevated", and participants "took varied approaches" in accounting for it. "Many" said the "current high degree of uncertainty" called for a gradual approach. "Several" cited "considerable uncertainty" about the labor market outlook. The Committee statement describes the outlook as "uncertain". Elevated, but not framed as extreme or unbounded.
