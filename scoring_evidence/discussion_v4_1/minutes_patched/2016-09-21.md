## Evidence for changed dimensions

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants did not assess the banking system. The only related passage is a few participants' concern that very low rates "might be encouraging excessive borrowing and increased leverage in the nonfinancial corporate sector", which is a risk-taking concern outside banks. The old score of 38 inferred a sound banking system with no supporting text, so the dimension is N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** No participant comments on the dollar or exchange rate. The old 50 came from background context rather than the participants' views. The word "dollar" appears only in the directive's "dollar roll and coupon swap transactions". N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 56).** The Committee held at 1/4 to 1/2 percent, but participants "generally agreed that the case for increasing the target range... had strengthened". Many saw slack and, with inflation below 2 percent, a majority favored waiting for "further evidence"; "some other participants" and "several members" wanted a hike relatively soon, and three preferred a 25bp increase at this meeting. This is a hold with a tightening bias, with a hawkish minority large enough to dissent; score 56, lean hawkish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 60).** Three formal dissents (George, Mester, Rosengren), all for an immediate 25bp hike, with a couple citing credibility risk. The disagreement extends to the assessment of slack ("some" saw "little or no remaining slack", "some others" saw slack remaining), yet the dissenters share one prescription and the tone is measured ("a close call", "a reasonable argument could be made either" way). Score 60, upper end of the 46-60 band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Limited attention: MMF reform effects were expected to have "only a small effect" on borrowing costs, "a few participants" worried about leverage in the nonfinancial corporate sector, and "one participant" raised the pension and endowment saving channel. Conditions were called "broadly accommodative" in passing. These are context rather than a focus; score 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** The statement signals that "the case for an increase in the federal funds rate has strengthened", and "several members" saw it as pointing to a hike "relatively soon" if developments unfold as expected. This is tempered by "only gradual increases", a funds rate likely to stay "below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run", and a few members stressing data dependence. Mild tightening bias; score 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 35).** "A substantial majority" saw near-term risks as "roughly balanced", with Brexit risks receding for several; "a few" still saw downside risks. Several noted uncertainty over how long r* would stay low, and the longer-run unemployment rate was called uncertain. Standard caveats with some elevated areas; score 35.
