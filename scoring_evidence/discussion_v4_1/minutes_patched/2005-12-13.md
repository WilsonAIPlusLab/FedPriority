## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 52).** A number of participants saw the economy as possibly producing "in the neighborhood of its potential", with persistent spending strength suggesting resource markets "could tighten further". Near-potential language fixes the score in 48-52; the tightening risk puts it at the top of the band.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 51, Low).** Participants do not cite the unemployment rate or occupational shortages in the rebuilt text. The only evidence is indirect: employment and income advances expected to support consumption, and "resource markets could tighten further" alongside near-potential output. This points to a labor market near full employment, slightly firm, scored 51 with Low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old quote on corporate bond spreads being "about unchanged" does not appear in the participants' views. Participants mention "supportive financial conditions" only for the foreign economy and "favorable financial conditions" generally, so spreads were not discussed. N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old quote on equity price gains is not in the participants' text. The only reference is the general effect of favorable financial conditions "on asset prices and aggregate demand", with no valuation view on equities. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 56, Low).** The only dollar reference is "the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar owing to global demands for dollar assets", mentioned as a modest offset to foreign growth supporting exports. Appreciation puts the score above 50; the brief, incidental mention supports a modest 56 with Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 62).** All members favored a 25bp increase to 4-1/4 percent and members "generally anticipated" further firming. Most saw the number of additional steps as "probably ... not large", and some thought "measured" was no longer necessary. This is a moderately hawkish lean (consensus tightening, tempered forward lean).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 17).** The vote was unanimous with no dissents. Minor differences: "views differed on how much further tightening might be required" and "some members" thought "measured" unnecessary. Broad agreement with small emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 20).** Only passing attention: the "sustained period of favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand", a possible housing "downshift in attitudes" damping demand by "investors and speculators", and "supportive financial conditions" abroad. Limited discussion, not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** The statement says "some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed", policy "should no longer be characterized as accommodative", and most members read it as implying that additional steps "probably would not be large", with action dependent on data. Mild tightening bias with a limited path.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** Members thought "the policy outlook was becoming considerably less certain" with decisions depending more on data, and market effects of the statement changes were "especially difficult to judge". Otherwise standard caveats and a confident description of the expansion. Somewhat elevated.
