## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 42 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see equity valuations as modestly above fair value but not stretched. "A couple of participants" said favorable macro factors backed current valuations and that, with no greater investor leverage, the rise "might not pose appreciable risks to financial stability". "Several" linked the equity rise and low yields to easing financial conditions, and "elevated asset prices" is mentioned. Score 42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' views never address the dollar or exchange rates. The only international remark is the "brighter international economic outlook" boosting exports. The old 35 used outside knowledge, so the dimension becomes N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 53).** The Committee held at 1 to 1 1/4 percent. Participants "reaffirmed" a gradual approach to removing accommodation, and the vote record says conditions "would warrant gradual increases". "Some participants" argued against further adjustments until inflation firmed, while "some other participants" worried about full employment and easing conditions and cautioned that delay could cost them. "Most" preferred to defer the balance-sheet start date, although "several" were ready to announce it. This is a mild tightening bias. Score 53.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 30).** The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None"). Two camps were visible on the timing of the next increase, and "several" versus "most" differed on announcing the balance-sheet start date. A few participants questioned the Phillips-curve framework that "most" kept using. These are differences of emphasis and pace, not formal dissent. Score 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 55).** Participants "considered equity valuations in their discussion of financial stability". "Several" noted that banks were well capitalized, and "participants underscored the need to monitor" lending standards and funding. "A couple" raised CRE, multifamily and GSE risks. "Participants agreed" that post-crisis regulation mattered. "Some other participants" tied the timing of policy to financial-stability risks. This is substantial attention, though not dominant. Score 55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 60).** The agreed language expects economic conditions to "warrant gradual increases in the federal funds rate", with the rate staying below longer-run levels "for some time". Balance sheet normalization is to begin "relatively soon". The path is data dependent, and the Committee will "carefully monitor" inflation. This is a mild tightening bias. Score 60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 32).** "Some participants" noted "increased uncertainty about the outlook for inflation". "Several" said federal policy uncertainty weighed on spending and hiring, and the size of the term-premium effects was "uncertain". Yet "most" were confident inflation would stabilize near 2 percent, and the vote record says risks were "roughly balanced". Score 32.
