## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants gave no statement of the level of credit spreads. The old score of 58 treated European stress and reduced risk-taking as implied wide spreads, which is an inference and not participants' words. The only level-type language is that "credit costs were low" (below normal) in the business paragraph, versus "still-tight credit conditions for many households and small businesses" (availability, not spreads) and worse market liquidity. Mixed and indirect, so the score sits just below the midpoint (48) with Low confidence, within the 45-55 band for no level information.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants lean moderately dovish but are not pushing for new action. "A few members indicated that they believed the economic outlook might warrant additional policy accommodation," but most of these agreed to retain the current stance, and one member (Evans) dissented for more easing. "A few members" could support the decision "even though they had not favored recent policy actions," a small hawkish-leaning minority. All members agreed to continue the maturity extension program. Score 40.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (Evans, wanting more accommodation now), a few participants favoring more easing but deferring to a future communications initiative, and a few reluctant supporters from the other side. Economic assessments are largely shared ("participants generally agreed"). Typical one-dissent meeting with some reservations. Score 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants spend a full paragraph on volatile markets, worsened liquidity, European sovereign and bank vulnerabilities, U.S. bank health and MF Global, and list spillovers to U.S. financial markets as a main downside risk; "it was also noted" that prolonged accommodation could build financial imbalances. Several participants address it meaningfully, but the economy still leads the discussion. Score 55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The existing date-based guidance ("exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013") was retained, "members generally preferred to retain the existing forward guidance." "A few members" wanted period-based rather than date-based wording, and Evans wanted more explicit conditional guidance, both in an easing direction. The Committee also stated it is "prepared to adjust" holdings to promote recovery. Strong easing commitment; not scored at the very bottom because guidance was only reiterated, not strengthened. Score 7.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants saw "significant downside risks," with the European plan's "many details...not yet worked out" and "a number of important issues remained unresolved"; business contacts were "cautious and uncertain about the economic and political outlook." The structural-versus-cyclical unemployment question remained open and a few saw upside growth risk. Inflation risks were seen by "a number of participants" as "roughly balanced." Elevated, but with a stable central outlook ("most probable outcome was a moderate pace"). Score 55.
