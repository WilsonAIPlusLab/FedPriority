## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 51 +-6, High).** Participants see spending near trend now but with weakening ahead. "Participants observed that consumer spending grew solidly in March" and business fixed investment "was solid in the first quarter"; offsetting this, surveys show a "sharp deterioration in consumer sentiment" (though "several" say sentiment has not predicted spending) and "many participants remarked" that firms were "pausing or delaying their capital expenditure plans". Net is about at trend: 51.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, N/A).** The new input contains no participant comment on productivity. The old score came from non-participant context.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 55 +-8, Low).** Participants say conditions are somewhat tighter, though not tight. "Some participants discussed the heightened volatility seen across a range of asset markets", with markets still functioning "despite lower measures of liquidity". "Several" judge balance sheets "solid". There is no explicit tight or loose characterization, so the score is modestly above neutral at Low confidence.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED, 48 +-10, Low).** Participants see orderly functioning under strain. "Markets had continued to function and were able to accommodate a surge in trading volumes despite lower measures of liquidity", and "several" stress that Treasury resilience "was of special importance". No dysfunction is described, and the discussion is brief, so the old value of 48 is kept on new evidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old 57 was built on "spreads widened" from staff text, a direction-only reading with no level information. Participants in the new input mention only "potential concerns about private credit", not spreads or credit availability. N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 45 +-10, Low).** Participants see prices as possibly still above fundamentals. "While noting that asset prices had declined somewhat, several participants observed that downside risks to the outlook had increased, leading them to question whether asset prices had actually gotten closer to fundamental valuations." Prices fell but valuation concern is not resolved, which implies modestly above fair value (31-45 band, upper end). Brief discussion, so Low.

**BANKING_STRESS (FILLED, 37 +-8, Low).** Participants see banks as sound, with a caveat. "Balance sheet conditions for households, nonfinancial businesses, and banks appeared to be solid", while "several participants noted that an economic downturn or higher interest rates could lead to a deterioration". Bright-line (solid means below 50) satisfied. Banks are not discussed separately, so Low.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 35 +-10, Low).** Participants note depreciation without sizing it. "Some participants commented on ... the dollar depreciating despite the decline in the prices of equities", and said a "diminution of the perceived safe-haven status of U.S. assets" could matter. Depreciation means below 50, but without magnitude the score is only modestly below neutral.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant discusses services prices; the old evidence was general context. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED, 47).** "Several participants" list "reduced housing inflation pressures from lower immigration" as a mitigating factor, pointing to easing housing pressure. Single passing mention, so only slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 57).** Short-term expectations have risen ("recent increase in short-term inflation expectations") while "most indicators" show longer-term expectations "well anchored". "Some participants saw the risk that they could drift upward", and participants "emphasized the importance of ensuring" anchoring. Slightly above neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50 +-5, High).** Balanced. "All participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range", the Committee "well positioned to wait for more clarity". "Almost all participants" flag persistent-inflation risk, while risks of higher unemployment also rose. No easing or tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8 +-4, High).** Near-consensus. Vote record shows no dissent ("Voting against this action: None"), with "all participants" agreeing on the hold and on risks, so the score sits in the near-consensus band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 38 +-8, Moderate).** One dedicated paragraph, with "some" and "several" participants on volatility, Treasury market resilience, asset correlations, valuations, hedge fund leverage and private credit. Real but not a dominant topic relative to the tariff and economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50 +-5, High).** Neutral, data-dependent. "Well positioned to wait for more clarity" and "take a cautious approach", the Committee "prepared to adjust the stance of monetary policy as appropriate", and the "extent and timing of additional adjustments" is not tilted either way.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 72 +-6, High).** High. "Participants saw the uncertainty about their economic outlooks as unusually elevated"; "considerable uncertainty" on trade policy, additional uncertainty on fiscal, regulatory and immigration policy; and the "ultimate extent of changes to government policy ... was highly uncertain". All participants, across several domains, but short of "uncharted territory".
