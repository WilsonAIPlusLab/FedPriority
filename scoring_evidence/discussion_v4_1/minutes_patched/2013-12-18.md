## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants did not describe credit spreads. The old evidence was equity-related (small-cap forward P/E, equity repurchases, margin credit), and the "compressed spreads" reading was an inference, not text. The only credit-market items are one participant noting "the increase in issuance of leveraged loans this year and the apparent decline in the average quality of such loans", plus the general view that credit conditions are easing ("a further easing of credit conditions"). That supports credit somewhat looser than normal (below 50) but not the 16-30 band. Score 38, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Mildly dovish to balanced. "Most participants saw a reduction in the pace of purchases as appropriate"; "many" favored a modest initial reduction with future cuts data-dependent, and "some" preferred a larger reduction (hawkish side). On the other side, "some" questioned slowing "when inflation was running well below" the objective, "for some" slack and low inflation warranted continuing at the current pace, and the vote record shows Rosengren calling the taper "premature". The agreed outcome pairs a modest $10 billion cut with stronger easing guidance, so QE continues at $75 billion monthly. Score 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (9-1), disagreement over the pace of tapering and, for "a few", the unemployment threshold; "one member objected" to the guidance wording as potentially inflexible. Differences are on tactics and risk weighting, not a broad split on the economy. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants "reviewed indicators of financial vulnerabilities", concluding that risks "were moderate"; "several" raised small-cap P/E, equity repurchases and margin credit, "one" leveraged loans, and "a couple" discussed incorporating financial stability into policy and forward guidance. A distinct but modest part of the discussion, above the typical 2013 level of attention. Score 38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Strongly easing, with a threshold-based commitment retained and reinforced. "Most were inclined to retain the current thresholds"; "almost all members agreed" to add that the target range will be maintained "well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6½ percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below" 2 percent; policy "highly accommodative ... for a considerable time". Partly offset by the signal of further reductions "in measured steps". Score 12.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Low to normal. "Most expressed greater confidence in the outlook and saw the risks ... as more nearly balanced"; budget accord prospects were "reducing uncertainty". Caveats: "several" stressed that "the projected pickup in economic growth was not assured", and some were unsure about labor-force participation and inflation persistence. Score 22.
