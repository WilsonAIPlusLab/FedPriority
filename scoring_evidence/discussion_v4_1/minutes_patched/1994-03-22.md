## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** Participants mention "sharp changes in bond and, to a lesser extent, equity prices" and "recent declines in the value of securities held by households" (as a limit on consumers), without valuation commentary. Under the rubric default for price moves without valuation views, score 53, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED).** The only banking-related participant language is "more accommodative lending policies of many depository institutions" (a number of members), which describes credit availability rather than bank health. Banking system health is not specifically discussed, so N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** The only support in the new input is the directive statement that the trade-weighted dollar "depreciated over the intermeeting period", plus a passing reference to "influences from foreign exchange" markets. Direction is depreciation, so below 50; kept at 32 with Low confidence and a wider band.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** "All the members supported a further move toward a less accommodative policy stance" and viewed policy "as too stimulative"; "many" said rates must rise by a relatively sizable amount but "a majority" preferred another small move; a few urged caution; two dissenters wanted a stronger move. Moderately hawkish with a tightening consensus: 68.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Two formal dissents (Messrs. Broaddus and Jordan) wanted "a stronger move toward a more neutral policy stance"; "views differed on how much further" to move, and some members had reservations about immediate announcement. Differences are about pace and tactics, same direction, shared economic assessment: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Members discussed the bond market backup, volatility, and a low-probability risk that "weakness and volatility in financial markets" could inhibit confidence; "many were concerned about a possible overreaction in financial markets"; the press-statement rationale cites market overreaction. Several participants, but secondary to the economic and inflation outlook: 38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Members concluded policy "needed to move fairly quickly toward ... a more neutral position", which was "still some distance away", with further tightening beyond that depending on inflation. Against that, a majority preferred a symmetric directive and did not see further firming as very likely during the intermeeting period. Clear but conditional tightening direction: 70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** The resources that could be mobilized were "subject to substantial uncertainty"; markets were at a time of "considerable uncertainty and volatility"; a few raised the chance of a sharper slowdown. Most other language is standard caveats: 40.
