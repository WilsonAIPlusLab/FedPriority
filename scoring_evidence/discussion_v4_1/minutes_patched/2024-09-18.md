## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 47 relied on a sound banking system, CRE risks and private credit, none of which appear in the participants' views or the vote record in the new input. Participants say nothing about spreads, credit pricing or credit availability (the only credit references are household credit card and auto loan delinquencies, which bear on consumption, not spreads). N/A with N/A uncertainty and confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quotes ("banking system was sound", unrealized losses, uninsured deposits) are absent from the new input; the banking system is not discussed by participants. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 33 +-6, High).** Participants lean dovish: "all participants agreed that it was appropriate to ease the stance of monetary policy", and "a substantial majority" supported a 50bp cut as a "recalibration". "Some" would have preferred 25bp and "a few" could have supported it, and Governor Bowman dissented for 25bp, which pulls slightly toward the middle, but the action is a large cut and most see downside employment risks as having increased. Moderate dovish, 26-40 band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38 +-7, High).** The vote record shows "One member voted against", Governor Bowman, who cited core inflation "well above the Committee's objective" and a "premature declaration of victory". "Some participants" also would have preferred 25bp, and "several" saw 25bp as fitting a gradual path. Most of the difference is tactical (size and pace), but Bowman's rationale reaches the inflation assessment. One dissent plus several reservations, so the moderate band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12 +-5, Moderate).** Financial stability and conditions get only passing references: "some participants" cited the risk of a "larger-than-anticipated easing in financial conditions", and "a couple" mentioned strains on low- and moderate-income households including card and auto-loan delinquencies. No discussion of financial-system vulnerabilities. Brief mentions only.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 38 +-7, Moderate).** Easing lean with data dependence. Participants "anticipated that ... it would likely be appropriate to move toward a more neutral stance of policy over time" if data come in as expected, but stressed that the move should not signal faster easing and that decisions are "conditional ... and therefore not on a preset course". "Some" favored reducing restraint "gradually". The statement says the Committee will "carefully assess incoming data". Mild easing direction, conditional.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 35 +-7, Moderate).** Members "viewed the economic outlook as uncertain". "Many participants" found labor-market evaluation "challenging" (immigration, payroll revisions, productivity), and "some" cited uncertainty about the longer-term neutral rate. Against that, "almost all" had "greater confidence" in the inflation path and risks were seen as "roughly in balance". Somewhat elevated, standard caveats plus specific measurement difficulties.
