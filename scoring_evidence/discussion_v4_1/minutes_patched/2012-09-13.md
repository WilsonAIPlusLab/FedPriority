## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 20).** Participants lean strongly dovish. The vote record says "all members but one agreed that the outlook for economic activity and inflation called for additional monetary accommodation", delivered through $40 billion per month of agency MBS purchases with a conditional commitment to "undertake additional asset purchases". "Many participants anticipated" that a new large-scale asset purchase program would support recovery, and "most participants thought these risks could be managed". The hawkish side is a small minority: "a few" skeptical of the benefit, "several" concerned about exit difficulty, and one dissenter. QE-debate era, so 15-25; scored 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** One formal dissent: Mr. Lacker, who opposed additional stimulus (unlikely to improve growth "without also causing an unwanted increase in inflation"), opposed MBS purchases, and preferred to omit the calendar date. Beyond that, "some participants" were less confident of the efficacy of additional purchases and "a few expressed skepticism", and participants differed over numerical thresholds ("some were reluctant"). The Committee otherwise agreed on the action. This is a typical one-dissent meeting with some substantive reservations on efficacy, so 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 40).** Moderate attention. Participants discussed improved sentiment after ECB decisions, "significant risks related to the euro-area banking and fiscal crisis", and that "indicators of financial stress in the United States were not especially high". Specific stability concerns came from single participants: "few current signs of excessive risk-taking" but low rates could "lead to financial imbalances", and "excessive risk-taking on the part of some investors" from extended accommodation. The vote record also cites "strains in global financial conditions" as downside risk. It is a sizable but not dominant topic, so 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 5).** The strongest easing commitment: a date-based extension "through mid-2015" together with language that a highly accommodative stance "will remain appropriate for a considerable time after the economic recovery strengthens". The participants' paragraph shows "many participants" preferring economic thresholds over the date, and "most participants agreed that the use of numerical thresholds could be useful". Both routes point to prolonged near-zero rates (threshold-based and date-based guidance, rubric range 1-5), so 5.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Elevated. "Many participants also noted that a high level of uncertainty regarding the European fiscal and banking crisis and the outlook for U.S. fiscal and regulatory policies was weighing on confidence", business contacts were "highly uncertain", and "a number of participants highlighted the uncertainty about the overall effects of additional purchases". "Significant downside risks" were seen, although "most participants saw the risks to the outlook for inflation as roughly balanced" and "others questioned" the role of policy uncertainty, which tempers the level. Scored 55.
