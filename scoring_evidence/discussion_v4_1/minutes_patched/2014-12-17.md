## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 44).** Participants see activity expanding at a moderate pace, with labor underutilization diminishing but not eliminated; "some participants" saw "more labor market slack than was indicated by the unemployment rate alone", and participants expected activity to move labor indicators "toward levels consistent with" maximum employment. No participant language places output above potential, and the old "faster than potential" phrase is absent from the input. Slightly below potential, 44, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 38).** Participants said policy "will still be highly accommodative for a time after the first increase", and the Committee statement says holdings "should help maintain accommodative financial conditions". Markets were influenced by foreign growth concerns and "very low term premiums". Accommodative side of 50; the old cited passage is missing, so confidence falls to Low. Score unchanged at 38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 58).** Participants generally expected inflation to decline "reflecting the reduction in oil prices and the effects of the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar on import prices", and noted that "the stronger dollar" would keep inflation below target. Appreciation is acknowledged but not described as large or as a major restraint, so modest strength, 58, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 47).** The Committee maintained 0 to 1/4 percent; "most participants" agreed to state it "can be patient", and most read this as meaning no liftoff for "at least the next couple of meetings". "Some participants" worried the language concentrated expectations around mid-2015. Dissents ran both ways (Fisher and Plosser wanted earlier liftoff; Kocherlakota wanted more accommodation). Hold with a mild easing/balanced tilt, 47.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 70).** Vote 7 to 3. Fisher dissented because the outlook warranted a sooner increase; Plosser wanted guidance "more data dependent" and a measurable chance of first-quarter liftoff; Kocherlakota said inflation credibility "was at risk", calling for more accommodation. Three formal dissents from opposite directions with distinct reasoning, but no heated exchanges reported, so 70.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 30).** Participants discussed asset-price moves, term premiums and inflation-compensation premiums in one paragraph, and "many participants" flagged downside risk if oil and foreign weakness "had a substantial negative effect on global financial markets". Plosser mentioned risks of "volatility and instability" from late tightening. Limited, context-level attention, 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 40).** The "considerable time" link to the end of asset purchases was replaced by "patient in beginning to normalize", seen as giving more flexibility and consistent with the earlier statement. Timing "would depend on the incoming data". Participants "emphasized that policy will still be highly accommodative for a time after the first increase", and rates may stay below normal "for some time". Normalization is flagged but with an easing-leaning, data-dependent posture, 40.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 38).** Risks to activity were "nearly balanced"; "many" saw the international situation as an important downside risk; participants "agreed that it would take more time and analysis" on inflation compensation; members cited "uncertainties about the outlook for inflation". Somewhat elevated but not unusual, 38.
