## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 43 cited "stock market indexes were modestly higher amid favorable economic news and lower oil prices". That sentence is not in the participants' views in the new input, and the old input did not contain it either (the diff shows nothing only in the old input). The only equity-adjacent text is "merger and acquisition activity was strong", "infusions of private equity continued at a rapid pace" and a "quite weak" IPO market. That describes activity, not a valuation or concern view. Participants did not address equity valuation, so N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 55 cited "trade-weighted foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated slightly". This is not in the new input. Participants mention only "foreign economic growth appeared to have strengthened" and "firming of monetary policy by several foreign central banks", and that imports would outpace exports. They say nothing on the dollar's direction, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58).** Lean hawkish. All members favored a 25 bp hike to 4 3/4 percent because "keeping rates unchanged would run an unacceptable risk of rising inflation", and "checking upside risks to inflation was important". This is tempered on the forward side: "most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near" and "some expressed concerns about the dangers of tightening too much". A 25 bp hike with a likely-near end sits below the moderate-hawkish band, hence 58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 17).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows eleven votes for and "Vote against this action: None". The action was supported by "all members". Disagreement was limited to "some difference in views about the appropriate level of detail" in the statement and "some members" worried the risk-assessment phrase could be misconstrued. These are reservations on communication, not on policy or economics.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** Limited. Financial conditions get one paragraph, framed as "supportive of growth" with narrow spreads and banks lending, plus a remark on mortgage-rate effects on housing and an "abrupt rise in long-term interest rates" risk. No participants raise financial stability concerns, and the attention is context for the growth outlook.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 58).** Mild tightening bias with strong data dependence. The vote record approves the statement paragraph "some further policy firming may be needed", and "the need for further policy firming would be determined by the implications of incoming information". "Several members" worried markets would not appreciate the data dependence "especially when an end to the tightening process seems likely to be near", and "some members" feared the phrase could suggest "several further tightening steps". The signal is therefore a modest tightening bias, not a clear tightening commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 35).** Somewhat elevated. Participants "saw both upside and downside risks"; the housing adjustment's "magnitude... and its effects on household spending were hard to predict"; and "participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained". The sustainable potential "could not be observed directly or estimated with precision". These are standard-plus caveats, not unusual uncertainty.
