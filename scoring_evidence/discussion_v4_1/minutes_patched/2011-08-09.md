## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 58 -> 55).** Participants see conditions as somewhat tighter than neutral. The text says markets were "volatile," that there were "broad declines in stock prices," a "reduced willingness of investors to bear risk," and that "conditions in funding markets had tightened"; several participants cited "still-tight credit conditions." Offsetting this, interest rates fell and U.S. bank condition "had strengthened." The old "less accommodative" and "risk spreads had widened appreciably" language does not appear in the new input, so the score drops to 55 (> 50, bright line satisfied).

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 62 -> 54, Low).** The text gives no spread level and no explicit spread direction. The indirect indications are a "reduced willingness of investors to bear risk," tightened funding markets, and several participants citing "still-tight credit conditions" for households. Under the V4.1 rule (direction only, no level), the score is 45-55 on the side of the move, so 54 with Low confidence. The old High-confidence 62 rested on a quote not in the input.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 48 -> 40).** Participants described the "condition of U.S. banks" as having "strengthened in recent quarters" and credit quality of businesses and households as having "continued to improve" (sound -> < 50). Funding markets "had tightened," which keeps the score above the healthiest range. The old reference to pressure on large U.S. banks' stock prices and CDS spreads is not in the new input. Score 40.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 48 -> 50).** Participants said "measures of inflation expectations had remained stable," and the Committee noted that longer-term expectations "remained stable." Most members expected inflation to settle at or below mandate-consistent levels. Anchored near target, so 50. The old TIPS deflation-probability sentence is not in the new input.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Moderately dovish. "Most members agreed that the economic outlook had deteriorated by enough to warrant a Committee response," "some members" wanted additional accommodation, and "a few members" wanted a more substantial move; participants also discussed asset purchases, maturity extension and the IOER as further tools. The three dissenters and "some participants" doubting that any tool would help are a hawkish counterweight, but the adopted action was easing via stronger guidance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 70).** Three formal dissents (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser), each with distinct reasoning: Fisher on nonmonetary factors and information, Kocherlakota that inflation had risen and unemployment fallen, Plosser that the statement was excessively negative. Disagreement extends to the assessment and to whether any tool would help, but the vote dispute is confined to the guidance wording and there is no description of sharp exchanges, so below the top of the range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 35).** Limited-to-moderate attention: one paragraph on volatile markets, tightened funding markets and U.S. bank strength, plus a few mentions (financial market strains cited by "some," a reduction in IOER to ease financial conditions by "a few," Fisher's concern about appearing responsive to volatility). The discussion is dominated by the growth and policy-tool debate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 5).** Strongest easing direction. The Committee agreed to state that exceptionally low rates are likely warranted "at least through mid-2013," and "most members" judged this conditional date-based expectation useful guidance; the discussion also covered strengthening guidance as an easing tool. Date-based commitment to near-zero rates matches the 1-5 band; the conditional wording and one member's credibility concern keep it from the very bottom.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 64).** High. "Participants generally saw the degree of uncertainty surrounding the outlook for economic growth as having risen appreciably"; "the underlying strength of the economic recovery remained uncertain"; "a couple" said the reasons for weaker cyclical impetus were unclear; "many" saw increased downside risks and "several" noted vulnerability to shocks including Europe.
