## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** The old evidence quote is not in the participants' views, so the old score of 35 had no support from that quote. In the new text participants cite that "market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low" while "survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations were little changed". Several participants said inflation returning to 2 percent "would help ensure that longer-term inflation expectations remained consistent" with the objective, and a few feared downward pressures that "could continue to suppress inflation". Expectations are read as slightly below target without a drift or an explicit concern about them being too low. Score 42, Moderate confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants favored holding at 1 1/2 to 1 3/4 percent. They judged that "the current degree of monetary policy accommodation would be useful in facilitating a return of inflation to 2 percent", several welcomed inflation "modestly exceeding 2 percent for a period", and a few said the labor market might still be below maximum employment. A couple of participants worried that tight labor markets have been linked with "economic and financial imbalances". Net: hold with a mild dovish tilt, scored 45.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote record reads "Voting against this action: None." with all ten voters in favor. Differences are limited to emphasis: a few wanted the Committee to be more explicit about achieving its inflation goal, several versus a couple on overshooting and imbalances, and several skeptical of the trade deal's effect. Near-consensus with minor emphasis differences, scored 15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** The discussion has its own paragraph. Several participants observed that "equity, corporate debt, and CRE valuations were elevated" and noted high corporate indebtedness and weak leveraged loan underwriting. Some raised overvaluation, excessive indebtedness, and cyber risk. Several raised the decline in bank capital buffers. A couple tied tight labor markets to imbalances. This is moderate attention (several participants, one paragraph) that does not rival the economic assessment. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Participants viewed the stance as "likely to remain appropriate for a time, provided that incoming information ... remained broadly consistent", with an adjustment "if developments emerged that led to a material reassessment of the outlook". The postmeeting statement now describes policy as consistent with inflation "returning to" the symmetric 2 percent objective and refers to "muted inflation pressures". This is a neutral, data-dependent posture with a slight easing flavor, scored 45.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants said "uncertainties about the outlook remained, including those posed by the outbreak of the coronavirus", expected trade uncertainty to remain "somewhat elevated", and were "mindful of the possibility that the tentative signs of stabilization in global growth could fade". The risk distribution was seen as "somewhat more favorable than at the previous meeting". Standard caveats plus a new risk, somewhat elevated but not unusual. Score 36.
