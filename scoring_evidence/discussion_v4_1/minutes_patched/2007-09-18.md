## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 66).** Participants see conditions as clearly tighter than normal: the financial shock "could well lead to a more general tightening of credit availability", markets "while seeming to have improved somewhat ... were still fragile", the "impaired functioning of financial markets might persist", and risk spreads and credit terms would remain tighter than a few months ago. Subprime mortgages were "essentially unavailable" and lower-rated issuance "slowed sharply". Offsets: investment-grade access "remained unimpeded", conforming mortgage rates had declined. Substantially tight but not crisis-level in their language; 66.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only support in the old analysis was staff description of bill yields and bill-market trading. Participants' views contain no Treasury-market discussion (the interbank/libor comment is not Treasury). N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 55, Low).** Participants say "risk spreads probably would be wider and credit terms tighter than they had been a few months ago" and that lower-rated financing slowed sharply, while investment-grade access was unimpeded. This is direction (widening, from a compressed starting point) with no stated level, so the V4.1 rule gives 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence; 55.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old score rested on a staff statement about stock indexes. No participant comment on equity markets or valuation in the new input. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 35, Low).** The staff report of dollar decline is not a participant view. Participants only note that "inflation risks could be heightened if the dollar were to continue to depreciate significantly", implying ongoing weakening of unspecified size, and a precautionary demand for dollar funding in Europe. Below 50 by the bright-line rule, but modest and thinly evidenced; 35, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 25).** All members favored easing and agreed a 50 basis point cut was "the most prudent course of action", to offset tighter financial conditions and forestall "significant broader weakness in output and employment"; easing seen as unlikely to hurt inflation. Strong, unanimous dovish action without an emergency or QE signal; 25.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12).** Vote record shows ten votes for and none against; the text says "all members" favored easing and agreed on 50 basis points. Only minor emphasis differences (for example "some participants" on consumer weakness). Near-consensus; 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 74).** Participants "focused on the potential for recent credit market developments to restrain aggregate demand"; most of the account concerns bank balance sheets, funding strains, fragile markets and impaired market functioning, and the easing rationale is explicitly about tighter financial conditions. Prominent, near-dominant topic in an early-crisis meeting; 74.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 42).** The approved statement says the Committee "will act as needed to foster price stability and sustainable economic growth"; members declined to characterize the balance of risks, and "future actions would depend on" market developments and prospects. Data-dependent with downside growth risks and a mild easing tilt, partly offset by the retained statement that "some inflation risks remained"; 42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 68).** Participants "regarded the outlook ... as characterized by particularly high uncertainty", incoming data were "of limited value", and the Committee omitted a balance-of-risks assessment so as not to imply it was "more certain about the economic outlook than was in fact the case". Broadly emphasized and genuine, but short of deep-crisis language; 68.
