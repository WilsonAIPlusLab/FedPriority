## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 42 +-10, Low).** The old accommodative quotes and the repo-operations evidence do not appear in the new input. Participants indirectly point to somewhat easy conditions: residential investment helped by "lower mortgage rates", and "a few participants" worried that "keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking". Against that, "a few" noted "tight financing conditions for energy-producing firms". Net slightly loose, 42, with Low confidence because no participant characterizes overall conditions.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The valuation and "overvaluation" quotes are not in the new input. Participants do not discuss equity prices or valuations, only "excessive risk-taking" and "imbalances in the financial sector" (a few participants). N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The dividend, capital-buffer and "Capital levels ... quite high" quotes are not in the new input. Banks and bank capital are not discussed by participants. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 45).** Participants judged it "appropriate to maintain the target range" and regarded the stance as "likely to remain appropriate for a time"; "participants generally expressed concerns regarding inflation continuing to fall short of 2 percent", and a number saw holding as cushioning against global developments. A few raised low-rate risk-taking concerns, which does not make a tightening lean. Hold with a mild easing bias to balanced, 45.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 13).** The vote record shows ten votes for and "Voting against this action: None". Minor differences: "a few members" preferred language about returning inflation to the symmetric 2 percent objective, while "other members" saw "near" as covering modest deviations. Broad consensus, 13.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 25).** "A few participants raised the concern that keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking", with various perspectives (a more severe next recession, macroprudential tools). Limited, minority attention treated as context rather than the focus of the discussion, 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 44).** The stance was seen as "likely to remain appropriate for a time as long as incoming information ... remained broadly consistent with the economic outlook", but "if developments emerged that led to a material reassessment of the outlook, the stance of policy would need to adjust" (no direction given). The vote record retains "global developments and muted inflation pressures" as factors the Committee will monitor. Patient, data-dependent, with a slight easing tilt, 44.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 33).** Participants cite "persistent uncertainty regarding international trade", new trade uncertainties (Argentina, Brazil, France) and Hong Kong tensions, and "many saw the risks as tilted somewhat to the downside". Offsetting this, "some risks were seen to have eased", recession-probability models had fallen, and members agreed "the text on uncertainties about the outlook could be removed". Somewhat elevated, but near normal caveats, 33.
