## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE (CORRECTED, 20 to 25).** Participants see inflation as well below the midpoint ("already low level", forecasts of "quite low inflation rates" of 1-1/4 to 1-1/2 percent), with "some further disinflation" possible and "concern that inflation could be approaching a level that would begin to complicate the implementation of monetary policy". But "a substantial further decline in inflation was viewed as having a low probability" and at least some members saw the disinflation prospect reduced. The old "predominant concern" phrase is not in the new input. Score 25.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 26 to 34).** Members cite "elevated levels of unused labor and other resources", "large margins of unemployed labor" and a "lack of significant job growth", while "labor and product markets might be stabilizing"; participants' own projections put unemployment at 6 to 6-1/4 percent in Q4 2003. This is below full employment but in the 5.5-7% band rather than a deep-slack reading. The old payroll and 6.1% evidence is not in the participants' text. Score 34.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 40 to 44).** The only spread statement is "a narrowing of risk spreads" (direction, no level), together with "low cost and ample availability of financing for most business firms". Level information points somewhat below normal; direction is narrowing. Score 44, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 32 to N/A).** No participant discussion of banks or the banking system appears in the new input; the old score relied on absence of mention. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 23 to 28).** Members refer to "the weaker dollar" expected to help exports and "a drop in the dollar on foreign exchange markets" as one of the disinflation-limiting developments. Depreciation, so below 50, without the sharper "continued to depreciate/current account concerns" language of the old evidence. Score 28.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 28).** "All but one of the members" supported a 25bp cut to 1%; "most" preferred limiting it to 25bp, "some" saw a good case for 50bp, and some were "not prepared" to judge the easing cycle ended. Moderate dovish. Score 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 33).** One formal dissent (Parry preferred a 50bp cut "as insurance against continued sluggishness"), with "one member" who saw merit in unchanged policy but did not oppose, and "some members" expressing reservations about the extent of fiscal effects on spending. Disagreement is over the size and timing of easing, not direction. Score 33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 27).** Financial conditions appear as supportive context ("much improved financial conditions", "narrowing of risk spreads", balance-sheet strengthening, governance concerns damping risk appetite), and "no member" cited dislocations from a very low funds rate. A few passages, not a focus. Score 27.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 20).** The press statement says the balance of risks is "weighted toward the downside in the foreseeable future" and inflation risks "weighted toward the downside", and members were unwilling to signal the end of easing. A mild-to-moderate easing bias, no explicit commitment. Score 20.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 42).** "The timing and dimensions of a significantly improved economic performance remained uncertain"; "no conclusive evidence of an appreciable overall strengthening"; consumer acceleration "likely but not inevitable". Somewhat elevated, mostly standard caveats. Score 42.
