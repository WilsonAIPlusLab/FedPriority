## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 58).** Participants see spending as somewhat above trend: recent data on consumer spending were "stronger than anticipated," supported by a solid labor market, elevated household net worth and rising real wages; equipment and intangibles investment was "strong over the past year" though it slowed in Q4. Several participants cautioned about strains on low- and moderate-income households (a few cited rising card and auto delinquencies). Net slightly above trend, 58.

**PRODUCTIVITY (FILLED, 58).** Participants view productivity as favorable: consumption supported by real wage gains "associated in part with productivity gains"; some participants noted aggregate supply developments including productivity "continued to support a solid expansion"; some said "recent productivity gains" mean labor market conditions are unlikely to add to inflation. Above trend but not described as a boom, 58.

**TREASURY_STRESS (FILLED, 50).** "Several participants commented on vulnerabilities in the Treasury market, including concerns about dealer intermediation capacity and the degree of leveraged positions," with central clearing noted by a few. These are latent vulnerabilities, not reported dysfunction (no liquidity breakdown, auction failures), so neutral, 50.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 35).** The old "very tight" quote was from non-participant market text. In the new input only "a couple of participants" said "low credit spreads were providing some support to economic activity" and a few flagged valuation pressure in corporate debt. The stated level is low (below 50); thin evidence, so modest distance from 50 and Low confidence: 35. Corporate bond yields rising is a yield statement and is not used for spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 40).** Old price-move text is not participants' views. A few participants "noted concerns about asset valuation pressures in equity and corporate debt markets" and a couple cited "high equity valuations": modestly elevated valuation concern (lower on this scale), limited number of speakers, 40, Low.

**BANKING_STRESS (FILLED, 42).** Several participants raised banks: "bank funding risks had lessened" and discount window access improved (a few), but a couple noted untested reciprocal deposits and several said some banks "remained vulnerable to a rise in longer-term yields" and unrealized losses; CRE risks "remained" though lessening (a few). Sound with minor concerns, 42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only support was non-participant market text. Participants do not discuss the exchange rate; "international developments" appears only in boilerplate statement language. N/A.

**INFLATION_GOODS (FILLED, 52).** "Several participants observed that core goods prices had not declined as much on net in recent months compared with earlier in 2024": goods disinflation has stalled, roughly flat to slightly firm, 52.

**INFLATION_SERVICES (FILLED, 54).** Market-based core nonhousing services inflation "had shown a decline," while "several participants" noted less improvement in financial and insurance services (a few said these are unreliable signals). Still somewhat elevated, easing: 54.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (FILLED, 53).** Housing services inflation, "elevated for much of the previous year," "had shown a decline." Level still somewhat above normal, direction down: 53.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 52).** "Many participants emphasized that longer-term measures of expected inflation had remained well anchored," while "some participants noted that some market- or survey-based measures of expected inflation had increased recently," and tariff pass-through was cited. Anchored with a small upward tilt, 52.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 50).** All participants backed holding at 4 1/4-4 1/2 percent after three cuts; the vast majority saw policy as still restrictive; many said policy could stay restrictive if the economy stayed strong and inflation elevated, several said it could ease if the labor market or activity weakened or inflation fell faster. Balanced hold, no tightening or easing push: 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15).** Vote record shows 12 in favor and none against; "all participants" favored holding; "almost all members" saw risks as balanced, with "a couple" seeing greater price-stability risk. Near-consensus with minor emphasis differences: 15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** A separate financial-stability discussion: "several participants" on banking, nonbank vulnerabilities, cyber risk and the Treasury market, "a few" on valuations and CRE; also "a number of participants" on financial conditions bearing on spending and "many" noting tightening. Moderate, several participants meaningful: 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 45).** The Committee is "well positioned to take time to assess"; participants want "further progress on inflation before making additional adjustments"; decisions "not on a preset course," conditional on data. Two-sided contingencies (hold if strong, ease if weaker) and a data-dependent wait, with the next move implicitly an easing but no tightening signal: slight easing lean with data dependence, 45.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** "A majority of participants observed that the current high degree of uncertainty made it appropriate... to take a careful approach"; outlook "uncertain"; uncertainties about the neutral rate, the restraint from higher long-term rates and government policies; inflation "harder than usual to interpret" at the start of the year (some). Elevated, not extreme: 58.
