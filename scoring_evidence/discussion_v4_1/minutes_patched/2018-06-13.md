## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score rested on a statement that equities were "near historical highs", which does not appear anywhere in the participants' views or vote record of the new input. Participants do not discuss equity markets, so the dimension is N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score rested on a quote about regulatory reforms producing "appreciably stronger capital and liquidity positions". This is absent from the new input and no participant addresses the banking system. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score rested on a quote about "further strengthening of the U.S. dollar" as a downside risk. It is absent from the new input; the only use of "dollar" is the "dollar roll" operational term in the directive. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 68).** The lean is hawkish. "Almost all participants" judged it appropriate to raise the range 25bp and to keep "gradually raising" it to "at or somewhat above" the longer-run level by 2019 or 2020; the vote was a hike to 1 3/4-2 percent. Moderated below the 70-75 hiking-cycle anchor because the approach is gradual, stance stays "accommodative", one participant favored postponing, and a few stress that inflation expectations are not firmly anchored. Score 68.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18).** Near-consensus. Vote record shows eight in favor and "Voting against this action: None". One participant argued for postponing the hike, and participants differed on how to read the yield curve ("Some" vs "several others") and on labor slack ("Several" see less tightness), but these are emphasis differences. Score 18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 30).** Limited attention. "Some participants" worried that a prolonged period beyond potential could lead to "financial imbalances", the vote record names "material financial imbalances" as the risk of moving too slowly, and "several" asked that the yield curve be read within "the broader context of financial conditions". These are context, not the focus. Score 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 66).** Mildly to moderately hawkish. Members "favored the removal" of the language that the funds rate would stay "below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run", as "no longer appropriate" given the strong economy; "a number" thought wording on an "accommodative" stance might soon change; "many" saw the funds rate at or above neutral next year. Tempered by "did not reflect a shift in the approach to policy" and data-dependent language. Score 66.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 33).** Somewhat elevated, not high. "Most participants" noted trade-policy uncertainty "had intensified" and "many" saw downside risks from Europe and some EMEs, but "participants generally viewed the risks ... as roughly balanced" and the outlook is described as unchanged and evolving "about as anticipated". Score 33.
