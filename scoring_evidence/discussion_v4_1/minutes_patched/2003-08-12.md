## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 32).** Participants see the labor market as slack but not depressed. Key phrases: "substantial margins of unemployed labor and other resources" would persist even with stronger growth (members, inflation discussion); "a weak job market" is a possible drag on consumers (some members); many business contacts plan "adding staff" before adding capacity. No unemployment figures appear in the views, so the old payroll and UE evidence no longer applies. Below 50, Moderate confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 46).** Members said "risk spreads had narrowed in recent months" as one reason higher rates may not hurt much. This is direction only; no level (wide, tight, normal) is stated. Under the V4.1 rule the score sits just on the narrowing side of 50, Low confidence. The old 38 rested on staff detail about high-grade and low-grade spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 47).** Stocks are mentioned only as price moves: "stock prices had held firm" after "upside surprises in second-quarter profit reports", and "improved performance of the stock market" as a positive for consumers. No valuation view is offered, so the score is in the 45-55 default band, slightly on the supportive side, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The participants' views do not discuss bank health, capital or lending standards; the earlier banking evidence was not in the participants' text.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The foreign-sector paragraph covers trading-partner growth, weak foreign demand and "strong foreign competition", with no reference to the exchange rate. The earlier dollar evidence was not in the participants' text.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Members discuss inflation measures, disinflation and pricing power, but not expectations or their anchoring; the earlier quote was not in the participants' text.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 28).** Strongly accommodative lean without a new easing step. "All the members endorsed" holding at 1 percent; policy "remained quite accommodative"; "many" want accommodation kept longer than in past upturns to "resist significant further disinflation". Risks to inflation weighted to the downside. Between "maintaining max accommodation" (11-25) and "some easing" (26-40), without QE or a cut: 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** Near-consensus: "all the members endorsed" the unchanged stance, and the vote record lists no votes against. Differences are of emphasis only ("many" versus "some" on the acceptable inflation range; "some" worried about the job market).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Financial conditions get one dedicated paragraph, framed as a "positive factor" despite the rise in long rates, plus brief mentions ("receptive", "generally favorable"). No stability concerns; limited context-level attention.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 16).** The Committee decided to say that "policy accommodation could be maintained for a considerable period", and "many" members expect accommodation for longer than usual, though it "could not commit itself". Clear low-for-long easing bias, not threshold-based.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** Members "acknowledged that the magnitude of the improvement remained subject to considerable uncertainty, notably" for business spending, citing "both upside and downside risks"; the housing slowdown was "uncertain". Offsetting this, the growth risks were "about balanced" and the unwelcome fall in inflation "minor". Elevated but concentrated in one area.
