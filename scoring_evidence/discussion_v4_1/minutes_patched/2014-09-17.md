## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (46).** Participants lean slightly dovish to balanced: policy stays at the lower bound with a further measured taper step (purchases still being added), and the guidance was reiterated. A hawkish minority pushes the other way: "several participants thought that the current forward guidance ... suggested a longer period before liftoff ... than they believed was likely to be appropriate". Two members dissented in favor of an earlier increase than the guidance suggests (Fisher, Plosser). Against this, "some participants saw the current forward guidance as appropriate in light of risk-management considerations" and wanted to "err on the side of patience". Net: hold steady with a modest tightening undercurrent, in the 2014 pre-liftoff range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (52).** Two formal dissents, both hawkish: Fisher (financial market excess, improved real economy and price outlook) and Plosser (objected to "a considerable time" and the failure to acknowledge progress). Disagreement reaches the economic assessment: "they continued to express differing views on the extent of remaining slack", and "a few members expressed reservations about continuing to characterize" underutilization as significant. Because the dissents share a direction and the broader body was cohesive, this sits in the lower part of the 46-60 band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (35).** Attention is moderate-to-limited. One paragraph lists "a deterioration in leveraged lending standards, stretched stock market valuations, and compressed risk spreads", with one participant seeing the leveraged loan market "moving into better balance" and a couple saying conditions do not impede the objectives. Several participants (a number) worried that guidance changes might cause "an unintended tightening of financial conditions". Fisher's dissent cites "continued signs of financial market excess" and Plosser's cites risk of "disruptive volatility in financial markets". It is a recurring but secondary theme, not the focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30).** The guidance is low for long: "considerable time" after asset purchases end, with rates below normal "for some time" even after targets are met, reaffirmed in the vote record. The debate points toward more data dependence rather than a tightening signal: "Most participants indicated a preference for clarifying the dependence of the current forward guidance on economic data", and "the date of liftoff could be earlier" if data converge faster. The forward signal remains an easing lean with data dependence, in the 26-40 band, nudged to the low end by the reiterated calendar-time phrase.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (30).** Ordinary-level uncertainty. "Most viewed the risks ... as broadly balanced"; "a number of participants noted" growth could be slower if foreign growth disappoints, productivity stays slow or housing lags; participants "commented on a number of uncertainties and risks" abroad (euro area, China, Japan, Middle East, Ukraine); views differed on slack and on the pace of inflation. These are standard caveats, not unusual or deep uncertainty. The staff statements on forecast uncertainty (present only in the old input) were not used.
