## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants' remarks are about direction and risk appetite: two participants "saw declining credit spreads, particularly on speculative-grade corporate bonds, as consistent with an increase in investors' appetite for risk," and a couple said leveraged loan conditions "had become stretched." No level statement is made. Under the v4.1 level rule this is direction only, so the score sits just below 50 on the side of the narrowing, with Low confidence: 46.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants and the Committee continued a taper ("further measured reduction in the pace of asset purchases"; "likely reduce ... in further measured steps") while stressing that purchases "were not on a preset course" and that "a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy remains appropriate." Policy is still easing-biased with tapering underway, with no tightening advocated. Score 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote was nine in favor, none against. The views record only differences of emphasis (a number see more slack, some report tight local labor markets, a few worry the return to 2 percent could be more gradual). Near-consensus. Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants "generally did not see imbalances that posed significant near-term risks," but a full paragraph reviewed stretched leveraged loans (a couple), declining spreads (two), low implied volatility (several) and the need to explore regulatory and policy responses (some). A moderate, contained discussion. Score 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The forward signal is easing-biased: rates to stay at 0-1/4 "for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends" and below normal even after goals are near. A number of participants wanted clearer communication as the first increase "moves closer," and a few noted a faster rise might be needed, which keeps the score above the strongest-commitment range. Score 17.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** The assessment "had not changed materially"; risks seen "nearly balanced," with a number pointing to housing, China and Russia/Ukraine downside risks and some saying it was "too early to confirm" above-trend growth. This is standard caveating with a debate about labor slack, not unusual uncertainty. Score 30.
