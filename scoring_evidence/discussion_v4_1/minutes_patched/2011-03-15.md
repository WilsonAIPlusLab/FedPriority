## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 30).** Participants lean dovish: the vote record says members "agreed that no changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted," and the $600 billion purchase program continues to completion. Counterweights are small: "a few participants" thought conditions "might warrant a move toward less-accommodative monetary policy this year" and "a few members" said stronger recovery or higher inflation could justify reducing the program, while "a few others" saw accommodation appropriate beyond 2011 and "several others" expected no adjustments. QE-era anchor (15-25) is moved up modestly for the firmer recovery and the hawkish minority. Score 30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20).** The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None."). Reservations are limited: "a few members remained uncertain about the benefits of the asset purchase program but judged that making changes... was not appropriate," and participants debated how much resource slack matters for inflation ("Some" vs "Others"). Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences, slightly above near-consensus because views split mildly on economics. Score 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Financial conditions get a dedicated but descriptive paragraph (credit "uneven", tight for small bank-dependent firms, auto loans normalized), and "a few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions in some sectors was becoming or might become excessive"; "banking and fiscal strains in the European periphery" appear among downside risks. This is limited discussion, context rather than the focus. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 25).** The forward signal is easing-leaning: the Committee "continued to anticipate" conditions "likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period," with asset purchases to be reviewed and adjusted "as needed." Hawkish elements are weak: "participants agreed that the Committee would continue its planning for the eventual exit," and "a few members" saw room to reduce purchases on stronger data. Date-free low-for-long language with data dependence. Score 25.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52).** Participants "noted that unfolding events in the Middle East and North Africa, along with the recent earthquake, tsunami, and subsequent developments in Japan, had further increased uncertainty about the economic outlook"; "the pace of expansion was uncertain"; implications of Japan "were not yet clear"; and exit planning was "prudent" "in light of uncertainty." Offsetting this, "participants generally judged the risks to their forecasts of growth... to be roughly balanced," so uncertainty is elevated but not extreme. Score 52.
