## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 46 +-8, Low).** The only credit-market passage says it "was observed that prices of ... high-yield corporate bonds ... had also risen significantly in recent months." This indicates spreads narrowed (direction) but gives no stated level (no "tight," "compressed," or "below average"). Under the V4.1 rule, direction only places the score at 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence; 46 is slightly below normal. The old 30 inferred a compressed level that the text does not state.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old evidence ("higher requirements for capital and liquidity ... increased resilience") does not appear in the new input. No participant comments on bank capital, lending standards or bank health; the only credit remark concerns mortgage access for housing. The topic was not discussed, so N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old evidence ("modest depreciation of the dollar ... some stimulus") is absent from the new input, which mentions the dollar only in "dollar roll" operations. No participant discusses the exchange rate, so N/A.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, 55 +-10, Low).** The old import-price quote is absent from the new input. Participants note the 12-month headline PCE rise "as the effects of firmer consumer energy prices were registered," and the vote record says "recent increases in consumer energy prices could cause inflation to temporarily reach or even rise a bit above 2 percent." Energy goods prices are therefore pushing modestly up; transitory language does not lower the score. Only passing mention, hence Low confidence and 55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60 +-6, High).** Lean is hawkish but gradual. "Almost all participants" saw the data as consistent with a 25bp increase and "all but one member agreed to raise"; yet "several" stressed risk management and a gradual removal, "a few" noted inflation could take time to reach 2 percent, while "several other participants" saw the goal as essentially met and a faster pace as warranted, and one member preferred to wait. Score 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 36 +-7, High).** One formal dissent (Kashkari, who wanted to hold and also asked for a balance-sheet plan first). Participants differed on inflation progress ("some" vs "a few other") and labor slack ("some" vs "several other"), but there were no sharp exchanges and members said the hike did not change their outlook. Moderate differences with one dissent: 36.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 40 +-8, Moderate).** One full paragraph on financial markets: "many participants" discussed the equity rally, "some" viewed prices as "quite high relative to standard valuation measures," plus risk assets and farmland valuations; "a number of participants" flagged financial conditions as an upside risk and a "significant correction" as a downside risk. More than a passing mention but not dominant: 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62 +-6, High).** Members "reiterated that they expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant gradual increases in the federal funds rate," with the rate "likely to remain, for some time, below levels expected to prevail in the longer run," and the path dependent on incoming data. A gradual-tightening signal within an ongoing hiking cycle: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52 +-8, Moderate).** "Participants continued to underscore the considerable uncertainty about the timing and nature of potential changes to fiscal policies," and uncertainty about the neutral real rate was cited; the vote record says "significant uncertainty." Offsetting this, risks were "roughly balanced," views were "essentially unchanged," and about half of the participants simply left fiscal assumptions out. Elevated but concentrated on fiscal policy: 52.
