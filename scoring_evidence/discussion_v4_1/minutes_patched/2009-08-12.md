## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32).** Participants and the Committee lean dovish but are holding rather than easing further. All members agreed the stance "should not be changed," the funds rate stays at 0 to 1/4 percent and will likely be "maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period." The Committee judged "neither expansion nor contraction" of asset purchases warranted, though it decided to "gradually slow the pace" of remaining Treasury purchases (a technical, smooth-transition move). Many participants stressed it would be important to communicate willingness to eventually withdraw accommodation. Hold at the zero bound with continued asset purchases and a mild exit-communication counterweight: 32.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (12).** The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None") and "Committee members agreed" on the stance. The minutes show only minor differences of emphasis (some pointed to difficulties measuring slack, "several were skeptical" that low resource utilization would reduce inflation appreciably, a few saw a risk of substantial disinflation, and participants held a "range of views" on recovery strength), with no reservations about the action: 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (58).** A substantial part of the discussion addresses financial conditions: market functioning "viewed as quite significant" in improvement, but "many markets were still strained and that financial risks remain," market functioning "might deteriorate as those programs wind down," tight bank lending standards, and "several participants" on bank credit losses and CRE. Prominent, but alongside a full economic assessment rather than dominating it: 58.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (15).** The forward signal is a strong easing bias: members "thought it most likely that the federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period," and the statement repeats that conditions "are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels... for an extended period." It is not threshold- or date-based, and the exit-communication point and slower Treasury purchases are minor offsets: 15.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (52).** Participants "expressed a range of views, and considerable uncertainty, about the likely strength of the upturn," particularly consumer spending and credit normalization, with the saving rate cited as "an important source of uncertainty," and "all saw [the economy] as still vulnerable to adverse shocks." Offsetting this, "many" saw "smaller downside risks" and baseline projections were unchanged since June. Elevated but not extreme: 52.
