## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 40 +-7, Moderate).** Participants see output as below potential: growth "quite sluggish and below the pace many members had anticipated," "greater slack in labor and product markets," and "relatively low capacity utilization rates in manufacturing," although "by some measures overall economic activity remained at a reasonably high level." That is a modest-to-clear shortfall, not the deep contraction implied by the old 24, whose supporting quotes are not part of the participants' views in the new input. Bright-line (< 50) satisfied.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 42 +-7, Moderate).** The narrative describes conditions as "generally supportive of greater spending" though "mixed" (equities "only holding their own," dollar appreciated), with "ready availability of finance to most borrowers" and policy having "become stimulative." Supportive language forces a score below 50; the mixed elements keep it close to neutral. The old 60 contradicted the bright-line rule.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 40 +-8, Moderate).** Credit is described as plentiful: "most businesses were finding ample credit available at attractive terms," with lenders cautious only about "marginally creditworthy firms." The level reads somewhat below normal; the high-yield quote in the old analysis is not in the new input.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 55 +-8, Moderate).** Participants refer to "adaptation to lower equity prices," a "substantial decline earlier," prices "only holding their own," and "adverse wealth effects." These are price-move and wealth-effect comments without valuation judgment, so the score sits modestly above neutral (depressed), not at the old 65.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 37 +-6, High).** Policy lean is moderately dovish. All but one member supported a 25bp easing, as "a further easing action was needed at this point to help stabilize the economy," and the "primary policy concern... remained the strength of economic activity rather than potentially worsening inflation." The move was smaller than earlier ones and "a number of members" thought the Committee "might well be near the end of its easing cycle," with some concern about stimulus lasting too long, which tempers the dovish score.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 36 +-6, High).** One formal dissent (Mr. Poole, vote 9-1), who opposed both the cut (policy "already... highly stimulative") and the press statement, with "strong reservations." The remaining members were largely aligned, differing on emphasis (end-of-easing view versus not ruling out more easing). Fits the typical one-dissent anchor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22 +-7, Moderate).** Attention is limited: a paragraph on financial conditions and credit availability, and references to equity-price wealth effects and the monetary aggregates, all as context for spending and policy. No discussion of financial stability risks as such.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 35 +-7, High).** The Committee kept its statement that "risks would continue to be weighted toward economic weakness," which Mr. Poole said would be read as a signal of further easing. "Several emphasized that they did not want to rule out further easing," while "a number" saw the cycle near its end and the Committee stood ready to "firm the stance of policy promptly" if demand pressures intensified. Net: easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 52 +-7, Moderate).** Elevated but not extreme: the "unique characteristics of the current cyclical experience... increased the uncertainty that surrounded any forecast"; "the dimensions and duration of the adjustment in spending on capital goods were a major source of uncertainty"; how much stronger productivity will be "was an open question"; "most" saw risks to the downside. Multiple passages, but standard-caveat tone elsewhere.
