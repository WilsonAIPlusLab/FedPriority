## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 47 rested on unit labor cost and productivity wording that is staff commentary and is not in the participants' views. Participants mention only that wage growth was "quite moderate" (some participants), which is a labor cost remark, not a productivity view. Not discussed, so N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED to 60).** Direction in words: participants see credit spreads as above normal in level. Most participants viewed markets as "under considerable stress"; other participants cited "the high level of risk spreads and the restricted availability of credit"; interbank term spreads "had declined, but remained elevated by historical standards". Offsetting: the leveraged loan market "had improved somewhat" and "corporate bond issuance had been strong". The stated level is high, so the score is >50 under the V4.1 level rule. Somewhat wide, partly improving: 60 +-8, Moderate (few spread-specific statements, mostly by "other participants").

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quote on broad stock prices comes from staff text. The new input mentions only that bank equity prices "had declined... reflecting increased concern about asset quality" and that housing-related firms' equity prices "had fallen". These are banking and housing signals, not a participant view on market valuation. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old evidence ("the trade-weighted foreign exchange value of the dollar... rose") is staff text. Participants mention only buoyant export demand and a narrowing current account deficit, with no view on the dollar. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** Direction: hold at 2 percent with a lean toward tightening. "Most members judged that an unchanged federal funds rate... represented an appropriate balancing of the risks"; members "believed that the next change in the stance of policy could well be an increase in the funds rate"; "one member thought that policy should be firmed at this meeting"; some participants said "some firming in policy would be appropriate very soon, if not at this meeting", while other participants argued conditions were "not especially accommodative". Current action is a hold and downside risks to growth remain, so lean hawkish at 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** Direction: moderate differences. Vote 9-1; Mr. Fisher dissented "because he preferred an increase", citing inflation expectations "becoming less well anchored". Some participants saw expectations as contained and others as broad-based concerns; some wanted firming "very soon" while others stressed tight credit. A single hawkish dissent with some disagreement on the economic assessment but a shared view that unchanged policy was appropriate for now: 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 68).** Direction: prominent topic. Most participants "viewed markets as remaining under considerable stress"; a long passage covers bank capital, financial guarantors, securitized products and credit availability "constrained for some time"; the joint session discussed extending the PDCF and TSLF "in view of the continuing significant strains" and measures "to enhance financial stability". Prominent but sharing the meeting with an inflation-focused policy debate: 68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** Direction: mild tightening bias. Members "believed that the next change in the stance of policy could well be an increase" and "might need to respond promptly to incoming information", but "the timing and magnitude of future policy actions was quite unclear" and downside risks to growth remained. Mild tightening bias with data dependence: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** Direction: elevated to high. "In the view of most members, the outlook for both economic activity and price pressures remained very uncertain"; participants' views of the inflation-expectations evidence "varied"; financial conditions "appeared to remain fragile" and members saw a possible "difficult adjustment process". Language is genuine rather than rhetorical, but it stops short of deep or Knightian uncertainty: 62.
