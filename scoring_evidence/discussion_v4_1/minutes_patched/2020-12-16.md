## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 46).** Participants say only that "corporate credit spreads continued to narrow" as financing conditions eased for large corporations, while smaller firms and bank-reliant households faced tight standards. This gives direction without level. The old "generally low" quote is not in the participants' text, so the v4.1 direction-only rule applies: score on the side of the move (narrowing) within 45-55, Low confidence. Score 46 +-8.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48).** The only equity reference is that "equity prices rose" alongside narrowing spreads. There is no valuation commentary, so the rubric default of 45-55 applies. Score 48, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, 48).** The bank-resilience, capital and stress-test language of the old analysis is absent from the new input. A couple of participants noted that banking data "had not indicated a significant increase in loan delinquencies for C&I loans thus far", possibly masked by CARES Act relief or business closures. Several participants noted tight lending standards for bank-reliant borrowers. Mixed, thin evidence with no bright-line wording: 48 +-10, Low.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss goods spending, not goods prices. Only "a few" mention a possible future pandemic-related supply-constraint upside to inflation. Goods price inflation is therefore not discussed: N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 18).** "All participants judged that maintaining an accommodative stance of monetary policy was essential"; all supported enhanced asset-purchase guidance with purchases "at least" $80B and $40B a month; "some" saw room to increase the pace later; "a number" discussed eventual tapering only after substantial further progress. Maximum accommodation maintained, not new easing: 18.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 10).** "Voting against this action: None"; "all participants" supported the guidance and the purchase pace. Differences were tactical: a couple open to longer-maturity weighting, a few stressing costs and risks of purchases, a couple stressing the funds rate as the primary tool. Near-consensus: 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 38).** One paragraph on financial conditions: accommodative overall but uneven; a few on small-business closures; a couple on delinquencies; some on 13(3) facilities and the December 31 expiry. Several participants address it, but it is not a focus and vulnerabilities are barely mentioned: 38, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 7).** The funds rate is to stay at 0-1/4 percent until labor market conditions reach maximum employment and inflation is "on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time". Purchases continue until "substantial further progress". Participants stressed that slower progress would bring "maintaining the current level of the target range ... for longer" and a larger balance sheet. A number mention a later tapering path, but no tightening is signaled: 7.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as elevated"; "the path ahead remained highly uncertain"; "significant uncertainties" about vaccine deployment; "considerable risks" over the medium term. Offsetting: vaccine news reduced downside risks and "a number" saw risks as more balanced. High but not extreme: 62.
