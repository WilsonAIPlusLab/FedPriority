## Evidence for changed dimensions

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The only mention of the dollar sits in the directive's "information reviewed at this meeting" paragraph, which summarizes staff-derived data. Participants' own discussion covers the foreign sector only through trade and partner-country growth ("several members questioned" whether partner-country strength would materialize). With no participant view on the dollar, the score is N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old quotes about lower prevailing inflation expectations and consumers expecting less inflation are not in the new input. Members talk about actual price and cost performance and the resource-use/inflation relationship, not expectations. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 43).** The lean is dovish-to-balanced. "All but one member favored or could accept an unchanged policy stance"; "several" thought policy "somewhat restrictive"; "some" saw an easing "at some point" as needed, though for "most of these members" the urgency was reduced; "one member" wanted an immediate easing. Another group preferred unchanged policy with a slightly restrictive stance acceptable. No participant argued for tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** There was one formal dissent, from Mr. Lindsey, who "believed that monetary policy should be eased". Members also held "somewhat differing views" on the right stance, and the "remaining members" who wanted an easing-tilted directive accepted a symmetric one. The difference is about timing and direction of the next move and does not reach the assessment of conditions. That is moderate differences with one dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 15).** Financial conditions appear only as context: "favorable developments this year in the bond and stock markets", the "ready availability of financing", and a possible "unsettlement in financial markets" tied to the shutdown. No participant dwells on financial stability. This is brief mentions only.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 45).** "A majority of the members" kept a symmetric directive and "some... believed that the direction of the next adjustment to policy was uncertain". The "remaining members" saw easing as "the most likely policy course over coming months" but accepted symmetry. That is neutral with a mild easing lean.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 50).** Members cite "an especially broad range of outcomes" on fiscal policy and an "uncertain picture of the underlying strength of aggregate demand", and say the Committee faced "an absence of any clear signs". Several caveats are standard, but the fiscal and shutdown uncertainty is emphasized by the members generally. That is elevated, not extreme.
