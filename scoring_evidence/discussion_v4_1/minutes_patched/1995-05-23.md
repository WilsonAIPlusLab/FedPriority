## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants did not discuss spreads, only credit availability, which they described as ample. Phrases: "a ready availability of financing" (cited in support of business investment), "banks continued to ease terms and standards on loans", and "aggressive extensions of consumer credit by a number of lenders". This reads as somewhat below-normal credit stress, with no level information on spreads. Score 38, Low confidence, below 50 under the level rule.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** In the policy discussion the dollar "had fallen against the currencies of many major industrial countries" since year-end. The directive preamble says it "after falling to low levels, rose on balance over the intermeeting period". The net view is weaker than at the turn of the year with a partial recovery, which is below the midpoint (depreciation bright line). Score 42, Low confidence.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED).** The direction is modestly to clearly above stable. The worsening in "the consumer price index and the producer price index for finished goods" was "a disappointing--if not unexpected--development". Several members attributed it to "partial pass-through of sizable increases in prices at earlier stages of production" and to "higher import prices". Score 60.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** Expectations are not discussed directly. As a proxy, "a number of members" were concerned that "inflation pressures were likely to intensify" if growth rebounded. Others thought "an upward trend in inflation was likely to be averted" given competitive pressures and restrained compensation. The split puts it slightly above neutral. Score 55, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The stance is held, and all members favored an unbiased directive. One member judged "the costs of being wrong currently seemed higher in the direction of accommodating too much inflation". Another thought recent performance "might argue for some easing" but favored a steady course. The two views offset, so the lean is balanced to marginally dovish. Score 48.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote was unanimous with no dissents ("Votes against this action: None"), and "all the members" endorsed both the hold and the unbiased instruction. The only visible differences are in emphasis on inflation risk versus downside risk. Score 18, High confidence.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** "A number of members" referred to the financial climate as "an important element" of the outlook. There were also "some concern" about the stock market's sustainability and "at least one view" that consumer credit growth was unsustainable. These are context mentions rather than a focus of the meeting. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Neutral and data-dependent. "No compelling case could be made at this point for potential adjustments to policy in either direction", and members would "monitor developments carefully and wait for additional information before deciding on the next policy move". Score 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Members said the longer-run outlook "remained subject to considerable uncertainty", with the inventory correction a "cyclical dynamic whose outcome could not be predicted with confidence", and fiscal policy "a major uncertainty". Offsetting this, risks were judged "reasonably balanced" and sentiment "generally favorable". Score 60.
