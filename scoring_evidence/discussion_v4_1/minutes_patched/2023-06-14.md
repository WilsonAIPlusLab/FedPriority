## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60).** Participants lean hawkish within a hold. "Almost all participants" judged it "appropriate or acceptable" to maintain 5 to 5 1/4 percent, "most of these" wanting time to assess; "some participants" favored a 25 bp increase or "could have supported" one, citing a "very tight" labor market and "few clear signs" that inflation was on a path to 2 percent. "Almost all" also projected "additional increases in the target federal funds rate during 2023", and "many" favored a "further moderation in the pace" of firming. Current action is a hold with a clear tightening bias, so upper lean-hawkish range: 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 20).** The vote record shows the action carried with "Voting against this action: None", and all members affirmed commitment to returning inflation to 2 percent. The only visible difference is that "some participants" preferred a 25 bp hike now or could have supported it, and "many" stressed a slower pace. Broad agreement with minor differences in timing: 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** Participants "generally noted that banking stresses had receded", "several" said credit conditions had not tightened beyond what policy implied, "some" stressed monitoring bank credit conditions closely, and "some" raised commercial real estate risks; "a few" mentioned money market rates and ON RRP usage. Several participants address it meaningfully, but it sits alongside the inflation and labor discussion rather than rivaling it: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68).** The forward signal tilts toward tightening: "all participants" anticipated a restrictive stance, and "almost all" saw additional 2023 increases as appropriate. Tempering this, "many" favored slowing the pace, "most" said more information would be valuable, participants stressed a "data-dependent approach", and the approved statement says the Committee "would be prepared to adjust the stance". Mild-to-clear tightening bias with data dependence: 68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** "Participants generally noted a high degree of uncertainty regarding the cumulative effects" of past tightening and bank credit tightening; "most" observed that uncertainty about the outlook for the economy and inflation "remained elevated"; the vote record repeats that the extent of credit-tightening effects "remained uncertain". Both-sided risk language (growth downside, expectations unanchoring) but no unusual-scenario or model-breakdown language: high but not extreme, 62.
