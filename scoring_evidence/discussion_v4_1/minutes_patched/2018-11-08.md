## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 60).** The Committee held at 2 to 2 1/4 percent, but participants lean toward tightening. "Almost all participants" expressed the view that "another increase in the target range... was likely to be warranted fairly soon" if data were in line with or stronger than expected. A few were uncertain about timing and a couple saw the funds rate as possibly "near its neutral level". Current action is a hold, with a clear but not aggressive tightening bias: lean hawkish at the top of the 51-60 band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 14).** The vote record shows nine votes in favor and "Voting against this action: None." Participants "agreed that it would be appropriate to maintain" the range. Differences were limited to a few participants uncertain about timing and a couple noting a near-neutral rate. Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** There is a distinct financial developments and stability paragraph. "A number" cited risks from "elevated CRE prices, narrow corporate bond spreads, or strong issuance of leveraged loans"; "several" were concerned about high nonfinancial business debt and leveraged loans; "a few" and "a couple" linked these to risks to the outlook, and "a couple" noted the first public Financial Stability Report. Moderate attention from several participants, above the 2013-19 typical level but not prominent for most.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 62).** The signal is a continued tightening path: "almost all" reaffirmed "further gradual increases" and the statement language retained them. This is offset by "Many participants" indicating it "might be appropriate at some upcoming meetings to begin to transition to statement language that placed greater emphasis on the evaluation of incoming data", and by "Monetary policy was not on a preset course". Mild-to-moderate tightening bias that is softening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 32).** "A few participants indicated that uncertainty had increased recently" because of fiscal and trade policy effects, and risks were listed on both sides, but "participants agreed that risks to the outlook appeared roughly balanced" and the economy was evolving "about as they had anticipated". Standard caveats with some elevated trade-policy uncertainty from a minority.
