## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED).** Participants see the economy as somewhat below potential and expect the gap to widen. Members expected "output likely to remain below the expansion of the economy's potential for a while", "continued softness" and "ebbing pressures on labor and product markets", but consumer spending "remained relatively strong" and housing "elevated". Bright line (<50) satisfied. Score 43 +-7, Moderate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED).** Participants see labor markets as still tight but loosening at the margin. "Labor markets in general remained tight throughout the nation" with "anecdotal reports of less scarce labor resources" in some areas and "rising concerns about job losses" amid layoffs. Score 57 +-6, Moderate.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED).** Participants see conditions as somewhat tighter, with the stimulus from cuts partly offset. Many businesses were held back by "less accommodative financial conditions associated with weaker equity markets and tighter credit terms"; "some commented" that stimulus was "much smaller" than the cuts suggest given equity declines, stringent financing and a stronger dollar; others cite "lower interest rates" and "generally sound financial institutions". Bright line (>50) satisfied. Score 60 +-7, Moderate.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants describe tighter credit availability but give no level for spreads. Key phrases: "tighter credit terms and conditions imposed by banking institutions" and "more stringent financing terms for many business borrowers". Direction only, so a score just above 50 with Low confidence: 56 +-9.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** Participants discuss realized inflation ("remain subdued") but never expectations; the old support was staff survey text. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Moderately dovish: "most of the members preferred" a 50bp cut to 5 percent and "all could support" it; "many" thought "additional policy easing might well prove to be necessary", and "a few" wanted 75bp. Most favored smaller, possibly more frequent steps. Score 27.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Broad agreement: the vote record shows ten votes for and "None" against. "A few members" preferred 75bp but supported the action; some differences over the stimulus effect. Score 18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Moderate attention. Several participants address equity declines, "tighter credit terms and conditions" and sound institutions, mainly as demand channels rather than stability threats. Score 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Clear easing bias. The approved press-statement sentence says risks are "weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness"; members agreed risks "would remain decidedly to the downside" even after the cut and flagged a possible intermeeting conference call. Data-dependent monitoring tempers this from the strongest easing commitment. Score 18.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** High: "considerable uncertainty surrounded this outlook", the consumer confidence/spending divergence was "a significant source of uncertainty", policy chosen "in rapidly changing and highly uncertain circumstances", and the capital overhang is "of unknown dimensions". Score 62.
