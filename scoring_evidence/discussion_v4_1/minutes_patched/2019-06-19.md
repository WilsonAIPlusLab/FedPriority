## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 35).** Participants lean dovish: nearly all agreed to hold at 2 1/4 to 2 1/2 percent, but "many indicated that the case for somewhat more accommodative policy had strengthened" and "many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted in the near term" if the downside developments persisted. A couple favored a cut now, and several saw a near-term cut as risk-management insurance. Some and a few pushed back (no "strong case" for a cut yet; risk of "overheating the labor markets"), which keeps this moderate rather than strong. The vote record adds the removal of "patient" and one dissent for a cut. Score 35, moderate dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 38).** One formal dissent (Bullard, preferring a 25 bp cut to "re-center inflation and inflation expectations"), "a couple of participants" in the same camp, and "some" and "a few" participants who argued against easing, so there were distinct camps about the policy path. Disagreement is mostly about timing and risk management, with a shared baseline of a strong labor market and the economy near its objectives. This is a typical one-dissent meeting with some added reservations. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 38).** A dedicated paragraph on financial developments: conditions "supportive" but "premised importantly on expectations that the Federal Reserve would ease"; "many" noted the inverted 10-year/3-month spread and "several" tied it to slowdown risk; "a few" flagged debt issuance and "generally high asset valuations", and "a few" warned that easing risked "fueling financial imbalances". A couple noted debt burdens could amplify a downturn. Several participants address it, but as context for the outlook rather than the focus. Score 38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 30).** The signal points toward easing with data dependence: members "agreed to remove the 'patient' language" and to say the Committee "will act as appropriate to sustain the expansion"; "many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted" if the recent developments persisted, and "several others" said it "could well be appropriate" if the outlook deteriorated. It is conditional, not a commitment, so it sits in the easing-lean band. Score 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 60).** "Participants judged that uncertainties and downside risks ... had increased significantly"; "many participants attached significant odds to scenarios with less favorable outcomes"; "many" said risks to growth and inflation had "shifted notably"; "participants generally agreed that downside risks ... had risen materially". The wording is genuine and unusually elevated, but the baseline outlook (strong labor market, moderate growth) is retained and some see the risk shift as recent. Score 60, at the upper end of "elevated".
