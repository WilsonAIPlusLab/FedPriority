## Evidence for changed dimensions

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 58).** The current action is a hold, which participants "agreed" was appropriate, so the lean comes from the forward view. Many participants said that if incoming data continued to support their outlook it "would likely soon be appropriate to take another step in removing policy accommodation." Participants generally expected "further gradual increases" in the target range, and many said the "accommodative" characterization would soon be dropped. Against this, many stressed that the path depends on incoming data, and some cited reasons for proceeding gradually (neutral-rate uncertainty, ELB constraints). A hold with a clear tightening bias, not a majority calling for an immediate hike, gives 58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15).** The vote record shows eight votes for and "Voting against this action: None." Differences were limited to emphasis and interpretation: a few saw "some labor market slack" while others judged conditions tight, several wanted close attention to yield curve inversion while others rejected inferring causality, and arguments for and against the countercyclical capital buffer were discussed. None of these split the Committee on policy, so this sits at the top of the near-consensus band.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 36).** Participants "remarked on the extent to which financial conditions remained supportive," and "participants who commented on financial stability" noted elevated asset valuations, easy corporate borrowing terms and stronger bank capital. A few emphasized that risks could be reduced by higher capital cushions, with the countercyclical capital buffer debated, and several discussed the yield curve. Some also raised the concern that an economy beyond potential could produce "financial imbalances." This is a distinct but not dominant topic, with several participants engaged, so it sits just above the limited-discussion band.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 68).** The signal is mildly tightening. Participants generally expected "further gradual increases in the target range," the vote record's statement repeats this, and many said the "accommodative" language would "no longer be appropriate" fairly soon because the funds rate was moving closer to neutral. The guidance stays data-dependent ("timing and size ... would depend on" realized and expected conditions), and the stance was still described as accommodative, so it falls in the 60-75 band, not the clear-tightening band.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** "All participants pointed to ongoing trade disagreements" as an important source of uncertainty and risk, and most saw a trade escalation as a "potentially consequential downside risk." A number emphasized "considerable uncertainty in estimates of the neutral rate." Offsetting this, participants generally viewed risks as "roughly balanced," most said incoming information had not altered their outlook, and the language is conventional risk discussion without scenario-range or model-doubt talk. That is somewhat elevated, not elevated or high.
