## Evidence for changed dimensions

The old analysis scored every dimension N/A on the stated ground that the file held only about 25 lines of administrative text. The new input contains the full participants' discussion, the directive and the vote record, so every dimension below is re-scored from it. The TEXT DIFF lists no sentences that are only in the new input, which is inconsistent with the old analysis's description of its input; the re-scoring rests on the new text alone.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE (CORRECTED, 40).** Participants see current inflation as below the midpoint: "subdued increases in prices" and inflation "relatively subdued in comparison with past experience". Upward risk is acknowledged ("risks remained in the direction of rising price inflation"; favorable influences "likely to erode"), which keeps the score from going lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 62).** Above potential: "already tight resources", "unsustainable pace of domestic demand and rising resource utilization" (a number of members), and one member saw activity expanding "at an unsustainable pace for some time".

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 66).** Tight: "tight labor markets throughout the nation", "demand for many types of workers exceeded the supply in many regions", "a number of members reported" growth held back by labor scarcity. Compensation pickup described as moderate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 66).** Strong: "strength in all major sectors", consumer spending an "important underpinning for robust economic expansion", capital spending "ongoing surge". Some vulnerability to financial markets noted.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, 64).** Above trend: "sharp increase in productivity in the second and third quarters", "encouraging development", possibly "a higher trend for a time", but the persistence was uncertain.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 32).** Loose: "broadly supportive financial conditions" and, from some members, "overall financial conditions remained quite supportive despite the recent market turmoil and high real short-term interest rates".

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 28).** Level reading from availability: "credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available on favorable terms, perhaps overly so". No spread language, hence Low confidence.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48).** "very large gains in stock market wealth" supported spending; "a substantial decline in equity values would reduce household wealth". Price and wealth channel without a valuation verdict, so near neutral, Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 57).** Some members worried about "additional appreciation of the dollar" with adverse effects on net exports; earlier appreciation had a "lagged response" on inflation. Modest strength concern.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 44).** Only Broaddus's dissent mentions "the recent apparent reduction in inflationary expectations"; slightly below anchored-at-target, thin evidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 56).** Lean hawkish: "all but one member endorsed" an unchanged stance, all agreed risks "tilted toward rising inflation", "other members" (a subset) wanted near-term tightening, and Broaddus dissented for "a modest tightening". Most preferred to wait because of Asian turmoil ("a tightening ... risked an oversized reaction").

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 36).** One formal dissent (11 for, 1 against) from the hawkish side. Two camps on timing (some stressed favorable inflation trends, others near-term tightening) but shared the risk assessment and nearly all backed the decision.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 48).** Moderate attention: "turmoil in Asian financial markets", "global financial markets had not yet settled down", "markets still skittish", plus credit "perhaps overly so" and equity-value risks; remains one theme among several.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 65).** Mild tightening bias: members wanted to "retain ... the existing asymmetry toward restraint" and viewed "the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing". No commitment to act soon.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** Elevated: "injecting new uncertainties", "the ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asia was unknown", "too early to judge the persistence" of productivity, the inflation outlook "subject to considerable debate", and an immediate tightening not needed "in light of the uncertainties about the economic outlook".
