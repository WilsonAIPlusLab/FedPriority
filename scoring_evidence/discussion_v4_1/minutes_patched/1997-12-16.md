## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, 38).** The earlier 27 relied on a producer-price statement that is not part of the participants' views in the new input. Participants see goods prices as being held down mainly prospectively: Asian turmoil and the dollar "would tend to intensify import competition and damp the prices of goods", and "declines in import prices would ease inflation pressures". Some key price measures were "indicating declining inflation". Mild downward pressure, thin direct evidence, so 38 with Low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** No member language on inflation expectations. The old quotes came from text not in the new input. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55).** Hold with a residual tightening bias. "nearly all the members favored ... an unchanged policy stance"; "many members" saw "the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening", but "most members agreed that the need ... did not appear to be imminent". A majority preferred a symmetric directive, "other members" a slight preference for keeping the tightening tilt, and one member (Broaddus) wanted tightening now. Lean hawkish but tempered, so 55.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 35).** One formal dissent (Mr. Broaddus, favoring a "modest tightening"), 11 votes for. Views "somewhat more divided" only on the directive's bias, with agreement on holding the funds rate at about 5-1/2 percent. One dissent plus a tactical split, so 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 40).** Moderate attention. Members discuss the Asian financial turmoil ("intensified in Southeast Asia ... and more recently in Korea"), "increased uncertainty about financial asset values", the effect of a "substantial decline in equity values", the risk of "outsized effects in financial markets, especially the foreign exchange markets", and supportive domestic conditions. It is mostly a channel into growth and inflation rather than a stability debate. Score 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 50).** The Committee adopted a directive with "no presumption about the likely direction" of adjustment, and members stressed "a cautious wait-and-see policy posture" and "a flexible approach". Many still anticipated tightening next and some wanted to keep the tilt, while some allowed that the next move could be easing. The signals roughly offset, so neutral at 50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Multiple members stress hard-to-bound uncertainty: the Asian effect "was difficult to judge", "the ultimate extent of the adjustment ... remained unknown", more substantial downward pressure "could not be ruled out", and the "crosscurrents" generated "present uncertainties in the outlook". Elevated but not extreme, so 55.
