## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, value unchanged at 72).** Participants see the labor market as very tight. The members cited "very tight conditions in labor markets" and "already tight labor markets", and housing activity was said to be held back by "shortages of labor". The numeric unemployment rate and claims figures in the old evidence are not in the input. The score stays at 72.

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED, value unchanged at 65).** Participants see productivity growth as above trend and still accelerating. Members noted "indications of further acceleration in productivity" and a "remarkable acceleration in productivity" that had let strong demand be accommodated without a pickup in inflation. Most members accept this as a key factor, although a further acceleration is judged insufficient to close the demand-supply gap. The score stays at 65.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 52 to 42).** Participants describe financial conditions as supportive, which sets the score below 50. Demand was said to be driven by "accommodative financial markets" and business optimism by "the ready availability of both debt and equity financing". Offsets are mortgage rates rising, "somewhat unsettled financial markets" (some members) and the view that further tightening is needed so "financial conditions would adjust sufficiently". The net is somewhat loose, at 42.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 38 to 30).** Participants regard high-tech valuations as stretched. Some members "expressed concern that the historically elevated valuations of many high-tech stocks were subject to a sizable market adjustment", underscored by "increased volatility". Members also noted that equity prices "had posted further gains during the intermeeting period", so no correction is described. The elevated-valuation reading puts the score at 30.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 50 to 52).** The only dollar references are to the "earlier appreciation" whose effects were "diminishing" or "fading". There is no stated current move, so the score leans only slightly above 50, at low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 70).** All members endorsed a tightening to a 6 percent funds rate, saw "substantial risks of rising pressures on labor and other resources", and judged that "additional tightening might well be needed". Some members said a 50 bp move might be needed at some point, but a gradual approach was preferred. This is consensus for continued, measured tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 8).** The vote was unanimous ("Votes against this action: None"), and "all the members endorsed" the proposal. The only nuance is that some members saw a larger move as possibly needed later. This is near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 32).** The topic gets limited attention. Members addressed stock market prospects mainly as a driver of consumer spending, some voiced concern over "historically elevated valuations", and one passage refers to "somewhat unsettled financial markets". It is context rather than the focus of the discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 78).** The forward signal is a clear tightening bias. The Committee "would continue to assess the need for further tightening", "additional tightening might well be needed", and the press statement should keep saying risks are "weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate heightened inflation pressures". The gradual-approach language keeps it below the very top of the range.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 45).** Members said the extent of excess demand and its inflation implications were "subject to a wide range of uncertainty as to both degree and timing", and that "uncertainties continued to surround the economic outlook". This is somewhat elevated but partly rhetorical, since the direction of the risks is stated firmly.
