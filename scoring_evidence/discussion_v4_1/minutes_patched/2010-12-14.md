## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, confidence only).** The participants said only that "credit spreads were roughly unchanged" over the intermeeting period. Neither a high nor a low level is stated, so under the V4.1 level rule the text offers direction (none) and no level information. Score kept at 50 +-7, confidence lowered from Moderate to Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED): 27.** Dovish. "Nearly all Committee members agreed to continue expanding" holdings of longer-term securities, and "members generally felt that the change in the outlook was not sufficient to warrant any adjustments"; "some" indicated a "fairly high threshold" for changing the program. Offsetting: one member dissented for a less accommodative stance, "a few participants" saw inflation-expectation risks from the balance-sheet expansion, and the Committee should "continue its planning for the eventual exit." QE-era anchor (15-25) nudged up for the hawkish minority and improving outlook.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED): 35.** One formal dissent: Hoenig judged accommodation "inconsistent with the Committee's long-run mandate" and wanted the Committee to "begin preparing markets for a reduction in policy accommodation." Otherwise "nearly all" agreed on the policy action, with differences mostly about the balance of risks ("a range of views about the risks"). A single explained dissent with some economic disagreement about risks fits the 31-45 band, below the QE2 debate anchor.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED): 45.** Several participants addressed the "significant backup in yields" and its causes (including year-end positioning and mortgage hedging flows), credit conditions having "eased further," and "market stresses in Europe" with possible spillovers to U.S. financial markets and institutions. A dissenting member cited "economic and financial imbalances." Meaningful but one topic among many, so a moderate score.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED): 20.** Easing bias. The Committee reiterated that conditions "are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period" and continued to buy $600 billion by end-June 2011. Pointing the other way, purchases would be reviewed "as needed" and a few spoke of exit planning, but no tightening signal was given. "Low for long" band (16-25).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED): 40.** Participants "held a range of views about the risks," listing upside possibility (a few), and downside risks (housing, state and local finances, European strains), with the outcome "most likely" a gradual pickup. Standard-to-somewhat-elevated caveats, with no claim of unusual or unbounded uncertainty.
