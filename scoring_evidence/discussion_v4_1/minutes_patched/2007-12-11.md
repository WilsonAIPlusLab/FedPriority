## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 44).** Participants see output growth slowing but no explicit slack language: spending outlook "somewhat weaker", growth "somewhat more sluggish", then expansion "at about its trend rate in 2009"; some data showed the economy "retained forward momentum". Mildly below potential, indirect evidence, so 44 with Low confidence.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 50).** Participants note "Job growth rebounded somewhat in October and November" and expect continuing gains, though slower than in recent years. The labor market is described as retaining momentum, with no level language, so near-neutral at 50, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 64).** Many participants describe lenders tightening terms, diminished interbank liquidity and "further tightening of credit and deterioration of financial market conditions", making policy "somewhat restrictive". Some participants cite offsets: the cost of credit to most nonfinancial firms "remained relatively low" and consumer credit "readily available". Somewhat-tight to tight, so 64.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants do not discuss the Treasury market; only term funding and interbank pressures are mentioned. N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 50).** Some participants note corporate spreads "had widened over the intermeeting period, especially for speculative-grade issues" (direction), while stating the cost of credit to most nonfinancial firms "remained relatively low" (level) and that firms outside real estate saw conditions "somewhat tighter". Term interbank spreads showed "pronounced widening", and nonconforming mortgage markets were troubled. Level information points to neither side clearly, so 50, Low confidence. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (64) sits above this because it reflects funding-market strain and credit availability, not corporate spread levels.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** No participant comment on equity markets in the new text. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 33).** Participants refer to "past declines in the dollar" putting upward pressure on import prices and "the weaker dollar" supporting exports, and some cited it as an inflation risk. A modest, repeated weakness theme, so below 50 at 33, Moderate confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 33).** "Nearly all members" judged a 25bp cut appropriate, saying policy "now appeared to be somewhat restrictive" with downside risks risen; one member (Rosengren) wanted a "more aggressive policy response"; some noted a feedback loop could "require a substantial further easing". Counterweights: 75bp already delivered, lagged effects, "some inflation pressures and risks remained", and possible reversal of cuts. Moderate dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 30).** Nine votes for, one against (Rosengren, wanting a "more decisive policy response"). Participants otherwise broadly agreed on the assessment and direction; a typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 68).** Participants "discussed in detail the resurgence of stresses in financial markets", covering major-firm losses, consumer credit delinquencies, lender tightening, bank balance-sheet pressure, term funding and the TAF, and warned of an "unfavorable feedback loop". A prominent topic that drives the policy decision.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 45).** The Committee "decided to refrain from providing an explicit assessment of the balance of risks" and agreed to "remain exceptionally alert", with members "prepared to adjust the stance of monetary policy if prospects for economic growth or inflation were to worsen". Two-sided data-dependent language, with downside and further-easing risks given more weight; slightly below neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 62).** Participants "agreed that heightened financial stress ... made the outlook for the economy considerably more uncertain"; the situation was "quite fluid and the economic outlook unusually uncertain"; the statement says developments "have increased the uncertainty". Elevated to high but not extreme: 62.
