## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE (CORRECTED, 58).** Participants see inflation modestly above target: it "had moved up since the beginning of the year and remained somewhat above" 2 percent, "somewhat elevated" near term, with "some" saying tariff effects were "somewhat muted". Only "a few other participants" said progress had stalled, and "a couple" saw inflation near target ex-tariffs. A majority stress upside risks, but "some" see less upside risk than earlier. Fits 51-60; score 58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (FILLED, 50).** Mixed, netting to trend. "Lower consumption growth" slowed H1 activity, yet "several participants" saw "some firming of consumption expenditures this quarter" and "many participants noted strong high-tech investment"; offsets are "continued weakness in the housing market" (several), greater household price sensitivity (some) and agricultural headwinds (a few). Score 50, Moderate confidence.

**PRODUCTIVITY (FILLED, 56).** Discussed only as an inflation channel: "a few participants suggested that productivity gains may be reducing inflation pressures" and "a couple" said continued gains would reduce inflation. Implies modestly positive, above-trend gains without a level assessment. Score 56, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 42).** Participants see conditions as accommodating: "several participants noted that financial conditions were supportive of economic activity," and "some" judged that "by several measures, financial conditions suggested that monetary policy may not be particularly restrictive." The old 55 reflected the stance of policy ("restrictive"), not conditions. "Supportive" requires < 50; score 42 (generally supportive).

**CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old evidence (private credit, stretched valuations, AI-related equity correction, nonbank credit failures) does not appear in the participants' views in the rebuilt input; it was staff/financial-stability text. Participants say nothing about spreads, equity valuations or banks, so each is N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants do not discuss services or shelter price inflation; the housing remark is about activity ("weakness in the housing market"). N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 36).** Moderately dovish: "almost all participants supported reducing the target range ¼ percentage point," and "most judged that it likely would be appropriate to ease policy further over the remainder of this year." Tempered by "a few participants" seeing "merit in keeping the federal funds rate unchanged" and "some" counselling a "cautious approach" on further cuts; one member wanted 50bp. Score 36.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** One formal dissent (Governor Miran, preferring a 50bp cut and citing a lower neutral rate), plus "a few participants" who could have supported holding, and "a range of views" on how restrictive policy is. Differences are chiefly about pace and risk balance, not the economic assessment. Score 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 18).** Passing references only: "several" on supportive financial conditions, "some" on financial-conditions measures of restrictiveness, and "a few" on money-market conditions and reserves. No financial-stability risk discussion. Score 18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 35).** Easing lean with data dependence: "most judged that it likely would be appropriate to ease policy further," yet policy "was not on a preset course," and the committee "will carefully assess incoming data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks"; "almost all" said the Committee is "well positioned to respond in a timely way." Score 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55).** Elevated but not extreme: participants "indicated that their outlooks for the labor market were uncertain," "uncertainty remained about the inflation effects" of tariffs, and "high uncertainty" affects hiring; the approved statement says "Uncertainty about the economic outlook remains elevated." Standard two-sided risk language rather than deep uncertainty. Score 55.
