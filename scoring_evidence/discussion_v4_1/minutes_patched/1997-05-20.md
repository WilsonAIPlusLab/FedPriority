## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 46).** The old quote about "currently elevated levels of stock market prices" and a possible correction does not appear in the input. The only equity reference is the Committee text that "share prices in equity markets have risen considerably", a price move with no participant valuation view. Under the rubric ("if members discuss price moves without valuation commentary, default to 45-55") this is scored 46, Low confidence.

**INFLATION_GOODS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The old score rested on producer-price declines and energy prices that are not in the input. Members discuss overall prices ("increases in prices had remained subdued", dollar appreciation "helped to damp domestic inflation") but not goods prices separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The cited "persisting uncertainties about behavior in labor markets and the level and growth of the economy's sustainable potential" is not about inflation expectations, and no survey or market expectations measure is mentioned. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 57).** "All but one" could support unchanged policy, "some also expressed a preference for some tightening at this meeting", and all supported an asymmetric directive "tilted toward tightening". Broaddus dissented for higher real rates. Hold with a tightening bias and a hawkish minority: lean hawkish, 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 33).** One formal dissent (Broaddus, in the hawkish direction). Some members preferred tightening now, but "all but one of these members could accept a wait-and-see policy stance". Disagreement is about timing and risk weighting, not the assessment. Score 33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 12).** Only brief, incidental references: "supportive financial conditions", "ready availability of financing", "large consumer debts", and "many banking institutions had tightened lending terms" for marginal consumer borrowers. Not a focus of the discussion. Score 12, Moderate confidence.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 66).** Those endorsing steady policy "agreed that some tightening might well be needed later", and the asymmetric directive was seen as consistent with a view that members "might be required to make such a decision in the not-too-distant future", while not implying action in the weeks immediately ahead. Mild tightening bias, 66.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 60).** Several participants stressed uncertainty: the slowdown was "subject to substantial uncertainty", consumer spending and the inflation outlook "subject to considerable uncertainty", members "found it very difficult to account for the surprisingly benign behavior of inflation", and "historical experience was not a fully reliable guide". Elevated but with standard-style phrasing, so 60.
