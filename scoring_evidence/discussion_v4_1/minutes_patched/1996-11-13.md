## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants do not discuss productivity anywhere in the input. The only related remark is a suggestion that restrained labor-compensation increases "probably were a transitory phenomenon", which concerns wages, not productivity growth. The old evidence about efficiency gains and technology investment is not present in the text, so the dimension is N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 44 +-8, Low).** The old valuation quotes are absent from the input. What participants actually say: "marked increases" in stock-holding values support consumer spending, and "the possibility of a correction in the stock market" is listed by members as a negative risk. This is price and wealth commentary with a mild correction worry but no explicit valuation judgment, so the rubric default of 45-55 applies, nudged slightly toward concern: 44, Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** The only mention of the dollar is the directive's information recap ("depreciated slightly over the intermeeting period"). That is a data summary, not a participants' view, and the vote-record block is permitted as evidence only for dissent and policy lean. No participant comments on the dollar, so N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 53 +-5, High).** Direction: slightly hawkish, a hold with a tightening bias. All members could support an unchanged stance with the bias toward restraint retained; "several" viewed the bias as desirable because risks "remained tilted... toward rising inflation", while "other members" thought a symmetric directive might be more consistent with diminished pressures but agreed a shift was "premature". The favorable data gave "a desirable opportunity to pause".

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15 +-5, High).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows twelve votes for and "None" against, and "all the members" supported the directive. Differences are limited to whether inflation risks were tilted up or "more evenly balanced" and the symmetric-versus-asymmetric wording, which was resolved without objection.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 15 +-6, Moderate).** Brief mentions only: "the level of consumer indebtedness which had strained the liquidity of many households", tightening consumer credit standards, and a possible stock market correction. These appear as inputs to the consumption outlook, not as a focus of deliberation.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 60 +-6, Moderate).** Mild tightening bias. The Committee retained "a bias toward the possible firming of reserve conditions"; several said an asymmetric directive "would best reflect their views even if, as seemed likely, intermeeting developments did not prompt a policy tightening". Moving to symmetric was seen as one that might signal less concern about a "modest upward trajectory in price inflation". The signal is a bias without an expected near-term move.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40 +-8, Moderate).** Somewhat elevated but partly formulaic. The outlook "remained subject, as usual, to considerable uncertainty"; the fiscal outlook had a "range of potential developments... especially wide" injecting "considerable uncertainty"; inventories were subject to "substantial uncertainties"; and the inflation outlook was "far from certain". The "as usual" framing and the largely favorable assessment keep it in the lower-middle range.
