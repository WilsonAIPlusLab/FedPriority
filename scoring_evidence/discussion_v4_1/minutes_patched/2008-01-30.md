## Evidence for changed dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE (60).** Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. "Participants agreed that the inflation data ... had been disappointing," headline inflation "generally above 2 percent over the past four years," and participants "related anecdotal evidence of firms ... being able to pass on those costs." Offsetting: "many believed" slack and flattening energy prices would ease pressures, and most expected moderation. Fisher's dissent cites headline above 3 percent. Score 60.

**OUTPUT_GAP (40).** Participants see output below potential: "the associated slack in resource utilization" and "reduction in pressures on resources" given weak growth. Below-potential language requires a score under 50; slack described as emerging rather than deep. Score 40.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44).** Labor market softening: "job growth had slowed appreciably," "emerging weakness in the labor market." Some participants nonetheless "cited evidence that workers in some sectors were still in short supply" and that the market "remained resilient." Score 44.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (33).** Below trend: consumer spending "had decelerated considerably," wealth effects from housing and equities, and "several participants reported that firms ... were reducing capital expenditures"; manufacturing "slowed or contracted in many districts." A few noted more upbeat technology and energy sectors. Score 33.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (72).** Tight: "credit conditions had become generally more restrictive," "broader financial conditions had tightened significantly," "availability of credit to consumers and businesses appeared to be tightening." Money-market functioning improved, a partial offset. Score 72 (above 50 as required).

**CREDIT_SPREADS (66).** Participants describe stressed credit but give no spread levels. Evidence is on level of stress and access: banks "tightened lending standards and pricing terms on business loans," standards raised "especially sharply" on commercial real estate, "strains remained evident in a number of other financial markets," and many worried about guarantor downgrades. Scored on the high stress level, not on the direction of change. Moderate confidence because no spread level is stated. Score 66.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (55).** Equity prices are mentioned only as a wealth-effect channel ("the drop in equity prices ... implied reductions in household wealth," many participants). No valuation or fair-value commentary, so the default band applies. Score 55, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (60).** Mixed with a stress lean: many perceived that guarantor downgrades "could put increased strains on financial markets," banks tightened standards, and markets were "still vulnerable." Offset: "Banks appeared to be making some progress in strengthening their balance sheets" via capital raising. Score 60.

**INFLATION_GOODS (60).** Commodity and energy prices "had remained stubbornly high for quite some time" (some participants), firms facing "increasing input cost pressures," though futures embed a leveling-off. Score 60.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (55).** "On balance, expectations seemed to remain fairly well anchored," but "some survey measures ... had edged up" and market compensation "had climbed" (attributed partly to inflation risk), and participants stressed that continued stability "was essential." Slightly above anchored. Score 55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (27).** Dovish: "most members believed that a further significant easing in policy was warranted," a 50 bp cut a week after 75 bp, with a low real rate seen as appropriate. Hawkish counterweights: "some noted" a reversal of part of the easing, possibly rapid, might later be appropriate; Fisher dissented in favor of no change. Score 27.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (35).** One formal dissent (Mr. Fisher, hawkish, citing headline inflation above 3 percent, unanchoring risk, upside inflation risks greater than growth risks). Nine voted in favor and the Committee otherwise "agreed" on the statement and on downside risks. The disagreement is about risk weights, not the broad assessment. Score 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (68).** Prominent topic: strains in financial markets, "many perceived" guarantor downgrades as a risk, "some" noted an "adverse feedback loop" of tighter credit and weaker spending, credit tightening in housing and business lending, plus the closing session on credit ratings and risk management failures at large institutions (the staff presentation portion not scored). Score 68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (27).** Easing bias: the statement says "downside risks to growth remain" and the Committee "will act in a timely manner as needed to address those risks." Tempered by inflation "monitor ... carefully" language and "some" noting a possible reversal once growth improves. Score 27.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (62).** "Significant uncertainties surrounded this outlook," "elevated volatility ... reflected increased uncertainty about the economic outlook," "several participants noted that the risks of a downturn ... were significant," and concern about a feedback loop. Elevated to high but not the extreme levels of late 2008. Score 62.
