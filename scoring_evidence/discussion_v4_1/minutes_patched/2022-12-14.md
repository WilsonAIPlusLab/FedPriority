## Evidence for changed dimensions

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The old score of 40 cited "elevated valuations ... particularly in the CRE sector", "susceptibility of some nonbank financial institutions to runs" and "large, unrealized losses on some banks' securities portfolios". None of this text is in the participants' views or the vote record in the new input, and the diff shows no such sentences in the old input either, so it was not participant text. Participants do not discuss bank health, capital or lending standards. Score N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 72).** Direction is hawkish. All participants agreed to raise the range 50 basis points, a slower pace, "to better allow the Committee to assess" progress. Participants "continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range ... would be appropriate", "all participants had raised their assessment of the appropriate path", and "no participants anticipated" cuts in 2023. Several cautioned against "prematurely loosening". The slowed pace and a couple of participants seeing inflation risks "becoming more balanced" keep this below the 75bp-era level of the 80s. Score 72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 12).** Direction is near-consensus. The vote record states "Voting against this action: None", and "all participants agreed" on a 50bp hike. Differences were minor and about emphasis or risk (a couple saw inflation risks becoming more balanced; a number stressed communicating that slower hikes do not signal weaker resolve). Score 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** Direction is limited attention. Participants noted financial conditions "had eased" and that "an unwarranted easing in financial conditions" would complicate policy; a few remarked that the inverted yield curve "bore watching". There is no discussion of financial-sector vulnerabilities in the participants' views, so attention is brief and mostly as context for policy. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 74).** Direction is tightening. Participants anticipated "ongoing increases", said a "restrictive policy stance would need to be maintained" until inflation is clearly on a sustained downward path "likely to take some time", and the vote record repeats that ongoing increases will be appropriate. Most participants stressed "flexibility and optionality" and meeting-by-meeting data dependence, which tempers the signal. Score 74.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 66).** Direction is high uncertainty. "Participants generally noted that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high", with "considerable uncertainty" around consumer spending and "heightened uncertainty" for inflation and activity; most stressed retaining flexibility. This is not extreme: there is no talk of unbounded or unknowable outcomes, and several risks are laid out in a conventional way. Score 66.
