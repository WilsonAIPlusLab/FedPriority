## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED).** Participants see conditions as supportive but fading; the old quote on ample financing is not in the new input. Support now: the forecasts reflect "a waning of the financial stimulus that had boosted domestic demand", rapid stock-market wealth gains boosting consumption, and "somewhat higher levels of market interest rates that business borrowers now faced". Mild, indirect evidence; below 50 but closer to neutral. Score 43, Low confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The new input contains no statement by participants on spreads, credit terms or availability; the old support ("improved markedly", "favorable terms") is not present. "Financial markets and foreign economies stabilized and recovered" is generic market recovery, not a credit-spread statement. N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** Participants discuss stocks only as a driver of spending: "rapidly rising stock market wealth" would fade "should prices in the stock market tend to level out as many expected." No valuation concern appears in the new input (the old "elevated valuations" quote is absent; "high-technology" refers to equipment). Price-move discussion without valuation commentary defaults to 45-55. Score 46, Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (FILLED).** Mixed and broadly stable: improving foreign conditions "supported the foreign exchange value of other currencies relative to the dollar", the directive says the dollar "has changed little", and McTeer (one member) says "the dollar remains strong". Stable, no concern. Score 50, Low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED).** Value kept at 44 but on different, indirect evidence: import-price declines "helped to suppress inflation and inflation expectations over the last two years" and "were not likely to be repeated". Expectations are seen as contained but losing a restraining force. Confidence lowered to Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Moderately hawkish: "all but one member supported" a 25bp increase to about 5 percent, "most members" saw it as "a desirable and cautious preemptive step" against "a significant risk of rising inflation", and "many members expressed growing concern" about demand and tighter labor markets. Tempered by a majority favoring a symmetrical directive and believing "further firming ... might not be necessary in the near term". Score 63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** One formal dissent (McTeer, who saw "few signs of inflation in the pipeline" and "would have preferred to continue to test the growth limits of the new economy"), plus a split over directive wording ("the members were divided") that was bridged because all tightening supporters could accept symmetry. Moderate differences extending to the inflation assessment for one member. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Brief, passing references only: the fall 1998 easings were against "unsettled global markets", the recovery removed the need for that protection, and stock prices matter for consumer wealth. No sustained discussion of stability. Score 14.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Mild tightening tilt but no commitment. The majority said risks "would still remain tilted toward rising inflation" and the post-meeting announcement would reference those concerns, yet chose a symmetrical directive because further firming "would depend importantly on future developments". Holdouts for asymmetry saw "the odds as reasonably high that further tightening would be needed before the end of the year". Score 56.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Several explicit statements: "the timing and extent of a potential rise in inflation were subject to considerable uncertainty", "the marked degree of uncertainty relating to the extent and timing of prospective inflationary pressures", "it remained unclear how long faster gains in productivity could continue", and uncertainty over productivity and velocity in the money-range discussion. Real but partly standard hedging around a decisive 25bp move. Score 58.
