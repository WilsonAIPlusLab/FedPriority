## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 70 to 66).** Participants see spending as above trend: "consumer spending had continued to increase at a very rapid pace", supported by reopening, fiscal and monetary support and accumulated savings, and "housing demand to remain strong". Offsetting this, construction was "restrained by shortages of materials", and manufacturing "continued to be solid but was restrained by production bottlenecks". The old quote about business equipment investment rising "at a strong pace" does not appear in the participants' views, so the investment leg is not supported. Net: well above trend on consumption, solid but supply-limited elsewhere; 66 +-7, High.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 52).** Participants "judged that the current stance of monetary policy remained appropriate" (balanced), and "generally" saw the "substantial further progress" standard as not yet met, particularly for labor. "Most participants" anticipated the standard "could be reached this year", and "some participants" would prepare to reduce purchases "relatively soon"; "a few" worried about financial-system risk build-up. Against this, "several" urged patience and "a few" stressed Delta downside risks. Hold with a slight tightening tilt toward tapering: 52 +-7, Moderate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 22).** The vote record shows all eleven voting members in favor ("Voting against this action: None") and "All members agreed to keep the target range" unchanged. Differences were about emphasis: timing of taper (this year vs early next year, which the participants' text frames as "some" vs "several"), reliance on pre-pandemic labor benchmarks, and inflation persistence versus downward-bias risk. No formal dissents, no sharp exchanges: broad agreement, 22 +-6, High.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 45).** A full paragraph: "Participants who commented on financial stability emphasized the risks associated with elevated valuations across many asset classes"; "a few" on low rates generating imbalances; "some" on house prices and on crypto and central-counterparty collateral risks; "a few" on stablecoins; "a few" tied accommodative financial conditions to risks to the dual mandate. Several participants addressed it meaningfully but not most: moderate attention, 45 +-7, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 55).** The rate-path guidance remains strongly accommodative (range kept at 0 to 1/4 percent until labor conditions reach maximum employment and inflation is on track to moderately exceed 2 percent), but the signal on purchases leans toward normalization: "most participants" saw the standard "could be reached this year", participants agreed "the Committee would provide advance notice", and the statement adds that the Committee "will continue to assess progress in coming meetings". "Several" stressed that tapering is not a predetermined path to rate increases. Mild tightening bias on the balance sheet, no rate-hike signal: 55 +-8, Moderate.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 58).** "Many participants remarked that uncertainty was quite high", with Delta and slowing vaccinations posing downside risks; "a number" said supply and labor disruptions complicate reading the data; "several" emphasized "considerable uncertainty" about resolution of labor shortages and bottlenecks, and business contacts expressed "uncertainty and pessimism" on supply easing. Many, not most, and risks framed as two-sided rather than unbounded: elevated to high, 58 +-7, High.
