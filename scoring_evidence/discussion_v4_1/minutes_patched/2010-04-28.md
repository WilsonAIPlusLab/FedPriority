## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants describe private-debt spreads as moving narrower and as helping policy rates pass through to the cost of capital ("narrowing credit spreads in private debt markets were allowing low policy rates to be reflected more fully in the cost of capital"). They give no level, such as tight, wide or normal. Under the V4.1 rule a direction-only description scores 45-55 on the side of the move, so it goes slightly below 50 (46) with Low confidence. The old 38 assumed a level that the text does not state.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** The lean is dovish but not toward more easing. The Committee maintained the 0 to 1/4 percent range, and "nearly all members" judged it appropriate to reiterate that conditions would warrant exceptionally low rates for an extended period. "A few members" saw the risks of an early start to tightening as exceeding those of a later start, and Mr. Hoenig dissented in favor of raising rates "toward 1 percent this summer." The majority position is maximum accommodation held in place, with a small hawkish minority, so 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** There was one formal dissent (Mr. Hoenig), about the forward-guidance language and the timing of liftoff, not about the economic assessment. Nine voted in favor. Most participants shared the same outlook, with mixed views only on inflation risks ("Some participants saw the risks to inflation as tilted to the downside... Others... higher inflation"). This is a typical one-dissent meeting, so 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Several participants discussed financial conditions: improved market functioning, "bank lending was still contracting," small-business credit, "many small and regional banks were vulnerable" to CRE losses, and "some participants expressed concern that a crisis in Greece... could have an adverse effect on U.S. financial markets." The dissent raised "the buildup of future financial imbalances." The attention is moderate and sits beside a mainly macroeconomic discussion, so 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The forward signal is easing-leaning: "nearly all members" endorsed "exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period." The language is a conditional extended-period commitment, not a threshold or date. A hawkish counter-signal exists, because all participants stress communicating the ability and intent to withdraw accommodation "at the appropriate time and pace," and one member dissented against the language. The net is a strong easing bias, so 14.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants note that firms' reluctance to hire or invest reflects "uncertainty about the economic outlook and future government policies." They also cite Greece and peripheral European fiscal strains, downside risks to housing and mixed inflation risks. Participants' views "had not changed appreciably," and the forecasts were described as "broadly in line," so the uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not high, 40.
