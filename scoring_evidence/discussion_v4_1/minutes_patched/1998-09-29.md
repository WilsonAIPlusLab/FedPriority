## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 55 +-10, Low).** Participants describe a clear widening in direction only. Private rates over Treasuries "had gapped higher," and the increases were "especially large for lower-grade borrowers." The new input states no level for spreads. Many members said risk spreads "previously had embodied an overly optimistic assessment," and that some correction was "a positive development," which implies spreads moved from too narrow toward normal. The old "still at unusually high levels" quote came from outside the participants' views. Under the V4.1 level rule, direction only scores 52-55 on the side of the move, with Low confidence. The tightening itself is captured in FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (72).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 32 +-7, High).** Dovish. All members endorsed a 25bp cut as "added insurance" against worsening financial conditions. The cut was kept to 25bp because several noted inflation risk "was still present" and many saw "the need for a cautious policy action." A small majority favored an easing-tilted directive. This is moderate dovish: unanimous easing, but limited in size and with some inflation caution.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18 +-5, High).** Near-consensus. The vote record shows no votes against. "All the members" supported the directive and "could accept" the easing bias. The disagreement was confined to whether the directive should be symmetric or asymmetric, a tactical difference. Some members' view that a credit crunch "could not be ruled out" is a minor emphasis difference.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 78 +-6, High).** Attention is dominant. Members discussed global turmoil spilling into U.S. markets, the risk of a "credit crunch," bank tightening, falling equities and the added LTCM/hedge-fund section. The stated rationale for the cut is to "cushion" the effects of tighter financial conditions. It rivals the real-economy assessment in emphasis.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 25 +-7, Moderate).** A mild-to-moderate easing bias. A "small majority" favored the asymmetric directive, which "would underscore the Committee's readiness to respond promptly." All accepted the tilt toward ease. Others saw uncertainties as "sufficiently great on both sides" and said unanticipated developments would drive future moves. The signal is a clear tilt but not a commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 68 +-7, High).** High, with genuine uncertainty. "At a time of abnormally high volatility and very substantial uncertainty, it was impossible to predict how financial conditions in the United States would evolve." How much business investment would weaken was "a major uncertainty." Net exports were "subject to a great deal of uncertainty." Some members said a credit crunch "could not be ruled out." Domestic data still showed strength, which keeps the score below the very-high band.
