## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 30 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see the labor market as well below full employment and weakening: businesses were said to "cancel capital and other discretionary expenditures and lay off workers" (reports from contacts), and consumer spending was "responding to lower employment and tighter credit". The unemployment-rate and payroll figures cited in the old analysis are staff or data text, not in the participants' views, so the score rests on qualitative language only; confidence lowered to Moderate and score nudged to 30.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 76 +-10, Moderate).** Participants said "risk aversion of investors increased, driving credit spreads sharply higher" and that, despite stabilizing efforts, "risk spreads remained higher, asset prices lower, and credit conditions tighter than prior to the recent disruptions", with intermediaries cutting back lending. The old "unprecedented levels" for CDS spreads is not in the new input. Direction is clearly sharply wider; the level is conveyed indirectly (higher than before the disruption, credit tight, reduced access), which places the score above 50 in the "wide, reduced access" band but below the old crisis-extreme value. No explicit "elevated"/"wide" level statement exists, hence Moderate confidence and a wider band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 60 +-10, Low).** The only participant statement is that "equity prices had varied widely and were substantially lower, on net" (plus a sharp drop in emerging-market equity and concern that hedge funds might have to sell assets). There is no valuation commentary, so the rubric's price-move default (45-55) applies, raised modestly for the size of the decline and the asset-sale concern. The old "wealth destruction" rationale is not in the text. Confidence Low (one passing statement).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 13 +-5, High).** Strongly dovish. Committee members "agreed that significant easing in policy was warranted", saw "a forceful policy response" as appropriate, and cut 50 bp to 1 percent; "the Committee agreed that it would take whatever steps were necessary to support the recovery"; "some suggested that additional policy easing could well be appropriate at future meetings". Offsetting: "some" noted limited room and urged moving slowly, so not in the emergency 1-10 band.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 18 +-6, High).** Unanimous vote ("Votes against this action: None."). Differences are about tactics: "some members" worried policy effectiveness was diminished and the Committee should "consider moving slowly", while "others" argued these were reasons for "a more aggressive policy adjustment". Economic assessments were shared. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 85 +-6, High).** Dominant topic: "financial conditions had worsened significantly", the "failure or near failure of a number of major financial institutions", "the negative spiral in which financial strains lead to weaker spending", and the "recent substantial tightening in financial conditions" cited as a reason for the forceful policy response; also review of liquidity facilities. Rivals the economic assessment in emphasis (peak-crisis month).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 20 +-8, Moderate).** Easing bias without a commitment: participants observed that developments "could require the FOMC to further lower its target ... in the future and to review the adequacy of its liquidity facilities", "some suggested that additional policy easing could well be appropriate at future meetings", and the statement says "downside risks to growth remain" and the Committee "will act as needed". "Several" also stressed the need to unwind actions later, and "some" urged caution on remaining room, so mild-to-moderate rather than strong easing bias.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 70 +-8, Moderate).** Turmoil produced "greater uncertainty among businesses and households about economic prospects"; "several participants" expected fiscal stimulus but "were uncertain about the extent and duration"; "some participants noted that the specifics and effectiveness of some government programs ... remained unclear"; views divided on whether recovery comes by mid-2009 or weakness "could persist for some time"; disagreement over whether slack would restrain inflation. High but not "uncharted territory" language, hence 70.
