## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** Participants see spreads as still above normal despite improvement: "risk spreads narrowed in many markets" but "risk spreads remained unusually wide and markets continued to be fragile." The level governs, so the score stays above 50; the old supporting quote about corporate bond spreads being "extremely high by historical standards" does not appear in the participants' views and is dropped. Level language is a single qualitative phrase plus the narrowing in many markets, so the score moves from 72 to 68, +-8, Moderate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Strongly dovish, QE-continuation stance. Members agreed to "continue making purchases" of up to $1.25 trillion of agency MBS, $200 billion of agency debt and $300 billion of Treasuries, and "Some members noted that a further increase in the total amount of purchases might well be warranted"; no tightening voices. Score 15 (QE-era anchor), High.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Near-consensus. "Voting against this action: None"; "all members concurred with waiting to see how the economy and financial conditions respond"; "All members agreed that the statement should note..." Only mild emphasis differences ("Some members" on larger purchases; "Several participants" relaying inflation concerns from contacts). Score 10, High.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** A substantial portion of the participants' discussion concerns financial conditions: "Financial market developments ... were mainly seen as positive" yet "markets continued to be fragile"; "a number of participants noted that investors were concerned" about stress-test publication; "Some participants also referred to mounting losses in commercial real estate"; banks abroad "exposed to substantial further losses"; and the vote record's discussion of borrower-identity stigma undermining "promoting financial stability." Prominent but not dominant alongside the economic assessment; score 62, Moderate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** Strong easing signal. The statement approved in the vote says the Committee "anticipates that economic conditions are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period," with purchases evaluated against the outlook and "all available tools" to be used. Extended-accommodation language without a numeric threshold: score 10, High.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Elevated but easing from the trough. "participants emphasized the tentative nature of the incoming data, which are volatile and subject to revision"; "participants continued to see significant downside risks"; "the global financial system remained vulnerable to further shocks"; financial-crisis recessions "tended to be more severe and protracted." Offset by "some tentative evidence" of stabilization and the improved near-term outlook. Score 60, Moderate.
