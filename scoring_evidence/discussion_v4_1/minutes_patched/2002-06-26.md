## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 38 +-6 Moderate).** Participants see output below potential, but not deeply. Several passages describe "continuing slack in labor and other markets" and "lingering effects of financial and other shocks ... exert[ing] a depressing impact on output and resource use". Members nonetheless expect activity to accelerate "to a pace in the vicinity of, and perhaps somewhat above" potential growth. The old "marked down forecasts" reading is not supported. Bright-line (slack, below) is satisfied.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 36 +-5 Moderate).** Members' forecasts put unemployment at 5-3/4 to 6 percent in Q4 2002, and the policy discussion cites "continuing slack in labor markets". Value unchanged; confidence lowered because the old specific data quotes are not participant text.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 38 +-8 Low).** Participants do not mention spreads. Credit conditions are described as favourable: "ample availability of credit to most consumers" and mortgage financing "at very attractive rates". Under the level rule this is below normal stress. The evidence is indirect (consumer and mortgage credit, not corporate spreads), hence Low confidence. The old 65 rested on staff-side "widened credit spreads" text.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 55 +-8 Low).** The only participant mention is that "weakness in stock prices" could persist and hurt "confidence and household wealth". There is no valuation commentary, so the score sits just above neutral on the depressed-prices side with Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants do not discuss the banking system; the old "sound" quote is not in the new input.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 30 +-6 Moderate).** Discussion of the external sector notes "the sizable decline in the foreign exchange value of the dollar since the start of the year", "market forecasts of appreciable further depreciation", and "recent depreciation of the dollar". Notable weakness, so below 50 as the bright-line rule requires.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 45).** In the policy discussion "inflation expectations appeared to be low and stable", consistent with inflation near 1-1/2 to 1-3/4 percent: anchored, slightly below target.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 55 +-5 Moderate).** Policy is held at 1-3/4 percent with all members agreeing, but "a number of members" said the stance "was too accommodative to be consistent over time" and that an adjustment "toward a less accommodative stance" would be needed at some point. Others stressed the accommodative stance remained "desirable" to counter shocks and that there was "little risk" in waiting. Hold with a mild tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 15 +-5 High).** The vote record shows ten votes for and "None" against. "All the members agreed" on the hold. Differences were limited to how soon and how markets would perceive eventual tightening.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22 +-7 Moderate).** Only brief, contextual references: financial markets "shaken by revelations of accounting irregularities", "financial and other shocks", and possible "financial crisis ... contagion" from South America. Not a focus of the deliberation.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 55 +-5 Moderate).** The committee kept the "balanced" risks sentence and said it "would not be an impediment to adjusting policy". Some members stressed the "inevitability of eventual policy tightening", though others thought markets already anticipated it. Essentially neutral, with a slight tightening tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 55 +-7 Moderate).** "The timing and strength of an upturn ... remained subject to considerable uncertainty" and business spending was "a major source of uncertainty". Counterbalancing this, the members' growth forecasts were fairly specific and they saw risks "on both sides". Elevated but not extreme.
