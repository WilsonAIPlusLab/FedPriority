## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The participants' only statement is that "credit spreads narrowed" over the intermeeting period, a direction of change with no information on the level of spreads (no "compressed", "tight" or "wide" language). The old score of 44 treated "narrowed" as "compressed" and so set the side of 50 by direction, which the v4.1 level rule does not allow. Under the direction-only rule, the score is 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence: narrowing, consistent with the broad easing in financial conditions, gives 47 +-8, Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Participants lean balanced, tilted slightly toward patience on the current action but with a tightening path expected. "Many judged that it was appropriate to wait for additional information", and "several" preferred to defer another increase; "some" others judged an increase "was or would soon be warranted", with "a couple" favoring one at this meeting, and one member (George) dissented for a 25bp hike. The "only gradual increases" language gives a hold with a mild tightening backdrop; the dovish-leaning majority on timing offsets the hawkish minority. Score 48.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** The vote record lists one dissent ("Voting against this action: Esther L. George"), who wanted a 25bp increase citing easier financial conditions, trend growth, solid job growth and the credibility of policy communications. Beyond the vote there were two visible camps on timing and on labor-market slack ("Other participants continued to judge that labor utilization remained below" maximum employment versus "most" at or approaching it), but the disagreement is mostly about timing and risk rather than framework. One dissent plus moderate differences gives 38 (31-45 band).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Participants "also discussed recent developments in financial markets and issues related to financial stability" in a dedicated passage covering Brexit-related market volatility, European (Italian) bank stress, and "several participants" raising CRE overvaluation, elevated equity values relative to earnings and reach-for-yield incentives; "several expressed concern" again in the policy discussion that low rates risked misallocation of capital. This is a moderate, multi-participant discussion, not the dominant topic, so 50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The committee-level signal is a mild tightening bias that is explicitly gradual and conditional: "only gradual increases in the federal funds rate", the rate "likely to remain, for some time, below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run", with the path depending on incoming data and members keeping "policy options open". The reinvestment policy continues until normalization is "well under way". Mild tightening, patient and data-dependent, gives 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** Participants judged that Brexit and the labor-market report had "alleviated two key uncertainties" and that near-term risks "had diminished". Residual uncertainty remained: "some" noted "significant uncertainty" about the medium- to longer-term foreign outlook, "other participants expressed greater uncertainty about the trajectory of inflation", and "a few" cited domestic uncertainty. "Some other participants" did not view uncertainty as unusually elevated and saw risks as balanced. This is normal to somewhat elevated, giving 36.
