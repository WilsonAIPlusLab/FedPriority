## Evidence for changed dimensions

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 40 +-8, Low).** Participants describe credit market conditions as "generally quite accommodative", which puts the level below normal. In the days just before the meeting credit availability for "some highly leveraged and other lower-rated borrowers appeared to be tightening a bit". Spreads themselves are not named by participants. The score is below 50 under the level rule, only modestly so, with Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, 36 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see the dollar as weaker. "The decline in the exchange value of the dollar" was seen as boosting exports, and "the decline in the exchange value of the dollar over recent quarters" was listed among forces that could sustain inflation pressures. This is a modest, multi-quarter depreciation and below 50 by the bright-line rule.

**INFLATION_HOUSING (CORRECTED, N/A).** The only support was a rent-deceleration quote that is not part of the input. Participants discuss housing activity, not shelter prices, so the sub-component is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 53 +-5, Moderate).** Members agreed that holding at 5-1/4 percent was appropriate. The policy text says the "predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate", while growth risks were seen as "more balanced". This is a hold with a slight tightening bias, balanced by the two-sided adjustment language.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 10 +-4, High).** The vote was unanimous (ten for, none against). Members "agreed" on the hold and the statement language. The differences are about emphasis (housing downside versus inflation persistence), not prescription.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22 +-7, Moderate).** A few passages cover subprime mortgage delinquencies, tighter credit for lower-rated borrowers and reassessed risk in "complex and illiquid financial instruments". They sit inside the housing and business-spending discussion, not as a separate theme.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 52 +-5, Moderate).** The statement text says future adjustments "will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth, as implied by incoming information". That is a neutral, data-dependent posture. Inflation as the "predominant concern" gives it a slight upward tilt.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 33 +-7, Moderate).** Housing is called "a key source of uncertainty". Several puzzles are acknowledged (labor tightness versus below-trend growth, the productivity trend), and participants were "wary of drawing any firm conclusions" from a few months of data. Members still thought a moderate expansion "most likely", so uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not unusual.
