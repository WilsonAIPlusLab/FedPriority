## Evidence for changed dimensions

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED).** Participants see the labor market as softening and below balance, citing falling private payrolls for a third month and "a softening in labor markets". The unemployment-rate level the old analysis used is not in the new input. Score stays at 43, with wider UNC (+-7) and Moderate confidence.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED).** No participant comments on Treasury market functioning in the new input. The old evidence came from market-developments text that is not part of the participants' views. Now N/A.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED).** The old evidence (spreads at highest since 2002, jumbo spreads, GSE CDS) is not in the participants' text and gives no level in the new input. Participants do say "many lenders pulled back from risk taking—notably by increasing collateral margins" and that strains would "raise the price and reduce the availability of credit to businesses and households". Credit is described as less available and dearer, which fits "less favorable" credit conditions, but no spread level is given. Score 60, Low confidence, wide band.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED).** The only equity reference is that "declines in equity prices were weighing on household wealth". This is a price move with no valuation commentary, so the default 45-55 applies. Score 55, Low confidence.

**BANKING_STRESS (CORRECTED).** Participants see significant stress in the financial sector: "many financial institutions experiencing significant balance sheet pressures", "many market participants were experiencing greater difficulties obtaining funding". They do not discuss bank capital, failures or solvency, so the old 78 is trimmed to the significant-stress band at 70, Moderate confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED).** The dollar is described as weaker, with "declines in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" supporting exports and "recent depreciation of the dollar" possibly boosting import prices. Weakening means below 50. The "declined notably" wording is not in the new input, so the score moves from 24 to 30 with Moderate confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED).** Strongly dovish on the action: "most members judged that a substantial easing in the stance of monetary policy was warranted" and "a 75 basis point easing ... was appropriate"; the vote was 8-2. Tempered by inflation concern ("inflation pressures had apparently risen") and two dissenters wanting less easing. Score 27.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED).** Two formal dissents (Fisher and Plosser) favoring "easing policy less aggressively", citing the risk that "inflation expectations could potentially become unhinged". The disagreement concerns the size of the cut and inflation versus growth risk, with the remainder of the Committee in agreement and the participants' discussion otherwise similar. Score 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED).** Financial markets get extended attention: stresses "intensified noticeably", markets "unusually fragile", an "adverse feedback loop" discussed, "several" noting persistent credit restriction, plus the new liquidity facilities and a policy rationale that includes "financial market disruptions". It is prominent, but growth, housing and inflation also get substantial space. Score 72.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED).** The statement keeps "downside risks to growth remain" and says the Committee "will act in a timely manner as needed", an easing lean tied to data. It also says it will "continue to monitor inflation developments carefully", which tempers the signal. Score 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED).** "Considerable uncertainty surrounded this forecast"; "some" feared a "more severe and protracted downturn"; house prices a "major source of uncertainty"; "significant uncertainty attended the near-term outlook for price pressures"; members said "appropriately calibrating the stance of policy was difficult". Many participants stress it, with a wide scenario range. Score 68.
