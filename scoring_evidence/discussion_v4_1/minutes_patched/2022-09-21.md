## Evidence for changed dimensions

**PRODUCTIVITY (CORRECTED to N/A).** Productivity is never raised in the participants' views or the vote record. The old score of 30 rested on a quote not found in the input. Topic not discussed, so N/A.

**TREASURY_STRESS (CORRECTED to N/A).** The only Treasury references concern balance-sheet runoff ("maximum planned pace") and the directive. No participant comments on Treasury market functioning, liquidity or volatility. The old "strained liquidity" evidence is absent. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to N/A).** Several participants cite "monetary policy tightening under way in many other economies" and a "weakening global economic outlook", but nobody mentions the dollar or exchange rates. The old "strong U.S. dollar" evidence is absent. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 80).** Strongly hawkish: "all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range ... 75 basis points", "ongoing increases ... would be appropriate", and "many" raised their assessed rate path because inflation was "showing little sign so far of abating". "Many" stressed staying the course "even as the labor market slowed". Tempered by "several" favoring calibrating the pace and noting that risks become "more two-sided", and "many" expecting to hold at a sufficiently restrictive level. This sits at the top of the strong-hawkish band, just under the 75bp-hike anchor of 80-85.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 13).** The vote was unanimous ("Voting against this action: None") and "all participants" supported the 75bp hike. Differences are of emphasis only (several on calibrating pace, a few on downside risks, a couple on MBS sales). Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 22).** Financial conditions are discussed as the transmission channel of policy ("notable tightening of financial conditions", "substantial increases in real interest rates"). Several participants mention a "highly uncertain global economic and financial environment" and global financial-market spillovers. There is no discussion of financial stability risks, market functioning or banking. Limited attention, context rather than focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 82).** The Committee "anticipated that ongoing increases in the target range would be appropriate", and participants "continued to anticipate" further increases. Offsetting: "it would become appropriate at some point to slow the pace", the pace "would continue to depend on" incoming information, and "many" saw holding the level "for some time" once sufficiently restrictive. A clear tightening signal, softened slightly by data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 66).** "Participants agreed that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high" and risks to inflation were "weighted to the upside"; "a few" stressed "high uncertainty" about the unemployment path; "several" cited a "highly uncertain global economic and financial environment"; GDP risks were weighted to the downside. This is high but not extreme, with no questioning of standard models.
