## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 22 +-8, Moderate).** Participants see activity falling well below potential, but the magnitude is not stated in the participants' views. Support: "All saw U.S. economic activity as likely to decline in the coming quarter"; the outlook "deteriorated sharply" and "profoundly uncertain"; the economy had come in "on a strong footing". The old quote about the largest post-war decline is not in the input and is dropped. Below-potential bright line satisfied; score sits in the deep-contraction band, with Moderate confidence because depth is inferred rather than quantified.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** Participants discuss "extraordinary turbulence" in financial markets generally, and Treasury, MBS, CP, corporate bond, money market fund and government bond fund conditions, but no participant addresses equity prices or valuations. The old 72 relied on an asserted equity crash that is not in the text, so the dimension is N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 4).** Most participants favored cutting 100bp to the ELB with "a forceful monetary policy response", alongside large Treasury and MBS purchases and readiness to increase them. A few preferred 50bp and the lone dissenter also wanted a cut; no one argued for tightening. Emergency dovish end of the scale.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 40).** The vote record shows one formal dissent (Mester, who "preferred to reduce the target range ... to 1/2 to 3/4 percent" while fully supporting the other actions), and "a few participants" wanted 50bp for transmission-mechanism and signaling reasons. Disagreement is over size and tactics within the same direction, plus communication worries held by some and several participants. Moderate end of the scale.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 88).** Participants "expressed concern about the disruptions to the functioning" of Treasury and agency MBS markets, "many" pointed to funding dislocations and "acute problems" in CP and corporate bond issuance, and discussed money market funds, discount window stigma, swap lines, and bank buffers and payouts. Market functioning is a dominant topic alongside the economic assessment.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 5).** Those favoring the cut judged it appropriate to hold 0 to 1/4 percent "until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track" for the dual-mandate goals, stressed the Committee "would be prepared to increase the size of the securities purchases, as needed", and noted that "new forward guidance or balance sheet measures could be introduced". Strong, outcome-based easing commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 90).** "All participants viewed the near-term U.S. economic outlook as having deteriorated sharply ... and as having become profoundly uncertain"; those favoring 100bp cited "the extremely large degree of uncertainty regarding how long and severe such a decline in activity would be"; participants "raised several alternative scenarios" differing in length and severity. Near the extreme end, matching the March 2020 anchor.
