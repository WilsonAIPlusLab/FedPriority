## Evidence for changed dimensions

**OUTPUT_GAP (CORRECTED, 47).** Participants see slack as smaller than in June but not eliminated. They note "current estimates of slack were narrower than those reviewed at the June meeting" and that "reduced resource slack" adds to inflation pressure, while "some slack remained in resource utilization" given low monthly core readings and modest wage pressures. The removal of the staff line that the gap would be "essentially closed" and the bright-line rule on remaining slack put the gap slightly below potential. Score 47.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS (CORRECTED, 48).** The only participant-level labor language is that members concurred "improving gradually" remained the right description, along with "modest wage pressures, at least by some measures" and some remaining slack. This reads as near full employment but not tight. Evidence is thin and indirect. Score 48, Low confidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (CORRECTED, 53).** Participants expect household spending to "advance at a moderate pace" (income and wealth support, energy a drag), housing "generally remained strong", and business investment "evolving roughly in line with their expectations" with strong fundamentals. Aggregate spending had "picked up ... by more than anticipated" but some slowing is expected later in the year. Net: at or slightly above trend. Score 53.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, N/A).** The prior score rested on a description of speculative-grade spreads that is not part of the participants' views and is absent from the new input. Members mention only "an inappropriate degree of risk-taking" (a few), with no level or direction for spreads. N/A.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, N/A).** The prior score rested on a description of equity index moves not in the new input. Participants do not discuss equities. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED, N/A).** The prior score rested on a description of trade-weighted dollar movement not in the new input. Participants discuss net exports and the trade balance but not the exchange rate. N/A.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 66).** All members favored a 25bp increase and judged that the funds rate would remain below the level needed, so "in all likelihood further policy action would be required"; but the pace was likely "measured" and the statement kept risks "roughly equal". Consensus for continued tightening at a gradual pace: moderate hawkish. Score 66.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 14).** Unanimous: "all of the members" favored the move, the vote record shows no votes against (Mr. Gramlich absent), and the only exchanges were over wording of the labor-market description and forward-looking language, settled by agreement. A few participants' risk-taking worries are an emphasis difference. Score 14.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 28).** "A few participants" voiced concern that low rates were fostering "an inappropriate degree of risk-taking"; at least some banks were tightening real-estate standards and cooling in local housing markets was noted. These appear as context within the financial-conditions and housing paragraphs rather than a major focus. Score 28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 70).** The signal is further tightening at a measured pace: further action "would be required" but its pace and extent "would depend on incoming data"; the statement says accommodation "can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured", with the Committee prepared to respond as needed. Members agreed to retain forward-looking language. A mild-to-clear tightening signal tempered by data dependence. Score 70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 35).** The slowing of home-price appreciation and its consumer implications were "quite uncertain", and "uncertainties about the underlying pace of productivity increases, trends in labor force participation, and the level of potential output complicated the inflation outlook". Elsewhere standard caveats, with the policy path dependent on data. Somewhat elevated. Score 35.
