## Evidence for changed dimensions

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED to N/A).** Participants do not discuss equity prices or valuations anywhere in the participants' views or the vote record. The old score of 65 drew on market history (the January-to-March crash, the later rebound) that is not in the input. Under rule 2 the dimension is N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH (CORRECTED to 58).** The direction is strengthening. Participants noted that "a stronger dollar and lower oil prices were factors likely to put downward pressure on inflation". The only other dollar reference is Fed measures "to reduce strains in global U.S. dollar funding markets", which is not a price-direction statement. A modest strengthening scores just above 50 under the bright-line rule. It is a passing mention, so uncertainty is wide and confidence Low.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 12).** The lean is strongly dovish, at maximum accommodation rather than new easing. "All participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent", with asset purchases continuing "in the amounts needed to support smooth market functioning". Some participants raised more explicit guidance, and a few raised capping Treasury yields. No participant is described as favoring tightening. Direction is clearly dovish, QE-type; score 12.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 10).** Near-consensus. The vote record lists ten votes for and "Voting against this action: None". The minutes say "all participants judged" the hold appropriate and "participants agreed that the current stance of monetary policy remained appropriate". Differences concern only the future form of guidance ("some" favoring more explicit guidance, "several" noting the framework review). Score 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 62).** A prominent but not dominant topic. A paragraph on financial markets, then "a number of participants commented on potential risks to financial stability": bank stress, nonfinancial corporate indebtedness, stress tests, payout limits, and, per "a few participants", nonbank vulnerabilities. Credit flow and lending facilities recur in the policy discussion and the vote record. It sits alongside the economic assessment, not above it; score 62.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 8).** Strong easing signal, short of a formal threshold. Participants judged the range should stay "until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track" for its goals. "Some participants" suggested outcome-based or date-based guidance that must be met before any rise. "Several participants" saw ongoing Treasury purchases as a way to "keep longer-term yields low", and "a few" a yield cap. The signal is firmly toward extended accommodation; score 8.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 82).** Very high uncertainty. The pandemic "created an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to economic activity in the medium term". Participants discussed "several alternative scenarios... that all seemed about equally likely", and "a number of participants" saw a substantial chance of further outbreak waves. The vote record adds "considerable downside risks". This is a wide scenario range but not Knightian, since the scenarios are enumerated; score 82.
