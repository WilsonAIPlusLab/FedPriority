## Evidence for changed dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (CORRECTED, 45).** Participants saw conditions as looser and more supportive than earlier: "financial market conditions generally had become more supportive of economic growth", equity prices up, spreads narrower, markets functioning "significantly better". Offsets are tight bank credit and impaired securitization markets. The bright-line rule forces a score below 50; 45 reflects mostly supportive capital markets tempered by tight bank lending.

**CREDIT_SPREADS (CORRECTED, 47 Low).** Private credit spreads "narrowed somewhat" (direction only); no level was given, while securitization markets were "still substantially impaired" and large firms had "relatively little difficulty" in debt markets. Score stays on the narrowing side of 50 within 45-55; confidence is Low per the V4.1 direction-only rule.

**EQUITY_VALUATION (CORRECTED, 48 Low).** Equity prices rose further, but participants judged "U.S. asset values did not seem out of line with improving fundamentals" and investors were "discriminating among risky assets". That is roughly fair valuation with no concern in either direction.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (CORRECTED, 47 Moderate).** Most participants anticipated "well-anchored inflation expectations" keeping inflation subdued; the statement says "longer-term inflation expectations stable". Some saw downside risk if expectations decline, others upside risk from monetary stimulus and deficits. Anchored near target, slightly below midpoint.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (FILLED, 38).** Unanimous hold at 0 to 1/4 percent and unchanged asset-purchase plans, with an accommodative bias. A few members said more stimulus (expanding and extending LSAPs) might become desirable if growth weakened; one member thought purchases could be scaled back or holdings reduced if the recovery strengthened. Dovish-leaning hold with a small hawkish outlier.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY (FILLED, 14).** Zero dissents ("Voting against this action: None"); "all members agreed" no changes were warranted. Differences were limited to inflation-risk tilt (some downside, others upside) and contingent future asset-purchase views by a few members and one member. Near-consensus.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (FILLED, 50).** A dedicated section covers market functioning, securitization, bank capital and loan contraction, CRE exposure of small banks, and the TALF ("several members noted" it still supported CMBS). Members "emphasized that they were prepared to modify these plans if necessary to support financial stability". Several participants address it meaningfully, though as part of an economic recovery narrative rather than the meeting's focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (FILLED, 15).** The Committee "decided to reiterate its anticipation" of "exceptionally low rates for an extended period", with all agreeing the path "would depend on the evolution of the economic outlook". All participants also agreed the Fed must communicate its ability to withdraw accommodation "at the appropriate time", and purchases are being slowed, but the signal is clearly low-for-long.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED (FILLED, 40).** "The near-term outlook remains uncertain"; some worried about a self-sustaining recovery without government support, and many cited policy uncertainty (health-care, tax, environmental). Offsetting: views "had not changed appreciably", downside risks "diminished a bit further", inflation risks "roughly balanced" for many. Somewhat elevated, not unusual.

Note: the block headed "Committee Policy Action (vote record)" in the new input contains only the post-decision staff presentations on inflation dynamics and the adjournment; the directive, statement and vote appear just above the header. The staff paragraph was ignored for scoring. The vote record is present and complete, with no truncation.
