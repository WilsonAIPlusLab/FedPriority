**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant mentions spreads, yields on corporate debt or risk premiums, so the score rests on availability only (V4.1.1 clarification 3). Evidence: consumer outlook supported by "reduced interest rates, still readily available credit"; housing forecasts rest on "the ample availability of housing finance" and the "considerable decline" in mortgage rates; equipment investment helped by "the reduced cost of financing". Offsetting: "increased consumer debt burdens that were showing up in rising delinquency rates on some types of loans" (a household-debt concern, not a statement about credit supply). Direction: participants see credit as readily available and cheap, so below normal on the stress scale. Score 38, UNC at least +-10 (+-12), Low confidence, resting on availability.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Evidence: forecasts of unemployment "near the current level, with a central tendency of 5-1/2 to 5-3/4 percent"; directive text gives unemployment at 5.6 percent; "resource utilization was expected to remain high and greater pressures could emerge in labor and product markets"; "a small rise in the number of anecdotal reports indicating that labor shortages were contributing to higher wages in some parts of the country"; but "a general sense of job insecurity ... was holding down increases in labor compensation" and "ongoing concerns about job security ... workforce reduction announcements". Rule applied: level governs; UE of 5.6 percent sits at the edge of the 31-45 band and just below the 46-52 "near NAIRU" band, while the qualitative language (high resource use, incipient shortages, but insecurity and muted pay) is balanced. Participants see the market as roughly balanced to slightly slack by the unemployment level. Score 46.

**OUTPUT_GAP.** Evidence: "resource use unlikely to vary appreciably"; "most members were forecasting high levels of resource use and little change in the rate of inflation"; "declining rates of capacity utilization augured reduced growth"; current softness described as temporary (shutdown, storms, inventory adjustment) with "moderate, sustainable growth" the most likely outlook. No "above potential" or "below potential" wording appears, so the bright-line rule is not triggered. High but stable resource use with a brief soft patch reads as close to potential, a shade below given the weak recent growth. Score 48.
