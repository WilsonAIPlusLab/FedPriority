# Second-pass scoring, minutes of 2008-08-05 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

## CREDIT_SPREADS
Participants' text: "increasing concerns about financial institutions had contributed to a widening of some risk spreads and a further tightening of credit to households and businesses"; "a recent rise in corporate bond yields across most risk categories"; "borrowing costs for nonfinancial firms had increased"; "terms and standards had been tightened on nearly all categories of loans"; "Most members did not see the current stance of policy as particularly accommodative, given that many households and businesses were facing elevated borrowing costs and reduced credit availability"; members "agreed that ... financial markets remained under considerable stress". Counter-view: "several other participants" thought risks had receded and credit conditions were "broadly consistent with the typical patterns observed during periods of weak growth or recession", and "others" noted bank lending still growing moderately.

Rule applied: the text gives spread and yield direction ("widening of some risk spreads", yields up across most risk categories) with no numeric or named spread level. The direction wording alone would give only 52-55. However, the participants also state elevated borrowing costs, reduced credit availability and tightening on nearly all loan categories, and the dimension is defined as "wider spreads, less availability", so the V4.1.1 availability rule lets that language move the score above the direction-only band. The "elevated borrowing costs" wording is the nearest thing to a level statement, which puts the score on the wide side of 50. The widening is described as of "some" spreads and a minority sees conditions as typical of a weak-growth period, so I stay well short of the 80s (freeze/rationing). The score is 66, at the bottom of the "wide; reduced access" band. It rests largely on availability and borrowing-cost language rather than a stated spread level, so CONF is Low and UNC is +-10.

## EQUITY_VALUATION
Participants' text: "broad equity price indexes had declined"; "Lower equity prices and the ongoing deterioration in house prices had reduced household wealth significantly".

Rule applied: the first phrase is a reported price move with no valuation or concern view (no "depressed", "stretched" or similar). The second is a wealth-effect channel for spending. Per V4.1.1 section 2 and the rubric default, a price move with no valuation view scores 45-55 on the side of the move, with CONF Low. The move is a decline, so I score on the stress side, at 55. This is the bright-line default and not a judgement that equities were undervalued.
