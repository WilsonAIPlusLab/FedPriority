# Second-pass scoring, meeting 2013-12-18 (minutes participants' views)

## CREDIT_SPREADS
No participant wording on spreads, yields or risk premia. The score therefore rests on availability and issuance language (V4.1.1 clarification 3), so CONF is Low and UNC is at least +-10. Evidence: participants expected growth to be supported by "a further easing of credit conditions"; "Business balance sheets remained in good shape, cash flow was ample"; and "One pointed to the increase in issuance of leveraged loans this year and the apparent decline in the average quality of such loans" (a single participant, a risk flag about quality, not a statement of wide spreads or scarce credit). The Committee is also said to expect "broader financial conditions more accommodative". Participants see credit as easing and readily available, which is below the midpoint (generally favorable, accommodative credit). The lone leveraged-loan comment points to loose rather than tight credit and is not offset by any stress language. Rule applied: availability/issuance language without spread wording supports a score on the favorable side. Score 38 (31-45 band: "generally favorable"; "accommodative credit"), +-10, Low confidence. Polarity check: higher means more credit stress; evidence implies easy credit, so below 50.

## EQUITY_VALUATION
Participants state a modest valuation concern. Evidence: "several participants commented on the rise in forward price-to-earnings ratios for some small-cap stocks, the increased level of equity repurchases, or the rise in margin credit", within a review of financial vulnerabilities that "generally suggested that such risks were moderate". The valuation view is limited to "some small-cap stocks", raised by "several", and is framed as a moderate risk, not a bubble or a stretched-market call. The separate mention of "gains in household wealth associated with rising house prices and equity values" is a wealth-effect channel and is not used. Rule applied: a stated valuation concern is scored on the table. Higher means more equity stress or concern. Valuations modestly above fair value in a pocket of the market puts the score in the 31-45 band ("somewhat elevated"). Score 40, +-8, Moderate confidence (several participants, but a narrow and mild set of remarks).
