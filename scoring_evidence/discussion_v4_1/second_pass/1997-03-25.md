## CREDIT_SPREADS

## DOLLAR_STRENGTH
Members' views: "Beyond the near term, the appreciated value of the dollar was expected to hold down net exports, restraining overall demand and growth. Some members observed in this regard that the deterioration in net exports might be substantial." The dollar is described as having appreciated, so the bright-line rule requires a score above 50. Participants treat it as a meaningful drag on net exports, but no magnitude is given and the language is modest ("appreciated", not "sharp" or "broad-based"). It falls in the 55-65 band, at the upper-middle of the band because of the "substantial" deterioration concern. Score 60, UNC +-7, CONF Moderate (one passage, "some members").

## OUTPUT_GAP
Direction: participants see the economy above its sustainable pace and at high resource utilization. Evidence: "prospects for a substantial period of economic expansion at a rate that exceeded the estimated growth of potential"; "an economy that already was operating at high levels of resource utilization"; "labor markets already were tight"; "tightness in labor markets appeared to be intensifying"; later, some members believe growth "was not likely to slow enough to alleviate excess demands for resources". Counterweights: "little evidence of growing demand pressures on capital resources" and subdued inflation. Bright-line: language of excess demand and growth above potential requires a score above 50. "High levels of resource utilization" with tight labor and "excess demands" (stated for some members) is more than "modestly above" but not overheating, so it sits at the upper edge of 51-58 and lower edge of 59-70. Score 60, UNC +-6, CONF Moderate (the gap is described through utilization and growth-versus-potential language, not an explicit level of the gap).
