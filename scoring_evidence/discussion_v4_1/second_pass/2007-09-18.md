# Second-pass blind score: 2007-09-18 (discussion_m), CREDIT_SPREADS

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** Direction statement: participants see credit stress as above the midpoint, mainly because of tightening availability, wider risk spreads relative to a few months earlier, and a stated wide interbank spread. They also report that high-grade credit stayed open, which holds the score well short of the "wide" band.

Evidence, with quantifier language:
- "Even after market volatility subsided and the recent strains eased, risk spreads probably would be wider and credit terms tighter than they had been a few months ago." This is participants' language, stated generally. It is direction only (relative to a few months earlier), with no stated level.
- "unusually high precautionary demand for dollar-denominated term funding in Europe had added to strains in U.S. interbank markets and contributed to a wide spread between libor and federal funds rates." This is a stated wide level, but for the interbank/funding spread, not a corporate credit spread.
- Availability language: "subprime mortgages remained essentially unavailable"; "prime jumbo mortgage borrowers faced higher rates and tighter lending standards"; "the pace of financing for lower-rated firms--including issuance of both speculative-grade bonds and leveraged loans--had slowed sharply over the summer"; "standards and terms for commercial real estate credit reportedly had tightened"; "financial market conditions ... were still fragile"; banks "might well ... tighten the availability of credit to households and firms."
- Offsetting evidence: "The access of investment-grade corporate borrowers to credit so far remained unimpeded, and rates on investment-grade bonds had declined in recent weeks"; "conforming mortgages remained readily available to creditworthy borrowers and ... rates on these mortgages had declined."

Rules applied (V4.1 bright-line and V4.1.1 clarification 3):
- A stated level governs the side of 50. The only stated level is the "wide" libor-fed funds spread, which points above 50.
- Direction-only wording ("risk spreads ... wider than a few months ago") does not by itself put the score on a side. It supports a score only within 45-55 on the side of the move.
- Availability and issuance language may support a score, with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10. That language is heavy here: unavailable subprime credit, a sharp slowdown in lower-rated issuance, and tighter CRE and jumbo terms.
- Investment-grade access and lower rates are a real offset, so this is not a severe or rationing reading (not 81+). Participants do not describe the level of corporate spreads as wide, and the funding-market spread is not a corporate credit spread.

Score: 62, +-10. The mix is a stated wide interbank spread, spreads wider than months earlier, and sharply tighter availability for lower-rated, mortgage and CRE borrowers, set against open investment-grade access. That puts it in the "somewhat wide" band (55-65), at the upper end of the direction-only range. Confidence is Moderate, not High, because the level of corporate spreads is never stated and part of the score rests on availability and a funding-market spread.

Polarity check: higher means more credit stress. The evidence (wider spreads, tighter availability, strained funding markets) implies more stress, so a score above 50 matches.
