# Second-pass scores, minutes of 2014-06-18 (CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, CREDIT_SPREADS, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, OUTPUT_GAP)

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT.** Participants see net spending as slightly below to at trend. Household spending "appeared to have risen moderately, on balance," with motor vehicle sales "rising strongly," but "several participants" read soft retail-sales and health-care information as "raising some concern about the underlying strength in consumer spending," and "a couple" noted income gains held back by modest wages. Housing: recovery "remained slow in all but a few areas," and "many participants expressed concern about the still-soft indicators of residential construction." Business investment: "capital spending was likely to increase going forward," "several heard businesses ... discuss plans to increase capital expenditures," while one said investment was largely replacement. Rule: net across consumers and business investment weighted by emphasis (the "moderate" consumer/improving capex are offset by soft housing and the consumer-strength concern). The vote record ("household spending ... rising moderately," "business fixed investment resumed its advance") confirms a moderate pace. Score 47.

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant mentions spreads, yields or risk premia. The "low implied volatility" and "increased risk-taking" remark is about risk-taking, not a spread level, so it is not used as spread evidence. Under the V4.1.1 availability rule, evidence rests on availability only: "improving credit conditions for households and businesses," "a number of participants reported increases in C&I lending," "a pickup in loan demand," "better credit quality," "small businesses reported improvements in credit availability," "favorable financial conditions"; offset by mortgage credit that is "restrictive ... particularly for households with low credit scores" and "information about mortgage lending was mixed." Net availability is favorable, somewhat easier than normal. Score 42, Low confidence, wide band because it rests on availability, not spreads.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Participants see the labor market as below full employment but improving. "Many judged that slack remained elevated, and a number of them thought it was greater than measured by the official unemployment rate" (part-time for economic reasons, depressed participation). Offsetting: "several" noted declines in long- and short-term unemployment, solid payroll gains, low claims, rising quits and openings; wages "continued to rise at only a modest rate," "several" reports of no wage pressure, "some others" noted tight markets or skill shortages in some areas. Most project unemployment declining gradually toward the longer-run normal. Rule: score the level, not the improvement ("slack remains" band 31-45). Score 37.

**OUTPUT_GAP.** Participants do not use the term directly, but describe slack: labor-market "slack remained elevated" (many), inflation "still ... running below" objective, accommodative policy needed, GDP rebounding after a surprisingly large Q1 decline. Unemployment is expected to fall to "levels close to their current assessments of its longer-run normal value" over two and a half years, which implies it is not there yet. "Some" see faster growth or lower potential growth and a quicker closing of slack, and "a couple" would let unemployment run below normal, but these are forecasts, not the current gap. Bright-line: slack remains means below 50. Score 40.
