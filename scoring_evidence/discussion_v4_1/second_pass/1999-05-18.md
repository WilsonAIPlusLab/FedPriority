# Second-pass scoring, 1999-05-18 minutes (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, LABOR_TIGHTNESS)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant wording on spreads, yields or risk premiums. The only evidence is availability and terms. The minutes say the improvement in financial conditions included "the ample availability of financing on relatively favorable terms for many borrowers", consumers had "a ready availability of financing", and members cited "the recovery in credit markets". Under clarification 3, availability language without spread wording may support a score, with CONF Low and UNC at least +-10. Participants see credit as somewhat below normal stress (easy, favorable), recovered from the fall of 1998. "Ample" and "favorable" fit the 16-45 bands. Because this rests on availability only, I place it in the upper part of that range, at 35. (The directive's "rates on private obligations show mixed changes" is in the vote record and is not a participant view on spread levels, so I gave it no weight.)

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Participants report price moves and a spending channel but give no valuation or concern view. Phrases: "the sharp rise in stock market prices", "the very large equity price increases in recent years", "the largely unpredictable behavior of the stock market", "a leveling trend in stock market prices ... would have a significant restraining effect", and "the rise in equity prices" as a reason the lower funds rate might no longer be appropriate. No word such as "stretched", "rich" or "bubble" appears. Under clarification 2, a price move with no valuation view gets 45-55 on the side of the move, CONF Low. The move is up, so I score slightly below 50 at 44. Equities are discussed as more than a pure wealth channel (uncertainty about the stock market, cost of business finance), so I scored rather than giving N/A.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Quotes: "already tight labor markets", "persistently tight labor markets" (widespread indications), "drawing down the pool of available workers", "shortages of skilled labor" in construction, and "very tight labor markets" in the directive. Wage demands are described as "relatively moderate" and labor costs as "quiescent", which tempers the score against the top of the very-tight band. There is no unemployment figure in the text. Rule applied: score the level of tightness, not the direction. "Persistently tight" plus pool-depletion plus explicit worker shortages places this in the 66-80 band (1999-2000 anchor 65-75), at the lower-middle of it because wage pressure is still moderate.
