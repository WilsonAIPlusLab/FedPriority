# Second-pass blind scoring, minutes of 1998-09-29 (CREDIT_SPREADS, DOLLAR_STRENGTH)

## CREDIT_SPREADS

Evidence (participants' views in the minutes):
- "spreads of private rates over Treasury rates had gapped higher. Increases in risk spreads were especially large for lower-grade borrowers and on bonds below investment grade, whose rates had increased considerably since mid-August."
- "many banks had tightened their credit standards and terms"; "tightening credit availability in U.S. financial markets"; "many business firms were experiencing increased difficulty and costs in their efforts to raise funds in debt or equity markets or to borrow from lending institutions."
- Members "did not believe" a "credit crunch" was likely, "though some members expressed the view that such an outcome could not be ruled out."
- "In the view of many members, equity prices and risk spreads in U.S. financial markets previously had embodied an overly optimistic assessment of business prospects and they saw some correction in these markets as a positive development"; "credit conditions were likely to remain tighter."

Rule applied: V4.1.1 clarification 3. The text gives strong direction (spreads "gapped higher", "especially large" for lower grades, "increased considerably") and strong availability and standards language (banks tightening, firms facing difficulty and cost raising funds). It states no explicit level such as "wide" or "elevated". The "many members" remark that risk spreads previously embodied overly optimistic assessments implies spreads had been too narrow, so some of the widening is read as a correction, not as a move to a stressed level. The Committee also rejects a credit crunch, which caps the score. Participants therefore see spreads and availability as somewhat to moderately stressed, from a compressed starting point. Because the side of 50 rests on direction plus availability and not on a stated level, I score on the wide side but modestly (62), with Low confidence and UNC at least +-10. The evidence rests on availability and direction, not a stated level.

Score: 62, +-10, Low.

## DOLLAR_STRENGTH

Evidence: the only participant-attributed dollar wording is in the inflation-outlook paragraph. Members cited, among favorable factors for inflation, "the lingering effects of the dollar's earlier appreciation." No participant is quoted on the dollar's current direction or on dollar-related concern. The directive block says the trade-weighted dollar "declined substantially over the intermeeting period", but that is the information-reviewed text of the directive, which reports staff and Desk-sourced data, not participants' views, so I do not use it as evidence.

Rule applied: bright-line (appreciation means a score above 50). The members' reference is to past appreciation still working through prices, so a score just above neutral. The reference is brief and indirect (a passing mention inside the inflation discussion), so confidence is Low with a wide band. If the directive's "declined substantially" were counted as participant evidence, the score would fall below 50. I did not count it.

Score: 53, +-10, Low.
