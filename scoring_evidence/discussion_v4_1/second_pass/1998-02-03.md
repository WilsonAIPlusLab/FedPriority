# Second-pass scoring, 1998-02-03 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

## CREDIT_SPREADS

No participant wording on spreads, yields or risk premiums on credit instruments. The only evidence is availability and terms language, so under V4.1.1 clarification 3 the score rests on availability, with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10.

Evidence: "both debt and equity financing continued to be readily available on attractive terms to most business borrowers" (members' view of business investment); "generally accommodative financial conditions"; "favorable financing conditions for purchases of homes, automobiles, and other consumer durables"; and in the policy discussion, financial conditions "seemed to be quite stimulative as evidenced by ... ready credit availability". The quantifier "most business borrowers" is attached to the availability claim. There is no stated spread level, so no bright-line level rule is triggered. The availability picture is clearly favorable, which puts it below normal stress ("ample credit availability" and "generally favorable" bands, roughly 16-45). Because it is availability-only and described as favorable rather than extremely easy, I place it in the upper part of that range. Polarity check: easy, plentiful credit means less credit stress, so the score is below 50. Score 33, UNC +-12, Low.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Participants do not state a valuation view ("stretched", "rich", "overvalued", "undervalued"). Mentions: (a) consumer sentiment reflecting "the very large cumulative increase in stock market wealth" and "the high level of stock market prices" as support for consumer spending. This is a wealth-effect channel and by itself would be N/A under clarification 2. (b) In the policy discussion, financial conditions "seemed to be quite stimulative as evidenced by lower nominal and perhaps real intermediate and long-term interest rates, rising equity prices, ready credit availability". This is a participant report of an equity price move (rising), used as an indicator of financial conditions and not only as a spending channel. It is a price move with no valuation view, so the default is 45-55 on the side of the move, with Low confidence. Rising prices means the low-stress side, so about 45. This is a close call: a stricter reader could call the whole thing wealth-effect only and give N/A. I score it because of (b). Score 45, UNC +-10, Low.
