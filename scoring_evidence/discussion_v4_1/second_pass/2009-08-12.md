# Second-pass scoring, minutes of 2009-08-12 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** Quoted: "Markets for corporate debt continued to improve, and private credit spreads narrowed further"; "participants noted that many markets were still strained and that financial risks remain"; "credit remained tight, with many banks—though fewer than in recent quarters—having reported that they again tightened loan standards and terms"; "households likely would continue to face unusually tight credit conditions"; "obtaining or renewing financing for commercial real estate ... was extremely difficult"; "recent issuance had neared levels observed prior to the second half of 2008". The only spread wording is a direction ("narrowed further") with no stated level, so under the clarification it alone would give 45-55 on the narrowing side. The availability language (tight credit, tightened standards, extremely difficult CRE financing, "markets still strained") points the other way, to credit still less available than normal, while improving issuance and fewer tightening banks pull back toward normal. Rule applied: direction-only spread language plus availability language, so Low confidence and UNC at least +-10, with the evidence resting mainly on availability. Net: modestly above normal stress, improving. Score 60. Polarity check: higher = more credit stress; the text says credit is still tight but improving, so somewhat above 50 is consistent.

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Quoted: "Higher equity prices appeared to result not only from generally better-than-expected corporate earnings ... but also from a reduction in the perceived risk of extremely adverse outcomes and a consequent increase in investors' appetite for riskier assets"; "recent increases in equity prices" only partly offset past wealth declines; "several participants noted the recent rebound in equity prices and thus household wealth as a factor that was likely to support consumer spending". Equities are discussed as a price move with an explanation and a wealth-effect channel, but no participant states a valuation view (no "stretched", "rich", "depressed" or "cheap"). Rule applied: price move only, no valuation view, so 45-55 on the side of the move with Low confidence. Prices rose and risk appetite recovered, so the side is slightly below 50 (less concern). Score 46.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS.** Quoted: "several were skeptical that temporarily low levels of resource utilization would reduce inflation appreciably, given the loose empirical relationship of economic slack to inflation and the fact that the public did not appear to have reduced its expectations of inflation"; "Participants noted concerns among some analysts and business contacts that the sizable expansion of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet and large continuing federal budget deficits ultimately could lead to higher inflation if policies were not adjusted in a timely manner"; counterweight: "a few saw a risk of substantial disinflation" and "Most participants anticipated ... subdued and potentially declining wage and price inflation". Participants see expectations as holding steady, not falling (several), with an upside concern raised by outside analysts and contacts and a downside-disinflation risk raised by a few. These roughly offset. No statement that expectations are rising or unanchored, and none that they are falling below target. Rule applied: anchored at roughly target with two-sided risks, 50 band. Score 50, Moderate confidence.
