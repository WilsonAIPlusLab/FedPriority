# Second-pass scoring: 2000-11-15 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

## CREDIT_SPREADS

Evidence (all participant/member language; the file has no staff text):
- "Actual and expected shortfalls in business profitability had led to tighter credit conditions for many borrowers"
- "tightening terms and standards at banks for business borrowers" (anecdotal comments supporting survey indications)
- "spreads in securities markets had widened, most sharply on obligations of borrowers rated below investment grade, and as a result those borrowers faced higher credit costs"
- "considerable weakening recently in overall growth of credit to nonfinancial businesses"; "overall financial conditions had tightened over the course of recent months"

Rule applied: V4.1 / V4.1.1. The spread wording is direction only ("had widened"), with no stated level (no "elevated", "wide" or "above normal"). Direction alone would cap the score at 52-55 with Low confidence. Clarification 3 lets lending standards and credit availability support a score, and here they are discussed at length and across many borrowers. That supports a score somewhat higher than the direction-only cap. It is still "somewhat wide / less favorable" (table band 55-65), not "wide / reduced access / flight to quality", because there is no level language and no rationing language. Part of the score therefore rests on availability, so CONF is Low and UNC is +-10.

Direction: participants see credit conditions as less favorable than normal, with spreads widening, most sharply in below-investment-grade debt. Polarity check: higher means more credit stress, and the evidence is more stress, so a score above 50 is consistent. No bright-line rule is violated.

Score: 60, +-10, Low confidence.

## EQUITY_VALUATION

Evidence:
- "lower equity prices, which would continue to restrain spending"
- "Rising interest and energy costs ... had depressed the earnings and stock market valuations of many firms, notably in the high-tech area, with adverse repercussions on their ability to borrow and willingness to invest"
- "decreases in the wealth of households"; "the ebbing of the wealth effects from strong earlier gains in stock market prices"
- "The available evidence did not indicate any material decrease in the optimism of equity market analysts as a group regarding the outlook for earnings over the long term."

Rule applied: Clarification 2. Equities are discussed beyond the wealth-effect channel. Participants say valuations were depressed, especially in high-tech, and they link this to the financing and investment effects. This is a mild valuation view in the "declined" direction, with no "sharp correction" or "distressed" wording. The comment that analysts' long-run optimism had not fallen implies no collapse in sentiment. No participant calls prices undervalued or overvalued. Table band 55-65 ("modestly depressed; declined but orderly") fits, at the lower end.

Direction: participants see equity valuations as moderately depressed, which means more equity-market concern than neutral. Polarity check: higher means more stress or depressed valuation, and the evidence is depressed valuations, so a score above 50 is consistent.

Score: 58, +-8, Moderate confidence.
