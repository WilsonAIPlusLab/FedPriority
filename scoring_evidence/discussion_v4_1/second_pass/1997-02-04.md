# Second-pass scoring, 1997-02-04 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, PRODUCTIVITY)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant wording on spreads, yields or risk premiums. The only evidence is availability language. Favorable side: members cited "the wide availability of equity and debt financing on favorable terms to business firms." Restraining side: "the effects of the high level of consumer debt and rising repayment problems on both the willingness of households to borrow and of financial intermediaries to lend." Under clarification 3, availability language without spread wording may support a score, with CONF Low and UNC at least +-10. Direction: participants see credit conditions as somewhat easier than normal for business and modestly cautious for consumer lending, so the net is below the midpoint (less stress). Business-financing availability is the more emphatic, general statement ("wide availability ... on favorable terms"). The consumer-lending remark is a restraining factor on spending, not a description of tight credit. Score 38, below 50 and consistent with "generally favorable" (31-45). This rests on availability, not spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Equities appear only as a wealth and spending channel: "the increased financial wealth of many consumers" and, among restraints on spending, "the possibility of a setback in the stock market." No valuation view (no "stretched", "rich", "overvalued" or "depressed") and no price-move report. The "equity ... financing on favorable terms" phrase concerns business financing, not valuation. Under clarification 2 (equities mentioned only as a wealth-effect or spending channel) the score is N/A.

**PRODUCTIVITY.** Evidence is brief and qualitative. "productivity gains also appeared to have had a favorable effect on unit labor costs"; "ongoing rapid technological improvements in computer and communications equipment"; "some noted a tendency to underestimate the strength of overall business investment in recent years, including the stimulus provided by efforts to improve productivity in highly competitive markets." Direction: participants see productivity as contributing favorably, mildly above a neutral trend, but give no magnitude or "above trend" language. "Some" and "appeared to" are weak quantifiers and hedges. Score 56, in the 55-65 "encouraging gains" band at its bottom edge. Confidence Low to Moderate given the thin, indirect text; I use Low.
