# Second-pass scoring, 2003-12-09 (DOLLAR_STRENGTH, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, PRODUCTIVITY)

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** Participants refer to the dollar only as having weakened: "the effects on the external sector of the weaker dollar" (supporting factors), "the lower dollar, was likely to foster a pickup in exports", and "the weaker dollar, higher commodity prices, and outsized increases in benefit costs were seen as suggesting some countervailing upward pressure on costs and prices". The language is members-wide ("members noted", "were seen as") but gives no size of the decline, and it is treated as a supportive factor, not an alarm. Bright-line rule: depreciation / weakening means the score must be below 50. Polarity: higher = more strength; the evidence says weakness, so the score is below the midpoint. Notable but not extreme weakness: 30.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** Participants describe slack: "persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack in labor and goods markets"; "the current unemployment rate likely understated the slack in labor markets, since the labor market participation rate had fallen significantly" (some members); the policy discussion cites "wide margins of unused labor and other resources" and "still considerable uncertainty about the speed with which those margins would be worked down". Employment "had revived" and was expected to rise, but the rubric scores the level, not the improvement. No unemployment rate is given in the text. Labor market is below full employment with slack that is being worked down, and some members think the slack is understated. Score in the 31-45 band, toward the lower middle: 34.

**PRODUCTIVITY.** Participants cite "Rapid productivity growth also could limit employment gains and so weigh on consumer confidence", "further gains in productivity would keep inflation low", and "further surprising strength in productivity, which would reduce labor costs". The words "rapid" and "surprising strength" point to growth above trend (55-65 band, upper end), but there is no stated size, so I stop short of the "productivity boom" band. Score: 64.
