# Second-pass blind scoring, minutes of 2001-01-31 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION, INFLATION_PRESSURE, LABOR_TIGHTNESS)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant wording on spreads, yields or risk premiums, so the level rule has nothing to act on. Availability language only (V4.1.1 clarification 3): business investment was depressed by "tighter supply conditions in segments of the credit markets", and a downside risk was that weaker earnings could spark "lower equity prices and tighter standards and terms on credit". The first is a present, segment-limited tightening; the second is a conditional future risk. Nothing in the text says credit is generally hard to get. Direction rule: participants see credit availability as somewhat less favorable than normal in places, so slightly above midpoint. Score 56, UNC at least +-10, Low confidence, resting on availability and not on spread language.

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Borderline between N/A and a Low-confidence default. Participants cite "the adverse wealth effects of the decrease in stock market valuations" among negative factors for consumers, which is a wealth-channel mention, and list as a risk "investor concerns about earnings could increase further, sparking lower equity prices". There is no valuation view (nothing like "stretched" or "depressed"). The second sentence is a price-move concern that goes beyond a pure spending channel, so I did not use N/A. Per clarification 2 (price move only): 45-55 on the side of the move (a decline, so above 50). Score 54, Low confidence. If one reads the first mention as wealth-effect only and the second as purely hypothetical, N/A would also be defensible.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE.** Participants said "current indicators continued on the whole to point to subdued price increases, with lagging demand and strong competitive pressures in many markets severely limiting the ability of business firms to raise their prices." They also said "the members anticipated that inflation would remain contained over the forecast horizon" and "pressures in labor and product markets ebbing", with energy and medical services the stated exceptions ("relatively subdued rates of inflation" except those). The outlook was "a source of diminished though persisting concern". Members' own PCE inflation forecasts centered on 1-3/4 to 2-1/4 percent, close to a 2 percent reference, falling from last year because of energy. "Subdued" and "contained" fall in the 26-40 band. The persisting concern, the elevated energy and medical prices, and PCE forecasts near 2 percent keep the score at the top of that band. Direction rule: participants see inflation pressure as modestly below the midpoint and easing. Score 40, +-7, Moderate confidence.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS.** "Labor markets were described as still tight across the nation, but reports of layoffs in specific industries were increasing and numerous business contacts indicated that openings were now much easier to fill in many job markets. There were some related indications that wage pressures might be easing." Unemployment was projected to "rise somewhat to an average of about 4-1/2 percent by the fourth quarter", which puts the current rate somewhat below 4.5 percent (UE 3.8-4.5 band, 53-65). The level is described as tight, but the direction is loosening; the rubric scores level, not change. Direction rule: participants see the labor market as above the midpoint (tight) but less tight than before. Score 60, +-6, Moderate confidence.
