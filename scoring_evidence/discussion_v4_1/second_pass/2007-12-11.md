# Second-pass scoring, 2007-12-11 minutes (CREDIT_SPREADS only)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** Direction first: participants see credit conditions as somewhat stressed and tightening, but with explicit level statements pulling the other way for ordinary corporate and consumer borrowers, so the net sits modestly above the midpoint.

Evidence pointing to stress (mostly direction or availability, not level):
- "Concerns about credit risk and the pressures on banks' balance sheet capacity appeared to be contributing to diminished liquidity in interbank markets and to a pronounced widening in term spreads for periods extending through year-end." This is a spread direction with strong wording ("pronounced"), and it gives no level.
- "Past and prospective losses appeared to be spurring lenders to tighten further the terms on new extensions of credit, not just in the troubled markets for nonconforming mortgages but, in some cases, for other forms of credit as well." This is availability language.
- "continuing illiquidity in the market for leveraged loans" and "persisting problems in the commercial paper market".
- "deterioration in the secondary market for commercial real estate loans".
- Members' policy discussion: "further tightening of credit and deterioration of financial market conditions" and "deterioration in credit market conditions".

Evidence pointing to a not-high level (attributed to "Some participants"):
- "although spreads on corporate bonds had widened over the intermeeting period, especially for speculative-grade issues, the cost of credit to most nonfinancial firms remained relatively low".
- Nonfinancial firms outside real estate and construction reported conditions "somewhat tighter" that were "not restricting planned investment spending".
- "consumer credit remained readily available for most households".
- Regional and community banks "had not tightened lending standards appreciably, except for those on real estate loans".

Rule applied (V4.1 bright-line and V4.1.1 clarification 3):
- The only stated spread levels are the "relatively low" cost of credit for most nonfinancial firms. These come from "some participants", not from a general view.
- The stress statements, which are the more numerous and more general ("participants discussed in detail the resurgence of stresses"), are all direction ("widened", "pronounced widening") or availability ("tighten further the terms"). No participant calls spreads "wide" or "elevated" as a level.
- Under the rule that level governs and direction alone is 45-55 with Low confidence, the corporate level statement does not allow a clearly high score. The widespread tightening of terms, interbank term-spread widening and illiquid leveraged-loan and commercial paper markets (availability evidence, with the usual UNC of at least +-10) push the net to the stressed side.
- I score 56, on the "some widening / less favorable" side. It is not higher because of the explicit "relatively low" cost of credit and the "readily available" consumer credit statements. It is not at or below 50 because the broad participant account is of tightening and worsening credit and funding markets.

Polarity check: higher means more credit stress (wider spreads, less availability). The evidence is of wider term and speculative-grade spreads and tighter lending terms, so a score modestly above 50 is consistent.
