# Second-pass scoring, 1999-08-24 (DOLLAR_STRENGTH, OUTPUT_GAP)

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH.** Direction: participants refer to the dollar only as a past or recent depreciation that feeds inflation risk, so the score sits below 50 (bright-line: depreciation means < 50). Evidence: members cited as harbingers of inflation "the direct and indirect effects of the dollar's depreciation," and, on trade, "the waning effects of the past appreciation of the dollar." The directive text also says the dollar "declined slightly," but that is Committee boilerplate, not a participant view, and I used it only as a consistency check. Mentions are brief, there is no quantifier on the depreciation, and no concern about dollar strength is expressed. This is modest weakness (rubric band 31-45), and the earlier appreciation is described as fading. Score 38, UNC +-8, Moderate confidence (brief mention, no quantifier).

**OUTPUT_GAP.** Direction: participants see the economy as modestly above potential, so the score is above 50. No member uses the literal words "above potential," so this is read from the tightness language. Evidence: "substantial underlying strength with few indications of slowing"; growth expected to rebound "possibly to a pace averaging around the economy's long-run potential," which "would represent a noticeable slowing from the pace that had prevailed in recent years," implying recent growth above potential. Also: "already tight labor markets" and "very tight labor markets"; "Several indicated that economic activity in some parts of the country was being held down by shortages of labor"; the "basic concern" that continued demand strength might put pressure on labor compensation and prices; "many members" worried about "accelerating costs even at current levels of labor resource utilization"; the tightening is needed "to hold the expansion of economic activity to a sustainable pace." Against this, one member (McTeer) saw low inflation and no need to tighten, and members found "no persuasive evidence" of rising price inflation, so the overheating is modest rather than severe. Rule applied: above-sustainable-pace and labor-shortage language means > 50. The late-1990s boom anchor is 57-63, and this is the lower part of that range because the evidence is indirect and inflation is still subdued. Score 58, UNC +-6, Moderate confidence.
