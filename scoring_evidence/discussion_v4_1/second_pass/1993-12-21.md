# Second-pass blind scoring: 1993-12-21 (CREDIT_SPREADS, INFLATION_GOODS, OUTPUT_GAP, PRODUCTIVITY)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** No participant mentions spreads, yields-over-Treasuries or risk premiums. The evidence is availability and financing-cost language only: "generally supportive conditions in financial and credit markets"; "Borrowers and lenders had strengthened their financial positions substantially and were less reluctant to use and extend credit"; "low financing costs"; "financial conditions became more supportive of economic activity"; and "no evidence of any surge" in credit demand. Under the V4.1.1 clarification, availability language alone may support a score with Low confidence and UNC of at least +-10. Participants see credit conditions as somewhat easier than normal (below the midpoint), so I score 38. The score rests on availability, not on spread wording.

**INFLATION_GOODS.** The evidence on goods prices is mixed and nets to roughly stable. Against pressure: "scattered examples of greater price pressures, notably the prices of lumber and some other building materials" passed through to new-home prices; "higher food prices stemming from weather conditions"; and "some members" tied the rise in gold and commodity prices to inflation concerns ("increases in a variety of commodity prices"). In favor of stability: "few indications of any change in inflationary trends in broad measures of prices"; "absence of inflationary cost pressures in local areas"; business contacts' "inability to raise prices"; "the drop in energy prices"; and rising delivery lead times "had not emerged". The pressure and restraint roughly offset, so I score 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP.** Growth is described as "an unsustainable pace" that should settle at "a trend rate close to or somewhat above the economy's long-run potential". Capacity usage was "approaching or had reached" levels that had signalled inflation, and margins of unemployed resources were "diminishing". The majority policy rationale, though, cites "the remaining slack in the economy and the lack of near-term inflation pressures". Because the text says slack remains, the bright-line rule puts the score below 50. Participants see the economy as slightly below potential and closing the gap, so I score 46. This is the 41-49 "approaching full capacity" band. UNC is +-6 because the growth-above-trend language pulls toward 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY.** The evidence is "ongoing efforts to improve productivity"; "Business executives continued to look to improvements in productivity to maintain or increase their margins, and there were numerous reports of considerable success in implementing productivity gains." This is anecdotal and gives no comparison with historical trend. "Considerable success" and "numerous reports" point to gains somewhat above trend, so I score 57. UNC is +-8 and confidence is Moderate.
