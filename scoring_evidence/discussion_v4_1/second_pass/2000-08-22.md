# Second-pass scoring, 2000-08-22 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** Participants never use spread, yield-premium or risk-premium wording for credit. The only evidence is on availability and cost of credit: "banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans", "real interest rates for private borrowers were still at relatively elevated levels", and "more cautious attitudes on the part of many business executives and especially their lending institutions". Counter-evidence: "the recent rally in domestic financial markets" and "recent declines in long-term interest rates" are noted as having partly eroded restraint, but these are Treasury and long-rate statements and say nothing about credit spreads. Rule applied (V4.1.1 clarification 3, availability branch): lending standards and terms language without spread wording may support a score, with CONF Low and UNC at least +-10, and the evidence rests on availability. Direction: participants see credit availability as somewhat tighter than normal (continued tightening of standards), so slightly above the midpoint, "somewhat wide / less favorable" territory but at the mild end because no level is stated. Polarity check: higher means more credit stress and less availability; tightening standards implies more, so above 50. Score 57, +-10, Low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Participants report price moves and offer a mild valuation remark. Quotes: "the leveling out of the broadest measures of equity prices this year", "equity prices had risen only modestly", "the leveling out in stock prices this year suggested that recent increases in productivity growth had been built into market expectations and prices some time ago", and "Members cautioned nonetheless that the possibility that long-term interest rates and equity prices did not yet adequately reflect ongoing productivity gains could not be ruled out". No participant calls valuations stretched, rich or a bubble, and none calls them depressed. The last quote is a hedged possibility of equities being not fully priced, which tilts very slightly toward the undervalued side (higher score on this scale), but it is offset by the view that productivity gains were already built into prices. Wealth-effect remarks ("diminishing wealth effects") are only a spending channel and are not counted. Rule applied: price move with at most a weak, hedged valuation view gives 45-55, CONF Low. Direction: roughly fair valued, a touch toward not-yet-fully-reflecting. Score 52, +-8, Low.
