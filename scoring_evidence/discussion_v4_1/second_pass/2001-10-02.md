# Second-pass scoring, 2001-10-02 (CREDIT_SPREADS, EQUITY_VALUATION)

**CREDIT_SPREADS.** Participants' only spread wording is in the policy discussion: "the decline in stock market prices and the widening of risk spreads had damped the stimulative financial effects of the Committee's earlier easing actions." This gives a direction (widening) with no statement of the level of spreads (no "elevated", "wide" or "above normal"). The banking-contact remark on weaker business loan demand is a demand-side point and not a lending-standards or availability statement, so it is not used. Rule applied: V4.1 bright-line and clarification 3, direction only means 45-55 on the side of the move, CONF Low. A clear widening that is cited as a reason for a further easing, with no level information, goes to 54. Score 54, UNC +-10, Low.

**EQUITY_VALUATION.** Participants discuss equities directly and more than once. They cite "a sharp drop, at least initially, in stock prices after the equity markets reopened on September 17" and name as a risk "the possibility that prices in equity markets might continue to decline and perhaps even overadjust to lower earnings expectations." They also cite "the decline in stock market prices" as damping the effect of easing. The "overadjust" wording is the only valuation-like view. It says a further fall might overshoot fundamentals, which points toward prices being depressed or at risk of becoming so. It does not say that valuations are currently depressed or distressed, and the move is not called a "sharp correction" or "distressed" in the valuation sense. Rule applied: clarification 2 (stated concern view scored on the table) combined with the rubric's "declined but orderly" band (55-65) and the Falling prices not equal to undervalued gotcha. This is more than a bare price move but short of a clear undervaluation call, so it goes at the low end of the 55-65 band. Score 58, UNC +-8, Moderate.
