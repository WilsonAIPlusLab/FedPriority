# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2003-05-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook). Era: May 2003. War over — "successful and expeditious completion." Oil prices tumbled $9/barrel. Stock prices up 13%. Risk spreads narrowed. Consumer confidence rebounding. Defense spending much higher than expected. BUT "forecast might come as something of a disappointment" — GDP revised DOWN despite good news. "Steady drumbeat of negative news on the real economy." Payrolls contracting 85K/month. Claims near 450K. IP declining. Housing soft. E&S below expectations. "GDP gap higher, on average, in 2004 than in our previous projection." Deflation probability ~35%.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "revised down the growth of real GDP throughout 2003." Year growth "nearly ½ percentage point less than in the March Greenbook." "Private payrolls have been contracting at an average monthly rate of 85,000 since the turn of the year." Claims "close to 450,000." "Few signs that businesses have, as yet, stopped shedding workers." IP declining — "decline in total industrial production of ½ percent in April." Defense spending "accounts for more than half of the GDP growth that we are projecting." Oil prices fell $9. Stock prices up 13%. Risk spreads narrowed. But: "our arms have been getting a bit tired" spinning forecast plates.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: deflation risk still high (~35% probability from prior analysis). GDP gap widening further. Payrolls declining. IP falling. Core inflation at lowest point. Productivity surge keeping costs down.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very subdued, deflation risk at peak.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP revised down. "GDP gap higher, on average, in 2004 than in our previous projection." Economy not breaking out of sluggish pattern. Manufacturing contracting. Only defense spending keeping GDP positive.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — wide and widening. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: payrolls "contracting at an average monthly rate of 85,000." Claims "close to 450,000." "Few signs that businesses have, as yet, stopped shedding workers." "Sharp drop in the workweek in April point to another contraction in hours worked." Worst labor market readings of the cycle.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very significant slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "consumption and investment taken together rose just 1½ percent at an annual rate" Q1. Housing "not been as buoyant as we had expected." Business spending "below expectations." Auto sales incentive-boosted but production cuts ahead. IP declining. Defense only strong component.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — weak private demand.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Firms squeezing more from stretched workforces. "Perhaps this is more good news on productivity" from sharp hours drop. But also means weak labor demand. Structural productivity still elevated.
- **Direction**: Above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "stock prices began to move noticeably higher" — up 13%. "Risk spreads on corporate bonds have narrowed." Oil tumbled $9. Consumer confidence rebounding. "Lifting of war-related uncertainties." Funds rate at 1.25%. Conditions significantly improved from March. But economy still weak.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improving significantly. Financial conditions easing.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "risk spreads on corporate bonds have narrowed as well, especially for lower-tier firms." Improving but still somewhat elevated.
- **Direction**: Near neutral — narrowing from stressed levels.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: N/A
### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Dollar continuing to decline. War conclusion removed some uncertainty but structural weakening ongoing. Oil price decline partly from dollar dynamics.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant weakness.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Very low inflation, deflation risk high. Expectations consistent with very subdued inflation outlook. War-end didn't change inflation expectations materially.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — low.
