# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2017-06-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. June 2017 meeting (rate hike to 1.00-1.25%).

**Era context**: June 2017. Third hike in cycle. UE 4.3%. Core PCE ~1.5%. GDP growth projected ~2.5% this year. Dollar depreciated ~5% from December. Global growth broad-based. Inflation puzzlingly low despite tight labor market. Fiscal placeholder maintained. Phillips curve debate active.

**Staff speakers**:
- MS. TEVLIN: U.S. outlook -- GDP growth ~2.5% this year, up 0.5pp from December. UE 4.3%, down 0.4pp from projection. Core inflation "marked down to just 1.5 percent." Wireless services and prescription drugs "alone can account for" the decline. Natural rate edged down 0.1pp. "We think the economic expansion is probably doing just fine."
- MS. ZARUTSKIE: SEP -- median UE 4.3%, below longer-run 4.6%. Inflation 1.6% this year. Fiscal stimulus included by 6 of 16 participants.
- MR. ERCEG: International -- foreign growth "more entrenched" since December. AFEs growing "well above potential." Euro area strong. Dollar depreciated ~5%. Net export drag diminishing. Phillips curve weak abroad too.

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE 1.5%, "down 0.2 percentage point since December." Wireless and drugs can account for it. Trimmed mean down only 0.1pp. Staff views as transitory. Score: 31 -- well below target, puzzling given tight labor market.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: GDP growth ~2.5%, "0.5 percentage point stronger" than December projection. UE 4.3%. "Payroll employment has come in below our expectations" but much is state/local. Economy "tighter" than estimated. Score: 53 -- above potential.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 4.3%, well below natural rate of ~4.8%. NFIB: "share of respondents who reported that jobs were hard to fill and the number of households who reported that jobs are easy to find are now on par with the shares at the peak of the previous expansion." Wage data "rising about as we would expect with a tightening labor market." Score: 55 -- tight.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Business investment "looking bullish." "Survey data indicate that C-suite executives, Wall Street analysts, and small businesses are all decidedly more optimistic." Capital goods orders "unusually widespread." Consumer spending recovering from Q1 dip. Score: 52 -- above trend.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Tevlin: "low rates of trend productivity growth." Implicit in compensation framework. Score: 30 -- below trend.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Potter: financial conditions easing despite FOMC tightening. "Financial conditions are easing -- not on this measure, but on all measures." Equities higher. Spreads narrow. Dollar weaker. "Exuberance and complacency" concerns. Score: 36 -- accommodative.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed. Normal. Score: N/A.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Not discussed in detail. Score: N/A.

9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Potter: equity prices rising. VIX low. "Exuberance" concerns. Earnings growth "very strong." Score: N/A (not enough specific data in this transcript).

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Erceg: "broad real dollar has depreciated since the December Tealbook and now lies about 5 percent below our December forecast." Dollar weakened due to improved foreign outlook and diminished fiscal expectations. Net export drag now only 0.2pp. Score: 54 -- moderately strong but depreciating.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 53 above potential -- consistent with UE well below NAIRU. DOLLAR_STRENGTH >50 -- still above neutral despite depreciation.
