### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from the November 3–4, 2009 FOMC meeting. Staff speakers are identified by name. The document is **segmented**: the financial briefing is delivered by **MR. SACK** (NY Fed), the economic/outlook briefing by **MR. STOCKTON** (Federal Reserve Board), and international content is embedded in MR. **Staff speakers and topics:**
- **MR. SACK**: Financial markets briefing — equity prices, Treasury yields, dollar, LSAP program review, credit spreads, agency/MBS spreads, reserve management/exit tools
- **MR. STOCKTON**: Economic outlook — GDP, labor market, productivity, inflation, international
- **MR. MADIGAN**: SEP projections summary (participants' views — not scored)
- **MR. NELSON / MR. CLOUSE / MR. SHEETS**: Operational tools briefings — not scored for macro content

### 2. Era Context

**November 2009** is a post-GFC recovery meeting. The U.S. economy emerged from the deepest recession since WWII. Real GDP turned positive in Q3 2009 after multiple quarters of contraction. The unemployment rate was rising toward 10 percent. Inflation was very low with deflation concerns lingering. Financial markets had stabilized markedly from the 2008 crisis lows but remained impaired. The Fed had deployed unprecedented balance sheet tools (QE1: Treasury, agency, MBS purchases). The dollar had been weakening since spring 2009.

Era anchors: OUTPUT_GAP should be well below 50 (deep recession, large slack), LABOR_TIGHTNESS well below 50 (UE near 10%), INFLATION_PRESSURE in the 15-25 range (deflation risk was real), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS improving from crisis extremes but still somewhat tight overall, BANKING_STRESS still elevated from crisis.

### 3. Directional Signals by Dimension

#### INFLATION_PRESSURE
Staff (MR. STOCKTON) reports core PCE running at "1.1 percent pace in 2010 and 1 percent" and total PCE at "1.5 percent in the third" quarter. He mentions that "disinflation is relatively muted owing to" the large slack and that inflation is being tracked carefully. The "higher oil prices" result in an upward revision to total PCE projections. Staff projects prices to remain subdued with significant labor market slack. Participants' projections (from Madigan) show "core inflation next year running noticeably below the 1¾ to 2 percent central tendency." Staff sees inflation below target, with deflation risk not acute but disinflation clearly present. The text does not show staff flagging deflation as a near-term danger, but does indicate subdued, below-target inflation with gradual recovery expected.

**Direction**: Staff sees INFLATION_PRESSURE as BELOW the midpoint — subdued, below 2% PCE target, with gradual expected normalization. This places the score in the 20-30 range.

#### OUTPUT_GAP
MR. STOCKTON states that real GDP "turned up last quarter and appears to be on track to expand again in the current quarter. On average, we project real activity to increase at an annual rate of about 3 percent in the second half of this year." However, he also emphasizes "a further narrowing of the equity premium, an additional decline in bond spreads, some easing of currently tight credit conditions" — all of which are described as *necessary preconditions* for the recovery he is projecting. He explicitly notes the unemployment rate is "projected to peak above 10 percent in coming months and to recede to 9½ percent by the end of next year and to 8½ percent in late 2011." He discusses significant "slack in this forecast." The broad macro backdrop: GDP recovered Q3 2009 after large contraction, but substantial output gap remains. The 3% growth projection is moderate, not enough to close the gap quickly. Staff discusses "how the recovery will play out" with a "wide range of possible outcomes."

**Direction**: Staff sees OUTPUT_GAP as significantly BELOW 50 — below potential, with "slack remains" language pervasive. Score in the 18-25 range (GFC trough anchor). GDP just turned positive but unemployment still rising toward 10%; this is consistent with a 20-25 score.

**Bright-line check**: "slack in this forecast" + UE "projected to peak above 10 percent" + explicit below-potential framing → score MUST be < 50. ✓

#### LABOR_TIGHTNESS

STOCKTON: "the decline of 210,000 in private payrolls in September was only about 100,000 jobs weaker than our expectation, and the rise in the unemployment rate to 9.8 percent was in line with our forecast. He discusses a "jobless recovery" risk. The unemployment rate is near 10% — this maps to the bottom anchor range (16-30) for LABOR_TIGHTNESS. The labor market is severely weak.

**Direction**: Staff sees LABOR_TIGHTNESS as BELOW midpoint — severe slack, UE at 9.8% and rising to above 10%. Score in the 15-22 range.

**Anchor check**: 2009 anchor = 15-22. UE near 10% → strongly supports low score. ✓

#### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

So consumer spending shows a "moderate pace" recovery, housing is beginning to stabilize from lows, and business investment is stabilizing. However, he also says "income so weak, consumer confidence still at a low level" and explicitly discounts some of the strength as temporary (defense spending surge, motor vehicle inventories). He describes spending as recovering from severe contraction but remaining below trend.

**Direction**: Staff sees CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT as slightly BELOW midpoint — recovering from crisis lows at a moderate/tentative pace, with significant headwinds. Score in the 35-42 range. Consumer spending is "moderate" but not strong; business investment is just "stabilizing." Net assessment suggests below-trend but no longer collapsing.

He raises the estimate of "structural productivity" and discusses whether these are permanent gains. He explicitly addresses the risk of a "productivity-driven jobless recovery." This is a prominent topic with specific data.

**Direction**: Staff sees PRODUCTIVITY as significantly ABOVE midpoint — "average annual rate of about 5 percent" is well above the ~2% historical trend. Score around 70-75.

#### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

He describes a scenario where the overall financial conditions remain supportive but less rapidly improving than earlier. MR. He also says the forecast is "predicated upon... some easing of *currently tight* credit conditions." The phrase "currently tight credit conditions" (emphasis mine) from Stockton suggests conditions are still somewhat restrictive, while Sack shows improvement in markets. The balance: credit conditions are tighter than normal but improving; accommodative monetary policy; spreads narrowing.

The "currently tight credit conditions" language is critical — this implies conditions are above neutral (i.e., score > 50 per the FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS polarity). However, "accommodative monetary policy" and significantly narrowed spreads push toward accommodation. The overall assessment is mixed but leaning slightly restrictive given the explicit "tight credit conditions" language and banking/lending stress. Score around 55-62.

**Bright-line check**: "currently tight credit conditions" from Stockton → score > 50. ✓

#### TREASURY_STRESS

He mentions a year-end concern about Treasury bill yields potentially going negative due to high demand for safe assets — but explicitly says "mildly negative Treasury yields are not particularly alarming." Market functioning has improved; no acute dysfunction.

**Direction**: Staff sees TREASURY_STRESS as at or slightly below neutral — market is functioning normally post-QE1. Score around 30-38. The concern about negative T-bill yields at year-end is a technical/liquidity issue, not true dysfunction.

**Bright-line check**: "no signs that our pullback from the market is causing that improvement to reverse" + normal yield movements = normal functioning, NOT dysfunction. Score well below 60. ✓

**Direction**: Staff sees CREDIT_SPREADS as below neutral — spreads have narrowed substantially from crisis peaks; below-normal credit stress conditions. Score around 35-42. Not extremely compressed (2005-06 levels) but materially below the crisis peak.

**Bright-line check**: "narrowed further" and "fallen well below" TALF spread = compressed/narrowing direction → score < 50. ✓

#### EQUITY_VALUATION

Crucially, he says "we do not see equity or corporate bond prices as stretched well beyond fundamental values." He notes the equity premium is "wide by historical standards" — meaning stocks are NOT overvalued; if anything they look cheap relative to fundamentals. He says "our assessment is that, overall, the levels of U.S. asset prices appear reasonable."

**Direction**: Staff sees EQUITY_VALUATION as roughly fair/neutral to slightly below concern — equity premium is wide (stocks look cheap), prices declined modestly but are "reasonable." Score around 48-53 (neutral with slight lean toward no stress/mild undervaluation by fundamental measures). The "wide equity premium" suggests if anything stocks look undervalued — lean toward score below 50 slightly.

Actually: wide equity premium = stocks trading cheap relative to bonds/fundamentals. This would suggest prices are below fundamental value (score toward 40-45 = "modestly depressed"?). But Sack says "reasonable" overall. Splitting the difference: 45-50.

"Banks' earnings reports continued to highlight many of the problems facing that sector." He notes financial shares "lost ground over the intermeeting period." MR. STOCKTON mentions "currently tight credit conditions" as a persistent headwind. The banking system is under stress but not in crisis-acute mode. **Direction**: Staff sees BANKING_STRESS as ABOVE midpoint — banks still under stress, regulatory uncertainty, tight lending standards, problem loans highlighted. Score around 62-70 (significant but declining from peak crisis).

#### DOLLAR_STRENGTH

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: FC slightly tight while CS are narrowing/compressed. 4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (32)**: FC slightly tight but Treasury stress is low/normal. In non-crisis times, Treasury stress can be low even when broader credit conditions are somewhat tight. The crisis is past the acute phase; Treasury markets function well while broader credit remains somewhat impaired. Coherent. ✓
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) ↔ sub-dimensions**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (42) is somewhat above what the headline pressure score implies, but this is correct — expectations are better anchored than actual inflation suggests, and the concern is that they might drift up (dollar, energy).
