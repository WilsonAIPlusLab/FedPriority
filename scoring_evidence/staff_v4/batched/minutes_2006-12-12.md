# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2006-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes, unsegmented. Staff cues: "The information reviewed at the December meeting..." (line 10), "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." (line 52).

**Era context**: December 2006. Fed funds held at 5.25%. Economy slowing — housing very weak, industrial production declining. But labor market solid, consumer spending strong. Core inflation moderating slightly. Dollar depreciating.

**Staff-attributable content**: Lines 10-49 (data, financial), 52 (staff forecast).

**Dimension analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "Additional sharp declines in energy prices reduced total consumer price inflation in October, but the twelve-month change in core prices remained above its year-earlier level" (10). "the change in the core price index for personal consumption expenditures over the twelve months ending in October was still somewhat higher than it was a year earlier, largely reflecting an acceleration in shelter costs" (40). "compensation per hour in the nonfarm business sector appeared to have risen at a subdued rate in the third quarter" (40). Staff forecast: "Core inflation was anticipated to edge down in 2007 and 2008 in response to a waning of the effects of higher energy and import prices, a step-down in rent increases, and the emergence of a small amount of slack in the economy" (52). Direction: Still elevated but moderating. Compensation subdued. Score ~58-60.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "economic activity was increasing at a subdued rate during the second half of the year" (10). "contraction in homebuilding was continuing to restrain overall activity" (10). Staff: "growth in economic activity had slowed to a pace below that of the economy's long-run potential" (52). "rate of increase in real GDP was expected to pick up gradually" (52). "emergence of a small amount of slack" (52). Direction: Below potential. Score ~47-48.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "payroll employment expanded solidly through November" (10). "Nonfarm payrolls increased at a solid pace" (13). UE fell to 4.4% in October, ticked up to 4.5% in November, "remaining below the average of 4.7 percent during the first three quarters" (13). Direction: Tight labor market at 4.4-4.5% UE despite economic slowdown. Score ~56-58.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "consumer spending and business investment were increasing at a moderate rate" (10). "Real consumer spending increased strongly in October" (22). Housing "continued to be very weak" — starts "dropped substantially in October" (25). Equipment spending "continued to increase at a solid pace in the third quarter" (28) but "Early indicators for the fourth quarter... suggested a slowdown" (28). Direction: Consumer strong, investment mixed, housing very weak. Net moderate. Score ~49-51.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: "The growth of structural labor productivity could be weaker than currently thought" (73 — participant). Staff: hourly compensation subdued Q3, "estimated increase in hourly compensation for the second quarter had been revised down substantially" (40). Implicit productivity concerns. Score ~40-42.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "yields on nominal and inflation-indexed Treasury coupon securities fell" (46). "Inflation compensation measures were little changed" (46). "those on speculative-grade corporate bonds rose a bit" (46). "Broad equity indexes showed solid gains" (46). Direction: Mixed — rates falling (easing), stocks up, spec-grade spreads widening slightly. Score ~37-39.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Normal yield movements. Score ~42.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of investment-grade corporate bond yields over those of comparable-maturity Treasury securities remained about unchanged, while those on speculative-grade corporate bonds rose a bit" (46). Score ~30-32.

9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity indexes showed solid gains" (46). No concern. Score ~43-45.

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: "mortgage debt increased at its lowest pace since the late 1990s" (49). Business debt "rose slightly more slowly" (49). "bank lending... waned" in earlier months. Direction: Some moderation in lending but not distress. Score ~38-40.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar against other major currencies fell, on net, over the intermeeting period, with pronounced declines against the euro and sterling" (46). Notable depreciation. Score ~35-38.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP: "below potential" → must be <50. Score 48. Pass. CREDIT_SPREADS: spec-grade "rose a bit" but from compressed levels → still <50. Score 31. Pass. DOLLAR: "fell" → <50. Score 36. Pass.

**Cross-dimension coherence**: OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR (57): Diverge by 9. This reflects the puzzle noted in the minutes — solid employment despite subdued growth. Explained by potential lags or measurement issues. Acceptable divergence. C_I (50) vs OUTPUT_GAP (48): Close. INFLATION (59) elevated despite output below potential — cost-push from energy/shelter.
