# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2006-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — housing, manufacturing, consumer, inflation).

**Era context**: December 2006 — on hold at 5.25%. Housing deep contraction. Manufacturing slowing. Consumer holding. Subprime not yet a major concern.

**Key evidence**: Stockton: "outlook for economic activity has not really changed much from the one presented in the October Greenbook." "Economy still appears to us to have entered a period of below-trend growth." Residential investment "taking about 1¼ percentage points off the annualized growth of real GDP in the second half." Nonresidential construction "arrived a bit sooner than expected." Equipment spending "unfolded pretty much as we had expected." Consumer "chugging along" at 3%. "Adjusting for measurement problems, we estimate that real GDP probably rose at an annual rate of 1½ percent in the second half." Manufacturing: "more widespread signs of some backup of inventories" — "managers, more of whom report that their customers' inventories are too high." But core PCE inflation "pretty high... around 2.5%." Energy prices down; higher stock prices.

1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: Core PCE around 2.5% currently. Expected to ease. Score: 60 ±5.
2. OUTPUT_GAP: GDP at ~1.5% in H2 — "noticeably below our estimate of the growth of potential." Below potential. Score: 44 ±4.
3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Steady employment gains supporting consumer. UE ~4.5%. Score: 52 ±5.
4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Housing deep contraction; nonresidential construction softening; equipment spending okay. Consumer at 3%. Score: 38 ±6.
5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A.
6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Stock market higher; "borrowing costs" mentioned as restraint. "Corporate balance sheets flush with cash, cost of capital low." Mixed. Score: 38 ±8.
