# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2003-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: March 2003. Iraq war imminent — "the room has become very small and the elephant very large." GDP growth revised down ~0.5pp for H1 to ~2.25%. But level of GDP actually higher due to Q4 2002 inventory upward revision. Payrolls contracted 321,000 in February — "widespread weakness." Spending data "usual mixed bag" — January strong, February weak. Housing strong January, collapsed February (weather + war fears). Business spending stronger than expected in some areas. Consumer confidence deteriorating "more than can be explained by macroeconomic fundamentals." Oil prices elevated. Dollar weakening further.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "revised down projected growth in the first half of this year to about 2¼ percent" — 0.5pp below January. "Private payrolls contracted by 321,000 in February." "Employment gap has been widening." Manufacturing IP declining — "contracting since the fall." Housing starts "off nearly 14 percent." Consumer sentiment deteriorating "noticeably." Oil prices higher. But "defense spending is one element of final demand that is likely to be considerably stronger." "Still expect a gradual acceleration of activity." Postwar bounce possible but staff uncertain if "more persistently vigorous" expansion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Inflation subdued. War-related energy prices elevated headline but core still declining. Output gap wide and widening. Deflation risk from prior meetings still relevant. Core PCE ~1%.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP growth only 2.25% H1 — well below potential. "Employment gap has been widening." Payrolls contracted 321K. Manufacturing contracting. Gap widest of the cycle.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very wide gap. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: payrolls "contracted by 321,000 in February." "Widespread weakness." Employment declining at ~100K/month. Unemployment rising. "Private payrolls more broadly" declining. "Few signs that businesses have, as yet, stopped shedding workers." Labor market at its weakest.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very significant slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: January spending strong but February "not as bright." Motor vehicle sales posted "another steep decline." Housing starts collapsed 14%. Retail sales weak. "War-related fears and uncertainties" weighing. But business E&S orders/shipments "exceeded our expectations."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — weak overall.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Structural productivity still elevated at ~2.5%. But firms shedding workers — more cyclical squeeze. "Stretched work forces beyond levels that are sustainable."
- **Direction**: Above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: equity prices "about 10 percent below" March Greenbook. Oil prices elevated. Consumer confidence at "unusual weakness." Dollar declining. But funds rate at 1.25% — accommodative. Fiscal stimulus increasing (defense). War uncertainty creating tight confidence/financial conditions despite low rates.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — war uncertainty and equity weakness dominating rate accommodation.
