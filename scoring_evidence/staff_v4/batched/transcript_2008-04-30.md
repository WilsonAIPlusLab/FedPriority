# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-04-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton/Sheets (domestic outlook and international — recession confirmed, commodity surge, inflation worries), Dudley (financial markets — TSLF, spreads down from March panic), Madigan (projections).

**Era context**: April 2008 — funds rate at 2%. Post-Bear Stearns stabilization. Still in recession but "relatively mild." Oil at $110+. Commodity prices surging. Inflation concerns rising despite recession.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: "We noted in the Greenbook, we believe the economy is in recession; and, with the data evolving, we have seen little reason to back away from that assessment." Payrolls down ~100K in March, "third consecutive monthly decline." UE "moved above 5 percent." Housing "continues its steep descent" subtracting 1.5pp from GDP. "Nonresidential construction now appears to be turning down." Real GDP "to decline at a 1.5 percent annual rate."
- BUT incoming data "turned out to be reasonably consistent" — "few adjustments to top-line GDP." "Bond spreads down, the stock market up, and market expectations revised higher — financial market situation is looking less menacing than at the time of the March meeting." "Risk of a very bad tail event seems to have declined."
- Inflation: Higher oil prices; "relentless march upward" in commodities. WTI up $5/barrel since last meeting. "Persistent upward creep to inflation" risks. Core PCE "should decline" with slack but staff sees "upside risks to inflation as deriving from commodity prices, further depreciation of dollar, and inflation expectations."
- UE forecast to rise to 5.75% at peak. "Relatively mild" recession. Tax rebates providing offset.
- Sheets: Oil at $110+. OPEC "unwilling or unable to increase supply." "Nonfuel commodity prices also been on a wild ride." EM central banks tightening — India, Brazil, Russia, China. Euro area inflation at 3.6%.
- Dollar "continues to expect the dollar to depreciate at annual rate of 3 percent." Against EM currencies.
- Madigan: "GDP growth forecasts for 2008 marked down about 0.5 percentage point." "More of you see the risks to growth as tilted to the downside." "Projections for total inflation in 2008 have been revised up a full percentage point."

1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: Core PCE ~2.1-2.5%. Oil/commodities surging. "Persistent upward creep" risk. Score: 63 ±5.
2. OUTPUT_GAP: In recession. UE above 5%, heading to 5.75%. GDP declining 1.5%. Significant slack. Score: 30 ±5.
3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Payrolls declining 100K/month. UE above 5%. "Hiring plans moving lower." Score: 36 ±5.
4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Housing steep descent -1.5pp. Nonresidential turning down. Consumer spending at "near standstill." Tax rebates buffer. Exports strong. Score: 22 ±6.
5. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Post-Bear Stearns: "less menacing." "Bond spreads down, stock market up." "Tail risk declined." But still tight — TSLF needed, credit conditions still stressed. Score: 70 ±6.
6. CREDIT_SPREADS: "Bond spreads down" from March peak. But still wide by historical standards. CLO recovering. Score: 65 ±6.
7. BANKING_STRESS: Post-Bear Stearns stabilization. TSLF, TAF, PDCF in place. Banks still stressed but tail risk reduced. Score: 68 ±6.
8. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar continuing to depreciate at 3% annual rate. "Post-Bretton Woods" context continues. Score: 15 ±5.
