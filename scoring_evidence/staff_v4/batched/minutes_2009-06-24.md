**Era context**: June 2009 — recession likely ending (NBER trough June 2009). Economy still very weak but declines lessening. UE at 9.4% and rising. QE1 ongoing. SCAP stress test results released in May.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "Consumer price inflation was fairly quiescent." Core prices posted "fairly small increases." Staff: "low level of resource utilization was projected to result in an appreciable deceleration in core consumer prices through 2010." Score ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending appeared to be holding reasonably steady." E&S "continued to contract" but decline smaller. Housing stabilizing. Score ~28.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Functioning in the market for Treasury securities generally improved and trading picked up, but some strains remained." Score ~53.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads "narrowed considerably." Speculative-grade bid-asked spreads returned to pre-crisis levels. But still elevated. Score ~64.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity risk premium "narrowed noticeably but remained high by historical standards." Score ~60.

**BANKING_STRESS**: SCAP: 10 institutions needed more capital. Banks raised ~$70B. CDS "declined considerably...although remained well above historical norms." Score ~66.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Dollar depreciated substantially...against all other major currencies." Score ~25.
