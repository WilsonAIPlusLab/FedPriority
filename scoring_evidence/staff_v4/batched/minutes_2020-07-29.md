# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2020-07-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes, segmented post-2005. **Era**: July 2020. GDP fell at historically rapid rate in Q2. UE at 11.1% (down from 14.7%). Economy picking up in May-June but COVID resurgence slowing gains. Inflation at 0.5% PCE. Fed at 0-0.25%, full QE.

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: PCE 0.5%, core 1.0%. Trimmed mean 2.0%. Monthly prices turning up after March-April declines. Inflation "projected to remain subdued this year." Score: 22.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: GDP "decreased at a historically rapid rate in the second quarter." Activity picked up in May-June but "remained well below its level at the start of the year." Recovery far from complete. Score: 18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 11.1%. Gains offset "only about one-third of the jobs lost." Employment gains slowing since mid-June. Score: 22.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: PCE "rebounded robustly in May" and increased further in June but "remained well below the levels that prevailed at the beginning of the year." Housing bouncing back strongly. Business investment "had generally not begun to recover." IP expanding briskly. Score: 28.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Equity unchanged, Treasury yields declined, corporate spreads narrowed. "Liquidity conditions continued to normalize." "Financing conditions for businesses with access to capital markets... remained broadly accommodative, although conditions remained tight for other borrowers." Bifurcated. Score: 55.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Short-term funding "generally stable." CP/CD spreads at pre-pandemic levels. "Little activity in the emergency liquidity facilities." Fed repo outstanding went to zero. Normalized. Score: 42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate spreads "narrowed somewhat and had retraced most of their pandemic-related surge." Corporate bond issuance strong. But "credit quality of nonfinancial corporations deteriorated further." "Defaults in May reached their highest single-month volume since 2009." Standards "at the tighter end of their range since 2005." Score: 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stocks roughly unchanged. VIX "elevated relative to its normal range." Staff judged "asset valuation pressures were notable" — corporate spreads within norms despite fundamentals. Score: 40.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Banks reported a notable tightening of lending standards." Standards at tighter end since 2005. "Financing conditions for small businesses remained tight." "Small business loan performance deteriorated significantly; short-term delinquencies were comparable with levels seen in early 2008." "Financial leverage vulnerabilities increased from low to moderate." Score: 55.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Broad dollar index declined slightly." Euro appreciated ~3%. Dollar weakening. Score: 45.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Surveys "little changed on balance." TIPS compensation continued to rebound from March lows "but remained below pre-pandemic levels." Score: 44.
