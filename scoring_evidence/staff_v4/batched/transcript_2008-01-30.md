# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-01-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Reifschneider (domestic outlook — recession risk 45-63%, GDP weakening, fiscal stimulus), Dudley (financial markets — TAF, bank funding, term spreads), Sheets (international), Madigan (projections).

**Era context**: January 2008 — funds rate at 3.5% (after 75bp emergency cut Jan 22 and prior cuts). Economy weakening sharply. Recession probability elevated. Fiscal stimulus being designed. Housing continuing steep decline.

**Key evidence**:
- Reifschneider: "On balance, the news we have been disappointing." Private payrolls fell in December. Manufacturing output declining. Housing permits and sales fallen. Nondefense capital goods disappointing. Consumer confidence down. Stock prices down. "Increased risk of recession" — nonfinancial indicators: 45%; financial indicators: 63%.
- "We are not forecasting a recession" BUT "it was a close call." GDP growth trimmed for first half 2008. UE "expected to edge up to 5.25 percent by next year." Output gap widening. R* "fallen more than 2 percentage points since the middle of last year."
- Housing: "New home sales will reach bottom in the first half of this year." Single-family starts to level at 660,000 units. "Builders still have a long way to go."
- Inflation: Core PCE "moved up since the summer" but taken "only a small signal." "Somewhat more persistent inflation pressure coming into 2008." Core PCE projected at 2.1% this year. "Survey measures of long-run inflation expectations remain stable."
- Fiscal stimulus: $75 billion tax rebates + investment incentive.
- Dudley: TAF described as "very positive." Bank term funding pressures: LIBOR-OIS spreads narrowed after year-end but "over the past week these spreads" rising again. "More anxiety that things are hidden" in European banks.
- Madigan: Central tendency for GDP growth "marked down about 0.5 percentage point." UE forecast "revised up about 0.4 percentage point, to around 5 percent." "More of you see the risks to growth as tilted to the downside."
