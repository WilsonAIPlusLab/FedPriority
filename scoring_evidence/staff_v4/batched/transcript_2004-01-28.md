# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-01-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Slifman (economic outlook), Reinhart (communications strategy), Johnson (international). Era: January 2004. This meeting was primarily a communications strategy discussion (Reinhart). Economic content comes from Slifman's presentation and Q&A. Economy expanding above trend. GDP grew 4.8% Q4 2003, projected 5% Q1 2004. Robust final sales. But labor market still a puzzle — "lackluster payroll employment numbers" vs. "pretty stellar incoming data for final sales and production." Productivity resolving this tension. Fiscal policy expansionary. Financial conditions very accommodative — low rates, risk spreads dropped dramatically, debt ratios at lowest levels in two decades. Housing strong. Dollar weaker. Inflation still low.

**Key signals**: Slifman: "real GDP grew at a 4.8 percent annual rate in the fourth quarter of 2003 and will expand at a 5 percent pace in the current quarter." "Tension between lackluster payroll employment numbers and pretty stellar incoming data for final sales and production." "We've resolved that tension by writing down sizable increases in productivity." Fiscal policy "quite expansionary." Funds rates "projected to remain relatively low." "Debt ratios have fallen sharply... now either at or near the lowest levels in the past two decades." "Risk spreads... have dropped dramatically on both investment-grade and junk bonds." Stock market assumed to rise 6.25% per year. Housing strong — starts near record. E&S spending rising rapidly with partial-expensing boost of 2.5pp. Orders and shipments "provided further confirmation of an ongoing pickup in business spending." Growth "sustained at rates faster than potential through the end of 2005."

- **Direction**: Above neutral — strong above-trend spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
