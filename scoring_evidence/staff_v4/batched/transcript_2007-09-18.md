# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — housing slashed, financial turmoil effects, recession risks), Johnson (international — industrial country slowdown, EM resilience), Dudley (financial markets implied from context), Madigan (projections).

**Era context**: September 2007 — just before first rate cut (50bp to 4.75%). Financial turmoil intensified over summer. Housing deep contraction. Staff making significant downward revisions.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: Opens with parallel to 1998 LTCM crisis — "once again, we have marked down our projection of real activity on the expectation that recent financial developments will impart considerable restraint on activity." GDP revised up for Q2 and Q3 by 0.25-0.5pp due to stronger data. BUT housing "slashed" — "new home sales to drop another 18 percent by the end of this year and single-family starts to drop another 25 percent by early next year." "This housing downturn will come close to matching in severity that of the late 1970s and early 1980s."
- Stockton: "Nonprime originations will remain virtually dead in coming months." Marked down nonresidential construction, equipment spending (moved "closer to the models"), consumption (tighter terms, weaker sentiment). "We are projecting what amounts to a very soft landing: The unemployment rate rises by a few tenths, growth converges to potential." But "it is easier to see sizable downside risks than sizable upside risks."
- Labor: UE at 4.5% — "still below the 4.75 that we are estimating the NAIRU to be." "Some complaints about availability of labor linger." "Not until next year would we have something approaching an equilibrium."
- Inflation: "We did not see the financial turmoil as having meaningfully affected our outlook." Core PCE forecast reduced 0.1pp. NAIRU revised down 0.25pp.
- Johnson: Industrial countries facing "negative effects on pace" from "impaired functionality in financial markets." Emerging markets "generally been positive." Foreign GDP growth revised down "a bit less than 0.5 percentage point for the second half and by about 0.25 percentage point for next year."
- Madigan: Participants revised down GDP growth expectations "several tenths." "Most of you see risks to growth as tilted to the downside." "Almost all of you now see inflation risks as roughly balanced rather than as tilted to the upside."

1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: Core PCE forecast trimmed 0.1pp. "Financial turmoil has not meaningfully affected inflation outlook." Core at ~2%, edging lower. Score: 53 ±4.
2. OUTPUT_GAP: GDP revised up for recent quarters but "marked down our projection." Growth to slow to "soft landing." "Below potential" in near term. UE drifting up but still below NAIRU. Score: 47 ±5.
3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE at 4.5%, "still below the 4.75 NAIRU." "Complaints about availability of labor linger." Tight but expected to ease. Score: 53 ±4.
4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Housing "slashed" — downturn matching 1970s/80s severity. "Nonprime originations virtually dead." Equipment spending marked down. Consumer adjusted for tighter terms. Score: 30 ±6.
5. PRODUCTIVITY: NAIRU revised down 0.25pp. Potential GDP revised lower. Score: 35 ±7.
6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Considerable restraint" from financial developments. Turmoil "of such magnitude as to significantly alter the outlook." Tightening credit standards. Nonconforming mortgage markets impaired. Score: 65 ±6.
7. CREDIT_SPREADS: Corporate and high-yield spreads wide. "Wider credit spreads." CDS, EMBI+, and similar measures "generally up." Score: 63 ±6.
8. BANKING_STRESS: Ongoing crisis deepening. "Downward path in mortgage lending." UK BoE concerns. Structured finance impaired. Score: 60 ±6.
9. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar "on a path slightly lower than in August." Depreciation continuing. Score: 33 ±7.

**Coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Divergence — labor lagging; UE still below NAIRU while output slowing. Explained by labor hoarding.
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (30): Gap of 17 — explained by exports surging and strong Q3 GDP offsetting weak domestic spending.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (65) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (63): Consistent.
