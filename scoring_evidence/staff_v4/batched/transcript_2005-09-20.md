# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2005-09-20

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from the September 20, 2005 FOMC meeting. Staff speakers are clearly identified:
- **Mr. Kos** (Manager, System Open Market Account): financial markets briefing — covers Fed funds forward rates, Treasury yields, breakeven inflation, equity performance, credit spreads, volatility measures, Treasury futures settlement dynamics.
- **Ms. Johnson** (Economist): international/energy outlook — covers crude oil prices, Katrina's effect on refining and energy supply, foreign growth/inflation implications.
- **Mr. Stockton** (Economist): domestic economic outlook — covers pre-Katrina macro assessment, Katrina's effect on output/employment, inflation outlook and risks.

The document is **segmented** (post-2005 format), with distinct staff briefing sections followed by participant Q&A. Scoring is based on staff presentations only.

---

### 2. Era Context

September 2005: The U.S. economy was in a late-expansion phase with ongoing FOMC tightening (funds rate approaching neutral). Hurricane Katrina had just hit (August 29, 2005), creating acute near-term uncertainty. Energy prices had been rising for approximately two years and were spiking post-Katrina. The housing market was near historic highs. Core inflation was running modestly below 2% but with visible upside risks. This is a period of broadly accommodative financial conditions (pre-crisis era, EMBI+ at all-time lows, spreads compressed).

---

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Staff sees inflation as modestly above the target/neutral midpoint going forward. Core PCE currently running at only 1¼–1½% annualized in Q3, which is below 2%. However, Stockton explicitly forecasts core PCE rising to 2¼% in 2006 before returning toward 2%. The staff identifies three nonlinear upside risks: (1) a broad breakout in inflation expectations (Michigan year-ahead jumped to 4.6%, highest since 1990), (2) workers seeking to restore real wages lost to energy costs, (3) businesses passing through energy costs to margins. Staff describes energy prices as placing "upward pressure on inflation" and notes that a dose of fiscal stimulus on an economy "already nearing its productive limits" raises the potential for "overshooting in output with accompanying upward pressure on inflation." However, staff stresses the incoming core inflation data "have been both well-behaved and to the low side of our expectations." The assessment is that current inflation is slightly below target but the forecast path runs above it, with meaningful upside risks. Net score: modestly above midpoint (~58-62).

**Direction:** Staff sees inflation pressure as slightly above the neutral/50 midpoint, with current core readings below 2% but forecast to accelerate, and clear upside risks articulated. "Higher = more inflation pressure." Evidence confirms above-midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Before Katrina, Stockton describes the economy as "growing at a brisk pace," with growth projected at "around 4 percent in the second half." He explicitly warns that fiscal stimulus "is about to be layered on top of an economy that was already nearing its productive limits" — this is a clear "at or slightly above potential" signal. Post-Katrina, the near-term disruption is projected to lop "more than ½ percentage point" off annualized GDP, and payrolls to drop 250,000 in September. But with recovery expected back to pre-hurricane baseline by mid-2006, the underlying position remains near/slightly above potential. The Q&A confirms that Stockton does not view Katrina as opening up a large, persistent output gap — he explicitly says not to take output gap estimates "in the second half seriously in terms of the notion that a lot more slack has opened up in the economy." The fundamental position is "at or slightly above potential."

**BRIGHT-LINE CHECK:** "Nearing its productive limits" and "brisk pace" pre-Katrina → above potential language. Score must be > 50. I score ~54–57.

**Direction:** Staff sees the economy as at or slightly above potential before Katrina, with temporary disruption but no persistent large gap. Score above 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Private payrolls had averaged ~175,000/month; initial claims in "low 300,000s." Stockton says the labor market has "tightened up" and that "a continuation of those [payroll] increases seemed likely." Housing activity "near historic highs." The household sector was "especially buoyant." Post-Katrina, payrolls expected to drop 250,000 in September, but with recovery anticipated. The unemployment rate at this time was approximately 4.9–5.0%, near NAIRU. Stockton does not see structural unemployment rising materially from Katrina displacement. Overall: labor market near full employment, tightening but not severely tight.

**Anchor check:** UE ~5.0% in September 2005 → rubric anchor 46-52 for "near NAIRU." Payroll gains of 175K/month and tightening language push toward upper end of that range or into 53-55.

**Direction:** Staff sees labor market as near full employment, slightly tight. Score: ~52–55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Pre-Katrina: Consumer spending "surprised us to the upside"; retail sales excluding motor vehicles "well maintained"; housing starts above expectations at >2M annual rate; motor vehicle sales "receiving a considerable boost." Business sector mixed: manufacturing IP slowing (0.2% increases per month, half prior pace); equipment spending (outside tech) "about flat since the turn of the year"; capital goods orders "soft side of expectations." Computers tracking for 16% increase but described as "modest gain." Net balance: consumer/housing strongly above trend, business investment somewhat soft. Weighted, this is above-trend but not dramatically so.

**Direction:** Staff sees consumption/investment as above trend, driven by strong consumer spending and housing, with business investment softer. Score: ~57–62.

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
Stockton does not discuss productivity explicitly. The discussion of capital stock destruction from Katrina is mentioned as trivially small ("rounded to not even a tenth on overall potential GDP"). There is no dedicated productivity assessment. **Score: N/A.**

### 4. Financial Dimensions — Directional Signals

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:**
Kos describes: equities "risen globally"; VIX "toward the lower end of its range"; credit spreads (investment-grade and high-yield) "rose slightly" but from a very compressed base; EMBI+ "narrowed to an all-time low spread"; volatilities on currencies "stayed low in high single digits"; real yields have been reduced/steepened. No "uniform anxiety" in financial markets. The era anchor for 2005-06 is explicitly 10-20 ("2005-06 pre-crisis ease"). Markets are broadly accommodative/supportive.

**BRIGHT-LINE CHECK:** "Accommodative" / "supportive" → score MUST be < 50. Score: ~25–35.

**Direction:** Staff describes financial conditions as broadly loose/accommodative. Score below 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS:**
Kos discusses Treasury yields moving (10-year heading toward 4.0% then rising to 4¼%), but this is yield movement in response to growth/inflation uncertainty — normal market functioning. He also discusses the Treasury futures settlement squeeze (large open interest in the 10-year futures contract, fails exceeding $90-$110 billion). This is not pure yield dysfunction — it is a specific settlement squeeze in one futures contract. Kos notes "all indications are that the answer is no" regarding whether this has affected the cash market or yield curve shape. The CTD security trades "slightly rich to the curve but not disproportionately so, and it doesn't seem to be affecting neighboring securities." This specific squeeze creates some market stress but Kos characterizes it as contained and normal settlement mechanics.

**Assessment:** The Treasury discussion has two elements: (1) yield movements from Hurricane uncertainty = normal functioning (score 40-50); (2) futures settlement squeeze = some localized stress but not systemic. Kos explicitly says the cash market is not affected. Score: slightly elevated, ~42-48.

**Direction:** Some localized Treasury futures settlement stress but staff characterizes cash market as unaffected. Modest elevation from pure neutrality.

**CREDIT_SPREADS:**
Kos: "credit spreads showed no clear pattern" — investment-grade "rose slightly," high-yield "rose slightly," EMBI+ "narrowed to an all-time low spread." Overall: spreads remain historically compressed. The era anchor for 2005-06 is 10-20 (extremely compressed). Slight widening from investment-grade and high-yield, but EMBI+ at all-time lows.

**BRIGHT-LINE CHECK:** "Historically tight" / compressed → score MUST be < 50. Score: ~18–25.

**Direction:** Credit spreads extremely compressed, slightly widening from very low base. Score well below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION:**
Kos: equities "risen globally" in period since July 1, "apparently unfazed by higher energy prices." VIX "toward the lower end of its range." S&P 500 and major equity indexes rose globally. No valuation concern language — no "bubble," no "stretched," no "distressed." The rise is treated as a normal fact. Default: roughly fairly valued or modestly elevated concern, but no valuation distress commentary.

**Direction:** Staff presents equity markets as rising with low volatility. No valuation concern. Score: ~45-50 (near neutral, no concern either way).

**BANKING_STRESS:**
Banking system not specifically discussed in the staff briefings. **Score: N/A.**

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:**
Dollar/FX not specifically discussed in staff briefings. Currency volatilities mentioned ("stayed low in high single digits") but no direction of dollar movement discussed. **Score: N/A.**

### 5. Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS:**
Kos notes breakeven inflation curve rose across the curve (more pronounced at short end), driven by oil/gasoline price increases. Stockton notes "prices of core intermediate materials have actually been falling over the past four months after the steep run-up experienced last year." Import prices decelerating per Ms. Johnson. Mixed: energy goods surging, non-energy goods prices moderating. Score: ~55 (net modest upward pressure from energy goods offsetting moderating non-energy goods/imports).

**INFLATION_SERVICES:**
No explicit services inflation discussion beyond the general core PCE running at 1¼–1½% annualized. **Score: N/A.**

**INFLATION_HOUSING:**
Stockton mentions housing prices (OFHEO repeat-transactions index up ~13% in year ending Q2), which boosts household wealth. This is house price appreciation (asset prices), not shelter inflation directly. However, shelter costs are implicitly elevated. The discussion is primarily about wealth effects, not CPI shelter. Moderate evidence. Score: ~60 (house prices rising strongly, expected to continue).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:**
Staff discusses this extensively. Michigan median year-ahead jumped to 4.6% (highest since 1990); 5-10 year expectations edged up to 3.1%, "just above the narrow range" held for several years. TIPS-based inflation compensation also increased, but by less. Staff counsels waiting before concluding "consequential deterioration." Stockton forecasts a "small but gradual erosion in inflation expectations" over the horizon, with core PCE rising to 2¼%. This is modestly de-anchoring/elevated.

**Direction:** Inflation expectations showing signs of moving higher. Score: ~60-65.

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53):** Both modestly above midpoint. Divergence of ~2 points — coherent. Pre-Katrina economy near/at potential with labor market near full employment.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (60):** 5-point divergence — acceptable. Consumer/housing spending above trend while the broader output gap is near potential; strong consumer demand is a driver of the above-potential output position.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (20):** Both well below 50. Coherent — loose financial conditions and compressed spreads co-move. No contradiction.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (45):** FC is loose; Treasury stress is near-neutral with a minor localized issue. In normal times, Treasury stress should be near 40-50 regardless of FC. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) ↔ Sub-dimensions:** Goods ~55, housing ~60, expectations ~63. Weighted average consistent with overall ~60. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 60 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stockton: "The economy has been subjected to a further energy price shock at a time when households and businesses already were having to cope with the effects of nearly two years of steadily climbing prices."
- Stockton: Core PCE forecast "goes up to 2¼ percent next year [2006] and then comes back" toward 2%; Michigan year-ahead at 4.6%, "highest level since 1990."
- Stockton: Three inflation risk channels identified: breakout in inflation expectations, workers restoring real wages, businesses repairing profit margins from energy costs.

**Center of gravity:** Staff views current core inflation as running below 2% but with a forecast path rising to 2¼%, meaningful upside risks from energy-driven expectations and pass-through, and preliminary signs of de-anchoring — modestly above the neutral/50 midpoint.

**Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral (score > 50) because energy-driven upside risks, above-2% core PCE forecast for 2006, and elevated survey expectations all exceed the current below-target reading.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 55 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stockton: "A massive dose of fiscal stimulus is about to be layered on top of an economy that was already nearing its productive limits."
- Stockton: Pre-Katrina economy "growing at a brisk pace" and on track for "growth in the vicinity of 4 percent in the second half."
- Stockton (Q&A): "We wouldn't necessarily tell you to take the output gap estimates in the second half seriously in terms of the notion that a lot more slack has opened up in the economy."

**Center of gravity:** Pre-Katrina, the staff assessed the economy as running at or slightly above potential. Post-Katrina disruption is viewed as temporary; the structural position remained near potential with no large persistent gap opening.

**Direction confirmation:** OUTPUT_GAP is above 50 because "nearing its productive limits" satisfies the bright-line rule for above-potential language; score of 55 is appropriate for "at/slightly above."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stockton: "Gains in private payrolls had averaged about 175,000 in recent months, and with the readings on initial claims remaining in the low 300,000s, a continuation of those increases seemed likely."
- Stockton: "While the labor market has tightened up, we don't hear much from our business contacts to suggest that the competitive environment has changed in a way that would have altered their ability or inclination to grant substantially larger pay increases."

**Center of gravity:** Labor market near full employment (UE ~5.0%), with solid payroll gains and tightening conditions, but not so tight as to generate outsized wage pressures. Near the NAIRU, modestly above neutral.

**Direction confirmation:** LABOR_TIGHTNESS is slightly above 50 because unemployment near NAIRU (~5%) with ongoing payroll gains and tightening, but wage acceleration not yet evident.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 59 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stockton: "The household sector seemed especially buoyant. Sales of motor vehicles were receiving a considerable boost from the employee discount programs. And last week's retail sales report for August suggested consumer spending excluding motor vehicles had been well maintained. Meanwhile, housing activity remained strong, with both starts and sales holding near historic highs."
- Stockton: Business sector mixed — "Growth of factory output has slowed noticeably" and equipment spending outside tech "about flat since the turn of the year." But consumer side "continued to surprise us to the upside."

**Center of gravity:** Consumer spending and housing robustly above trend; business investment modestly soft; net balance is above trend, driven primarily by the household sector. Score above 50 but not at "boom" level.

**Direction confirmation:** CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because consumer spending, housing, and vehicle sales all described as strong/above expectations, more than offsetting business investment softness.

---

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY

**Score: N/A**

Not discussed by staff in the briefing. Capital stock destruction from Katrina mentioned as trivially small relative to the overall economy; no explicit productivity assessment made.

---

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Score: 28 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kos: "In general, equities have risen globally in that period, apparently unfazed by higher energy prices." VIX "traded toward the lower end of its range and actually declined after the hurricane."
- Kos: "Credit spreads showed no clear pattern... the EMBI+ has continued to narrow to an all-time low spread despite higher energy prices."

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions in September 2005 remain broadly loose and accommodative — equities rising, VIX at the lower end, spreads compressed (EMBI+ at all-time lows), and no uniform anxiety in financial markets. Consistent with the 2005-06 pre-crisis ease anchor.

**Direction confirmation:** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well below 50 (accommodative/easy) as required by bright-line rule. Score of 28 reflects loose conditions but not the most extreme end.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kos: Discusses a Treasury futures settlement squeeze in the 10-year contract with fails exceeding $90-$110 billion and open interest slow to decay. However: "All indications are that the answer is no" regarding impact on the cash market or yield curve.
- Kos: The CTD security "is trading slightly rich to the curve but not disproportionately so, and it doesn't seem to be affecting neighboring securities."

**Center of gravity:** Some localized technical stress in the Treasury futures settlement mechanism, but staff explicitly characterizes the cash market as functioning normally. Yield movements from hurricane uncertainty are normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. Score near neutral with slight elevation for the settlement issue.

**Direction confirmation:** TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral (45) — below the 51+ threshold for "elevated concern" because staff says cash market is unaffected despite the futures settlement issue.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS

**Score: 20 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kos: "Credit spreads showed no clear pattern. The investment-grade spread at the bottom left and the high-yield spread in yellow at the bottom right both rose slightly."
- Kos: "The EMBI+ has continued to narrow to an all-time low spread despite higher energy prices, a political scandal in Brazil, and uncertain elections in 2006 in several large issuer countries."

**Center of gravity:** Credit spreads remain historically compressed. The EMBI+ at all-time lows; minor uptick in domestic spreads barely moves them from extremely tight levels. Consistent with the 2005-06 era anchor of 10-20.

**Direction confirmation:** CREDIT_SPREADS well below 50 (compressed/narrow) as required by bright-line rule. Score of 20 reflects historically tight spreads with slight widening beginning.

**Center of gravity:** Equities rose with low volatility; staff presents this as a fact without valuation concern. Absence of valuation language defaults to roughly fair-valued / no particular concern. Low confidence because staff does not explicitly address valuation.

**Direction confirmation:** EQUITY_VALUATION near 50 — no specific valuation concern in either direction expressed. Rising equities with low VIX suggests modestly favorable sentiment, scored slightly below 50 (no stress/concern).
