# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2020-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. December 2020 meeting.

**Era context**: December 2020. Rates at 0-0.25%. COVID winter surge -- highest cases/hospitalizations/deaths. Vaccines just authorized (Pfizer EUA Dec 11). Economy pausing amid virus resurgence but showing surprising resilience. Staff upgraded outlook due to less scarring than expected. UE ~6.7%. Core PCE ~1.3%. Fiscal relief package ($900B) expected.

**Staff speakers**:
- MS. TEVLIN: Domestic outlook -- "alarming rise in new COVID cases, hospitalizations, and deaths." Current surge "different" -- "just one-fourth of Americans live in counties with lower COVID death rates." Travel rose around Thanksgiving despite risks. "Consumer spending declined in November" -- goods spending "may have slipped" but "data are noisy." Food services employment "turned down in recent weeks." BLS private job gains "sharper than we had been expecting." ADP paid employment "moved down" in early December. Staff estimates "consumer spending declined in November and will fall again this month." Recovery "could pause over the next few months." But GOOD NEWS: economy "surprisingly resilient since the second-quarter plunge and shows fewer signs of scarring and recessionary dynamics than we had expected." Job-finding rates "stand at levels close to where they were in 2017, suggesting less labor market scarring than feared." Bankruptcies rose but "nowhere near the pace in 2009." New business applications "actually increased relative to their 2019 pace." Capital goods orders "rebounded smartly." Staff "pared back estimates of recessionary dynamics." Baseline GDP projection "a bit stronger." UE projected to "fall to 4.6 percent by the end of next year and to 3 percent by the end of 2023." With $900B fiscal relief, UE falls "by a full percentage point faster." Core PCE: "12-month change in core prices will stand at just 1.3 percent at the end of this year" then "rise steadily" over next several years. Weaker-than-expected rents weighing.
- MR. AHMED: International -- "two virus-related developments": resurgence in foreign countries and vaccine news. Foreign GDP "moderating sharply" near-term to "annual rate of just about 3 percent, significantly less than anticipated." But first quarter 2021 growth "about 3 percent" also, then strengthening. "Level of aggregate foreign GDP" still not returning to pre-pandemic by end of next year. Vaccine news has "buoyed" medium-term outlook. EME stress: "about a third of EMEs we follow are showing medium stress across all components" -- improvement from March when "high degree of stress." EME sovereign spreads "highly correlated with U.S. high-yield corporate spreads." Dollar: "sharp appreciation and subsequent retracement against EME currencies" following risk appetite. EME vulnerability "much different from March."
- MS. IONESCU (implied from SEP summary): "Almost all of you marked up your" growth projections. UE "median projection down notably." "Several more of you now project" inflation "modestly exceed 2 percent in 2023." Risks to GDP now "nearly balanced" (improved from downside). Inflation risks still "weighted to the downside."

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE 1.3% at end of this year. Expected to "rise steadily" but staff projects below 2% for years. Weaker-than-expected rents. Goods prices recovering but services depressed. Score: 24 -- well below target.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: GDP still well below pre-pandemic level. Recovery pausing due to virus surge. But less scarring than expected. Staff upgraded outlook -- UE to 4.6% by end of 2021, 3% by end of 2023. Still far below potential now. Score: 28 -- below potential, less bad than feared.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE ~6.7%. Job-finding rates at 2017 levels -- "less scarring than feared." But BLS job gains slowing, ADP "moved down." Food services employment turned down. Score: 28 -- slack but improving, less scarring.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "declined in November and will fall again this month." Food services contracting. But capital goods orders "rebounded smartly." New business applications up. Goods spending resilient. With fiscal support, near-term boost expected. Score: 35 -- below trend, winter pause, but resilient sectors.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed directly. Score: N/A.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Ahmed: Financial conditions recovered remarkably from spring crisis. EME stress much improved. Spreads correlated with U.S. HY -- which have recovered. Dollar retraced. Policy support massive. Score: 34 -- accommodative, policy-supported.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed as dysfunction. Score: N/A.
8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Ahmed: EME sovereign spreads "highly correlated with behavior of U.S. high-yield corporate spreads" -- both recovered significantly. Score: 35 -- below normal, recovered from crisis levels.
9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Not assessed. Score: N/A.
10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed specifically. Score: N/A.
11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Ahmed: "sharp appreciation and subsequent retracement" -- dollar weakened as risk appetite returned. Score: 40 -- mild weakness on retracement.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 28 below potential -- consistent with pandemic recession. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 34 accommodative -- consistent. CREDIT_SPREADS 35 below normal (recovered) -- consistent.

**Cross-dimension coherence**: OUTPUT_GAP 28 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 28 -- aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 28 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 35 -- close, slight divergence as capital goods strong while overall output still depressed.
