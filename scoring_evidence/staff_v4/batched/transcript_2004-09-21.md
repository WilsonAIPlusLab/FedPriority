# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international/dollar). Era: September 2004. Soft patch continuing but mixed signals. Manufacturing output surging — "6¼ percent at an annual rate" Q3. Consumer spending strong — auto sales 17 million. Housing starts up. BUT employment disappointing again — private payrolls "more likely to increase about 150,000 per month" vs. earlier 300,000 projection. Tech sector weakening — Intel, Cisco, Gartner downbeat. Inventory investment faster than expected (drag going forward). Oil prices elevated. Staff flattened funds rate path — now 2.25% by end of next year, 2.75% by end of 2006. Core PCE projected to slow from ~1.5% to 1.25% next year. Staff sees "moderate above-trend growth, moderate erosion of the output gap, moderate drop in inflation." Detailed discussion of equilibrium real rate being very low (~1%) vs. model-implied higher rate.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "biggest news is that there has been relatively little change to our Greenbook forecast." Manufacturing output "6¼ percent at an annual rate." Auto sales "nearly 17 million." Housing starts strong. But "employment in recent months have been well" below expectations. Private payrolls now ~150K vs. prior 300K projection. "Substantial shortfall in labor income." Tech sector — "Intel, Cisco, and Gartner Group have all indicated some deterioration in near-term prospects." Inflation: "core PCE inflation slowing from about 1½ percent this year to 1¼ percent next year." "Three-month change in the core PCE price index... receded to below 1 percent." Staff funds rate path: 2.25% end of next year. "Moderate above-trend growth" expected.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core PCE "slowing from about 1½ percent this year to 1¼ percent next year." "Three-month change in the core PCE price index... receded to below 1 percent." "Hourly labor compensation have surprised us to the downside." Earlier inflation spike fading. But oil prices still elevated.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — inflation moderating from H1 spike.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "no progress was made in reducing the margin of slack" if funds rate not adjusted. GDP growing modestly above trend. "Output gap" still present. Staff projects "gradual reduction in the output gap." Gap closing slowly.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — gap persists. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: private payrolls now ~150K/month vs. 300K expected. "Substantial shortfall in labor income." But "higher workweek" and "average hourly earnings have provided some" offset. Market improving but disappointing relative to expectations.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improvement stalled.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: auto sales ~17 million. Consumer spending "considerably faster than in the second quarter." Housing starts strong. E&S "projected to increase... about in line with the sizable second-quarter advance." Manufacturing output surging 6.25%. But tech weakness and inventory drag ahead. Consumer spending faces restraint from lower labor income.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still above trend but facing headwinds.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: employment disappointing while output holds up — implies continued strong productivity. But tech sector showing "noticeable slowing in the pace of high-tech production." Staff projects productivity deceleration as a feature of the forecast.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still elevated but decelerating.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: funds rate at 1.5% (two hikes done). Projected to reach 2.25% end of next year. "Fiscal policy is expected to swing from substantial stimulus to mild restraint in 2005." Net exports a "considerable drag." Still "reasonably accommodative" but less so. Dollar weakness, oil prices, and fiscal shift all adding restraint.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still accommodative but diminishing.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not extensively discussed. No indication of spread widening. Conditions still favorable.
- **Direction**: Below neutral.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not specifically discussed. Market factoring in slower growth path. No overvaluation concern raised.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 22 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson/Stockton: extensive discussion of dollar decline and external adjustment. "Widening external deficit creates a stiff headwind." Dollar depreciation "if the real exchange rate... declined 5 percent" would reconcile model forecasts. Dollar weakness a central theme.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant weakness. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "well-anchored inflation expectations" abroad. Domestically, expectations in economies with "well-anchored inflation expectations" — steady. Core PCE moderation consistent with stable expectations.
- **Direction**: Near neutral — stable.
