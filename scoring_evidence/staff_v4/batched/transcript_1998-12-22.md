# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 1998-12-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript — staff presentations. Key speakers: MR. PRELL (domestic — extensive), MS. JOHNSON (international/Brazil). Rich content. Economy continuing to surprise on the upside despite financial stress.

**Era context**: December 1998. Post-LTCM, Fed has cut 75bp total. Economy rebounding strongly — "once again to have surpassed our previous forecasts." Motor vehicle production boosted by competition. Housing strong due to weather cooperation and rate cuts. Stock market rallied strongly (eBay/Amazon boom). Brazil still a risk. Staff sees "reverse accelerator" effects slowing growth ahead but data continues to surprise.

**Dimension-by-dimension assessment**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Prell: "Recent news on wages and prices has been favorable, even in the face of tighter labor markets and higher capacity utilization rates"; "prices of oil and other raw materials have fallen out of bed recently"; lower input costs "passing through the production pipeline" and "helping to hold down inflation expectations." Very benign. Score ~36.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Prell: economy "once again to have surpassed our previous forecasts"; GDP growing above 3%; but staff expects "reverse accelerator" — spending/investment deceleration. Currently above potential. Score ~56.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Prell: "tighter labor markets"; unemployment low; but no specific new data. Still tight. Score ~62.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Prell: "motor vehicle production" strong; housing "cooperative weather" keeping construction elevated; "fundamentals at this point are still highly favorable." But expects deceleration from wealth effect waning. Investment surveys "extremely upbeat" but staff expects moderation. Score ~57.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Prell: "upside surprise in GDP growth does not appear to reflect any unexpected improvement in productivity trends. Rather, what we've seen has been consistent with the trend we have been assuming." Score ~58 (at revised-up trend).

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Prell: "Heartened at least in part by what has been viewed as a supportive Fed, investors have been willing to stick with equities even as corporate earnings have declined." Market has rallied. But "credit terms overall have deteriorated." Mixed — rate cuts helping but tighter credit. Score ~52.

7. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Prell: "PE ratios have reached very high levels"; eBay/Amazon suggesting "obsolete paradigm for share valuation"; but "market as a whole really hasn't made much further headway on net since the early spring." Staff still expects decline. Score ~22 (extreme overvaluation despite correction and rally back).

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Implied: some normalization from crisis peak but still wider than pre-crisis. Score ~55.

9. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Johnson: dollar stronger but expected to weaken; "drag from net exports" continuing. Score ~55.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 56 — economy above potential → >50. CHECK.
