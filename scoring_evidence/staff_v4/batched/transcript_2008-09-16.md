# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-09-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — weakness in consumption, labor markets deteriorating sharply, UE jumped 1pp+ since April, Okun's law discrepancy, housing glimmers of hope, inflation pass-through higher than expected but moderating), Sheets (international — foreign data extraordinarily soft, oil at $92, dollar strengthened 5%, EM financial vulnerabilities re-emerging), Dudley (financial markets — Lehman bankruptcy weekend, PDCF backstop, collateral discussions), Madigan (policy alternatives — strains intensified).

**Era context**: September 16, 2008 — the day after Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy. Funds rate at 2%. AIG on the verge. Oil collapsed from $134 to $92. Dollar strengthened 5% as foreign economies weakened. UE jumped to near 6%. Staff still projecting "gradual pickup" but with enormously increased downside risks.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: "Weakness in consumption" — revised current-quarter forecast to a decline. "Remarkable about that is that this is occurring despite...some support" from tax rebates — "without that effect, we'd be looking at something even weaker." Motor vehicle purchases "precipitous drop." "Head-faked a number of times by the retail sales data."
- Labor: "Payroll employment has been falling pretty sharply." UE rose more than 1 percentage point since April — "not going to be statistical noise." "Rise in the unemployment rate is a bit difficult to square with a GDP figure that looks as though it was running above 3 percent." Real GDI "risen about 2 percentage points less than GDP over the past year." "Entering the second half with more labor market slack than we had previously thought."
- Housing: "Few glimmers of hope" — sales of existing and new homes flat since turn of year. Mortgage rates dropped after Fannie/Freddie takeover. Builders "making significant progress in working down inventory." "Expecting to see some bottoming-out near the end of this year." But still a negative for 2009.
- Inflation: "This morning's CPI report for August actually came in a little better than we were expecting." CPI fell 0.1%. Core CPI fell back to 0.2%. BUT "seeing more upward impetus and pass-through from the higher energy prices, other commodity prices, and imports than we had previously expected." 3% core PCE projected for Q3. "For the first time since this process got under way, we are seeing more than just a futures price forecast flattening out." Oil prices, dollar appreciation, commodity declines "giving us at least a little more confidence that some of these cost pressures are going to abate."
- Sheets: "Incoming data...have been extraordinarily soft." Foreign growth "marked down by a full percentage point." "Oil prices were trading around $92 a barrel...down another $25 a barrel." Dollar "strengthened nearly 5 percent in broad nominal terms" — markets had priced in foreign resilience that didn't materialize. "Number of emerging markets" seeing "resurgence of certain sorts of financial vulnerabilities" — rising external debt spreads, falling equity prices, currency depreciation. Central banks intervening in FX markets.
- Trade: "Surprisingly strong export performance...continued through July." But "given the softening outlook for foreign growth, we're very near the peak of export growth going forward." Import price data "showed a marked deceleration in both core and finished goods import prices."
- Dudley: Lehman bankruptcy discussions. PDCF collateral haircuts increased for Lehman — "demanding a lot higher collateral." "PDCF has been successful in providing support to this market." Market expectations: "market probably will not cut rates today...but there's some probability placed on the idea" of a cut. "Either the financial turmoil will turn out to be a flash in the pan...or it won't, which will lead to a significant further easing."
- Madigan: "Strains in financial markets have intensified." Alternative A (25bp cut) — "strains in financial markets have increased significantly." Alternative B (hold) — keep powder dry, let facilities work. Alternative C (25bp hike) — focus on inflation. Statement language revised to note "strains in financial markets have intensified."
