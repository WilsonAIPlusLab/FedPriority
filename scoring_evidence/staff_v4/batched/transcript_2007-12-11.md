# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-12-11

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — GDP nearly flat Q4, housing deepening, consumer weakening, inflation), Dudley (financial markets — CDO crisis deepening, financial guarantors, bank write-downs, ABCP contraction), Sheets (international — industrial country slowdown, EM resilience, oil near $90, food inflation global).

**Era context**: December 2007 — funds rate at 4.5% (two cuts). Financial crisis deepening. CDO losses expanding to super-senior AAA tranches. GDP projected near-flat in Q4. Swiss National Bank swap line established. Housing downturn severe.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: "Basic story underlying our projection hasn't changed" but "considerable darkening of our outlook." "Real GDP will be about flat in the current quarter" despite Q3 at 5%. "Increased at an annual rate of 5 percent in the third quarter" to "about flat" in Q4. Growth 1.25% in 2008 (down 0.5pp). "Despite all the financial turmoil... with steeply higher prices for food and energy... we achieve some modest edging-off of inflation."
- Housing: "Single-family housing starts came in a bit below expectations." "Conditions in mortgage markets have deteriorated further." "Sales and starts will post a further drop of nearly 20 percent." "Delayed our projected recovery."
- Consumer: "Nearly flat between August and October" — "rather than increasing modestly as had earlier appeared." "Consistent with the slump in consumer sentiment." "Auto sales slowed to a 16.2 million unit pace."
- Business spending: "Mixed." High-tech below expectations. Other equipment "close to expectations."
- Labor: "Payroll employment figures are slightly stronger than we expected." "Firm conditions in labor markets suggesting some upside risk."
- Inflation: Total PCE at 3.5% Q4 (up 0.75pp). Core PCE revised up 0.25pp for Q3-Q4. "Core PCE prices for the third and fourth quarters by 1/4 percentage point." But "slight drop in core price inflation from its projected pace of 2 percent this year and next to 1.9 percent in 2009."
- Dudley: "Outlook for residential housing has continued to darken." "Problems in residential mortgages can no longer be thought of as a subprime or even an alt-A mortgage problem." "Delinquency and foreclosure rates on prime mortgages have moved up sharply."
- CDO crisis: "Highly rated CDO tranches — including so-called super-senior AAA-rated tranches — are now vulnerable." At 15% loss rate, "about half the super-senior tranches are expected to lose more than half their value." "Large write-downs at some major financial institutions." Financial guarantors (MBIA) under pressure — "somewhat likely to need to raise more capital." ABCP "begun to contract more rapidly again."
- "Corporate debt spreads have increased markedly." CDS spreads for banks "improvement has been reversed." "Thrift institutions remain under pressure."
- Four "rays of dim sunlight": participants "know a lot more now"; "may have finally defined the broad dimensions of the crisis"; market "resolving some aspects"; institutions raising capital (sovereign wealth funds, etc.).
- Sheets: Industrial countries: "economic activity abroad decelerated toward the end of the third quarter." Euro area bank lending and consumer confidence weakening. UK rate cut. "Headwinds from financial turmoil, this slowing reflects deliberate efforts of countries (particularly China) to rein in above-potential growth." EM resilient. Oil near $90. Food inflation globally — China at 6.9%.
- Dollar: "Strengthened on balance, rising 1 percent against major currencies." But "current account deficit exceeds 5 percent of GDP" continues to weigh. "Forecast incorporates some modest further real depreciation."
