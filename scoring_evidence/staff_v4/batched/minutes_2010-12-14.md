# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format.

**Era context**: December 2010 — recovery picking up somewhat. Consumer spending strengthened. E&S continuing. Manufacturing solid. But UE rose to 9.8% in November. Housing "quite depressed." Core inflation trending lower to record lows. European crisis — Ireland bailout announced November 28. Dollar appreciated on European concerns and better U.S. data. QE2 underway. Fiscal package (tax cuts extension) being negotiated.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "Core inflation continued to trend lower." "The 12-month change in the total PCE price index reached its lowest level of the past year; the 12-month change in the PCE price index for core goods and services also moved down." "Core PCE prices were unchanged for a second month." "The broad-based deceleration in underlying inflation was also apparent in other measures, such as the trimmed-mean PCE price index." "Labor cost pressures were still restrained." Staff: "core PCE prices would rise a bit more slowly in 2011 and 2012 than previously projected." Score ~20-23.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was increasing at a moderate rate." Consumer spending "picked up." Manufacturing "solid gain." But housing "quite depressed." UE increased to 9.8%. Staff: "unemployment rate was still projected to decline slowly." "Persistent wide margin of economic slack." Staff revised up near-term GDP but medium-term little changed on net (fiscal boost offset by worse housing/higher rates). Score ~38-41.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE rose to 9.8%. "Private nonfarm payroll employment in October and November was close to the pace over the preceding six months." "Average workweek for all employees edged higher." "Employment-population ratio remained depressed." "Initial claims for unemployment insurance trended steadily lower." Some positive signs but unemployment high and rising. Score ~17-20.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Pace of consumer spending picked up in recent months from the modest rate that prevailed earlier in the year." "Nominal retail sales...posted a strong gain in November." "Sales of new light motor vehicles stepped up." E&S "appeared to be increasing, although the pace...seemed to have moderated." Housing "quite depressed" — "Sales of new homes in October were at the lowest level in the 48-year history." Score ~43-46.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: "In the third quarter, the modest rise in hourly compensation in the nonfarm business sector was matched by a similar increase in productivity." Modest productivity growth — not remarkable either way. Score ~48-52.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities increased significantly" after QE2 announcement — due to revised expectations about program size and better economic data. Corporate bond yields "rose about in line with" Treasuries. Speculative-grade spreads narrowed. Stocks rose. "December...SCOOS...reported an easing of credit terms." But "some signs of modest stress emerged in certain short-term funding markets" — European concerns. "C&I loans edged up." Score ~52-55.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose significantly. But this was driven by fundamentals/QE repricing, not dysfunction. "Market participants pointed to abrupt changes in investor positions, the effects of the approaching year-end on market liquidity, and hedging flows associated with investors' holdings of MBS as factors that may have amplified the rise in yields." Some liquidity factors contributed to amplification — mild concern. But fundamentally normal. Score ~46-49.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Investment-grade spreads "about unchanged." Speculative-grade "moved down somewhat." Leveraged loan prices rose. "Net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations continued to be robust." Largely normalized. Score ~46-49.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased moderately." Bank stocks outperformed. "Equity risk premium—narrowed a bit, although it remained elevated." Implied vol "fell modestly." Score ~48-52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Bank profitability was little changed in the third quarter, remaining positive but well below pre-crisis levels." "Banks' net incomes were supported by declines in loan loss provisioning, while revenues declined." "Banks continued to boost regulatory capital ratios." CRE: delinquencies increased. "Conditions in the commercial real estate market remained tight." "C&I loans edged up, ending a nearly two-year string of monthly declines." Improving but residual stress. Score ~54-57.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Dollar "depreciated immediately following the FOMC's November announcement" but then "appreciated amid intensifying concerns about stresses in the euro area." "On net, the dollar ended the intermeeting period up against most currencies, with particularly large gains against the euro." Bright-line: appreciation → > 50. Score ~60-64.

### Inflation sub-components:
- **INFLATION_GOODS**: "Core PCE prices were unchanged...as goods prices declined and prices of non-energy services posted a small increase." Goods prices declining. Score ~20.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Prices of non-energy services posted a small increase." Score ~38.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Measures of house prices declined recently." Score ~28.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey measures of households' short- and long-term inflation expectations remained in the ranges that have prevailed since the spring of 2009." TIPS compensation mixed. Score ~43.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
OUTPUT_GAP (39) ↔ LABOR (18): Jobless recovery gap. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (53) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (47): Both near neutral. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 21 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Core inflation continued to trend lower." "12-month change in the PCE price index reached its lowest level of the past year." "Core PCE prices were unchanged for a second month." "Broad-based deceleration in underlying inflation." Staff lowered forecast.
- Direction: Well below neutral — inflation at very low levels and still trending down.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 39 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Economic activity was increasing at a moderate rate." Consumer spending picking up. But housing "quite depressed." "Persistent wide margin of economic slack." UE at 9.8%.
- Direction: Below neutral — moderate growth but massive slack persists.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 ±3 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: UE rose to 9.8%. Private payroll growth modest. "Employment-population ratio remained depressed." But initial claims "trended steadily lower."
- Direction: Far below neutral — extremely slack, UE increasing again.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending picked up." Strong November retail sales. E&S "increasing." But housing at "lowest level in the 48-year history" for new home sales.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral — consumer spending strengthening, investment moderate, housing collapsed.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 50 ±7 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Modest rise in hourly compensation...was matched by a similar increase in productivity." At-trend growth.
- Direction: At neutral — modest, trend-like productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Treasury yields rose. Corporate spreads stable/narrowing. Stocks up. Dealers eased credit terms. "Some signs of modest stress" from Europe. "C&I loans edged up."
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — conditions near normal with some European-related stress pockets.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 47 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Yields rose on fundamentals/QE repricing. "Abrupt changes in investor positions" and "approaching year-end on market liquidity" amplified moves. Not dysfunction per se.
- Direction: Near neutral — some amplification from positioning but fundamentally normal.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 47 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Investment-grade spreads "about unchanged." Speculative-grade narrowed. "Net debt financing...continued to be robust."
- Direction: Near neutral — normalized.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Stocks up moderately. "Equity risk premium—narrowed a bit...remained elevated." Bank stocks outperformed.
- Direction: At neutral — recovering, risk premiums still slightly elevated.

**BANKING_STRESS: 55 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Profitability...positive but well below pre-crisis levels." CRE delinquencies rising. But C&I loans turned up. Capital ratios improving. "Conditions in the commercial real estate market remained tight."
- Direction: Modestly above neutral — residual CRE stress, profitability weak, but improving trajectory.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Dollar ended the intermeeting period up against most currencies, with particularly large gains against the euro."
- Direction: Above neutral — notable appreciation driven by European concerns.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 20 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Goods prices declined." Core goods contributing negatively to inflation.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 38 ±7 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Prices of non-energy services posted a small increase."

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Measures of house prices declined recently."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 ±4 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Survey measures...remained in the ranges that have prevailed since the spring of 2009." TIPS 5-year compensation "moved up." Stable despite very low actual inflation.
