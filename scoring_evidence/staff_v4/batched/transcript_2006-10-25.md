# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2006-10-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — housing scorecard, GDP, inflation).

**Era context**: October 2006 — on hold at 5.25%. Housing deep contraction. GDP growth at ~1%. Oil prices declined. Core inflation still elevated.

**Key evidence**: Stockton: Q3 GDP "revised down about ¾ percentage point, to an annual pace of just 1 percent." But "downside surprises concentrated in motor vehicle production, defense spending, and net exports" — "good reasons exist for anticipating that these sources of restraint will abate." GDP projected to "rebound to a pace of 2¼ percent in the fourth quarter." For H2, output "anticipated to grow at an annual rate of 1½ percent." For 2007-2008: 2.2% and 2.5%. "Growth remains slightly below potential in 2007, and the output gap edges up to roughly ½ percent of GDP." Core PCE "currently running at a bit less than 2½ percent, edges down to about 2 percent in 2008." "Lower oil prices and a stronger stock market more than offset the effects of a slightly higher dollar and a bit weaker trajectory for house prices."

Housing: "single-family starts averaged about 1.4 million" in Q3; forecast another 12% decline. Inventories "remain at a very high level." Cancellations increasing. But sales "firmed a bit in late summer."

3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Not explicitly discussed. UE stable near 4.7%. Score: 50 ±6.
4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Housing deep contraction ("1¼ percentage points from GDP"). Consumer "chugging along." Equipment spending modest. Score: 37 ±6.
