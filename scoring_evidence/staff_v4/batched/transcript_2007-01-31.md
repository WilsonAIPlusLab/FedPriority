# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-01-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton/Wascher (domestic outlook — GDP, housing, labor, supply side), Dudley (financial markets — rate expectations, risk appetite, subprime), Gagnon/Johnson (international — oil, dollar, EM flows).

**Era context**: January 2007 — funds rate at 5.25%, on hold. Housing contracting but data "stronger than expected." Subprime emerging as concern. GDP growth revised up. Core inflation easing.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: "data received since the December meeting have been stronger than expected." PDFP growth revised up in Q4 and Q1 to ~2%. Consumer spending "increased briskly in November and December"; Q4 real PCE ~4.5%. Housing sales "appear to have stabilized"; new home inventory/sales ratio moved down.
- Wascher: Potential output growth edging down to 2.5% in 2008. Labor force participation rate: "cyclical response to steady improvement in the labor market." UE at 4.5% (equal to NAIRU estimate). Structural productivity revised down — "shortfall of productivity relative to our structural estimate was so much greater than we could explain by normal cyclical behavior." Kalman filter suggesting more substantial downward revision.
- Wascher: Core PCE inflation: "recent data on core consumer prices have been lower than expected." Core PCE revised down in Q4 to 2.1%. Forecast: core PCE easing to ~2% by 2008. "Waning indirect effects from energy and other commodity prices, declining import prices, and deceleration in shelter costs."
- Dudley: "Sharp shift in market expectations" — easing expectations largely reversed; now no easing priced through mid-2007. "Risk appetite remains very strong. Corporate credit spreads remain very tight — especially in the high-yield sector." Subprime: "particularly vulnerable sector" — originations climbed to 24% of total in 2006; "credit standards appear to have loosened"; 2006 vintage showing "more rapid rise in delinquencies than earlier vintages." "Risk of a vicious cycle" but "to date the news is still fairly favorable." Spreads "still well below the peaks reached in late 2002 and early 2003."
- Gagnon: Dollar declined on balance; "risk of significant depreciation is slightly greater than the risk of significant appreciation." EM capital outflows record — "net official outflows now estimated to equal 2.5% of combined industrial-country GDP." Low real rates globally.

1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: Core PCE revised down to 2.1% in Q4; "lower than expected." Forecast easing toward 2%. But still above target. Score: 55 ±4.
2. OUTPUT_GAP: Data stronger than expected. GDP growth at ~2% (PDFP), near potential (2.5%). UE at 4.5% = NAIRU. "Output gap closes over time." Near potential. Score: 50 ±4.
3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: UE at 4.5% = NAIRU. Beveridge curve "far upper left segment indicative of a tight labor market." Part-time for economic reasons "below its average." Capacity utilization "a little above long-run average." Score: 53 ±4.
4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Consumer spending at 4.5% in Q4 — "briskly." Housing stabilizing but still contracting. Equipment spending "slowed appreciably" — "unexpectedly soft." Nonres structures decelerating. Net: mixed but consumer very strong. Score: 52 ±5.
5. PRODUCTIVITY: Structural productivity revised down. "Shortfall much greater than cyclical." Kalman filter suggesting bigger downward revision. Score: 40 ±6.
6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Risk appetite remains very strong." Credit spreads "very tight especially in high-yield." Implied volatilities "unusually low." Subprime stress emerging but "economics of making and securitizing still work." Very accommodative. Score: 18 ±5.
7. CREDIT_SPREADS: "Corporate credit spreads remain very tight — especially in the high-yield sector." Subprime spreads rising but "still well below peaks." Very compressed. Score: 15 ±5.
8. BANKING_STRESS: Subprime delinquencies rising for 2006 vintage but system healthy. "Risk of a vicious cycle" noted as risk, not reality. Score: 40 ±7.
9. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar declined. "Risk of significant depreciation slightly greater than appreciation." Score: 35 ±6.

**Coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Consistent — near potential, labor tight.
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52): Consistent.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (18) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (15): Consistent — both very accommodative.
