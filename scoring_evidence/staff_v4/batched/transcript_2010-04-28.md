## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the April 27-28, 2010 FOMC meeting. It is segmented with individually identified staff speakers:
- **MR. SACK** (Brian Sack, Manager of the System Open Market Account): financial markets briefing — equity, credit spreads, Treasury markets, dollar, banking, Greece spillovers
- **MR. STOCKTON** (David Stockton, Economist): domestic economic outlook — GDP, consumption, investment, inflation, labor market
- **MR. SHEETS** (Nathan Sheets, Economist): international outlook — foreign growth, Greece/peripheral Europe, dollar, commodities
- **MR. MADIGAN** (Brian Madigan): balance sheet normalization options (policy operations briefing, not scored for economic/financial conditions)

### Era Context
April 2010 is in the early U.S. recovery from the Great Financial Crisis. The unemployment rate is approximately 9.5%, far above the natural rate. The economy is in a moderate but fragile recovery. The Fed is at the zero lower bound with "extended period" language. Greece is erupting in a sovereign debt crisis. The era expectation is: substantial slack, well-below-target inflation with disinflation risks, very tight labor market is wrong (high unemployment), accommodative financial conditions but with lingering post-crisis credit impairment. The balance sheet normalization discussion is prominent but is a policy/operations topic, not economic assessment.

### Directional Analysis by Dimension

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as below-target and decelerating. Stockton: "core PCE inflation has dropped from 1¾ percent in March 2009 to 1¼ percent in March of this year"; "we project total PCE inflation of 1¼ percent in 2010 and [approximately 1 percent in 2011]"; "we are projecting core PCE prices to decelerate from 1½ percent in 2009 to just below 1 percent over the four quarters of this year." Inflation expectations are stable, not unanchored downward. Disinflation risk is acknowledged as a risk but not the baseline. Score is well below 50 — this maps to roughly 28-33 (below target, disinflating, forecasting ~1% core PCE, not at deflation-scare levels).

**Direction**: Staff sees inflation as **below** the midpoint — disinflating, running well below 2% target.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees substantial slack but with moderate recovery underway. Stockton: "the economy is in a moderate recovery that is gradually picking up some steam"; "we acknowledge that the incoming data highlight some upside risks to the projection. But we still see counterbalancing downside risks, and for now, we remain comfortable standing pat with a forecast of moderate recovery." The unemployment rate is projected at 9-9.5% in Q4 2010, far above any estimate of natural rate (~5-5.25%). GDP growth of ~3.5-4.5% is above trend but not closing the gap rapidly. Nonresidential construction still contracting. State/local still weak.

**Direction**: Staff sees output gap as **substantially below** the midpoint — large slack remains, moderate recovery underway, maps to ~35-40 range.

Bright-line check: "slack remains," "below trend" → score MUST be < 50. CONFIRMED.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
The unemployment rate is approximately 9-9.5%, far above NAIRU (~5%). Stockton notes "Even the labor market has provided some tentative signs of improving—a development that we've considered key to our projection." Still deeply loose. With UE ~9.5%, this maps firmly to the 16-30 range ("High unemployment; significant slack; far from full employment").

**Direction**: Staff sees labor market as **far below** midpoint — high unemployment, substantial slack.

Anchor check: 2009-10 anchor is 15-22. With some signs of improvement but UE still ~9.5%, score ~22-27 is appropriate.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Stockton: "Consumer spending has been an important part of this story. Real PCE had been expanding fairly steadily since last summer, but the increases in spending have picked up further in recent months… we are now estimating that real PCE rose at an annual rate of 3½ percent last quarter." Equipment and software investment: "rose at an annual rate of nearly 20 percent in the fourth quarter, driven largely by spending on high-tech and transportation equipment." Factory output +6.5% in Q1. However, nonresidential building "plummeted at an annual rate of 25 percent in the first quarter." State/local government declining. Staff is cautious about whether the pace of consumption is sustainable.

Net: Consumer spending solid/above expectations, business investment in E&S very strong, but nonresidential construction very weak, state/local governments contracting. Staff projects 3.5% GDP for 2010 and 4.5% for 2011 — moderate above-trend but not a boom. Given the mix and staff's caution about sustainability, score is around 45-50 — slightly below to at trend (staff is calling it moderate recovery, not boom).

**Direction**: Staff sees C+I as **near the midpoint** — stronger-than-expected data on spending and E&S investment offset by weakness in structures and state/local; staff characterizes overall pace as moderate. Score ~44-48.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not directly discussed in the economic briefing. Stockton mentions unit labor costs "certainly been declining" with productivity gains running well ahead of compensation. This is a brief mention rather than a substantive assessment. Score at moderate confidence.

**Direction**: Staff implicitly sees productivity as reasonably good (productivity running ahead of compensation = positive productivity growth), but no explicit framing. Brief mention only. Score ~52 (slight positive signal with low confidence). Actually, given the brief nature — N/A is defensible, but the mention of "productivity gains running well ahead of any measure of labor compensation" suggests above-average productivity. Score ~55 with Low confidence.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Sack: Equity prices up >5% over intermeeting period; corporate bond spreads "narrowed further"; securitized credit markets "showed significant improvement"; CMBS spreads "narrowed sharply"; new-issue CMBS markets reopening; financial institution earnings generally improved. Policy expectations stable with "extended period" language holding. However, Greek/peripheral European concerns widening spreads there. Money market rates firming slightly (effective fed funds up to ~20 bps). Treasury yields rose 10-20 bps. Overall: improving accommodative conditions with some pockets of concern (Greece, regulatory uncertainty for banks).

Bright-line check: The overall assessment is accommodative/supportive → score MUST be < 50. Score ~32-38 (broadly accommodative, recovering, improving from post-crisis strains, but not fully loose pre-crisis conditions).

**Direction**: Financial conditions are **below** midpoint (more accommodative than neutral). Score ~35.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Sack: Treasury yields rose 10-20 bps; 10-year touched 4%. Swap spreads "reached unusually tight levels by historical standards" (a supply-pressure sign, not dysfunction). The Treasury is considering reducing coupon offering sizes. No mention of auction failures, settlement problems, liquidity dysfunction, or bid-ask spread widening. The yield moves are attributed to positive economic outlook and supply concerns — normal market functioning.

Bright-line check: Yield movements alone are NOT dysfunction (rubric rule). No actual market dysfunction described → score should be 36-50 range. Swap spreads being unusually tight is somewhat notable but reflects supply dynamics, not dysfunction. Score ~38-42 (normal functioning with mild supply pressure signals, no stress).

**Direction**: Treasury market is **near normal functioning** — no dysfunction.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Sack: "Corporate bond spreads, shown in the middle left panel, narrowed further over the intermeeting period." "Spreads on outstanding asset-backed securities generally narrowed, and issuance of ABS has been decent across several sectors." "Spreads on CMBS narrowed sharply over the intermeeting period, as investors sought higher yields and felt that the stress in the commercial real estate sector may be less severe than previously anticipated." Expectations for corporate defaults "continued to decline, as corporations have healthy balance sheets and ample access to markets."

This is a clearly below-normal spread environment (spreads narrowing, improving credit access, ample credit availability) → score MUST be < 50. Score ~28-35.

**Direction**: Credit spreads are **below** the midpoint — narrowing, improving, well below crisis levels.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Sack: "The S&P 500 index advancing by more than 5 percent" over intermeeting period; "despite the ongoing appreciation of equity prices, the staff's measure of the equity premium remains sizable." The equity premium being "sizable" means equities are not overvalued — they are seen as reasonably valued or even undervalued relative to bonds. This is essentially staff saying equities are fair to slightly undervalued (not a concern about overvaluation, not distressed). Goldman Sachs/SEC action created some near-term noise but didn't change the overall picture. Score ~45-50 (roughly fair valued; no particular concern in either direction).

**Direction**: Staff sees equity valuations as **near fair value** — equity premium still sizable, prices rising but not viewed as stretched.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Sack: "Nearly 75 percent of financial institutions reported first-quarter earnings that beat expectations, reflecting strong capital market and trading results and lower than expected loan loss provisions." "Equity prices for both the largest institutions and regional banks gained sharply through mid-April." However, concerns about SEC action against Goldman Sachs, pending financial regulation legislation creating uncertainty. Banking sector is generally improved/recovering but still subject to considerable uncertainty.

Given "mostly positive" earnings, improving but with notable uncertainty from regulatory changes and Goldman litigation → moderate stress level. Score ~45-50 (neutral: mixed signals — broadly improving but uncertainties remain). The improvement from crisis levels is notable but not yet "well-capitalized / sound / healthy" language that would push below 40.

Actually, the language is mostly positive for bank health with specific pockets of concern. The 75% beat rate suggests the system is healing. Score ~42-48.

**Direction**: Banking stress is **near the midpoint** — improving but still some stress from regulatory/legal uncertainty.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Sheets: "The jumping off point for the dollar in our current forecast is just slightly weaker than in March, as increases against the euro and the yen offset declines against the Canadian dollar and a range of emerging-market currencies." The euro weakened due to Greek contagion concerns. The broad real dollar is expected to depreciate at ~3.5% pace over the forecast period. Sack mentioned "a further weakening of the euro relative to the dollar" — so the dollar actually strengthened vs euro on Greek concerns, but overall the staff's forecast calls for dollar depreciation.

For the CURRENT assessment (not forecast): The dollar slightly weakened overall (slight net depreciation vs. EM currencies despite appreciation vs euro). The net is modest weakening/neutral. Score ~40-45 (modest weakness; "mixed" — some appreciation vs euro, depreciation vs EM and overall forecast of further depreciation).

Bright-line check: Dollar is characterized as "slightly weaker" than March forecast, with expected ongoing depreciation → score < 50.

**Direction**: Dollar is **modestly below** midpoint (slight weakness/depreciation on net, with ongoing depreciation expected).

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**
Core import prices are "rebounding, in part in response to the upturn in commodity prices." Core intermediate materials prices projected to increase more than 5% this year after declining 3% in 2009. Commodity prices up. But Stockton focuses on goods component being flat/declining in recent core PCE. Mixed signal. Score ~38-42 (modest goods price pressure from commodities, but recent data showing near-flat goods inflation).

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**
Not specifically broken out, but labor costs are the primary driver of services inflation. ECI +1.25% in 2009, productivity gains running ahead of compensation → very low services price pressure. Score ~28-33 (labor cost deceleration implies subdued services inflation).

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**
Not specifically discussed. Nonresidential vacancy rates high, rents weak mentioned briefly. Score N/A or ~32-38 given weak housing market. Housing barely mentioned in inflation context — N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**
Stockton: "we have been encouraged to largely stay the course on our price forecast by the continued stability of inflation expectations. If the recent string of very low core prices had been accompanied by a downdrift in survey measures of inflation expectations and in TIPS-based inflation compensation, we would have been inclined to mark down our forecast of core inflation by more than the tenth we took out of 2010 and 2011. But the survey measures have held steady, and the TIPS measures have only edged up." Near-term expectations stable, but TIPS have "only edged up." Staff sees risk from upside (balance sheet concerns, commodity prices) and downside (output gap). Well-anchored at approximately target level.

Score ~45-50 (well-anchored, stable, slight upward drift in TIPS).

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (24)**: Both below 50, consistent with deep post-crisis recession. Labor tightness slightly lower than output gap — consistent because unemployment tends to lag the output gap in recoveries. No contradiction.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (47)**: Output gap below 50, C+I near neutral. This divergence is explained by the fact that current spending/investment data have surprised to the upside (3.5% PCE, ~20% E&S investment), pulling C+I toward neutral, while the cumulative gap in the economy remains very negative. Staff forecasts a moderate recovery but acknowledges the cumulative output shortfall. This divergence (35 vs 47) is around 12 points — within the explanation threshold.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (30)**: Both below 50 (accommodative). FC slightly higher than credit spreads — consistent. Accommodative conditions with narrowing spreads. No contradiction.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC accommodative, Treasury functioning normally. Treasury stress is slightly higher than FC (40 vs 35) but both near lower ranges. In non-crisis periods this is acceptable — Treasury stress is approximately normal while FC is accommodative. No significant contradiction.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) ↔ Sub-components**: Parent ~30 consistent with goods ~40 (slight upward from commodities) and services ~30 (labor cost disinflation). Weighted average approximately supports 30. Inflation expectations ~47 (slightly higher) consistent with the stability Stockton describes.
