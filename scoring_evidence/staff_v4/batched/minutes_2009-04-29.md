# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with separate Staff Review of the Economic Situation, Staff Review of the Financial Situation, and Staff Economic Outlook sections.

**Era context**: April 2009 — deep in the GFC. QE1 underway. Economy in severe recession but pace of decline slowing. UE at 8.5% and rising. Massive fiscal stimulus (ARRA) recently enacted.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff says "Headline and core consumer prices rose at a moderate pace." Near-term inflation expectations increased but longer-term stayed at "low end of the range." Staff outlook: "the low level of resource utilization and a gradual decline in inflation expectations would lead to a deceleration in core PCE prices." Subdued inflation with downside risk. Score ~30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy still contracting sharply. IP "fell substantially," capacity utilization "decreased to a very low level." GDP expected to "edge higher in the second half." Far below potential. Bright-line: below potential → < 50. Score ~23.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE at 8.5% and climbing. "Fifth consecutive large monthly decrease" in payrolls. "Individuals receiving unemployment benefits...reached its highest level since 1982." Score ~16.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending firmed in the first quarter after dropping markedly." But "fundamentals remained weak." E&S "dropped markedly." Housing "remained depressed but seemed to have leveled off." Score ~26.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Conditions in financial markets improved more than had been expected." But "credit availability was still tight." Banks "continued to tighten their credit standards on all major loan categories." Corporate bond spreads "remained extremely high." Score ~70.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Trading conditions in secondary market for nominal Treasury securities showed some signs of improvement." But "trading volumes remained low." Score ~56.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate bond spreads remained extremely high by historical standards." Score ~75.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity risk premium "narrowed but was still very high by historical standards." Implied vol "stayed well above historical norms." Score ~64.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banks "continued to tighten credit standards." "Commercial bank credit contracted." "Ongoing lack of financing capacity at major financial institutions." Score ~72.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated against the other major currencies." Bright-line: depreciation → < 50. Score ~36.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks: All pairs coherent. OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR both deeply depressed. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and CREDIT_SPREADS both very tight.

### Bright-line verification: All pass.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Headline and core consumer prices rose at a moderate pace." Staff expects "deceleration in core PCE prices" from low resource utilization.
- Center-of-gravity: Core inflation modestly positive but expected to decelerate significantly.
- Direction: Below neutral — subdued with massive slack pushing prices lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 23 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Industrial production fell substantially...manufacturing capacity utilization decreased to a very low level." Staff projects GDP to "edge higher in the second half."
- Center-of-gravity: Deep recession with economy far below potential, though pace of decline moderating.
- Direction: Far below neutral — massive output gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 ±3 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: UE at 8.5%. "Fifth consecutive large monthly decrease" in payrolls. "Individuals receiving unemployment benefits...reached its highest level since 1982."
- Center-of-gravity: Extremely slack labor market.
- Direction: Far below neutral.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 26 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending firmed" but "fundamentals remained weak." "Real spending on equipment and software dropped markedly."
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer stabilizing from freefall; investment still contracting sharply.
- Direction: Well below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 70 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Credit availability was still tight." Banks "continued to tighten credit standards on all major loan categories." But "conditions improved more than expected."
- Center-of-gravity: Still very tight despite meaningful improvement from crisis peak.
- Direction: Above neutral — credit tight, lending standards restrictive.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 56 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Trading conditions showed some signs of improvement." But "daily trading volumes remained low."
- Center-of-gravity: Modest residual impairment, improving from crisis.
- Direction: Modestly above neutral.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 75 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Corporate bond spreads remained extremely high by historical standards."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads still at crisis-elevated levels despite narrowing.
- Direction: Well above neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 64 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Equity risk premium...was still very high by historical standards." "Option-implied volatility...stayed well above historical norms."
- Center-of-gravity: Markets recovering from lows but still at distressed/depressed levels.
- Direction: Above neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS: 72 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Commercial bank credit contracted." "Ongoing lack of financing capacity." Banks tightening all categories.
- Center-of-gravity: Severe stress — contracting credit, tightening standards, impaired capacity.
- Direction: Well above neutral.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 36 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "The dollar depreciated against the other major currencies in an environment of seemingly increased investor appetite for risk."
- Center-of-gravity: Dollar weakening as risk appetite returns.
- Direction: Below neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 35 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Energy prices rebounded somewhat." But "prices of non-oil imports moved down."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 25 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "House prices stayed on a downward trajectory."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Near-term inflation expectations increased in early April" but longer-term "at the low end of the range."
