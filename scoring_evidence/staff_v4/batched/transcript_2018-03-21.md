# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2018-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. March 2018 meeting.

**Era context**: March 2018. Rate at 1.25-1.50% (hiked to 1.50-1.75% at this meeting). TCJA + Bipartisan Budget Act providing massive fiscal stimulus. UE declining. Core PCE rising toward 2%. Dollar strengthening, breaking 2017 downward trend. Tariff risks emerging (steel/aluminum, Section 301 on China).

**Staff speakers**:
- MR. WILCOX: U.S. outlook -- Near-term spending "softer than anticipated." GDP growth averaging 2.5% annual rate. Output "1.5 percent above potential currently" and "widened nearly 1/2 percentage point over the past couple of months." Fiscal stimulus from TCJA + BBA adds "1 3/4 percent to the level of real GDP by the end of 2020." Output gap "widening to 3 1/2 percent" by 2019. UE projected to "level out in 2020 at 3.1 percent" -- "lowest level it would have attained since the early 1950s." Private payrolls averaging 230K/month over past three months. ADP-based pooled estimate "substantially exceeds the pace of hiring needed to absorb new entrants." Core PCE "rising to 2.2 percent by June." Inflation trend "a little below 2 percent" but "creeping up to 2 percent." Supply constraint allowance built in -- "incremental output would not be forthcoming" at usual rate in extremely tight labor market. ECI compensation growth "evolving about as one would expect" given tight labor market and "lackluster trend productivity gains."
- MR. GRUBER: International outlook -- Foreign growth "more or less unchanged from the time of the January meeting." Dollar "strengthened a touch over the intermeeting period, breaking its downward trend apparent through much of 2017." Dollar expected to "appreciate at about a 2 percent rate through 2020." Trade policy risks: steel/aluminum tariffs cover "relatively small share of U.S. imports" but "could become more material if the tariffs led to an escalation." Section 301 on China coming. Twin deficits analysis: current account to deteriorate 1.25% of GDP through 2020. Global savings glut analysis suggests "continuation of the global savings glut" putting "downward pressure on global interest rates."
- MR. GAGNON: SEP summary -- Median GDP 2.7% this year, 2.4% next. UE to 3.6% by end of next year. Core PCE to 1.9% this year, 2.1% by 2020. "No participant sees the risks to core and headline inflation as tilted to the downside."

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE rising to 2.2% by June. Trend "a little below 2 percent" but "creeping up." "Rising to... about 1.95 percent" on core basis. ECI consistent with expectations. Staff expects core to "flatten out" in second half and "edge back below 2 percent." Score: 46 -- approaching target, nearly there.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Output "1.5 percent above potential currently." Gap "widening to 3.5 percent" by 2019. Resource utilization "extraordinarily tight." UE to 3.1%. "Forward thrust" in economy very strong. Score: 60 -- clearly above potential, widening substantially.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE headed to 3.1% (lowest since early 1950s). Private payrolls 230K/month. ADP pooled estimate "substantially exceeds hiring needed." Supply constraints building in -- "incremental productivity of the last workers drawn in will be lower." ECI compensation growth "as one would expect" in tight market. Score: 62 -- very tight, tightening further.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Near-term spending indicators "softer than anticipated." GDP revised down from 3% to 2.5% near-term. But fiscal stimulus adding 1.75% to GDP by 2020. PCE "running at a more subdued pace." Medium-term strong. Score: 52 -- above trend on fiscal boost, but near-term softer.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Wilcox: "lackluster trend productivity gains." Supply constraint discussion implies productivity not sufficient to absorb tight labor. Score: 30 -- below trend.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Potter/Logan discussion at top: financial conditions indexes showing "tighter" from money market rate movements, "expected to be persistent." But equities recovering from February volatility. Gruber: dollar breaking downward trend. Score: N/A (insufficient direct staff FC assessment in this transcript).

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed as dysfunction. Score: N/A.
8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.
9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Not discussed in this transcript's staff sections. Score: N/A.
10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Gruber: dollar "strengthened a touch over the intermeeting period, breaking its downward trend." Expected to "appreciate at about a 2 percent rate through 2020." Score: 56 -- modest appreciation, expected to continue.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 60 above potential -- consistent with 1.5% above potential, widening to 3.5%. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 56 appreciation -- consistent.

**Cross-dimension coherence**: OUTPUT_GAP 60 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 62 -- aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 60 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 52 -- slight divergence explained by near-term softness vs medium-term fiscal thrust.
