# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript — Wilcox (domestic outlook), Kamin (international), Kiley (financial stability), Reifschneider (policy), Remache/Ihrig (balance sheet). Segmented. 864 lines.

**Era context**: December 2012. QE3 ongoing ($85B/month). Fiscal cliff imminent. UE ~7.7% (declining faster than expected). European crisis stabilizing after ECB OMT. Threshold-based forward guidance about to be adopted. Housing recovering.

**Wilcox key points**: "Labor market has been improving about in line with our expectations or perhaps a little faster." "Employment growth in November" better than anticipated. UE "declined two-tenths in November to 7.7 percent." "Spending and income data... point to a little less momentum in aggregate demand." "Consumer spending surprised us to the downside." "Orders and shipments somewhat better than anticipated" for business. "Fiscal cliff" drag assumed but not the severe version. "Core PCE prices about in line with expectations." "Relatively flat path for core inflation." Drought effects passing through to food. Oil declining slightly.

**Kamin key points**: Greek debt deal reached — "less stringent conditionality." "Somewhat less worrisome situation." European financial outlook "improving" but "still very anemic." Euro area contracting through mid-next year. Advanced economies outside Europe showing "less underlying momentum" — Japan, UK, Canada. China "growth picked up." Oil prices stable.

**Kiley (financial stability)**: "Little evidence that low rate environment has fostered additional vulnerabilities to date." Private sector deleveraging. Corporate balance sheets strong. Equity valuations "essentially no froth." Housing "on the low side of fair value." Treasury yields at historical lows — term premiums falling. Corporate bond spreads "above-average spreads relative to Treasuries." But "some increased willingness to take on risk." Leveraged loan issuance robust. Covenants weakening. Reach for yield developing. "Market conditions significantly tighter than... 2006 and 2007."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Core PCE "in line with expectations." "Relatively flat path for core inflation." "Inflation expectations anchored." Drought pushing food prices up. Oil declining. Slack narrowing but still large. Medium-term projection subdued.
- Direction: Below neutral — near target, contained by slack.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 36** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: UE 7.7%, "declining faster than expected." But "less momentum in aggregate demand." GDP growth modest. Fiscal cliff drag looming. European drag. Still below potential but improving.
- Direction: Below neutral but improving.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: UE 7.7% — significant improvement from 8.1% in September. "Labor market improving about in line with expectations or perhaps a little faster." Employment growth ~150K/month. But Wilcox notes lower UE "may not imply commensurate narrowing in the margin of unused labor resources." Still slack.
- Direction: Below neutral but notably improved.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Consumer spending surprised us to the downside." "Less momentum in aggregate demand." But "orders and shipments somewhat better." Business investment "subdued in near term" from fiscal cliff uncertainty. Housing showing signs of improvement. Mixed.
- Direction: Below neutral — consumer weak, business mixed.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: QE3 ongoing. ECB OMT stabilizing Europe. Stock prices modestly higher. "Less worrisome" European situation. But fiscal cliff uncertainty weighing. "Less-supportive financial conditioning assumptions" in forecast. Market expects larger QE than staff baseline. Overall mixed — policy easing vs fiscal uncertainty.
- Direction: Near neutral — roughly balanced.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38** ±7 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: Treasury yields at "historical lows." Term premiums "falling to levels not seen over the past several decades." Safe-haven demand plus QE. No dysfunction whatsoever.
- Direction: Below neutral — no stress, extremely low yields from QE and safe-haven.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 50** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: "Above-average spreads relative to Treasuries" but "investors should be adequately compensated for bearing credit risk." Leveraged loan issuance robust. Some reach for yield emerging. Corporate bond yields at historic lows. But spreads still above pre-crisis norms. "Market conditions significantly tighter than 2006-2007" levels of excess.
- Direction: Neutral — spreads normalized, adequate compensation for risk.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 46** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: "Essentially no froth in stock prices." "Required return to equity has fallen little since the initial decline after the financial crisis." "Implied equity risk premium widened to a value outside that seen in recent decades" (stocks cheap vs bonds). No overvaluation.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral — equities fairly valued to modestly cheap.

**BANKING_STRESS: 52** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: "Some large banks remain weak despite substantial increases in capital." "Unstable funding model of global dealer firms." "Persistent risk of runs on money market funds." But private sector deleveraging. "In the hangover, rather than the buildup, phase of the credit cycle." Banks taking interest rate risk. Overall modest residual concern.
- Direction: Near neutral — lingering concerns but systemic risk diminished.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52** ±7 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Dollar still somewhat elevated from earlier safe-haven flows. European stress easing should allow some depreciation. Not directly discussed in detail.
- Direction: Near neutral — slightly strong from prior safe-haven but normalizing.
