# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-05-04

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Reinhart (financial markets/policy). Era: May 2004. Pivotal meeting — inflation surprises to the upside. GDP miss on inventories (truck inventories) but "the forest is looking pretty good." Private payrolls rose 160,000 in March — improvement but still tentative. Consumer spending up 4%. E&S up 12%. BUT inflation surprises: core PCE revised up 0.25-0.5pp. Deflation risk discussion completely replaced by inflation risk discussion. NAIRU concerns raised. Staff raised funds rate path. Market pricing 3pp of tightening over two years. Financial conditions tightened "appreciably" over intermeeting period — Treasury yields up 75-90bp. First meeting where rate hike becomes imminent.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "forest is looking pretty good." "Private payrolls rose 160,000 in March, up from the 60,000 per month pace." Consumer spending "rose 4 percent in the first quarter." E&S "up about 12 percent." Housing "remained extremely strong." But inflation: "breadth of the surprises... suggested to us that recent wage and price developments could not prudently be ascribed to special factors alone." Core PCE raised "between ¼ and ½ percentage point" — now projected 1.25%. NAIRU alternative: "core PCE inflation increases to around 2 percent." Reinhart: "remarkable runup" in rates — funds rate expected one year ahead advanced 85bp. Market pricing rate hike by August. "Financial conditions tightened appreciably."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core consumer prices revised up 0.25-0.5pp. "Breadth of the surprises... could not prudently be ascribed to special factors alone." But still projecting core PCE flat at 1.25% going forward. Energy prices rising. NAIRU uncertainty raised. Risks shifted — no longer about deflation.
- **Direction**: Below neutral but rising notably. Major shift from prior meetings.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP miss in Q1 on inventories but final sales strong. Q2 boosted to 5%. "Firing on all cylinders" — tax cuts, government spending, brisk final sales. Gap narrowing but still present. Participation rate effects complicate gap measurement.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — closing. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Private payrolls rose 160,000 in March." "Long-awaited jobs recovery looked to be getting under way." But "should be viewed as encouraging but not definitive." Some employers reporting difficulty finding workers with right skills. Labor market improving but still tentative.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improving but slack remains.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: consumer spending "rose 4 percent in the first quarter." E&S "up about 12 percent." Housing "remained extremely strong" — near record starts. Orders "continuing to rise." GDP projected 5% Q2. Broad-based strength.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — robust above-trend spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 67 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: productivity growth expected to decelerate — "end to the acceleration of structural productivity." But still elevated. "Large increases in productivity over the past few years" allowed firms to rebuild margins. Staff uncertainty about persistence.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still elevated but beginning to slow.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Reinhart: "financial conditions tightened appreciably over the intermeeting period." Treasury yields up 75-90bp. But some narrowing of corporate risk spreads. Dollar appreciated slightly. Equity prices showed small net change. Still accommodative overall (funds rate at 1%) but less so than before.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — less accommodative but still supportive. Bright-line: still accommodative.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Reinhart: "some narrowing of corporate risk spreads." Credit conditions still favorable despite rate backup.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still compressed.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Reinhart: "small net change in equity prices." Market processing the shift to tightening. Not particularly stressed or overvalued.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Reinhart: "appreciation of the dollar" over intermeeting period. But still well below levels of a few years prior. Dollar weakness ongoing trend, temporary intermeeting bounce.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still weak overall despite recent appreciation.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "inflation expectations... stable to somewhat higher in recent months." Near-term expectations "stepped up noticeably in the last few months." But long-term expectations "remained reasonably well anchored." Shift from disinflationary to inflationary concerns.
- **Direction**: Near/slightly above neutral — expectations firming.
