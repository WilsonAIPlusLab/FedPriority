# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-03-18

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from the FOMC meeting held March 17–18, 2009. It is a segmented, post-2005 format with individually identified staff speakers. The relevant staff presentations are:

- **MS. MOSSER** (Patricia Mosser, Markets Group / SOMA Manager): Financial market developments, balance sheet, asset purchase programs — the primary financial briefing (pages 3–8 of the transcript).
- **MR. STOCKTON** (David Stockton, Director of Research): Economic outlook, Greenbook-based forecast — the primary economic briefing (pages 112–116).
- **MR. KAMIN** (Steven Kamin, International Finance): International economic outlook (pages 116–119).
- **MR. HILTON** (Spence Hilton): Balance sheet mechanics and reserve projections — operational/balance sheet, not the primary economic assessment.
- **MR. NELSON**, **MR. REIFSCHNEIDER**, **MR. SHEETS**: Q&A responses and supplementary presentations on TALF, LSAP analysis, and FX swap lines.

Participant/member views (go-around beginning with President Bullard at page 121) are explicitly excluded from scoring.

### 2. Era Context

March 2009 is the trough zone of the Global Financial Crisis. The Lehman bankruptcy occurred in September 2008; by March 2009 the economy is in free fall. The S&P 500 bottomed near March 9, 2009. Unemployment is rising rapidly (8.1% in February). GDP is contracting sharply. The FOMC has already cut the federal funds rate to 0–25 bps. This is the meeting at which the Committee announced a large-scale expansion of asset purchases (QE1 expansion). Era anchors: OUTPUT_GAP deep below 50; LABOR_TIGHTNESS very low; INFLATION_PRESSURE below 50 with deflation risk; FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS very tight; BANKING_STRESS very high.

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Economic Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff (Stockton) reports total PCE prices projected to rise only ½ percent in 2009 and ¾ percent in 2010, down from 2% in 2008. Core PCE decelerating from 2% to 1% in 2009, then ½% in 2010. Models projecting possible outright deflation. Risks include "appreciable decline in inflation expectations" and "move into outright deflation." Staff takes middle ground but marks down forecast. *Direction: Significantly below target. This is in the 15–22 anchor range for 2009 deflation fears.* Score will be around 18–22.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff projects real GDP to fall 2¼% in 2009 (Greenbook baseline), and notes incoming data suggest it could be closer to –7% annualized in Q1. Manufacturing capacity utilization at ~67%, "lowest level since we began publishing that series in 1948." Unemployment expected to rise to 9½% in early 2010. "Downward revision to forecast." "Deep recession." Bright-line: clearly far below potential. Score: 12–18 range (GFC trough).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.1% in February, expected to rise to 9½% in early 2010. Private payrolls dropping 650,000/month since November. Employment level 550,000 below January forecast. "Job losses likely remained appreciable." Bright-line: far from full employment, severe labor market deterioration. Score: 12–18 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Business fixed investment falling at 22% annual rate in Q4, projected –25% this quarter. Consumer spending stabilizing (retail sales posted increases in Jan/Feb) but only modest support from fiscal stimulus. "Mounting job losses, deteriorating household balance sheets, and sour sentiment." Capital spending contracting sharply due to "financing constraints and heightened economic uncertainty." State/local spending cut. Housing starts at historic lows. *Direction: Far below trend, consistent with GFC collapse.* Score: 10–15 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed in the staff presentations. Score: N/A.

### 4. Directional Signals — Core Financial Dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Mosser describes "continued sharp deterioration in global economic conditions, uncertainty and pessimism about the health of financial firms." "Market liquidity continues to deteriorate in nearly every market where the Fed has not intervened." "The rules coming from Washington change every other day; no one is willing to invest in anything." However, some partial positive signals: markets directly supported by Fed programs "continued to function reasonably well"; mortgage rates declining; some ABS spreads narrowing. Stockton notes "some limited comfort from the lack of further deterioration" in summary measures of financial stress. Bright-line: Overall financial conditions described as highly restrictive with unprecedented policy response. Score: 82–88 range (severe crisis conditions, though not quite at the acute Oct 2008 panic phase).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Mosser mentions the Treasury yield curve is "steep" and "seems consistent with past episodes of economic weakness" — this is normal market functioning, not dysfunction. Treasury repo rate rises reflect "large Treasury issuance and associated ample supply of Treasury collateral for lending" — also normal. No mention of auction failures, bid-ask dislocation, or settlement problems in Treasuries. Bright-line: Yield movements noted but no actual dysfunction described. Score: 40–48 (somewhat elevated but not stressed).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Bank CDS spreads "now significantly wider than they were during the height of the financial turmoil last fall, following the Lehman bankruptcy." "This is truly extraordinary." Financial firms' equity and preferred have fallen sharply. LIBOR elevated reflecting "increased credit risk of banks." MBS spreads "remain very high by historical standards" even after Fed purchase program. Consumer ABS spreads "narrowed somewhat" but still stressed. Bright-line: Spreads widened and elevated. Score: 86–92 range (severe crisis-level widening, described as extraordinary / exceeding Lehman levels).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: S&P 500 down 16% year-to-date (down 25% as of the prior Monday before a partial rally). Foreign stocks down 5–17%. Financial firms' equity "very distressed." Preferred equity of financials down 20%+ since late January. Staff (Mosser) discusses this in terms of distress and concerns about viability, not as a valuation question per se, but the framing is clearly one of significant price depression with ongoing distress signals. Score: 72–78 range (significantly depressed; distressed financial firm valuations).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Mosser: "financial firms...remain very distressed." Bank CDS spreads "significantly wider than...during the height of the financial turmoil last fall." "Lack of clarity about the size of 'toxic' assets and how legacy accrual assets will be marked has led to questions about their viability." "Deep concern that the current bank stress assessment will result in actual or de facto nationalization of some institutions." Stockton projects "financial conditions to gradually improve" — implying they are currently poor. Kamin notes Mexico banking system "less risk" than Eastern Europe — comparative concern about banking systems globally. Bright-line: Banking system described as severely stressed; solvency concerns. Score: 82–88 range.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Mosser (exhibit 7): "the dollar has appreciated against nearly every major currency since the beginning of the year." Stockton: "stronger exchange value of the dollar" was one of three principal factors behind the downward GDP revision. Kamin: "nearly 20 percent rise in the dollar since last summer" — helps support imports and restrain exports as an additional headwind. Bright-line: Dollar appreciation confirmed. Score: 70–76 range (notable broad-based appreciation cited as a headwind).

### 7. Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not explicitly broken out by goods vs services in staff text. However, CPI shows energy up sharply (+3.3% in month) but headline CPI only +0.4%, and total PCE only +0.5% projected for the year. Lower oil prices cited. No specific goods inflation concern raised. Score: N/A (not decomposed by staff).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI +0.2% monthly cited; core PCE decelerating from 2% to 1% this year. Not specifically attributed to services. Score: N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing starts at historic lows, sales declining, "no matter how low we set the forecast, the data surprise us to the downside." Housing is a source of deflationary pressure. Score: N/A (not discussed as an inflation sub-component explicitly).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Stockton: "many of our models are projecting a more pronounced deceleration of prices and a move into outright deflation by next year...expecting an appreciable decline in inflation expectations in response to wide margins of slack." However: "it is very difficult to discern any noticeable shift down in longer-term inflation expectations since last summer." Staff "took the middle ground" — assumes expectations drift lower but less than models project. Anchor: 25–35 for 2009 "risk of unanchoring to downside." Staff sees some downside drift risk but not yet unanchored. Score: 32–38 (drifting lower but not unanchored).

### 8. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (13) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (13)**: Both at catastrophic lows. Gap: 0. Consistent — deep recession. CHECK.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (13) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (12)**: Both at catastrophic lows. Gap: 1. Consistent. CHECK.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (84) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (88)**: Both very high. Gap: 4. Consistent — systemic crisis. CHECK.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (84) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC extremely tight, Treasury market functioning reasonably. This divergence is explained by the rubric: in normal conditions Treasury stress is near 40–50 regardless of FC. The specific language supports Treasury markets as not dysfunctional per se, even as overall financial conditions are crisis-level. This is consistent with the March 2009 situation where the Fed's interventions kept Treasury markets orderly even as broader credit markets seized. EXPLAINED.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (20) ↔ INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (34)**: Parent score slightly below sub-dimension; consistent — staff sees some downward drift in expectations but inflation pressure itself is already very low (near deflation). CONSISTENT.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 20 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STOCKTON: "total PCE prices are now projected to increase ½ percent over the four quarters of this year and to increase ¾ percent next year...We expect a smoother deceleration in core PCE prices, from 2 percent last year to 1 percent this year and ½ percent in 2010."
- MR. STOCKTON: "many of our models are projecting a more pronounced deceleration of prices and a move into outright deflation by next year."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff projects PCE inflation well below target and decelerating toward possible deflation, with risks explicitly skewed toward further disinflation.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far below neutral because staff sees inflation running at ½% headline and 1% core in 2009, approaching deflation territory, consistent with 2009 anchor range of 15–20.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 13 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STOCKTON: "we are now projecting that real GDP will fall 2¼ percent this year...those are downward revisions of about 1½ and 1 percentage point...the unemployment rate will rise to 9½ percent in early 2010."
- MR. STOCKTON: "Plummeting output left the capacity utilization rate in the manufacturing sector at about 67 percent in February, its lowest level since we began publishing that series in 1948."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff describes a catastrophic output gap — GDP in freefall, capacity utilization at all-time record lows, unemployment rising toward 9.5% — placing this firmly at the GFC trough.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral. Score of 13 is within the 15–22 GFC trough anchor range; the record-low capacity utilization and severity of language justify landing at the lower end of that range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 13 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STOCKTON: "private payrolls have been dropping at an average monthly pace of 650,000 since November...The unemployment rate rose to 8.1 percent in February...we expect that the unemployment rate will rise to 9½ percent in early 2010."
- MR. STOCKTON: "the level of payroll employment in February was 550,000 lower than was anticipated in our January forecast."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is in a severe contraction, with unemployment already at 8.1% and rising to 9.5%, massive monthly job losses, and indicators still pointing downward.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral. Unemployment at 8.1% and rising firmly anchors this below the 16–30 "high unemployment" range, at the lower end toward the 1–15 "depression-level" range.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 12 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STOCKTON: "Business fixed investment fell at an annual rate of 22 percent in the fourth quarter and is projected to drop at a 25 percent pace this quarter...The pullback in capital spending has been greater than can be explained by business sales and interest rates alone, suggesting that financing constraints and heightened economic uncertainty are part of the story."
- MR. STOCKTON: "Assemblies of light vehicles fell to a 3.7 million unit pace in January, the lowest level since the 1950s...sales, starts, and permits all fell more than we expected."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Business investment in freefall (–22% to –25% annualized), auto assemblies at 1950s lows, housing at historic lows, consumer spending stabilizing only modestly — overall spending and investment at catastrophic depths.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is far below neutral, consistent with the 2008 Q4–2009 Q1 anchor of 15–20 (scored slightly below given language severity of "lowest level since the 1950s" and record-level production cuts).

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Score: 84 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "market liquidity continues to deteriorate in nearly every market where the Fed has not intervened...the rules coming from Washington change every other day; no one is willing to invest in anything."
- MS. MOSSER: "Bank CDS spreads are now significantly wider than they were during the height of the financial turmoil last fall, following the Lehman bankruptcy. This is truly extraordinary."

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is far above neutral. Score of 84 reflects conditions approaching but not quite at the Sept/Oct 2008 acute panic (85–95 anchor), as some markets supported by Fed programs are functioning "reasonably well."

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

**Score: 42 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "the Treasury yield curve, while steep, seems consistent with past episodes of economic weakness" — framing yield movements as normal.
- MS. MOSSER: "The rise in Treasury repo rates...reflects the large Treasury issuance and associated ample supply of Treasury collateral for lending" — normal supply/demand, not dysfunction.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market functioning is described as orderly and explicable by economic and supply factors; no language of dysfunction, dislocation, or illiquidity in Treasuries specifically.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral. Yield movements noted but attributed to policy expectations and supply factors, consistent with the bright-line rule that yield movements alone are not dysfunction.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS

**Score: 88 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "Bank CDS spreads are now significantly wider than they were during the height of the financial turmoil last fall, following the Lehman bankruptcy. This is truly extraordinary."
- MS. MOSSER: "MBS purchase program has helped to lower MBS spreads from the exceptional levels seen last fall, they remain very high by historical standards."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit spreads are at or above post-Lehman peaks — bank CDS described as "truly extraordinary" even exceeding Lehman episode levels — placing this at the extreme end of the widened-spreads range.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is far above neutral. Score of 88 reflects the language of spreads exceeding the Lehman bankruptcy episode, which itself was a historic extreme.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION

**Score: 74 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "global equity prices are sharply lower. The S&P 500 has fallen 16 percent since the beginning of the year...as of last Monday, the S&P 500 was down 25 percent for the year."
- MS. MOSSER: "financial firms...remain very distressed...preferred equity of financials has fallen more than 20 percent since late January...the possibility of losses further up the capital structure is perceived to be largest."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equities are described as significantly depressed — S&P 500 down ~25% from year-start before a partial bounce — with financial sector equities especially distressed at levels consistent with solvency concerns.

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress/concern). The staff focuses on price declines and "very distressed" financials rather than assessing whether equities are at "fair value," but the framing of continued distress justifies a high-stress score in the 66–80 range.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS

**Score: 85 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "financial firms...remain very distressed. The continued uncertainty regarding both the health of financial firms as well as the nature of government assistance programs has weighed on their capital structures."
- MS. MOSSER: "in the U.S., there is deep concern that the current bank stress assessment will result in actual or de facto nationalization of some institutions; this raises the possibility that debt holders as well as equity holders will take a hit."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system described as severely stressed with solvency-level concerns — nationalization fears, CDS spreads exceeding Lehman levels, opaque balance sheets, and "questions about viability" of major institutions.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is far above neutral. Score of 85 is within the 70–85 GFC systemic crisis anchor range. The language of potential de facto nationalization and debt holders taking losses pushes this toward the higher end.

---

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH

**Score: 72 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MOSSER: "the dollar has appreciated against nearly every major currency since the beginning of the year. Certainly, the dollar's position as a reserve currency and its continued status as a safe haven have contributed to its appreciation."
- MR. KAMIN: "nearly 20 percent rise in the dollar since last summer...helps support imports and restrain exports...adding yet another headwind."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar has appreciated broadly (~20% since last summer) and is functioning as a safe haven; staff explicitly cites dollar strength as one of three major negative factors in the GDP forecast downward revision.

**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral. Dollar appreciation is confirmed by both Mosser (against nearly all currencies) and Kamin (20% since last summer), placing this in the 66–80 "notable strength" range.
