# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-03-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook — "five lessons"), Johnson (international). Era: March 2002. Recovery underway but "recession that failed to begin" narrative. GDP growth revised up to 3.5%+ H1. Productivity surging — 5.25% Q4 2001, 5.75% Q1 2002. Consumer spending surprisingly strong. Tax cuts effective. But labor market still very weak — hiring subdued. Structural productivity revised up. Dollar beginning to depreciate. Core PCE 1.25%. Staff projects funds rate to 4% by end 2003.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "five lessons learned." GDP "revised up our forecast for the growth of real output in the first half" from 2% to 3.5%+. Productivity "risen at an annual rate of about 5¼ percent in the final quarter of 2001." Consumer spending "advanced at a 2¼ percent annual rate in the fourth quarter" and "nearly 5½ percent this quarter." Housing "moved up smartly." But "pace of hiring has remained subdued." Structural productivity revised up 0.1-0.3pp. Core PCE 1.25% both years. Monetary policy "still works" — "real interest rates well below" equilibrium. Staff projects tightening starting, funds rate to 4% by end 2003. Johnson: "lower our projected path for the exchange value of the dollar."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "revised down a bit our forecast for inflation." Core PCE 1.25% both years. "Virtually all of the major measures of total and core inflation have moved lower." Productivity gains holding down unit labor costs. "Few signs of any notable inflation pressures."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP growing above trend at 3.5%+. But "economy has had and will have a larger margin of excess capacity than previously projected." Recovery underway but gap still sizable. Unemployment near peak.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "pace of hiring has remained subdued." "Many business people continue to express concern about the outlook." Firms "should begin to hire more vigorously" but gradual. Employment weak despite strong output.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: consumer spending surprisingly strong — "nearly 5½ percent this quarter." Housing "moved up smartly." But business investment still weak — "recovery in capital spending for high-tech equipment will take slightly longer." "Nonresidential building faces an even greater retrenchment."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — strong household offset by weak business spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: productivity "5¼ percent" Q4 2001, implied "5¾ percent" Q1 2002. Structural estimate revised up. "There has been some signal about underlying productivity." "Faster productivity growth probably helps to explain some of the strength" in demand.
- **Direction**: Well above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 50 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "real interest rates well below" equilibrium — accommodative. But "lower trajectory for the stock market—about 8 percent across the board—implies greater saving and less consumption." Equity decline offsetting rate accommodation. Mixed.
- **Direction**: At neutral — low rates vs. stock market decline.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: "lower our projected path for the exchange value of the dollar." Dollar beginning to decline from elevated levels. "Nominal index for the major currencies" depreciating 2-3.5% against several currencies. But still above equilibrium.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still elevated but declining.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not explicitly discussed but inflation subdued. Expectations consistent with low and stable inflation environment.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.
