# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2001-10-02 (October 2 meeting)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2005 format. Score only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: October 2001. First regular FOMC meeting after 9/11 attacks. Economy was already weak; terrorist attacks appear to have "induced a mild downturn." Emergency 50bp cut on Sep 17 brought FFR to 3%. Massive financial market disruptions post-9/11. UE at 4.9%. Consumer confidence plunging.

**Staff-attributable content**: "The information reviewed at this meeting," "the staff indicated that its downward revised outlook," data paragraphs.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "Consumer price inflation remained relatively limited in July and August." Core PCE "rose a bit less" year-over-year. "Core prices declined in August." Staff forecast: "modest deceleration in core PCE and CPI inflation." Score ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Attacks of September 11 might well have induced a mild downturn." "Industrial production fell substantially further in August." Capacity utilization "lowest level since mid-1983." Staff: "A mild downturn in overall economic activity probably was now under way." Score ~22.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unemployment rate rose to 4.9 percent in August." "Sharp increase in initial claims for unemployment insurance." "Additional deterioration in labor markets." Score ~38.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending picked up somewhat in July and August" but "anecdotal reports...pointed to a downturn in September." "Business capital spending contracted substantially further." Nonresidential construction "falling appreciably." Score ~25.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed in staff forecast section. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Post-9/11 massive disruptions. "Rates on high-yield bonds...rose sharply in very thin markets." "Broad equity price measures fell considerably further." But "the yield declines did not extend to long-term Treasury bonds." "Record infusions of Federal Reserve credit." Score ~72.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "For a few days after September 11, with federal funds brokerage disrupted..." "Massive dislocations in the distribution of deposits and reserves." "Disruptions to the infrastructure of financial markets." Actual market dysfunction, not just yield movements. Score ~75.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Rates on high-yield bonds...rose sharply in very thin markets." "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds also were little changed" while safe-haven Treasuries fell. Score ~72.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price measures fell considerably further in volatile trading after the markets reopened on September 17." Score ~72.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Record infusions of Federal Reserve credit through open market operations, the discount window, and other sources." Emergency swap lines established. But this is liquidity support, not bank insolvency. "A sound banking system...as sources of underlying strength." Score ~45.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Trade-weighted value of the dollar...was about unchanged on average." Score ~50.

### Bright-line checks: OUTPUT_GAP "downturn" → < 50. Check. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS extreme disruptions → > 50. Check. BANKING_STRESS "sound banking system" → < 50. Check. TREASURY_STRESS actual dysfunction → > 60. Check.

### Cross-dimension coherence: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (72) and CREDIT_SPREADS (72): Coherent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (72) and TREASURY_STRESS (75): Coherent — genuine crisis. OUTPUT_GAP (22) and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (25): Coherent.
