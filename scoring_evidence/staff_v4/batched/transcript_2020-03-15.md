## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from an emergency videoconference FOMC meeting held on Sunday, March 15, 2020. It is a segmented, post-2005 format with identifiable staff speakers. The meeting was convened amid the unfolding COVID-19 pandemic and represents one of the most acute emergency meetings in the Fed's modern history.

Staff speakers identified:
- **MS. LOGAN** (Lorie Logan, Manager of the System Open Market Account, FRBNY) — financial markets and Desk operations briefing (pages 3–11 of transcript)
- **MS. WILSON** (Beth Anne Wilson, Director of International Finance, Board) — international economic outlook (pages 12–13)
- **MS. TEVLIN** (Stacey Tevlin, Economist/Director of Research, Board) — domestic economic outlook (pages 13–15)
- **MR. LEHNERT** (Andreas Lehnert, Director of Financial Stability, Board) — brief interjection on commercial paper market

The subsequent content (pages 15+) transitions to the go-around/participant discussion. Only the staff briefing sections are scored.

### 2. Era Context

This is the March 2020 COVID-19 emergency meeting — one of the most extreme macro dislocations in modern history. The economy entered the meeting in relatively good shape (unemployment ~3.5%, growth at/above trend), but the COVID shock was hitting in real time. The era anchors for output gap and labor tightness must reflect the rapidly deteriorating conditions being described, not the pre-pandemic state. Staff clearly describes a sharp near-term contraction.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff (Governor Clarida, echoing staff baseline) explicitly says "the net effect of the virus is likely to be disinflationary, not inflationary" and baseline view is ending the year "further away from our price-stability objective than we began." MS. TEVLIN's scenarios project unemployment rising to 4.3–6.5%+ which implies disinflationary pressure. Staff sees inflation pressure **well below target and deteriorating**.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: MS. TEVLIN describes real GDP declining "2 percent next quarter" in baseline scenario and "8 percent next quarter" in the severe scenario. She says "we feel reasonably certain that real GDP will decline next quarter." The direction is unmistakably downward. Staff sees output gap **deeply negative and collapsing**. Magnitude is extreme — "GDP drops 8 percent next quarter" in severe scenario. The baseline scenario projects a modest recovery only by fall. This is a catastrophic output shock.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Pre-crisis, unemployment was at 50-year lows (~3.5%). But MS. TEVLIN projects unemployment "reaching 4.3 percent" in baseline and "above 6.5 percent" in severe scenario. Staff is forecasting a very rapid deterioration from extremely tight to significantly loose. The staff's current assessment describes an economy that was tight but is now rapidly deteriorating. The forward-looking tone dominates — staff expects significant labor market weakening. Score must reflect what staff currently sees/forecasts as the direction: severely deteriorating from an extremely tight starting point, with the baseline showing quick movement to 4.3% and severe scenario to 6.5%+. Given the assessment is about current conditions turning negative extremely rapidly, I'll score this reflecting a quickly-deteriorating labor market — still not yet at recessionary lows but rapidly heading there.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: MS. TEVLIN cites "extensive social distancing measures, along with sharply lower consumer and business sentiment, will take a serious toll on economic activity." MS. WILSON describes "rapid spread of virus and draconian containment measures... sharp deterioration in financial conditions... plummeting commodity prices." Staff language about both consumer and business spending is uniformly catastrophic — "collapsing" activity in airlines, hospitality, restaurants, public events. Score reflects severe contraction/freefall.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not directly discussed in the staff briefings. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: MS. LOGAN provides the most detailed assessment. Key phrase: "The extraordinary events of recent weeks have taken their toll on asset prices, market functioning, and the flow of credit." She describes: S&P 500 down 18%, Treasury market "severely impaired," credit spreads widened 120bps (investment-grade) and 360bps (high-yield), commercial paper market "nearly nonexistent," corporate bond trading "very strained," MBS market "very strained." This is an extreme tightening of financial conditions — systemic stress. Score must be very high (crisis level).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: MS. LOGAN provides extensive detail: "dealers reported that they had stopped making markets in off-the-run securities, and that this segment of the Treasury market had ceased to function effectively." She describes "acute decline in [market] conditions toward the end of last week," dealers "unable to pass them on," "extraordinary flows" causing breakdown. Vice Chair Williams calls it "extraordinarily low, low levels of liquidity" — "extraordinarily." This is clearly the "dash for cash" episode well documented in the literature. Score: severe dysfunction, approaching top of range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: MS. LOGAN states investment-grade spreads widened ~120bps and high-yield spreads widened ~360bps. "Approximately one-fifth of the benchmark high-yield index is now trading in distress, with spreads of greater than 1,000 basis points." Primary issuance has "become sporadic for investment-grade firms and has largely halted for lower-rated names." This is severe/extreme widening.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: MS. LOGAN states S&P 500 "has fallen 18 percent" since the January meeting. She does not make explicit valuation comments (cheap/expensive), but the focus of the briefing is on market dysfunction, not valuations per se. Score reflects "sharply declining, significant correction" language — consistent with distressed/correction territory, not extreme panic-selling valuation commentary.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Limited direct discussion of bank capital/solvency. MS. LEHNERT and participants note banks are "well-capitalized and meeting liquidity requirements" but "their behavior is more consistent with hoarding capital and liquidity." MS. LOGAN notes banks drawing down credit lines. The banking system appears stressed in behavior but not yet in solvency. MS. WILSON mentions "weak banking systems" in the downside scenario (abroad). Score: elevated stress emerging, but system not yet in systemic solvency crisis — moderate stress level.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: MS. LOGAN states "The U.S. dollar has appreciated notably against most currencies, with the exception of other safe havens." MS. WILSON's downside scenario includes "10 percent or so appreciation of the dollar." Bright-line rule: dollar appreciation → score > 50. Clear notable appreciation confirmed.

- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Staff confirms notable appreciation. Score 65-75. Check: Yes.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Staff describes acute dysfunction — dealers stopped making markets. Score 75-85. Check: Yes.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Staff describes massive contraction below potential. Score must be < 50, likely 10-20. Check: Yes.

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (12) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (22)**: Both below 50. Output gap is more extreme because it reflects the severity of near-term GDP collapse. Labor market is rapidly deteriorating but not yet at recessionary lows in the data — staff projects 4.3–6.5% unemployment ahead. This near-term divergence (output gap more extreme than labor so far) is consistent with a shock where layoffs lag GDP collapse by weeks/months. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (12) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (12)**: Both very low and consistent. Consumer spending and investment are collapsing simultaneously with the output gap. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (88) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (85)**: Both very high. Consistent — severe tightening of financial conditions is reflected in dramatically widened credit spreads. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (88) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (80)**: Both high, consistent with a crisis episode. Treasury market dysfunction is a core element of the financial conditions deterioration described. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Inflation is expected to fall further from target due to demand collapse. No goods/services/housing inflation data specifically discussed. Expectations not discussed in detail. Parent score at 22 is consistent with the disinflationary narrative.

### 7. Emergency Meeting Note

Per the rubric's Edge Cases: this is an emergency meeting (March 2020). The briefings are focused — Logan covers financial markets in detail, Wilson and Tevlin give compressed but substantive economic outlooks. The document is sufficient for scoring but confidence is Moderate rather than High for economic dimensions given the unusual brevity and heavy use of "scenarios" rather than point forecasts.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 80 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- (MS. LOGAN): "A number of primary dealers reported that they had stopped making markets in off-the-run securities, and that this segment of the Treasury market had ceased to function effectively." "This disruption to intermediation was due to extraordinary flows."
- (MS. LOGAN): "Remains severely impaired." The Desk executed the "largest-ever daily amount" of Treasury purchases "by three times," spanning "six operations and taking five hours to complete" — and "we saw only short-lived improvement in market functioning."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff describes actual market dysfunction — not just yield volatility. Dealers stopped making markets, primary dealers reporting inability to intermediate, liquidity described as "severely impaired." This is squarely in the "severe dysfunction" range, consistent with the March 2020 "dash for cash" episode (rubric anchor: 65-80).

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is far above neutral. Score of 80 reflects severe but not complete market breakdown — some functioning persists and the Desk's purchases provided some relief. The bright-line rule (actual market dysfunction required for >60) is clearly met.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 85 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- (MS. LOGAN): "Investment-grade and high-yield credit spreads have widened about 120 basis points and 360 basis points, respectively." "Approximately one-fifth of the benchmark high-yield index is now trading in distress, with spreads of greater than 1,000 basis points."
- (MS. LOGAN): "Primary issuance has become sporadic for investment-grade firms and has largely halted for lower-rated names." Bonds of certain airlines "already trading at close to their expected recovery values in default."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff describes dramatically widened spreads — HY at +360bps on average with 20% of the index at distress levels (>1000bps), investment-grade primary markets sporadic. This is in "severely wide / credit rationing" territory.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is far above neutral. Score of 85 consistent with "severely wide / markets seized" conditions. Bright-line rule satisfied — widened/elevated language throughout.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
