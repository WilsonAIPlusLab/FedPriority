# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 1998-09-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript — staff presentations. Key speakers: MR. PRELL (domestic, extensive), MR. TRUMAN (international), MR. FISHER (markets/LTCM). September 1998. LTCM crisis at peak; Russia aftermath; global financial markets in turmoil.

**Era context**: September 1998. Fed at 5.50% (about to cut). Fisher: "extreme loss of confidence" in markets; "delevering process" underway. Prell: domestic economy "still shows considerable forward momentum" with "strength in domestic final demands" — but financial markets in crisis. Stock market "dropped roughly" 15-20%; "volatility has been high"; investment grade yields "only edged down despite sharp drop in rates on comparable Treasuries"; junk yields "risen steeply"; "senior loan officers revealed that large banks have shifted from a somewhat accommodative to a more restrictive posture for business loans." Staff now assumes 75bp in cuts by next spring. GDP forecast: "about 1 percent in the first half of next year." Truman: Latin America growth cut by 1.5-2pp; "no growth in real exports"; dollar weaker against major currencies. Consumer confidence slipping. Brazil risks.

**Dimension-by-dimension assessment**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Prell: consumer prices "projected to pick up some next year" but oil and import prices both falling; "important factors limiting the deterioration in inflation"; "weak demand and ample capacity in the factory sector." Score ~42.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Currently above potential but sharp deceleration forecast to 1% in H1 1999. Score ~54.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Prell: "labor markets will be tight through the middle of 2000"; payroll increases "fell below the gains of the first half" in August; but still tight. Score ~62.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Prell: consumer spending "nearly matched the phenomenal gains of the first half"; motor vehicles "rebounding smartly"; housing at "exceptionally high level." But wealth effects reversing. Score ~55.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Not directly discussed. Continuation. Score ~57.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: CRISIS: yields on investment grade "only edged down despite sharp drop" in Treasuries; junk yields "risen steeply"; banks shifted to "more restrictive posture"; "pulling back in financial markets." Score ~68.

7. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Market dropped 15-20% from peak; P/E "still seem to be very generously valued." Score ~32.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Prell: investment grade/Treasury spread widened; junk yields "risen steeply." Score ~68.

9. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Truman: dollar weaker against major currencies; expected to "remain lower." Score ~50.

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Prell: "banks have shifted from accommodative to more restrictive posture"; "pulling back." Score ~62.
