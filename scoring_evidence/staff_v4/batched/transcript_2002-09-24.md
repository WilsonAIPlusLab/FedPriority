# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-09-24

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: September 2002. One year post-9/11. Expansion "nearly a year old" but "significant questions still attach to the strength of this expansion." Domestic spending data "almost uniformly stronger than our expectations" — autos surged to 18.5 million, consumer spending nearly 3%. E&S spending surged 10% Q3. But labor market weak — employment flat, hours declining, claims rising. Manufacturing IP flat since June. Productivity surge continues — projected 4% Q3, four-quarter growth 5.25%. Growth not carried forward — GDP revised up short-term but long-term unchanged. Dollar declining further.

**Key signals**: Stockton: domestic spending data "almost uniformly stronger." Auto sales 18.5 million. Consumer spending "nearly 3 percent for the quarter." E&S "projecting a 10 percent annual rate increase" Q3. But "carried none of that stronger near-term growth forward" — inventory and timing effects. "Employment has been moving sideways... hours worked have, on net, continued to drift down." Initial claims "might even suggest some deterioration." Manufacturing IP "flat since June." Productivity "an increase in nonfarm business output per hour of about 4 percent in the current quarter." Real funds rate "close to zero."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Inflation subdued. Core PCE still low. Commodity prices "relatively firm" but not a concern. Productivity surge keeping unit labor costs down. Large output gap persisting.
- **Direction**: Below neutral.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP "will not move above that of potential until the second half of next year." Still significant slack. Manufacturing capacity utilization low. Labor market suggesting even weaker than output data implies.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Employment has been moving sideways." "Hours worked have, on net, continued to drift down." Initial claims "might even suggest some deterioration." Manufacturing output excluding motor vehicles "flat since June."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack persisting.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: consumer spending "nearly 3 percent" — strong. Auto sales surged. E&S spending up 10% annualized Q3. But "carried none of that stronger near-term growth forward." Housing strong. Telecommunications spending "shown no sign of improvement."
- **Direction**: At neutral — spending stronger near-term but not sustained.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 68 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "increase in nonfarm business output per hour of about 4 percent in the current quarter." "Four-quarter growth in labor productivity to about 5¼ percent." "Another apparent outsized increase in labor productivity."
- **Direction**: Well above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "real short-term interest rates close to zero" — accommodative. But "considerable hit taken by the asset side of balance sheets." "General climate of caution pervading the business environment." Dollar declining — positive for trade but reflecting weakness. Still accommodative on net but corporate stress.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — rates accommodative but equity/corporate stress offsetting.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "associated drop in the exchange value of the dollar" adding to pickup in activity. Dollar continuing decline. "Further impetus to aggregate demand from the federal government."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — depreciating.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not directly discussed. Inflation subdued. Expectations stable at low levels.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.
