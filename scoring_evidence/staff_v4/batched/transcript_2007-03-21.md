# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — housing/subprime, equipment spending, consumer, inflation), Johnson/Karen (international — foreign growth, financial volatility, trade), Reinhart (projections).

**Era context**: March 2007 — funds rate at 5.25%. Subprime crisis intensifying. GDP growth at 2.1-2.3%. Equipment spending surprisingly weak. Housing adjustment deepening with subprime problems.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: GDP forecast "marked down our forecast for the growth of real GDP by 1/4 percentage point both this year and next, to 2.1 and 2.3 percent, respectively." "Below-trend growth" continuing. Housing: "the rapid intensification of problems in the subprime market" — explicit adjustment for "significant pullback in nonprime originations." "Lowered the level of our forecast of starts and sales a further 3 percent." Subprime estimated "equal to 10 percent of total home sales in 2005 and 7 percent of sales in 2006."
- Equipment spending: "principal surprise has been equipment investment outside high-tech and transportation, which now seems poised to fall about 7 percent" — "well below our earlier expectations." "Businesses may have become a bit more cautious."
- Consumer: "coming in very close to our expectations." But fundamentals weakening — oil prices up $5/barrel, equity prices down ~4%.
- Labor: "labor market continues to flash stronger signals than would be expected." UE "fell back to 4.5 percent." "Not clear that payroll employment gains have slowed at all."
- Inflation: core PCE forecast raised to "2.5 percent for 2007." Oil up $5/barrel adds 0.25pp to PCE. "Pressures on inflation do not seem to be abating much either."
- Johnson (international): "vigorous growth in China" at 5.5% in Japan. "Credit spreads widened for risky credit abroad." Financial volatility February 27 was "global event" but limited spillover expected.
- Stockton: "Financial conditions remain favorable, corporate balance sheets generally look healthy."
