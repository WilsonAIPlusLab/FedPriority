# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript — Wilcox (domestic), Kamin (international), Potter (financial markets), Kiley (financial stability). Segmented. 993 lines.

**Era context**: September 2013. Taper expected but not yet implemented (surprise no-taper at this meeting). UE 7.3%. Rates up 120bp since April/May. Government shutdown looming. Syria concerns. EME stress from taper fears — Turkey, India, Indonesia, Brazil currencies hit. Europe — "recession over." Japan strong. China data improving.

**Wilcox key points**: GDP outlook "shade weaker than July" from "slightly tighter financial conditions" — higher rates, stronger dollar. UE "came in at 7.3 percent, 0.2 below July projection." "Unbroken string of one-sided forecast errors" on UE for a year. Natural rate revised down again. "Payroll job growth came in weaker." Participation rate lower than expected. Core inflation "about as expected" — "core inflation edging up toward 2 percent objective at glacial pace." Medium-term: GDP "beginning to decelerate toward potential rate of growth" by 2016. One-year-ago comparison: "despite an economy that arguably has made a little more progress toward full recovery, longer-term interest rates are no higher." But "a range of other asset prices, including equity prices and house prices, are considerably higher."

**Kamin key points**: Good news — "recovery is finally underway" in AFEs. Japan 4% Q2. UK surged to 3%. Euro zone "GDP eked out a gain in Q2 following year and a half of steady contraction." "Ready to declare the euro-zone recession over." EME growth picking up — "emerging Asia moved up to almost 4 percent." China improving. But Mexico weak. EME financial stress from taper fears — "financial stresses in some emerging markets intensified early in period but conditions have stabilized."

**Potter (financial markets)**: "U.S. interest rates rose moderately over intermeeting period." "10-year Treasury yields and mortgage rates up roughly 120 basis points since April/May." "Shifting expectations for Federal Reserve policy." "Uncertainty about the policy outlook increased." "Leadership succession" — Summers withdrawal drove rates down. "S&P 500 and trade-weighted dollar little changed." European peripheral spreads continued to narrow. EME differentiation — Turkey/India/Indonesia/Brazil currencies weak vs others stable.

**Kiley (financial stability)**: "Vulnerability of financial system appears moderate." "Loss-absorbing capital has increased." "Reliance on short-term funding decreased." "Private nonfinancial credit relative to GDP moved sideways at below-trend level." Banks "reduced leverage." But "structural vulnerabilities including run-prone structure of money market funds remain significant." "Leveraged lending" guidance being issued. "Interest rate risks at banks" being scrutinized. "Potential problems in emerging market economies" being monitored.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Core PCE "about as expected" — edging up from ~1% but still well below 2%. "Core inflation edging up toward 2 percent objective at glacial pace." "Core inflation projection shaved a tenth" from measurement changes. Headline below core from projected energy price declines.
- Direction: Below neutral — persistent undershoot.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 38** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: UE 7.3% — "0.2 below July projection." GDP "shade weaker." By 2016, "beginning to decelerate toward potential." UE expected to reach 6.5% one quarter earlier. By end 2016, "5.3 percent, essentially back to natural rate." Narrowing gap.
- Direction: Below neutral but closing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: UE 7.3%. Declining faster than expected. But "payroll job growth came in weaker" — "level of payroll employment 130,000 below July projection." Participation rate dropping. "Job availability" measures show "improvement but remains appreciably short of full employment." Mixed — UE down but payrolls weak.
- Direction: Below neutral — improving on UE, weaker on payrolls.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42** ±6 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Not extensively discussed separately. GDP "shade weaker than July." Consumer side somewhat better from house price/equity wealth effects. Business investment mixed. Fiscal drag diminishing.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Potter: Rates up 120bp since April/May. Mortgage rates up. "Slightly tighter financial conditions." Dollar stronger. But equity prices up substantially from year ago. House prices "roughly 15 percent higher." "Consequence of markedly easier stance of monetary policy than anticipated" (QE). Overall tighter than earlier in year but still broadly accommodative from asset price gains and QE.
- Direction: Near neutral — rate tightening partially offset by asset price gains.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 52** ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "10-year yields up roughly 120 basis points since April/May." "Deleveraging and convexity hedging likely amplified" earlier moves. "Uncertainty about policy outlook increased." "Implied volatility" rose. But no dysfunction — rates rising from expectations, not market breakdown. "Interest rate implied volatility" elevated.
- Direction: Near neutral — rates elevated from taper expectations but no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 46** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: "Private nonfinancial credit-to-GDP below trend." Corporate spreads still compressed. "Leveraged loan issuance" robust but being addressed by supervisory guidance. "Pass-through to corporate debt" from rate rise but "does not convey a" significant signal. "Speculative-grade issuance a bit below first half pace."
- Direction: Near neutral to slightly below — spreads still tight despite rate backup.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: S&P 500 "little changed" over intermeeting period. But Kiley: "re-pricing" reduced some "valuation pressures." "Equity valuations edged up a bit but appear" reasonable. House price rise "perhaps most notable" area to monitor.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral — fairly valued after rate adjustment.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Kiley: "Loss-absorbing capital has increased." "Reliance on short-term funding decreased." "Financial system appears moderate" vulnerability. "Banks reduced leverage." But "structural vulnerabilities" including money market fund run risk remain. Stress tests showed resilience.
- Direction: Below neutral — banking sector in good shape.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Exchange value of the dollar few percentage points stronger than expected" from year ago. EME currencies weak — Turkey, India, Indonesia, Brazil. But dollar "little changed" over intermeeting period. Dollar stronger from taper expectations.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — dollar appreciating.
