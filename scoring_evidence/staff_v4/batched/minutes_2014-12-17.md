# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2014-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes, post-2005 segmented format.

**Era context**: December 2014. QE ended in October. "Patient" language introduced. UE fell to 5.8%. Employment gains accelerating. PCE ~1.5% y/y but total CPI ~1.25% (energy drag). Oil prices in freefall. Consumer confidence highest since before recession. Dollar appreciating substantially (divergent monetary policy). Corporate spreads widening (energy sector stress). VIX spiked late period. Russian ruble crisis. Staff revised up GDP slightly.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Total PCE and core "about 1½ percent over the 12 months ending in October"; CPI only "1¼ percent" (energy decline); core CPI "1¾ percent"; "Labor compensation continued to increase only a little faster than consumer prices"; compensation per hour "about 2 percent"; market-based inflation compensation "moved lower"; but survey measures "remained stable"; staff: "inflation would move up gradually toward...but run somewhat below...2 percent"
- Direction: Below neutral — inflation at 1.5% with declining energy pulling headline lower.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "economic activity was increasing at a moderate pace"; employment "expanded...at a faster pace than in the third quarter"; UE "edged down to 5.8 percent"; participation rate "rose slightly"; industrial production "rose...led by strong increases in manufacturing output"; staff: GDP revised up, "real GDP was still expected to rise...more quickly than potential output"; UE "expected to...temporarily move slightly below...natural rate"
- Direction: Below neutral but approaching balance — UE near natural rate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: UE "5.8 percent"; payrolls "expanded...at a faster pace"; participation and E-P "rose slightly"; part-time for economic reasons "declined"; "rate of private-sector job openings stayed...at its recent elevated level"; "rates of hiring and of quits stepped up"
- Direction: Near neutral — approaching full employment, hiring and quits rising.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Real PCE "appeared to be rising robustly"; retail sales "rose strongly"; light motor vehicle sales "increased noticeably"; consumer sentiment "at its highest level since before the most recent recession"; real disposable income "rose further"; energy prices declining (boosting purchasing power); but housing "generally remained slow"; E&S "appeared to be decelerating"; nonresidential structures "edged down"
- Direction: Neutral — strong consumer, weak housing/investment.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 47 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Not explicitly discussed; staff continued with low potential growth assumptions.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Longer-term yields "fell"; but corporate spreads "widened"; energy-sector spreads "rose substantially"; equity prices "little changed"; dollar "appreciated substantially"; VIX "rose sharply late in the period"; October FOMC statement read as "slightly less accommodative"
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — mixed; dollar strength and spread widening offset lower yields.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Longer-term nominal Treasury yields declined significantly"; TIPS measures "decreased" (oil/global growth concerns); no dysfunction.
- Direction: Below neutral — yields falling significantly.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond spreads widened over the period"; "Spreads on speculative-grade bonds for energy-related firms rose substantially"; but "Business financing flows were robust"; bond issuance "the strongest in more than a year"; C&I loans "continued to expand briskly"; leveraged loans and CLOs "strong"
- Direction: Near neutral — widening from energy stress but still relatively favorable overall.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes were about unchanged"; VIX "rose sharply late in the period to levels close to those in mid-October"; mixed results globally (Japan/China up, oil exporters down)
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — flat with elevated volatility.

**BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Financing broadly available; lending conditions accommodative; consumer credit expanding; CRE financing available; no specific bank stress indicators mentioned.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "dollar appreciated substantially against most currencies"; "moved up significantly against the yen" (BOJ expansion); "against the currencies of oil exporters"; "monetary policy in Europe was likely to be put on a more accommodative path"
- Direction: Well above neutral — strong broad appreciation driven by policy divergence.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "consumer energy prices declined"; oil prices in sharp decline; food prices "rose more than overall prices"; core goods subdued; CPI total only 1.25% y/y.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Core CPI ~1.75%; core PCE ~1.5%; services component holding up better than goods.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Survey measures "remained stable" (Michigan, Blue Chip, SPF, primary dealers); but "market-based measures of inflation compensation moved lower" reflecting oil prices and global growth concerns.
