# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2020-09-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. September 2020 meeting (first under new consensus statement with AIT framework).

**Era context**: September 2020. Rates at 0-0.25%. COVID pandemic recovery underway. Initial sharp rebound moderating. UE 8.4% (down from 14.7% peak). Core PCE depressed. Massive fiscal support. Vaccine not yet available. New consensus statement adopted. Staff implementing new monetary policy rule.

**Staff speakers**:
- MS. TEVLIN (implied from domestic briefing): Recovery "tenuous, with steeply rising COVID cases" earlier but "new cases have fallen." Outlook "upgraded substantially compared with our July" Tealbook. But "better-than-expected data" partly reflect "pull-forward" of activity; accordingly "trimmed growth" for later quarters. "Path of activity remains well below our" pre-pandemic trend -- "a lot of ground to make up." Recovery pattern: "rapid" initial rebound, then "much more moderate gains since then." Retail sales above pre-pandemic level (hollow dot). Manufacturing "rose again in August" but "by less than in the preceding months." Private payrolls: between mid-July and mid-August, "both measures increased by 1 million jobs. Since then, the ADP-FRB data point to continued gains." But "pace of job gains has slowed considerably as the stock of temporarily laid-off workers has declined sharply." UE "continued to improve rapidly" and improvement "widely shared across various racial and ethnic groups" though gaps remain -- "gap has widened for the black unemployment rate." Core PCE: "dropped sharply this year" -- prices of social-distancing items (hotels, retail clothing, airfares) haven't recovered. But goods prices "rebounded vigorously and by more than we expected" -- "used cars, furniture, and appliances." Staff raised "projection of core inflation at the end of this year from 1.1 percent to 1.5 percent." Staff expects "core inflation briefly breaching 2 percent in the middle of next year before settling back down below 2 percent through 2023."
- MS. IONESCU: SEP -- "all of you upgraded your growth" forecasts for 2020. Median UE 7.6% end of year (down notably). "Vast majority of you expect inflation to still fall short of 2 percent by the end of the" forecast horizon. Risks "weighted to the downside" for GDP, "upside" for unemployment.
- MS. WILSON: International -- Six-month stocktaking of March outlook. COVID and containment measures "still dominates our outlook." Financial conditions remarkably recovered -- "policy tidal wave has proved highly effective." "Financial market conditions and commodity prices have declined far less and recovered far faster this crisis than during the GFC." Removed "Deep Recession" from Tealbook uncertainty section. Second waves in European countries. China bounced back strongly vs Mexico lagging. EME vulnerability dashboard showing moderate stress levels.

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE dropped sharply. Staff raised end-2020 forecast from 1.1% to 1.5%. Expected to "briefly breach 2 percent" mid-2021 before settling below 2% through 2023. Goods prices rebounding strongly but services depressed. Score: 28 -- well below target, pandemic-driven deflation in services.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Path of activity remains well below" pre-pandemic trend. "A lot of ground to make up." Recovery rapid but slowing. Still massive shortfall. UE 8.4%. Score: 25 -- far below potential despite recovery.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE around 8.4% (SEP median 7.6% by year-end). Pace of job gains slowing. "Stock of temporarily laid-off workers has declined sharply." But still massive employment shortfall. Score: 22 -- very slack, recovering.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Retail sales above pre-pandemic level. Goods consumption strong (autos, housing). But services depressed. "Pull-forward" of activity. Manufacturing recovering but slower. Score: 38 -- below trend on net but goods sector strong.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Wilson: Financial conditions "declined far less and recovered far faster this crisis than during the GFC." Policy "tidal wave" effective. Removed "Deep Recession" scenario. Remarkable recovery. Score: 38 -- somewhat accommodative, much improved from crisis.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed as dysfunction. Score: N/A.
8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Wilson: EME sovereign spreads "highly correlated with behavior of U.S. high-yield corporate spreads." But spreads have recovered significantly. Score: N/A (insufficient specific U.S. credit spread discussion).
9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Not assessed by staff. Score: N/A.
10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.
11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Wilson: "sharp appreciation and subsequent retracement of the dollar against EME currencies" following "fall and then rise of risk appetite." Score: N/A (insufficient direct dollar assessment).

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 25 below potential -- consistent with massive COVID shortfall. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 38 accommodative -- consistent with policy tidal wave restoring conditions.

**Cross-dimension coherence**: OUTPUT_GAP 25 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 22 -- aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 25 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 38 -- slight divergence because goods spending rebounded strongly while overall output still depressed.
