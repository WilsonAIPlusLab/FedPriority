# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2017-12-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. December 2017 meeting (rate hike to 1.25-1.50%).

**Era context**: December 2017. Fifth hike in cycle. Tax reform imminent (TCJA). UE 4.1%. Core PCE ~1.5%. GDP growth boosted by tax reform estimates. Global growth "widespread boom." Dollar below December 2016 forecast. Balance sheet normalization underway. Net exports contributing positively.

**Staff speakers**:
- MR. STEVENS: Labor market -- UE 4.1% in November. Payrolls +170K (3-month avg, smoothing hurricane effects). "Jobs were consistent with continued further improvement in labor utilization." ADP-based indicator suggests underlying pace "a little stronger than indicated by the BLS measure." Reserve Bank surveys: ~50% of firms plan to increase employment, ~60% will raise starting pay, ~50% restrained by difficulties finding workers.
- U.S. outlook staff: Tax legislation estimated to reduce revenues $220B/year (1% of GDP) through 2020 -- "twice as large as we had assumed." GDP boosted ~1% by 2020. UE projected to reach 3.3% by 2020, "nearly 1.5 percentage points below the downwardly revised natural rate" of 4.7%. Core PCE 1.5% this year, rising to 1.8% in 2018, 2% by 2019-2020. Inflation "lower than we anticipated last year" but "judge much of the weakness to be related to transitory factors." Underlying trend "constant at a little below 2 percent."
- MR. AHMED: International -- "widespread boom." Trade recovery. AFE output "at or above potential" in Canada, Japan, UK. Less than 1% gap in euro area. Inflation still "stubbornly below" targets. Dollar "fallen this year and is significantly below the path we projected last December." Net exports contributing positively for first time. China risks still prominent -- corporate debt-at-risk exceeds Asian crisis levels.
- MR. TETLOW: SEP -- median GDP 2.5%. UE 3.9%. Core PCE 1.9% in 2018. Fiscal policy boosted projections.

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE 1.5% this year. Expected 1.8% in 2018, 2% in 2019. "Lower than we anticipated" but transitory factors. Trend "constant at a little below 2 percent." ECI rising 2.75%. Score: 32 -- below target but firming.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: UE 4.1%. Natural rate 4.7%. Tax reform boosts gap: "output gap is somewhat higher than a year ago." UE projected to 3.3% by 2020. "Real GDP expected to be 2 percent above potential." Score: 56 -- above potential, widening.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 4.1%. ~50% of firms restrained by difficulty finding workers ("a little higher than in the past couple of years"). Payrolls ~170K. Plans to raise pay. ECI rising 2.75%. Score: 58 -- tight, getting tighter.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Tax reform to boost PCE and BFI. "PCE likely will be boosted by higher disposable personal income." "BFI likely will be boosted by a lower user cost of capital." GDP growth strong. Score: 54 -- above trend, fiscal boost.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Tax reform expected to raise capital stock and "boost labor productivity." Supply-side effects built in. Score: N/A (not enough direct pre-tax-reform productivity data).

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Not directly assessed in this transcript beyond context. Tax reform pushing equities higher. Dollar weaker. Score: N/A.

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Ahmed: dollar "fallen this year and is significantly below the path we projected last December." Net exports positive for first time. "Solidifying foreign growth prospects have boosted foreign currencies." Staff still expects "moderate appreciation" ahead. Score: 48 -- near neutral, having depreciated substantially.
