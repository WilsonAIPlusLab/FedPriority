### 1. Document Type Identification

Note: The transcript text has OCR artifacts (split columns across pages) but the key content is recoverable.

### 2. Era Context

March 2012 is early in the post-GFC recovery. The economy has been growing since mid-2009 but at a subpar pace. Unemployment at 8.3% is elevated but declining from 10% peak. Inflation was near or modestly above 2% near-term due to energy, but core trending lower. The Fed was at the zero lower bound, implementing the Maturity Extension Program ("Operation Twist"), and forward guidance had just been extended to "late 2014." European sovereign debt crisis had eased (ECB LTROs) but remained a major risk.

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as roughly at or slightly above 2% target near-term, driven by energy/oil (+14% for Brent crude since January), with PCE projected to go "slightly above 2 percent over the first half of this year." However, over the medium term, staff projects core PCE drifting down to 1.5% in H2 2012 and remaining at that pace in 2013, with "inflation expectations remaining well anchored" and "resource utilization remaining well below normal." Near-term is at/slightly above target; medium-term is below target. The rubric says score what staff reports NOW, including the current elevated headline. The dominant signal is near-term slightly above 2% with medium-term drift back below. This puts us in the 41-52 range — slightly above target near-term but staff confidence the medium-term path is well below 2%.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output gap as clearly below potential (slack remains). Wascher says "we continue to anticipate a recovery that is subpar by historical standards" with "significant headwinds" and growth of ~2.5%/2.75% in 2012/2013 — "not much above the assumed rate of growth in potential output." The staff revised the gap to be "½ percentage point narrower than in the January projection" due to productivity reassessment, but still says unemployment is declining only gradually to ~7.75% by end-2013. The resource utilization is "well below normal." This is firmly below-potential territory. Bright-line: below potential → score MUST be < 50. Era anchor: 2012 recovery = 35-42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.3%, projected to decline to ~7.75% by end-2013. This is clearly in the high-unemployment territory. Payrolls growing at a strong pace (~250k/month for 3-month average) but from a high unemployment level. The rubric anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. Unemployment at 8.3% clearly maps to the 16-30 or 31-45 range by the rubric (8.3% > 7% = range 31-45 boundary; the rubric says "UE > 7%" = 16-30 range). The unemployment is 8.3%, which is above 7%, placing it in the 16-30 range, but there is notable improvement momentum. Score around 28-33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending on PCE looks weak: "real PCE looked to be coming in well below our expectations," PCE growth marked down to "annual rate of just 1¼ percent" for Q1. Business fixed investment "more upbeat than we anticipated." Auto sales up. Housing "faint signs of life." Net picture is below-trend consumer spending with somewhat better investment, netting to below trend overall. Era context: 2011-13 "moderate" = 35-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Wascher discusses productivity dynamics at length — arguing that the decline in unemployment was partly explained by firms reversing earlier "excessive labor shedding" and bringing productivity back to sustainable levels, implying a structural productivity reassessment (potential output was lower than thought). No direct assessment of current productivity as above/below trend. This is more of a structural discussion. Score as moderate/at-trend with low confidence, or N/A. Given the indirect nature of the discussion, N/A is appropriate.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: MR. SACK paints a clearly accommodative picture: equities up 4%+ since January meeting, S&P 500 advancing, risky assets performing strongly, yield spreads on corporate bonds and some structured credit products narrowing, 10-year Treasury yield "little changed" (held steady while stocks rose 15%), low term premium (minus 50 bps, 125 bps below average), MBS option-adjusted spread under downward pressure, LTRO improved European bank funding conditions, dollar-denominated LIBOR-OIS spread "well off its peak." Forward guidance keeping rates accommodative. Bright-line: "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: MR. SACK discusses Treasury yields extensively but there is NO mention of market dysfunction. The 10-year yield was "little changed" over the intermeeting period. Some upward pressure on short-term Treasury bill rates (3-month bill rate up ~10 bps since year-start) attributed to increased Treasury bill issuance and dealers accumulating inventory, but Sack describes this as normal price adjustment from supply, not market dysfunction. The term premium model shows low/negative term premium. No liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues mentioned. Bright-line: yield movements alone ≠ dysfunction → score 36-50. Score ~35 (well-functioning, low volatility).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: MR. SACK says "yield spreads on corporate bonds and some structured credit products narrowing," dollar-denominated LIBOR-OIS spread "well off its peak," FX swap market cost of dollar funding "down sharply from last year's levels." European peripheral sovereign spreads "continued to decline," returning "to levels observed last summer." CDS spreads for major U.S. financial firms "improved since late last year" but "remain well above the levels observed in the middle of 2011." The overall picture is clearly improving/narrowing credit conditions. Bright-line: compressed/narrowing → score MUST be < 50. The U.S. financial firm CDS remaining elevated provides some offset. Score ~30-35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: MR. SACK notes equities up 4%+ (S&P 500 advancing), risky assets performing strongly. No valuation commentary (not described as "elevated," "stretched," or "cheap"). The staff revised up the stock market forecast. Given rising equities with no concern about overvaluation or undervaluation, the default for no valuation commentary on rising prices is ~45-50. Staff actually raised their forecast for equities, suggesting comfort with the level.

**BANKING_STRESS**: MR. Score ~45-50 (some concern, mixed signals).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: MR. The dollar is modestly weakening overall (risk-on flows reducing safe-haven demand). Exception: Japanese yen depreciated ~6% against the dollar (so dollar strengthened vs yen, specifically). MR. KAMIN notes "the lower dollar" helps U.S. exports and references "a roughly 1 percent" depreciation of the dollar as assumed. Wascher references "a lower foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a positive for the medium-term forecast. Bright-line: dollar depreciation/weakening → score MUST be < 50. Score ~35-40.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy/gasoline prices are the key driver of near-term inflation concerns. Oil prices +14% since January. "Run-up in gasoline prices" contributing to headline PCE above 2%. Commodity prices also up but less than oil. Score ~55-60 (upward pressure from energy goods).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core PCE is coming in "a little above our expectations" in recent data, but medium-term projection has core PCE (which is predominantly services) drifting down to 1.5%. Staff sees services inflation as slightly elevated near-term but returning below target. Score ~48-52.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not specifically discussed as a separate sub-component. Housing market mentioned only as showing "faint signs of life" in terms of activity. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff explicitly says "inflation expectations remaining well anchored" and the "five-year, five-year-forward measure" shows longer-term inflation expectations "remain well contained" despite near-term breakeven moves. Score ~48 (anchored at target, no drift).
