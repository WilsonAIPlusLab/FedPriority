# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-11-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: November 2004. Soft patch ending — "the economy has, for the most part, appeared to us to be growing at or modestly above trend." Big employment surprise: private payrolls +296,000 in October after months of disappointment. September revised up too. But staff still cautious — "175,000 per month over the past three months is probably a better estimate." Manufacturing IP weaker than expected — "3¾ percent at an annual rate in the fourth quarter, nearly 2 percentage points less than" September projection. Inventory overhang. Oil prices still elevated but came down recently. Core PCE "relatively little" changed — about 1.5%. Compensation measures surprised to downside. Dollar fallen sharply. Fiscal policy shifting to neutral. Staff sees economy "fundamentally less fragile" than earlier in the recovery.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "Private payrolls increased 296,000 in October" but "employment in September had been revised up" partly from hurricane-related construction. Staff estimate underlying pace "pretty close to our forecast back in the September Greenbook." Manufacturing IP "softer." Inventory investment higher than expected — "drop-off in inventory investment is expected to subtract about ¾ percentage point from the growth of real GDP in the current quarter." Oil: "$7 per barrel higher on average over the projection period." But prices "come down noticeably over the past week." Inflation: "changed relatively little." Core CPI for Aug-Sep "on net, a bit higher." But "hourly labor compensation have surprised us to the downside" — ECI at "3½ percent at an annual rate... ½ percentage point less than in our previous projection." "No discernable trend in compensation inflation." Core PCE ~1.5% going sideways. Dollar "fallen sharply against many of the other industrial country currencies."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core PCE "about sideways at its current annual pace of about 1½ percent." Core CPI "a bit higher than we had been projecting" but "much of our miss was in prices for used cars and lodging." Compensation flat at 3.5%. Energy prices adding to headline but expected to stabilize. No breakout.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — elevated from 2003 lows but stable and not threatening.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 45 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: economy "growing at or modestly above trend." GDP projected ~3.75% Q4. Stronger employment "allayed our concerns that weakness in the labor market might sap the vigor of the expansion." Gap still present but closing. Staff raised funds rate path.
- **Direction**: Just below neutral — gap narrowing.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: private payrolls +296,000 October. "Hiring has been running around 160,000 per month" over recent period. "We expect it to pick up to around 225,000 per month by the spring." Initial claims edged up but not yet alarming. Improvement solidifying.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improving substantially but not yet tight.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Consumer spending also surprised us to the upside." Real PCE "advanced at an annual rate of more than 5 percent in the third quarter." But auto sales weakening. Inventory drag of 0.75pp ahead. Manufacturing IP softer. "E&S up about 17 percent at an annual rate in the third quarter" and "11 percent in the fourth quarter." Business investment strong. High-tech investment weaker — "below our expectations."
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still above trend despite some softening.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 62 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "slowing of structural labor productivity during the next two years." High-tech investment weaker, suggesting possible deceleration in tech-driven productivity gains. But "We believe that it is too soon to draw that conclusion." Still above historical norms. Yellen notes structural productivity could average "around 3¼ percent" — well above consensus.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still strong but uncertainty about trajectory.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: funds rate at 1.75% (three hikes done). "Monetary policy, at least by our reckoning, will remain reasonably accommodative over the projection period." But fiscal policy "projected to shift from providing considerable stimulus to being a roughly neutral factor." Oil prices restraining. Dollar decline provides some offset. Stock market up 4%. House prices up 5%.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still accommodative but diminishing. Bright-line: still accommodative.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not extensively discussed. No indication of deterioration. Business financial conditions remain healthy.
- **Direction**: Below neutral.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Stock market is about 4 percent higher than in our previous projection." Upside risk factor. Not described as overvalued.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — supportive.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 18 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton/Johnson: "dollar has fallen sharply against many of the other industrial country currencies." Staff "lowered the projected path for the real value... about 2 percent." Dollar depreciation ongoing and accelerating. Current account deficit projected to widen to "nearly $890 billion."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant and accelerating weakness. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: inflation "about sideways." Compensation stable. No evidence of expectations breakout. Energy price effects expected to fade. Expectations appear anchored.
- **Direction**: Near neutral — stable.
