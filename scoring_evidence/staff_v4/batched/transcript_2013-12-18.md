# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-12-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript — Reeve (domestic outlook), Kamin (international), Liang (financial stability), DeBoer/Weinbach (efficacy/costs of purchases), Potter (operations). Segmented. 1126 lines.

**Era context**: December 2013. Taper announced at this meeting ($10B reduction). UE ~7.0%. GDP accelerating to ~2.8% in second half. Consumer spending mixed — "surprisingly weak over the summer." Housing flattened after mortgage rate jump. Core PCE at 1.3% — well below target. Europe exited recession. Japan growing but consumption tax hike ahead. China growing ~7.5-8%. EME stress moderating. Financial stability: "signs of froth emerging in some areas" — leveraged lending, reach for yield.

**Reeve key points**: "Retail sales rose more than we were expecting." "Motor vehicle sales also strong." "Real GDP rising at annual rate of 2.8 percent in second half." But "PDFP stronger than in October but down 1 percentage point since June projection." "Consumer spending surprisingly weak during the summer." Housing "permits flattened out after mortgage rates jumped in spring." "Number of underwater mortgages moved down sharply." Core PCE "running at a 1.3 percent pace in second half" — "low end of range." "All of these measures have tended to trend lower since early last year." "Core import prices declining." Medium-term outlook "just a shade weaker" than June from stronger dollar and higher rates.

**Kamin key points**: Total foreign growth "2¼ percent pace" rising to 3% by 2016. AFEs "gaining traction." Euro area "exited its lengthy recession" but "euro-area inflation has fallen uncomfortably close to the edge of deflation." UK "solid 2½ percent pace." Japan growing but consumption tax hike in 2014 "sharp drop in fiscal impulse." China: "very high investment share of GDP" and "rapid rise in credit" — risks. EMEs becoming "increasingly important" U.S. trade partner. European banking union progress: "single rulebook" approved, "single resolution mechanism" under discussion. Bank credit in Europe "continues to contract."

**Liang (financial stability)**: Leveraged lending — "debt multiples greater than 6 have been increasing." "Signs of froth" but "pockets." "Not prevalent." CLO issuance, covenant-lite issuance elevated. Cost/efficacy survey: most see "moderate" costs. "Marginal benefit currently relatively low." About half see benefit "more or less equal to" costs.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Core PCE "running at 1.3 percent pace" — "low end of range." "All measures have tended to trend lower since early last year." "Core import prices declining." "Expected to edge up to pace of 1¾ percent in 2016." Below target, persistent undershoot.
- Direction: Below neutral — significant, persistent undershoot.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 39** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: GDP accelerating to 2.8% in second half. UE ~7.0%, declining. "GDP growth will accelerate over the next year." But "consumer spending surprisingly weak." Housing flattened. PDFP "down 1 percentage point since June." Still below potential but closing.
- Direction: Below neutral but closing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 34** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: UE ~7.0%. Approaching 6.5% threshold. Employment improving. But labor force participation still declining. "Unemployment gap narrower." Recovery broadening. Natural rate ~5.25%.
- Direction: Below neutral — still slack but substantially improved from 2012.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Retail sales rose more than expecting." "Motor vehicle sales strong." But "consumer spending surprisingly weak over summer." "PCE growth trimmed by ¾ percentage point" since June. Housing "permits flattened." Business investment indicators "recently improved" — "analysts' expectations picked up, ISM improved." Mixed overall.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 48** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Slightly higher interest rates played only a small role" — much of backup pre-June. "Exchange value of dollar higher than anticipated." Equity prices higher. House prices substantially higher. QE ongoing but taper announced. "Overall financial conditions more accommodative" than in taper scenarios but tighter than pre-May 2013. Broadly near neutral.
- Direction: Near neutral — roughly balanced.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 50** ±7 [Low confidence]
- Evidence: Rates stabilized after summer volatility. "Increase in rates happened prior to June Tealbook" and interpreted as "pull-forward of increases." No dysfunction. Market well-functioning. Taper announcement orderly.
- Direction: Neutral — rates elevated but stable, no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 44** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: Corporate credit still accessible. Leveraged loan issuance robust. "Covenant-lite" increasing. "Debt multiples greater than 6 increasing." Spreads still tight. Liang: "signs of froth in some areas" but not systemic. Reach for yield continuing.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral — spreads compressed, ample credit availability.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 44** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Equity prices higher." "Not prevalent" overvaluation. Financial stability assessment sees "pockets" but not broad froth. Equity gains supported by earnings and improving outlook.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral — fairly valued.

**BANKING_STRESS: 42** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: U.S. banks well-capitalized. European banking: "single rulebook approved." But "bank credit in Europe continues to contract." "Banks continue to tighten lending standards" in some European countries. U.S. banking sector healthy. Leveraged loan supervision being strengthened.
- Direction: Below neutral — U.S. banks in good shape, European credit contraction lingering concern.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 54** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- Evidence: "Exchange value of the dollar higher than anticipated at midyear." "Led us to mark down projection for net exports." Dollar stronger from taper expectations and improved U.S. outlook relative to trading partners. Yen weak from Abenomics.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — dollar appreciating.
