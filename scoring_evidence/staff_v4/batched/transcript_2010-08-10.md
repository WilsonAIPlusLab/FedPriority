# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — August 10, 2010

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from the August 10, 2010 FOMC meeting. Staff speakers are clearly identified by name. The briefing is organized into two sections:
- **Financial briefing**: MR. SACK (Brian Sack, Manager of the System Open Market Account / NY Fed Markets Desk) — covers financial market conditions, policy expectations, asset prices, dollar movements, MBS portfolio, and financial stability
- **Economic briefing**: MR. SLIFMAN (Larry Slifman, Board Staff) — covers domestic economic conditions, GDP, labor market, inflation, and forecast risks; and MR. SHEETS (Nathan Sheets, Board Staff) — covers international/trade developments

This is a post-2005 segmented transcript with clear staff speaker identification. The go-around responses (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Lockhart) are participant views and are excluded from scoring.

### 2. Era Context

August 2010 is mid-recovery from the GFC. The economy has been expanding since mid-2009, but the recovery is weak and uneven. Key macro backdrop:
- Unemployment rate: 9.5% (July 2010)
- Real GDP growth: estimated ~2% in Q2 2010, below the Tealbook forecast
- Core PCE inflation: below 1%, well below the 2% target
- Fed funds rate: at effective lower bound (0–0.25%)
- Discussion ongoing about possible additional policy accommodation (QE2)

Era anchors imply: OUTPUT_GAP well below 50 (deep slack), LABOR_TIGHTNESS well below 50 (UE near 10%), INFLATION_PRESSURE in 20-30 range (well below target with deflation risk discussion), FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS modestly accommodative (20s-30s range).

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Economic Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff (Slifman) states "core PCE inflation is projected to hold steady at a bit below 1 percent over the forecast period, and total PCE inflation is projected to average slightly more than 1 percent." Staff characterizes risks as "elevated but roughly balanced" on the downside, explicitly noting that inflation expectations could "prove less stable than we expect and fall significantly." The Blue Chip comparison shows the staff already has one of the lowest inflation outlooks. Staff sees inflation as BELOW TARGET and declining, with deflation risk discussed (though deemed a "fairly remote possibility"). Direction: clearly BELOW midpoint. Given core PCE "a bit below 1 percent," this is well below target — score in the 20-28 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Slifman explicitly says "resource slack still will be substantial" and the unemployment rate is expected to "move down slowly to 8.9 percent by the end of next year." Real GDP estimated at ~2% in Q2, revised down from projections. Baseline GDP projected at ~2.5% Q3 and ~3.6% over 4 quarters of 2011, but "output increasing faster than potential" only moves unemployment "slowly." The "weaker recovery" scenario shows GDP at 1.5% pace. Staff's language: "margin of slack very wide." Direction: clearly BELOW potential. Score in the 30-37 range (substantial slack but recovery underway, consistent with 2010 anchor of 35-42).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment rate held at 9.5% in July; private employment rose only 70,000 (below expectations); businesses "remain cautious about taking on the costs of adding new workers." Staff notes extended unemployment benefits are "boosting the unemployment rate by approximately 1 percentage point." Even with that adjustment, the effective rate is ~8.5%, still far above full employment. Slifman: employers trying to "meet their output goals by using their current workforces more intensively" — i.e., no new hiring. Direction: WELL BELOW full employment. Score in the 20-25 range (UE ~9.5% anchors this firmly below 30; slightly above the 2009 trough).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Slifman highlights "disappointing labor market report," "sluggish retail sales," "more downbeat consumer confidence," "weaker than anticipated housing activity." Real GDP rose ~2% in Q2 — "somewhat less than the BEA's currently published figure." Consumer spending has "shortfall" per revised NIPA data; higher saving rate; households deferring spending. Business investment in the "virtuous circle" scenario is possible upside but baseline is modest. Imports surged 30% in Q2 — a sign of domestic demand, but attributed to capital goods, not consumer. Net: spending is below trend but recovering slowly. Direction: BELOW midpoint, but not in collapse. Score in the 35-40 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not directly discussed by staff in this briefing. The NIPA revision affected GDP levels and potential output estimates. Slifman briefly mentions firms "meeting output goals by using current workforces more intensively" — which could imply productivity gains — but this is not framed as a productivity assessment. Setting to N/A.

### 4. Directional Signals — Financial Dimensions

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Sack describes financial conditions as having improved somewhat. He notes "corporate bond yield spreads narrowed slightly" and equity prices "slightly higher." The key passage: "Financial conditions appear slightly more supportive of economic growth than in June" (confirmed in Q&A when President Fisher asks about this and Sheets affirms it). However, Sack notes the improvement is partly a reflection of lower expected policy rates (bad economic signal) and partly improved risk sentiment. Standards on business loans from banks "still remain relatively tight." Sack also describes the SOMA portfolio context and MBS market. Overall: conditions are accommodative relative to neutral due to low rates, but with some lingering tightness from bank lending standards. Direction: BELOW midpoint (accommodative/supportive). Score in the 30-38 range. The language "slightly more supportive" confirms improvement from already-accommodative levels.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Sack discusses Treasury yields falling 10-50 basis points across the curve, 2-year yield at lowest on record ~50 basis points. This is driven by revised policy expectations (expectations of lower rates for longer) and decline in term premium. There is NO discussion of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction demand shortfalls. Treasury market functioning is normal — the yield movements are policy/macro driven, not dysfunction. Bright-line check: yield movements alone are NOT stress. Score should be in the 25-35 range (calm markets, this era anchor).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Sack notes corporate bond yield spreads "narrowed slightly" on balance. CDS spreads for European financial institutions "narrowed notably." LIBOR-OIS spreads declined. High-yield spreads have "returned to the norm" per Fisher's Q&A. Issuance "robust" especially in corporate bonds for the better part of the past year. However, bank lending standards "still remain relatively tight." Overall: spreads are near normal or slightly below normal. Direction: BELOW midpoint (spreads tighter/more compressed than typical). Score in the 35-40 range.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Sack notes U.S. equity prices "recovered in recent weeks to leave them slightly higher than they were at the time of the last meeting." Supported by "second-quarter earnings reports that were generally stronger than expected." Sack notes stock market supported by lower interest rates and by decline in risk premiums. No discussion of bubble/overvaluation or of distressed/depressed levels — prices are described as broadly in line with fundamentals. Staff memo concludes "valuation measures in most markets appear to be broadly in line with fundamental factors or historical norms." Direction: near neutral, slightly above depressed. Score ~48-52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Sack describes U.S. financial sector as not suffering from "types of aggregate imbalances that were observed in the period leading up to the financial crisis." But he notes ongoing challenges: "bank earnings better than expected but much of that performance reflected declines in loan loss provisioning"; banks face "questions about the sustainability of earnings going forward"; "remaining hurdles for the banking sector." European banks went through stress tests with mixed results. U.S. bank lending standards still "relatively tight." Banking system is not in crisis but faces moderate ongoing headwinds. Direction: ABOVE neutral — some stress remains but not crisis. Score in the 50-58 range.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Sack explicitly states "the dollar has depreciated sharply against the euro in recent weeks as sentiment about the European situation improved." He also notes "the dollar has also weakened against a broader set of currencies, suggesting that the recent depreciation has been driven not only by developments in Europe but also by the heightened concerns about U.S. growth prospects and the associated decline in U.S. yields." Sheets confirms "dollar, which has deprecated about 3 percent in broad real terms since the last FOMC meeting." Direction: DOLLAR DEPRECIATING (weakening). Bright-line: depreciation → score < 50. Score in the 20-28 range (notable weakness, 3% broad real terms decline with dual drivers).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not directly broken out by Slifman. N/A.

Market-based measures discussed (breakeven inflation rates, deflation probabilities). Overall: expectations broadly anchored but showing some downward drift risk, slightly below target anchor. Score in the 35-42 range.

### 7. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (33) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (22)**: Both below 50, consistent. Small divergence: output gap ~33 vs labor ~22. Explanation: unemployment rate is very high (9.5%) which anchors labor at lower end, while the output gap measure is calibrated slightly higher due to the potential output adjustment in NIPA revision. This is consistent with the Slifman discussion of calibrating the tension between GDP gap and unemployment gap.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (33) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (37)**: Both below 50 and similar levels. Consistent — soft spending is driving the output shortfall. CHECK.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (33) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Both below 50, both in accommodative/tight-spread territory. Broadly consistent — both point to accommodative/favorable financial conditions. Minor divergence: bank lending standards somewhat tight (slight upward pull on FC). No contradiction. CHECK.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (33) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (28)**: Both low, consistent with easy/calm conditions. Treasury yields are low (policy expectations driven), not reflecting stress. CHECK.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) ↔ INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (38)**: Inflation well below target (22) with expectations showing some downward drift but broadly anchored (38). The gap is plausible — expectations hold up better than realized inflation, which is typical. The 16-point gap is explainable. CHECK.

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral. UE at 9.5% firmly anchors this in the 20-25 range per rubric guidance (UE > 7% = 16-30 range, and this is near the top end of that difficulty band given it has been slowly declining from the 10%+ peak).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 28 ±5 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yields fell sharply but for fundamental macro/policy reasons; the market itself is functioning normally with no stress signals — consistent with the calm-market anchor for this era (25-35).

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because yields moved for economic reasons, not dysfunction. Bright-line rule: yield movements alone are not stress; score 28 is appropriate and satisfies the < 50 threshold for no dysfunction.
