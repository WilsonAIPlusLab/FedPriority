**Era context**: October 2008 — post-Lehman, post-AIG. TARP enacted. Treasury capital purchase program and FDIC temporary guarantee announced October 14. Coordinated global 50bp rate cut earlier in month. Funds rate at 1.5%. Oil collapsed. Global financial crisis in full force. Staff projects funds rate to 0.5% by early 2009.

**Key evidence**:
- Sheets: Swap lines with Mexico, Brazil, Korea, Singapore — "$30 billion for each" — "dollar funding pressures in Brazil and Korea appear to have intensified significantly." Iceland rejected for swap line — liabilities of $170B, swap of $1-2B "would have been a speed bump." "Widespread fears about the condition of financial firms, accompanied by increased risk aversion, have prompted further sharp falls in asset prices in overseas markets." Dollar "strengthened nearly 11 percent against the major currencies." "Depreciation against the yen as carry trades unwind." EM "CDS premiums on sovereign debt of emerging market economies have skyrocketed."
- Foreign outlook: "Marked down our estimate of total foreign growth" by more than 1 percentage point. "Outright recessions in the advanced foreign economies." UK, euro area, Japan all contracting. "Chinese real GDP growth estimated to have slowed considerably." Oil prices "plummeted." Nonfuel commodity prices fallen as well.
- Europe: "Banks remain leery of lending to each other" — ECB deposit facility usage surging. BoE bank lending survey showed "further tightening of U.K. lending standards." "Business confidence has disintegrated." PMIs "in the downturn range."
- Stockton: "Payrolls fell steeply in September." Initial claims "quite elevated." Motor vehicle sales "dismal" — expected to stay depressed. Consumer spending "significantly softer than we were expecting" — "sharp drop in household wealth, tepid real income gains, a weakening labor market picture, historically low levels of sentiment, and reduced credit availability." "Revised down the projection for overall real PCE." Single-family starts "fell to about 550,000 units — 6 percent below our expectation." Business surveys "plunged to very low levels" in October.
- Key conditioning factors: Stock market "markedly below the path anticipated" — about 7% lower. "Yields and spreads on corporate bonds...soared over the intermeeting period." Dollar jumped — "more than 4 percent above the level assumed in our previous forecast." Oil prices plunged.
- GDP projection: "Real GDP will fall at an annual rate of nearly 1 percent in the first half of 2009 and then turn up modestly in the second half." 2010: GDP increases 2.3%. Funds rate "lowered to 0.5 percent by early next year."
- Financial turmoil: Staff index of financial market stress "continued to rise." Model-based estimates suggest "financial turmoil, outside the usual channels" dragging real activity significantly. Staff "more optimistic than the models" because accounting for "actions taken by the Federal Reserve and others in supporting the financial system."
- Inflation: Core PCE inflation "to move down to 1.5 percent in 2009 and to slow further to 1.25 percent in 2010." Total PCE at same rate as core. "Long-term inflation expectations...more than retraced the run-up observed earlier this year."
- Bassett: "Persistent strains in financial markets and a sharp drop in asset prices." CDS spreads "quite volatile" — declined after TARP/FDIC announcements October 14 but "ended the period almost 70 basis points lower." Insurance company CDS "increased substantially." "Liquidity and price discovery in the CDS market remain strained." Corporate debt "impaired" — proxy for bid-asked spread on high-yield "spiked to more than 4 percent." "No high-yield issuance this month, and bond issuance by financial firms has come to a near halt." BBB spreads "rose to more than 600 basis points." High-yield "reached nearly 1,000 basis points."
- Senior Loan Officer Survey: "Remarkably widespread continued tightening of terms and standards for both households and businesses." "70 percent of domestic respondents tightened" C&I loans. "Nearly 90 percent...tightened" CRE. 75% tightened credit card lines. "About 60 percent" tightened other consumer loans.
- Equity: "Broad equity prices dropped more than 25 percent over the intermeeting period." Implied volatility "increased to record levels." Investors withdrew "$10 billion from equity funds" in October.
- Household: Mortgage rates volatile around 6%. ABS spreads on credit cards and auto loans "risen more than 150 basis points since mid-September." Consumer credit "decelerated." "Significant further weakness in consumer credit in coming quarters."
- Fannie/Freddie: Agency MBS spreads widened. Some foreign central banks "actually cut their positions." "Removal of this bid was one factor" widening spreads.
