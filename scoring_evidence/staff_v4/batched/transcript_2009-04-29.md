### 2. Era Context

April 2009 is the **trough of the Great Financial Crisis recession**. The economy was in free fall in H2 2008 and Q1 2009. By April 2009:
- Unemployment was rising sharply (approaching ~9%) and heading toward a projected 9.2–9.6% by Q4 2009
- GDP was contracting at a ~6% annual rate in recent quarters
- Core inflation was decelerating toward ~1.25%
- The Federal Reserve was at the zero lower bound with a massive balance sheet expansion underway
- Financial conditions had improved modestly from the acute crisis of late 2008 but remained deeply stressed
- This is anchor territory: OUTPUT_GAP should be well below 50 (16–22 range per rubric), LABOR_TIGHTNESS 15–22, INFLATION_PRESSURE 15–20.

### 3. Directional Signal Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation WELL BELOW the midpoint. Stockton: "further deceleration in core inflation, to 1¼ percent for 2009 as a whole and ¾ percent in 2010." Core PCE has slowed from ~2.25% a year ago to just under 2% by March. "Sizable economic slack and reduced cost pressures… should put downward pressure on price inflation over the next two years." Downside risk flagged (models forecast deflation below zero). Magnitude: this is strongly below target — deflationary risk language present but not acute spiraling. Score: 18–22.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy FAR BELOW potential. Stockton: GDP contracting at ~6% rate in recent quarters, "output gap of 6 to 7 percent" explicitly mentioned. Contraction to continue through midyear, then stabilization H2, recovery starting mid-2010. "The recession is letting up some" but still deeply contracted. Bright-line rule: MUST be < 50. Era anchor: 15–22. Score: 15–18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as EXTREMELY LOOSE (low tightness score). Stockton: "largest single challenge… comes from the labor market. Initial claims… have moved up further… pointing to another sizable decline in payroll employment this month. Other labor market indicators are similarly bleak." Unemployment projected 9.2–9.6% Q4 2009. "Undaunted, we are forecasting some gradual lessening in the pace of job loss." Era anchor: 15–22. Score: 14–17.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees consumption and investment DEEPLY BELOW trend. Stockton: consumer spending "plummeted in the second half of last year, knocking more than 2½ percentage points off the change in real GDP. More recently, spending appears to have flattened out" — very tentative stabilization signal. Manufacturing IP "continue to drop appreciably." Equipment spending "will continue to decline rapidly this year." Nonresidential construction "remains on a steep downtrend." "Few green shoots among the indicators of capital spending." However, consumer spending showing tentative signs of stopping its freefall. Net: still deeply below trend but possibly beginning to stabilize. Era anchor: 15–20. Score: 18–22 — slightly above the absolute floor since there are hints of stabilization in consumer spending, but still deep contraction.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed in the staff economic briefing. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as SIGNIFICANTLY TIGHTER than normal, but improved from acute crisis lows. Mosser: "Financial conditions have improved modestly in the last month and a half… Despite these modest improvements, financial markets and institutions, by any normal measure, are very stressed." "The calm in markets is very uneasy." Credit spreads narrowed but still elevated. Banking system stressed (CAP results pending). However, liquidity facilities shrinking as conditions improve. Bright-line: MUST be > 50. Era anchor for 2008–09 crisis: 70–85. Given improvement from acute crisis but still "very stressed," score: 72–78.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff (Mosser) discusses Treasury market but specifically notes: "better liquidity and trading conditions" with the absolute difference between actual and modeled Treasury yields declining — reflecting "increased willingness by market participants—banks, dealers, hedge funds—to commit balance sheets and to arbitrage away the price differences." No language about auction failures or settlement failures. A new market standard for charging failures to deliver is mentioned but described as a technical liquidity-improvement measure. Treasury yields have drifted but this is described as orderly, driven by new issuance and macro news. Per bright-line rule, rising yields from supply/policy expectations is NOT dysfunction. Score: 40–45 (some yield volatility, Treasury purchase program in place, but orderly functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as ELEVATED (high score). Mosser: "High-yield spreads have fallen more than 500 basis points since early March" — BUT still at very wide levels. "Cash bond spreads of both investment-grade and high-yield corporates have fallen even more than CDS spreads." Bank CDS spreads still elevated — "despite rising stock prices and declining volatility, most U.S. bank CDS spreads have widened in the last month and a half." "The primary–secondary mortgage spread… remains quite wide by historical standards." TALF-eligible ABS spreads down 100–700 bps but spreads still wide. This is GFC territory — spreads have narrowed from extreme peaks but remain significantly elevated. Score: 75–82.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes equities UP since last meeting — Stockton: "equity prices in the this forecast are nearly 20 percent above the levels anticipated at the time of the March Greenbook." Mosser notes global equity prices "have moved up since the time of your last meeting, potentially suggesting that some severe outcomes may now be seen as less likely." The staff does not describe equities as overvalued or at fire-sale prices — they describe a recovery from depressed levels. Banks' Q1 earnings beat expectations. The stress concern is about sustainability/quality of earnings, not about acute equity market collapse. Score: 60–68 (equities still modestly depressed/recovering from stress, not at extreme panic levels, staff not expressing concern about overvaluation).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff sees banking system as HIGHLY STRESSED. Mosser: "financial markets and institutions, by any normal measure, are very stressed." CAP results pending — "the May 4 release of the CAP results is a major risk event for financial markets." CDS spreads of U.S. banks widened in the last month and a half. Low tangible common equity ratios. Uncertainty about ability to absorb future losses — "leveraged loans and commercial real estate — remains high." European banks in even worse shape. Bright-line rule: "stressed" language → score MUST be > 50. Era anchor: 70–85. Score: 78–84.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff notes dollar DEPRECIATION — below 50. Stockton: "since the March Greenbook the broad nominal dollar has fallen roughly 3½ percent, providing additional impetus to demand for domestic production." Sheets: "the broad nominal dollar has fallen roughly 3½ percent… despite this depreciation… the broad dollar is still up more than 2½ percent since the end of last year." Bright-line: depreciation → score MUST be < 50. The dollar is still elevated on a year-to-date basis but has recently weakened. Score: 35–42 (notable recent weakness, but not extreme; dollar still elevated vs. end-2008).

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (16) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (15)**: Both deeply below 50. Consistent — deep recession with massive job losses and GDP contraction. Difference of only 1 point — coherent.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (16) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (20)**: Both well below 50. Slight divergence of 4 points. Plausible: consumer spending may be stabilizing at the margin (Stockton's "flattened out"), justifying slightly higher score, while GDP still deeply negative. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (75) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (78)**: Both well above 50. Consistent — tight financial conditions with wide credit spreads. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (75) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: Treasury market is functioning more normally than overall financial conditions; FC is elevated due to banking stress and credit spreads, not Treasury dysfunction. This is a reasonable divergence — per rubric, Treasury market can score near neutral even in crises if it's not specifically dysfunctional. Coherent with explanation.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (20) ↔ Inflation sub-components**: Core goods prices: modest declines (retailer discounting), now recovering somewhat. Core services: rising. Housing/OER: not specifically discussed. Expectations: well-anchored, but downside risk discussed. Weighted average of sub-components aligns with overall score of ~20. Coherent.
