## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. May 2017 meeting.

**Era context**: May 2017. Two hikes so far. Q1 GDP weak (~0.7%). UE ~4.4%. Core PCE ~1.6%. Dollar modestly stronger. Global growth improving. Fiscal stimulus uncertain. Financial stability concerns rising -- valuations "notable" to approaching "elevated."

**Staff speakers**:
- MR. WILCOX: U.S. outlook -- Q1 GDP weak but "expansion is probably doing just fine." Expects Q2 rebound to ~2%. UE revised down 0.1 to 4.9% natural rate. Core PCE 1.6%, "notably lower than we expected." Wireless services price drop. ECI 0.25pp higher than expected. Race/ethnicity labor analysis.
- MS. WILSON: International -- foreign GDP growth ~2.5%, "delightfully mundane." Risks becoming "more two sided." Dollar slight appreciation projected. Low probability of global recession.
- MS. EDGE: Financial stability -- valuation pressures "increased with regard to asset valuation pressures, though not by enough to warrant upgrading our assessment to elevated." P/E at early 2000s levels. Corporate spreads narrowed. CRE cap rates declined. Business leverage elevated. Banking leverage low.

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE 1.6%. "Notably lower than we expected." Wireless services and prescription drugs dragging. Trimmed mean PCE down only 0.1pp. Staff judges "much of this year's downward surprise to be transitory." Score: 33 -- below target.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Q1 weak but staff sees it as "transitory." UE 4.4%, "a percentage point below our revised estimate of the natural rate" by end-2019. Economy "running a little hotter than its longer-run sustainable level." Score: 52 -- slightly above potential.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 4.4%. Payrolls ~180K smoothed. Natural rate revised down to 4.9%. ECI rose 2.5% (stronger than expected). UE rates for minorities near pre-recession levels. Score: 53 -- moderately tight.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Q1 consumer spending weak but expected transitory. "Indicators of business investment are looking bullish." Capital goods orders rising. Financial conditions accommodative. Score: 46 -- slightly below trend (Q1 drag).

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Wilcox mentions "weak underlying productivity growth" in context of compensation. Not main topic. Score: 30 -- below trend.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Edge: P/E at early 2000s levels. Corporate spreads narrowed from 2016 peaks. CRE tightening. VIX low. "Financial conditions remain accommodative." Dollar slightly appreciated. Score: 38 -- accommodative.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed as stress. Normal. Score: N/A.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Edge: "Near- and far-term speculative grade corporate bond spreads have continued to narrow." HY spreads at post-crisis lows. Leveraged loan spreads narrow too. "Nonprice underwriting terms remain quite accommodative." Score: 28 -- very narrow.

9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Edge: Forward P/E at "levels last seen in the early 2000s." Valuation pressures at 80th percentile of historical distribution. Small-cap P/E at 98th percentile. Score: 65 -- notably elevated.

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Edge: Bank capital at "multiyear highs." Financial leverage "broadly unchanged." Score: 25 -- low stress.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Wilson: "slight appreciation of the dollar between now and the end of the projection period." Past appreciation still creating drag. Wilcox: "slightly tighter stance of fiscal policy" weakened dollar revisions. Score: 58 -- moderately strong.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 52 above potential -- consistent with UE below NAIRU. CREDIT_SPREADS 28 narrow -- correct. BANKING_STRESS 25 healthy -- correct. DOLLAR_STRENGTH >50 -- correct.
