### 2. Era Context

June 1999 is the late-1990s expansion. The U.S. economy was in the longest peacetime expansion on record. Unemployment was very low (~4.2–4.3%). GDP growth had been strong. The Fed had eased three times in late 1998 (LTCM/Russia crisis) and was now moving toward tightening (the May 1998 meeting introduced a tightening bias; this June meeting would result in the first rate hike since 1997). Inflation was modest but trending up from oil price rebound. The global financial crisis (Russia/Asia/LTCM) was seen as largely passing.

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff (Prell) projects CPI to accelerate: "the rate of inflation will trend higher," "core consumer prices accelerating over the next year and a half," reflecting "labor cost pressures." Oil price rebound pushes total CPI to 2¼ percent this year. Core acceleration is driven by tight labor market wage pressures and passthrough from oil and import prices. PPI intermediate goods posting "first quarterly increase since 1997." The staff acknowledges inflationary forces are building but not yet a broad-based surge. Direction: **above midpoint** — inflation is trending upward but still modest/moderate, not yet at crisis levels. Score: ~58–62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff projects "a considerable moderation in the trend of real GDP growth" toward "a slightly sub-par growth" pace. Key language: "slightly sub-par" growth going forward, but unemployment projected to hold "in the low 4s through next year." The economy has been running hot and is beginning to slow toward (slightly below) potential. However, current quarter still shows solid growth (strong non-defense capital goods shipments, robust PDE). The overall picture is that the economy is operating near-to-slightly-above potential currently, transitioning to slightly sub-par. Direction: **near to modestly above potential currently** — with the forecast moving to slightly below. Given that unemployment is projected in the "low 4s" and labor market remains tight, I read the current characterization as slightly above potential, slowing. Score: ~55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Explicit language: "In this tight labor market, workers have achieved some leverage in the wage-setting process." "We don't foresee much relief from the labor market tightness." Unemployment projected "in the low 4s through next year." Conference Board survey shows high share saying "jobs easy to get." Anchor: unemployment ~4.2–4.3% in June 1999 → rubric says 4%–4.5% range maps to 55–65. Direction: **tight, clearly above NAIRU**. Score: ~65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff notes "final demand can scarcely be said to have turned weak." Strong non-defense capital goods shipments through May. "Hefty increase in PDE this quarter." Residential investment "probably will be up, but only modestly, this quarter." Overall picture is solid above-trend spending/investment, with some moderation expected. Direction: **above trend, robust**. Score: ~60.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff discusses productivity explicitly: "prospective structural gains in labor productivity." "Growth in output per hour will slow in the near term, not because the structural improvements will ebb, but because we expect that hiring won't be geared down immediately as production decelerates." Prell also mentions "a pretty optimistic assessment of prospective structural gains in labor productivity" and real pay increases "quite sizable" when measured against product prices. Staff sees continued structural productivity improvements, slightly slowing near term but structurally strong. Direction: **above historical trend** — the late 1990s New Economy productivity boom. Score: ~62.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Fisher (financial briefing) describes bond markets backing up on data releases (yields rising), credit spreads "backed up a little, but not very much." The Fed funds rate stable. There is anxiety in bond markets but conditions are not tight — the three 1998 easing moves are still in place (the hike hasn't happened yet at time of briefing). Euro forward rates up ~25 bps since last meeting. Overall financial conditions are somewhat accommodative to neutral, with some tightening pressures building from yield backup. Direction: **modestly loose to neutral** — moving toward less accommodative. Score: ~42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Fisher discusses bond market behavior extensively: yields backed up significantly (11 bps on NAPM data, various other moves), but this is described as normal market reactions to data releases and FOMC communications — "anxious reactions to several particularly significant data releases." No mention of liquidity dysfunction, settlement failures, or auction problems. Rising yields = normal price adjustment. Direction: **normal functioning with some volatility** — not stress. Score: ~42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Fisher: "credit spreads and volatilities did back up a little, but not very much — less than I would have expected given all the fits and starts in the bond market." Junk bond market: "looks better today than it did last fall, as reflected in the yield spread at the right; but with defaults already rising the spreads have remained appreciable." Latin American stripped spreads "have moved back down following the previous spikes." Overall: spreads are somewhat elevated (junk) but well below the fall 1998 crisis peak; core credit conditions roughly normal. Direction: **slightly above normal** — elevated in junk but generally okay. Score: ~52.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff (Prell) discusses the stock market as central to the forecast: "the stock market outlook is quite central to our forecast." Discussion of what would happen "if one were to think about how the banking system might be affected by a stock market plunge." Fisher notes "the implied volatility on the S&P futures contract was actually lower on the period" — markets show complacency. Staff appears to view equities as elevated/stretched (the stock market drag on external sector is "diminishing," implying prices remain high). No explicit "bubble" language but the stock market is treated as elevated and as a risk factor. Direction: **somewhat elevated valuation concern** — modestly stretched. Score: ~35.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Prell: "short of a severe market setback — we are not talking about financial dislocations of such a scope and intensity that we need to mark up the customary wealth and cost-of-capital effects substantially." Banking system risk is discussed as a tail scenario tied to stock market plunge, not a current stress. Junk bond exposure is a risk but "my sense... is that short of a severe market setback" banks are okay. Direction: **low stress, healthy system** — minor tail risks. Score: ~35.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Fisher: "the steady weakening of the euro against the dollar since its launch in January has been widely attributed to the surprising strength of the U.S. economy." MS. JOHNSON: "during 1999 the dollar has risen in real terms relative to an average of the major foreign currencies... that rise reflects the strengthening of the dollar relative to the euro." Johnson also notes forecast for "a gradual depreciation of the dollar in real terms against these currencies, as relative macroeconomic conditions become less supportive of the dollar." Currently: dollar has strengthened against euro; mixed against yen. Direction: **dollar has strengthened** (notably, especially vs. euro). Score: ~62.
