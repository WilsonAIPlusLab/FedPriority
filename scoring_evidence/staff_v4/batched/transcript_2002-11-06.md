# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-11-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: November 2002. Soft patch intensifying. "Notable feature of the incoming data was the uniformity with which they surprised us to the downside." Production, consumer spending, business outlays, and foreign activity all below expectations. GDP projected 1% Q4 and <2.5% through mid-2003. Manufacturing IP declining again. Employment "stalled out." But housing very strong. Stockton discusses recession risk ("How likely?"), deflation risk, and vigorous recovery scenario. Deflation probability: 25-30%. Business caution/pessimism dominating.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "marked down considerably our forecast through the middle of next year." GDP "grow at an annual rate of 1 percent in the current quarter and at a pace of less than 2½ percent through the second quarter of 2003." "Industrial production is likely to have dropped about ¾ percent in October." Business spending "below our earlier expectations." Consumer outlays "come in below expectations." Auto sales "down more than we had projected." Only housing strong. Deflation risk: "about a 25 to 30 percent probability of deflation" in FRB/US model. "We estimate that we would need to see the funds rate drop below zero" to adequately respond. Business caution: spending "running well below the fundamentals."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: 25-30% probability of deflation. Core inflation declining. Output gap widening. "Few signs that this period of weakness is spreading or cumulating into something more serious" but deflation risk material.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very subdued, deflation risk emerging.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP 1% Q4, <2.5% through mid-2003. "Economy does not really break out of the recent pattern of sluggish growth until late next year." Gap widening. Manufacturing declining.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant and widening. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Employment gains appear to have stalled out after a brief upturn this summer." "Recent readings on initial claims for unemployment insurance suggest there has been little improvement." Unemployment rising.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack, deteriorating.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: consumer spending and business outlays both below expectations. Auto sales "dropped off sharply." "Non-auto spending also has been disappointingly weak." E&S below expectations. Only housing a bright spot. "Broadly global" softness.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — broad-based weakness.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Structural productivity still elevated. But cyclical: weak hours and weak output now — productivity growth implications uncertain. Business caution constraining hiring.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — structural strong but cyclical uncertain.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "real federal funds rate below the estimated range of its equilibrium" — accommodative. But "steep declines in household net worth" still weighing. Business caution, stock market weak, corporate confidence low. Fiscal stimulus projected.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — accommodative rates but asset/confidence drag.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Dollar continuing to decline. Weakening further as US economic outlook deteriorates relative to trading partners.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Deflation risk 25-30%. Expectations at risk of drifting lower if economy weakens further.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — slightly declining.
