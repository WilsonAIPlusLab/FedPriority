# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-12-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook). Era: December 2002. "Reasonably tranquil period for the forecast." Soft patch continuing. Unemployment jumped to 6.0% — "surprise of timing rather than magnitude." Private payrolls dropped 48,000 in November. Motor vehicle industry a major drag — sales slumped, production cuts lopping 1.5pp from Q4 GDP. Manufacturing IP declining again. But housing still strong. Funds rate cut to 1.25%. Staff builds in more fiscal stimulus. Projects 3.25% growth in 2003 and 4.25% in 2004. "Inflation picture about unchanged — everything at the dollar store still costs a dollar."

**Key signals**: Stockton: "Private payroll employment dropped 48,000 in November." Unemployment "jump to 6.0 percent." Motor vehicle "cutbacks in motor vehicle production account for essentially all of the slowing in the growth of real GDP in the current quarter." Manufacturing IP "outside of motor vehicles to drop about ¼ percent in November." But "few signs that this period of weakness is spreading or cumulating into something more serious." Housing starts "far above the level that we had projected." Inflation: "little news with regard" to inflation. Funds rate lowered to 1.25%. Fiscal stimulus built in — $25 billion tax cuts in 2004. Growth projected "3¼ percent in 2003 and 4¼ percent in 2004."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "inflation picture also is about unchanged." Core inflation stable at low levels. Large output gap persisting. "Everything at the dollar store still costs a dollar." No inflation pressure.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP growing ~2.25% Q4, well below potential. "Economy does not really break out" until late 2003. Unemployment at 6%. Manufacturing declining. Gap widening further.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — wide gap. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Private payroll employment dropped 48,000." Unemployment jumped to 6.0%. "Employment increases that had occurred over the summer have now been reversed." Labor market deteriorating.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant and worsening slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: motor vehicle sales "dropped back considerably." Auto assemblies "lopping off nearly 1½ percentage points from growth in the fourth quarter." "Non-auto spending" weak. Manufacturing output declining. Only housing strong. E&S spending "running well below the fundamentals."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — broad weakness, auto industry drag.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 63 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Structural productivity still elevated. Hours worked "increased a bit more than projected" — positive. But overall output weak. Business caution persisting.
- **Direction**: Above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 50 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: funds rate cut to 1.25% — very accommodative. "More stimulative monetary policy accounts for the bulk of the upward revision." "Recent pieces of good news" in financial markets. But "considerable hit taken by the asset side" of balance sheets. Business caution persisting.
- **Direction**: At neutral — very accommodative rates but ongoing asset/confidence drag.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Inflation very subdued. Expectations stable but at low level. Deflation risk still present from prior meetings.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — slightly low.
