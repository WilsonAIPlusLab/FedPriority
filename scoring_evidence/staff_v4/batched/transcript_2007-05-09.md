## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — productivity revision, housing, capital spending), Johnson (international — oil prices, global growth, commodities), Reinhart (projections summary).

**Era context**: May 2007 — funds rate at 5.25%. Subprime concerns exist but contained. GDP growth "somewhat soft" but expected to pick up. Structural productivity revised down. Oil prices rising.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: Structural productivity revised down. "Surprised by the extent to which actual productivity has slowed over the past six to eight months." "The tension just seemed to be growing greater over time." Kalman filter suggesting "more substantial downward revision to structural productivity."
- Housing: "Leveling-off in sales of existing homes." "Some signs of demand stabilizing." Subprime: "credit restraints could well damp the participation of subprime borrowers."
- Equipment spending: "The latest readings on capital good orders and shipments were encouraging." "Downside risks around growth in our forecast are a bit less."
- Johnson (international): "Economic conditions are favorable and appear likely to remain so." "Real GDP growth in the foreign economies seems poised to continue at an average annual rate of about 3.5 percent." Oil prices up $4.50/barrel from March. "Dollar depreciation of approximately 2 percent."
- Inflation: Multiple "small changes" pointing to "slightly higher import prices, a bit tighter labor and product markets, a lower rate of the growth of structural productivity" — all revising inflation up. Core PCE forecast "2.3 percent this year" and edging down to 2% next year. "Very accommodative financial conditions."
- Reinhart: Central tendency for GDP "somewhat soft this year but will pick up." Core PCE "will edge a little lower." UE "inch up to the vicinity of 4.75 percent."
