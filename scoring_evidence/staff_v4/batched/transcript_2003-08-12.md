# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2003-08-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: August 2003. Bond market selloff dominated intermeeting period — rates up significantly from mid-June lows. Economy strengthening but staff cautious about interpreting rate rise. Stockton's key judgment: treat rate rise as "increase in real rates" not inflation expectations. Staff forecast for GDP growth "unchanged from our forecast two rounds ago" in June Greenbook. Productivity data revised up dramatically — "nothing short of stunning." Structural estimates already at ~2.5% may still be too low. Inflation "very subdued" — core PCE expected to finish below 1%. Dollar declining further. Stock markets abroad up. Bond selloff global.

**Key signals**: Stockton: bond market rate increase treated as "reflecting an increase in real rates" — a restraining factor. "For all our angst over the proper interpretation of the run-up in rates, projected growth—both for the second half of this year and for next year—is about unchanged from our forecast two rounds ago." Productivity: "growth of productivity over the last three years was marked up" in BEA revisions — "nothing short of stunning." Core PCE "will finish next year below" — very subdued. Johnson: "somewhat more optimistic about near-term activity abroad." Stock markets abroad up. Dollar depreciating. Ten-year rates "have risen sharply" globally.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core PCE "inflation continues to look very subdued." Expected to "finish next year below" current pace. Market-based core PCE "came in right on our projection." Non-market components show bulge but "measurement problems are especially acute."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — very subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 33 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP growth above trend projected for H2 but gap still wide. Productivity revisions imply even larger potential output. Bond selloff could restrain. Economy strengthening but gap still significant.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 26 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Hiring through early July seemed to be in the same rut" — no breakout. "Claims has come down" — encouraging. But "payrolls has come to a halt" in declining, not yet rising. "Investment data also show no evidence of a dramatic breakout."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still very slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Economy strengthening but from a weak base. Bond selloff acting as restraint. GDP growth unchanged from June forecast — modest. "Neither I nor my colleagues have undertaken any substantial financial commitments in recent months." Consumer spending and investment data mixed — some improvement but not yet a breakout.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improving but still below trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: BEA revisions to productivity "nothing short of stunning." "Growth of productivity over the last three years was marked up." Structural estimates may still be too low. "Whether our assumption for the growth of potential output—already well above the market consensus—needs to be marked up."
- **Direction**: Well above neutral — exceptional and rising.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: bond rates up sharply — "higher rates, the associated lower stock prices, and the lower level of stock prices" subtract from GDP. Funds rate at 1%. But dollar declining helps. Stock markets abroad up. "Financial conditions generally supportive of recovery." Mixed.
- **Direction**: Near neutral — bond selloff offset by low funds rate and improving confidence.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: dollar declining further. Johnson: dollar depreciation ongoing. "Weaker exchange value of the dollar" part of forecast assumptions. Significant ongoing decline.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant weakness.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "inflation compensation in the TIPS market was taking off" but "near-term inflation expectations as measured by consumers were dropping like a rock." "Long-term inflation expectations remain reasonably steady." Conflicting signals — TIPS may reflect liquidity/term premium changes, not inflation expectations.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — generally low despite TIPS move.
