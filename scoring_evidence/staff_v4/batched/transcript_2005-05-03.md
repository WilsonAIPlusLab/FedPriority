# FOMC Staff Briefing Scoring — 2005-05-03

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the May 3, 2005 FOMC meeting. Staff speakers are individually identified. The document is **segmented** into distinct staff presentations. The files decompose as:
- `staff_economic_briefing.txt` — MR. KOS (financial markets) and MR. STOCKTON (economic outlook), plus Q&A with MS. JOHNSON
- `staff_outlook_briefing.txt` — MS. JOHNSON (international sector, exchange rates, trade)

**Staff speakers and topics:**
- MR. KOS: Eurodollar futures, Treasury yield curve, credit spreads, GM/Ford CDS
- MR. STOCKTON: GDP growth, labor market, consumer spending, energy prices, inflation (core/headline PCE, ECI)
- MS. JOHNSON: Foreign industrial country growth, dollar/FX, trade deficit, import prices

### Era Context
May 2005: The Fed had been on a gradual tightening path (25 bps per meeting). The economy had been growing at or above trend through 2004, but Q1 2005 data came in weak. Oil prices were elevated. Core inflation was modest. This is a mid-cycle period with growing uncertainty about whether the slowdown was transitory or reflected weaker underlying demand.

---

### Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation pressure as modestly above neutral but contained. Key signals:
- Core PCE ~1.75% over past 12 months — "a pace that remains low even by the standards of the past decade"
- Headline PCE revised up to ~2.75% for first half of 2005 (energy/import price driven), ~0.25pp upward revision
- Core PCE inflation forecast revised up to 1.9% for 2005, 1.7% for 2006 — slightly above neutral
- Staff explicitly worried about pass-through of energy/commodity prices and potential upside risks to inflation expectations
- ECI hourly compensation a subdued 2.5% annual rate — "no wage-price spiral"
- Stockton: "the consequence of a series of cost shocks could look to many people an awful lot like an accelerating price level"
- Staff projects core PCE "at or below 2 percent" over forecast period
- Direction: Slightly above neutral (energy/import price pressures elevating headline; core modest but upside risk present). Score: ~52-56 range.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees output gap as below neutral (slightly below potential) after revision.
- GDP Q1 2005 ~3% (consistent with BEA advance); Q2 revised down ~0.5pp to ~3.5%
- "The GDP gap at the end of 2006 is about 1 percent in this forecast rather than being nearly eliminated as it was in our previous forecast"
- Economy was "at or above potential over the past year" but now projecting a widening gap
- Stockton: "the outlook still remains quite favorable. The economy has been averaging growth at or above potential over the past year"
- But the current quarter shows weakness and output gap is now projected to remain open longer
- Direction: The staff is projecting the gap will widen to ~1% by end of 2006, meaning below potential going forward. The current quarter still shows decent growth but below prior projections. Score in range 43-47 (approaching potential but slight slack emerging).

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as firm but not overly tight.
- Initial claims averaging ~325,000/month — "a figure at the low end of the range that has prevailed as the labor market has gradually but steadily improved"
- Insured unemployment "continued to drift lower"
- Private payrolls increased only 100,000 in March — "weak"
- Staff does NOT say labor market is at full employment or tight; rather it remains resilient despite economic softening
- Unemployment rate context: mid-2005 UE was ~5.1-5.2% (near NAIRU)
- Direction: Near full employment / NAIRU vicinity. Score: ~47-50 (near balanced, not clearly tight).

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees consumption/investment as mixed-to-below trend.
- "Weak retail sales report for March held down the growth of overall consumer spending in the first quarter"
- Capital equipment orders/shipments "dropped sharply in March"
- "Growth of equipment and software spending is looking a bit weaker in the first half of this year"
- But: "auto sales were solid in April"; "home sales remained strong"
- Business anecdotal reports "generally remained favorable both with respect to their order books and their capital spending plans"
- Consumer sentiment sag; purchasing managers consistent with slower manufacturing growth
- Direction: Below trend, but not severely so. The mix suggests moderate weakness driven by energy prices and one-quarter softness. Score: ~40-45.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not specifically discussed in the briefings. Score: N/A.

### Financial Dimensions

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees financial conditions as accommodative.
- Stockton: "We still see monetary policy as accommodative, at least from a medium-term perspective, but we recognize that we are now edging into grayer territory"
- Kos: Eurodollar markets showed credit spreads "narrowed" earlier in period; conditions broadly supportive
- "The assurance the Fed was providing regarding the [rate path] ... credit spreads narrowed and implied volatility declined"
- Bright-line rule: "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50. Score: ~25-32.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Kos discusses yield curve movements and the 2-10 spread, but this is standard market functioning — price adjustments from growth/inflation expectations, not market dysfunction.
- 10-year at 4.19%, higher than mid-February
- Yield curve flattening discussed as a signal, not as a market dysfunction
- No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, auction failures
- Direction: Normal market functioning with yield movements. Score: ~38-42. Score just above the "normal" anchor given some unusual yield curve signals being flagged.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Kos describes credit spreads as narrow/compressed but with recent slight widening.
- "Credit spreads narrowed and implied volatility declined" — in context of search for yield
- "Investment-grade corporate and emerging market spreads have widened a bit in recent weeks, as investors became somewhat more risk averse following unexpected credit events"
- "On the right side of the page" this widening is visible, but "barely registers in the longer-term charts"
- "The market is still pricing in a fairly benign outlook"
- GM/Ford CDS elevated — idiosyncratic credit event
- Bright-line rule: Still compressed/narrow overall by historical standards → score MUST be < 50. Score: ~22-27 (historically tight with only minor recent widening).

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Kos mentions stock prices dropped ~4% over the intermeeting period: "reflecting the softer tone of the economic data, stock prices dropped about 4 percent"
- This is a price move, not a valuation commentary
- No explicit valuation language (stretched, fair, cheap, expensive)
- Given the era (2005), equities are roughly around fair value — no staff commentary on valuation per se
- Score near neutral with modest decline: ~50-55. Default to 50 with slight downward price movement.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Banking system not specifically discussed in these briefings. Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Johnson: "The dollar appreciated broadly over the intermeeting period ... versus the Canadian dollar and the euro"
- "The higher starting point leaves us with a forecast path for the dollar that is about [higher] over the forecast horizon"
- Bright-line rule: Dollar appreciation → score MUST be > 50. Score: ~57-62.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 54 ±6 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Stockton: "core consumer prices are up only 1¾ percent over the past 12 months—a pace that remains low even by the standards of the past decade. And we are projecting core PCE inflation to remain at or below 2 percent over the projection period."
- Stockton: "we have revised up our forecast for headline PCE inflation in the first half of the year by about ¼ percentage point to 2¾ percent at an annual rate... the consequence of a series of cost shocks could look to many people an awful lot like an accelerating price level. And if that view were to cement itself, the implications for inflation expectations and the feedback into wages and prices could be a less favorable inflation outcome."

**Center-of-gravity:** Staff sees core inflation as modest and contained (just below 2%), but headline is elevated due to energy/import prices, and staff flags non-trivial upside risk if cost-push shocks persist or if inflation expectations become less anchored.

**Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is modestly above neutral — core near target, headline above, upside risk acknowledged. Score of 54 is above 50 consistent with slight net upside pressure.
