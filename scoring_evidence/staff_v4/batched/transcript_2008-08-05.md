# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-08-05

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Wilcox (domestic outlook — labor market weaker than anticipated, GDP puzzle, motor vehicle sales plunge, inflation outlook), Kamin (international — oil price plunge from $145 to $119, foreign industrial economies slowing sharply, UK heading to recession), Dudley (financial markets — two distinct phases, Fannie/Freddie crisis, IndyMac failure, Merrill Lynch CDO fire sale).

**Era context**: August 2008 — funds rate at 2%. Oil plunged from $145 to $119. UE at 5.5%. IndyMac failed. Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac viability questioned. Merrill Lynch fire sale of CDOs. Staff projecting GDP at 2.7% Q2 but labor market much weaker. Housing legislation enacted providing GSE backstop.

**Key evidence**:
- Wilcox: Three changes — "(1) weaker labor market, (2) apparently stronger aggregate demand, (3) more hostile financial environment — did not easily fit together." Ruled in favor of labor market data: "the story being told by the labor market seemed by far the more credible one." Housing prices "continuing to decline at a rapid pace." Consumer sentiment "sub-basement levels." Loan officers "pervasive tightening of terms and standards." "Nearly 4 percent drop in manufacturing IP during the second quarter fueled our skepticism."
- GDP revised down "0.1 percentage point lower over the second half of this year and 0.2 lower next year." Financial recuperation period "extending into 2010" (previously mid-2009).
- UE "held at 5.5 percent rather than dropping back." "Part time for economic reasons had moved up sharply." "Initial claims for unemployment insurance were trending up."
- Motor vehicle sales: "annual pace of 12.5 million units, much weaker than our already weak forecast of 13.3 million." "Drop in sales and production is certainly large enough to give renewed urgency to the question as to whether a broader retrenchment in spending might be in train."
- Inflation: Core PCE "stepping up from a little more than 2 percent during the first half of this year to a little more than 2.5 percent in the second half." Food prices much higher than forecast — CPI for food in June at 0.8% vs forecast of 0.3%. "Headline PCE inflation running at an average annual pace of 3.5 percent over the second half." "Upside risks have, in my view, diminished somewhat" — downtick in Michigan long-term expectations, drop in oil prices.
- Kamin: Oil plunged from $145 to $119 — "largest on record in nominal dollar terms." But "premature to pop open the champagne." Saudi Arabia added production but "bring total OPEC production up only to their level in early 2006." "Advanced economies — Canada, euro area, United Kingdom, and Japan — came in below 1 percent in the second quarter." UK "into a mild recession in the second half." Net exports added 2.25pp to GDP in Q2 — "largest quarterly positive contribution since 1980."
- Dudley: "Two distinct phases." First half — share prices weakened, spreads widened, housing price declines pressured sector, IndyMac failure "led to uninsured depositors taking losses." Fannie/Freddie "equity prices plummeted, ongoing viability was called into question." Second half — housing legislation provided GSE support, "modest recovery in share prices."
- "Underlying news, especially from the financial sector, remains quite bleak in most respects." "Little conviction that financial shares have reached a bottom." "Unusually high volatility of financial share prices." Merrill Lynch CDO fire sale — sold $30.6B par value CDOs for $6.7B (carried at $11.1B), provided 75% nonrecourse financing to buyer. "Net CDO exposure" declined only $1.7B. Financial markets "not back to normal" across asset classes.
- Foreclosures: "2.5 million foreclosures" projected, "foreclosures next year." "Very bleak landscape."
