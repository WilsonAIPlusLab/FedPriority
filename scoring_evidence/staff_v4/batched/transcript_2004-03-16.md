# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: March 2004. "Mixed bag of data." GDP growth revised down ~0.5pp for H1. Labor market disappointing — payrolls below expectations. Manufacturing IP softer. But E&S spending strong. Core PCE ~1% but inflation concerns starting to emerge. Oil prices rising sharply ($30 to $37). Commodity prices surging. Dollar weaker. Greenbook introduced confidence intervals for first time. Staff highlights downside risks to inflation (disinflation) but also notes "straws in the wind" suggesting inflation could surprise upward.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "mixed bag of data." GDP growth "revised down fairly considerably" for H1. Labor market — "the employment situation" remains concerning, "soft February jobs report." "Employment gap has been widening." But E&S spending strong — "orders and shipments were very robust." Productivity growth — staff view it will decelerate but acknowledges "we have been wrong and wrong rather consistently" in anticipating the slowdown. Stockton on inflation: "lower inflation expectations" in baseline but "straws in the wind that suggest that perhaps inflation will not decline as much." Oil prices risen from $30 to $37. Commodity prices surging — "nearly 50 percent" annual rate. Johnson: foreign growth "averaging more than 4¼ percent." Dollar depreciating. Current account deficit expanding.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: baseline "incorporates lower inflation expectations." Core PCE ~1%. But "straws in the wind" — dollar decline, commodity acceleration, suggest upside risk. Higher NAIRU alternative simulation shows core PCE rising to 2% by 2005. Staff sees "risk balance" could shift.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still low but risks emerging.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP growth revised down for H1 but still above trend. "Projected output gap has increased appreciably." "Wider gap presumably would put downward pressure on core inflation." Still below potential but closing.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Gap widened slightly from prior forecast. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Soft February jobs report." "Employment gap has been widening, given that job growth... has fallen well short of most estimates of the growth of the working-age population." Employment situation remains "stabilizing at a very low level" with little evidence of "really moving up."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack persists.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: E&S spending — "orders and shipments were very robust." Housing strong. Consumer spending supported by income growth. But GDP growth revised down for H1. Mixed signals: strong business spending offset by disappointing employment and softer manufacturing IP.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — spending still above trend despite mixed signals.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 68 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: staff forecasts deceleration but admits "we have been wrong and wrong rather consistently" in anticipating slowdown. "An area where our confidence is not terribly" high. Structural productivity still estimated well above historical average. "Wide range of plausible estimates."
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still elevated, though staff questions persistence.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Fed funds at 1%. Risk spreads narrowed. Dollar weaker. Stock market supportive. "Accommodative policy... has been in place in many foreign economies for some time." Financial conditions remain very supportive despite some tightening in long rates.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — accommodative. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Credit spreads continue to narrow from 2002-03 peaks. "Improvement in business financial conditions." Corporate bond defaults declining.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — compressed.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stock market supportive of household wealth. Not described as overvalued or particularly stressed.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — no particular concern.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 20 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: "exchange value of the dollar" continuing to decline. Current account deficit "rising in volume." Dollar oil prices rising partly from depreciation. Staff projects only "slight downward tilt" in dollar forecast, but "has fallen sharply against many" currencies.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — notable weakness. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: baseline "incorporates lower inflation expectations." But "inflation expectations" could decline further or could stabilize. Mixed signals — survey expectations "stable to somewhat higher in recent months."
- **Direction**: Near neutral — stable with slight upward bias emerging.
