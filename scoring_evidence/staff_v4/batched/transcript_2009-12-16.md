# FOMC Staff Briefing Scoring — 2009-12-16

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from the December 15–16, 2009 FOMC meeting. The document is **segmented** post-2005 style. Staff presenters are identifiable by name:

- **MR. SACK** (Brian Sack, Manager of the System Open Market Account) — financial markets, balance sheet, exit strategy tools; covers pages 4–18+ of the transcript
- **MR. WILCOX** (David Wilcox, standing in for Dave Stockton who was ill with H1N1) — domestic economic outlook, GDP, labor market, inflation; pages ~40–44
- **MR. SHEETS** (Nathan Sheets) — international/foreign economic conditions, FX; pages 44–46

The go-round participants (Rosengren, Bullard, Plosser, etc.) begin at page 47 — their views are excluded from scoring.

---

### 2. Era Context

December 2009. The U.S. economy is emerging from the deepest recession since the Great Depression. The GFC trough was approximately mid-2009. The Fed has been at the zero lower bound since December 2008. Unemployment is around 10%. The balance sheet is $2.2 trillion. GDP has just turned positive in Q3 2009. This is the canonical "deep slack" period with deflation risk.

**Era anchors expected:**
- OUTPUT_GAP: deep below potential → 15–25 range
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: very high unemployment ~10% → 15–22
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: deflation risk, well below 2% → 15–25
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: substantially improved from crisis lows but still working through credit market stress → somewhere in the 40–60 range
- CREDIT_SPREADS: still elevated vs. pre-crisis but narrowed dramatically from 2008 peaks → 55–65

---

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Staff sees inflation as *below target* with significant disinflationary pressure. Wilcox reports core PCE inflation projected at approximately 1.3% for 2010 and 1.2% for 2011 — well below the 2% target. The incoming PCE data had surprises driven entirely by energy pass-through, which staff explicitly says "have little signal content for underlying inflation." Staff made "very minor adjustments to inflation projection." The Sack briefing notes that inflation concerns "seem to be lingering" among some market participants but characterizes these as driven by technical factors and inflation risk premiums, not a staff view that inflation is elevated. Rosengren (in go-round, excluded from scoring but consistent) echoes "little evidence of inflationary pressures... significant risk of further disinflation."

Direction: **Well below target**. Score expected: 18–25 range (meaningful deflation risk / well below target).

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Staff sees the economy as *significantly below potential*. GDP Q4 estimated at ~4.25% annual rate — strong but coming off very deep recession. Wilcox: "moderate recovery is under way and, indeed, that the recovery is gaining greater traction." However, this is growth from a deep trough. Staff notes "final sales appear to be increasing at about a 2 percent pace in the second half" — still very modest. Greenbook projects unemployment at 9.6% end-2010 with ZIRP; "slow in bringing the unemployment rate down." "Slack remains" language is strongly implied throughout.

Direction: **Far below potential** (recovering but deep gap persists). Score expected: 20–28 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Staff sees labor market as very loose. Private payroll declines averaging 90,000/month at time of meeting (down from 700k/month peak). Unemployment projected at 9.6% end-2010 (currently ~10%). Wilcox: "the deterioration in the labor market is abating" but "few signs of a widespread pickup in hiring." Temporary employment and workweek "beginning to turn." Initial claims "continued to drift down." But the level is deeply below full employment.

Direction: **Far below full employment**. Score: 16–22 (UE ~10%, well above full employment).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Staff sees spending/investment as *below trend* but recovering. Real PCE up ~2.5% annual rate in Q4 (after declines). Consumer sentiment improved "modestly." Business investment stabilizing in capital goods. Housing recovering. Exports stronger than expected. But "final sales" only at ~2% pace, well below trend. Downside risk: final sales "peter out next year." Staff language: "moderate recovery," "gaining traction," "firming domestic final sales."

Direction: **Below trend but improving**. Score: 35–42 (sluggish but recovering from severe lows).

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
Not specifically discussed by staff in the briefing presentations. Sack, Wilcox, and Sheets do not address productivity directly.

Direction: **N/A**

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:**
Sack: "financial conditions have generally become more supportive of economic growth." Yields declined at short- and intermediate-term maturities. Equity prices posted solid gains. Dollar broadly unchanged. However: "banks continue to be wary of taking additional credit risk onto their balance sheets. They have continued to tighten the terms on many types of lending, and the volume of bank loans has continued to contract." Also notes CMBS spreads "widened since the summer" and ABS spreads "edged higher." So capital markets are more accommodative, but bank lending channels remain tight.

The staff characterization is that conditions are "generally become more supportive" — i.e., improving but not fully accommodative. The bright-line rule: "supportive" → score MUST be < 50.

Direction: **Moderately accommodative on balance, but with remaining credit tightness in bank lending**. Score: 38–45 (somewhat loose to neutral; "generally supportive").

**TREASURY_STRESS:**
Sack discusses Treasury yield movements extensively but in the context of normal market functioning. Yields are historically low (10-year ~3.5%, 2-year ~80bp). Year-end pressures causing 4-week bill rates to stop out at precisely 0%, but Sack says "the downward pressure on rates does not appear to be intensifying or causing significant market disruption, and thus does not clearly necessitate a policy response." Greek sovereign CDS rising, but "little upward pressure on U.S. yields from this factor." No language about auction failures, settlement failures, or liquidity dysfunction in Treasuries.

Direction: **Normal functioning with some unusual year-end technical pressure, no dysfunction**. Score: 38–45 (normal, some technical pressure but orderly).

**CREDIT_SPREADS:**
Sack provides detailed discussion. Corporate bond spreads "appear to have leveled out to a considerable degree" — implying they had been wide and have narrowed but are not tight. Equity risk premium "still appears wide by historical standards." CMBS spreads "widened since the summer." ABS spreads "edged higher." Corporate bond spreads are not "unusually narrow." Bank loans still contracting. TALF supporting CMBS activity. Overall picture: spreads have narrowed significantly from 2008 crisis levels but remain above historical norms in several markets.

Direction: **Elevated above normal but improving**. Score: 58–65 (somewhat wide; reduced access in bank lending; CMBS/ABS still stressed).

**EQUITY_VALUATION:**
Sack: "broad equity indexes posted another sizable gain since the last FOMC meeting, with the S&P 500 index up more than 5 percent on net." However, on valuation: equity premium "still appears wide by historical standards, suggesting that the run-up in equity prices since earlier this year has not pushed them too high relative to fundamentals." Corporate bond spreads also "not unusually narrow." Staff assessment is that equities are NOT overvalued — the premium is still wide, prices are reasonable relative to fundamentals.

Direction: **Modestly below fair value to roughly fair** — staff sees no overvaluation concern, if anything slight undervaluation given wide premium. Score: 48–54 (no particular concern; recent gains but premium still wide).

**BANKING_STRESS:**
Sack: banks "continue to be wary of taking additional credit risk onto their balance sheets. They have continued to tighten the terms on many types of lending, and the volume of bank loans has continued to contract." Bank equity prices "were mixed over the intermeeting period." Focus on TARP repayments by Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo — raising capital. From the go-round (Rosengren, excluded but corroborating): "percent of bank assets in institutions with low supervisory ratings continues to grow, and bank failures are artificially being held down by resource constraints at the FDIC."

The staff (Sack) characterizes banks as still in stress — tightening lending, contracting loan books — but large banks are recapitalizing. The banking system is under meaningful stress, not healthy.

Direction: **Significant stress persisting**. Score: 65–72 (significant stress; capital concerns; tightened lending standards; loan volume contracting; TARP repayments signal ongoing capital pressure).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:**
Sheets: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar fell through the month of November but has since rebounded vigorously on stronger-than-expected U.S. data. On net, the dollar is little changed since the last FOMC meeting." Forecast: "real broad dollar index will depreciate at roughly a 2 percent pace on average over the next two years." Dollar "still remains roughly 7 percent above its 2008 trough." Net intermeeting: little changed.

Direction: **Stable to slight weakness** (on net unchanged; forecast calls for modest depreciation). Score: 43–48 (modest weakness to stable; "little changed").

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (22) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (19)**: Both far below neutral, consistent. Deep recession trough. The 3-point gap is explained by the labor market lagging output — layoffs slowing before hiring resumes. ✓

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (22) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38)**: 16-point gap. This needs explanation. Output gap is deeper than consumption/investment score suggests. Explanation: GDP is recovering at 4.25% in Q4 (largely inventory-driven), which pulls consumption/investment to "below trend but recovering" rather than "collapsing." PCE grew ~2.5%, housing recovering, capital goods stabilizing. The level is below trend but the rate of improvement is notable, hence consumption/investment score is not as extreme as output gap. ✓

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (62)**: Financial conditions overall are "supportive" (accommodative, score 40), but credit spreads remain elevated (62). This apparent tension is reconciled by the bifurcated market: capital markets (bonds, equities) have recovered significantly — supporting FC score below 50 — while bank lending and some structured credit (CMBS, ABS) remain stressed — pushing credit spreads above 50. Sack explicitly contrasts "the narrowing of credit spreads in capital markets" with banks "continuing to tighten the terms on many types of lending." ✓

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Both in the same general range; Treasury market is functioning normally, consistent with broadly supportive financial conditions. ✓

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) ↔ Sub-dimensions**: Core PCE ~1.3% in 2010, 1.2% in 2011. Headline driven by energy noise. Expectations: staff notes "inflation concerns seem to be lingering" in markets (5-year 5-year forward breakevens at upper end of recent range), but characterizes this as partly technical/risk-premium driven. Staff sees underlying inflation well below target. Expectations sub-score ~35–40 (concerns lingering but not unanchored). Parent score 22 is consistent with core well below target. ✓

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 22 ± 5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. WILCOX: "We lowered projected [overall PCE] inflation by a tenth in 2010 to 1.3 percent and raised the projection by two tenths in 2011 to 1.2 percent." Core PCE revised to "about 1 percent" in Q4 current quarter.
- MR. WILCOX: "The incoming data on overall PCE prices were a bit above our projection. But the surprises were almost completely the result of a somewhat faster pass through of crude oil and natural gas prices to the retail level and higher than expected nonmarket prices, which we think have little signal content for underlying inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Staff projects core PCE well below 2% (~1.3% in 2010, 1.2% in 2011) with energy-driven headline surprises explicitly dismissed as lacking signal content for underlying inflation; risk of disinflation is a primary concern.

**Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is well below neutral (50); core PCE ~1.3% vs 2% target, with disinflationary risks cited. Score 22 is in the "meaningful deflation risk / well below target" range. ✓

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 22 ± 5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. WILCOX: GDP Q4 current quarter estimated at "annual rate of about 4¼ percent" but "considerable risks continue to attend our forecast." "The Greenbook has the unemployment rate at 9.6 percent at the end of 2010."
- MR. WILCOX: "A moderate recovery is under way and, indeed, that the recovery is gaining greater traction." But "final sales appear to be increasing at about a 2 percent pace in the second half" — very modest underlying growth from severe trough.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Economy is emerging from an extremely deep recession with unemployment projected near 10% through end-2010 and a very large resource gap; 4.25% GDP growth in Q4 is largely inventory-driven and comes off a catastrophic prior contraction.

**Direction confirmation:** OUTPUT_GAP is far below potential (well below 50). Large slack, 9.6% unemployment projected, "moderate recovery" just beginning. Score 22 consistent with GFC trough anchor of 15–22. ✓

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 19 ± 5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. WILCOX: "Declines in private payrolls averaged 90,000 per month over the past three months. That compares with job losses that were averaging about 700,000 per month earlier this year." Unemployment projected at "9.6 percent at the end of 2010."
- MR. WILCOX: "We have seen few signs of a widespread pickup in hiring... businesses remain very cautious. If that caution persists longer than we expect, hiring could remain weak and slack in labor markets could fail to recede to the extent we are projecting."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unemployment near 10%, payrolls still declining (albeit at a much slower pace than the crisis peak), no hiring pickup evident, and labor market slack expected to persist well into 2010.

**Direction confirmation:** LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below full employment (well below 50). UE ~10%, score 19 is consistent with 2009–10 anchor of 15–22. ✓

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 38 ± 6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. WILCOX: "We now estimate that real PCE increased at an annual rate of about 2½ percent in the second half [of 2009]." "The key elements of that firming include a recovery in housing, the aforementioned turnaround in consumer spending, and a stabilization in spending on capital goods."
- MR. WILCOX: "We are projecting a 3 percent advance in [final sales in] 2010. Much of next year's acceleration occurs in spending for durable goods, especially business investment... But it is possible that we are too optimistic in our expectation of further easing of financial conditions, including the terms of lending from the banking sector."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending recovering at ~2.5% pace, housing stabilizing, capital goods spending stabilizing; but final demand still well below pre-crisis trend and substantial downside risks remain; below trend but recovering from severe lows.

**Direction confirmation:** CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below trend (below 50), in the "sluggish / cautious" range. Score 38 is consistent with early recovery from 2008–09 lows. ✓

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Score: 40 ± 6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. SACK: "Since the last FOMC meeting, financial conditions have generally become more supportive of economic growth. Yields have declined at short- and intermediate-term maturities, and equity prices have posted a solid gain, while the dollar has remained about unchanged on net."
- MR. SACK: "Banks, in particular, continue to be wary of taking additional credit risk onto their balance sheets. They have continued to tighten the terms on many types of lending, and the volume of bank loans has continued to contract. These patterns contrast sharply with the narrowing of credit spreads in capital markets since earlier this year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Capital markets (bonds, equities) have become broadly accommodative with lower yields and rising asset prices, but bank lending standards remain tight and loan volume is contracting, creating a bifurcated picture that on balance is "generally supportive" — i.e., modestly accommodative.

**Direction confirmation:** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative / supportive → score MUST be < 50). Score 40 in the "somewhat loose to neutral" range. Bright-line rule satisfied. ✓

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

**Score: 40 ± 6 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- MR. SACK: "The downward pressure on rates does not appear to be intensifying or causing significant market disruption, and thus does not clearly necessitate a policy response." (Regarding year-end pressure on Treasury bill rates.)
- MR. SACK: "Overall, any upward pressure on Treasury yields arising from investors' concerns about longer-term inflation risks or the fiscal outlook is being dominated by the downward pressure arising from the prospect of low short-term interest rates for an extended period, leaving Treasury yields quite low."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Treasury market is functioning normally; year-end bill rate pressure is technical and non-disruptive; yield movements are attributed to policy expectations and inflation risk premiums, not market dysfunction.

**Direction confirmation:** TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral — no actual dysfunction, orderly market. Score 40 reflects slightly below normal (smooth functioning). Bright-line rule satisfied (no dysfunction language). ✓

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS

**Score: 62 ± 7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- MR. SACK: "Corporate bond spreads, shown to the right, appear to have leveled out to a considerable degree. Moreover, yield spreads on commercial mortgage-backed securities, the bottom left panel, have widened since the summer, and ABS spreads across most other asset classes have also edged higher recently."
- MR. SACK: "An analysis of corporate bond spreads suggests that their risk premiums are also not unusually narrow." Equity premium "still appears wide by historical standards." Banks "continued to tighten the terms on many types of lending."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Capital market credit spreads have leveled off at above-normal levels (not tight), CMBS and ABS spreads have re-widened, bank lending terms remain tight, and even equity risk premiums are wide by historical standards — overall credit conditions remain elevated relative to normal.

**Direction confirmation:** CREDIT_SPREADS are above neutral (elevated). Score 62 is in the "somewhat wide" range. Spreads are not compressed or narrow. Bright-line rule satisfied. ✓

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION

**Score: 48 ± 8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Direction confirmation:** Score 48 reflects roughly fair valued / no particular concern, with the slight lean toward not overvalued (premium still wide). ✓

### 10. BANKING_STRESS

**Score: N/A** — services not separately discussed; limited basis to score.

**Score: N/A**

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:**

**Score: 38 ± 8 | Confidence: Moderate** — breakevens near upper end of recent range, some lingering inflation concerns in markets, but staff view is that these reflect risk premiums and technical factors rather than genuine unanchoring; expectations remain roughly anchored if elevated within range.
