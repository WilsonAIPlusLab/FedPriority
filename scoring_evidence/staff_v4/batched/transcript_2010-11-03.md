### 2. Era Context

November 2010 is the early recovery from the Great Financial Crisis. The FOMC is meeting to announce QE2 (large-scale asset purchases). Key macro context: unemployment ~9.6%, recovery is underway but tepid, core PCE inflation is well below 2% and trending down, financial conditions have improved partly on QE2 anticipation, the dollar has depreciated. This is a period of:
- Significant labor market slack (unemployment still near 10%)
- Large negative output gap (economy well below potential)
- Below-target and declining inflation (disinflation concern, not deflation yet)
- Broadly accommodative financial conditions (equity prices up, dollar down, yields lower)

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Dimension

WILCOX: "core PCE inflation running at 0.9 percent annual rate over the second half of the year" (a couple of tenths BELOW their September forecast). Also "consumer price inflation remains subdued." This is not deflation but clearly below target and declining. Score should be in the 26-35 range.

"Job creation continues at an anemic pace." Unemployment expected to "edge back up to 9.7 percent" near-term, then slowly decline. This is clearly a large negative output gap in the 30-38 range given the 2010 early-recovery context and tepid growth.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor as WELL BELOW the midpoint (very loose). Unemployment rate at 9.6%, expected to "edge back up to 9.7 percent." Private payroll gains "only about 90,000 per month" — "anemic pace." "Available indicators of hiring remain weak." ### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (23)**: Both below 50, consistent with recession recovery. Gap divergence: OUTPUT_GAP is 35, LABOR_TIGHTNESS is 23 — a 12-point gap. This is within tolerance. The slight divergence reflects that the labor market is even weaker than output (unemployment still near 10%, below-trend hiring) while GDP has been growing slowly upward. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50 — consistent. Consumption/investment slightly higher than output gap because demand components showed some unexpected strength (E&S spending, consumption) even as overall GDP stayed tepid due to import surge offsetting domestic demand. Divergence of ~5 points is reasonable.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (35)**: Both well below 50, both indicating loose/accommodative conditions. Consistent direction. Difference of 5 points is acceptable — FC assessment is more broadly accommodative (equity gains, dollar drop, yield decline) while credit spreads are less directly discussed.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (38)**: FC is accommodative, Treasury functioning is normal. Consistent — no stress in either measure. Gap of 8 points is acceptable; Treasury stress is near neutral-normal even as overall FC is clearly accommodative.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) ↔ INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Core PCE at ~0.9%, below target and declining. INFLATION_GOODS: dollar weakness and commodity price rises pushing import prices up (4.5% rate this quarter), but core goods likely still subdued. INFLATION_SERVICES: not directly addressed but core PCE low. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: "well-anchored" mentioned in context of keeping inflation from falling further, but anchored at low level. Sub-dimensions should cluster in the 25-40 range, consistent with parent score of ~30.
