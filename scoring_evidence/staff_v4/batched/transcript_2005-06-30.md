# FOMC Staff Briefing Scoring — 2005-06-30

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type and Structure

This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (June 29-30, 2005). It is a **segmented** post-2005 format with separate staff briefing files. Staff speakers identified:

- **MR. WILCOX** (David Wilcox) — economic outlook, labor market, inflation (staff_outlook_briefing.txt)
- **MR. STOCKTON** (Dave Stockton) — GDP growth, inflation forecast (staff_outlook_briefing.txt, staff_economic_briefing.txt)
- **MR. LEAHY** (Mike Leahy) — international/foreign outlook (staff_economic_briefing.txt)
- **MS. JOHNSON** — dollar/FX outlook (staff_economic_briefing.txt)
- **MR. OLINER** — financial conditions, asset valuation (staff_financial_briefing.txt)
- **MR. REINHART** — TIPS/inflation compensation (staff_economic_briefing.txt)

### Era Context

Mid-2005: The U.S. economy was in a mid-cycle expansion. The Fed had been on a gradual tightening path since June 2004. Oil prices had risen substantially. Core PCE inflation was running near/slightly above 2%. Unemployment around 5.1%. Housing boom in full swing. Financial conditions broadly accommodative. This is the pre-crisis era of the mid-2000s.

---

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**

Staff sees inflation as modestly ABOVE the midpoint / slightly above target.

Key evidence:
- Wilcox: "we project core PCE prices to rise a bit more than 2 percent this year and a tad less than 2 percent in 2006" — core PCE at ~2.1% for 2005.
- Wilcox discusses upward revisions: forecast for core PCE inflation stepped up ~½ percentage point from January Greenbook; current estimate for 2005 is 2.1%.
- Topline inflation running at ~2½% (driven by energy).
- Staff identifies oil and intermediate materials/import prices as sources of upward pressure; but views much of it as transitory.
- Compensation per hour showed a spike (options-related), but ECI decelerating — staff is not alarmed.
- "we project core PCE inflation to come in this year at 2.1 percent" with expectation it moderates to ~1¾% in 2006.

Direction: slightly above target. The 2.1% core PCE for 2005 is modestly above the 2% objective. Staff sees it as transitory/moderating. Score: **54-58** range — modestly above 50.

**Magnitude check**: "a bit more than 2 percent" = "somewhat" above → 5-10 points above 50 → 55-60. Staff expects return to below 2% next year. Score around **57**.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**

Staff sees output as NEAR but slightly BELOW potential — output gap closing over the forecast period.

Key evidence:
- Wilcox: "as shown on line 7, the output gap closes only a little [throughout the projection period]" — gap is closing slowly.
- "we project that real GDP will expand roughly 3½ percent this year and again in 2006, just a bit faster in both years than the growth of potential."
- Unemployment "currently just a tenth of a percentage point above our estimate of the NAIRU" — effectively at NAIRU.
- "total hours worked remain a little below the estimated trend despite the recent gradual improvement."
- "the balance of evidence as suggesting that a little slack remains in the labor market."
- GDP growth ~3½% projected, close to potential growth rate.

Direction: output gap is slightly BELOW potential — "a smidgeon of slack still remains." But it is very close to potential, essentially at/near neutral. The unemployment rate is just 0.1pp above NAIRU. Score around **47-49** — just below 50 but very close.

**Bright-line check**: "a little slack remains" → must be < 50. Score **48**.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**

Staff sees labor market as NEAR full employment / slightly below NAIRU level of tightness, but essentially at equilibrium.

Key evidence:
- Wilcox: "the unemployment rate is currently just a tenth of a percentage point above our estimate of the NAIRU."
- "the balance of evidence as suggesting that a little slack remains in the labor market, but that the margin is narrow, and narrower today than it was six months or a year ago."
- Unemployment rate at 5.1% — very close to NAIRU.
- Participation rate below trend, some hidden slack.
- "total hours worked remain a little below the estimated trend."
- Some indicators: consumer confidence in job finding below 1997 levels (implying slightly less tight than that tight period), NFIB not encountering quite as much difficulty filling positions as in first half of 1997.

Direction: near NAIRU, slight slack. Unemployment 5.1%, essentially at full employment. Score: **50-52** range. Just at/slightly below NAIRU but very close. The rubric says 46-52 = "near NAIRU / full employment / balanced / UE ~4.5-5%." At 5.1%, this is at the upper end of that range or right at NAIRU.

Score: **50** — essentially at NAIRU with a narrow margin of slack remaining.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**

Staff sees spending/investment as at trend or modestly above — "pretty robust" underlying demand.

Key evidence:
- Wilcox: "the underlying growth of demand—supported by low interest rates and appreciable gains in housing and stock market wealth—has been pretty robust."
- Real GDP projected at ~3½% for 2005 and 2006 — above potential (~3%).
- Final sales are described as key driver of aggregate demand, remaining strong.
- Housing boosting household wealth.
- Net exports a drag.
- Consumer spending supported by wealth effects; household balance sheets appear in "reasonably well" shape with low delinquency rates.

Direction: above trend but not dramatically so. GDP growing ~3½%, final sales robust. Score: **55-58**.

**Magnitude check**: "pretty robust," "low interest rates," positive wealth effects = "above trend" language → 55-65 range. Not "boom" level. Score **56**.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**

Discussed briefly — Wilcox references "structural productivity" in the compensation discussion.

- Wilcox mentions "the continued pass-through of increases in structural productivity to real wages" as a factor in compensation trajectory.
- No extensive dedicated productivity discussion.
- GDP growth of ~3½% implies reasonable productivity growth (economy near potential).

Direction: at trend or slightly above. Brief discussion only. Score: **52**, confidence Low-Moderate.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**

Staff sees financial conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/LOOSE.

Key evidence:
- Leahy (international context): "financial conditions appear [supportive of foreign growth]... BBB corporate bond spreads...are relatively low."
- Oliner: equity risk premium "back to a level that appears to provide appropriate compensation for risk in the current economic environment" — not stressed.
- "positive financial conditions" referenced (for foreign economies too).
- Low long-term interest rates globally despite Fed tightening.
- Banks "easing their lending standards for consumer loans."
- Household delinquency rates "relatively low."
- High-yield risk premium improved after GM/Ford concerns.

Direction: accommodative. This is 2005 — the Fed Funds Rate is rising but financial conditions remain loose (the "conundrum" — long-term rates haven't risen). Broad financial conditions are supportive.

**Bright-line check**: "accommodative" / "supportive" → score MUST be < 50. Score: **28** (loose, early 2005-06 era anchor is 10-20; conditions are accommodative but some tightening underway from FF rate hikes). More like 25-35. Score **30**.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

Long-term Treasury yields discussed extensively but in the context of the "conundrum" — yields falling despite Fed tightening. No market dysfunction described.

Key evidence:
- Leahy: "the widespread decline of long-term rates [in the U.S. and abroad]" — about the level and why rates have fallen, not about dysfunction.
- Treasury yields moved ~40 basis points lower over the 20-month period (in context of foreign central bank comparisons).
- No discussion of auction failures, liquidity impairment, settlement issues, bid-ask widening.

Direction: this is yield movements from macro factors, NOT dysfunction. Per bright-line rules, yield movements alone ≠ stress. Score: **35** (normal, orderly market with notable yield decline; no stress signals).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

Staff sees credit spreads as TIGHT/COMPRESSED — historically low.

Key evidence:
- Oliner: "the implied [high-yield] risk premium fell to the lowest level since the go-go days of the late 1990s" before GM/Ford concerns briefly boosted it.
- After GM/Ford issues, "appear to have sparked a renewed appreciation for risk among investors, boosting the premium back to a level that appears to provide appropriate compensation for risk."
- Leahy: "bond spreads...are still tight by historical standards."
- MS. BIES references "we're back to skinny spreads now" regarding high-yield debt.
- "BBB corporate bond spreads...are relatively low."

Direction: spreads are tight/compressed, though slightly less extreme than the tightest point after GM/Ford correction. Still "tight by historical standards." Score: **18-22** — below normal, tight spreads.

**Bright-line check**: "compressed" / "historically tight" → MUST be < 50. Score: **20**.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

Oliner discusses equity valuations in detail.

Key evidence:
- Oliner: earnings-price ratio for S&P 500 vs. synthetic Treasury perpetuity — "the gap lies within the range that prevailed from the mid-1980s to the mid-1990s, before the bubble period in the stock market. By this admittedly crude metric, stocks appear to be priced with an adequate allowance for risk."
- High-yield risk premium "back to a level that appears to provide appropriate compensation."
- No bubble-level concern, no crash/depression-level concern — equities roughly fairly valued.

Direction: roughly fair value, no significant over- or under-valuation concern. Score: **45** (slightly below neutral, modest overvaluation concern but "adequate allowance for risk").

**10. BANKING_STRESS**

Staff sees banking system as healthy/low stress.

Key evidence:
- Oliner: "the corporate sector is in excellent financial shape overall" — default rate on corporate bonds and C&I delinquencies "are both very low."
- Household sector "appears to be doing reasonably well."
- "banks, on net, have been easing their lending standards for consumer loans" — implies banks feel comfortable enough to ease.
- Low delinquency rates across household and corporate sectors.
- Banks easing lending standards, not tightening.

Direction: low stress. "Excellent financial shape," low defaults, easing lending standards.

**Bright-line check**: "sound" / "healthy" → MUST be < 50. Score: **28** (very healthy, in line with 2005-06 anchor of 25-35).

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**

Johnson discusses the dollar.

Key evidence:
- Johnson: "the dollar, on balance, has risen...and indeed it has risen rather significantly over the [recent period]."
- "the recent appreciation of the dollar" cited as a negative for growth forecast.
- "the recent depreciation of the euro and yen in foreign exchange markets...should provide some boost to net exports [for foreign economies]" — implies dollar has been strengthening.
- Leahy: "the recent appreciation of the dollar" listed as a negative for the U.S. growth forecast (among key background factors).

Direction: dollar has been appreciating / strengthening.

**Bright-line check**: Dollar appreciation → score MUST be > 50. Score: **62** (notable appreciation that is a meaningful headwind for exports and growth, consistent with the dollar rising "rather significantly").

Direction: above neutral. Score: **60**.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**

Direction: at trend or slightly below. Score: **50**.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**

- Housing wealth appreciated substantially (boosting household net worth).
- No specific CPI shelter/rent discussion.
Direction: housing prices rising, contributing to wealth effects. Moderate-elevated. Score: **60** (housing inflation discussed as ongoing but not yet alarming for CPI purposes).

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**

- The discussion suggests TIPS breakevens reasonably stable but some near-term elevation.
- "long-term Treasury [rates] about steady through next year."
- No discussion of unanchored expectations; staff does not signal concern about expectations.
Direction: anchored, but near the upper end of anchored range given near-term oil pressures. Score: **52** (well-anchored, minor elevation from energy).

### Self-Check: Cross-Dimension Coherence

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (50)**: Both near 50, output slightly below potential, labor essentially at NAIRU. Gap of 2 points — coherent. Both reflect an economy very near but marginally below full capacity.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (56)**: Gap of 8 points — requires explanation. GDP growth is ~3½% which is above potential (~3%), but output gap is still slightly negative because of some hours/participation shortfall. Final demand/spending is robust (>trend) while the overall output gap remains mildly negative. This is coherent with a situation where demand is driving growth above potential rate, but full gap closure takes time. Acceptable.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (20)**: Both below 50, both indicating loose/accommodative/compressed conditions. Coherent — tight spreads go with easy financial conditions.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (35)**: Both in the 30s — orderly Treasury market with broadly accommodative conditions. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (57) ↔ Sub-components**: Goods (60), Services (50), Housing (60), Expectations (52). Weighted average of discussed components ~55-58. Parent score of 57 is consistent.
