# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Oliner/Wilcox (economic outlook, labor market, inflation), Stockton (business sector/financial conditions). Era: June 2004. First rate hike meeting — funds rate raised from 1% to 1.25%. Economy "clearly strengthened." Payrolls +250K/month in Apr-May. Factory output surging 9%. Housing starts/sales strong. E&S up 12%, projected 9% next year. BUT consumer spending softer after Greenbook close. Core PCE now projected ~1.5% this year (up from 1% a year ago), edging back to 1.25% next year. Housing valuation concerns emerging (rent-price ratio disparity). Partial-expensing expiration looming. Staff projects gradual tightening sufficient.

**Key signals**: Wilcox: payrolls "will average a little more than 300,000 jobs through the end of this year." Unemployment rate "remaining slightly above our estimate of the NAIRU throughout the projection period." Productivity "likely to come back into closer alignment with its structural trend." Inflation: "PCE price inflation has stepped up this year from the very low rates registered last year." Core PCE: "some of the pickup in core inflation this year can be attributed to the indirect effects of higher energy prices" and "acceleration of import prices." But "inflationary pressures will remain fairly well contained." Confidence interval for 2005 core PCE: 0.75% to 2.5%. Stockton: Business financial conditions healthy — "profit share of GNP now stands at its highest level in more than three decades." Debt-to-asset ratio at lowest in many years. E&S spending "above the fitted line" of historical relationship. BFI projected 12% this year, 9% next.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: "PCE price inflation has stepped up this year." Core PCE accelerated from ~1% to ~1.5%. Energy price pass-through, import price acceleration, diminishing slack. But "inflationary pressures will remain fairly well contained" going forward. Core PCE projected to edge back to 1.25%. Staff sees balanced risks. 70% confidence interval 0.75-2.5%.
- **Direction**: Below neutral but approaching it — clear upward shift from 2003.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 45 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: unemployment "remaining slightly above our estimate of the NAIRU throughout the projection period." GDP growing above trend. Gap narrowing steadily. Payrolls surging. Manufacturing output up 9%. Near but not yet at potential.
- **Direction**: Just below neutral — gap nearly closed.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: monthly payroll increases "will average a little more than 300,000 jobs" through year-end. But unemployment still "slightly above our estimate of the NAIRU." Participation rate expected to recover. Workweek rebounding. Major improvement from 2003 but not yet tight.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improved significantly but not tight.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: E&S up about 12%, projected 9% next year. "Profit share of GNP now stands at its highest level in more than three decades." Housing strong. Payrolls surging supports income. BFI 12% this year. But consumer spending softer after Greenbook close. Partial-expensing expiration creates pothole risk.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — robust investment/spending, though consumer side softening.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: productivity "likely to come back into closer alignment with its structural trend." Still above historical average structurally but decelerating cyclically as firms hire. Yellen notes "labor productivity growth in the nonfarm business sector has averaged 4¼ percent—about 1½ percentage points above its pace" pre-2000.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still strong but normalizing.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "interest rate that corporations face on new debt" jumped. But average rate on stock of debt more stable. Business balance sheets very healthy — debt ratios at lowest in many years, profits at highest share in three decades. Funds rate still only 1.25% after hike. Still accommodative overall.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — tightening but still accommodative. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "default rate on corporate bonds has dropped even more sharply." "Delinquency rate on C&I loans at banks has retraced much of the run-up." Credit conditions very favorable.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — compressed.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stock market providing support. Not described as particularly overvalued or stressed. Market processing rate hike expectations.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: dollar depreciation ongoing. Current account deficit widening. "Real net exports are expected to knock more than ⅔ percentage point off the growth of real GDP" by 2006. Dollar weakness a significant ongoing theme.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — weak. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 53 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: "near-term inflation expectations... have stepped up noticeably in the last few months." But "longer-term expectations, shown by the red line, thus far seem to have remained reasonably well anchored."
- **Direction**: Slightly above neutral — short-term firming, long-term anchored.
