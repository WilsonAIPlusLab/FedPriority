# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Madigan/Meyer (zero lower bound analysis — 21 staff notes, nonstandard policy tools, deflation risk), Aaronson (domestic outlook — private payrolls plunged 540K November, GDP declining nearly 5% in Q4-Q1, UE projected to 8.25%), Ahmed (international — foreign growth weakest since 1982, trade collapse, commodity prices plunging), Sheets (European banks, EM exposures).

**Era context**: December 2008 — funds rate at 1%, effective rate already significantly lower. Zero lower bound imminent. Staff prepared 21 notes on unconventional policy. GDP projected to decline at nearly 5% annual rate in Q4 and Q1. UE projected to reach 8.25% in 2010. Deflation risk: "roughly 1-in-4 chance that the core PCE price index will decline over one or more of the next five years." Global recession deepest since early 1980s.

**Key evidence**:
- Aaronson: Private payroll employment "plunged 540,000 in November, and the figures for September and October were revised down noticeably." Three-month decline in private employment at "annual rate of 4.2 percent — a much steeper pace of job loss than we were expecting." "Retrenchment in November was pervasive across both goods- and service-producing industries." Initial claims "continued their steep climb." "Real PCE on goods other than motor vehicles" falling at "annual rate of 5.25 percent, not as bad as the 9.25 percent decline we projected in the Greenbook but still very weak." Consumer sentiment "recessionary low levels." Motor vehicle sales at "10.1 million units, a much slower pace than we had anticipated."
- Housing: Single-family starts "fell further, to 441,000 units — somewhat lower than our expectations." Permits continued to move down.
- Business: "New orders for nondefense capital goods...dropped for a third straight month." Total IP "fell 0.6 percent in November." Three-month diffusion index of manufacturing IP "plummeted to 21" — "contraction in industrial activity has been remarkably widespread."
- GDP outlook: "We expect output to decline at an average annual rate of nearly 5 percent" in Q4 and Q1. "Firms have been acting aggressively to limit unwanted increases in their inventories." Fiscal stimulus: $500 billion assumed for early next year on top of $165 billion enacted earlier. Recovery projected "sluggish" — "more prolonged" than 1973 or 1981 recessions. UE projected to reach "8.25 percent in 2010." "Federal funds rate is already close to the zero lower bound" — "limited scope for conventional monetary policy."
- Financial conditions: "Financial conditions have deteriorated further." Index of financial stress "continued to rise sharply." "Widening of corporate bond spreads for investment-grade issues." Equity prices lower than projected. Mortgage rates fallen 0.5pp since October Greenbook — spread over 10-year Treasury "still quite wide."
- Inflation: Total PCE inflation "will slow to about 1 percent in 2010, while the core rate falls to 0.8 percent." CPI "fell 1.7 percent" in November — "driven by a sharp drop in energy prices." Core CPI "unchanged last month." "Wide unemployment rate gap puts substantial downward pressure on inflation." Concern about "sectoral reallocation" potentially raising NAIRU. Beveridge curve analysis.
- Meyer: "Roughly 1-in-4 chance that the core PCE price index will decline over one or more of the next five years." "Forecasts generally suggest that additional stimulus would be desirable." Discussion of quantitative easing, longer-term securities purchases, targeted lending facilities, communication strategies. November 25 announcement to buy $100B GSE debt and up to $500B MBS "reacted positively" — yields declined.
- Ahmed: "Total foreign growth to minus 1.5 percent in the current quarter and minus 1.25 percent in the next." "Weakest since 1982." "Plummeted" PMIs in UK and euro area "reaching levels well below those observed during the 2001 downturn." Japan exports and IP contracted. Korea, Singapore, Taiwan exports "plummeting." BoE and ECB "slashed policy rates by a total of 250 basis points and 125 basis points, respectively." Emerging markets implementing fiscal stimulus — China's 16% of GDP package (though overstated).
- Dollar: "Moved down about 2 percent on balance" against broad index. "Dollar has appreciated somewhat against some of its important trading partners" (EM). Mixed — appreciated against pound/EM, depreciated against yen/euro.
- Sheets: "European banks are particularly exposed" to EM. "Significant risks to the European banking sector." Hungary, Ukraine — Fund stepping in.
