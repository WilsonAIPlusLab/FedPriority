# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2003-10-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: October 2003. Economy booming — Q3 GDP at 6.25% annual rate. "Things have been going very well." Capital spending surging (E&S up 13%). Housing phenomenal. Nascent labor recovery signs. But inflation still subdued. Dollar weaker.

**Key signals**: Stockton: "things have been going very well for the U.S. economy in recent weeks. The data have been coming in almost uniformly stronger." Q3 GDP up 6.25%, "nearly 2 percentage points above that in the September Greenbook." "All of that upward revision reflects stronger readings on final sales." "Private payrolls expanded by 72,000 in September" — first increase. Manufacturing production firming. "Outlays for defense have also been providing a sizable boost." But Stockton also flags risks: "Had developed a vast vocabulary of weakness." Inflation subdued. "Available capacity combined with continued rapid growth of structural productivity should keep a lid on cost pressures."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "available capacity combined with continued rapid growth of structural productivity should keep a lid on cost pressures." "No signs that inflation expectations have deteriorated." Core inflation stable at low levels, ~1%.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — subdued inflation despite growth surge.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: GDP surging at 6.25% Q3. "GDP about ½ percent above that in the September Greenbook throughout." But "slack is expected to diminish only slowly" — unemployment projected at 5.5% end 2004 and 5% end 2005. Manufacturing capacity utilization "only 78 percent at the close of 2004."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — below potential but closing rapidly.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Private payrolls expanded by 72,000 in September" — first gains. "Initial claims have been averaging close to 390,000." "Insured unemployment has moved lower." But "nascent recovery" — still early.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still significant slack despite nascent improvement.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "sizable third-quarter surprise" — all in final sales. E&S spending up 13%. Housing "phenomenal" — starts, permits, sales all surging. Consumer spending boosted by tax cuts. Defense adding significant boost.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — robust spending above trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "continued rapid growth of structural productivity" at 2.5-2.75%. Structural estimates continue elevated. "Technology and organizational know-how are probably providing a bigger boost than we had previously recognized."
- **Direction**: Well above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Low interest rates... a waning aversion to risk, sharp gains in corporate profitability, rising sales, and the partial-expensing tax provisions appear to be creating a considerably more hospitable climate for business investment." "Zero real interest rates." Dollar weakening further. Conditions very accommodative.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — accommodative. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Waning aversion to risk" and improving corporate conditions. Spreads narrowing.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — favorable.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Sharp gains in corporate profitability." Equity markets rising. Not described as overvalued.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — reasonably valued in recovery.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "weaker exchange value of the dollar in this forecast." Dollar continuing decline. "Potency of zero real interest rates" and "recent weakening of the dollar might be another indication."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant weakness.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "No signs that inflation expectations have deteriorated." Stable but at low level.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.
