### Era Context

September 2010 is a mid-recovery period following the Great Financial Crisis. The economy had returned to growth in mid-2009 but is experiencing a significant soft patch / growth scare. Growth has been revised down repeatedly. Unemployment is approximately 9.6% at this time (consistent with "450,000 initial claims" and "87,000 private payrolls"). The Fed is debating a second round of QE (QE2 was announced November 2010). Era anchors: OUTPUT_GAP should be well below 50 (deep slack), INFLATION_PRESSURE in the 25-35 range (well below target, mild deflation risk discussion), LABOR_TIGHTNESS around 20-25 (very elevated unemployment).

### Dimensional Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint — clearly below 2% target. Stockton says: "core price inflation to run just above 1 percent in the second half of this year and then to drift down just below 1 percent in 2011 and 2012." Total PCE inflation decreasing from 1.2% to 1.1% to 1.0%. "Wide margin of slack and subdued labor costs put some mild downward pressure on price inflation." Expectations "largely anchored." This is in the 26-35 range — below target, disinflation risk, but not acute deflation spiral. The staff does NOT use words like "deflation risk is acute" — it's more that slack and labor costs are exerting mild downward pressure. Score: ~30.

Direction: BELOW midpoint (50). Score < 50. The rubric range 26-40 = "below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent." Core PCE at ~1% and drifting lower fits squarely in this range, closer to the lower end (26-30).

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees output gap as WELL BELOW the midpoint — substantial slack remains. Stockton explicitly states: "the level of the output gap is not much different at the end of 2012 than in our August projection" — implying the gap persists through the entire forecast horizon. "Wide margin of slack" is explicitly mentioned. GDP growth is ~1¾% in Q2, marked down, "above-trend growth not expected to emerge until the middle of next year." The economy is "generating less income, less spending, and less production" than expected. "The constellation of incoming data indicates that the economy is generating less income, less spending, and less production in the near term than we had been expecting."

Bright-line check: "below potential," "slack remains," "wide margin of slack" → score MUST be < 50. Era anchor for 2010 recovery: 35-42. Given ~9.6% unemployment, repeated downward revisions, stall-speed concerns, and the gap not closing through 2012, this is solidly in the 31-38 range. Score: ~35.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as WELL BELOW the midpoint — very high unemployment, slack-ridden. "Private payrolls averaged 87,000 in July and August, about half the pace that occurred in the spring." Initial claims at 450,000 — "remain at a level consistent with only modest employment gains." Unemployment is ~9.6% (consistent with these data points). NAIRU reference: staff is revising NAIRU upward ("accumulating evidence of a decline in the NAIRU" [actually an increase in NAIRU from structural factors] — though the text is partially truncated here).

Anchor: 15-22 (2009-10, 2020 Q2). With UE ~9.6%, private job gains only 87K/month (well below what's needed to reduce unemployment), initial claims at 450K — this is unambiguously in the 18-25 range. Score: ~22.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. My evidence = very slack labor market. Score should be LOW. Score 22 < 50. Correct direction.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees consumption and investment as BELOW the midpoint — sluggish. Consumer spending: retail sales August upside surprise (+0.6% instead of flat), but "employment expanding only slowly, wages subdued, sentiment at depressed levels." PCE expected to grow "at an annual rate of just over 2 percent in the second half, about ¼ percentage point faster than August forecast." But consumer spending "seems to be muddling along, with few signs of either a pickup or a deceleration." Housing investment: "dismally weak," single-family starts flat/declining, housing demand "damped" by high unemployment. Nonresidential construction: "likely fell" in Q2, "continuing to drift lower." Equipment/software: "slowing sharply" after double-digit gains, projected to grow "just 2 percent" (several points less than August). The overall picture is sluggish — not collapsing (no freefall), but below trend.

Net assessment: Consumer spending is muddling along (~2% PCE growth), business investment is decelerating significantly, housing is dismally weak. Weighted together this is in the 35-42 range ("below trend; sluggish; cautious consumers; soft"). Score: ~38.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
