# FOMC Staff Briefing Scoring — 2005-08-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from a scheduled FOMC meeting on August 9, 2005. It is **segmented** (post-2005 structure): the staff economic briefing is presented by named speakers, distinct from participant go-around content. Key staff speakers are:

- **MR. KOS** (Dino Kos) — Manager of the System Open Market Account; covers financial markets, domestic operations, foreign exchange.
- **MR. WILCOX** (David Wilcox) — Division of Research and Statistics; covers domestic economic outlook, GDP, inflation, labor, the Greenbook projection.
- **MS. JOHNSON** (Karen Johnson) — International Finance; covers foreign economic conditions, trade, exchange rates, oil prices.
- **MS. MOSSER** (Patricia Mosser) — New York Fed; participates in discussion of mortgage hedging and GSE behavior.
- **MR. MADIGAN** (Brian Madigan) — Division of Monetary Affairs; comments briefly on Treasury cheapest-to-deliver issue.

All scoring is based solely on staff presentations. Participant commentary in the go-around is excluded.

### 2. Era Context

August 2005 is mid-cycle Greenspan-era tightening. The FOMC had been raising rates in 25 bps increments since June 2004. The federal funds rate had been raised to approximately 3.25% by this meeting (with the Greenbook now assuming a trajectory to 4.25%). Oil prices had recently surged above $60/barrel (WTI near $61). The U.S. economy was growing at or slightly above trend; the labor market was improving. Housing was strong. The era anchor is: output modestly above potential, inflation at or just above target, financial conditions accommodative, credit spreads near historical lows.

### 3. Dimensional Analysis — Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Staff (Wilcox) describes core PCE running "in the neighborhood of 2 percent or a little above." The official core PCE (including the nonmarket component) was revised up ~0.2 ppt by BEA methodology changes. The nonmarket component now assumed to grow ~3% (vs ~2% assumed before), adding 0.2 ppt to core PCE. Energy prices contributed additional upward force. The core market-based PCE projection was left "unrevised from last Greenbook." The overall core PCE is projected to run at 2.2% in 2006 and 2007. Staff characterizes this as slightly above target but policy-controllable. The trajectory is described as "nudging down" over time. Direction: modestly above target. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" language, 2.2% projection. Score around 55-60. Not extreme; staff is calm about it.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Wilcox explicitly states the GDP gap is **0.9 percent** (positive, above potential). Output is projected "above its potential in 2006 and 2007." This is a bright-line case: score MUST be > 50. The gap is described as modestly positive, not dramatic overheating. Score around 55-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Unemployment rate in August 2005 was approximately 5.0%. Wilcox discusses the labor force participation rate trend being revised downward (steeper decline), which means the effective employment gap is slightly smaller than it appeared. He notes the employment situation is not dramatically tight. However, 5.0% unemployment in 2005 era puts labor near NAIRU (roughly 5.0-5.2% by Fed estimates at the time). Score: near neutral, slightly above 50 — around 50-53. The labor market is approaching balance, neither slack nor tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Motor vehicle sales hit 20 million annual rate in July (very strong, described as "eye-popping"). However, staff expects payback in August/September as consumers who believed discount offers would expire now see extension. Private domestic final demand is described as "hardly revised" on net. Inventory investment stepped down sharply in Q2. The underlying pace of final sales "looks a little stronger" but "not by much." The net picture is moderate-to-solid domestic demand, with a notable near-term auto sales distortion. Score: moderate strength, around 52-55.

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
Wilcox mentions BEA revisions to the NIPA data affecting potential GDP estimates; capital deepening contribution was cut by a quarter point. The participation rate trend was revised. There is discussion of slower capital stock growth reducing potential GDP growth going forward. This implies somewhat below-trend productivity/potential output growth, but staff does not directly characterize productivity as "disappointing" or "strong." The discussion is mostly about methodological revisions to potential, not current productivity dynamics. Score: N/A — productivity is not discussed directly enough to score confidently. The discussion focuses on potential GDP revisions via capital and participation adjustments, not a direct productivity reading.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:**
Kos describes: risk appetites "remained strong," "search for yield remained intact," credit spreads "remains low by historical standards," high-yield spreads narrowed more than 100 bps since early May, MBS spreads "slightly wider than earlier this year but remains low." Equities rose. The overall picture is clearly accommodative/loose financial conditions. Era anchor for 2005-06 is 10-20 (loose). Bright-line: "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50. Score around 20-25, reflecting clearly easy/accommodative conditions.

**TREASURY_STRESS:**
Kos mentions Treasury yields rose ~45 bps since the last meeting. There is a brief discussion of cheapest-to-deliver tightness in the September futures contract (the Feb 2012 note), but Kos characterizes it as a "supply issue" comparable to normal market forces, with "no evidence" of anything like the Salomon squeeze of the early 1990s. The Treasury is looking into it, but there is no described liquidity impairment, auction failure, or market dysfunction. This is normal yield movement from policy expectations. Per the bright-line rule: rising yields from policy expectations = normal (45-55). The cheapest-to-deliver issue warrants slight elevation above pure normal. Score: 40-45, reflecting orderly markets with minor technical concerns.

**CREDIT_SPREADS:**
Kos explicitly states: high-yield spreads narrowed more than 100 bps since early May; "even at recent peaks spreads are at very low levels"; MBS option-adjusted spreads "remain low by historical standards." However, quality has "shown some signs of deterioration" — 40% of high-yield issuance rated B- or lower, 20% financing LBOs (highest since 1989). The spreads are compressed/narrow. Bright-line: "narrow" / "historically tight" → score MUST be < 50. Score around 15-20 given "historically narrow" and "very low levels" language. Era anchor for 2005-06 is 10-20 (extremely compressed).

**EQUITY_VALUATION:**
Kos notes that equities rose during the intermeeting period (along with yields). There is no explicit valuation concern expressed. Staff does not call equities overvalued, frothy, or undervalued. With equities at reasonable levels and rising, and no valuation commentary, default to around 40-48 (no particular concern). Score: 45, slightly below neutral since the market rose without staff flagging distress.

**BANKING_STRESS:**
No direct discussion of banking system health or stress. GSEs (Fannie/Freddie) are discussed in the context of hedging strategies and portfolio changes, but there is no mention of bank capital, NPLs, or bank failures. Score: N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:**
Johnson projects the dollar to depreciate modestly over the long run due to global imbalances. She notes "the real index of the dollar in terms of all our important trading partners is likely to move down on balance over a reasonably long horizon." The renminbi barely moved after the initial 2.1% revaluation. The yen remained "broadly weaker compared to its level at the beginning of the year." This is a mixed picture: yen weak vs. dollar, renminbi essentially unchanged, but staff's long-run forecast is for dollar depreciation. The near-term dollar position is described as mixed. Score: Slight dollar weakness projected; ~40-45 range, leaning toward slight depreciation bias.

**INFLATION SUB-COMPONENTS:**
- INFLATION_GOODS: Not specifically broken out.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: Not specifically discussed separately.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: Not mentioned.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Johnson notes "inflation expectations appear to remain well anchored" abroad. Wilcox's policy discussion implies anchored domestic expectations (core PCE trajectory is "nudging down"). Score: around 45-48 (anchored at/near target, not elevated).

### 4. Polarity Verification

- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = more restrictive. Evidence says accommodative/easy. Score < 50. CHECK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Evidence says compressed/historically narrow. Score < 50. CHECK.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says 0.9% positive gap, above potential. Score > 50. CHECK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says projected depreciation, yen weak vs. dollar (dollar stronger vs. yen), renminbi unchanged. Mixed but modest. Score around 40-45 given the longer-run depreciation bias expressed by staff. CHECK.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. No banking discussion → N/A. CHECK.

### 5. Bright-Line Checks

- OUTPUT_GAP: "GDP gap at 0.9 percent" and "output above its potential in 2006 and 2007" → score MUST be > 50. Score = 57. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Risk appetites remained strong," "search for yield remained intact," spreads low → "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50. Score = 22. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "Remains low by historical standards," "very low levels" → "compressed" → score MUST be < 50. Score = 18. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A. N/A.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Staff projects gradual dollar depreciation; yen weaker vs. dollar (dollar stronger vs. yen) but renminbi unchanged; overall bias is depreciation. Modest net: score = 42. (Below 50 = net depreciation bias). PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No market dysfunction, only normal yield rises from policy expectations and minor cheapest-to-deliver technical issue. Score = 40. PASS (not elevated above 55).

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. OUTPUT_GAP (57) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52): Slight divergence of ~5 points. Consistent with 2005 context where output is modestly above potential but labor is near NAIRU. Wilcox's revision to the participation rate trend reduced the apparent employment gap. Acceptable divergence.

2. OUTPUT_GAP (57) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (54): Close alignment. Both above 50, both modest. Consistent with a moderate-to-solid growth environment.

3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (22) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (18): Both well below 50 — tightly consistent with accommodative, compressed-spread environment. CHECK.

4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (22) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40): FC is very loose, Treasury market orderly (score near 40 = normal functioning). In normal times Treasury stress can be near 40-50 regardless of FC. Consistent.

5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (58) ↔ INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (48): Core PCE at 2.2% is modestly above target, but expectations remain anchored. Slight divergence is appropriate — expectations are anchored even while current readings are modestly elevated. Acceptable.
