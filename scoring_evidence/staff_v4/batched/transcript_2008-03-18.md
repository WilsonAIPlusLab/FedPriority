# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2008-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Stockton (domestic outlook — recession call, Bear Stearns collapse, housing continuing decline), Dudley (financial markets — Bear Stearns demise, PDCF, severe stress, breakeven inflation), Sheets (international — dollar post-Bretton Woods low, commodity surge, foreign resilience).

**Era context**: March 2008 — Bear Stearns collapse/JPM acquisition. PDCF established. Funds rate at 2.25%. Staff now forecasting recession. Oil above $110. Dollar at post-Bretton Woods lows.

**Key evidence**:
- Stockton: "Recession is now more likely than a period of weak growth." But "how much this remains a forecast of recession; a lot has to happen that we haven't seen yet." Series peaking "late last year or early this year." "Private payrolls fell" — "the third consecutive decline." UE "moved above 5 percent." Housing starts fell 6.5% to 707,000. "Recession that we are forecasting is relatively shallow" — tax rebates, exports providing support. UE rises "only 1.25 percentage points, to 5.75 percent."
- Inflation: "Despite marking down our forecast of real activity considerably, we raised our projection of consumer price inflation." Core PCE "probably did not increase" — estimates around 0.15% Feb. "We also incorporated higher oil and higher import prices." "Breakeven rates of inflation have continued to widen." "Inflation expectations have become less well anchored."
- Dudley: Bear Stearns collapse — "old-fashioned bank run." "Customers moving their business elsewhere and investors' unwillingness to roll over their collateralized loans." "Rapidity of the Bear Stearns collapse has had significant contagion effects to the other major U.S. broker-dealers." PDCF established to backstop repo market.
- "Financial markets have been under severe stress." Dollar "fallen more than 5 percent against the major currencies since mid-February and reaching a post-Bretton Woods low." Commodity prices "surged more than 15 percent, briefly reaching $110." "Breakeven rates of inflation have continued to widen."
- Sheets: Foreign GDP "stepped down to 3.2 percent" from 4.5%. But "foreign economies appear to be showing somewhat more resilience than we would have expected." Dollar "broad real dollar to decline at a 3 percent annual rate." Net exports contributing 1.2pp to growth.
