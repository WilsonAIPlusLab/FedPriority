**Era context**: June 2008 — funds rate at 2%. Oil at $136/barrel ("staggering rise"). Commodity prices surging across the board. Staff still projecting mild recession with gradual recovery. UE at 5.3%, heading to 5.7%. Inflation expectations showing signs of becoming unmoored. Core PCE at 2% pace in Q2 but expected to step up to 2.5% in H2.

**Key evidence**:
- Sheets: Oil "spot price of WTI closing yesterday at $136." Far-dated futures at same level. Oil forecast "$50 a barrel higher than at the time of the January chart show." "Staggering rise in oil prices." "Constrained (and price inelastic) oil supply coupled with relatively strong (and price inelastic) oil demand." Nonfuel commodity prices also risen significantly. Corn surging, wheat fallen back, zinc down, copper flat.
- Foreign growth: Stepped down from 4.5% to 3% in Q4 2007, declining to 2.2% current quarter. EMEs "relatively resilient." Dollar: "continue to expect the dollar to depreciate at annual rate" — "just a little weaker than anticipated." Core import prices "surging at annual rate of 10 percent" in Q2 — "fastest pace in decades."
- Inflation abroad: ECB warning may raise rates. BoE tightening rhetoric. EM central banks tightening — Mexico, China, Hungary. "Long-term inflation expectations have recently edged up."
- Slifman: Consumer sentiment "far deeper than can be explained by its usual predictors." BUT spending data stronger than expected — "real outlays for consumer goods...rose rapidly in March and April." "The difference between what people are saying about the economy...and what they apparently are producing and spending." Motor vehicles "appear to be plummeting in June" — "canary in the coal mine" risk. Payroll declines "much smaller than we were expecting."
- Housing: "New and existing single-family homes" still under "downward pressure on prices." House prices fell at annual rate of 6.7%. "Expect home prices to continue declining." Starts projected to stabilize but recovery slow.
- Business investment: Nondefense capital goods shipments rose, orders jumped. Nonresidential structures spending for buildings "projected to be very weak." Drilling/mining component strong due to energy prices.
- GDP: Revised up first two quarters. Real GDP at 1.1% Q1, 1.7% Q2. But judgmental weakness built in for H2. UE at 5.3%, heading to 5.7% by early next year.
- Inflation: Core PCE at 2% annual rate in Q2 (down from expectations). "Step up to a 2.5 percent annual rate in the second half" due to pass-through from energy/commodities/imports. "Higher input costs add more than 0.6 percentage point to core inflation this year." "No signs of acceleration in labor costs." Longer-run inflation expectations "roughly unchanged to higher." Staff included "alternative simulation" with expectations becoming unmoored — core inflation rising to 2.6%.
- Potential output revised up — structural productivity growth and trend hours both higher. GDP gap "starts lower than in the April forecast."
