# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring -- 2018-06-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript -- staff economic briefing. June 2018 meeting.

**Era context**: June 2018. Rate at 1.50-1.75% (hiked to 1.75-2.00%). TCJA + BBA fiscal stimulus in full effect. UE 3.8% -- lowest since 1969. Core PCE at 2%. EM stress rising (Turkey, Argentina, Brazil, Mexico). Italy political risk. Dollar appreciating. Tariffs escalating.

**Staff speakers**:
- MR. MORIN: U.S. outlook -- Economy "continues to expand at an above-trend pace this year" before settling to potential in 2020. GDP revised up "1/2 percentage point faster this year and next" vs December. UE declined to 3.8% -- "lowest level since 1969." Jobless rates declining "across most races and ethnicities." Private payroll gains around 175K. UE expected to "move down into 2019 and to move sideways in 2020." Core PCE 1.8% in April (12-month), but May CPI "increased 0.2 percent" -- revised up core PCE forecast, "12-month core inflation in each of June and September now rounds up to 2 percent." Core PCE projected to "edge up from 1.9 percent this year to 2.1 percent in 2020" -- "a bit above our estimate of its underlying trend." Manufacturing capacity utilization analysis: "gap in the gaps" between manufacturing utilization (low) and broader economy (tight) is "largest on record." But labor in manufacturing "appears stretched" -- workweek "at levels not seen since World War II," overtime elevated, "insufficient supply of local labor or skills" rising sharply. Federal debt as percent of GDP "grown substantially."
- MR. POTTER: Markets -- Contacts "continued to express confidence in the trajectory of the U.S. economy." Treasury yields "slightly lower on net." Dollar "appreciated around 2 percent." S&P 500 "rose over 4 percent." VIX declined. EM currencies under pressure -- Turkey, Argentina largest depreciators. Brazil, Mexico elections pressuring currencies. Dollar appreciation "2 percent" was largest contributor from EM weakness. Italian sovereign spreads to Germany widened, "approaching the highest levels" since euro crisis. MBS market "functioning quite well."
- MR. KAMIN: International outlook -- Foreign growth "maintain that pace" (~3%). AFE Q1 weakness "likely owed to transitory factors." EME growth surprised on upside. Trade risks: tariffs, Section 301, NAFTA. EM financial stresses: Turkey, Argentina hardest hit due to "large fiscal and external deficits, reliance on foreign financing." But "financial pressures on EMEs remain muted compared with previous stress episodes." Italy: new government's "inclination toward fiscal laxity and its ambivalence toward the European Union" keeping markets on edge. Low-for-long interest rates study: bank NIMs would fall in low-for-long but ROAs "would not" fall much, "only minimal effects on overall bank profitability."

**Dimensional analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Core PCE 1.8% (April 12-month), revised up to round to 2% in June and September. Projected to 2.1% in 2020 -- "a bit above our estimate of its underlying trend." Total PCE 2.1% this year. Score: 50 -- at target, hitting 2%.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy "above-trend pace." GDP revised up. UE 3.8% well below natural rate. Output gap widening. Manufacturing "gap in gaps" largest on record but labor resources "stretched." Score: 62 -- clearly above potential, fiscal stimulus driving.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 3.8% -- "lowest level since 1969." Workweek at "levels not seen since World War II." "Insufficient supply of local labor or skills" at highest since late 1970s. Per rubric, UE below 4% should be above 55. Score: 65 -- very tight.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: GDP above-trend. Fiscal stimulus effects large. PCE "rebound" expected from Q1 lull. "First Data support our view of a rebound in PCE growth." Score: 55 -- above trend, fiscal boost.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Morin's manufacturing analysis: low capacity utilization partly due to "subpar pace of overall" growth in manufacturing output and "outsourcing of domestic demand." Plant hours below historical norms suggest structural shift, not slack. Implies productivity question is structural. Score: N/A (discussion was structural composition, not direct productivity assessment).

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Potter: S&P up 4%, VIX declined, contacts confident, dollar appreciated 2%. But EM stress, Italy spreads widening. Overall: "financial conditions ended little changed over the period." Score: 48 -- roughly neutral, mixed signals.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Potter: 10-year "rose above 3.1 percent to levels last observed in the middle of 2011" but then yields "slightly lower on net." No dysfunction described. Score: N/A.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Not discussed for U.S. corporate. EM credit spreads "muted compared with previous stress episodes." Score: N/A (insufficient U.S. corporate spread discussion).

9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: Potter: S&P 500 "rose over 4 percent." Contacts "expressed confidence." "Positive domestic economic backdrop and its implications for corporate earnings." No valuation concern language. Score: N/A (price movement without valuation assessment).

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Kamin: Low-for-long study -- bank ROAs "only minimal effects." "Credit-to-deposit ratios have declined." Bank risk-taking evidence "benign." Score: 28 -- low stress, healthy system.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Potter: Dollar "appreciated around 2 percent." EM currencies major contributor. Gruber (from March context continued): dollar breaking downward trend. Score: 58 -- modest appreciation continuing.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP 62 above potential -- consistent. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 roughly neutral -- consistent with "little changed." BANKING_STRESS 28 healthy -- consistent. DOLLAR_STRENGTH 58 appreciation -- consistent.

**Cross-dimension coherence**: OUTPUT_GAP 62 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS 65 -- aligned. OUTPUT_GAP 62 and CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT 55 -- aligned. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS 48 neutral makes sense -- accommodative equities/low VIX offset by EM stress and dollar appreciation.
