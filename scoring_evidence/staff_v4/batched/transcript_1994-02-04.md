# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 1994-02-04

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff speakers: MR. PRELL (economic outlook, potential output, labor markets), MR. KOHN (short-run policy), MR. FISHER (financial markets). District roundtable: MR. KEEHN (Chicago). Early 1990s unsegmented transcript with OCR truncation.

**Era context**: February 1994. Tightening cycle begins — Fed raised funds rate from 3%. Economy expanding robustly (~4% GDP), unemployment declining (~6.7% new survey), inflation contained.

**Dimension-by-dimension**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Prell: forecast has "a slight down tilt in the inflation rate." Keehn: "sensing some price pressures" — "purchased products are edging up" but "marketplace pressures" limit pass-through. "A subtle change" at the margin. Direction: slightly below neutral. Score ~44.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE at 6.7% new survey (~6.1-6.2% old basis). Workweek at "relatively very high level." NAIRU around 6%. Near equilibrium. Score ~46.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Keehn: auto production 15% above prior year, heavy trucks improving (5-10% above last year), retail "surprisingly strong," Christmas "terrific," farm equipment production +16%. Very strong. Score ~62.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: Prell: potential growth 2.4%, "possibility on the positive side is even stronger than anticipated" for productivity. At-trend. Score ~50.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: 3% funds rate is very accommodative. Prell: debt growth in line with income, "moderate" financing gap. Score ~27.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Fisher: bond market volatile on employment data — normal. Score ~42.

**Bright-line checks**: OUTPUT_GAP above trend → > 50 CHECK. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS accommodative → < 50 CHECK.
