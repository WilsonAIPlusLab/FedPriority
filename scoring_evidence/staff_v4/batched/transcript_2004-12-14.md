# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook). Era: December 2004. Economy growing "modestly above trend." Employment improved — Oct/Nov gains "noticeably stronger than had been incorporated," running ~160K/month, expected to pick up to 225K by spring. Consumer spending surprised to upside — real PCE 5%+ Q3, 3.5% Q4. E&S up 17% Q3, 11% Q4. But high-tech equipment spending weaker — "below our expectations." Staff raised funds rate path 50bp (to 2.75% end of next year, 3.25% end of 2006) to prevent overshooting potential. Key upside risk: asset markets — stock market 4% higher, house prices 5% higher, oil $3 lower. Dollar retraced some decline but still below prior projection. Inflation: core PCE "roughly unchanged at 1½ percent." Risks to inflation — productivity slowdown (Yellen: structural could average 3.25%), pricing power, but compensation steady at 4%. Staff sees risks "potentially more potent" on upside for inflation but "inflation simply is not very sensitive to the output gap."

**Key signals**: Stockton: "the positives for the economic outlook significantly outweighed the negatives." Raised funds rate 50bp — "without an adjustment to our policy assumption would have resulted in actual real GDP overshooting potential." Employment running ~160K, expected 225K by spring. Consumer spending surprised upside. E&S 17% Q3, 11% Q4 but approaching "moment of truth" on partial-expensing effects. High-tech weaker. Oil $3/barrel lower. Stock market 4% higher. House prices 5% higher. Core PCE: "roughly unchanged at 1½ percent over the next two years." But inflation risks: "firms will act more aggressively to raise prices" or "protect their profit margins by slowing productivity." Compensation measures "running a very steady 4 percent." TIPS compensation "steady in recent months." Yellen: structural productivity could be 3.25% — "about equal to the average rate of productivity growth during the last seven years."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core PCE "roughly unchanged at 1½ percent." "Stability in core inflation is brought about by several small offsetting effects." Diminishing slack and productivity slowdown push up; energy price reversal and smaller import price gains push down. Upside risks from pricing power and productivity slowdown more potent. But compensation steady, expectations anchored.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — stable at 1.5% but upside risks more prominent.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 47 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: economy growing above trend. Staff raised funds rate to prevent "overshooting potential." Asset market strength "greater upward pressure." Employment improving. GDP projected to grow above trend with gap closing. Near potential.
- **Direction**: Just below neutral — nearly closed. Bright-line: below but almost at potential.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: hiring "running around 160,000 per month" and "expect it to pick up to around 225,000 per month by the spring." October/November gains "noticeably stronger." But initial claims "increase over the past two weeks" a downside risk. Improvement continuing.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — improving steadily toward balance.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: real PCE "advanced at an annual rate of more than 5 percent in the third quarter" and 3.5% Q4. E&S 17% Q3, 11% Q4. But auto sales weakening. High-tech spending below expectations — "relative prices for tech equipment not declining at the pace they had a few years back." Partial-expensing "moment of truth." Still strong overall.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — robust but some moderation ahead.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "slowing of structural labor productivity" in forecast. High-tech weakness could signal slower tech-driven gains. But Yellen: structural could average 3.25% over next two years. Businesses "continuing to adapt and reorganize" using new technologies. "ICT technologies... continuing to play out." Risk of faster or slower productivity both plausible.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — elevated but uncertain trajectory.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: funds rate at 2% (four hikes done), path raised to 2.75% end of 2005. "Accommodative stance of policy" but becoming "less accommodative." Stock market 4% higher, house prices 5% higher — "boost to household spending." Oil $3 lower. Dollar "still below our previous projection." Fiscal policy shifting to roughly neutral. Multiple offsetting forces.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still accommodative but steadily tightening.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Kos: "spreads are narrower, the yield curve is flatter." Credit conditions still favorable.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — compressed.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 43 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Stock market is about 4 percent higher." Asset market strength an upside risk. Not described as overvalued.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — supportive.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 18 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: dollar "has retraced some of its earlier declines" but "was still below our previous projection." Current account deficit widening. Kos: "betting against the dollar reflects the unsustainability of our deficit." Dollar weakness ongoing, structural concern.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant weakness. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "Inflation expectations also" — TIPS five-year compensation "steady in recent months." "Five- to ten-year inflation compensation" stable. "Survey measures—either short-term or long-term" show no breakout. "Still few signs that this year's price shocks will propagate into sustained higher inflation."
- **Direction**: Near neutral — well anchored.
