# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-08-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Wilcox (economic outlook), Johnson (international/oil). Era: August 2004. Soft patch emerging. "A lot has changed since the June Greenbook." Employment report disappointed — only 32,000 private payroll gain in July, well below expected 300,000/month pace. GDP growth trimmed from 3.9% to 3.3% Q3. Core PCE inflation "moderation we were looking for seems to be in train" — slowing from 1.75% this year to 1.5% next. Oil prices surging — WTI above $44, up $9 since June. Major oil market discussion: Yukos/Russia risk, Iraq violence, strong global demand. But staff still sees "above-trend growth" resuming. Manufacturing IP still a bright spot. Deficit in hours interpreted as "mainly about productivity in the current quarter rather than output."

**Key signals**: Wilcox: "we still see overall macroeconomic conditions as likely to support a return to above-trend growth." But "taken quite a bit out of our forecasts for payroll employment." GDP forecast trimmed to 3.3% Q3. "Private nonfarm payrolls" only 32,000 plus downward revision to June. Manufacturing employment "looked, if anything, a little better than we had expected." Core PCE "slowing from 1¾ percent this year to 1½ percent next year." Oil: WTI above $44, "strong global demand for oil has contributed importantly." Johnson: growth abroad "little changed." Oil discussion extensive — Russia/Yukos, Iraq, Nigeria, refinery capacity. Futures curve slopes down but market keeps being wrong.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: core CPI "moderation we were looking for seems to be in train." Core PCE slowing from 1.75% to 1.5% next year. "Good enough to cause us to shave our projection" slightly. But oil prices surging — adding to headline. Energy price pass-through continuing. Still above 2003 levels.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — inflation has risen from 2003 lows but moderating from H1 spike.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: GDP gap had "narrowed to just ½ percent" but soft patch has reopened it. GDP trimmed to 3.3% Q3. Still see above-trend growth eventually. Employment shortfall means gap not closing as fast as expected.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still below potential, closure delayed.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: "only a 32,000 advance in private nonfarm payrolls, a downward revision to June employment of 51,000." "Misery over coming up short nearly 300,000 jobs." But payrolls still expected to track "into the neighborhood of 300,000 in the fourth quarter." Soft patch, not a fundamental deterioration.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — disappointing but temporary.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: consumer spending — "step-down in the growth of real activity." Tax-cut stimulus fading. Oil prices draining purchasing power. But still above-trend growth expected to resume. Exports expanded "almost 12½ percent." Manufacturing still rising. Investment data mixed.
- **Direction**: Near/slightly above neutral — softened from June's strong reading.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: "deficit in hours relative to our expectation informed us mainly about productivity in the current quarter rather than output." Productivity holding up as firms produce more with less hiring. Structural trend still elevated.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — still strong.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Funds rate at 1.25% (one hike done). Oil price surge acts as a drag. Still relatively accommodative overall. Long rates have moved up. Staff projects "moderate rise in the federal funds rate."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — still accommodative but less so.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not extensively discussed. Credit conditions still favorable from prior meetings. No indication of deterioration.
- **Direction**: Below neutral.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not extensively discussed. Market processing soft patch and oil shock.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: dollar path incorporated into forecast. Current account deficit widening. Trade deficit significant. Dollar still weak overall.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — ongoing weakness.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Oil price surge affecting near-term expectations. But core moderation suggests expectations not unanchored. Staff expects inflation to edge down.
- **Direction**: At neutral — balanced.
