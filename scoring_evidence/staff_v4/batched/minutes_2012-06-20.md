# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-06-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes, post-2005 segmented format.

**Era context**: June 2012. Economy slowing again. UE 8.2% but job gains slowing. European crisis intensifying (Spain bank bailout, Greek elections). Oil prices falling. Financial conditions tightening. Staff revised down GDP forecast. Maturity extension program ongoing.

**Directional signals**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "Consumer price inflation declined." Energy prices falling. Core "increased moderately." "nominal wages continued to be subdued." Staff: "inflation would be subdued through 2014." Below target.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "economic activity was expanding at a somewhat more modest pace than earlier in the year." Staff "revised down somewhat the medium-term forecast." "wide margin of slack in labor and product markets only slowly." Below potential.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE 8.2%. "Private nonfarm employment rose at a slower pace in April and May." "rate of long-duration unemployment remained very high." "indicators of job openings and firms' hiring plans were mixed." Still very weak.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Nominal retail sales "declined" in April-May. E&S "increased moderately." "real disposable income rose at a subdued pace." Housing "generally improved." Consumer sentiment "lower in early June" and "continued to be subdued." Below trend.

5. **PRODUCTIVITY**: "with small gains in productivity" — brief mention, unit labor costs rose only slightly.

6. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Growing concerns about developments in the euro area and weaker-than-expected economic data both weighed on financial markets." Equities declined. Spreads widened. "risks to the U.S. financial system emanating from strains in Europe appeared to increase." Tightening.

7. **TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields "moved down substantially." 10-year reached "historically low level." Flight to safety. No dysfunction.

8. **CREDIT_SPREADS**: "spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened a bit." But still "low by historical standards." Slight widening.

9. **EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes declined." Option-implied volatility "rose." Bank equity "significantly underperformed." Declining.

10. **BANKING_STRESS**: Large trading loss at a major bank. Rating downgrades expected. But C&I loans increasing. Core loans expanded. CRE "remained strained." Elevated stress.

11. **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Dollar index "ended the intermeeting period up moderately." Appreciation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer price inflation declined"; consumer prices "flat in April and then fell in May"; core "increased moderately"; "nominal wages continued to be subdued"; staff: "inflation would be subdued through 2014"
- Direction: Below neutral — declining headline, moderate core, subdued wages.

**OUTPUT_GAP: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "economic activity was expanding at a somewhat more modest pace"; staff "revised down...the medium-term forecast"; "growth rate of real GDP would not materially exceed that of potential output until 2014"; "wide margin of slack"
- Direction: Below neutral — slowing growth, wide and persistent slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: UE "8.2 percent"; "Private nonfarm employment rose at a slower pace"; "rate of long-duration unemployment remained very high"; "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was little changed"
- Direction: Well below neutral — job growth decelerating from already slow pace.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Nominal retail sales "declined" in April-May; E&S "increased moderately"; "real disposable income rose at a subdued pace"; housing "generally improved"; consumer sentiment "subdued"
- Direction: Below trend — weakening consumption with slow income growth.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 48 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "small gains in productivity"
- Direction: Near neutral — modest.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 58 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Growing concerns about developments in the euro area...weighed on financial markets"; CRE "remained strained"; mortgage markets "continued to be tight"; "risks to the U.S. financial system emanating from strains in Europe appeared to increase"
- Direction: Above neutral — tightening from European crisis and domestic weakness.

**TREASURY_STRESS: 38 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Yields "moved down substantially"; 10-year "reached a historically low level"; flight-to-safety rally; no dysfunction.
- Direction: Below neutral — strong safe-haven demand, orderly markets.

**CREDIT_SPREADS: 43 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "spreads...widened a bit"; but yields "remained low by historical standards"; C&I loans and commercial paper "increased"; syndicated loans "appeared to remain solid"
- Direction: Below neutral — slight widening but still favorable levels.

**EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes declined"; option-implied volatility "rose"; bank equity "significantly underperformed"
- Direction: Below neutral — declining prices.

**BANKING_STRESS: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Disclosure of a large trading loss at a major U.S. bank"; expected credit rating downgrades for 5 large banks; but core loans expanding; lending conditions "eased slightly"; European bank indicators deteriorating
- Direction: Above neutral — elevated stress from idiosyncratic events and European spillovers.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Dollar index "ended the intermeeting period up moderately"
- Direction: Above neutral — moderate appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Energy prices declining considerably; food "edged up"; core goods moderate.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 35 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: Not separately detailed; core "increased moderately."

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "longer-term inflation expectations in the survey remained stable"
