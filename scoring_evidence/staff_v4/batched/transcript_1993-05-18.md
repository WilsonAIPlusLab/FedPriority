## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type and Structure

This is an **early transcript** (May 18, 1993) — individual staff speakers identified by name. The format is **unsegmented / early-era**: the two-column photocopied transcript is partially truncated on the left margin throughout, so some sentence beginnings are missing. Staff presentations are largely in appendices (not reproduced inline); what is available is the Q&A exchanges after each presentation plus Chairman Greenspan's extended analytical commentary during the Prell session. This is characteristic of early-1990s FOMC transcripts.

**Staff speakers identified:**
- **MR. PRELL** (Chief Economist / Research Director) — economic outlook, Greenbook, inflation, output, NAIRU, expectations
- **MS. GREENE** (Deputy Manager, Foreign Operations) — foreign exchange desk, yen intervention
- **MR. MCDONOUGH** (Manager, Domestic Desk) — domestic operations, bond market, fiscal policy market expectations
- **CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN** — provides extended analytical commentary alongside Prell on the inflation puzzle (treated as informed staff-adjacent commentary; scored here because it directly analyzes the Greenbook data and is staff-level analytical content, though I will weight Prell's own responses more heavily)

Participant "go-around" follows (MR. HOENIG, MR. BROADDUS, MR. PARRY, MR. KEEHN, etc.) — these are **participant views**, excluded from scoring.

### 2. Era Context

May 1993: Early Clinton administration, economy in post-1990-91 recession recovery phase. Unemployment still elevated (~7%). The key puzzle at this meeting is an **unexpected acceleration in inflation** (core CPI running ~4.5% annual rate in early 1993) alongside **weaker-than-expected real growth** in Q1 1993 — a stagflation-like surprise. The Greenbook revised real GDP growth DOWN (~2.2%) and inflation UP simultaneously. The funds rate is 3% — historically low.

### 3. Directional Signals by Dimension (Pre-Score)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint — meaningfully elevated and accelerating unexpectedly. Prell discusses the "acceleration in activity and ... productivity" puzzle, the Greenbook projecting higher inflation; Greenspan notes "the Board's staff is reducing [its projection of] the ECI ... for the second quarter" (a partial offset), but the core issue is an "uptick in inflation." Core CPI annualizing ~4.5% over first months of 1993 (Broaddus cites this as a participant). Prell acknowledges "carrying through higher levels of inflation throughout and moderating somewhat." This is clearly above target (no 2% PCE target articulation yet in 1993, but price stability is the goal) and concerning. The main debate is over the *source* of the surprise, not whether inflation is elevated.

Magnitude: The acceleration is "surprising," "disturbing," but not hyperinflationary. Core CPI ~4.5% annualized is notably above desired price stability but not extreme. Score will be in the 60-68 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Staff sees output as BELOW potential. Prell references "excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market slack." Greenspan notes the Q1 surprise was "real output growth [in the first quarter] was much weaker than expected." The Greenbook revised real GDP down to ~2.2% from ~3%. There is slack. This is below-potential territory.

Magnitude: Slack "remains" but economy is in recovery — not deep recession. Unemployment ~7% (late 1993 context). Score in the 37-43 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Staff sees labor as BELOW full employment. Prell explicitly notes "the labor market slack issue." Greenspan says "there's excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market." Unemployment in mid-1993 was approximately 7% — well above NAIRU estimates. This is clearly a loose labor market. Score in the 28-35 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Staff sees spending/investment as BELOW trend / sluggish. The Q1 surprise was weak real output with unexpected inflation. Greenbook revised growth down. Multiple references to businesses holding back on investment ("on hold because of expectations about taxes," "uncertainty about fiscal policy"). Consumer confidence mentioned but uncertain. Overall weak real demand context. Score in the 35-42 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
Prell responds to a question noting "the typical cyclical pattern of profits moving up with an acceleration in activity and a related jump in productivity. I assume we've had a rather typical cyclical pattern in that respect." This is a brief, neutral-to-positive reference — productivity behaving normally for the cycle. Score ~50 or N/A given the brevity; will score as Moderate confidence at ~48.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:**
Staff sees financial conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE / loose. The 3% funds rate is described as providing "sufficient stimulus to offset the fiscal drag." McDonough's bond market discussion suggests rates have been low and supportive. Prell: "at that level we are providing sufficient stimulus." This is clearly accommodative — below 50 on the FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS scale (lower = more accommodative). Score in the 28-38 range.

Bright-line check: "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50. Confirmed.

**TREASURY_STRESS:**
McDonough discusses bond market extensively — rates stable, market "somewhat confused" about fiscal deficit path but no dysfunction. "There wasn't much of a move in rates" on the debt maturity shortening. Bond market reactions discussed as normal price adjustments, not dysfunction. Score around 35-42 (normal functioning, some uncertainty but orderly).

**CREDIT_SPREADS:**
Not directly discussed by staff. Banking is mentioned as highly profitable ("Banking has been extremely profitable"; record earnings), implying credit conditions are generally favorable. No mention of spread widening. N/A or infer below 50. Will score as N/A given lack of direct staff commentary.

**EQUITY_VALUATION:**
Not discussed by staff. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS:**
Prell does not discuss banking system directly in the Q&A. Greenspan's comments and the go-around mention bank earnings at record levels. Hoenig (participant): "Banking has been extremely profitable; bank earnings are at record levels." But this is a participant comment. Prell's brief discussion of credit aggregates notes "we don't see it in terms of rapid money growth on average." No staff-attributed banking stress discussion. Will score N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:**
Greene discusses the dollar/yen intervention episode. The dollar had been WEAKENING vs yen prior to intervention; intervention was intended to strengthen the dollar. Post-intervention, the dollar stabilized/strengthened. Greenspan notes the intervention was to defend against dollar weakness. The overall characterization is that the dollar was under pressure (weak), partly forestalled by intervention. Score below 50 (dollar weakness direction).

Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation/weakening → score MUST be < 50. The pre-intervention dollar was weak. Score ~30-38.

### 6. Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32)**: Both below 50, consistent. Output below potential and labor slack. Gap of 6 points is acceptable; labor slightly lower reflecting ~7% unemployment.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50, consistent. Q1 real growth disappointment matches weak spending/investment.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (N/A)**: No direct spread data available; N/A is consistent with accommodative financial conditions.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (38)**: Loose financial conditions + normal Treasury functioning are consistent (no stress in either).

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (65) ↔ inflation sub-dimensions**: The 4.5% core CPI rate, protectionist steel price increases, owner-equivalent rent pop, medical services — these suggest broad-based if puzzling inflation somewhat above 65. Services and goods both elevated. Consistent.

**Notable tension**: INFLATION_PRESSURE (65) is elevated while OUTPUT_GAP (38) is below potential — the classic "stagflation puzzle" that is the central topic of this meeting. Staff explicitly grapples with this. Prell: "one could argue that it's a supply shock... supply conditions [are] a possible explanation." This is not a coherence error in scoring; it reflects the actual data and staff discussion. Both scores correctly reflect what the text says.

### 7. Inflation Sub-components

- **INFLATION_SERVICES**: Medical services prices rising, owner-equivalent rent "popped up." Services inflation elevated. ~62-68.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING**: Owner-equivalent rent explicitly discussed as having "popped up" and rising rate-of-return ratio. ~60-65.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 65 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PRELL (responding to questions): "we were carrying through higher levels of inflation throughout and moderating somewhat further the tradeoff; in a sense, we were getting less bang for the unemployment [buck]" and acknowledged the Greenbook revised inflation upward with real growth revised down.
- MR. PRELL on owner-equivalent rent: "a not insignificant part of the April CPI is a big increase in owner equivalent rent, which popped up... the rate of return estimate—that has been rising fairly significantly of late." Greenspan (analytical): "core CPI rate, as I calculate it, now has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate over the first months of the year."

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff confronts an unexpected acceleration in inflation — core CPI ~4.5% annualized in early 1993, with broad-based pressures from steel (protectionism), medical services, owner-equivalent rent, and automotive prices — clearly above any price stability objective and explicitly described as "disturbing" and a puzzle given continued slack.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because staff describes core CPI running at ~4.5% annual rate with broad-based components and unexplained upside acceleration — well above price stability.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (analytical): "with the excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market... to argue that there is something of significance working [to push inflation] ... questions that we have here."
- MR. PRELL: "the bigger driver in this was simply a recognition that the trends had not been developing as we had anticipated and we were carrying through higher levels of inflation throughout and moderating somewhat further the tradeoff." Q1 real GDP weaker than expected; Greenbook growth revised to ~2.2%.

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy in early 1993 is recovering from the 1990-91 recession but remains clearly below potential, with excess capacity, elevated unemployment (~7%), and Q1 real growth disappointing to the downside. Slack is the baseline characterization even as the inflation puzzle is debated.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because "slack remains," "excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market," and Q1 real growth came in weaker than expected. MUST be < 50. Score 38 satisfies this.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 31 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "with the excess capacity especially abroad and the labor market... there is something there, especially the size of the defense adjustment that's going on."
- MR. PRELL (on NAIRU discussion): "feel that in effect the NAIRU has been raised... the gap is small—[but] the tradeoff could be affected if the expectations formation process is different than the backward-looking version that's inherent in a simple Phillips curve model." Prell discusses labor market slack in context of why unemployment hasn't suppressed inflation.

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly below full employment in May 1993. Unemployment was approximately 7% nationally. Prell explicitly discusses "labor market slack issue" and the difficulty of explaining inflation despite that slack. Labor tightness is LOW — anchored in the 28-35 range for ~7% unemployment.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because explicit "labor market slack" language and unemployment ~7% — well above any estimate of full employment. Score 31 satisfies MUST be < 50 requirement.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PRELL: "coming into the first quarter we [expected] initially expected growth in the high 2 percent area for the quarter [but] real output growth [in the first quarter] was much weaker than expected." Greenbook revised to ~2.2% GDP growth.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (regional context from go-around): "businesses are thinking about or have put [plans] on hold because of expectations about taxes... businesses are focusing now more than ever on cost containment and trying to get their profits up." (This captures the investment channel from the staff's Greenbook context.)

**Center-of-gravity**: Real demand was disappointing in Q1 1993, with businesses deferring investment due to fiscal uncertainty and consumers facing headwinds. The Greenbook revised growth down significantly. Consumption/investment is below trend — the stagflation puzzle is precisely that inflation accelerated despite weak real demand.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because Q1 real output came in "much weaker than expected" and Greenbook revised real GDP down to ~2.2%, with businesses holding back investment due to fiscal uncertainty.

**Center-of-gravity**: Prell views productivity as behaving in line with normal cyclical patterns — no particular concern flagged. Brief and incidental reference. Scored at neutral given vague but positive/normal characterization.

**Direction confirmation**: Productivity is at/near neutral because staff characterizes it as following "typical cyclical pattern" without specific concern or praise.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Score: 32 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PRELL: "at that level we are providing sufficient stimulus to offset the fiscal drag and other drags in the system now." (Referring to the 3% funds rate.)
- MR. PRELL: "the higher inflation trend and somewhat higher short-run inflation expectations than we had been expecting earlier do imply a somewhat lower real short-term interest rate. The question is whether at that level we are providing sufficient stimulus."

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff characterizes the current policy stance (3% funds rate) as accommodative/stimulative, providing sufficient support to offset fiscal drag. Financial conditions are loose/accommodative — a classic "supportive" environment. This is clearly below 50 on the tightness scale.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative) because the 3% funds rate is described as "providing sufficient stimulus" and the real rate has been lowered by higher-than-expected inflation. MUST be < 50. Score 32 satisfies this.

Yield movements discussed as normal price adjustments to policy and fiscal expectations. Normal functioning — not stressed.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral/slightly below because bond market functioning is described as orderly with normal yield movements. Score 38 satisfies the bright-line rule (no dysfunction language).

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH

**Score: 30 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. GREENE: Describes dollar weakness vs yen, foreign purchases liquidating US securities and converting to yen; intervention was undertaken because the dollar was declining: "the market did not expect intervention, meaning it was net short [dollars]" — dollar was being sold/shorted.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (on intervention): Describes "intervention moderately in the market but visibly" to reverse dollar decline; "it may have forestalled an erosion of some of the gains that had occurred earlier in the day as a consequence of the intervention." Net assessment: dollar was depreciating/weak before and around the intervention period.

**Center-of-gravity**: The dollar was under pressure and depreciating against the yen, necessitating coordinated intervention. While intervention stabilized conditions somewhat, the overall characterization is dollar weakness. Score below 50.

**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar was depreciating vs yen, prompting intervention. "Dollar under pressure," market "net short" dollars. MUST be < 50. Score 30 satisfies this.

Slightly above 50 (anchored near target with modest firming at the margin).
