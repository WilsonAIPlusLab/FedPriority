# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-05-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Stockton (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: May 2002. GDP surged 5.75% Q1 but driven by inventory correction (3pp). "Cognitive dissonance" — markets negative but staff forecast unchanged/improved. Productivity still surging. Final sales growth raised. Core PCE 1.25%. Markets and staff converging on outlook. Dollar depreciating. Business investment still weak but consumer resilient. Staff projects GDP slowing to 2% Q2 then gradual tightening.

**Key signals**: Stockton: GDP 5.75% Q1 but "more than 3 percentage points of the increase reflected less rapid liquidation of inventories." "Staff projection for real GDP this year is about unchanged" but final sales raised and inflation lowered. Productivity: "jump in productivity" — estimated 1.5% Q1, structural revised up. Core PCE "1¼ percent both this year and next." Johnson: "lower our projected path for the exchange value of the dollar" — declining 2% from Greenbook. Dollar still elevated but declining. "Mixed feelings."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: core PCE 1.25% both years. "Virtually all of the major measures of total and core inflation have moved lower." Productivity growth and larger output gap pushing inflation down further. "Few signs of any notable inflation pressures."
- **Direction**: Below neutral — subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "economy has had and will have a larger margin of excess capacity than previously projected." GDP surged Q1 on inventories but underlying growth modest. Gap wider than previously thought due to supply-side revisions.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "job picture is beginning to improve" and "unemployment rate is near a peak." But hiring still subdued. "Businesses responded swiftly and aggressively" in cutting — now slowly adding back.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack, early improvement.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: consumer spending "½ percentage point faster than we had anticipated." Housing "more than offset somewhat weaker business investment." But E&S recovery "slightly longer to develop." Nonresidential construction "even greater retrenchment." GDP slowing to 2% Q2.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — consumption strong but business spending weak.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 73 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: productivity jumping 5.5-8.5% in Q4 and Q1. Structural revised up. "If productivity were to turn out stronger than in our forecast, growth of real GDP would be higher as would corporate profits, and inflation would come in lower."
- **Direction**: Well above neutral — exceptional.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Stockton: "real rates set at very accommodative levels." "Another dose of fiscal stimulus." Stock market "off 8 percent" and "Nasdaq dropped an even larger 16 percent." Financial conditions mixed — rates accommodative but equity weakness and corporate uncertainty.
- **Direction**: Slightly above neutral — market decline offsetting rate support. Tighter than neutral.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: continuing to "lower our projected path for the exchange value of the dollar." Declining broadly but still above long-run equilibrium. Swiss franc attracting safe-haven flows.
- **Direction**: Slightly above neutral — still elevated but declining.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Inflation very subdued. Expectations consistent with continued low inflation. Not directly discussed.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.
