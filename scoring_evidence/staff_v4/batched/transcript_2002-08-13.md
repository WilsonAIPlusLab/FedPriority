# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2002-08-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Staff: Wilcox (economic outlook), Johnson (international). Era: August 2002. WorldCom/Enron fallout. "Sharp decline and heightened volatility in equity prices" — European equities particularly hard hit. "General reduction in the willingness on the part of investors to bear risk." S&P down 8%, Nasdaq down 16% intermeeting. GDP growth revised down ~0.6pp. Potential GDP growth revised down (productivity adjustment). But revision split: about 0.3pp from demand weakness, 0.3-0.4pp from lower potential. Manufacturing output soft. Labor market weak. Brazil crisis unfolding. Foreign central banks holding off tightening.

**Key signals**: Wilcox: GDP growth revised down "about 0.3 this year and 0.4 next year." "About 0.3 of the roughly 1 percentage point revision for the second half reflects the adjustment to our trend rate of growth of potential GDP." Johnson: "sharp decline and heightened volatility in equity prices over the intermeeting period was a common feature across most global stock markets." "General reduction in the willingness on the part of investors to bear risk seems to be widespread." European insurance firms "particularly penalized." Brazil IMF program $30 billion. "Less favorable financial conditions will weigh on confidence and spending abroad."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Inflation subdued. Potential GDP revised down — partly offsetting gap effects. Core PCE still low ~1%. No inflation concerns despite corporate accounting scandals.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — subdued.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: GDP growth revised down. Gap widening slightly. Manufacturing output soft. Recovery disappointing. Still below potential.
- **Direction**: Below neutral. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: "workweek" dropped sharply — "very large drop." Hours worked declining. Employment flat to declining. Initial claims elevated. Manufacturing employment weak.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — significant slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Consumer spending resilient but business sector still weak. Stock market crash weighing on confidence. GDP growth revised down. Corporate scandal effects damaging business confidence.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — recession hangover deepening.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Wilcox: structural productivity revised up but cyclical productivity strong as firms squeeze more from existing workforces. Potential GDP growth revised, incorporating better productivity estimates.
- **Direction**: Above neutral.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: "sharp decline and heightened volatility in equity prices." "General reduction in the willingness on the part of investors to bear risk." Corporate governance crisis. European equity prices "particularly hard hit." Risk spreads widening. Brazil crisis. Despite accommodative rates, financial conditions tightened significantly.
- **Direction**: Above neutral — tighter conditions. Equity crash and risk aversion dominating.

### TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
### CREDIT_SPREADS: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: "reduction in willingness to bear risk." Corporate bond spreads widening amid Enron/WorldCom accounting scandals. Risk aversion "widespread."
- **Direction**: Above neutral — widening.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 58 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: S&P down 8%, Nasdaq down 16% intermeeting. Corporate accounting scandals. "European equity prices were particularly hard hit."
- **Direction**: Above neutral — stressed/declining.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Johnson: "long rates in the other major industrial countries generally moved down less than did those in U.S." Dollar depreciating as US corporate crisis dampens confidence. Swiss franc gaining safe-haven flows.
- **Direction**: Below neutral — weakening.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Not directly discussed. Inflation expectations stable at low levels. Corporate crisis not affecting inflation outlook.
- **Direction**: Near neutral.
