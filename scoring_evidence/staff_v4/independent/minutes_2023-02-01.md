# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2023-02-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. Staff content is clearly separated into "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. I will score ONLY staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
February 2023: The Fed is deep into its most aggressive tightening cycle in decades. Inflation has peaked (mid-2022) and is coming down but remains well above target. The labor market is historically tight with unemployment at 3.5%. GDP growth has been volatile quarter to quarter. The dollar had strengthened significantly in 2022 but has started to weaken. No banking crisis yet (SVB is March 2023).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE midpoint. Total PCE at 5.0% over 12 months, core at 4.4%. "Continued to step down" but "still elevated." Inflation is clearly well above the 2% target but coming down from peak. The word "elevated" appears repeatedly. The staff forecasts total PCE at 2.8% and core at 3.2% for 2023, reaching near 2% in 2025. Current readings are high but decelerating. Per the rubric gotcha, I score what staff reports NOW — inflation is elevated. This maps to the 61-75 range ("clearly above target; broadening pressures; persistent"). Given 5.0% total and 4.4% core, with clear deceleration language ("stepped down," "eased," "lower than"), this is in the lower part of that range or upper part of 51-60. The deceleration is notable but levels remain far above target. I'll place this at 68.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as operating ABOVE potential, but the signal is mixed. Real GDP grew at 2.9% in Q4, but real PDFP was only 0.2% — "subdued." Growth was driven by volatile inventory investment and net exports. The staff projects GDP growth to "slow markedly this year" and output to "move down to the staff's estimate of potential near the end of 2025." The labor market is tight (UE at 3.5%), which implies above potential. The staff projects unemployment to "gradually move up to the staff's estimate of its natural rate at the end of 2025." So the CURRENT position is above potential (tight labor market, UE below natural rate), but the underlying demand (PDFP) is very weak. Bright-line check: economy is above potential currently (UE below natural rate) — score MUST be > 50. But just barely above given the weak PDFP. Score: 54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT — clearly above midpoint. UE at 3.5% ("historical low"), job openings "remained high," nominal wage growth "continued to be elevated" (ECI 5.1%, AHE 4.6%). Some signs of moderation (wage growth "slowed at the end of last year"). Per the rubric, UE below 4% should be above 55. With 3.5% UE and elevated wage growth, this maps to 66-80 ("very tight"). Score: 70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. Real PDFP increased at only 0.2% — "subdued." Manufacturing output "declined sizably." Real GDP was 2.9% but driven by inventories and net exports, not final demand. The staff outlook describes "sluggish growth in real private domestic spending expected this year." This maps to 31-45 ("below trend; sluggish"). Score: 38.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as TIGHT — above midpoint. "Businesses and households continued to face elevated borrowing costs." "Lending standards tightened further for bank-dependent borrowers." SLOOS shows tightening C&I and CRE lending standards. Credit "remained tight for lower-rated borrowers." Mortgage rates "remained well above their levels in the previous tightening cycle." The staff outlook references "persistently tight financial conditions." Bright-line check: tight/restrictive language — score MUST be > 50. This is clearly tight with multiple reinforcing signals. Score: 63.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Nominal Treasury yields were "little changed" / "roughly unchanged." Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. This is normal market functioning. Score: 40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed signals. Investment-grade and high-yield spreads "narrowed somewhat." Municipal bond spreads "narrowed substantially." But lending standards tightened, and "credit remained tight for lower-rated borrowers." Leveraged loan issuance "notably subdued." Corporate bond issuance "subdued in December." CMBS issuance "remained slow." The staff notes "valuation pressures had eased for corporate bonds and leveraged loans, as spreads in both markets had increased from recent lows." So market spreads narrowed during the intermeeting period but remain wider than recent lows (which were very compressed). Credit availability is tightening. Net position: modestly above normal — some stress. Score: 55.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "forward price-to-earnings ratio for S&P 500 firms remained above its median value despite the decline in equity prices over the past year." "Asset valuation pressures remained notable." Stock prices were "slightly higher." The staff is expressing concern about elevated valuations, not depressed valuations. This maps to 31-45 ("modestly above fair value; somewhat elevated"). Per the rubric, this dimension is about stress/concern — but the concern here is about overvaluation, not undervaluation. The rubric places overvaluation concern at 1-30 range. "Notable" valuation pressures with P/E above median — score: 30.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff characterizes "financial vulnerabilities of the U.S. financial system as moderate." "Risk-based capital ratios for banks increased slightly." Business leverage "at or near a historically high level" but "ability of firms to service their debt has kept pace." Vulnerabilities associated with financial leverage "remained moderate." Funding risks "characterized as moderate." Banks are lending with tightening standards but system is functioning. This is a moderate-stress picture with some concerns but no acute stress. Bright-line check: no "well-capitalized/sound/healthy" language that would push below 50, and no "stressed/troubled" language. The repeated "moderate" vulnerabilities plus tightening standards suggests neutral to slightly above. Score: 48.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad dollar index declined over the intermeeting period." "Improved economic outlook in China and Europe... weighed on the exchange value of the dollar." "Narrowing yield differentials... also contributed to the depreciation of the dollar." Clear depreciation language. Bright-line check: depreciation/weakening — score MUST be < 50. Score: 33.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Core goods inflation is projected to "move down further this year and then remain subdued." The supply-demand imbalances in goods markets are "expected to further unwind." Goods disinflation is underway. Score: 42 (still some residual pressure but clearly easing).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Housing services inflation was expected to peak later this year and then move down." "Core nonhousing services inflation was forecast to slow as nominal wage growth eased." Services inflation remains elevated given tight labor markets and elevated wage growth. Score: 68.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing services inflation "expected to peak later this year and then move down." Still rising toward peak — high pressure currently. Score: 72.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-term inflation expectations... remained within the range of their values reported in recent months." "Near-term measures of inflation expectations... moved down along with actual inflation." Expectations appear well-anchored with near-term moving down. Score: 52 (anchored, near target, with slight upside given recent history).

### Cross-Dimension Coherence

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70)**: Divergence of 16 points. Explanation: The labor market is a lagging indicator. The tight labor market reflects accumulated momentum, while the weak PDFP (0.2%) signals the economy is already softening in real-time demand terms. The staff itself projects this gap will close as unemployment rises toward its natural rate. This divergence is expected and well-explained.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38)**: Divergence of 16 points. Explanation: GDP was driven by volatile inventories and net exports, not final demand. PDFP was 0.2% — very weak. The output gap reflects the overall economy still being above potential (due to tight labor market), while consumption/investment specifically captures the weakness in private domestic spending. This is a genuine and important divergence in the data.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (63) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (55)**: Co-moving — both above neutral. Consistent. Gap of 8 points is acceptable; financial conditions are tighter than spread levels alone because borrowing costs are elevated across the board.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (63) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC is tight but Treasury market is functioning normally. This is entirely expected — tight financial conditions from rate hikes but no Treasury dysfunction.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (68) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (42), Services (68), Housing (72), Expectations (52). Weighted average heavily toward services/housing given staff emphasis = roughly consistent with 68 headline.
