# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary). It is **unsegmented pre-2005** format, meaning staff and participant content are intermingled. I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by cues such as "the staff forecast," "the information reviewed," "the staff noted," and factual data summaries that precede participant discussion paragraphs.

### Era Context
Mid-2004: The economy was in a solid recovery phase following the 2001 recession and the slow "jobless recovery" of 2002-2003. The Fed had kept rates at 1% for an extended period and was about to begin a measured tightening cycle. This meeting raised the funds rate to 1.25%. Inflation had been very low but was beginning to pick up. Unemployment was still elevated around 5.5%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint. Evidence: "Core consumer price inflation picked up, reflecting in part the pass-through of substantial advances in energy prices and non-energy import prices." "Consumer price inflation turned up this year from the very low rates registered in 2003, both for overall measures and for those that exclude food and energy." "The step-up in core inflation was also due in part to the pass-through of higher energy and import costs into core consumer prices." Staff forecast: "Core inflation was projected to fall back later this year from its pace in the first five months and to remain low in 2005, as the transitory effects of higher energy and non-oil import prices waned."

Per the rubric gotcha, transitory language does NOT reduce the score — score what staff reports NOW. Current readings show a clear pickup from very low levels. The phrase "turned up" and "picked up" from very low 2003 levels suggests moving from below-target toward target or slightly above. Labor costs also turning up. But the staff forecast still calls it relatively low and expects it to fall back. The level is probably in the 1.5-2% range for core PCE. This places it around the 50 mark or modestly above.

Self-check: "Somewhat" / "picked up" / "turned up" = modest movement. Starting from "very low rates" in 2003. The language suggests moving from below target toward or slightly above target. Score: 53. This reflects the pickup but acknowledges the starting point was low.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation picked up, pass-through of energy/import costs, labor costs turning up. Score above 50 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees the economy as still BELOW potential but closing fast. Evidence: "Capacity utilization moved higher but stayed below its long-run average." The unemployment rate at 5.25-5.5% was above estimates of NAIRU (probably around 5%). The staff forecast describes the economy "moving toward full utilization of its resources" (from the participant section, but echoing staff). The staff says expansion is "solid" and the forecast suggests continued expansion.

Bright-line check: "Below its long-run average" for capacity utilization implies below potential. Score MUST be < 50. But the economy is expanding briskly and closing the gap. Score: 44. This reflects below potential but rapidly closing.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Capacity utilization below average = still below potential. Score < 50 is correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment but improving notably. Evidence: "The labor market rebounded strongly in recent months." "Private nonfarm payrolls grew rapidly in April and May, with hiring widespread across industries." BUT "the unemployment rate changed little in recent months" (around 5.6%) and "the labor force participation rate remained low." UE at 5.5% per the projections for Q4 2004.

Per the rubric: UE 5.5-7% corresponds to 31-45 range ("Below full employment; slack remains"). At 5.5%, this is at the top of that range. The rapid improvement in hiring is notable but per the gotcha, score the LEVEL of tightness not the rate of improvement.

Score: 40. Unemployment around 5.5% with participation rate still low indicates meaningful remaining slack despite rapid job creation.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. UE at 5.5% with slack = below tight. Score < 50 is correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees spending as ABOVE trend on net. Evidence: "the economy continued to expand at a solid pace." Consumer spending "slowed somewhat" but business investment "grew rapidly" — "surging outlays on equipment and software." Housing "increased from its robust first-quarter pace." The staff forecast calls for continued solid expansion. Business investment is described with strong language: "brisk pace," "surging," "favorable environment for capital spending."

NET: Consumer spending slightly below trend language ("slowed somewhat") but investment very strong, housing very strong. The overall characterization is "solid pace" expanding. Score: 58. Above trend, driven by strong investment and housing, partially offset by moderating consumer spending.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Evidence is net positive. Score > 50 is correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: Staff sees productivity as strong. Evidence: "persisting strong gains in structural productivity would likely continue to provide significant impetus to spending" (staff forecast). "With the pace of productivity advance moderating, unit labor costs moved higher" — this says productivity growth is moderating from high levels but still strong. "Robust employment growth and the cumulative productivity gains of recent years" in the staff forecast.

Score: 60. Productivity growth described as strong but moderating. Above trend but coming down.

Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Strong gains in structural productivity" = above trend. Score > 50 is correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/LOOSE. Evidence: The staff forecast references "a favorable financing environment." The policy rate had been at 1% (extremely low). "Short-term interest rates were quite low." Risk spreads "about unchanged." "Accommodative financial conditions" is explicitly stated in the participant section but echoes staff characterization.

Bright-line check: "Accommodative" / "favorable financing environment" = score MUST be < 50.

Score: 28. Very accommodative conditions — 1% fed funds rate, favorable financing, low capital costs. Conditions had tightened somewhat as markets priced in rate hikes, but were still quite loose.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/supportive. Score < 50 is correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

Bright-line check: Rising yields from policy expectations = normal, score 45-55. No dysfunction described.

Score: 42. Normal market functioning; yields rose in orderly fashion reflecting policy expectations.

Polarity check: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction language. Score near or below 50 is correct.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

Evidence: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate securities rose about the same amount as those on comparable Treasuries, leaving risk spreads about unchanged." Spreads unchanged at what were historically narrow levels in 2004. "A favorable financing environment" in the staff forecast.

Bright-line check: In 2004, credit spreads were relatively tight. "About unchanged" and "favorable financing" suggests compressed/narrow. Score should be < 50.

Score: 30. Spreads unchanged at favorable levels, consistent with ample credit availability.

Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress/wider spreads. Evidence says favorable, unchanged spreads. Score < 50 is correct.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress/concern. No concern language. Score near 50 is appropriate.
