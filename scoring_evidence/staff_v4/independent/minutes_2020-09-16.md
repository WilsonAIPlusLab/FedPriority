# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2020-09-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly delineated "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: September 2020 — roughly six months into the COVID-19 pandemic. The economy experienced a catastrophic contraction in Q2 2020 and is now in a sharp but incomplete recovery. Unemployment remains very elevated at 8.4%. The federal funds rate is at the effective lower bound. The FOMC has just adopted its flexible average inflation targeting framework.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as well below midpoint. PCE inflation is 1.0% over 12 months, core PCE is 1.3%. The trimmed mean is 1.8%. Staff notes "weak aggregate demand" and "considerable drop in consumer energy prices." Some recent monthly increases in durable goods prices are noted, but staff projects inflation to be "subdued this year, reflecting substantial slack in resource utilization." This is clearly below target — in the 26-40 range. The recent monthly firming in goods prices keeps it from the lowest ranges.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff describes GDP as "rebounding at a rapid rate" but the recovery is "far from complete by year-end." There is "substantial slack in resource utilization." The unemployment rate is 8.4%, payrolls have only retraced "about half of the jobs lost." Bright-line check: "substantial slack" = MUST be < 50. The economy is deeply below potential despite rapid recovery. Anchor: 15-22 for GFC/COVID trough. By September the rebound is underway but far from complete — score should be in the 25-32 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.4% is very high. Payrolls retraced only about half of pandemic losses. Initial claims are declining but pace has slowed. The labor force participation rate rose and employment-to-population ratio increased, but employment is "still below its level at the beginning of the year." With UE at 8.4%, this is clearly in the 16-30 range (high unemployment, significant slack, far from full employment).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "expanded strongly in July" supported by fiscal/monetary policy. Retail sales increased. Housing starts and sales "at or near pre-pandemic levels." Business equipment spending had three consecutive monthly increases. BUT: "spending on some consumer services—such as restaurant dining, hotel accommodations, and air travel—were still subdued." Nonresidential structures "declined." Consumer sentiment below pre-pandemic levels. Personal saving rate "quite elevated." The recovery is strong but uneven and incomplete — above contraction but below trend. Score around 40-45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff says "financing conditions for businesses with access to capital markets and households with high credit scores remained broadly accommodative, although conditions remained tight for other borrowers." Capital market financing "remained accommodative." Short-term funding markets "stable." Municipal financing "accommodative." Consumer credit "accommodative for borrowers with relatively strong credit scores but continued to be tight for subprime borrowers." Small business conditions "remained tight." Bright-line check: "accommodative" = MUST be < 50. The dual nature (accommodative for prime, tight for sub-prime/small business) suggests not extremely loose — around 32-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury market is functioning normally. "Near-dated implied volatility on 10-year Treasury securities was little changed... and remained near the bottom of its historical range." TIPS liquidity improved. Yields changed modestly on sentiment. No dysfunction mentioned. CP spreads "around levels observed before the pandemic." "Normalizing market conditions" with "little activity" in emergency facilities. This is normal to well-functioning — score around 28-35.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond yields... narrowed somewhat and remained near their historical medians." Speculative-grade spreads "narrowed moderately." CRE triple-B CMBS spreads "remained wide" but narrowing. ABS spreads "edged down." Credit quality "tentative signs of stabilization" but defaults still "elevated relative to pre-pandemic levels." Spreads near median with some pockets of wideness — score around 48-55.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose, on net" with "generally better-than-expected news." VIX roughly unchanged. Longer-term downside risk measures increased "somewhat." Staff describes price movements without expressing valuation concern. Score near 45-50 (no particular concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banking system not directly discussed as a standalone topic. CRE loan growth at banks was "weak." C&I loans "declined." Delinquency rates on CMBS mortgages high in hotel/retail. Mortgage credit tight for lower-score borrowers. Small business delinquency rates "comparable with early 2008 levels." These are indirect signals of moderate stress. Score around 50-55.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The exchange value of the dollar depreciated modestly." "The staff's broad dollar index declined moderately, on net, with the dollar depreciating more against EME currencies." Euro at highest vs dollar since 2018. Bright-line check: depreciation = MUST be < 50. Score around 35-40 (modest weakness).

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Durable goods prices increased, "reflecting the strong demand for consumer goods as household purchases shifted away from many consumer services." Monthly readings "bolstered by increases in durable goods prices." Some upward pressure. Score around 48-52.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Services spending "still subdued" — restaurant, hotel, air travel. Weak demand in services implies low services inflation. Not explicitly stated but implied. Score around 30-35.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity expanding, starts/sales at pre-pandemic levels. Mortgage rates at historical lows. But no direct mention of housing inflation/shelter costs. N/A — housing activity is discussed but not shelter inflation specifically.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Latest readings on survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations moved up a bit but remained within their ranges in recent years." Michigan 5-10 year "edged up," NY Fed 3-year "crept up." TIPS measures of inflation compensation "close to their pre-pandemic levels but were still in the lower end of their historical ranges." Expectations anchored, slightly below typical — score around 43-47.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low inflation. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says substantial slack. Score below 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE at 8.4%. Score well below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative. Score below 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress/wider. Near median, some pockets of wideness. Score near 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence shows moderate stress signals. Score near 50-55. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says depreciation. Score below 50. Correct.

### Bright-line checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP: "substantial slack" → score < 50. Score 28. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" → score < 50. Score 35. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "near historical medians" with some wide pockets → score near 50. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed signals, moderate stress → near 50-55. OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "depreciated" → score < 50. Score 37. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction described → score < 55. Score 30. PASS.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (28) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (24)**: Both well below 50, consistent — deep slack in both output and labor markets. Difference of 4 points. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (28) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42)**: 14-point gap. Reasonable: spending is recovering rapidly (strong consumer goods, housing) but the level of output is still far below potential. The spending flows are improving faster than the stock of output gap is closing.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (50)**: 15-point gap. Explainable: overall financial conditions are accommodative (low rates, strong issuance) but credit spreads are near median (not tight/compressed), with some pockets of wideness in CRE and small business. Conditions are loose from policy support, not from compressed spreads.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (30)**: Both below midpoint, co-moving. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~50, Services ~32, Expectations ~45. Weighted average with heavy slack emphasis consistent with overall 30.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Total PCE price inflation was 1.0 percent over the 12 months ending in July, reflecting both weak aggregate demand and a considerable drop in consumer energy prices early this year."
- "inflation was still projected to be subdued this year, reflecting substantial slack in resource utilization"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Headline PCE at 1.0% and core at 1.3% are well below the 2% target, with staff attributing weakness to both demand slack and energy price declines, though some firming in durable goods prices provides modest upward pressure.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running well below target with substantial slack dampening price pressures.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "U.S. real GDP was rebounding at a rapid rate in the third quarter" but "the recovery was forecast to be far from complete by year-end"
- "inflation was still projected to be subdued this year, reflecting substantial slack in resource utilization"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite a rapid rebound in Q3, the economy remains far below potential with substantial slack, payrolls only half recovered, and the staff explicitly projecting incomplete recovery.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff identifies "substantial slack in resource utilization" and a recovery that is "far from complete." Bright-line: "slack" language → score < 50. PASS.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 24 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate moved down further to 8.4 percent in August"
- "payrolls had retraced only about half of the jobs lost at the onset of the pandemic"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: With unemployment at 8.4% and only half of pandemic job losses recovered, the labor market has significant slack despite ongoing improvement.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.4% indicates very loose labor conditions (far from full employment). Anchor: 15-22 for COVID Q2 2020; by September recovery is underway, warranting a somewhat higher score of 24.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Real PCE expanded strongly in July and continued to be bolstered by supportive fiscal and monetary policy actions"
- "spending on some consumer services—such as restaurant dining, hotel accommodations, and air travel—were still subdued"
- Housing "at or near their pre-pandemic levels"; business equipment had "third consecutive monthly increase" but nonresidential structures "declined"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer goods spending and housing are recovering strongly, but services spending remains subdued, business structures are declining, and the overall level is still below pre-pandemic trend.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because while flows are improving rapidly, the level of spending remains below trend with key services sectors still depressed.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative for investment-grade borrowers and prime households, supported by historically low yields and strong issuance, though conditions remain tight for sub-prime borrowers and small businesses.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (looser) because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line: "accommodative" → score < 50. PASS.
