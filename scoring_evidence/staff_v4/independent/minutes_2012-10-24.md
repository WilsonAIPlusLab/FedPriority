# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2012-10-24

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It has clearly delineated sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
October 2012 falls in the post-GFC recovery period. The economy has been recovering slowly from the 2008-09 recession for about three years. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.8%, well above pre-crisis levels. The Fed had just announced QE3 (MBS purchases) and forward guidance at the September 2012 meeting. Fiscal cliff concerns were prominent. This is solidly in the "below potential with slow recovery" era.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Consumer prices "picked up" due to energy costs, but core inflation ("excluding food and energy") was "subdued." Longer-run expectations "remained stable." Wage gains were "subdued." Staff forecast: "inflation would be subdued through 2014" with "significant resource slack." This is clearly below-target inflation. Direction: below 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. "Economic activity continued to increase at a moderate pace." Unemployment "still elevated" at 7.8%. Manufacturing "declined," capacity utilization "edged down." "Significant resource slack was projected to persist over the projection period." GDP growth "would not materially exceed increases in potential output in 2013." Progress in reducing unemployment "expected to be relatively slow." Bright-line: slack remains = MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 7.8% (down but still elevated). Part-time for economic reasons "continued to be elevated." Long-duration unemployment "remained high." Other indicators "did not show decided improvement." Wage gains "subdued." Per the anchors, UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range. Direction: well below 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending was "solid" - real PCE rose at "a solid pace," retail sales "increased considerably." But business investment was weak - equipment/software "appeared to edge down," shipments "continued to decrease," nonresidential construction "decreased further." Housing improved but from low levels ("relatively low level"). Net assessment: consumer strength partly offset by business investment weakness. Direction: near midpoint, slightly below.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. Direction: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as EASING / ACCOMMODATIVE. "Financial conditions generally eased appreciably early in the intermeeting period." Investment-grade yields "reached a record low level." Spreads narrowed. Bond issuance "picked up significantly" at "already robust pace." Consumer credit "expanded briskly." Bank lending standards easing. Mortgage rates declined. Bright-line: "eased" = MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved modestly - short/medium-term "up slightly," long-term "about unchanged." Real yields on TIPS "decreased somewhat." This is normal market functioning with no dysfunction described. Direction: normal, around 35-40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as COMPRESSED/NARROW. Investment-grade spreads "narrowed on net." Speculative-grade spreads "also decreased." Investment-grade yields "reached a record low level." Strong issuance across categories. Bright-line: narrow/compressed = MUST be < 50. Direction: well below 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Broad equity indexes "a little lower, on balance." Gains from September FOMC "more than offset by concerns about corporate profitability." Option-implied volatility "increased, on net." This is modest, orderly decline - no valuation distress language. Direction: near neutral, slightly above 50.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Mixed signals but generally healthy direction. CDS spreads for large financial institutions "declined." Earnings "generally in line with expectations." Unsecured funding "appeared to improve some." But equity prices for financial institutions "modestly lower." CRE "remained weak amid elevated vacancy and delinquency rates." Bank credit expanding at "moderate rate." Lending standards easing. Bright-line: mostly healthy signals = below 50. Direction: below 50 but with some lingering concerns.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on net." Rose against most advanced economies, declined against euro and Asian EM currencies. Bright-line: "little changed" = near 50. Direction: at midpoint.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Increases in consumer prices excluding food and energy were subdued in August and September." "The staff continued to forecast that inflation would be subdued through 2014." Also: "significant resource slack was projected to persist."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation as contained and below target, with energy-driven headline pickup viewed as temporary and core readings subdued.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because core inflation is subdued, wages are modest, and the staff projects continued subdued inflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined but was still elevated." "Significant resource slack was projected to persist over the projection period." "Real GDP growth would not materially exceed increases in potential output in 2013."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing moderately but still well below potential, with significant slack and slow progress expected toward closing the gap.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly describes persistent significant resource slack. Bright-line satisfied (slack = below 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate fell to 7.8 percent... The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons increased somewhat and continued to be elevated, while the rate of long-duration unemployment... remained high." "Gains in average hourly earnings for all employees in the third quarter were subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market very loose with UE at 7.8%, elevated underemployment, high long-duration unemployment, and subdued wage growth.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE 7.8% indicates substantial slack. Per anchors, UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose at a solid pace in August." But: "Real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to edge down in the third quarter." "Nominal business spending for new nonresidential construction decreased further."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending offset by declining business investment and weak industrial production. Net position is slightly below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the business investment weakness partly offsets the solid consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- **Evidence**: No discussion of productivity growth in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial conditions generally eased appreciably early in the intermeeting period." "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds reached a record low level." Banks "continued to report having eased their lending standards." Consumer credit "expanded briskly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly accommodative, with easing across credit markets, record low corporate yields, expanding bank lending, and lower mortgage rates.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff explicitly describes appreciable easing. Bright-line satisfied (accommodative/eased = below 50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Short- and medium-term nominal Treasury yields ended the intermeeting period up slightly, and long-term yields were about unchanged on net." Repo rates "spiked around quarter-end but subsequently more than retraced that move."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with modest yield movements and a temporary repo spike that fully reversed. No dysfunction.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no market dysfunction described, only normal yield adjustments. The repo spike was transient and fully reversed.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds reached a record low level, and their spreads to yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed on net. Yields and spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds also decreased."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads compressed to record or near-record levels across both investment-grade and speculative-grade, with strong issuance volumes.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are at record lows and narrowing further. Bright-line satisfied (narrow/compressed = below 50).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes were a little lower, on balance, as gains following the September FOMC meeting... were more than offset by concerns about corporate profitability." "Option-implied volatility for the S&P 500 index... increased, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities modestly lower with rising volatility, but no distress or valuation concern language. Small, orderly decline driven by earnings concerns.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral, very slightly above due to modest decline and rising vol, but nothing suggesting real stress.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads on credit default swaps for large financial institutions declined in recent months, and third-quarter earnings... were generally in line with expectations." But: "Financial conditions in the commercial real estate sector remained weak amid elevated vacancy and delinquency rates."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system mostly healthy with improving CDS spreads and adequate earnings, but lingering CRE weakness and elevated mortgage delinquencies keep stress modestly present.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the dominant signals (declining CDS spreads, in-line earnings, easing lending standards) indicate a healthy banking system with residual legacy stress. Bright-line satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on net, over the intermeeting period. The dollar rose against the currencies of most advanced economies but declined against the euro and most Asian emerging market currencies."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar essentially stable on a broad trade-weighted basis, with mixed cross-currency movements that netted out.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is at neutral because the broad index was "little changed." Bright-line satisfied (stable = near 50).

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 43 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Longer-term inflation expectations in the survey moved down to near the lower end of the narrow range where they have remained for some time." "Near-term inflation expectations... declined in September and early October."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well anchored but drifting modestly toward the low end, consistent with subdued inflation environment.
- **Direction confirmation**: Below neutral because expectations moved down, but still within a stable range, hence only modestly below 50.
