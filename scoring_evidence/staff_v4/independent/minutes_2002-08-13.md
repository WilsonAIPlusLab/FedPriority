### Document Type

### Staff-Attributable Content

- "The information reviewed at this meeting indicated..." (paragraph starting line 19) — staff review of economic data

### Era Context
August 2002: The economy is in the early, sluggish phase of recovery from the 2001 recession. The dot-com bust aftermath continues with weak equity markets and corporate governance scandals (Enron, WorldCom). The fed funds rate is at 1.75%. Unemployment around 5.9%. This is a period of below-potential growth, slack labor markets, low inflation, and financial stress from corporate accounting scandals.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff says: "Overall price inflation had fallen sharply over the past year, largely reflecting developments in the food and energy sectors, and core inflation had eased a little." Consumer prices exhibited "little change in total and core prices" in May-June. Producer-level core inflation was "at a low rate." The staff forecast: "persistence of underutilized resources was expected to foster some moderation in core price inflation."
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW neutral. Inflation has fallen sharply, core has eased, expected to moderate further.
- Magnitude: "fallen sharply" and "eased" suggest 15-20 points below midpoint. But it's not deflation — prices are just low and easing.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees low and declining inflation. Score below 50. Correct.
- Score: 30 (below target, disinflationary, "contained," "eased")

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff says: "economic activity expanded only slightly in the second quarter." "Capacity utilization in manufacturing... remained substantially below its long-run average." Staff forecast: "the level of activity would remain below the economy's potential for a somewhat longer time" and "a less robust pickup in final sales was now expected... which would put somewhat less pressure on resource margins."
- Direction: Staff sees economy clearly BELOW potential.
- Bright-line check: "below the economy's potential" and "substantially below long-run average" capacity utilization = MUST be < 50. Check.
- Magnitude: "substantially below" capacity utilization, "only slightly" expanding, below potential "for a somewhat longer time" — significant slack but not catastrophic (not in deep recession, there is some growth).
- Score: 33 (below potential, recovering but gap persists, slack remains)

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff says: "the demand for labor services had increased only slightly and the unemployment rate had risen." UE at 5.9% in June, unchanged July. "Private nonfarm payroll employment inched up in July after a mild increase in June." "Aggregate hours worked by production or nonsupervisory workers declined steeply." Help-supply services had "substantial net job losses."
- Direction: Labor market is LOOSE — well below full employment.
- UE at 5.9% maps to range 31-45 per rubric (UE 5.5-7%).
- Magnitude: Rising unemployment, steep decline in hours, only inching payrolls — weak labor market with slack.
- Score: 32 (below full employment, slack remains, UE elevated at 5.9%)

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff says: "final sales changed little." Consumer spending growth "slowed." "Business capital spending weakened somewhat further." "Business investment in equipment and structures declined further in the second quarter." BUT "Retail sales were relatively brisk in June and July" and "residential housing activity remained strong." Inventory investment turned slightly positive.
- Direction: Mixed — consumer spending brisk but growth slowing; business investment still declining. Net position is below trend.
- Magnitude: "weakened somewhat further" for business capex, "slowed" for consumer spending, but housing is strong and retail "brisk." Net: modestly below trend.
- Score: 38 (below trend; consumer holding up but business investment dragging)

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff says: "labor productivity continued on a strong upward trend." Staff forecast references "continuing sizable gains in structural productivity."
- Direction: Clearly above trend.
- Magnitude: "strong upward trend" and "sizable gains" = meaningfully above historical trend.
- Score: 65 (above trend, "strong," "sizable gains")

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff/Manager report: "equity prices plunged before recovering somewhat... on net, the major broad equity indexes were down substantially." "Yields on Treasury securities also fell markedly... as investors sought a safe haven." "Steep increases in corporate debt yields, particularly for lower-quality issues." "Borrowing by domestic nonfinancial businesses had been weak recently, likely reflecting deteriorating conditions in credit markets." Staff forecast: "persisting volatility and weakness in equity prices would tend to offset some of that support."
- Direction: Financial conditions are TIGHT. Equity prices plunging, corporate yields rising, credit market deteriorating, borrowing weak.
- Bright-line check: "deteriorating conditions" = tight = must be > 50. Check.
- Magnitude: Equity plunge, widened corporate spreads, safe-haven flows — but not systemic crisis. "Deteriorating" not "frozen."
- Score: 63 (tight; credit tightening, equity weakness, safe haven flows, but not crisis-level)

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff says: "Yields on Treasury securities also fell markedly on balance in volatile trading, as investors sought a safe haven for their funds." This describes a flight-to-quality rally, not market dysfunction. Yields fell because of safe-haven demand. No mention of liquidity problems, settlement issues, or auction failures.
- Bright-line check: Yield movements from risk-off flows are normal market functioning. No actual dysfunction described.
- Score: 42 (normal functioning; volatile but orderly safe-haven flows)

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff says: "steep increases in corporate debt yields, particularly for lower-quality issues." "Heightened degree of risk aversion that had led to widened credit spreads in financial markets and the curtailment of credit availability to potential borrowers." "Borrowing by domestic nonfinancial businesses had been weak recently, likely reflecting deteriorating conditions in credit markets."
- Direction: Spreads are WIDE — elevated stress.
- Bright-line check: "widened" = must be > 50. Check.
- Magnitude: "steep increases," "curtailment of credit availability," "deteriorating conditions" — significant but not crisis-level seizure.
- Score: 65 (significant widening, reduced access, flight to quality)

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff says: "equity prices plunged before recovering somewhat... on net, the major broad equity indexes were down substantially." "Plunging equity prices" mentioned alongside consumer spending. "Cumulative losses in financial wealth incurred since early 2000" (participant language but corroborates staff data). No explicit staff valuation commentary.
- The staff describes significant price declines but no valuation level language. Per rubric: "equities declined" alone -> 50-55. But "plunged" and "down substantially" is stronger.
- Score: 62 (significantly depressed; sharp declines, "plunged," but no distress-level language from staff)
