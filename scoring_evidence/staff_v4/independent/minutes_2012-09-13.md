# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-09-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections.

**Era context**: September 2012 — three years into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment still elevated at 8.1%. The economy is growing at a moderate pace but well below potential. Europe remains a significant concern. The Fed is about to launch QE3. Inflation is subdued.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. "Consumer price inflation stayed subdued." PCE price index was "flat in July." Core prices also "flat in July." Wages "remained modest." Staff forecasts "inflation would be subdued through 2014." Some upward near-term pressures from drought/energy but described as temporary. This fits the 26-40 range ("contained," "subdued," "running below 2 percent"). Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Staff says subdued. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy BELOW potential. "The expansion in economic activity was expected to narrow the significant margin of slack in labor and product markets only slowly." GDP growth "would not materially exceed the growth of potential output in 2013." "Substantial resource slack persisting over the projection period." Bright-line check: "slack remains" / "below potential" language present -> score MUST be < 50. Anchors suggest 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery period. Given "significant margin of slack," closer to 33-37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 8.1%. "Employment rose slowly." Part-time for economic reasons "remained large." Long-duration unemployment "continued to be high." Job openings "little changed." UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range. At 8.1%, this is significant slack. Score around 25-28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed/below trend. Consumer spending "increased in July" with "fairly widespread" gains after being "roughly flat" for a couple months. But business equipment spending "appeared to be decelerating" — shipments and orders "declined in July." Nonresidential construction "declined in July." Housing improving but "still at a low level." Government spending declining. Net picture is below-trend growth: moderate consumption partially offset by weak investment. Score around 38-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "small gains in productivity." This suggests below-trend productivity growth. Score around 38-42.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat accommodative but mixed. Equity prices rose. Corporate bond yields at "near-record low levels." Corporate spreads "narrowed." Debt issuance "continued to be strong." C&I loans "remained brisk." However, CRE "still somewhat strained," mortgage underwriting "tight," "tight credit standards for mortgage loans." Funding conditions stable. Overall financial conditions are somewhat accommodative despite pockets of tightness. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language → score MUST be < 50. Score around 38-42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No evidence of market dysfunction. Yields moved around based on policy expectations and European concerns — all normal functioning. "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable." "Secured funding markets, conditions were also little changed." Score around 30-35 (normal/calm).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads are narrowing/below normal. Corporate spreads "narrowed." Corporate bond yields at "near-record low levels." "Net debt issuance by nonfinancial firms continued to be strong." Investment- and speculative-grade issuance "increased in August from an already robust pace." CLO issuance "on pace to post its strongest year since 2007." C&I loan spreads "little changed" but spreads on new commitments "narrowed materially." Bright-line check: spreads narrowing/compressed → score < 50. Score around 28-33.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities rose moderately. "Broad price indexes for U.S. equities rose moderately." VIX "fell in early August to levels not seen since the middle of 2007." Staff explicitly assessed valuations: "Valuations for broad asset classes did not appear stretched." No concern language. Score around 42-48 (roughly fair valued, no concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: CDS spreads for largest bank holding companies "continued to move down." Equity prices for large banks "rose about in line with the broad equity price indexes." Bank credit expanding at "moderate pace." No mention of bank stress or failures. Staff found no evidence of "excessive risk-taking" being "widespread." The CRE segment is "somewhat strained" and lending standards are tight in some areas, but the banking system itself appears sound. Bright-line check: indicators suggest low stress → score < 50. Euro-area bank conditions "improved, although these conditions remained fragile" but that's foreign banks. Score around 38-42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar declined." Bright-line check: dollar depreciation → score MUST be < 50. Score around 35-40.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: PCE flat. Food prices "essentially unchanged" but drought points to "some temporary upward pressures on retail food prices later this year." Energy prices declined in July but rose in August. Mixed signals, slightly below neutral with upward near-term risks. Score around 42-45.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not specifically discussed as a price component (housing activity discussed but not shelter inflation). N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable." Near-term expectations "increased somewhat" but longer-term "edged up but remained within the narrow range." TIPS-based measures "edged up over the period but stayed in the ranges observed over recent quarters." Staff assumes "long-run inflation expectations assumed to remain stable." Anchored at target. Score around 48-52.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (27)**: Both below 50, labor somewhat lower reflecting 8.1% unemployment which is more extreme than the "moderate pace" GDP growth. Reasonable divergence — GDP growing moderately but labor slack very significant. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50, close together. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (30)**: Both below 50, spreads narrower than overall FC because of CRE/mortgage tightness pockets that drag FC less accommodative. Coherent direction.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (32)**: Both below 50, no crisis conditions. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE ↔ sub-dimensions**: Parent around 32, goods ~43, expectations ~50. Parent slightly lower reflecting the very flat/subdued headline language. The sub-components are limited. Reasonable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation stayed subdued." "Overall U.S. consumer prices, as measured by the PCE price index, were flat in July." "Consumer prices excluding food and energy also were flat in July." "The staff continued to forecast that inflation would be subdued through 2014."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target with flat monthly readings, subdued wages, and staff projecting continued subdued inflation through 2014 due to substantial resource slack.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff repeatedly describes inflation as "subdued" and "flat" with modest wage gains.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity was expected to narrow the significant margin of slack in labor and product markets only slowly over the projection period." "Substantial resource slack persisting over the projection period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing at a moderate pace but well below potential, with significant slack in both labor and product markets expected to persist for years.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly describes "significant margin of slack" and "substantial resource slack." Bright-line satisfied: slack language → score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 27 ±4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 8.1 percent in August." "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons remained large, and the rate of long-duration unemployment continued to be high." "Employment rose slowly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market deeply slack with 8.1% unemployment, elevated part-time and long-duration unemployment, and only slow employment gains.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because 8.1% unemployment is far above NAIRU, with widespread slack indicators. UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Spending increased in July, and the gains were fairly widespread across categories." But "real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to be decelerating" and "nominal shipments and new orders for nondefense capital goods excluding aircraft declined in July."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showing modest improvement after flat months, but business investment clearly decelerating with declining orders and weak forward indicators. Housing improving but still low. Government spending contracting. Net: below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the mix of sluggish consumption recovery and decelerating business investment produces a below-trend aggregate.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 40 ±10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "With small gains in productivity, unit labor costs rose only slightly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Only a brief mention indicating small/below-trend productivity gains.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly below neutral because "small gains" suggests below the historical ~2% trend.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds were little changed at near-record low levels." "Net debt issuance by nonfinancial firms continued to be strong." But "financial conditions in the commercial real estate (CRE) market were still somewhat strained" and "tight credit standards for mortgage loans."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Broadly accommodative financial conditions in corporate markets (record-low yields, strong issuance, narrowing spreads) partially offset by tight conditions in CRE and mortgage markets.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative) because the dominant tone is easy corporate credit and strong issuance. Bright-line satisfied: accommodative conditions → score < 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period. In secured funding markets, conditions were also little changed." Yield movements attributed to policy expectations and European sentiment — normal price adjustment.
- **Center-of-gravity**: No evidence of Treasury market dysfunction. Yield moves were orderly and driven by policy expectations. Funding markets stable.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because staff describes stable, well-functioning markets with no dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The spread of yields on corporate bonds over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed." "Net debt issuance by nonfinancial firms continued to be strong." "Investment- and speculative-grade bond issuance increased in August from an already robust pace." "Issuance of collateralized loan obligations remained on pace to post its strongest year since 2007."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads narrowing, issuance robust across categories, indicating below-normal credit stress and favorable credit conditions.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and issuance is strong. Bright-line satisfied: narrowing/compressed → score < 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Broad price indexes for U.S. equities rose moderately." "Valuations for broad asset classes did not appear stretched, or supported by excessive leverage."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising moderately with no valuation concerns flagged by staff. Roughly fair valued.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral (slightly below) because staff finds no stretched valuations and no distressed conditions.

### BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "CDS spreads for the largest bank holding companies continued to move down." "Bank credit continued to expand at a moderate pace." "The staff also did not find evidence that excessive risk-taking was widespread." CRE market "still somewhat strained" with "tight underwriting standards."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system sound with improving CDS spreads and expanding credit, though CRE remains a mild concern and underwriting is tight in some sectors. Below-average stress but not fully healthy given lingering post-crisis residuals.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because bank CDS improving, credit expanding, no systemic concerns. Bright-line satisfied: indicators of health → score < 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 37 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar declined."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar weakened over the period as safe-haven demand eased with improving European sentiment.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar declined. Bright-line satisfied: depreciation → score < 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 43 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer food prices were essentially unchanged, but the substantial increases in spot and futures prices of farm commodities...pointed toward some temporary upward pressures on retail food prices later this year." "Consumer energy prices declined slightly in July, but survey data indicated that retail gasoline prices rose in August."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices currently flat but near-term upward pressures from drought and energy. Slightly below neutral reflecting current conditions.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 ±4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Longer-term inflation expectations in the survey edged up but remained within the narrow range that they have occupied for many years." "Long-run inflation expectations assumed to remain stable."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well anchored at target across multiple survey and market-based measures.
