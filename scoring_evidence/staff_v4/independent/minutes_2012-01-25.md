# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-01-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly separated "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: January 2012. The U.S. economy is roughly 2.5 years into recovery from the Great Recession, but recovery is slow. Unemployment remains elevated at 8.5%. European sovereign debt crisis is the major external risk. The Fed funds rate is near zero. This is a period of moderate but below-trend growth with significant remaining slack.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation was subdued," prices "were unchanged in November" and "flat in December," core prices rose only "modestly," inflation expectations "remained stable," wage gains "continued to be modest," and the outlook projects inflation will "remain subdued in 2012 and 2013" given "substantial slack." This is clearly low inflation pressure with no concern about rising prices.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as clearly BELOW potential. Evidence: "slack in product and labor markets" is referenced explicitly, growth will "reduce the slack... only slowly," the unemployment rate was "anticipated to still be high at the end of 2013." The economy expands "moderately" but from well below potential. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language => score MUST be < 50. Confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment. Evidence: Unemployment at 8.5%, "both long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still quite high," employment growing only "moderately," initial claims consistent with "only modest employment growth." UE of 8.5% anchors this firmly in the 16-30 range per the rubric.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend but mixed. Evidence: Consumer spending rose "moderately" but December retail sales "declined." Business equipment spending "appeared to have decelerated," nonresidential construction "unchanged" and "held back." Housing "improved a bit" but "continued to be held down." This is a below-trend picture with some segments softening.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat tight but improving. Evidence: "some improvement in conditions in financial markets," lending standards eased "a bit further," but CRE conditions "remained strained," residential mortgages "remained extremely tight," corporate bond spreads "continued to be elevated," financial stress indicators "generally continued to be elevated." Net: conditions are tight by historical standards but easing at the margin. Bright-line check: Multiple references to "tight" and "strained" conditions => score MUST be > 50. Confirmed.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff mentions Treasury yields "rose slightly at intermediate and longer maturities" — this is normal price movement, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction in Treasuries. The repo market "continued to function normally." Score should be in the normal-functioning range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as elevated but improving. Evidence: Investment-grade spreads "declined a bit," speculative-grade spreads "decreased noticeably," but CDS spreads of banks "remained elevated," CRE conditions "remained strained," residential mortgage markets "remained extremely tight." Net picture: still-elevated spreads across several sectors, but with some improvement at the margin. Bright-line check: "elevated" spreads => score MUST be > 50. Confirmed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes equity prices "increased more than 6 percent" over the intermeeting period, volatility "declined notably." Financial institution equity "outperformed the broader market." However, "the ratio of the market value of bank equity to its book value remained low for some large financial firms." The staff reports price moves without strong valuation commentary, suggesting a roughly neutral to modestly positive read. Default toward 45-55 per rubric guidance.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff sees notable stress still present. Evidence: CDS spreads of large banks "remained elevated," market-to-book ratios "remained low for some large financial firms," "increased concern over counterparty risk," fourth-quarter earnings "mixed" with "poor capital market revenues." But there is some easing: CDS spreads "moved lower," some institutions issued "significant quantities of new long-term debt," and financial stress indicators "eased somewhat." This is a picture of moderately elevated stress with some improvement. Bright-line check: Language includes "elevated" and "concern" => score should be > 50. Confirmed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff reports the dollar "declined slightly over the intermeeting period," with the dollar falling "against most other currencies" though it "appreciated against the euro." Net: modest weakness. Bright-line check: depreciation => score MUST be < 50. Confirmed.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out separately as a clear sub-category. Consumer energy prices "decreased," food price increases "slowed," core prices rose "modestly." Some implicit information but limited.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing costs not discussed as an inflation component (only as a real activity measure). N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff reports near-term expectations "essentially unchanged" and longer-term expectations "remained stable." This is well-anchored at target territory.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued/low. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential, slack. Score below 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says 8.5% UE, elevated. Score well below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says strained/tight. Score above 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress/wider. Evidence says elevated. Score above 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence says elevated CDS, low market-to-book. Score above 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says declined slightly. Score below 50. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (33) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25)**: Both well below 50, consistent with significant remaining slack. Gap of 8 points is reasonable — labor market lagging output recovery slightly.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (33) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (37)**: Both below 50, consistent. Gap of 4 points — coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (58)**: Both above 50, co-moving. Gap of 2 points — coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC elevated but no Treasury market dysfunction. Consistent with credit tightness being sector-specific (CRE, mortgages) rather than systemic Treasury market stress.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) <-> INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Inflation expectations anchored (48), goods show low pressure — consistent with subdued headline.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was subdued"; "Overall U.S. consumer prices... were unchanged in November"; "consumer energy prices decreased"; core prices rose "modestly"; staff projects inflation will "remain subdued in 2012 and 2013" given "substantial slack in labor and product markets."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff presents a clearly below-target inflation picture with no upward pressure, flat headline prices, and subdued outlook — consistent with the 26-40 "below target, contained" range.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes inflation as subdued, flat, and expected to remain so given persistent slack.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 33 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "slack in product and labor markets" to be reduced "only slowly over the projection period"; "the unemployment rate was anticipated to still be high at the end of 2013"; economic activity expanded "moderately."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is clearly operating below potential with significant slack, though moderate growth continues. Fits the 31-40 "below potential, recovering but gap persists" range.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies persistent slack in product and labor markets. Bright-line rule satisfied: "slack remains" => score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 8.5 percent in December"; "both long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still quite high"; initial claims "consistent with only modest employment growth."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: With 8.5% unemployment, high long-duration unemployment, and elevated underemployment, the labor market has significant slack. This is firmly in the 16-30 "high unemployment, significant slack" range.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.5% indicates substantial labor market slack, far from full employment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Consumer spending rose "moderately" but December retail sales "declined"; "real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to have decelerated in the fourth quarter"; nonresidential construction "held back by high vacancy rates and tight credit conditions"; housing "improved a bit" but from "depressed levels."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending is below trend across most categories — consumer spending decelerating, business investment weakening, housing barely improving from depressed levels. Fits the 31-45 "below trend, sluggish" range.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because staff describes deceleration in business spending, weak retail sales, and a housing market still at depressed levels.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity in the staff assessment.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Indicators of financial stress eased somewhat over the intermeeting period, although they generally continued to be elevated"; CRE conditions "remained strained"; residential mortgage markets "remained extremely tight"; corporate bond spreads "continued to be elevated"; but lending standards "eased a bit further" and there was "some improvement in conditions in financial markets."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly tighter than neutral — multiple sectors (CRE, mortgage, bank funding) show tight/strained conditions despite marginal improvement. Fits the 55-65 "somewhat tight" range.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff uses "elevated," "strained," "extremely tight" across multiple credit markets. Bright-line rule satisfied: "tight"/"strained" => score > 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities rose slightly at intermediate and longer maturities"; "the general collateral repurchase agreement, or repo, market continued to function normally"; no mention of liquidity problems or dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market is functioning normally with orderly yield movements. Fits the 36-50 "normal market functioning" range.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because there is no indication of market dysfunction — only normal yield adjustments.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "CDS spreads of most large domestic bank holding companies remained elevated"; "corporate bond spreads continued to be elevated"; CRE conditions "remained strained"; but investment-grade spreads "declined a bit" and speculative-grade spreads "decreased noticeably."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spreads remain elevated across multiple sectors despite marginal improvement. Fits the 55-65 "somewhat wide" range, on the lower end given the improving trajectory.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because staff repeatedly describes spreads as "elevated." Bright-line rule satisfied: "elevated" => score > 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes increased more than 6 percent"; "option-implied equity volatility declined notably"; "financial institutions outperformed the broader market." But "the ratio of the market value of bank equity to its book value remained low for some large financial firms."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff reports strong recent price gains without valuation concern language, though bank valuations remain depressed. Net: roughly neutral, with the price increase suggesting no elevated distress but no overvaluation concern either.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — strong recent gains without valuation commentary default to 45-55 per rubric.

### BANKING_STRESS: 57 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "CDS spreads of most large domestic bank holding companies remained elevated"; "the ratio of the market value of bank equity to its book value remained low"; "increased concern over counterparty risk"; but stress indicators "eased somewhat" and some institutions issued "significant quantities of new long-term debt."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system shows moderately elevated stress — CDS spreads high, market-to-book low, counterparty concerns up — but with marginal improvement. Fits the 55-65 "emerging stress" range on the lower end.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because staff describes elevated CDS spreads, low valuations, and counterparty risk concerns. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 43 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff's broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar declined slightly over the intermeeting period. While the dollar fell against most other currencies, it appreciated against the euro."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar weakened modestly on a broad basis despite euro appreciation. "Declined slightly" suggests modest weakness — fits the 31-45 "modest weakness to stable" range.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated on a broad-weighted basis. Bright-line rule satisfied: depreciation => score < 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 30 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices decreased in recent months"; "increases in consumer food prices slowed"; core goods prices implied by the "modestly" rising core CPI.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Goods prices show declining energy, slowing food, and modest core — below-neutral goods inflation pressure.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No separate discussion of services inflation.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing discussed only as real activity (prices, starts), not as a CPI/PCE inflation component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Near-term inflation expectations... were essentially unchanged in early January, and longer-term inflation expectations remained stable"; "stable long-run inflation expectations" in the outlook section.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Expectations are well-anchored and stable — close to target, consistent with the 45-55 "anchored at target" range. Scored 48 rather than 50 given the subdued inflation backdrop suggesting anchoring slightly below target.
