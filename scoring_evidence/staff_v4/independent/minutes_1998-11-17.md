# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — November 17, 1998

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from a **pre-2005 unsegmented** format. Staff and participant content are interleaved. I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by cues such as "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested," "the staff forecast," and factual data paragraphs that precede the "In the Committee's discussion" sections. Participant content ("members observed," "members noted," "members commented") must be excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
November 1998 falls during the late-1990s expansion, amid the global financial crisis stemming from Asia (1997-98), the Russian default (August 1998), and the LTCM collapse (September 1998). The Fed had been cutting rates (September and October 1998 intermeeting cut). The U.S. economy was still growing but showing signs of moderation, while financial markets were recovering from severe stress but remained fragile.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "Recent wage and price developments had been mixed" (line 72). Core CPI rose faster than overall CPI, but overall consumer prices were restrained by falling energy prices. Producer prices "were down on balance over the past year." The ECI showed a "sizable increase" in Q3, but average hourly earnings "moderated considerably." The staff forecast projects "inflation was projected to rise considerably over the year ahead in association with a partial reversal of the decline in energy prices" — but the current reading is clearly subdued. Current inflation is below target with mixed wage signals. Direction: below 50. "Somewhat" and "mixed" language suggests 5-10 points below midpoint. Score: ~40.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says muted current inflation with mixed wage signals. Score 40 = below midpoint. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as operating modestly ABOVE potential but decelerating. Key evidence: "some moderation in the expansion of economic activity from a brisk pace during the summer months" (line 59-60). The staff forecast says "considerable slowing in the expansion of economic activity to a pace appreciably below the estimated growth of the economy's potential" (lines 276-278). This means the economy is currently above potential but heading below. The current level: unemployment at 4.5% (tight), GDP growth had been brisk. The staff still describes the expansion as robust with tight labor markets. The economy is currently running above potential.
- Bright-line check: The staff forecast describes slowing to "below the estimated growth of the economy's potential" — this is about growth rates, not levels. The level is still above potential given tight labor markets and brisk growth. Score must be > 50.
- Late 90s boom anchor: 57-63. Score: ~55. Growth is decelerating and inventory-driven, industrial production declining, but labor markets still tight and consumer spending solid.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor markets as TIGHT. Evidence: "tight conditions had persisted in most labor markets" (line 71-72). Unemployment "remained near 4-1/2 percent" (lines 82-83). Employment growth slowed appreciably, but conditions remain tight. The staff forecast says "pressures on labor resources were likely to ease somewhat" (line 291).
- UE at 4.5% puts this in the 53-65 range per rubric. "Tight conditions persisted" is clear. Employment growth slowing but level of tightness still elevated.
- Anchor: late 90s = 65-75. But UE 4.5% is higher than the very tightest late-90s readings, and growth is slowing. Score: ~62.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 4.5% with "tight conditions persisted" = above midpoint. Matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees this as MIXED but net slightly above trend. Consumer spending: "Personal consumption expenditures rose considerably further during the third quarter" (line 99). Retail sales posted a "sharp rise" in October (line 107). Housing "remained quite strong" (lines 131-132). Business investment: "softened after having surged in the first half" (line 68). "Nonresidential building activity apparently fell somewhat further" (line 120-121). Capital spending expected to pick up from Q3 lull but moderate going forward.
- Consumer side is solid-to-strong; business investment is softening. Net: slightly above trend. Score: ~53.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Consumer strong, investment softening. Net slightly above trend. Matches.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff does not explicitly discuss productivity trends in the staff-attributable sections. The only oblique reference is in participant discussion about firms "absorbing rising labor costs" offset by "improvements in productivity" (lines 445-447) — but this is participant content. Score: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees financial conditions as TIGHT/STRAINED. Key staff-attributable evidence: "Strains in financial markets continued to mount, with intermediaries and final investors much more cautious about risks and leverage" (lines 220-222). "These developments in turn disrupted flows of funds in a number of financial markets" (lines 223-224). Post-October easing: "strains gradually moderated after mid-October and sizable issuance of securities resumed in a number of key markets, but uncertainty remained high and relatively illiquid conditions persisted" (lines 237-240). Staff forecast: "private final demand would be restrained a bit by the tighter terms and conditions that were now imposed by many types of lenders" (lines 287-289). "The availability of financing for various types of construction appeared to lessen substantially" (lines 126-127).
- Bright-line check: "tighter terms and conditions," "illiquid conditions persisted," "strains" — all indicate tight. Score must be > 50.
- Financial conditions were severely strained in September-October but improving by mid-November. Still tight but recovering. Score: ~67.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

But there is no specific discussion of Treasury market dysfunction (no mentions of liquidity problems, settlement failures, auction issues in the Treasury market specifically). The broader financial market stress was more in credit/corporate/funding markets. Yield movements alone are not dysfunction.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

But member discussion (which I note but don't score) says "many risk spreads were still at unusually high levels" (line 332). The key staff-attributable content around credit: "the availability of financing for various types of construction appeared to lessen substantially in late summer, and financing costs rose for many borrowers" (lines 126-128). Also from staff forecast: "tighter terms and conditions that were now imposed by many types of lenders" (lines 288-289).
- Direction: Spreads had widened dramatically, were narrowing from peaks but still elevated. Score well above 50. Score: ~65.
- Bright-line: "elevated" spreads, less availability = > 50. Matches.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

**10. BANKING_STRESS**

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices rose appreciably in October, but they were still down sharply from a year earlier and on balance limited the increase of overall consumer prices over the past year. Core consumer prices moved up at a faster pace than overall consumer prices in recent months." Staff forecast: "inflation was projected to rise considerably over the year ahead in association with a partial reversal of the decline in energy prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is subdued with energy prices dragging headline down and core modestly firming; overall below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because overall prices are restrained by energy declines and producer prices are down on the year, despite some firming in core/services.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "some moderation in the expansion of economic activity from a brisk pace during the summer months"; "the staff forecast prepared for this meeting continued to point to considerable slowing in the expansion of economic activity to a pace appreciably below the estimated growth of the economy's potential."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy still operating above potential as evidenced by tight labor markets and brisk recent growth, but with clear deceleration underway.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because "brisk pace" and tight labor markets indicate above-potential output, even as growth is moderating. Bright-line: no "below potential" language for the current level.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "tight conditions had persisted in most labor markets"; "The civilian unemployment rate remained near 4-1/2 percent during the two months."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets clearly tight with UE at 4.5%, persistent shortages, though employment growth has slowed.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE at 4.5% with "tight conditions persisted" clearly indicates above-NAIRU tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Personal consumption expenditures rose considerably further during the third quarter"; "business fixed investment had softened after having surged in the first half"; "housing activity had remained at a high level."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and housing solid; business investment softening from elevated levels. Net position slightly above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral because strong consumer spending and housing offset softening business investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No staff-attributable discussion of productivity trends.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 67 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "strains gradually moderated after mid-October and sizable issuance of securities resumed in a number of key markets, but uncertainty remained high and relatively illiquid conditions persisted"; staff forecast: "private final demand would be restrained a bit by the tighter terms and conditions that were now imposed by many types of lenders."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions had been severely strained, were improving but remained tight with persistent illiquidity and tighter lending standards.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because "illiquid conditions persisted" and "tighter terms and conditions" clearly indicate restrictive financial environment. Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific staff discussion of Treasury market dysfunction. Interest rates rose but this reflects normal market adjustment.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 65 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "yields on the bonds of lower-rated firms have declined" (narrowing from peaks); "the availability of financing for various types of construction appeared to lessen substantially in late summer, and financing costs rose for many borrowers."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened dramatically during the crisis, narrowed somewhat but remain elevated; credit availability reduced for riskier borrowers.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because financing became less available and more expensive, and spreads remain elevated despite some recent narrowing. Bright-line: "elevated" → > 50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "share prices dropped in the weeks following the September meeting, but the market rallied strongly after mid-October and key market indexes posted sizable gains on balance over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities recovered with sizable gains; no staff valuation commentary. Price movements described neutrally.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because staff describes price movements without expressing valuation concern or distress.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 55 ±12 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: Staff forecast references "tighter terms and conditions that were now imposed by many types of lenders" (lines 288-289); M2/M3 discussion notes "bank efforts to fund heavy demand for loans" (line 268).
**Center-of-gravity**: Banks active in lending (as borrowers turned to backup lines) but tightening standards — modest emerging stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly above neutral because tighter lending standards indicate some stress, though banks remain operational.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar fell moderately over the period in relation to other major currencies"; "The dollar also fell somewhat in terms of a broad index of currencies of other countries."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar depreciated moderately on a trade-weighted basis against both major and broad currency indices.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar fell/depreciated. Bright-line: depreciation → < 50. Satisfied.

**Evidence**: "prices of finished goods edged up in recent months but were down on balance over the past year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices declining year-over-year, held down by Asian competition and weak commodity prices.
