### Era Context
January 2019: Late-expansion economy. The Fed had just raised rates in December 2018. Financial markets experienced a sharp sell-off in Q4 2018 but were recovering. Unemployment was near 50-year lows (~3.9%). PCE inflation was running slightly below 2%. Trade tensions with China were a concern, and a partial government shutdown had limited data availability.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as slightly below the midpoint. PCE at 1.8%, core PCE at 1.9%. Core CPI at 2.2%. Staff expects core PCE to step up to 2% over the year. Energy prices declining. Longer-run inflation expectations "little changed." This is modestly below target — consistent with 2013-19 anchor of 38-42. Direction: slightly below 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says GDP growth was "solid" in Q4, economy projected to grow "at a rate above the staff's estimate of potential output growth" in 2019, unemployment "somewhat further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate," and "labor market conditions judged to already be tight." The economy is described as operating above potential — the staff explicitly says the economy is "projected to move further above its potential." Bright-line: "above potential" → MUST be > 50. Score should be modestly above 50 — say 53-56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 3.9% (below 4%), payroll employment "expanded strongly," job openings and quits at "high levels," initial claims "near historically low levels," average hourly earnings up 3.2%. But "supportive" / "accommodative" → < 50 per bright-line. The staff uses both — they say conditions tightened but remain supportive. The net is slightly above neutral given the emphasis on tightening from September levels. Score ~52-55.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "fluctuated substantially" with "heightened risk aversion contributing to a significant decline in yields early." Year-end repo market pressures: "General collateral repo rates moved up sharply at year-end but subsequently returned to normal levels." The yield fluctuation is normal price adjustment (not dysfunction). The repo spike was transient and resolved. No actual Treasury market dysfunction described. Score near normal, maybe slightly elevated for the transient repo stress. ~45-48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "widened notably" early in the period then "narrowed a bit, on net." Spreads stayed "at levels well above those that prevailed a year ago." Credit quality "continued to show signs of deterioration." On net, spreads are elevated relative to the prior year but narrowing. This is somewhat above normal. Direction: above 50 (wider than normal). Score ~55-58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes S&P 500 was "down as much as 8 percent at one point" but "ended the period notably higher." Staff "judged asset valuation pressures in equity and corporate debt markets to have abated somewhat." Private-sector analysts "significantly revised down their projections for corporate earnings." The staff's own view is that valuation pressures have "abated" (i.e., less overvaluation concern). This is roughly in the middle — not overvalued, not distressed. Score ~48-52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: C&I lending standards "remained basically unchanged." CRE financing conditions "remained accommodative." Banks tightened standards for CRE loans but also for credit cards. Overall: "risks associated with financial leverage" and "vulnerabilities related to maturity transformation were viewed as being low." This indicates low banking stress. Bright-line: low risk → below 50. Score ~32-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated broadly amid falling U.S. yields and greater investor optimism." "Depreciated notably against the British pound." "Depreciated considerably against the Brazilian real and Mexican peso." Bright-line: depreciation → MUST be < 50. Clear depreciation. Score ~30-35.
