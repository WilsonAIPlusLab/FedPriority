# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — January 29-30, 2002 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes**. Staff content is mixed with participant discussion and policy action. I must identify and score only staff-attributable language (e.g., "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," descriptions of data without participant attribution) and ignore participant/member views ("members commented," "several members," "the Committee agreed").

### Era Context
January 2002: The U.S. economy is in the aftermath of the 2001 recession (officially March-November 2001) and the September 11 attacks. The fed funds rate is at 1.75%. The economy appears to be bottoming out. Unemployment is rising (5.8% in December 2001). Inflation is low and declining. This is an early-recovery, slack-economy period.

Key staff-attributable passages:

- Lines 411-414: Financial market developments (Manager's report content woven in).

- Lines 420-421: "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." — explicit staff forecast.

Participant content to IGNORE: Lines 423 onward ("In the Committee's discussion..."), lines 426-427 (member projections), lines 429+ (members' views on recovery, consumer spending, etc.), lines 453+ (policy discussion).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint.
Evidence: "Consumer price inflation was quite low at year-end." "Both the CPI and the PCE chain-type price index edged down on balance in November and December." "Core consumer price inflation was held down late in the year by widespread discounting of goods." "Core PCE index declined somewhat on a year-over-year basis." Staff forecast: "Sub-par expansion over the next few quarters was expected to foster an appreciable further easing of pressures on resources and some moderation in core consumer price inflation."
Magnitude: "Quite low," "edged down," "declining" — this is clearly below target. Core PCE declining year-over-year. Language is moderate-to-strong below midpoint. Energy prices falling adds to the picture. Score range: 25-32.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low/declining inflation. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy BELOW potential.
Evidence: "Economic activity probably steadied in the fourth quarter after a sizable drop in the summer." "The rate of utilization of total manufacturing capacity declined a little further in December, and the average rate for the fourth quarter was at its lowest quarterly level since 1983." Staff forecast: "economic activity likely would start to turn up early in 2002... and would gather strength only gradually." "Sub-par expansion over the next few quarters was expected to foster an appreciable further easing of pressures on resources."
Bright-line check: Below potential / slack / sub-par — score MUST be < 50. The economy is bottoming out from recession with capacity utilization at lowest since 1983. This is significant slack.
Magnitude: Deep below potential but beginning to stabilize. Not catastrophic (not GDP collapsing) but clearly recessionary with significant slack. Score range: 28-33.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE (below midpoint).
Evidence: "The labor market deteriorated somewhat further in December, and the unemployment rate continued to climb, to 5.8 percent." "Private nonfarm payrolls fell considerably." Staff forecast projects unemployment would "begin to edge down" only later, and member projections center on 6-6.25% by Q4 2002.
Magnitude: UE at 5.8% and rising toward 6%+. Payrolls still falling. This is clearly slack. Per the rubric, UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45.
Score: ~33 (unemployment elevated and still rising).

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Mixed but net below trend.
Evidence: "Final demand appeared to have increased appreciably" but this was largely motor vehicle surge from financing incentives. "Business expenditures on durable equipment and software contracted less rapidly." "Nonresidential construction declined sharply further." Staff forecast: recovery would "gather strength only gradually" with "marked slowing in the contraction of business capital investment."
Net assessment: Consumer spending strengthened (motor vehicles) but business investment still contracting. The surge in consumer spending is partly temporary (incentives), and business investment is deeply weak. Net position: below trend but not collapsing.
Score range: 35-40. Below trend with signs of stabilization.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
The staff forecast mentions "the favorable outlook for productivity growth and related profit opportunities" in context of future capital spending, but this is not a direct assessment of current productivity levels. Not enough direct discussion for scoring.
Score: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff sees conditions as accommodative/loose (below midpoint for tightness).
Evidence: "Monetary policy had been eased substantially over the past year" — but this is policy, not conditions. For conditions: "the real federal funds rate at an unusually low level." "Very low mortgage rates" supporting housing. "Risk spreads on corporate debt securities changed little." "Yields on Treasury coupon securities declined slightly." Credit appears available.
Staff language cues point to accommodative conditions. The fed funds rate at 1.75% is very low. Mortgage rates are "very low." However, some residual stress from 2001 recession may still exist.
Bright-line: "Accommodative" → score MUST be < 50.
Score: ~35. Generally accommodative conditions, supportive monetary policy feeding through, but not extremely loose — still in recession recovery.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Evidence: "Yields on Treasury coupon securities declined slightly over the intermeeting period." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction difficulties. Normal market functioning.
Score: N/A (no specific discussion of Treasury market dysfunction).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Evidence: "Risk spreads on corporate debt securities changed little." This suggests spreads are stable but doesn't characterize them as tight or wide. Given the recession context, spreads were likely somewhat elevated from the 2001 downturn but not dramatically so.
Score: ~52. "Changed little" suggests near-neutral, but post-recession context suggests slightly elevated. Moderate confidence due to sparse information.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

**10. BANKING_STRESS**

Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: Dollar strengthening (above midpoint).
Evidence: "The trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the major foreign currencies increased somewhat on balance over the intermeeting period and reached its highest level since the mid-1980s." "The dollar also appreciated slightly against the euro."
Bright-line: Dollar appreciation → score MUST be > 50.
Magnitude: "Increased somewhat," "highest level since the mid-1980s" — this is notable strength. Score: 63-68.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was quite low at year-end." "Both the consumer price index (CPI) and the personal consumption expenditure (PCE) chain-type price index edged down on balance in November and December." "Core consumer price inflation was held down late in the year by widespread discounting of goods." Staff forecast: "Sub-par expansion...was expected to foster an appreciable further easing of pressures on resources and some moderation in core consumer price inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes inflation as quite low, with core measures declining and energy passthrough further easing prices; the forecast expects additional moderation.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff characterizes inflation as "quite low" with declining core measures and expects further moderation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Economic activity probably steadied in the fourth quarter after a sizable drop in the summer." "The rate of utilization of total manufacturing capacity...was at its lowest quarterly level since 1983." Staff forecast: "economic activity likely would start to turn up early in 2002...and would gather strength only gradually" with "sub-par expansion over the next few quarters."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is far below potential with historically low capacity utilization, just beginning to stabilize from recession, with sub-par growth expected to persist.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because capacity utilization is at its lowest since 1983 and staff expects sub-par expansion to continue. Bright-line satisfied: below potential → score < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 33 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The labor market deteriorated somewhat further in December, and the unemployment rate continued to climb, to 5.8 percent." "Private nonfarm payrolls fell considerably, with manufacturing again experiencing the largest job losses." "The unemployment rate would begin to edge down" only later per staff forecast.
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly slack with unemployment at 5.8% and rising, payrolls still falling, though the pace of decline is moderating.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is elevated at 5.8%, still climbing, with payrolls falling.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth of consumer spending strengthened considerably late in the year" driven by "a surge in purchases of motor vehicles in response to attractive financing incentives." "Business expenditures on durable equipment and software contracted less rapidly." "Nonresidential construction declined sharply further in the fourth quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showed temporary strength from auto incentives, but business investment remains in contraction and nonresidential construction is declining sharply; the net position is below trend but not collapsing.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the mix of temporarily boosted consumer spending and deeply weak business investment nets to below-trend overall.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No direct staff assessment of current productivity levels or trends. A passing reference to "the favorable outlook for productivity growth" appears in forward-looking context but does not constitute a scored assessment.
**Center-of-gravity**: N/A — insufficient discussion.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Very low mortgage rates tended to offset the effects of a weakening job market." "Yields on Treasury coupon securities declined slightly over the intermeeting period, risk spreads on corporate debt securities changed little." Fed funds rate at 1.75% with "the real federal funds rate at an unusually low level."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are generally accommodative following substantial monetary easing, with low mortgage rates and stable spreads, though the recession context limits how loose conditions feel for businesses.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative) because of very low rates and supportive credit conditions. Bright-line satisfied: accommodative → score < 50.

**Center-of-gravity**: N/A — no discussion of Treasury market stress or dysfunction.

**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are roughly stable in post-recession environment; the single sentence provides minimal information but "changed little" suggests near-neutral conditions with possible residual elevation from the downturn.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is near neutral; spreads are described as stable without characterization as tight or wide.
