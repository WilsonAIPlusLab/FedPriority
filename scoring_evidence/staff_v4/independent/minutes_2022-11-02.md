### Era Context
November 2022: The Federal Reserve is deep into an aggressive tightening cycle. Inflation remains well above the 2% target (PCE at 6.2%, core at 5.1%). The labor market is very tight with unemployment at 3.5%. The economy rebounded in Q3 after declining in H1 2022. The dollar is very strong. Financial conditions are tightening considerably.

### Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly ABOVE midpoint. Total PCE inflation 6.2%, core PCE 5.1%, trimmed mean 4.7%, ECI 5.2%. "Consumer price inflation remained elevated." Staff raised their projection for core PCE in coming quarters. Risks to inflation projection "skewed to the upside." This is firmly in the 76-90 range per the rubric ("substantially above target"; "well above objective"). The anchor for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak is 82-88. By November 2022, inflation was beginning to moderate slightly from peak but remained very elevated. Score ~80.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output near or modestly above potential currently but expects it to move below potential. "Real GDP had increased at a moderate pace in the third quarter after having declined over the first half of the year." The staff outlook says "output was expected to move below the staff's estimate of potential early in 2024." This implies output is currently at or modestly above potential but heading down. The recession risk is "almost as likely as the baseline." Current assessment: near potential, maybe slightly above given tight labor markets. Score ~52. Bright-line: no explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for the CURRENT state; the below-potential language is forward-looking (2024-2025). The current picture is moderate growth with tight labor markets, suggesting near or slightly above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor as clearly ABOVE midpoint. "Labor market conditions remained quite tight." Unemployment at 3.5%. "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid" at 5.0% AHE and 5.2% ECI. Job openings "remained high." This is very tight. With UE at 3.5%, per rubric guidance, score should be 65+. The anchor for 2022-23 is 65-75. Score ~72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED, near midpoint. "Real PCE rose modestly in the third quarter." "Residential investment dropped further." "Business fixed investment growth was held back." Government purchases rose. Net exports contributed positively. The consumer spending is modest, residential investment weak, business investment constrained. This nets to slightly below trend. Score ~42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed explicitly. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as clearly ABOVE midpoint (tighter). "Broad financial conditions were expected to be considerably more restrictive." "The rise in borrowing costs appeared to have slowed the volume of financing." "Tightening financial conditions" mentioned as a downside risk. Banks tightened C&I and CRE lending standards. Mortgage rates "nearly reaching their highest levels since 2002." The anchor for 2022 rate hike effects is 55-65. Given "considerably more restrictive" language, this pushes toward upper end. Score ~65.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes yield movements but NOT market dysfunction. "U.S. Treasury yields... moved substantially higher" — this is normal price adjustment from policy expectations, NOT dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or settlement issues. Per bright-line rule, rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 45-55). However, there's a mention of "structural vulnerabilities" in short-term funding markets and hedge fund leverage concerns. Score ~48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees credit stress as modestly ABOVE midpoint. Corporate bond yields "increased notably." CMBS issuance "slowed amid higher spreads." Speculative-grade downgrades outpaced upgrades. Default expectations "increased markedly." But "credit quality remained sound overall" and credit "continued to be generally available." This is mild widening. Anchor for 2022 mild widening is 55-65. Score ~58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes equity prices were "essentially unchanged on net" but VIX "remained at the upper end of its range since mid-2020." "Prices of risky assets generally fell, though real estate valuations remained elevated." No strong valuation commentary. Per gotcha, equities unchanged → default near 50. The elevated VIX and general risk-off tone suggest mild stress. Score ~53.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff sees banking system as relatively LOW stress. "Large banks remain resilient to a substantial economic downturn." "Funding risks at domestic banks remained low." But "elevated leverage at hedge funds and other nonbank financial institutions" and "structural vulnerabilities" in short-term funding. Banks are healthy but nonbank risks are noted. Score ~38. Bright-line check: "resilient" and "low funding risks" → must be below 50. Check passes.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff sees dollar as clearly ABOVE midpoint. "The U.S. dollar appreciated against most major currencies, driven by widening yield differentials." The yen weakened, the renminbi "depreciated significantly against the dollar." This is broad-based dollar strength. Anchor for 2022 Q3 DXY at 20-year highs is 75-85. By November the dollar was still very strong. Score ~75. Bright-line check: appreciation → must be > 50. Check passes.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 80 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation remained elevated. Total PCE price inflation was 6.2 percent over the 12 months ending in September, and core PCE inflation... was 5.1 percent." "The staff raised their projection for core PCE price inflation in coming quarters." Risks "skewed to the upside."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is substantially above target with upside risks, though some signs of potential moderation ahead. The staff raised near-term projections.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is well above neutral because PCE is 6.2%, core 5.1%, and the staff sees upside risks.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. real gross domestic product (GDP) had increased at a moderate pace in the third quarter after having declined over the first half of the year." "Output was expected to move below the staff's estimate of potential early in 2024."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is near potential currently with moderate growth, but the staff projects it will fall below potential by 2024. Current state is roughly balanced.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is at/slightly above neutral because growth is moderate and the economy has not yet moved below potential, though it is expected to.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions remained quite tight." "The unemployment rate declined 0.2 percentage point to 3.5 percent." "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid: Average hourly earnings rose 5.0 percent... the employment cost index... rose 5.2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Very tight labor market with historically low unemployment and rapid wage growth, though some tentative signs of moderation (ECI three-month change slowing, job openings moving lower).
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE is 3.5% and wage growth is 5%+.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE rose modestly in the third quarter." "Residential investment dropped further, however, and business fixed investment growth was held back by a decline in nonresidential structures investment."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending grew modestly but investment components were weak. The net picture is slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is modestly below neutral because consumer spending is only "modest" and investment is declining.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends or output per hour.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad financial conditions were expected to be considerably more restrictive over the projection period." "The rise in borrowing costs appeared to have slowed the volume of financing in many credit markets." Banks "reported having tightened C&I and commercial real estate (CRE) lending standards." "Residential mortgage rates rose further... nearly reaching their highest levels since 2002."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened significantly, with higher borrowing costs reducing credit demand and banks tightening lending standards. "Considerably more restrictive" signals meaningful tightness.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff explicitly describes conditions as "considerably more restrictive."

### TREASURY_STRESS: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. Treasury yields... moved substantially higher." "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yields rose substantially but this reflects policy expectations, not dysfunction. Short-term funding markets stable. No signs of market breakdown.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral because yield movements are from policy expectations (normal functioning) and funding markets are stable.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Corporate bond yields increased notably." "CMBS also slowed amid higher spreads." "In the speculative-grade segment, downgrades outpaced upgrades, and market-implied expectations of defaults over the next year increased markedly." But: "Credit quality remained sound overall."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads have widened somewhat with some deterioration in lower-rated segments, but overall credit quality remains sound. Mild-to-moderate stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because of widening spreads, rising default expectations in speculative grade, and tightening lending standards.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 53 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad domestic equity prices were little changed, on net, amid high market volatility." "The VIX... remained at the upper end of its range since mid-2020." "Prices of risky assets generally fell."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities roughly flat on the intermeeting period but with high volatility and generally depressed risk appetite. No strong valuation commentary from staff.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral, slightly above, because equities were flat but amid elevated volatility and risk-off tone.

### BANKING_STRESS: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Large banks remain resilient to a substantial economic downturn." "Funding risks at domestic banks remained low." But: "There were indicators of elevated leverage at hedge funds and other nonbank financial institutions."
**Center-of-gravity**: The banking system is healthy and well-capitalized, but nonbank risks are emerging. Overall stress is below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are "resilient" with "low" funding risks.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 75 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated against most major currencies, driven by widening yield differentials between the United States and the rest of the world." "The Chinese renminbi depreciated significantly against the dollar." Japanese authorities intervened to support the yen.
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based dollar strength driven by yield differentials and weak foreign growth outlook. Multiple currencies depreciating significantly.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is well above neutral because the dollar appreciated broadly with significant moves against major currencies.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 65 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Supply–demand imbalances in goods markets expected to unwind" implies goods inflation is currently elevated but expected to ease. No specific goods inflation figure given.
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods inflation is elevated but the staff expects it to moderate as supply-demand imbalances unwind.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 72 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Strong wage growth and the effect of supply constraints on prices—would persist for longer than previously thought." Core PCE at 5.1% is heavily services-driven.
**Center-of-gravity**: Services inflation is elevated, driven by persistent wage growth. Staff raised near-term projections.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: No direct discussion of shelter/housing inflation as a price component. Residential investment decline is discussed but this is a quantity measure, not a price measure.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff's common inflation expectations index... was little changed in the third quarter but remained above pre-pandemic levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are modestly elevated above pre-pandemic levels but stable, not drifting further upward.
