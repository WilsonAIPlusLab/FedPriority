# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-07-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content from these sections.

**Era context**: Mid-2016. The U.S. economy is in a moderate expansion, with unemployment near estimated natural rate levels (~4.9%). Inflation remains below the 2% target. Brexit referendum just occurred (June 23, 2016), causing temporary financial market volatility but no lasting dislocation. The Fed had raised rates once in December 2015 and was proceeding cautiously.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation ~1% over 12 months ending May, core PCE "a little above 1-1/2 percent." Staff projects inflation "slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018." This is clearly below target, restrained by energy price declines and non-energy import price decreases. Direction: below 50. Magnitude: running roughly 0.5-1% below target with "continued to run below" language = moderate shortfall. Score range: 33-38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as SLIGHTLY BELOW potential but closing. Key phrase: "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." The unemployment rate "was projected to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." GDP growth is "moderate." The staff forecast implies the gap is near closure — GDP growing faster than potential means gap is closing but not yet above. However, unemployment projected to be "somewhat below" natural rate suggests the labor market side is slightly beyond full employment. This is a mixed signal: output growing modestly above potential (closing remaining gap) while unemployment is near/slightly below natural rate. Net: approximately at potential, maybe slightly below. Bright-line check: no explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language — the language is about growth RATE relative to potential growth rate, not level. Score: ~48-50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 4.9% in June, which the staff expects to "remain flat over the second half" then "gradually decline through the end of 2018." Unemployment projected to run "somewhat below" the natural rate. Payroll employment increased "briskly" in June but Q2 as a whole "noticeably slower." Average hourly earnings +2.5% over 12 months. Job openings declined "albeit from an elevated level." Initial claims "remained low." This is a labor market near full employment — not tight by historical standards but not slack either. UE at 4.9% anchors this around 48-52. With the forecast of declining below natural rate and "brisk" June payrolls, I'll score slightly above neutral. Score: ~50-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending: "Growth in real PCE appeared to have picked up" — "solid pace," "reasonably upbeat" sentiment, gains in income and wealth. This is positive. Business investment: "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to have declined for a third consecutive quarter." Nonresidential structures also declining. Housing recovery "had slowed." Government purchases declining. Net exports "near-neutral." The consumer side is solid (~55) but business investment is weak (~30-35), housing sluggish. Net: the mix is below-trend overall given the investment drag. Score: ~42-45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states: "Domestic financial conditions remained accommodative." Also: "Overall financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained accommodative." Residential mortgage conditions "became more accommodative." Consumer credit "remained largely accommodative." Bright-line check: "accommodative" = MUST be < 50. Score: ~30-35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields fell sharply post-Brexit then mostly reversed. The language describes orderly yield movements driven by policy expectations and safe-haven flows — no dysfunction. Brief mention of "some financial markets, particularly certain foreign exchange markets, experienced brief periods of strained liquidity" post-Brexit, but this was FX, not Treasuries. "Broad-based market dislocations did not develop." No Treasury-specific dysfunction described. Yield movements are normal price adjustment. Score: ~38-42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Investment-grade spreads "ended the period somewhat lower, on net"; speculative-grade spreads "declined notably." Corporate bond issuance "stayed robust." However, credit quality "continued to weaken" with "default rates...remained elevated relative to the ranges that typically prevail during expansions." CMBS spreads "remained elevated." SLOOS showed "modest net fractions...tightened their C&I lending standards." Mixed: spreads compressing but from elevated levels, with credit quality deteriorating. Net level: still somewhat favorable overall given accommodative conditions but with pockets of stress. Score: ~40-45.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities increased on net over the period. VIX fell to "the lower end of its distribution of the past few years." Staff notes "equity risk premiums were unchanged on net" in the financial stability section, and "valuation pressures also remained at a moderate level." No alarm about overvaluation, no distress. Score: ~42-46 (roughly fair valued, no particular concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff judges "vulnerabilities overall remained at a moderate level." Financial-sector leverage "assessed as still low." "Financial system had been resilient to the Brexit vote." However, maturity and liquidity transformation vulnerabilities "judged to be somewhat higher in the near term." Money market fund concerns noted. European bank equities underperformed (Italian banks). Some C&I and CRE lending standard tightening. Net: low to moderate stress. Bright-line check: "resilient" and "low" leverage suggest below 50. Score: ~38-42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Post-Brexit, "the U.S. dollar...strengthened on what appeared to be safe-haven flows." Staff outlook mentions "a slightly stronger path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a restraining factor. Dollar "was weaker against most emerging market currencies." Mixed but net modest strength. Bright-line check: strengthening language = must be > 50. The "slightly stronger path" and post-Brexit strengthening support this. Score: ~55-58.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declining, non-energy import prices decreasing. These feed into goods prices being low. Score: ~30-33.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed in detail. Core CPI at 2.25% includes services, but no staff-specific services breakdown. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Most survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance, while market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low." Michigan survey "edged up in June and was unchanged in early July." Other measures "generally little changed." Staff sees downside risk: "the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged lower." Anchored but with some downside concern. Score: ~43-46.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "inflation was still projected to be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is running well below the 2% target at about 1% headline and 1.5% core, with the staff projecting it to remain below target through 2018.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running significantly below the 2% target with persistent restraining factors.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 49 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the staff continued to forecast that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018"
- "The unemployment rate was projected to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is approximately at potential — GDP growth is modestly above potential growth (closing any residual gap), and unemployment is near the natural rate, projected to edge below it.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is approximately at neutral because GDP growth slightly exceeds potential growth and unemployment is near the natural rate. No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" level language.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate rose to 4.9 percent in June, partly reversing its decline in the previous month."
- "The unemployment rate was projected to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is near full employment with unemployment at 4.9%, near the natural rate, with moderate wage growth of 2.5% and low initial claims.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is approximately at neutral — unemployment near natural rate implies balanced labor market conditions.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) appeared to have picked up in the second quarter" and "continued to rise at a solid pace"
- "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to have declined for a third consecutive quarter."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Solid consumer spending is partially offset by persistent weakness in business investment (declining three consecutive quarters), a slowing housing recovery, and declining government purchases, yielding a below-trend net reading.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the investment drag and weak housing/government spending offset solid consumption.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No staff discussion of productivity trends.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Domestic financial conditions remained accommodative over the intermeeting period."
- "Overall financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff explicitly and repeatedly characterizes financial conditions as accommodative across multiple sectors (corporate, residential mortgage, consumer credit).
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because "accommodative" means loose/supportive conditions, which scores low on the tighter=higher scale. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "broad-based market dislocations did not develop, apparently because market participants had prepared for a significant risk event."
- "some financial markets, particularly certain foreign exchange markets, experienced brief periods of strained liquidity" (FX, not Treasuries)
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury yields moved on fundamentals (policy expectations, safe-haven flows) without market dysfunction. Brief FX strains post-Brexit did not spread to Treasuries.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there was no Treasury market dysfunction — yield movements reflected normal repricing.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds...ended the period somewhat lower, on net, and spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds declined notably."
- "the credit quality of nonfinancial corporations continued to weaken...default rates on bonds issued by nonfinancial corporations...remained elevated relative to the ranges that typically prevail during expansions."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spreads are compressing and corporate bond issuance is robust, but underlying credit quality is weakening with elevated default rates, keeping the overall level in the low-normal range rather than deeply compressed.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and conditions are accommodative, but elevated defaults prevent a very low score.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad stock price indexes increased, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- "Valuation pressures also remained at a moderate level...corporate bond and equity risk premiums were unchanged on net."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equities rose with VIX declining, and staff describes valuation pressures as "moderate" — roughly fair valued with no particular alarm.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly below neutral — no valuation distress, moderate valuations noted.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 40 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the staff continued to judge that vulnerabilities overall remained at a moderate level and noted that the financial system had been resilient to the Brexit vote."
- "vulnerabilities stemming from financial-sector leverage were assessed as still low" but "those from maturity and liquidity transformation were judged by the staff to be somewhat higher in the near term"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system is broadly sound with low leverage, resilient to the Brexit shock, but with some emerging concerns around maturity transformation and CRE lending standard tightening.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the system is "resilient" with "low" leverage, though not at the lowest stress levels given pockets of concern.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the U.S. dollar and the Japanese yen strengthened on what appeared to be safe-haven flows" (post-Brexit)
- Staff outlook incorporates "a slightly stronger path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a restraining factor.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar strengthened post-Brexit on safe-haven flows, and the staff forecast embeds a slightly stronger dollar path, though the dollar weakened against EM currencies later in the period.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because net dollar appreciation occurred and the staff projects a stronger dollar path. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 12. INFLATION_GOODS: 31 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports"
- "energy prices and the prices of non-energy imports were expected to begin steadily rising this year"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Goods price inflation is subdued, held down by declines in energy and import prices, though expected to begin rising.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — goods prices are being restrained by energy and import price declines.

### 13. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific staff discussion of services inflation separately from headline/core measures.

### 14. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing activity discussed but shelter/housing inflation not specifically addressed.

### 15. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance, while market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low."
- Risk to inflation projection "weighted to the downside, reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged lower."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Expectations are generally anchored but with market-based measures remaining low and the staff flagging downside risk that expectations may have edged lower.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — anchored but low market-based compensation and downside risk concern.
