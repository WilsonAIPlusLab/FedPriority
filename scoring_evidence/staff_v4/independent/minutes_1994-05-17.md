### Era Context
May 1994: Mid-cycle tightening. The Fed had begun raising rates in February 1994. The economy was expanding briskly after the sluggish early-1990s recovery. Unemployment at 6.4% was still above NAIRU estimates of that era (~5.5-6%). Inflation was subdued but commodity/materials prices were rising. The bond market was in a significant selloff.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint but with upward pipeline pressures.
- "Broad measures of inflation had remained subdued and labor cost increases had been moderate" -- clearly contained.
- "Indexes of consumer and producer prices had increased slightly thus far in 1994."
- Core CPI over 12 months "increased less than they had over the previous twelve months" -- decelerating.
- BUT: "prices of industrial materials had continued to rise," "producer prices of crude materials other than food and energy was substantially above its year-ago level," "prices of some important construction materials had increased sharply."
- Labor costs: "employment cost index...rose more slowly in the first quarter of 1994 than in any quarter of 1993."
- Staff forecast: "limited margins of slack...little or no further reduction in the core rate of inflation was anticipated."
- Net: Finished goods/consumer inflation is subdued and decelerating. Pipeline pressures (crude/intermediate materials) are building. Staff sees no further disinflation ahead. Score: ~42. Below midpoint because current inflation is subdued, but pipeline pressures prevent going lower.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Subdued current inflation with rising materials = modestly below neutral. 42 fits.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees economy as ABOVE midpoint -- expanding substantially, nearing/at capacity.
- "economic activity had expanded substantially on balance thus far in 1994"
- "Rates of industrial capacity utilization had risen rapidly in recent months and were at very high levels in many industries"
- Staff forecast: "would expand in the second half of 1994 at a rate close to the growth of the economy's potential" -- implying it's currently at or slightly above potential.
- "limited margins of slack in labor and product markets over the forecast horizon"
- "diminishing slack in the economy" (from intermeeting policy context, line 162)
- Bright-line: "diminishing slack" and capacity at "very high levels" and "limited margins of slack" = economy near or slightly above potential. Score should be >50.
- Score: 55. The economy is modestly above potential -- substantial expansion, high capacity utilization, limited slack, but not overheating language.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor as BELOW full tightness but improving rapidly.
- Unemployment rate: 6.4% in April -- "another slight decline."
- "Nonfarm payroll employment increased sharply in March and April"
- "average workweek of production or nonsupervisory workers remained at an unusually high level"
- "Increases in labor costs continued to moderate in early 1994" -- no wage pressure.
- Staff forecast: "limited margins of slack in labor...markets"
- UE at 6.4% in 1994 context: NAIRU was estimated around 5.5-6%. So labor market is approaching but not yet at full employment. Some slack remains.
- Per the rubric: UE 5.5-7% = 31-45 range. But "limited margins of slack" and sharp employment gains push toward upper end.
- Score: 43. Approaching full employment but UE still at 6.4% with moderating wage growth. Below the neutral/tight threshold but getting close.

- Net: Very strong investment, solid but moderating consumer spending. Clearly above trend.
- Score: 62. Strong investment growth, above-trend consumption even with April dip.
