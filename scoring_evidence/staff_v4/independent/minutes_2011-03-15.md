# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-03-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: March 2011 — roughly 20 months into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment still at 8.9%, economy growing at a moderate pace, QE2 underway. Oil prices rising sharply due to MENA unrest. Japan earthquake/tsunami just occurred. Recovery proceeding but substantial slack remained.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as modestly below neutral. Headline pushed up by oil/food commodity prices ("sizable increases in prices of crude oil and other commodities pushed up headline inflation"), but core PCE "stayed near the very low levels seen in late 2010," and "labor cost pressures remained muted." Staff outlook: inflation increase "expected to be mostly transitory." The key tension: headline up, core very low. Staff clearly signals core subdued, headline temporarily elevated. This nets to slightly below neutral — headline rising but core anchored very low. Score in the 42-48 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy below potential. "Economic recovery continued to proceed at a moderate pace." Capacity utilization "remained well below its 1972-2010 average." Unemployment at 8.9% — far above natural rate. Staff outlook: "persistence of slack in labor and product markets." "Jobless rate was still expected to decline slowly and to remain elevated at the end of 2012." Bright-line: slack remains = MUST be < 50. Score in the 33-38 range — recovering but substantial slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as quite loose. UE at 8.9%, "long-duration unemployment was still elevated," "wide margin of slack in the labor market." Some firming: "labor market continued to show signs of firming," private payrolls rose, initial claims trending lower. But UE at 8.9% anchors this firmly in the 25-32 range per rubric (UE > 7% = 16-30). Giving credit for improvement signals, score ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "appeared to have increased at a modest pace in early 2011 after rising briskly in the fourth quarter." Housing "continued to be depressed." Business E&S "appeared to rise further" — solid. Nonresidential construction "dropped further." Consumer sentiment "dropped back in early March." Net: below-trend consumer spending offset by decent E&S investment, but housing very weak. Score ~40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff mentions "productivity rose further" in the context of labor costs. Brief mention, suggests above-trend. Score ~55 with low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes generally easing conditions. Dealer survey: "further easing...in the price and nonprice terms." "Increasing credit availability." Corporate bond yields edged down relative to Treasuries. Consumer credit "showed further signs of improvement." BUT: "financing conditions for commercial real estate generally remained tight," CRE borrowing "persistently tight," small business credit "still tight." Leverage "remained well below the levels reached prior to the recent financial crisis." Net: conditions accommodative/easing overall but pockets of tightness. Bright-line: "accommodative" / easing = MUST be < 50. Score ~35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No mention of market dysfunction. Treasury yields moved around due to economic data, MENA events, and Japan — all normal price adjustments. "Little changed over the intermeeting period." No liquidity problems, auction issues, etc. Score ~35 (normal functioning, no stress).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on corporate bonds "edged down." Syndicated loan prices "continued to move up." Municipal bond strains "eased." Bond issuance "robust." But C&I loan spreads "remained elevated," CRE conditions "tight." Bright-line: spreads narrowing overall suggests < 50. Score ~38.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Broad stock indexes "about unchanged." Equity risk premium "continued to be quite elevated relative to longer-term norms" — this suggests equities are NOT overvalued (high risk premium = cheap). Volatility rose on MENA events. Staff doesn't express valuation concern. Score ~48 (roughly fair, slight stress from volatility but risk premium suggests undervaluation if anything).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff reports: bank credit declined, core loans contracted. But credit quality improving, consumer delinquencies declining, dealer financing terms easing. No mention of bank failures or capital concerns. Some weakness (loan contraction, elevated C&I spreads) but no acute stress. Bright-line: No "well-capitalized" or "stressed" language explicitly. Score ~48 — mixed signals, mild residual stress from crisis.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined about 1 percent" and staff outlook notes "a slightly lower foreign exchange value of the dollar." Bright-line: depreciation = MUST be < 50. Score ~40.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "Uptick in prices of core goods after four months of declines." "Prices of nonfuel industrial commodities also rose sharply." Energy prices up sharply. Score ~55 — goods prices turning up from declines.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed separately from core. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Measures of house prices softened again in December and January." Housing depressed. This suggests deflation in housing/shelter. Score ~30.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." Near-term expectations up substantially on gasoline, but longer-term "still within the range that prevailed over the preceding few years." 5-10yr forward inflation compensation "little changed." Score ~50 — well-anchored.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30)**: Both well below 50, consistent. Gap is 6 points — coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50, gap is 4 points — coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Both below 50, gap is 3 points — coherent. Both reflect easing/accommodative conditions.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (35)**: Both low, consistent — no crisis, accommodative conditions.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (45) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~55, Housing ~30, Expectations ~50. Weighted average around 45 — consistent with parent score.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of underlying inflation were subdued and longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "The 12-month change in this core price index stayed near the very low levels seen in late 2010." But also: "Sizable increases in prices of crude oil and other commodities pushed up headline inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Core inflation very low, but headline rising on energy/food; staff views the increase as mostly transitory. Net slightly below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is slightly below neutral because core remains very low despite commodity-driven headline increases.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The rate of capacity utilization in manufacturing...remained well below its 1972-2010 average." "The projected persistence of slack in labor and product markets." "The jobless rate was still expected to decline slowly and to remain elevated at the end of 2012."
**Center-of-gravity**: Substantial slack remains in both labor and product markets despite moderate recovery. Economy well below potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references persistent slack. Bright-line satisfied (slack remains = < 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate...ticked down to 8.9 percent in February." "Labor cost pressures remained muted...restrained by the wide margin of slack in the labor market." "Long-duration unemployment was still elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 8.9% with wide slack anchors this firmly in the loose range, despite signs of improvement in payrolls and claims.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.9% signals substantial labor market slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer spending appeared to have increased at a modest pace in early 2011 after rising briskly in the fourth quarter." "Real business investment in equipment and software appeared to rise further." "Activity in the housing market continued to be depressed." "Nonresidential construction expenditures dropped further."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — modest consumer spending and decent E&S investment, but depressed housing and falling nonresidential construction drag the net below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because housing depression and modest consumer spending outweigh E&S gains.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 55 +-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Productivity rose further" (in context of explaining muted labor costs).
**Center-of-gravity**: Brief mention suggests continued productivity growth, modestly above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly above neutral based on limited evidence of continued gains.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Dealers reported a further easing, over the previous three months, in the price and nonprice terms." "Credit conditions continued to improve for many firms." "Financing conditions for commercial real estate generally remained tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based easing in financial conditions with pockets of tightness (CRE, small business). Overall accommodative.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative). Bright-line satisfied (easing/accommodative = < 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities were little changed over the intermeeting period." Yield movements were driven by economic data, MENA events, and Japan — all normal price adjustments. No mention of liquidity problems or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning; yield movements reflect standard repricing, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral — no market dysfunction described. Bright-line satisfied.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds edged down relative to those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities." "Net issuance of investment- and speculative-grade bonds was robust." But: "Spreads of interest rates on C&I loans...remained elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads generally narrowing and credit availability improving, though some residual tightness in bank lending. Below-normal stress overall.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and issuance is robust. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes were about unchanged, on net." "The staff's estimate of the spread between the expected real equity return for S&P 500 firms and the real 10-year Treasury yield...continued to be quite elevated relative to longer-term norms."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities roughly flat; elevated equity risk premium suggests if anything undervaluation rather than overvaluation. Near neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — no staff valuation concern expressed; elevated risk premium suggests equities not overpriced.

### BANKING_STRESS: 48 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Bank credit declined, on average, in January and February as a result of a contraction in core loans." "Measures of the credit quality of nonfinancial firms continued to improve." "Delinquency rates on credit card loans...continued to decline through January, nearly returning to their longer-run averages."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed — core loans contracting and C&I spreads elevated suggest lingering stress, but improving credit quality and declining delinquencies point toward healing. Approximately neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is near neutral — residual weakness (loan contraction) balanced by improving credit quality.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined about 1 percent, on balance, over the intermeeting period." Staff outlook: "a slightly lower foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest dollar depreciation over the period.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because dollar depreciated. Bright-line satisfied (depreciation = < 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 55 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "An uptick in prices of core goods after four months of declines." "Prices of nonfuel industrial commodities also rose sharply on net." "Consumer energy prices posted further sizable increases."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices turning up from a period of declines, with commodity and energy prices rising sharply.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_GOODS is above neutral due to rising commodity and goods prices.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 30 +-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of house prices softened again in December and January." Housing market "depressed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Continued house price declines suggest deflationary shelter conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_HOUSING is below neutral due to falling house prices.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Measures of forward inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead were little changed." Near-term expectations up on gasoline but longer-term "still within the range that prevailed over the preceding few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Well-anchored at target; near-term fluctuations do not disturb the longer-term anchor.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is at neutral — anchored at target.
