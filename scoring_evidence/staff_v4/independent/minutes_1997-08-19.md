# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — August 19, 1997 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** from a pre-2005 FOMC meeting. The text is **unsegmented** — staff analysis and participant/member discussion are intermingled. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable language ("the information reviewed," "the staff forecast") from participant-attributable language ("members noted," "the Committee's discussion").

### Era Context
August 1997 is in the late-1990s expansion. The economy is growing solidly, unemployment is low (4.8%), and inflation is remarkably subdued despite tight labor markets. The Asian financial crisis is beginning but has not yet hit the US economy. The Fed funds rate is at 5.5%.

- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested that..." (lines 211-228)
- The formal directive summary (lines 431-500)

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint.
- Evidence: "price inflation was still subdued" (line 47-48); "Consumer price inflation picked up slightly in July from the slow pace" — core CPI "rose in July at the same low rate recorded for both the first six months of 1997 and the twelve months ended in July" (lines 130-134); PPI "prices of finished goods edged down for a seventh consecutive month" (lines 135-136); "Prices of finished goods other than food and energy were unchanged over the twelve months ended in July" (lines 137-138). Staff forecast: "core consumer price inflation was forecast to drift slightly higher" (line 228).
- Magnitude: Inflation is "subdued," prices of finished goods falling/flat. This is clearly below target in level terms. However, the staff forecast expects it to drift higher with "higher resource utilization." The current reading is very benign — "subdued" maps to 26-40 range. Given that core CPI is running at a "low rate" and PPI is outright declining, I'll place this around 32.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation is subdued/low. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees economy as at or modestly ABOVE potential.
- Evidence: "economic activity was expanding moderately" (line 39); Staff forecast: "the expansion of the economy would be damped in the second half" and "In 1998, the economy would expand at a pace in line with the growth of its estimated potential" (lines 211-215); "higher resource utilization" (line 227). The directive: "economic activity is expanding at a moderate pace" (line 432).
- The staff forecast implies the economy is currently running slightly above potential (hence needing to slow to potential pace in 1998), and references "higher resource utilization." The phrase "close to, or perhaps even above, its sustainable potential" appears in member discussion (line 253), so I exclude that. But staff's own "higher resource utilization" and the structure of the forecast (economy needs to slow TO potential) imply modestly above.
- Bright-line: "higher resource utilization" implies above potential -> must be > 50.
- Score: 54. Modestly above potential; growth is moderate, not overheating.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT, above midpoint.
- Evidence: "employment had continued to expand at a strong pace and the unemployment rate was at a low level" (lines 44-46); "The civilian unemployment rate, at 4.8 percent in July, matched its low for the current economic expansion" (lines 57-58); "hiring remained robust at midyear" (line 433); "Increases in labor compensation had remained moderate even though labor markets were tight" (lines 46-47). Staff forecast: "labor compensation gradually accelerating in the context of higher resource utilization" (lines 226-227).
- UE at 4.8% — this is below the estimated NAIRU for this era (roughly 5-5.5%). The text explicitly says "labor markets were tight." Per the rubric, UE at 4.8% with tight labor market language should score in the 53-65 range. "Tight" with UE at 4.8%, strong hiring, matching expansion low — I'll score 62.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Staff says tight. Score > 50. Confirmed.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Direction: Staff sees spending as ABOVE trend.
- Evidence: Consumer spending "picked up" after spring lull, retail sales "rose briskly in June and July" (line 73); "Real business fixed investment increased substantially further in the second quarter, reflecting a broad-based surge in spending on producers' durable equipment" (lines 88-89); "Business fixed investment increased substantially further in the second quarter and available indicators point to further sizable gains" (lines 441-443). Housing "well maintained" (line 42), "relatively buoyant" (line 85). Nonresidential construction was "sluggish" (line 96).
- Net: Consumer spending rebounding strongly ("briskly"), business equipment surging, housing buoyant, but nonresidential construction sluggish. The overall picture is above trend. "Substantially" for business investment, "briskly" for retail. Score: 60.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says strong. Score > 50. Confirmed.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Direction: Staff implies productivity is notably strong.
- Evidence: Staff forecast section does not directly discuss productivity. However, in the data section, the combination of subdued inflation despite tight labor markets is noted. The member discussion (which I must exclude) mentions productivity explicitly. In the staff-attributable content, I find limited direct discussion. The staff forecast mentions "higher resource utilization" pushing costs up, which implicitly accounts for productivity. The data section describes computer prices on "steep downtrend" and rapid equipment spending, consistent with a productivity story, but staff doesn't explicitly discuss productivity growth.
- Since the staff-attributable text does not explicitly discuss productivity, I'll score N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Direction: Staff sees conditions as accommodative/loose.
- Evidence: "credit was readily available to most consumers, although lenders continued to tighten terms for marginal borrowers" (lines 80-81); "Market interest rates generally have declined somewhat further" (lines 451-452); "Share prices in equity markets increased on balance" (lines 452-453). Staff forecast mentions conditions are supportive. Member discussion (excluded) references "accommodative financial climate" (line 295).
- Staff-attributable: credit readily available, market rates declined, equity prices up. This paints a picture of loose/accommodative conditions. But I should be cautious about member content. The staff data notes credit readily available, rates declining, equities up — these all point to accommodative conditions.
- Bright-line: Accommodative/supportive -> must be < 50.
- Score: 32. Conditions are clearly on the loose side — credit available, rates declining, equities rising.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Direction: No dysfunction discussed.
- Evidence: "Most other market interest rates declined further on balance over the period in an atmosphere of greater volatility in financial markets" (lines 166-167). There is mention of "heightened demand for liquidity as a result of recently higher volatility in bond and equity markets" (lines 196-197), but this is about money fund flows, not Treasury market dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or settlement issues.
- The mention of volatility is notable but does not constitute market dysfunction. Normal functioning with some volatility.
- Score: 42. Some volatility noted but orderly markets, no dysfunction.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
- Direction: Staff sees credit conditions as favorable/loose.
- Evidence: "credit was readily available to most consumers" (line 80); "lenders continued to tighten terms for marginal borrowers" (line 81); total domestic nonfinancial debt expanding at moderate pace (lines 206-208). No explicit spread levels mentioned, but credit availability is described as ample for most borrowers.
- The tightening for marginal borrowers is a small offset but overall credit is readily available.
- Score: 35. Below normal stress — credit available, though with some selectivity at the margin.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- Direction: Staff notes equity prices rose.
- Evidence: "Share prices in equity markets increased on balance over the period" (lines 171-172). No valuation commentary from staff. Large gains in household financial wealth mentioned (line 79, 216-217).
- Per the rubric, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55. Prices are rising, no staff concern expressed. Score: 45.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
- Direction: Not specifically discussed in staff-attributable content. The mention of credit availability and monetary aggregate flows through banks is operational, not about banking health or stress.
- Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
- Direction: Dollar is strengthening, above midpoint.
- Evidence: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the other G-10 currencies rose significantly on balance over the intermeeting period" (lines 176-177); "The dollar's substantial increase against the German mark and other continental European countries" (lines 179-180).
- Bright-line: Dollar appreciation -> must be > 50. "Rose significantly" and "substantial increase" — this is notable strength.
- Score: 67. "Significantly" and "substantial" warrant a score well above 50.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed by staff. N/A.

Score: 45. Slightly below midpoint given market judgment of improved inflation outlook.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62)**: Gap of 8 points. Reasonable — labor market can be tighter than the overall output gap suggests given productivity growth absorbing some of the demand pressure. No contradiction.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (60)**: Gap of 6 points. Consistent — strong spending is driving modestly above-potential growth. No contradiction.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (35)**: Both in the loose/accommodative range. Consistent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC is loose while Treasury stress is low-normal. Consistent — no crisis, markets functioning well.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) vs sub-dimensions**: INFLATION_GOODS at 28, INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS at 45. The parent score of 32 is consistent with very low goods inflation pulling down the headline, while expectations are near-anchored.
