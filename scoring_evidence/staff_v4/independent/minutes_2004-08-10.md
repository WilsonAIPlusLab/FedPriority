# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2004-08-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (unsegmented, pre-2005). Staff review content is mixed with participant/member discussion. I must carefully separate staff-attributable language from Committee member views.

**Staff-attributable content** is found primarily in:
- Paragraphs beginning "The information reviewed at this meeting..." through the trade/international data paragraph (lines 16-49)
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." paragraph (line 52)

**Participant/member content** (to IGNORE for scoring): paragraphs starting with "In the Committee's discussion...", "Committee members generally agreed...", "Policymakers focused...", "In their remarks...", "In their discussion of recent labor market trends...", "In their review of the outlook for prices...", "In the Committee's discussion of policy..." etc.

### Era Context
August 2004: The economy is in the recovery phase following the 2001 recession and the subsequent "jobless recovery." The Fed just began raising rates in June 2004 from the 1% floor. Unemployment at 5.5%, still above pre-recession levels. Energy prices were rising. Growth had been strong but was showing signs of softening.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Staff says: "Core consumer price inflation moderated substantially in May and June, though sizable increases in food and energy prices continued to push up overall consumer price inflation." Households' expectations "fell somewhat." Unit labor costs "increased faster in the second quarter than in the first." ECI "advanced at about the same rate as it had over the previous year-and-a-half."
- Staff forecast: "Consumer price inflation was projected to remain low over the forecast period as the sharp increases in energy and import prices experienced earlier in the year partially unwound. Slack resource utilization through 2005 was also expected to help hold down inflation."
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Core inflation was moderating, energy/food pushed headline up but that was expected to ease. The staff forecast explicitly projects low inflation ahead.
- Magnitude: "moderated substantially" for core; energy/food pushing up headline. Mixed signals but net below neutral. Score ~42. The energy component adds some pressure, but the staff's overall characterization is "low" and "moderated."
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says core moderated, projected to remain low. Score below 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Staff says: "economic growth softened somewhat in recent months." Consumer spending "fell off significantly." Employment gains "slowed sharply." Industrial production "decelerated modestly."
- Staff forecast: "Slack resource utilization through 2005 was also expected to help hold down inflation." Economy expected to "continue to expand at a solid pace."
- Direction: Staff sees economy as BELOW potential. "Slack resource utilization" is the key phrase.
- Bright-line check: "Slack resource utilization" = below potential. Score MUST be < 50.
- Magnitude: The economy was growing (expanding at solid pace) but with slack remaining. Not deep recession. Score ~40. Recovery with remaining slack.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Slack remains = below potential. Score < 50 correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Staff says: Unemployment rate "edged down to 5-1/2 percent in July." Employment growth "slowed sharply in June and July." Weakness "widespread." Labor force participation "moved up slightly."
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as below full employment. UE at 5.5% is above NAIRU estimates of the era (~5%). Gains slowed sharply.
- Magnitude: UE 5.5% with slowing payrolls. The rubric says UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45. But we're at the bottom of that range with some improvement trend. Score ~38.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 5.5% with slowing gains = not tight. Score < 50 correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Staff says: Consumer spending "slowed sharply in the second quarter, posting only a small increase." Business investment "was solid" with growth "a little above the pace of the first quarter." Housing "remained strong."
- Direction: Mixed. Consumer spending sharply weaker; business investment solid; housing strong. Net assessment: below trend given the sharp consumer slowdown.
- Magnitude: The consumer weakness dominates the discussion. Business investment was solid but uneven. Net ~42.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Sharp consumer slowdown with solid business investment = moderately below trend. Score < 50 correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Staff forecast mentions "robust growth in structural productivity" as supporting income. Also mentions "rising disposable income, which would likely continue to be propelled by robust growth in structural productivity."
- Direction: Staff sees productivity as clearly above trend. "Robust growth in structural productivity."
- Score ~62. "Robust" is a moderately strong positive characterization.
- Polarity check: Higher = more productivity. "Robust" = above trend. Score > 50 correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Staff forecast: "relatively accommodative monetary policy" and "Favorable financial conditions" supporting growth.
- Staff review of market developments: Treasury yields "dropped significantly." Corporate credit quality "remained strong." Equity markets "declined appreciably."
- Bright-line check: "accommodative" / "favorable" = MUST be < 50 (loose conditions).
- Direction: Conditions are accommodative/loose.
- Score ~30. "Accommodative" and "favorable" with strong credit quality.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Staff says accommodative/favorable. Score < 50 correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Staff says: "Yields on intermediate- and long-term nominal Treasury securities dropped significantly over the intermeeting period." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, etc. Normal yield movements from policy expectations.
- Direction: Normal market functioning. Yields moved on data/policy expectations.
- Score ~40. Normal functioning, no stress signals.
- Bright-line check: No actual dysfunction mentioned. Score in normal range.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
- Staff says: "corporate credit quality remained strong, and yields on investment-grade bonds moved roughly in line with those on Treasury securities. Speculative-grade yields, however, fell by less."
- Direction: Credit quality strong, IG spreads roughly stable, HY spreads maybe slightly wider relative to Treasuries (since spec-grade yields fell less than Treasury yields). But overall credit quality is described as "strong."
- Score ~35. Generally favorable credit conditions with a hint of differentiation in speculative grade.
- Bright-line check: "Strong" credit quality suggests compressed/favorable spreads, score < 50.
- Polarity check: Higher = more stress/wider spreads. Strong credit quality = low stress. Score < 50 correct.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- Staff says: "broad indexes declined appreciably, reflecting the soft economic data, concerns about energy prices, and guidance from corporations pointing to a less-optimistic trajectory for earnings."
- No valuation commentary from staff per se. Just price movement with fundamental attribution.
- Score ~55. Stocks declined appreciably but no distress language. Default toward 50-55 for price decline without valuation commentary.
- Polarity check: Higher = more stress/concern. Appreciable decline = modestly above neutral. Score ~55 correct.

- Bright-line check: No clear appreciation or depreciation. Stable = near 50.
