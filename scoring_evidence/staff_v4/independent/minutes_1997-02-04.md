### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as modestly contained but with emerging wage pressures. "Recent data pointed to little change in underlying inflation trends" (749). Core CPI "increased less over the twelve months ending in December than over the previous twelve months" (753-754). But "worker compensation...rose considerably further during the closing months of 1996" (757-758) and "both measures were up appreciably more than in 1995" (759-760). Staff forecast: "consumer price inflation...was forecast to increase slightly this year in the context of some further pickup in the growth of labor compensation" (831-834).
- The staff view is: underlying price inflation stable/slightly declining, but compensation rising and forecast to push CPI up slightly. This is a mixed signal — current inflation contained but staff projects a slight uptick.
- Direction: Slightly below to at neutral. Core inflation below previous year. But rising compensation and staff forecast of slight increase push toward neutral.
- Magnitude: "little change in underlying inflation trends" and "consumer prices rose modestly" = near neutral. Staff forecasts "increase slightly" = modest upward bias.
- Score: 45. Below the 2% target era (pre-explicit target), but inflation contained with a slight upward forecast. Not quite at neutral because current readings are declining.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees stable-to-slightly-increasing inflation with rising wages. 45 is slightly below neutral — fits "contained but with upward wage pressures beginning." Adjust to 47 — the compensation acceleration and forecast of slight CPI increase suggest near-neutral with a slight upward lean.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy operating above potential. "The expansion would be sustained at a rate a bit above the economy's estimated growth potential" (819-820). "With resource utilization high and rising" (830). The economy "expanded briskly" in Q4 (670). Industrial production "increased sharply" (691). Manufacturing capacity utilization "rose considerably further...to a level slightly above its long-term average" (696-697).
- Bright-line check: "a bit above the economy's estimated growth potential" and "resource utilization high and rising" = above potential. Score MUST be > 50.
- Magnitude: "a bit above" = modest. Capacity utilization "slightly above its long-term average." Not "considerably" or "significantly" above.
- Score: 55. Modestly above potential, consistent with the late-90s anchor of 57-63, but staff language is "a bit above" which is on the modest end.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees tight labor markets. "The tightness in labor markets had persisted" (671). Unemployment at 5.3% (684). "Private payroll employment rose appreciably further" (678). "Gains remained widespread among employment categories" (680). Compensation "rose considerably further" (758).
- UE at 5.3% in 1997. NAIRU was estimated around 5.5-6% at the time, so 5.3% would have been seen as somewhat below NAIRU.
- The rubric says UE ~4.5-5% = near NAIRU (46-52 range). But in 1997, the estimated NAIRU was higher. Staff explicitly says "tightness in labor markets had persisted" — this is clearly tight, not neutral.
- Score: 58. Tight labor market with persistent tightness, widespread employment gains, rising compensation. Not extreme but clearly tight.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees strong spending overall but with mixed components. "Consumer spending also increased substantially" (666-667). "Consumer spending registered a sizable increase over the fourth quarter" (699-700). But "growth of business fixed investment moderated considerably" (717). Housing starts "fell appreciably" (709). Staff forecast: consumer spending to "moderate somewhat" (821), business investment to "expand less rapidly" (827-828), housing to "decline somewhat" (823).
- Net: Q4 was strong (driven by consumption and exports). But investment moderated and housing weakened. Staff expects moderation ahead.
- The Q4 data is the "reviewed" period. Economy "expanded briskly" overall. Consumer spending "sizable increase." But investment slowdown and housing decline partially offset.
- Score: 58. Above trend overall reflecting brisk Q4 expansion, but moderated by investment slowdown and housing softness. Not "robust" enough for 60+.
