# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2017-11-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered.

### Era Context
November 2017: The economy is in the latter stage of a long, slow expansion. Unemployment has fallen to 4.2%, well below most estimates of the natural rate. Inflation remains persistently below the 2% target despite tightening labor markets — the "missing inflation" puzzle. The Fed has begun balance sheet normalization and markets are pricing in a December rate hike. Equity markets are elevated, volatility is historically low, and financial conditions are accommodative.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Total PCE inflation was "a bit more than 1.5 percent" and core PCE was "about 1.25 percent" — both well below the 2% target. Core was "a little lower than in 2016." The staff revised down its forecast slightly for next year, acknowledging "a bit of the unexplained weakness in core inflation this year may carry over." Staff expects transitory factors but projects inflation not reaching 2% until 2019. This is clearly below target — direction: below 50.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. The staff "continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output through 2019" and the unemployment rate was "projected to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." The upside risk that "inflation could increase more than expected in an economy that was projected to move further above its longer-run potential" directly confirms above-potential language. Bright-line rule: must be > 50.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 4.2% is below most estimates of the natural rate. "Labor market conditions generally continued to strengthen." Unemployment rates for demographic groups were "close to the levels seen just before the most recent recession." The staff projects unemployment to "decline gradually" and "continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." ECI up 2.5%, average hourly earnings up "almost 3 percent." With UE at 4.2% and projected below natural rate, this is clearly tight — above 50.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as SOLID but mixed. Real PCE "slowed in the third quarter, likely reflecting in part temporary effects of the hurricanes." However, fundamentals "remained supportive of solid increases in real PCE in the near term." Consumer sentiment at "its highest level since before the most recent recession." Business equipment spending rose "at a brisk pace." Nonresidential structures declined. Residential investment "declined further." Net: consumer spending temporarily soft but fundamentally solid; business equipment strong; structures and housing weak. Overall around trend or modestly above.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE. "Domestic financing conditions generally remained accommodative." "Financing conditions for large nonfinancial firms remained accommodative." "Credit also remained readily available to households, except for higher-risk borrowers." Corporate bond spreads "narrowed modestly." Short-term funding "remained stable." Bright-line rule: "accommodative" language must be < 50. However, the rubric anchor says 2017-19 neutral period should be 46-54. The staff explicitly uses "accommodative" repeatedly, which points to < 50 per the bright-line rule. I'll score in the low 40s — accommodative but not excessively so.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "increased modestly" due to FOMC communications, tax reform expectations, and better economic data. MBS spreads "little changed." Option-implied volatility on swap rates "near historically low levels." No dysfunction language whatsoever. Normal market functioning with low volatility. Score around 30-35 (quiet, well-functioning).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds "narrowed modestly." CMBS spreads "remained near the lower end of the range seen since the financial crisis." Banks reported "a notable increase in the share of banks that narrowed loan spreads for C&I loans." Bright-line: "narrowed" = compressed = must be < 50. Spreads are tight/narrow. Score in low 30s.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes rose notably." VIX "remained near historically low levels." Staff vulnerability assessment: "Asset valuation pressures across markets were judged to have increased slightly... and to have remained elevated." The word "elevated" regarding valuation pressures suggests overvaluation concern — score below 50 (lower = more overvaluation concern). Score around 30-35.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: Staff says leverage and vulnerabilities from maturity/liquidity transformation in the financial sector "continued to be low." However, CRE lending standards "continued to be somewhat tight" and banks were tightening auto/credit card lending standards. Banks are healthy but exercising some caution. Low stress. Bright-line: healthy = below 50. Score around 35.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose nearly 3 percent." Multiple currencies depreciated against the dollar. Bright-line: appreciation = must be > 50. A 3% rise in the intermeeting period is notable. Score around 60-63.

**Inflation Sub-components:**
- INFLATION_GOODS: Gasoline prices "moved up sharply" from hurricanes but subsequently moved down. Consumer goods prices not separately discussed in detail. N/A.
- INFLATION_SERVICES: Not separately discussed. N/A.
- INFLATION_HOUSING: Not separately discussed as an inflation component. N/A.
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Measures of inflation compensation from TIPS "were little changed on net." Well-anchored, stable. Score ~48 (anchored, perhaps trivially below target given the context of below-target inflation).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core PCE price inflation... was about 1.25 percent over that same period." "The staff's forecasts for core inflation and, thus, for total inflation were revised down slightly for next year, reflecting the judgment that a bit of the unexplained weakness in core inflation this year may carry over into next year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Core PCE at 1.25% is well below the 2% target, with the staff acknowledging persistent unexplained weakness and revising forecasts down.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because core PCE is running roughly 75bp below target with downward revisions to the forecast.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output through 2019." "The unemployment rate was projected to... continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential and projected to move further above, consistent with "modestly faster pace than potential output."
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says GDP growth exceeds potential and unemployment is below natural rate. Bright-line satisfied: "above potential" -> >50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate moved down to 4.2 percent in September." "The unemployment rate was projected to decline gradually over the next couple of years and to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: With UE at 4.2% and projected below the natural rate, the labor market is tight. Wage growth is picking up (ECI 2.5%, AHE ~3%) but not dramatically. Tightness is moderate — clearly above NAIRU but not extreme.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because unemployment is below the natural rate and labor conditions "continued to strengthen."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP expanded at a solid pace in the third quarter despite hurricane-related disruptions." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property continued to rise at a brisk pace." "Real PCE growth slowed in the third quarter, likely reflecting in part temporary effects of the hurricanes."
**Center-of-gravity**: Overall GDP growth is solid; business equipment investment is brisk; consumer spending temporarily soft from hurricanes but fundamentals supportive; residential investment declined. Net assessment is roughly at trend to slightly above.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is approximately at neutral — solid GDP growth and brisk business investment offset by temporary hurricane disruption and weak housing.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in the staff review.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financing conditions generally remained accommodative." "Credit also remained readily available to households, except for higher-risk borrowers in some markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff repeatedly characterizes conditions as "accommodative" with readily available credit and narrowing spreads. Some tightening for subprime borrowers provides a modest offset.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff explicitly says conditions are "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 32 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Near-term measures of option-implied volatility on 10-year swap rates remained near historically low levels." "The FOMC's September announcement... appeared to have had little effect on either Treasury yields or MBS spreads."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets are functioning smoothly with historically low volatility. Yield changes were orderly and driven by fundamental factors. No dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are quiet and well-functioning with no signs of dysfunction. Bright-line satisfied: no dysfunction language, score < 55.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed modestly." "CMBS spreads... remained near the lower end of the range seen since the financial crisis."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are narrowing and near post-crisis lows across corporate bonds and CMBS, indicating ample credit availability and low credit stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrow and narrowing further. Bright-line satisfied: "narrowed" -> <50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes rose notably." "Asset valuation pressures across markets were judged to have increased slightly, on balance... and to have remained elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly flags "elevated" asset valuation pressures that have increased further. VIX at historically low levels. This indicates overvaluation concern.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (overvaluation territory) because staff judges valuation pressures as "elevated" and increasing.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Leverage and vulnerabilities from maturity and liquidity transformation continued to be low." "The staff continued to judge that the overall vulnerabilities were moderate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial sector leverage is low, but the staff notes moderate overall vulnerabilities and some tightening of lending standards in CRE and consumer lending. Low stress with minor concerns.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because financial sector leverage is low and vulnerabilities are at most moderate. Bright-line satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 62 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose nearly 3 percent over the intermeeting period." Multiple foreign currencies (Canadian dollar, euro, EME currencies) depreciated against the dollar.
**Center-of-gravity**: A nearly 3% appreciation of the broad dollar index in a single intermeeting period is a notable move, driven by rising U.S. rates and tax reform expectations.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated nearly 3%. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation -> >50.
