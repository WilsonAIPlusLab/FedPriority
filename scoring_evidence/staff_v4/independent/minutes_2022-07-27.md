# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-07-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2022. This is near the peak of the post-pandemic inflation surge. The Fed had begun aggressive rate hikes (75bp in June 2022). CPI hit 9.1% in June 2022. The labor market was extremely tight with unemployment at 3.6%. GDP had declined in H1 2022 (technical recession debate), but the labor market remained strong.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as far above midpoint. PCE inflation at 6.3% (12-month), core PCE at 4.7%, CPI at 9.1%, core CPI at 5.9%. Trimmed mean PCE at 4.0%. "Consumer price inflation remained elevated." Wage growth at 5.1%. Short-run expectations elevated. Price pressures broadening to core goods and services. Staff projects core PCE at 4.0% for 2022, stepping down to 2.6% in 2023. Risks to inflation skewed to the upside. This is squarely in the 76-90 range per the rubric anchors (82-88 for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees GDP above potential but the gap is closing. "The projected level of real GDP remained above potential this year, the gap was expected to have closed by the second half of 2023." Real GDP declined in H1 2022 but labor market remained tight. Bright-line rule: "above potential" -> must be > 50. However, GDP has declined, momentum is weakening, and the projection was "noticeably weaker." The staff still says above potential, so score must be > 50 but only modestly, given the weakening trajectory. Score around 53-56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very tight. "The labor market continued to be very tight, and labor demand remained strong." Unemployment at 3.6%. Job openings rate "remained at a high level." Wage growth "rapid and broad based" at 5.1%. Unemployment projected to start rising in H2 2022. With UE at 3.6% and "very tight" language, this maps to 68-73 per anchors (65-75 for 2022-23).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. "Real PCE rose at a modest pace in the second quarter, while business investment, residential investment, and government purchases all posted declines." Manufacturing output moved lower. Forward-looking indicators weakened broadly. The projection is "noticeably weaker." Consumer spending modest + investment declining = below trend. Score around 35-40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes tightening conditions. "Current and prospective financial conditions that were expected to provide less support to aggregate demand growth." Credit availability "appeared to tighten for most businesses and some households." Borrowing costs for shorter-term rates increased. Small businesses faced "notably higher borrowing costs." Mortgage rates near highest since 2010. However, the financial stability assessment characterized vulnerabilities as "moderate, down from notable in January." Credit remained "generally available." This is somewhat tight — score around 58-63.

Bright-line check: Conditions are tightening/less supportive -> score must be > 50. Confirmed.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined significantly over the intermeeting period. "Market liquidity had deteriorated in the oil and equities markets since January, but market functioning continued to be orderly." No mention of Treasury market dysfunction — yields moved in an orderly way reflecting growth concerns. Score around 42-47 (normal functioning, some liquidity deterioration noted but orderly).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate bond spreads ended the period slightly wider." "Credit spreads widened." Spreads on municipal bonds widened slightly. But credit quality remained strong — low defaults, solid household credit quality. Spreads are widening but from compressed levels. This maps to 55-60 range (somewhat wide, mild widening). Bright-line: "widened" -> score > 50. Confirmed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Over the intermeeting period, "Broad equity price indexes were higher." But in the broader assessment: "Equity and corporate debt prices declined significantly since the last assessment, reflecting concerns over slower growth and lower risk appetite." VIX "remained significantly above pre-pandemic levels." Staff doesn't give explicit valuation commentary beyond noting prices declined. Score around 55-60 (modestly depressed/stressed, declined but not distressed).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banks passed stress tests, "participating banks could absorb losses from a severe recession without breaching regulatory minimums." "The staff assessed that leverage in the financial sector remained moderate." Bank capital ratios declined somewhat but system sound. Credit quality on bank books "remained sound." SLOOS indicated concerns about future tightening. This is low stress with minor forward-looking concerns. Score around 35-40. Bright-line: system described as sound/moderate -> must be < 50. Confirmed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The exchange value of the dollar continued to appreciate." "The dollar appreciated somewhat further against most currencies and particularly against the euro." Latin American currencies depreciated against the dollar. This is clear dollar strength. Bright-line: appreciation -> must be > 50. 2022 Q3 anchors suggest 75-85 for DXY at 20-year highs. "Continued to appreciate" and "somewhat further" suggest ongoing strength. Score around 68-73.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Goods prices elevated. CPI at 9.1% includes energy/food. Core goods inflation implied by the broad measures being well above target. Commodity prices "moved lower from elevated levels" recently but overall inflation broadening. Score around 72-78.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Continued broadening of price pressures to core goods and services." Core PCE at 4.7%. Services inflation clearly elevated. Score around 68-72.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Residential real estate prices continued to rise" and "valuations have been elevated." This refers to asset prices not shelter CPI directly, but housing costs were clearly rising. Score around 68-72.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of short-run inflation expectations remained elevated; by contrast, some measures of longer-term inflation expectations moved lower in recent weeks." Staff notes risk that "inflation expectations would become unanchored." Mixed — short-run elevated, longer-term still somewhat anchored but risk flagged. Score around 62-67.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70)**: Divergence of ~15 points. Explained by the GDP declining while labor market remains very tight — this is exactly the mid-2022 puzzle where GDP contracted but employment kept growing.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (37)**: Divergence of ~18 points. Explained by the fact that output is still above potential in level terms (per staff) but spending/investment is weakening rapidly. The flow measures (consumption, investment) are deteriorating while the level of output relative to potential hasn't fully adjusted.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (57)**: Coherent — both modestly above neutral, tightening.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs TREASURY_STRESS (44)**: Coherent — financial conditions tightening but Treasury markets functioning normally.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-dimensions**: Parent ~83. Goods ~75, Services ~70, Housing ~70, Expectations ~64. The parent is higher because the headline numbers (PCE 6.3%, CPI 9.1%) are extremely elevated and the sub-dimensions capture components. Energy and food (not separately scored) are major contributors pushing the parent higher.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 83 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.3 percent over the 12 months ending in May, and core PCE price inflation...was 4.7 percent." "The 12-month change in the consumer price index (CPI) was 9.1 percent." "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid and broad based." "The staff viewed the risks to the inflation projection as skewed to the upside."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes inflation as deeply elevated across all measures, with broad-based pressures, rapid wage growth, and upside risk — placing this firmly in the "substantially above target" range.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far above neutral because PCE at 6.3%, CPI at 9.1%, and core measures well above 2% target with upside risks.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 55 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The projected level of real GDP remained above potential this year, the gap was expected to have closed by the second half of 2023." "U.S. real gross domestic product (GDP) had declined over the first half of the year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly states output is above potential but the gap is rapidly closing, with downside risks and weakening momentum. Modestly above potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff says "above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied (above potential -> > 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The labor market continued to be very tight, and labor demand remained strong." "The unemployment rate was unchanged in June at 3.6 percent." "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid and broad based...5.1 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff uses explicit "very tight" language with unemployment at 3.6% and rapid wage growth — clearly in the very tight range.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE at 3.6% is far below natural rate with broad-based wage pressures.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE rose at a modest pace in the second quarter, while business investment, residential investment, and government purchases all posted declines." "Manufacturing output moved lower in May and June, and forward-looking indicators of manufacturing activity weakened broadly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest consumer spending combined with declining investment across business, residential, and government sectors points to clearly below-trend spending.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because only consumer spending was positive (and only modestly so), while all other components declined.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity in staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Current and prospective financial conditions that were expected to provide less support to aggregate demand growth." "Credit remained generally available, though credit availability appeared to tighten for most businesses and for some households." "Small businesses that borrow on a regular basis faced notably higher borrowing costs in June."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes conditions tightening from multiple angles — higher borrowing costs, reduced credit availability for some, less support to demand — but credit still generally available. Somewhat tight.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because conditions are tightening and providing "less support." Bright-line rule satisfied (tightening -> > 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 44 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Market liquidity had deteriorated in the oil and equities markets since January, but market functioning continued to be orderly." Treasury yields declined over the intermeeting period in orderly fashion reflecting growth concerns.
**Center-of-gravity**: No Treasury market dysfunction. Yields moved in response to growth outlook and policy expectations. Market functioning orderly. Some broader liquidity deterioration noted but not in Treasuries specifically.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because markets functioned in orderly fashion. Bright-line rule satisfied (no actual dysfunction -> score 36-50).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 57 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Corporate bond spreads ended the period slightly wider." "Credit spreads widened." "Spreads on municipal bonds widened slightly." But "The credit quality of nonfinancial corporations remained strong with low volumes of defaults."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened across corporate and municipal markets but only slightly, with credit quality still strong. Somewhat above normal.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads "widened." Bright-line rule satisfied (widened -> > 50).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 56 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Equity and corporate debt prices declined significantly since the last assessment, reflecting concerns over slower growth and lower risk appetite." VIX "remained significantly above pre-pandemic levels." Over the intermeeting period, equities were higher on net.
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff notes significant declines in equity prices from the broader assessment period with elevated volatility, but no explicit valuation distress language. Modestly depressed.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral because prices declined significantly with reduced risk appetite, though no extreme distress language.

### BANKING_STRESS: 36 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff assessed that leverage in the financial sector remained moderate." "The recently concluded stress tests suggested that participating banks could absorb losses from a severe recession without breaching regulatory minimums." "The credit quality for C&I and CRE loans on banks' books remained sound."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system described as sound with moderate leverage, passing stress tests, and solid credit quality. Low stress with some minor forward-looking concerns from SLOOS.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are well-capitalized and sound. Bright-line rule satisfied (sound/moderate -> < 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The exchange value of the dollar continued to appreciate." "The dollar appreciated somewhat further against most currencies and particularly against the euro." "Most Latin American currencies depreciated against the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based dollar appreciation continuing, driven by widening yield differentials. Notable strength especially against the euro.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "continued to appreciate" broadly. Bright-line rule satisfied (appreciation -> > 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 75 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.3 percent." "The 12-month change in the consumer price index (CPI) was 9.1 percent." Commodity prices moved lower recently but from elevated levels.
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods inflation clearly elevated, with energy and food prices major contributors to the 9.1% headline CPI. Some recent easing in commodity prices but overall level still very high.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 70 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core PCE price inflation...was 4.7 percent." "Continued broadening of price pressures to core goods and services."
**Center-of-gravity**: Services inflation elevated as price pressures broaden beyond goods, with core measures well above target.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 70 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Residential real estate prices continued to rise, and the staff noted that although valuations have been elevated." "CRE prices continued to rise, and valuation pressures appeared to be increasing."
**Center-of-gravity**: Housing/shelter prices rising with elevated valuations. This captures asset-price increases that feed into shelter CPI with a lag.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 63 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of short-run inflation expectations remained elevated; by contrast, some measures of longer-term inflation expectations moved lower in recent weeks." Staff flagged risk that "inflation expectations would become unanchored."
**Center-of-gravity**: Short-run expectations elevated but longer-term showing some anchoring. Staff views unanchoring as a risk rather than current reality.
