# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score ONLY staff-attributable content and ignore participant views (the final paragraphs referencing "participants" are excluded).

**Era context**: March 2016. The economy is in a mid-cycle expansion. The Fed raised rates in December 2015 for the first time since the GFC. Global growth concerns and oil price declines are prominent headwinds. Unemployment is near 5%, inflation running below 2%. This is a "moderate growth, below-target inflation" environment.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.25% over 12 months, core PCE at 1.75%, both below the 2% target. Staff notes restraint from energy prices and non-energy import prices. Staff projects inflation will "increase gradually" but remain "slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018." Survey-based expectations "little changed" but Michigan 5-10yr "below its typical range." Risks to inflation "weighted to the downside." This is clearly below-target inflation: score in the 35-42 range per the anchor for 2013-19.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as slightly BELOW potential but closing. The staff projects "real GDP would expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018" and "the unemployment rate was expected to gradually decline further and to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This means the gap is currently slightly negative but closing — staff expects it to move above potential. GDP growth "picking up somewhat" from previous quarter but still moderate. Risks to GDP "tilted to the downside." The current state is slightly below potential with the expectation of moving above. Score 45-48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as NEAR the midpoint, maybe slightly above. Unemployment at 4.9%, "labor market conditions were continuing to improve," payroll gains "at a solid average monthly pace," participation rate increased. However, compensation rising at only a "modest pace" (ECI ~2%, AHE 2.25%). The unemployment rate of 4.9% is near estimated NAIRU but the staff revised the natural rate down slightly. Per the rubric, UE at 4.9% maps to "near NAIRU" (46-52 range). The solid job gains and declining part-time for economic reasons push slightly higher. Score ~50-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED, slightly below trend overall. Consumer spending "appeared to pick up some" with "solid growth" expected, supported by wealth effects and sentiment. But business investment is weak: equipment spending "increasing only modestly," forward-looking indicators "generally soft," drilling/mining "continued to fall." Trade is a drag. Net: consumer spending is decent but business investment is soft, so the composite is somewhat below trend. Score ~43-46.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: No explicit discussion of productivity growth. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat LOOSE/accommodative. "Domestic financial conditions generally eased, on balance" since the January meeting. Stock prices rose, spreads narrowed, volatility declined. Over the longer December-to-March period, "U.S. financial market conditions had registered relatively small changes, on balance." Financing for investment-grade firms "continued to be relatively accommodative." Consumer credit "generally remained accommodative." CRE conditions "tightened somewhat" but "remained accommodative." The word "accommodative" appears multiple times. Bright-line rule: accommodative = MUST be < 50. Score ~35-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined at first then rose back, ending "little changed, on balance." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. The market was "turbulent" early in the period but this was about risk sentiment, not Treasury-specific dysfunction. Normal functioning. Score ~40-42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed picture. Corporate bond spreads narrowed over the intermeeting period. Speculative-grade spreads "declined appreciably." Investment-grade spreads "slightly lower." But over the December-March period, spreads were "little changed." Default rate on nonfinancial bonds "remained somewhat elevated." Downgrades outpaced upgrades. Speculative-grade credit quality "continued to show signs of deterioration." CRE/CMBS spreads "continued to widen." So the level is somewhat elevated with recent improvement. Score ~50-55: spreads are modestly above normal given the elevated defaults, downgrades, and CRE widening, even though the intermeeting direction was toward improvement.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity prices rose on balance over the intermeeting period. "Broad equity market indexes increased." VIX declined. No staff commentary on valuations being stretched or depressed. Prices "ended this interval only a bit lower" vs December. No valuation concern expressed. Default to 45-50 per rubric. Score ~48.

**BANKING_STRESS**: No specific discussion of banking system health or capital adequacy. CRE lending continued to grow. C&I loan growth "strong." No mention of bank stress. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated against most foreign currencies" since January meeting. But "the broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was also roughly unchanged, on net, since the December meeting." Staff outlook notes the dollar's FX value could "rise substantially" as a downside risk to inflation. The intermeeting move was depreciation, but over the longer period roughly unchanged. Mixed: recent weakening but level still elevated from prior appreciation. The net since December is "roughly unchanged." Score ~45-48 (slight weakness to stable).

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declining, non-energy import prices declining. Goods prices seem depressed. Score ~30-35.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI at 2.25%, boosted by "some categories of goods and services whose prices tend to be volatile." Housing/shelter likely contributing. Limited specific services discussion. Score ~50-55.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Further increases in home values through January." Housing recovery continues. But no specific discussion of shelter/rent inflation. N/A (not enough direct inflation discussion for housing prices as inflation component).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "generally little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10yr "below its typical range of the past 15 years." Market-based inflation compensation "remained low" and at "the lower end of its historical range." 5-year compensation rose with oil prices but 5-10yr ahead "little changed." Staff sees risks as "weighted to the downside, reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down." This is below anchored-at-target. Score ~38-42.

### Bright-Line Checks
- **OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy slightly below potential currently. Score 46 < 50. CHECK.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Accommodative" multiple times. Score 36 < 50. CHECK.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed but defaults elevated, downgrades outpacing upgrades. Score 53 > 50 reflects the elevated stress component. The recent narrowing doesn't erase the elevated level. CHECK.
- **BANKING_STRESS**: N/A. N/A.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Net roughly unchanged since December. Score 46 near 50. Since intermeeting period showed depreciation, slightly below 50. CHECK.
- **TREASURY_STRESS**: No dysfunction. Score 40 < 55. CHECK.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) ~ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51)**: Close, 5-point gap. Labor market slightly ahead of output gap, consistent with labor being a lagging indicator and UE already near NAIRU while GDP growth is still moderate. Acceptable.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) ~ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (44)**: Within 2 points. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) ~ CREDIT_SPREADS (53)**: 17-point gap. Explanation: Overall financial conditions are described as accommodative (equities up, consumer credit easy, investment-grade credit easy), but credit spreads specifically show stress in speculative-grade, CRE, and energy sectors. This is a genuine split in the data: broad conditions are easy but credit risk in lower-quality segments is elevated.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) ~ TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Both below 50. Coherent for non-crisis period.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) ~ sub-dimensions**: Goods ~32, expectations ~40. Weighted toward below-target. Parent score 38 is consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer price inflation was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by decreases in both consumer energy prices and the prices of non-energy imports." "Core PCE price inflation...was 1 3/4 percent over the same 12-month period." Staff projects inflation "still to be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018."
- Center-of-gravity: Inflation is clearly below target with multiple restraining factors, and staff projects it will remain below target for years. Risks weighted to the downside.
- Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below 2% with downside risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real GDP would expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018" (forward-looking, implying current level is still below potential). "The pace of expansion in real gross domestic product (GDP) was picking up somewhat from the previous quarter." Risks to GDP "tilted to the downside."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy is growing but still slightly below potential; staff expects the gap to close and move above potential over the forecast horizon, but currently it has not yet done so.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is slightly below neutral because GDP is currently below potential with the gap expected to close ahead.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate declined to 4.9 percent in January and remained at that level in February." "Labor market conditions were continuing to improve." "Labor compensation continued to rise at a modest pace" (ECI ~2%, AHE 2.25%).
- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment is very near the staff's estimate of NAIRU (which was revised down slightly). Job gains are solid but wage growth is still modest, suggesting the labor market is roughly at full employment but not yet tight.
- Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is approximately at neutral, consistent with unemployment near the natural rate and modest wage growth.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 44 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Consumer spending "appeared to pick up some" with factors pointing "toward solid growth in real PCE." But "real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to be increasing only modestly" and forward-looking indicators "were generally soft." Net exports "would continue to weigh on growth."
- Center-of-gravity: Decent consumer spending is offset by soft business investment and trade drag, yielding a slightly below-trend net assessment.
- Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because business investment softness and trade drag partly offset solid consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- No discussion of productivity in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Domestic financial conditions generally eased, on balance, since the January FOMC meeting: Stock prices rose, equity price volatility declined, and credit spreads on corporate bonds narrowed." "Financing conditions for investment-grade nonfinancial firms continued to be relatively accommodative." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets generally remained accommodative."
- Center-of-gravity: Multiple sectors described as accommodative; conditions eased over the intermeeting period. Clearly loose/supportive.
- Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly characterizes conditions as "accommodative" and "eased." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Yields on 5- and 10-year nominal Treasury securities...were little changed, on balance, over the intermeeting period." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or dysfunction. Markets were "turbulent" early but that was broad risk sentiment, not Treasury-specific stress.
- Center-of-gravity: Normal Treasury market functioning with some yield volatility that resolved. No dysfunction signals.
- Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no evidence of market dysfunction. Yield movements reflected normal risk-sentiment shifts.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "The credit quality of speculative-grade nonfinancial corporations continued to show signs of deterioration." "The default rate on nonfinancial bonds remained somewhat elevated compared with typical levels outside recession periods." "The volume of downgrades of ratings outpaced that of upgrades." But intermeeting movement was favorable: speculative-grade spreads "declined appreciably," and over the December-March period, spreads were "little changed."
- Center-of-gravity: The level of credit stress is modestly above normal given elevated defaults and deteriorating speculative-grade quality, even though the recent trajectory improved.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly above neutral because defaults are elevated and credit quality is deteriorating in speculative-grade, even as spreads have recently narrowed.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 48 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad equity market indexes increased, on balance, over the intermeeting period." "Broad equity price indexes ended this interval only a bit lower" since December. No staff commentary on valuation levels.
- Center-of-gravity: No valuation concern expressed; equities roughly stable with recent recovery. Default to near-neutral per rubric guidance.
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is approximately neutral because staff discusses price movements without expressing valuation concern.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: N/A**
- No specific discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or stress in the staff sections.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 45 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "The dollar depreciated against most foreign currencies" (intermeeting period). "The broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was also roughly unchanged, on net, since the December meeting." Staff notes risk that "the foreign exchange value of the dollar could rise substantially."
- Center-of-gravity: Net roughly stable since December with intermeeting depreciation. Slight weakness bias.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly below neutral, reflecting the intermeeting depreciation, with the level roughly unchanged since December. Bright-line: depreciation mentioned -> below 50. CHECK.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Restrained in part by decreases in both consumer energy prices and the prices of non-energy imports." Energy prices declining; import prices declining.
- Center-of-gravity: Goods prices are clearly depressed by energy and import price declines.

"Market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low." Staff sees risks "reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down."
- Center-of-gravity: Expectations are somewhat below the well-anchored-at-target level, with market-based measures low and survey measures showing some slippage.
