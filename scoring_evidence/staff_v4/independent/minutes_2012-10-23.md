# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-10-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly delineated sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant views are included.

### Era Context
October 2012: The U.S. economy is in a slow recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.8%. The Fed had just announced QE3 (MBS purchases) at the September meeting. Europe is in recession, providing headwinds. Inflation is generally subdued. This is consistent with the 2010-14 recovery anchors.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Consumer prices picked up due to energy, but core inflation ("excluding food and energy") was "subdued." Longer-run expectations "remained stable." Near-term expectations declined. Wages "stayed relatively modest" and "subdued." Staff forecasts inflation "would be subdued through 2014" with "significant resource slack." Direction: below neutral, around the 35-40 range.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. "Economic activity continued to increase at a moderate pace." Unemployment at 7.8% is "still elevated." Manufacturing "declined," capacity utilization "edged down." "Significant resource slack was projected to persist over the projection period." GDP growth "would not materially exceed increases in potential output in 2013." Progress in reducing unemployment expected to be "relatively slow." Bright-line check: "slack remains" language clearly present — score MUST be < 50.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 7.8% (well above NAIRU). Part-time for economic reasons "continued to be elevated." Long-duration unemployment "remained high." Other indicators "did not show decided improvement." Wage gains "subdued." Per the rubric, UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending "rose at a solid pace," retail sales "increased considerably," motor vehicle sales "expanded." But business equipment spending "appeared to edge down," shipments "continued to decrease," nonresidential construction "decreased further." Housing improving but from low base. Manufacturing "declined." Net assessment: consumer spending solid but business investment weak — this nets to slightly below trend, around 40-43.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. Score N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE (below midpoint). "Financial conditions generally eased appreciably early in the intermeeting period." Corporate bond yields at "record low level," spreads "narrowed." Mortgage rates "declined." Bank credit "continued to expand at a moderate rate." Banks "continued to report having eased their lending standards." Consumer credit "expanded briskly." However, CRE "remained weak" and some late-period deterioration in sentiment. Bright-line check: "eased" / "accommodative" language → score MUST be < 50.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved modestly — "up slightly" for short/medium, "about unchanged" for long-term. No discussion of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. Repo rates "spiked around quarter-end but subsequently more than retraced." This is normal functioning. Score near 35-40 (normal, quiet).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as BELOW normal (favorable). Investment-grade yields at "record low level," spreads "narrowed on net." Speculative-grade yields and spreads "also decreased." Bond issuance at "robust pace" and "picked up significantly." Leveraged loan issuance "remained solid." Consumer ABS issuance "strong." Bright-line check: "narrowed" / "compressed" → score MUST be < 50.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes were a little lower, on balance." Concerns about "corporate profitability." Option-implied volatility "increased, on net." Financial institution equity "modestly lower." This is a mild decline, not distressed. No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 50-55 range per rubric guidance.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: Mixed signals but leaning healthy. CDS spreads for large financial institutions "declined." Earnings "generally in line with expectations." Banks easing lending standards. Unsecured funding markets "improved some." But CRE conditions "remained weak" with "elevated vacancy and delinquency rates." Mortgage delinquency "remained elevated." Overall the banking system is functioning reasonably well with some legacy stress pockets. Bright-line check: no "well-capitalized" language but easing standards and declining CDS suggest moderate health → score below 50 but not deeply so.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on net." Dollar rose against most advanced economies but declined against euro and Asian EM currencies. "Little changed" → score near 46-50 (stable). Bright-line check: "little changed" is neutral.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices drove headline increase; "sharp increases in retail gasoline prices." But this is energy, not core goods. Core goods implicitly subdued. Farm commodities "remained elevated" pointing to "temporary upward pressures." Mixed but leaning slightly above stable for goods narrowly. Score ~45.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: House prices "continued to rise" and "modest increases in house prices added to households' net worth." But this is asset prices, not shelter/rent inflation per se. Minimal direct discussion. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Near-term expectations "declined." Longer-term expectations "moved down to near the lower end of the narrow range." "Long-run inflation expectations were assumed to remain stable." Anchored but on the low side. Score ~42.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (37) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28)**: Both well below 50, consistent. Labor somewhat lower given 7.8% UE and explicit language about elevated slack. Gap ~9 points, reasonable.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (37) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42)**: Both below 50, consistent. C/I slightly higher because consumer spending was "solid" even as investment weak. Gap ~5 points, fine.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (28)**: Both below 50, consistent. Both indicating accommodative/favorable conditions. Gap ~4 points, fine.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (37)**: FC loose, Treasury stress low/normal. Consistent for non-crisis period.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (37) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Goods ~45 (energy-driven temporary), expectations ~42 (anchored low). Parent at 37 reflects core "subdued" assessment, consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Increases in consumer prices excluding food and energy were subdued in August and September." "The staff continued to forecast that inflation would be subdued through 2014."
- Center-of-gravity: Core inflation running below target with energy-driven headline pickup expected to be temporary; staff projects continued subdued inflation.
- Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff characterizes core inflation as "subdued" and expects it to remain so through 2014 with "significant resource slack."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Significant resource slack was projected to persist over the projection period." "Real GDP growth would not materially exceed increases in potential output in 2013."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy growing at moderate pace but well below potential, with persistent slack expected for years.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because "significant resource slack" persists. Bright-line rule satisfied: "slack remains" → score < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate fell to 7.8 percent" (still elevated). "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons...continued to be elevated, while the rate of long-duration unemployment...remained high."
- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 7.8% with extensive underemployment and weak wage growth indicates substantial labor market slack.
- Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 7.8% with elevated underemployment and "subdued" wage gains signals a loose labor market. Per rubric, UE > 7% maps to 16-30.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose at a solid pace in August." "Real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to edge down in the third quarter."
- Center-of-gravity: Solid consumer spending partially offset by weak business investment and declining manufacturing, netting to below-trend aggregate demand.
- Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because business investment edged down and manufacturing declined, despite solid consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- Evidence: No explicit discussion of productivity trends or levels.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financial conditions generally eased appreciably early in the intermeeting period." "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds reached a record low level, and their spreads to yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed on net."
- Center-of-gravity: Conditions clearly accommodative — record low corporate yields, easing bank standards, declining mortgage rates, brisk credit expansion.
- Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff explicitly says conditions "eased appreciably." Bright-line rule satisfied: "eased" → score < 50.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 37 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Short- and medium-term nominal Treasury yields ended the intermeeting period up slightly, and long-term yields were about unchanged on net." Repo rates "spiked around quarter-end but subsequently more than retraced that move."
- Center-of-gravity: Normal Treasury market functioning; modest yield movements with no dysfunction signals. Quarter-end repo spike was transient and fully reversed.
- Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no indication of market dysfunction — only orderly yield adjustments.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 28 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds reached a record low level, and their spreads to yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed on net." "The pace of investment- and speculative-grade bond issuance by nonfinancial firms picked up significantly."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads compressing to historically tight levels with robust issuance across investment-grade and speculative-grade markets.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads "narrowed" to "record low" levels. Bright-line rule satisfied: "narrowed" → score < 50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad equity price indexes were a little lower, on balance." "Option-implied volatility for the S&P 500 index...increased, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- Center-of-gravity: Mild equity decline driven by corporate profitability concerns; no extreme valuation commentary. Essentially neutral with a slight tilt toward concern.
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — modest decline with no staff valuation assessment provided.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Spreads on credit default swaps for large financial institutions declined in recent months." "Financial conditions in the commercial real estate sector remained weak amid elevated vacancy and delinquency rates."
- Center-of-gravity: Banking system improving (declining CDS, easing lending standards, earnings on track) but legacy stress remains in CRE and mortgage delinquency. Below-midpoint stress.
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because CDS spreads declined, earnings were on track, and banks are easing lending standards — indicating improving health (lower stress). CRE weakness prevents going much lower.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- Center-of-gravity: Dollar essentially stable on a trade-weighted basis despite mixed bilateral movements.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is near neutral because the broad index was "little changed." Bright-line rule: no clear appreciation or depreciation → neutral range.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer prices picked up in August and September, primarily reflecting sharp increases in retail gasoline prices." "Consumer food prices rose modestly."
- Center-of-gravity: Energy-driven headline pickup but core goods prices implicitly subdued; food pressures modest and expected temporary.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: N/A**
- No separate discussion of services inflation.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: N/A**
- House prices discussed in asset/wealth context, not shelter inflation.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Longer-term inflation expectations in the survey moved down to near the lower end of the narrow range where they have remained for some time." "Near-term inflation expectations...declined."
- Center-of-gravity: Expectations well-anchored but drifting toward the low end — slightly below the target-consistent midpoint.
