# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format. Three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." No participant content needs to be filtered out.

**Era context**: September 2016 — mid-cycle recovery. Unemployment at 4.9%, inflation running below the 2% target, the Fed had raised rates once in December 2015 and was contemplating further tightening. Dollar had appreciated significantly in 2014-15 but stabilized. Brexit vote was recent (June 2016) but impact was less than feared.

### Directional signals by dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at ~0.75% over 12 months ending July; core PCE at "a little above 1½ percent." Restrained by earlier energy price decreases and non-energy import prices. Inflation expectations measures "little changed" or "remained low." Staff projects inflation to "increase over the next several years" but to remain "marginally below" 2% even in 2019. Risks to inflation "weighted somewhat to the downside." This is clearly below-target inflation.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy approaching/slightly below potential, transitioning toward above. Key language: "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2019" and unemployment "projected to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." GDP growth "increasing at a faster pace in the third quarter than in the first half." But the staff "slightly lowered its assumption for potential output growth." The economy is near potential and projected to move modestly above. Bright-line: "faster pace than potential" → must be > 50 for the forward-looking view. But current conditions are near potential. Score should be around 49-52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor as near full employment. Unemployment at 4.9%, payrolls "expanded strongly," LFPR and E-pop edged up, initial claims "continued to be low," job openings and hires increased. Wage compensation rising at "moderate pace" (~2-2.5%). At 4.9% UE, this is roughly near NAIRU. Projected to decline below natural rate. Score around 50-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed. Consumer spending "increasing solidly" with "solid real PCE growth." But residential investment "continued to be soft." Business equipment spending "rising slowly." Manufacturing "generally been moving sideways since the end of 2014." Government purchases roughly flat. Inventory investment turning positive. Net: solid consumption offset by soft investment. Score around 48-52.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Explicitly discussed: "Labor productivity in the business sector declined slightly over the four quarters ending in the second quarter of 2016." This is clearly below trend. Staff also "slightly lowered its assumption for potential output growth over the medium term" which is related. Score well below 50.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states "Domestic financial conditions remained accommodative." Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms "remained generally accommodative." CRE markets "also remained accommodative." Household financing "generally continued to be accommodative." Bright-line: "accommodative" → must be < 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "increased moderately, on net" reflecting expected policy path and global bond yield movements. No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in Treasuries. This is normal market functioning. The MMF reform caused some disruption in money markets but that's not Treasury market dysfunction per se.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on yields of both investment-grade and high-yield nonfinancial corporate bonds over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities declined somewhat to levels fairly close to their historical norms." Corporate bond issuance was "quite large." CMBS spreads narrowed (though still wider than typical). EME and corporate bond spreads narrowed further. This is near-normal, slightly favorable. Bright-line: spreads near historical norms = around 45-50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes moved down, on net." Volatility was "relatively low" but increased somewhat at end of period. No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 50-55 range per rubric guidance.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed in terms of banking system health or capital adequacy. European bank equity prices "increased on net" mentioned in passing. No discussion of domestic banking stress. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Not explicitly discussed as a standalone topic. There's mention of "decreases in the prices of non-energy imports" and "weak export demand" which could be dollar-related but are not attributed to dollar movements. The euro strengthened after ECB meeting; yen appreciated after BOJ announcement. These are foreign currency moves. No direct staff assessment of dollar strength/weakness. N/A.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: PCE restrained by "earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." Consumer food prices decreased. Goods prices clearly soft.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI at ~2.25% while core PCE at ~1.5%. No specific services breakdown.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing mentioned only in investment context, not shelter/rent inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "little changed, on balance." Market-based measures "remained low" / "near the lower end of their historical range." Michigan survey "edged down." SPF "unchanged." Risks to inflation "weighted somewhat to the downside, partly reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down." This suggests anchored but with some downside concern.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence = below target. Score below 50. ✓
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Near potential trending above. Score ~50. ✓
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. "Accommodative" = low score. ✓
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider. "Near historical norms" / declined = near or slightly below 50. ✓
- TREASURY_STRESS: Normal functioning. Score 36-50. ✓

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (~50) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~51): Consistent — both near neutral. ✓
2. OUTPUT_GAP (~50) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (~49): Consistent. ✓

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." Core PCE "a little above 1½ percent." Staff projects inflation to remain "marginally below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below target at ~1.5% core PCE, with downside risks. Persistent below-target reading with no near-term resolution expected.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running notably below the 2% target and projected to remain below target through 2019.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The pace of real GDP growth was forecast to be faster over the second half of this year than in the first half." "The staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2019." Unemployment at 4.9%, projected to "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is currently near potential with GDP growth picking up and projected to modestly exceed potential going forward. The current moment is approximately at potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is at neutral because the economy appears near potential currently, with the staff projecting it to move modestly above potential. Bright-line: "faster pace than potential" for the projection period supports ≥50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions strengthened in recent months." Unemployment at 4.9%. "Total nonfarm payroll employment expanded strongly, on average, in July and August." Wages rising at "moderate pace" (~2-2.5%). Unemployment projected to "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment with solid job gains but only moderate wage growth, suggesting near-NAIRU conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is roughly at neutral because UE at 4.9% is approximately at NAIRU, with wages rising only moderately.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 49 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be increasing solidly, on net, in the third quarter." But "real residential investment spending continued to be soft" and business equipment spending "appeared to be rising slowly." Manufacturing "generally been moving sideways since the end of 2014."
**Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending is offset by soft residential investment and sluggish business investment, producing a near-trend aggregate.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral because strong consumption is counterbalanced by weak investment components.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 28 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor productivity in the business sector declined slightly over the four quarters ending in the second quarter of 2016." Staff "slightly lowered its assumption for potential output growth over the medium term and in the longer run."
**Center-of-gravity**: Productivity actually declined over the prior year — well below historical trend and a notable concern implied by the lowered potential growth assumption.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is well below neutral because productivity growth was negative over the past year.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 34 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial conditions remained accommodative since the July FOMC meeting." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained generally accommodative." "Financing conditions for households generally continued to be accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff uses "accommodative" repeatedly and explicitly across multiple sectors. Conditions are clearly loose, though not extremely so (some tightening in mortgage access for low-credit borrowers, MMF reform effects).
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because "accommodative" = loose conditions = low tightness score. Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields increased moderately, on net, since the July FOMC meeting, reflecting the slight upward revision in the expected path for the federal funds rate and a rise in global bond yields." No dysfunction language.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with orderly yield increases driven by policy expectations and global factors. MMF reform caused some money market rate pressure but not Treasury dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because yields moved for normal fundamental reasons with no market dysfunction described.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 44 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on yields of both investment-grade and high-yield nonfinancial corporate bonds over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities declined somewhat to levels fairly close to their historical norms." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds was quite large." CMBS spreads narrowed.
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads near historical norms and narrowing, with strong issuance — slightly favorable but not extreme.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly below neutral because spreads are at/slightly below historical norms and declining. Bright-line: "fairly close to historical norms" with "declined somewhat" → slightly below 50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes moved down, on net, since the July FOMC meeting." Volatility was "relatively low" but "increased somewhat in the last few days."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest equity decline with no staff valuation commentary. Default to near-neutral per rubric guidance for price-only moves.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is at neutral; equities declined modestly but no valuation concern expressed.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of domestic banking system health, capital adequacy, or lending standards stress. European bank equity prices mentioned in passing (increased on net) but not scored.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: N/A
**Evidence**: No explicit staff assessment of dollar direction. Foreign currency movements (euro, yen, pound) mentioned in context of foreign central bank actions but no characterization of the dollar's value or trajectory.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed as a standalone topic.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." "Recent decreases in consumer food prices." Goods prices clearly running below stable.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific services inflation breakdown provided by staff.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing discussed only in terms of investment/construction, not shelter/rent costs.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance, while market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low." Michigan survey "edged down." Risks to inflation "weighted somewhat to the downside, partly reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down."
