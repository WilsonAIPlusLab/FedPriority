**Era context**: January 2018. Late-cycle expansion. Tax Cuts and Jobs Act just enacted. Unemployment at 4.1%, well below estimates of NAIRU. Inflation still running below 2% target but expected to firm. Equity markets strong. Dollar weakening. This is a period of solid growth with the economy moving above potential.

Staff projects core to reach 2% in 2019 and total in 2020. Inflation expectations "little changed." This is below target but not severely so — the gap is closing. Direction: below 50.
- Magnitude: "below 2 percent," "1 1/2 percent" core — running moderately below target. Score ~40-43.
- Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Below-target inflation = less pressure. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output ABOVE midpoint. "Real GDP was projected to increase at a somewhat faster pace than potential output through 2020." The unemployment rate was "projected to decline further... and to continue to run well below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Staff also notes the economy "was projected to move further above its potential." Clear above-potential language.
- Bright-line check: "above its potential" and "faster pace than potential output" → MUST be > 50. Confirmed.
- Magnitude: "somewhat faster than potential," "well below" natural rate for unemployment — modestly above potential. Score ~55-58.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 4.1% and projected to "decline further" and "run well below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Wage gains "remained modest" at 2.5%.

Consumer credit conditions "largely supportive." CRE financing "remained accommodative." Residential mortgage conditions "remained accommodative."

- Magnitude: "eased considerably further" is strong language — well into accommodative territory. Score ~25-30.

- Polarity check: Higher = more stress. Evidence says low vulnerability. Score below 50. Correct.
