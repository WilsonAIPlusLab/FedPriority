# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-01-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
- **Type**: Minutes (synthesized summary with staff-attributable language: "the staff revised down," "the staff's projections," "the information reviewed at the meeting indicated")
- **Segmentation**: Post-2005 segmented minutes. Clear staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic and Financial Situation" and "Staff Economic Outlook." The "staff_financial_review" section contains only boilerplate foreign currency authorization language, not financial market analysis. Financial market conditions are instead covered within the economic review section.
- **Scoring boundary**: I stop at "Meeting Participants' Views" — everything before that in the staff sections is scorable.

### Era Context
January 2009 — deep into the Global Financial Crisis. Lehman collapsed in September 2008, TARP enacted October 2008, fed funds rate cut to 0-0.25% in December 2008. The economy is in severe contraction, financial markets remain deeply stressed but showing nascent signs of limited improvement in some pockets.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as well BELOW midpoint.
- Evidence: "Headline consumer prices fell in November and December," "core consumer prices were about flat," "PCE prices appeared to have decelerated over the final three months," "moderation in core PCE prices was widespread across categories of goods and services," "producer prices excluding food and energy fell sharply in the last three months of 2008." Staff outlook: "growth in both core and overall PCE prices expected to be unusually low over the next few years in response to slack in resource utilization."
- Magnitude: "fell," "fell sharply," "unusually low" — strong deflationary language. This is 2009 deflation-fear territory. Anchor: 15-20.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff reports falling prices and expects unusually low inflation. Score should be low. Confirmed.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees output as FAR BELOW potential.
- Evidence: "continued sharp contraction in real economic activity," "staff revised down its outlook for economic activity in the first half of 2009," "staff again expected that unemployment would rise substantially through the beginning of 2010," "slack in resource utilization," "no more than a gradual economic recovery."
- Magnitude: "sharp contraction," "slack in resource utilization," "steep downtrend" — extreme negative gap language. Anchor: 15-22 for GFC trough.
- Bright-line check: "slack in resource utilization" → MUST be < 50. Score ~15. Confirmed.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as extremely LOOSE.
- Evidence: "Conditions in the labor market continued to deteriorate rapidly," "Private nonfarm payrolls fell sharply in December, with substantial losses over a wide range of industries," "unemployment rate increased to 7.2 percent," "share of individuals working part time for economic reasons surged," "staff again expected that unemployment would rise substantially through the beginning of 2010."
- Magnitude: UE at 7.2% and rising — anchor 16-30. "Rapidly deteriorating" + broad-based losses = low end of that range.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. UE 7.2% and surging = very loose. Score low. Confirmed.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Direction: Staff sees consumption and investment as in SEVERE contraction.
- Evidence: "consumer spending continued its significant decline," "Real consumer spending appeared to decline sharply again in the fourth quarter," "investment in equipment and software appeared to contract noticeably," "Real residential construction contracted," "deterioration in business equipment investment intensified."
- Magnitude: "decline sharply," "significant decline," "intensified contraction" — freefall language. Anchor: 15-20.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence is severe weakness. Score low. Confirmed.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Not discussed. Score N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Direction: Staff sees financial conditions as very TIGHT.
- Evidence: "extraordinary financial stresses remained apparent and credit conditions became still tighter for households and businesses," "credit remained unusually tight for both households and businesses," "banks had continued to tighten credit standards and terms on all major loan categories," "the market for nonconforming loans remained severely impaired," "financing for new projects had become even more difficult to acquire."
- Magnitude: "extraordinary stresses," "still tighter," "severely impaired," "unusually tight" — extreme language but with "some signs of easing" in certain pockets. Still crisis-level but slightly off the September 2008 peak.
- Bright-line check: "tight" / "strained" → MUST be > 50. Score ~82. Confirmed.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Direction: Staff sees Treasury market as experiencing significant dysfunction, though with some improvement.
- Evidence: "The market for Treasury coupon securities showed signs of increased impairment late in 2008, followed by some improvement early in 2009," "Trading volumes fell to very low levels at the end of 2008," "Bid-asked spreads in the on-the-run market declined sharply at the beginning of 2009 after having increased at the end of 2008," "the Treasury market remained much less liquid than normal," "the on-the-run premium for the 10-year nominal Treasury note was little changed at very elevated levels."
- This is actual dysfunction language: liquidity impairment, bid-ask widening, volume collapse, elevated on-the-run premium. Improving from worst but still "much less liquid than normal."
- Magnitude: "much less liquid than normal," "very elevated levels" — significant stress. Score ~68.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
- Direction: Staff sees credit stress as very ELEVATED.
- Evidence: "Yields and spreads on corporate bonds decreased noticeably, particularly for speculative-grade firms, but spreads remained high by historical standards," "Conditions in the leveraged loan market remained very poor," "Secondary market prices for leveraged loans stayed near record lows and the average bid-asked spread in that market continued to be very wide," "CMBX index... widening during the intermeeting period from already very elevated levels."
- Magnitude: "High by historical standards," "very poor," "record lows" in loan prices, "very wide" spreads, "very elevated" CMBS spreads. This is near-crisis level but with some narrowing from peak. Anchor: 80-95 for 2008 Q4. Now slightly improved but still extreme.
- Bright-line check: "widened" / "elevated" → MUST be > 50. Score ~82. Confirmed.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- Direction: Staff sees equity market as significantly depressed.
- Evidence: "Broad equity market indexes fell over the intermeeting period," "equity prices had fallen further" (staff outlook), financial firms "substantially underperformed the broader market" with "sizable losses," "equity prices for such companies fell on net, ending the period below their November lows."
- This is price decline language without explicit valuation commentary. However, the context of falling below November lows (which were already crash lows) and distressed financial sector suggests significant stress. Score ~72.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
- Direction: Staff sees banking system as under SEVERE stress.
- Evidence: "a number of large and regional banks reported sizable losses stemming from weak trading results, asset write-downs, and additional increases in loan-loss provisions," "CDS spreads for U.S. bank holding companies rose sharply in mid-January to near their historical highs," "Commercial bank credit fell for the second consecutive month," "Treasury, the FDIC, and the Federal Reserve announced... agreement with Bank of America to provide a package of guarantees, liquidity access, and capital," "banks had continued to tighten credit standards and terms on all major loan categories."
- Magnitude: TARP interventions, BofA bailout, CDS near historical highs, sizable losses. This is systemic crisis. Anchor: 70-85.
- Bright-line check: "stressed" / "sizable losses" / government bailout → MUST be > 50. Score ~80. Confirmed.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
- Direction: Staff sees dollar as strengthening.
- Evidence: "The dollar increased on balance against the currencies of major U.S. trading partners."
- Magnitude: "increased on balance" — modest appreciation. Score ~58.
- Bright-line check: Dollar appreciation → MUST be > 50. Confirmed.

Expectations stable-to-slightly-below, with downside risk from deflation environment. Anchor: 25-35 for 2009 deflation fears. Score ~30.

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (15) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (16)**: Both very low, consistent. Deep recession, massive slack. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (15) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (13)**: Both very low, consistent. Consumption and investment collapsing drives output gap. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (82) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (82)**: Both very high, consistent. Crisis-level tightness with elevated spreads. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (82) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (68)**: FC higher than Treasury stress, which makes sense — Treasury dysfunction is significant but improving, while broader credit conditions remain near crisis levels. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (15) ↔ Sub-dimensions**: Goods ~15, Services ~30, Housing ~20, Expectations ~30. Weighted average roughly 20-25, consistent with parent score of 15 (headline is even lower due to energy price collapses pulling it down further). Reasonably coherent.
