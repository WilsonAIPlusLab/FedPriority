# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-06-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary). It is marked as **unsegmented pre-2009 minutes**, meaning staff and participant content are mixed. I will score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by cues such as "the information reviewed," "the staff reduced/revised/forecast," and the standard economic data review paragraphs that precede participant discussion.

### Staff-Attributable Sections
- **Economic data review** (paragraphs on employment, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, inventories, trade, international, prices): These use characteristic staff review language ("The information reviewed at the June meeting suggested...", "Employment continued to rise...", "Industrial production increased...").
- **Financial markets review**: "Market participants had largely anticipated..." through bond issuance/M2 discussion.
- **Staff forecast**: "In preparation for this meeting, the staff reduced its estimate..."

**Excluded** (participant/member content): Everything from "In their discussion of the economic situation and outlook, participants noted..." onward.

### Era Context
June 2007: Pre-crisis period. The economy was growing moderately, housing was weakening but the broader financial crisis had not yet materialized. The fed funds rate was at 5.25%. Subprime concerns were emerging but not yet systemic.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as modestly above midpoint. Evidence: "Sharp increases in energy prices drove up overall inflation...core inflation seemed to have remained subdued." Core PCE rose only 0.1% in April and similarly in May — "held down, in part, by declines in volatile categories...that were likely to prove transitory." The staff "trimmed its forecast for core PCE inflation slightly in coming quarters" but acknowledged transitory factors were helping. Headline was elevated on energy/food. Staff revised up headline forecast, revised down core. Net: Core moderating but still somewhat elevated (staff notes some decline is transitory), headline elevated. Direction: Modestly above neutral.
- Direction check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff reports headline up on energy, core declining but partly transitory. Score modestly above 50.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy near or slightly below potential. Evidence: "economic activity was expected to increase at a rate a little below that of the economy's long-run potential for the remainder of the year and to rise at a pace broadly in line with potential output growth in 2008." The economy "rebounded in the second quarter from its subpar pace in the first quarter." Capacity utilization "close to its long-run average."
- Bright-line check: "a rate a little below that of the economy's long-run potential" — growth below potential means the gap is slightly negative or closing. Score MUST be < 50 or near 50. The economy is currently near potential (capacity utilization at long-run average) but growth is slightly below potential rate, suggesting a very slight negative gap or at-potential. Score ~48.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as moderately tight. Evidence: Unemployment rate at 4.5% — steady. "Employment continued to rise at a moderate pace." The 4.5% UE rate in 2007 was close to or slightly below NAIRU estimates of the time. The twelve-month change in average hourly earnings "edged lower."
- With UE at 4.5%, this is near NAIRU — slightly tight side. Score ~52-55.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees a mixed but roughly at-trend picture. Consumer spending "slowed somewhat" from earlier rapid pace — "deceleration primarily reflected a flattening out of outlays for goods." But "determinants of household spending were broadly supportive." Business investment: "continued to rise at a modest rate"; spending on equipment and software "about unchanged" but non-transport orders "moved up markedly." Residential investment weak. Nonresidential construction "remained robust."
- Net: Consumer spending moderating from strong to moderate, business investment mixed but improving ex-transport, housing weak. Overall slightly below trend. Score ~44.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not explicitly discussed in staff sections, though there is participant discussion of slower productivity growth. The staff section mentions "the production of high-tech industries also rose, albeit at a relatively sluggish pace." No explicit staff productivity assessment.
- Score: N/A (staff sections do not directly discuss productivity levels/trends).

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff reports: "Gross bond issuance by nonfinancial businesses surged"; "bank lending maintained a strong pace"; "firms retained ample cash in reserve to finance investment"; spreads on speculative-grade bonds "narrowed"; investment-grade spreads "little changed." The expected path of fed funds was "marked up sharply." Interest rates for mortgages increased. Credit conditions described as strong issuance, ample availability.
- Bright-line check: Evidence points to accommodative/loose conditions — strong issuance, ample cash, narrowing speculative-grade spreads. Score MUST be < 50. However, there are hints of tightening: mortgage rates rising, and "in the days just prior to the meeting, the availability of credit to some highly leveraged and other lower-rated borrowers appeared to be tightening a bit" — but this is from participant section, not staff. Staff section shows accommodative conditions. Score ~32.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff reports: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities at all maturities also rose over the intermeeting period, with the most pronounced gains in forward rates three to five years ahead." This is normal yield movement from policy/economic expectations — NOT dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, or market dysfunction.
- Bright-line: Rising yields from policy expectations = normal market functioning. Score 40-45.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff reports: "Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds rose in line with those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving their spreads little changed. In contrast, spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds narrowed." Issuance was surging. Credit freely available.
- Bright-line: Spreads "little changed" to "narrowed" + surging issuance = below-normal stress. Score ~25-30.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff reports: "Equity prices were volatile at times during the intermeeting period, but broad stock price indexes advanced modestly, on net." No valuation commentary — just price movement.
- Per rubric: price moves without valuation commentary → default 45-55. Modest advance → ~47.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff section: "Mortgage debt expanded at a slower pace in the first quarter, reflecting the slowdown in home-price appreciation." The staff mentions tightening of lending standards for nonprime borrowers and rising delinquency rates — but the delinquency/subprime language appears in the housing section as contributing to housing weakness, not as banking system stress per se. "bank lending maintained a strong pace."
- Some early signs of subprime issues but banking system overall sounds operational and lending strongly. Score ~42 (emerging concerns but system still functioning well).
- Bright-line: No "well-capitalized" or "sound" language, but no stressed language either. Moderate, slightly below midpoint.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff reports: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar against other major currencies was little changed, on balance."
- Bright-line: "little changed" = stable → score ~47-50. Score 48.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Not explicitly broken out as a separate category in staff discussion. Consumer goods spending flattened but not goods prices specifically. N/A.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Rent components decelerated; spending on services rose at a solid pace. Brief mention. The rent deceleration suggests modest downward services inflation pressure. Score ~45.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: "the rent components had also decelerated"; "house-price appreciation continued to slow." Score ~38 (below neutral, decelerating shelter costs).

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "household surveys conducted in early June indicated that the median expectation for year-ahead inflation increased further." But "median expectations of longer-term inflation fell back in June and remained in the narrow range seen over the past two years." Also in financial section: "Measures of long-horizon inflation compensation based on inflation-indexed Treasury securities edged slightly higher." Net: short-term expectations up (energy-driven), long-term anchored but very slight upward drift. Score ~54.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53)**: Divergence of 5 points — acceptable. Economy near potential with labor market slightly tight; the staff notes growth slightly below potential rate but UE at 4.5% which is snug.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (44)**: Divergence of 4 points — coherent. Both near/slightly below midpoint.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (27)**: Both below 50 — coherent. Accommodative financial conditions with narrow/stable spreads.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC loose, Treasury normal — coherent for non-crisis period.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (56) ↔ Sub-dimensions**: Services ~45, Housing ~38, Expectations ~54. The headline is elevated on energy (not captured in sub-dimensions), and core is modestly above target. The parent score of 56 captures the overall picture including energy-driven headline inflation and transitory factors in core. Consistent.
