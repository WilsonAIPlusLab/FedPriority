# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2015-10-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: October 2015. The U.S. economy is in the mid-stage recovery from the GFC. Unemployment has fallen to 5.1%, near estimated NAIRU. The Fed is considering liftoff (first rate increase) but has not yet done so. Inflation remains well below the 2% target, partly due to energy price declines and dollar appreciation. Global growth concerns (China slowdown) contributed to market volatility in August-September 2015.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Total PCE prices unchanged from 12 months earlier. Core PCE at 1.25%, well below 2% target. Restrained by energy price declines and non-energy import price declines. Staff projects inflation will increase gradually but remain "slightly below" 2% through end of 2018. This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 26-40 range per the rubric ("below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent").

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential but closing. "Real GDP was increasing at a moderate pace." The staff projects GDP will "expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output from 2016 through 2018," implying the economy is currently below potential but expected to close the gap. Unemployment is expected to "decline gradually and to run a little below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" -- meaning it's currently at or slightly above the natural rate, suggesting the output gap is nearly closed but still slightly negative. Risks to GDP tilted to the downside. Bright-line check: economy is below but near potential -- score should be in the 44-49 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as NEAR but slightly below full tightness. Unemployment at 5.1%, which in 2015 was near the staff's estimate of NAIRU. However, "improvement in labor market conditions had slowed somewhat in recent months." Payroll growth at a "slower pace than earlier this year." Labor force participation edged down. But job openings still at high levels. Score should be around 46-50 -- near NAIRU, balanced conditions.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed conditions, roughly AT trend. Consumer spending "appeared to rise at a solid rate." Real disposable income grew at a "solid pace." Vehicle sales at new high. BUT: industrial production decreased, manufacturing declining due to dollar and oil effects. Business equipment spending appeared "solid" but forward indicators pointed to "more modest increases." Inventory investment "slowed markedly." Housing "mixed" and recovering "slowly." Net: solid consumer spending offset by weak industrial/manufacturing and modest investment outlook. Score around 46-50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE (below midpoint for tightness). "Financing conditions for most businesses and households remained accommodative." However, "tightened somewhat for businesses with lower credit quality." CRE financing "broadly available." Consumer credit "accommodative on balance." Mortgage conditions "eased modestly." This is clearly accommodative overall with some pockets of tightening for lower-quality borrowers. Bright-line: "accommodative" must be < 50. Score around 35-40.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined, reflecting policy expectations and global growth concerns. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. This is normal market functioning. Score in the 30-40 range per rubric guidance for calm markets (2010-15 anchor: 25-35).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees SOME widening. Investment-grade spreads "changed little" but speculative-grade "widened notably across sectors." Spreads "generally near their highest levels in several years and ended the period above their historical medians." Leveraged loan spreads increased. CMBS spreads widened. This is above-neutral credit stress -- spreads are above historical medians and widening for lower-quality credit. Bright-line: "widened" and "elevated" must be > 50. Score around 58-63.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities fell then recovered -- "fell in the first few weeks after the September FOMC meeting but subsequently more than retraced those declines." The financial stability section notes "an increase in risk aversion eased valuation pressures in the corporate bond market." No strong valuation concern language for equities specifically. Score around 48-52 (roughly neutral, price fluctuations but no strong valuation commentary).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff explicitly addresses banking health in the financial stability section: "The U.S. financial system appeared to absorb the shocks without systemic strains, supported by relatively high capital positions of large banking organizations." This is clearly low stress. Bright-line: language indicating system absorbed shocks and is well-capitalized means score MUST be < 50. However, there are concerns about speculative-grade leverage near "record levels" and CRE valuation pressures with "loosening underwriting standards in CMBS markets." Score around 32-38 (healthy with minor concerns).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the dollar's foreign exchange value ended the period little changed on balance." Dollar depreciated then appreciated, netting to roughly unchanged. However, the economic review prominently notes "the appreciation in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a drag on manufacturing and exports. This references the cumulative appreciation since mid-2014, which is still weighing on the economy. The intermeeting period was flat, but the overall level reflects prior appreciation effects. Score around 55-60 reflecting the ongoing effects of prior dollar strength, though the intermeeting move was flat. Bright-line: the lingering effects of appreciation justify > 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Prices of non-energy goods imports declining. Energy prices falling. Industrial/goods sector under pressure from dollar appreciation. This suggests below-neutral goods inflation. Score around 25-30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out for services. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "remained stable." Market-based measures "moved slightly lower." Longer-run expectations assumed "consistent with the Committee's objective." This is anchored but with slight downside drift in market measures. Score around 43-47 (slightly below neutral due to market-based measures edging down).

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low inflation. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slightly below potential. Score below 50. Correct.

- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence says system healthy, absorbed shocks. Score below 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says flat intermeeting but cumulative appreciation still weighing. Score slightly above 50. Correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction mentioned. Score below 50. Correct.

### Bright-Line Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential -> score < 50. Score 46. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Accommodative" -> score < 50. Score 37. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "Widened" / "above historical medians" -> score > 50. Score 60. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: "Absorbed shocks" / "high capital" -> score < 50. Score 35. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Net little changed but cumulative appreciation effects -> score modestly > 50. Score 57. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction -> normal range. Score 32. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48)**: Close together, both near neutral. Coherent -- economy near potential, labor near NAIRU. OK.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (48)**: Close together, both near neutral. Coherent. OK.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (37) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (60)**: Divergence of 23 points. Explanation: Overall financial conditions remain accommodative for most borrowers (low rates, easy mortgage and consumer credit, strong CRE lending), but credit spreads have widened notably for speculative-grade borrowers specifically. The two-speed nature (accommodative for IG/households, tightening for HY/leveraged) explains the divergence.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (37) vs TREASURY_STRESS (32)**: Both below 50, consistent with accommodative/calm environment. OK.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods inflation low (28), expectations slightly below neutral (45). Weighted toward goods (which gets most discussion), parent score of 32 is consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer prices...were unchanged from 12 months earlier." "Core PCE inflation...was 1 1/4 percent over the same 12-month period, restrained in part by declines in the prices of non-energy goods imports." Staff projects inflation "would still be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent at the end of 2018."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below the 2% target at 0% headline and 1.25% core, with staff projecting only gradual return toward target.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because headline PCE is flat year-over-year and core is 1.25%, well below the 2% target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real GDP was increasing at a moderate pace." Staff projects GDP will "expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output from 2016 through 2018" (implying currently below potential). "The risks to the forecast for real GDP...were seen as tilted to the downside."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing at a moderate pace, still slightly below potential but closing the gap. Downside risks noted.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because the staff implies the economy is currently below potential (it needs to grow faster than potential to close the gap), with downside risks.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate remained at 5.1 percent." "The improvement in labor market conditions had slowed somewhat in recent months." "The rate of private-sector job openings declined in August but was still at a high level." Average hourly earnings up 2.25%.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment near NAIRU at 5.1% with slowing improvement, suggesting the labor market is approximately at but not yet through full employment.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is approximately at neutral -- unemployment near the natural rate but with some remaining slack indicators (slowing payrolls, declining participation).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to rise at a solid rate in the third quarter." But: "Industrial production decreased in September." "Real private inventory investment appeared to have slowed markedly." Forward indicators for business equipment "pointed to more modest increases."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending offset by declining industrial production, weak manufacturing, and slowing inventory investment, netting to roughly trend growth.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is approximately at neutral -- strong consumer side but weak industrial/manufacturing side balance out.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- **Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financing conditions for most businesses and households remained accommodative." "Conditions in consumer credit markets remained accommodative on balance." "CRE financing appeared to remain broadly available." But: "tightened somewhat for businesses with lower credit quality."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Broadly accommodative financial conditions across most sectors, with some tightening for lower-quality borrowers.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (looser) because staff repeatedly characterizes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative" -> < 50.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields declined further, reflecting FOMC communications, concerns about global economic growth, and generally weaker-than-expected U.S. economic data releases." No mention of market dysfunction or liquidity issues.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally with orderly yield declines driven by fundamental factors.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no mention of any dysfunction; yield movements reflect normal price adjustment.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds widened notably across sectors. Across the credit spectrum, spreads were generally near their highest levels in several years and ended the period above their historical medians." "Spreads on leveraged loans increased...over the intermeeting period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads above historical medians and at multi-year highs, with notable widening in speculative-grade and leveraged loan segments.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads are described as above medians and widening. Bright-line satisfied: "widened" / "elevated" -> > 50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 50 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes fell in the first few weeks after the September FOMC meeting but subsequently more than retraced those declines." The financial stability section notes "an increase in risk aversion eased valuation pressures."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities fluctuated but recovered; no strong valuation concern expressed by staff. The easing of valuation pressures is noted but not as a major concern.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is approximately at neutral -- price movements were volatile but net positive, with no strong stress or overvaluation signal.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The U.S. financial system appeared to absorb the shocks without systemic strains, supported by relatively high capital positions of large banking organizations and insurance firms and by restrained use of short-term wholesale funding." However: "leverage of speculative-grade and unrated nonfinancial corporations stayed near record levels. Rising CRE prices, accompanied by loosening underwriting standards in CMBS markets, continued to suggest valuation pressures."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system healthy and well-capitalized, absorbing recent volatility without strain, though some pockets of concern around CRE underwriting and corporate leverage.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the system is described as absorbing shocks without strains, with high capital positions. Bright-line satisfied: healthy/well-capitalized -> < 50.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The broad nominal index of the dollar's foreign exchange value ended the period little changed on balance." But the economic review emphasizes ongoing effects: "the appreciation in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" contributing to declining manufacturing and export softness.
- **Center-of-gravity**: The intermeeting dollar move was flat, but the cumulative prior appreciation continues to weigh on the economy, indicating the dollar is at an elevated level.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is modestly above neutral. While the intermeeting move was neutral, the staff prominently discusses the effects of prior dollar appreciation as a drag, indicating the dollar remains strong.
