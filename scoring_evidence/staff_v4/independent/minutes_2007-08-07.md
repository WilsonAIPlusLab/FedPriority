# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-08-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary). Although the file header says "unsegmented pre-2009," the content does have identifiable staff sections. The staff review of the economy runs from "The information reviewed at the August meeting..." through "the staff left its projection for core PCE inflation unchanged for 2008." Participant discussion begins at "In their discussion of the economic situation and outlook, meeting participants indicated..." I will score ONLY staff-attributable content (paragraphs using phrases like "the information reviewed," "the staff lowered," "the staff revised," etc.). I will also include the financial market review paragraphs that describe factual market conditions (these are staff briefing material), but exclude participant and Committee views.

### Era Context
August 2007 — the very early stages of the financial crisis. Subprime mortgage markets are deteriorating, credit spreads widening, but the broader economy still appears moderate. The fed funds rate is at 5.25%. Housing is in correction but broader indicators still show moderate growth. This is the pre-crisis period where stress is emerging but not yet systemic.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Staff sees inflation as modestly BELOW the midpoint but with upside risk language. "Overall inflation receded in June because of a decline in energy prices, while the core PCE price index rose a bit less than its average pace over the past year." Core PCE rose 0.1% in June. However, staff notes some of the favorable readings were "transitory" and "held down, in part, by declines in prices of some categories of goods... that tend to be volatile." Staff revised down slightly its forecast for core PCE in H2 2007 but left 2008 unchanged due to "slower growth in structural productivity and prospects of somewhat greater pressure from import prices." Employment cost index "rose somewhat faster." Per the gotcha rule, the current reading is favorable but staff explicitly flags transitory factors. Net: modestly below target but not convincingly so. Direction: slightly below midpoint.
- Score reasoning: Recent readings favorable but staff doubts sustainability. ECI rising. Staff left 2008 unchanged. This is "somewhat" language territory — about 5-8 points below midpoint for the current readings, but offset by upside risk. Score ~48.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Staff says "economic activity picked up in the second quarter from the slow pace in the first quarter. On average, the economy expanded at a moderate pace during the first half of the year despite the ongoing drag from the housing sector." Staff lowered GDP growth forecasts and revised down structural productivity/potential GDP, but "the near-parallel revisions to the forecasts for potential and actual GDP left the staff's projections for resource utilization about unchanged." The June FOMC statement referenced "high level of resource utilization." Unemployment at 4.4-4.6% range — near NAIRU or slightly below. Staff describes "moderate" expansion, roughly near potential. The resource utilization being "about unchanged" and described as high suggests at or slightly above potential.
- Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language from staff. "Near-parallel revisions" left resource utilization unchanged. The June statement (reflecting staff input) says "high level of resource utilization." This suggests at or slightly above potential. Score ~52.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Staff reports: "Private nonfarm payroll employment continued to increase at a healthy pace." "The unemployment rate edged up to 4.6 percent; it had remained between 4.4 percent and 4.6 percent since September 2006." ECI "rose somewhat faster." UE at 4.6% — near the NAIRU estimate of that era (~4.5-5%). Payroll growth is solid. Staff describes "high level of resource utilization" (from the forecast section referencing June statement).
- With UE at 4.6%, this is near or slightly above full employment. Labor market is healthy with solid hiring. Score ~52-55.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Consumer spending: "The growth of real consumer spending slowed considerably in the second quarter after substantial increases earlier in the year." The deceleration reflected "sharply slower growth in outlays for goods" and motor vehicle declines. But "solid gains in wages and salaries" and household wealth "moved markedly higher."
- Business investment: "Business fixed investment picked up in the second quarter after little net change in the preceding two quarters." Nonresidential construction "rose rapidly." Equipment/software spending posted "solid increase."
- Net picture: Consumer spending slowed but from a rapid pace; business investment picked up. On balance, spending is moderate — near trend but mixed. Score ~48.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Staff explicitly discusses productivity: "the annual revision of national income and product accounts (NIPA)... revealed somewhat less rapid growth in output and productivity during the past three years than previously reported and led the staff to trim its estimates of the growth rates of structural productivity and potential GDP." Also: "slower growth in structural productivity." This is a downward revision — below previous estimates.
- Direction: Below trend. Score ~38.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Staff describes: "Financial market conditions were volatile during the intermeeting period, particularly over the last few weeks." "Conditions in markets for subprime mortgages and related instruments... deteriorated sharply." "Credit conditions for speculative-grade corporate borrowers tightened substantially." "Spreads on speculative-grade bonds increased to near their highest levels in the past four years." Multiple leveraged loan deals delayed/restructured. However: "credit conditions for investment-grade businesses and prime households were relatively little affected." "Mortgage loans and consumer credit appeared to remain readily available to households with strong balance sheets." Staff forecast section: "less accommodative financial conditions."
- Bright-line check: "tightened substantially" for spec-grade, but investment-grade "relatively little affected." Mixed but tilting tight. "Less accommodative" = tighter. Score must be > 50. Score ~60.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Staff reports: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities fell on balance, possibly reflecting an increased preference by investors for safe assets as well as revisions in policy expectations." This is a flight-to-quality dynamic — yields fell. There is no mention of Treasury market dysfunction, liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues. The Treasury market was functioning as a safe haven.
- Per the bright-line rule, yield movements alone are not dysfunction. Flight to quality is normal. Score ~42.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
- Staff reports: "Spreads on speculative-grade bonds increased to near their highest levels in the past four years." "Yields on investment-grade corporate issues rose relative to yields on Treasury securities." "Credit conditions for speculative-grade corporate borrowers tightened substantially." But investment-grade spreads widened only modestly and investment-grade bond issuance continued.
- Bright-line check: "widened" / "elevated" → score must be > 50. Spec-grade near 4-year highs. Score ~63.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- Staff reports: "Broad stock price indexes declined substantially, on net, over the intermeeting period despite generally solid second-quarter earnings reports. Share prices of financial firms fell especially sharply." Earlier: "household wealth moved markedly higher in the second quarter, mostly reflecting rising equity prices" (but that was Q2 data, before the intermeeting decline).
- The decline is described as "substantial" with financial firms hit hard. No explicit valuation commentary from staff. Per gotcha, falling prices without valuation commentary → default 50-55. "Declined substantially" → 55-58. Score ~57.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
- Staff discusses subprime mortgage market deterioration, but there is no direct discussion of bank capital, bank health, or banking system stress per se. The discussion is about credit markets and mortgage-backed securities, not bank balance sheets. "The pace of bank lending to businesses picked up from an already solid clip" suggests banks are still lending. However, "some evidence pointed to diminishing availability of jumbo mortgages" and concerns about financial firm exposures to subprime.
- This is emerging stress but banks still lending. Score ~48-52. The stress is in markets, not yet in banks themselves. Score ~50.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
- Staff reports: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar against other major currencies fell, on balance." This is dollar depreciation/weakening.
- Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation → score must be < 50. Score ~35.

### Inflation Sub-components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**
- "A decline in the price index for core goods nearly offset a rise in the index for core services." Goods prices declining. "Held down, in part, by declines in prices of some categories of goods, such as apparel." Score ~35.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**
- "A rise in the index for core services." But no quantification of how elevated. Score ~55.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**
- "House-price appreciation continued to slow, with some measures again showing declines in home values." This is about house prices, not shelter/rent inflation per se. No explicit discussion of shelter CPI or rent inflation from staff. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**
- "Household surveys conducted in early July indicated that the median expectation for inflation over the next year remained unchanged from June's elevated level." "Median expectations of longer-term inflation ticked up and were near the top of the narrow range that had prevailed over the past few years." "Measures of long-horizon inflation compensation based on inflation-indexed Treasury securities edged down."
- Short-term expectations "elevated"; long-term "ticked up" near top of range but within it; market-based measures edged down. Mixed but slightly elevated. Score ~55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53)**: Both slightly above neutral. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (48)**: Gap of 4 points. Reasonable — economy near potential but spending mixed due to housing drag offset by business investment pickup.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (63)**: Both above 50, co-moving. Coherent — credit conditions tightening with spreads widening.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC elevated while Treasury stress low. This is consistent — Treasuries served as safe haven (flight to quality) while credit markets tightened. Normal divergence in early-crisis conditions.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (48) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (~35) pulling down, services (~55) pushing up, expectations (~55) slightly elevated. Weighted average consistent with ~48 overall.

### Bright-Line Verification
- OUTPUT_GAP: No explicit "above/below potential" from staff. Resource utilization "about unchanged" at high levels. 52 is reasonable.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "tightened substantially" (spec-grade), "less accommodative" → >50. Score 60. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "widened," "near highest levels in four years" → >50. Score 63. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: No clear "well-capitalized" or "stressed" language. Score 50 (neutral). OK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "fell, on balance" → <50. Score 35. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction described. Score 42. PASS.
