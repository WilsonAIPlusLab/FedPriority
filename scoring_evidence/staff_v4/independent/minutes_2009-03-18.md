# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary) from the March 17-18, 2009 FOMC meeting. It is a **post-2005 segmented** document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic and Financial Situation," "Developments in Financial Markets and the Federal Reserve's Balance Sheet," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content and stop at "Meeting Participants' Views."

### Era Context
March 2009 is the trough of the Great Financial Crisis. The S&P 500 hit its intra-crisis low on March 9, 2009. Unemployment was surging, GDP was contracting sharply, the banking system was under severe stress, and the Fed had already cut rates to the zero lower bound. This context should align with very low output gap scores, very low labor tightness, high financial stress, and high banking stress.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. "Both headline and core consumer prices edged up in January and February" — but in the context of "low readings on overall and core consumer price inflation in recent months" and the staff outlook noting inflation "expected to be damped by low rates of resource utilization, falling import prices, and easing cost pressures." Near-term inflation expectations "well below their high levels in mid-2008." Core intermediate materials prices dropped for a fifth month. The language suggests inflation pressure is clearly subdued but not yet deflationary — prices are still edging up, not falling. Direction: below midpoint. Score range: 20-28.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy far below potential. "Economic activity had fallen sharply in recent months." "The contraction was reflected in widespread declines." "Capacity utilization in manufacturing declined to a very low level." Staff outlook: "the staff revised down its outlook for economic activity," GDP expected to "flatten out gradually over the second half of this year." Bright-line check: massive slack, far below potential → score MUST be < 50. Direction: deeply below midpoint. Score range: 12-18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as extremely slack. "Labor market conditions continued to deteriorate." "Private payroll employment dropped considerably." Unemployment rate at 8.1% and rising. "The deterioration in labor market conditions was rapid in recent months, with steep job losses across nearly all sectors." Unemployment projected to rise further. At 8.1% UE, the rubric anchors at 16-30 range (UE > 7%). Direction: far below midpoint. Score range: 14-20.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as deeply weak. Consumer spending "held steady, on balance" after "having fallen sharply" — stabilizing at a low level. Business spending: "continued to fall across a broad range of categories," "new orders fell significantly further." Housing: "continued to be subdued," starts at "post-World War II low." Nonresidential construction falling. Direction: far below midpoint. Score range: 15-22.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. → N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as very tight/restrictive. "Financial conditions overall were even less supportive of economic activity." Equity indexes down significantly, dollar stronger, long-term rates higher. Short-term funding markets "mixed." Libor spreads "trended higher." The market for nonconforming loans "remained severely impaired." CMBS "under heavy stress." Bright-line check: conditions described as unsupportive/strained → score MUST be > 50. Direction: well above midpoint. Score range: 78-85.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff discusses Treasury market conditions specifically. "Trading conditions in the secondary market for nominal Treasury coupon securities showed some limited signs of improvement." Bid-ask spreads "relatively stable near their pre-crisis levels." However, "daily trading volumes... inched lower" and "spreads between the yields of on- and off-the-run 10-year Treasury notes remained very high." Also "poor liquidity in the market for Treasury inflation-protected securities." This is mixed — some improvement but still impaired. Bright-line check: there IS actual dysfunction described (high on-off-the-run spreads, poor TIPS liquidity, low volumes). Direction: above midpoint. Score range: 60-68.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees credit stress as very elevated. "Spreads of yields on both BBB-rated and speculative-grade bonds relative to... Treasury securities were little changed on net" (at already elevated levels). CDS indexes "widened significantly." Speculative-grade issuance "remained sluggish." CMBS CDS spreads "widened to record levels." Leveraged loan market still very weak. However, investment-grade issuance was "very strong" and liquidity in speculative-grade bonds "remained significantly better than in the fall of 2008." Direction: well above midpoint but not at peak crisis. Score range: 75-82.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff describes significant equity market distress. "Broad equity price indexes dropped significantly." "Bank stock prices were particularly hard hit." Insurance company stocks "dropped sharply." CDS spreads on banks "rose above the peaks recorded last fall." This is not just price decline but genuine distress language. Direction: well above midpoint (more stress/concern). Score range: 75-82.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff sees banking system under severe stress. "Renewed concerns about the financial condition of some large banks." Bank CDS spreads "rose above the peaks recorded last fall on anxieties about the financial conditions of the largest banking firms." "Concerns about the health of the financial sector." "Uncertainty regarding the efficacy of government support." AIG reported $60B+ losses. European bank shares fell steeply. Bank credit continuing to decline. C&I loan spreads rising. Bright-line check: banks described as stressed/troubled → score MUST be > 50. Direction: far above midpoint. Score range: 80-88.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff notes dollar strengthened. "The major currencies index of the dollar rose, on net, over the intermeeting period." "The dollar stronger" in the staff outlook section. Bright-line check: dollar appreciation → score MUST be > 50. Direction: above midpoint. Score range: 58-65.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Low readings on overall and core consumer price inflation in recent months"; "Both core and overall PCE price inflation were expected to be damped by low rates of resource utilization, falling import prices, and easing cost pressures." "The producer price index for core intermediate materials dropped for a fifth month in February."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is subdued with downside risks from massive economic slack, falling commodity prices, and weak demand, though not yet in outright deflation as consumer prices still edged up.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff reports low and falling inflation with continued downward pressure from slack and commodity declines.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 15 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Economic activity had fallen sharply in recent months"; "capacity utilization in manufacturing declined to a very low level"; staff outlook: "the staff revised down its outlook for economic activity" with GDP expected only to "flatten out gradually over the second half of this year."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is in deep contraction with output far below potential, widespread production cuts, and a worsening inventory overhang — consistent with the worst of the GFC trough.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because the economy is contracting sharply with massive slack. Bright-line rule satisfied: far below potential → score well below 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions continued to deteriorate. Private payroll employment dropped considerably over the three months ending in February." "The civilian unemployment rate climbed ½ percentage point in February, to 8.1 percent." "The deterioration in labor market conditions was rapid in recent months, with steep job losses across nearly all sectors."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is in severe distress with surging unemployment at 8.1%, massive and widespread job losses, and rapidly deteriorating conditions — fully consistent with deep recession.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because the labor market is extremely slack with high and rising unemployment. UE at 8.1% anchors firmly in the 16-30 range.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer spending appeared to remain at a low level after changing little, on balance"; "Business spending on equipment and software continued to fall across a broad range of categories"; "new orders fell significantly further"; "Single-family housing starts had fallen to a post–World War II low."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending has stabilized at a deeply depressed level after sharp declines, while business investment continues to plunge and housing is at historic lows — a broadly collapsing demand picture.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is far below neutral because both consumer spending (depressed) and business investment (still falling sharply) are deeply weak.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No staff discussion of productivity trends.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 80 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financial conditions overall were even less supportive of economic activity, with broad equity indexes down significantly amid continued concerns about the health of the financial sector"; "the market for nonconforming loans remained severely impaired"; Libor spreads "trended higher, on net... on renewed concerns about the financial condition of some large banks."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are severely tight, with worsening bank funding stress, impaired mortgage markets, and equity declines further weighing on the outlook — though some pockets (CP market, repo) show marginal improvement from the fall 2008 peak.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is far above neutral because conditions are described as increasingly unsupportive with severe impairment in multiple markets. Bright-line rule satisfied: strained/restrictive → score > 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 63 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads between the yields of on- and off-the-run 10-year Treasury notes remained very high"; "poor liquidity in the market for Treasury inflation-protected securities continued to make these readings difficult to interpret"; though "trading conditions in the secondary market for nominal Treasury coupon securities showed some limited signs of improvement."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market functioning remains impaired with very high on/off-the-run spreads and poor TIPS liquidity, though nominal coupon trading shows limited improvement — significant stress but not at the acute levels of fall 2008.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because staff describes actual market dysfunction (high on/off-the-run spreads, poor liquidity), not merely yield movements.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 78 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The investment- and speculative-grade CDS indexes widened significantly, on net"; "The market for commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) also remained under heavy stress. Indexes of CDS spreads on AAA-rated CMBS widened to record levels"; "speculative-grade issuance, however, remained sluggish."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit spreads remain severely elevated with further widening in CDS indexes and record stress in CMBS, though conditions are somewhat better than the fall 2008 peak and investment-grade bond issuance has been strong.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is far above neutral because spreads are highly elevated and widening further. Bright-line rule satisfied: widened/elevated → score > 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 78 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes dropped significantly, on balance, over the intermeeting period amid continued concerns about the health of the financial sector"; "Bank stock prices were particularly hard hit, and the credit default swap (CDS) spreads of many banks rose above the peaks recorded last fall."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equity markets are in significant distress with broad declines driven by financial sector fears, bank stocks particularly devastated, and insurance company stocks dropping sharply — near the GFC trough.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is far above neutral (more stress/concern) because staff describes significant, distress-level equity market declines.

### BANKING_STRESS: 83 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "CDS spreads of many banks rose above the peaks recorded last fall on anxieties about the financial conditions of the largest banking firms"; "uncertainty regarding the efficacy of government support to the sector"; "AIG reported losses of more than $60 billion"; "Bank credit continued to decline"; "C&I loan rate spreads... rose modestly."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The banking system is under severe systemic stress with CDS spreads exceeding fall 2008 peaks, major government interventions underway, declining bank credit, and deep uncertainty about the viability of the largest institutions.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is far above neutral because staff describes severe, systemic-level banking stress. Bright-line rule satisfied: stressed/troubled → score > 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The major currencies index of the dollar rose, on net, over the intermeeting period; foremost among the contributors to the rise was a significant appreciation of the dollar against the yen"; staff outlook: "the dollar stronger."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar appreciated modestly over the period, consistent with safe-haven flows during the crisis, though the discussion is relatively brief.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar strengthened. Bright-line rule satisfied: appreciation → score > 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The producer price index for core intermediate materials dropped for a fifth month in February, reflecting, in part, weaker global demand and steep declines in the prices of a wide variety of energy-intensive goods."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Goods prices are under significant downward pressure from collapsing commodity prices and weak global demand, though consumer goods prices edged up slightly.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral; goods price pressure is clearly subdued.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific discussion of services inflation by staff.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: House price declines are discussed but in the context of housing market activity, not shelter/rent inflation as a CPI component. Insufficient evidence to score shelter inflation specifically.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Near-term inflation expectations reported in surveys well below their high levels in mid-2008. In contrast, measures of longer-term expectations remained close to their averages over the past couple of years." "Longer-term inflation compensation lower over the period."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-term expectations have fallen significantly and inflation compensation has declined, but longer-term expectations remain anchored — suggesting some downside risk to anchoring but no unmooring.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral; expectations are below target at the near-term horizon though long-term anchoring provides partial offset.
