# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-06-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2018. The U.S. economy is in the late-expansion phase. Tax cuts were recently enacted. Unemployment has fallen to 3.8%, well below most estimates of NAIRU. PCE inflation has reached 2.0%. The Fed has been gradually raising rates. This is a broadly strong economy with inflation near target.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff reports PCE inflation at exactly 2.0% over 12 months ending April. Core PCE at 1.8%. CPI at 2.8%, core CPI at 2.2%. Staff projects total PCE to "remain near the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term." Core PCE "slightly higher" than total. Longer-run inflation expectations "little changed." Staff sees inflation as right at target, with core slightly below. Direction: at midpoint. Score should be near 50. The anchor says early 2018 "at target" = 50. Headline exactly at 2%, core slightly below. Score ~49-50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Critical bright-line dimension. Staff says "the economy would expand at an above-trend pace," "output was projected to rise further above the staff's estimate of its potential," and "the unemployment rate was projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." The word "further" implies already above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" = MUST be > 50. The staff also notes supply constraints may restrain growth. Score should be solidly above 50 — "above potential" and projected to rise "further above." This is clearly above but not dramatically overheating. Score ~56-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 3.8% in May — very low. Payrolls expanding at "strong pace." Job openings "elevated." Quits edging up. Initial claims "continued to be low." Part-time for economic reasons "close to that from just before the previous recession." Compensation stepped up — 2.7% vs 1.9% a year earlier. Staff projects unemployment to "decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This is very tight. With UE at 3.8% and below NAIRU, the rubric says UE < 3.8% = 66-80 range, UE 3.8-4.5% = 53-65 range. At exactly 3.8% with accelerating wages and "further below natural rate" language, this should be at the upper end of the 53-65 range or lower end of 66-80. Score ~63-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to be increasing briskly in the second quarter" after "only a modest pace in the first quarter." Real PCE "increased at a robust pace in April." Key factors "continued to be supportive of solid real PCE growth." Consumer sentiment "remained elevated." However, residential investment "appeared to be declining further" — a negative. Business equipment rising at "moderate pace," business structures "expanding at a solid pace," equipment spending indicators point to "robust gains." Net: consumer spending is strong/brisk, business investment is moderate-to-solid, but residential investment is weak. The net position is above trend given the strong consumer language. Score ~57-60.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff says "financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households remained supportive of economic activity on balance." CRE financing "remained accommodative." Residential mortgage conditions "remained accommodative for most borrowers." Consumer credit conditions "remained largely supportive of growth in household spending." However, short-term funding spreads (LIBOR-OIS) "remained significantly wider than at the start of the year." Subprime consumer conditions "continued to tighten." Overall the language is clearly "supportive" and "accommodative" — bright-line rule says this MUST be < 50. But there's a minor tightening note. Score ~38-42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "ended the period largely unchanged despite notable movements during the intermeeting period." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. Normal market functioning with some volatility. Score ~40-43. No actual stress described.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of yields on nonfinancial corporate bonds over those of comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened moderately for both investment- and speculative-grade firms. However, these spreads remained low by historical standards." CMBS spreads "little changed... remaining near their post-crisis lows." EME sovereign spreads widened. Bright-line: spreads described as "low by historical standards" even after moderate widening. Still below normal. Score ~35-38.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Broad equity indexes "increased about 5 percent" since May meeting. VIX "down somewhat" remaining near low levels. No valuation commentary from staff — just price movement. Per rubric, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55. Prices rising, VIX low — no stress. Score ~42-45.

**BANKING_STRESS**: No direct discussion of banking system health/stress. Growth of C&I loans moderated. CRE loan growth ticked down. No mention of bank capital, NPLs, or stress. However, European bank shares "came under pressure" due to Italy — this is foreign. For U.S. banking, there's minimal discussion. Financing conditions described as accommodative, which suggests a healthy banking backdrop. Score ~30-35 given accommodative lending conditions imply low stress. Alternatively N/A since banking system per se isn't discussed. The lending standards discussion is more about credit conditions than banking stress. I'll score it as N/A — there is no explicit discussion of U.S. banking system health or stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated notably as a range of AFE and EME currencies and sovereign bonds came under pressure." "Supported an increase in the broad trade-weighted index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar." "The dollar appreciated notably against several EME currencies (primarily those of Argentina, Turkey, Mexico, and Brazil)." The staff also revised GDP growth down "in response to a higher assumed path for the exchange value of the dollar." Bright-line: dollar appreciation = score > 50. Language is "notably" appreciated — this is moderately strong. Score ~62-65.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Headline PCE exactly at 2% target, core slightly below at 1.8%, with staff projecting inflation to stay near 2%. This is essentially at-target inflation.
