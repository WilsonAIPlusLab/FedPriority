# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-12-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Late 2013. The economy is in a moderate recovery from the GFC, with unemployment at 7.0% (still elevated), inflation running well below the 2% target (core PCE only slightly above 1%), and the Fed considering tapering its asset purchases. Financial conditions are broadly accommodative. This is a mid-recovery period.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation "less than 1 percent" over 12 months ending in October. Core PCE "only a little above 1 percent." Wage increases "modest." Staff forecasts inflation to be "subdued through 2016." Longer-run expectations "remained stable." This is clearly below the 2% target. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. Given "less than 1 percent" total PCE and core "only a little above 1 percent," this is at the lower end of the below-target range, around 30-35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW potential. "Resource slack" persists, economy "expanding at a moderate pace," GDP projected to "rise significantly faster than the growth rate of potential output" implying gap is still negative and needs closing. "Slack in labor and product markets persisting over most of the projection period." Bright-line: slack remains = MUST be < 50. The economy is recovering — GDP growth revised up, acceleration expected — but still below potential. Score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment. Unemployment at 7.0% — "remained elevated." Participation rate declined. Part-time for economic reasons and long-duration unemployment "still high." Hiring rate "still somewhat low." But conditions are "gradually improving" — payrolls rose at a faster pace, job openings edged up, claims trended down. UE 7.0% per the rubric puts this in the 16-30 range (UE > 7%), but 7.0% is right at the boundary. With improving indicators, score around 30-32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but modestly positive. Consumer spending: PCE "increased modestly in the third quarter but rose at a faster pace" in recent months, retail sales "increased at a strong pace in November," auto sales "moved up significantly," consumer sentiment "improved significantly." Business investment: equipment spending "subdued in the third quarter," shipments "edged down" in October, but forward-looking indicators suggest "moderate gains." Housing: "continued to slow somewhat." Net: consumer spending strengthening, business investment soft, housing slowing. The consumer strength is the dominant signal. Score ~45-48.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "gains in productivity were small." This is below trend. Score ~35.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as accommodative/supportive. "Continued improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" in the outlook. Corporate risk spreads "narrowed somewhat." Business finance "robust." Gross equity issuance at "levels not seen in a decade." Leveraged loan and CLO issuance "strong." CRE financing "consistent with increased confidence." CMBS "remained strong." Auto loans "continued to expand." However, mortgage rates rose ~100bp from May lows, and mortgage lenders "continued to be reluctant to lend to borrowers with less-than-pristine credit scores." Bright-line: "accommodative" / "supportive" = MUST be < 50. Score ~30-33.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose (5-year +21bp, 10-year +34bp) but this was driven by better economic data and tapering expectations — normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market stress. Bright-line: yield movements from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50). Score ~40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate risk spreads narrowed somewhat." CDS spreads for largest bank holding companies "moved lower." Business finance "robust." This is favorable credit conditions, below normal stress. Bright-line: "narrow" = MUST be < 50. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 generally favorable. Score ~33.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices "about unchanged" but "some broad equity price indexes temporarily touched all-time nominal highs." Staff financial stability report: "Valuations in most asset markets seemed broadly in line with historical norms." But also noted "some apparent increases in investor appetite for higher-yielding assets." The "broadly in line" language suggests roughly fair valued. Score ~45.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff stability report: "Relatively strong capital profiles of large domestic banking firms," low and moderate growth of credit, "some reduction in reliance on short-term wholesale funding." Bank stock prices outperformed. CDS spreads moved lower. But staff notes "complexity and interconnectedness of large financial institutions" as a risk source. Bright-line: "strong capital" = MUST be < 50. Score ~30.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated" and "ended the intermeeting period higher on balance." Staff outlook notes "a higher path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a drag on growth. Bright-line: appreciation = MUST be > 50. Modest appreciation. Score ~57.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "recent declines in the prices of many nonfuel imported goods." Consumer energy prices declined. CPI "flat" in November. Goods prices are declining. Score ~25.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "subdued increases in medical services prices." Core CPI rose "slightly faster than in the preceding few months." Limited detail on services specifically. Score ~38.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices rose significantly in October" but "the pace of house price gains continued to decelerate." Housing sector "continued to slow somewhat." This is asset prices, not shelter inflation per se. Not enough direct shelter/rent inflation discussion. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." Michigan survey near-term and longer-term expectations "little changed." TIPS-based: 5-year compensation dipped 5bp, forward measure +7bp. Well-anchored. Score ~47.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (31)**: Both below 50, separated by 7 points. Coherent — both reflect ongoing slack, with labor slightly weaker given 7% UE.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (47)**: Separated by 9 points. Consumption is strengthening (driving the gap closure) while the overall output gap remains negative. This is coherent for a recovery period where spending leads potential output closure.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (33)**: Both below 50, closely aligned. Coherent — accommodative conditions with narrow spreads.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC is accommodative while Treasury market is functioning normally. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~25, Services ~38, Expectations ~47. Weighted average leans below target, consistent with parent score of 32.

### Bright-line verification:

- OUTPUT_GAP: "slack remains" -> score 38 < 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" -> score 32 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "narrowed" -> score 33 < 50. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: "strong capital" -> score 30 < 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "appreciated" -> score 57 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yield rise from policy expectations -> score 40 in 36-50 range. PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Total U.S. consumer price inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, was less than 1 percent over the 12 months ending in October"
- "core PCE price inflation... was only a little above 1 percent"
- Staff forecast: "inflation was projected to be subdued through 2016"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: With headline PCE below 1% and core barely above 1%, staff clearly sees inflation running well below the 2% target with no near-term normalization expected.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running roughly half the 2% target with subdued outlook.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "economic activity was expanding at a moderate pace"
- "the expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack over the projection period"
- "slack in labor and product markets persisting over most of the projection period"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is recovering and GDP growth is being revised up, but significant slack remains in both labor and product markets with slow closure expected.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies persistent resource slack. Bright-line satisfied: slack remains -> < 50.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 31 +/-4 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate declined, on net, from 7.2 percent in September to 7.0 percent in November" but "remained elevated"
- "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons declined slightly... but both measures were still high"
- "the rate of gross private-sector hiring was still somewhat low"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: At 7.0% unemployment with elevated underemployment and low hiring rates, the labor market has substantial remaining slack despite gradual improvement.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE is 7.0% (well above NAIRU) with persistent slack indicators.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) increased modestly in the third quarter but rose at a faster pace in September and October"; retail sales "increased at a strong pace in November"
- "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products was subdued in the third quarter"
- Housing: "the pace of activity in the housing sector appeared to continue to slow somewhat"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is accelerating and showing strength, but business equipment investment is subdued and housing is slowing, netting to roughly at-trend or slightly below.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is near but slightly below neutral, reflecting the mix of strengthening consumption against weak investment and slowing housing.

---

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +/-10 [Low confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "gains in productivity were small"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff briefly notes small productivity gains, suggesting below-trend performance.

**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because "small" gains indicate below-trend growth.

---

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "continued improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" (staff outlook)
- "Business finance flows were robust over the intermeeting period. Gross equity issuance by the nonfinancial corporate sector in October and November reached levels not seen in a decade."
- "Corporate risk spreads narrowed somewhat"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative with robust issuance activity, narrowing spreads, and improving credit availability, though mortgage credit remains somewhat restricted.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose/accommodative). Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative" -> < 50.

---

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "the 5- and 10-year yields higher by 21 and 34 basis points, respectively" — driven by better economic data and tapering expectations
- No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction stress

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury yields rose from normal policy expectation adjustment, not market dysfunction. Market functioning appears orderly.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near but slightly below neutral. Yield movements from policy expectations are normal, not stress.

---

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Corporate risk spreads narrowed somewhat"
- "Spreads on credit default swaps for the largest bank holding companies also moved lower, on net"
- Robust issuance across corporate bonds, leveraged loans, CLOs, CMBS, auto ABS

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spreads are narrowing with robust credit market activity across multiple segments, indicating favorable below-normal credit conditions.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (low stress). Bright-line satisfied: "narrowed" -> < 50.

---

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Stock prices were about unchanged, on net, over the intermeeting period, even though some broad equity price indexes temporarily touched all-time nominal highs"
- "Valuations in most asset markets seemed broadly in line with historical norms"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff sees equity valuations as roughly fair, with prices near all-time highs but assessed as in line with historical norms.

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral, slightly below (less concern), reflecting the "broadly in line" assessment.

---

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Relatively strong capital profiles of large domestic banking firms, low levels and moderate growth of aggregate credit in the nonfinancial sectors, and some reduction in reliance on short-term wholesale funding"
- "Stock prices for large and regional domestic banking firms outperformed the broad equity market"
- CDS spreads for largest banks "moved lower"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system is well-capitalized with strong performance, low stress, though staff notes complexity and interconnectedness as potential risks.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral (low stress). Bright-line satisfied: "strong capital" -> < 50.

---

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated following the October FOMC meeting... and ended the intermeeting period higher on balance"
- "a higher path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" partially offset positive factors in the medium-term GDP forecast

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar appreciated modestly, driven by better US data and expectations of easier policy abroad, with staff treating it as a headwind to growth.

**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation -> > 50.
