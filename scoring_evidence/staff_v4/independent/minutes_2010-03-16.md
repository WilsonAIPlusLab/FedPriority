# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. The staff content is clearly separated into three sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score ONLY staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
March 2010 — roughly nine months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in the early stages of recovery. Unemployment is very high at 9.7%. The Fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound. The Fed is winding down emergency facilities and large-scale asset purchases. Greek fiscal concerns are emerging in Europe. This is a classic "early recovery with substantial slack" environment.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key evidence: "core consumer price inflation was quite low"; "consumer prices excluding food and energy were soft, as a wide variety of goods and services exhibited persistently low inflation or outright price declines"; core PCE inflation slowed on a 12-month basis; hourly compensation deceleration was "even sharper than was evident at the January meeting"; staff revised DOWN its core inflation projection; core inflation "projected to be quite subdued at rates below last year's pace." Energy pushed up headline, but the staff's emphasis is overwhelmingly on soft core prices. Direction: clearly below 50. Magnitude: "quite low," "persistently low," "outright price declines," "quite subdued" — strong language suggesting 20-30 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low/subdued/declining prices. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Key evidence: "economic activity expanded at a moderate pace" — recovery, but from deep hole; capacity utilization "remained well below its longer-run average"; unemployment at 9.7%; housing "flattened out"; nonresidential construction "weakened further"; staff anticipates "a moderate pace of economic recovery over the next two years." The economy is growing but still far below potential.
- Bright-line check: Capacity utilization "well below its longer-run average" = below potential. Score MUST be < 50.
- Anchor: 2010 recovery period anchor is 35-42. The economy is expanding but still has enormous slack.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees the labor market as VERY loose. Key evidence: unemployment at 9.7%; "initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits remained high"; "labor market might be stabilizing" (note: stabilizing, not tight); "declines in private payrolls slowed markedly" (still declining!); compensation deceleration was "even sharper."
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. 9.7% unemployment = extremely loose. Score well below 50.
- Anchor: 2009-10 anchor is 15-22. UE at 9.7% with initial claims still high places this squarely in that range.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending/investment as mixed, slightly below trend. Consumer spending "continued to move up" but "measures of consumer sentiment remained relatively downbeat"; "household income appeared less supportive of spending"; housing "flattened out"; nonresidential construction "generally remained poor." On the positive side: equipment and software investment "increased at a solid pace" and "apparently rose further"; IP continued to expand. Net assessment: recovery underway but still below trend.
- Anchor: 2011-13 "moderate" is 35-42. This is early 2010, with mixed signals — some sectors up, others weak.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees financial conditions as somewhat accommodative but with pockets of tightness. Key evidence: Short-term funding spreads "stayed low"; Fed tapering its LSAPs with "no apparent adverse effects"; stock prices rose; investment-grade spreads "near the levels that prevailed late in 2007"; speculative-grade spreads "edged down." BUT: "banks' tight credit policies"; total bank credit "contracted substantially"; C&I loan spreads "climbed further"; "difficult financing conditions for new projects" (CRE). Mixed picture — market conditions improving but bank lending still tight.
- Bright-line check: The overall picture is mixed. Market conditions are accommodative/improving, but bank credit is tight. Net: slightly above neutral, maybe 50-55, because bank lending tightness is real.
- Actually reconsidering: "tight credit policies," "contracted substantially" in bank credit, "difficult financing conditions" — these all point to residual tightness even as market conditions improve. Score modestly above 50.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields "edged up, on net." TIPS yields rose partly due to supply expectations. No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, settlement failures. This is normal market functioning.
- Bright-line: Rising yields from supply/policy expectations = normal. Score 36-50.
- N/A considerations: Treasury market IS discussed but only in terms of normal yield movements. Score in the normal-functioning range.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff sees credit spreads as below normal (improving). Key evidence: Investment-grade spreads "near the levels that prevailed late in 2007"; speculative-grade spreads "edged down"; Libor-OIS spreads "stayed low"; CP spreads "little changed at low levels." BUT: C&I loan spreads "climbed further"; CRE lending conditions are "difficult"; net debt financing "about zero" reflecting both weak demand and tight bank policies.
- The market-based spreads are clearly below normal/favorable. Bank lending spreads are still elevated. Weighting by discussion emphasis: market spreads get more attention and are described as near pre-crisis levels. Score below 50 but not extremely so given the bank lending tightness.
- Bright-line: "low levels," "near 2007 levels" = compressed/narrow. Must be < 50.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff notes equity prices "rose, on net"; bank equity "outperformed"; option-implied volatility "dropped back to post-crisis lows." The equity risk premium "remained well above its average over the past decade" — this suggests equities are NOT overvalued (high risk premium = relatively cheap). Staff does not express explicit valuation concern in either direction. The risk premium comment leans toward modest undervaluation/caution.
- Score slightly above neutral (modestly depressed/cautious) given the elevated risk premium language. Around 52-55.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff sees significant banking stress still present. Key evidence: "banks' tight credit policies"; total bank credit "contracted substantially"; C&I loans "continued falling"; CRE loans "posted significant declines"; CRE delinquency rates rising at both securitized pools and banks; construction loan delinquencies ticked higher; several banks issued equity "to facilitate the repayment of capital received under TARP." Consumer loans declined.
- Bright-line: Tight lending, rising delinquencies, TARP repayment context, contracting credit. This is clearly stress > 50.
- The system is not in acute crisis (no bank failures mentioned), but there is significant ongoing stress. Score 60-68.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff reports: "the dollar appreciated notably against sterling and the euro" due to Greek concerns and weak European data. BUT "the dollar declined against most emerging market currencies," and "the broad trade-weighted value of the dollar down a bit since the January meeting."
- Net: the broad trade-weighted dollar was DOWN slightly. Bright-line: depreciation/weakening = score < 50. The decline was small ("down a bit"), so modestly below 50.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: "a wide variety of goods... exhibited persistently low inflation or outright price declines." Clearly below 50 — goods deflation/very low. Score ~20-25.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: "services exhibited persistently low inflation"; "real outlays for other services remained on a gradual uptrend" (not about prices specifically). Limited separate service price info beyond the broad "soft" description. Score ~30-35.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing sector "flattened out"; "falling property prices" mentioned in CRE context; housing prices near "trough." But specific shelter/rent inflation not discussed in detail. Weak evidence. Score ~30 with low confidence, or N/A. Given that the discussion is about housing activity, not shelter inflation specifically, I'll score N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey expectations for near-term inflation were unchanged"; "median longer-term inflation expectations edged down to near the lower end of the narrow range that prevailed over the previous few years." Expectations edging down, but still within the narrow range. Inflation compensation declined. Below 50 but not alarming. Score ~38-42.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (20)**: Both well below 50. Labor is even weaker than output gap, which makes sense — labor is a lagging indicator and 9.7% UE is extreme. Divergence of ~15 points is explainable by labor market lagging the output recovery.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38)**: Very close. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Some divergence. Market spreads are favorable (low credit spread score) but bank lending is tight (higher FC score). This reflects the "two-track" recovery — market conditions improving while bank credit remains tight. Explainable.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC modestly tight, Treasury functioning normally. Consistent with non-crisis period where residual bank tightness coexists with normal Treasury markets.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (25) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~22, Services ~32, Expectations ~40. Weighted average consistent with parent score of 25 (goods deflation dominates).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "core consumer price inflation was quite low"; "consumer prices excluding food and energy were soft, as a wide variety of goods and services exhibited persistently low inflation or outright price declines"; staff revised down core inflation projection, which was "projected to be quite subdued at rates below last year's pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes a broad-based environment of very low core inflation with some outright price declines, while energy provided temporary headline uplift.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is well below neutral because core prices are "quite low" with "outright price declines" across many categories. Not yet at deflation-spiral territory (which would be 1-10), but clearly in the meaningful disinflation zone.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "economic activity expanded at a moderate pace in early 2010"; capacity utilization "remained well below its longer-run average"; unemployment at 9.7%; staff anticipated "a moderate pace of economic recovery over the next two years."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is recovering at a moderate pace but remains well below potential, with substantial slack evident in capacity utilization and labor markets.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because capacity utilization is "well below its longer-run average" — bright-line rule satisfied (below potential = score < 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was unchanged at 9.7 percent"; "the level of initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits remained high"; "last year's deceleration in hourly compensation was even sharper than was evident at the January meeting."
**Center-of-gravity**: Extremely loose labor market with 9.7% unemployment, high initial claims, and sharply decelerating compensation — only modest stabilization signs.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because UE at 9.7% with still-high claims indicates deep slack. Consistent with 2009-10 anchor of 15-22.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer spending continued to move up" but "measures of consumer sentiment remained relatively downbeat" and "household income appeared less supportive"; "real spending on equipment and software increased at a solid pace"; housing "flattened out"; nonresidential construction "generally remained poor."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture with consumer spending and equipment investment rising modestly, offset by flat housing and weak nonresidential construction. Below trend overall.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral — recovery underway but uneven, with several sectors still weak.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No explicit discussion of productivity trends in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Short-term funding spreads "stayed low"; investment-grade spreads "near the levels that prevailed late in 2007." BUT: "banks' tight credit policies"; total bank credit "contracted substantially in January and February"; "difficult financing conditions for new projects."
**Center-of-gravity**: Market-based financial conditions have improved significantly, but bank lending remains tight with contracting credit volumes and elevated loan spreads — net assessment is modestly tight.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is slightly above neutral (tighter) because despite improving market conditions, bank credit policies remain "tight" and bank credit is contracting substantially. Bright-line: "tight credit policies" = score > 50. Satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "yields on 2-year and 10-year nominal Treasury securities edged up, on net"; TIPS yields rose "reportedly buoyed by investor anticipation of heavier TIPS issuance and by reduced demand for TIPS by retail investors." No mention of dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with orderly yield adjustments driven by supply expectations and economic data.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral — no dysfunction mentioned, only normal yield movements. Bright-line satisfied: no actual market dysfunction described.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Investment-grade risk spreads "near the levels that prevailed late in 2007"; speculative-grade spreads "edged down"; Libor-OIS spreads "stayed low"; CP spreads "little changed at low levels." C&I loan spreads "climbed further."
**Center-of-gravity**: Market-based credit spreads have normalized to near pre-crisis levels, though bank lending spreads remain elevated. Net level is somewhat below normal (favorable).
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because market spreads are described as at "low levels" and near 2007 levels. Bright-line: "low levels" / narrow = score < 50. Satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes rose, on net"; "option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index dropped back to post-crisis lows"; but "the equity risk premium remained well above its average over the past decade."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities are rising with declining volatility, but the elevated equity risk premium suggests stocks are not overvalued. Roughly neutral to slightly cautious assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — prices are rising (less stress) but risk premium is elevated (suggesting residual caution/modest undervaluation). Net approximately balanced.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "banks' tight credit policies"; "total bank credit contracted substantially in January and February"; "delinquency rate on commercial mortgages in securitized pools increased in January"; "delinquency rate on commercial mortgages at commercial banks rose in the fourth quarter"; "percentage of delinquent construction loans at banks also ticked higher."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banks are experiencing significant ongoing stress with contracting credit, rising delinquencies across CRE and construction, and tight lending policies, though the system is not in acute crisis.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because of tight lending, contracting credit, and rising delinquencies. Bright-line: "tight credit policies" + rising delinquencies = stress > 50. Satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "the dollar appreciated notably against sterling and the euro over the intermeeting period"; "the dollar declined against most emerging market currencies"; "the broad trade-weighted value of the dollar down a bit since the January meeting."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed dollar movements with strength vs. Europe but weakness vs. EM; the broad trade-weighted index was down slightly on net.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly below neutral because the broad trade-weighted dollar declined modestly. Bright-line: net depreciation = score < 50. Satisfied.
