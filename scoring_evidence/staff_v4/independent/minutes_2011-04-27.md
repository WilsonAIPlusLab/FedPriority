# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-04-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: April 2011 — roughly two years into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment still very high at 8.8%. The Fed is completing its QE2 asset purchase program. Commodity prices (food, energy) are rising, pushing headline inflation up, but core inflation remains subdued. Housing remains deeply depressed. The Japan earthquake/tsunami is creating near-term supply-chain uncertainty. European sovereign debt concerns (Greece, Portugal) are escalating.

### Directional signals by dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as modestly above neutral in headline terms due to food/energy pass-through, but core inflation is "relatively subdued," the 12-month core CPI is essentially unchanged from a year earlier, longer-term inflation expectations "remained stable" and within their historical range, and wage growth is "restrained by the presence of a large margin of slack." Staff expects inflation to "recede over the medium term." The net picture is: headline elevated by transitory commodity factors, but underlying inflation still below target. Per the gotcha rule, I should score the current elevated headline, not the forecast. But the staff's own characterization of underlying inflation is subdued. Balancing headline up / core subdued: modestly below neutral, around 43-47.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says economy expanded at a "moderate pace," GDP growth was revised down, unemployment was expected to "remain elevated at the end of 2012," and there is "persistent slack in labor and product markets." Manufacturing capacity utilization "continued to be a good bit lower than its longer-run average." Bright-line: "slack remains" language requires score < 50. Staff sees economy clearly below potential. Score ~35-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.8%, "long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still very high," "large margin of slack in the labor market," wages restrained. With UE at 8.8%, this is solidly in the 16-30 range per anchor guidance (UE > 7% = 16-30). Labor market improving gradually but from very weak levels. Score ~28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "slowed to a moderate rate" from a stronger pace, consumer sentiment "declined noticeably." Housing "remained very weak." But business E&S investment "increased more robustly," with strong orders and backlogs. Nonresidential construction "remained extremely weak." Government spending falling. Net: below trend overall, with pockets of strength in business equipment. Score ~38.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Corporate bond spreads "narrowed slightly," syndicated loan prices moved up, net debt financing "remained robust," C&I loans increased, bank lending standards "eased somewhat further," equity prices rose, option-implied volatility fell. But municipal bond market "remained somewhat strained," commercial real estate tight credit, core loans still contracting. Overall: conditions are accommodative/supportive with some lingering pockets of strain. Bright-line: "accommodative" language requires < 50. Score ~35-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "changed little, on net." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. Normal functioning. Score ~38-42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Investment- and speculative-grade spreads "narrowed slightly." Corporate debt financing "remained robust." Consumer credit improving, delinquencies approaching pre-crisis levels. Municipal bonds "somewhat strained." Bright-line: narrowing/compressed spreads = < 50. Score ~33-36.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices rose on better-than-expected earnings, option-implied volatility at "the low end of its recent range." No valuation concern expressed. Price moves described factually without alarm. Score ~45 (roughly fair, no concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank lending standards "eased somewhat further." Consumer credit improving, delinquencies declining toward pre-crisis levels. Commercial real estate delinquencies "leveled off." But core loans still contracting, CRE loans running off. Mortgage delinquencies "remained at elevated levels" for subprime/prime. Mixed but gradually improving — moderate remaining stress. Bright-line: easing standards suggests declining stress, but elevated delinquencies suggest residual stress. Score ~48 — near neutral with lingering stress pockets.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined more than 2 percent over the intermeeting period." Bright-line: depreciation = < 50. A 2% decline is notable but not extreme. Score ~30.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Food and energy prices "continued to be passed through to the retail level," "additional increases in retail gasoline prices." Goods inflation clearly elevated. Score ~62.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Home prices "slid further in February." Housing costs declining. Score ~30.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Near-term expectations "remained elevated" but longer-term expectations "moved down" and "stayed within the range that has prevailed over the past several years." Staff models attribute increase in inflation compensation to "liquidity and inflation-risk premiums rather than higher expected inflation." Well-anchored at target. Score ~50.

### Polarity checks:
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/supportive = low tightness. Score 36 < 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Evidence says narrowing. Score 34 < 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence is mixed — easing standards but elevated delinquencies. Score 48. Reasonable.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says dollar declined 2%. Score 30 < 50. Correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction described. Score 40. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: "Slack remains" = < 50. Score 36. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both below 50, labor even lower reflecting very high UE. Gap of 8 points is acceptable — output recovering faster than labor (typical lag). OK.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (36) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38): Very close, both below 50. Coherent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (34): Both below 50, very close. Coherent — both show accommodative/favorable conditions.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40): FC accommodative, Treasury normal. Coherent.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (45) vs sub-dimensions: Goods ~62, Housing ~30, Expectations ~50. Weighted average with goods elevated but housing declining and expectations anchored gives a mid-40s parent score. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Headline consumer price inflation was boosted by large increases in food and energy prices, but measures of underlying inflation were still subdued and longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Core consumer price inflation remained relatively subdued."
**Center-of-gravity**: Headline inflation pushed up by commodity pass-through, but core inflation subdued and expectations anchored — net slightly below neutral.
**Direction**: Inflation pressure is slightly below neutral because headline is elevated but core measures and expectations indicate limited underlying pressure.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 36 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Persistent slack in labor and product markets." "The manufacturing capacity utilization rate...continued to be a good bit lower than its longer-run average." Staff revised down GDP growth projection; unemployment expected to "remain elevated at the end of 2012."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly below potential with persistent slack, moderate pace of recovery insufficient to close the gap soon.
**Direction**: Output gap is below neutral (below potential) because staff explicitly cites persistent slack. Bright-line satisfied: slack remains => < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged down further to 8.8 percent in March." "Both long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still very high." "A large margin of slack in the labor market."
**Center-of-gravity**: Very high unemployment with extensive slack; gradual improvement insufficient to raise tightness. UE 8.8% anchors this firmly in the high-slack range.
**Direction**: Labor tightness is well below neutral because unemployment is 8.8% with large slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The rise in consumer spending appeared to have slowed to a moderate rate in the first quarter from the stronger pace posted in the fourth quarter." "Activity in the housing market remained very weak." "Real business investment in equipment and software appeared to have increased more robustly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending decelerating, housing deeply depressed, government cutting back; business equipment investment is a bright spot but insufficient to offset weakness elsewhere. Below trend overall.
**Direction**: Consumption/investment is below neutral because most components are weak, with only business E&S strong.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the document.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds relative to comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed slightly." "Bank lending standards and terms had eased somewhat further during the first quarter." "Net debt financing by nonfinancial corporations remained robust."
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit conditions are generally accommodative — spreads narrowing, lending standards easing, robust issuance — though pockets of strain remain in municipal bonds and CRE.
**Direction**: Financial conditions are below neutral (accommodative/loose) because staff describes easing standards, narrowing spreads, and robust financing. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities changed little, on net, amid swings in investors' assessments of global risks." No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally with little net change in yields and no dysfunction signals.
**Direction**: Treasury stress is at/slightly below neutral because markets functioned normally despite global uncertainty.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 34 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds relative to comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed slightly." "Net issuance of investment- and speculative-grade bonds by nonfinancial corporations continued to be strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads narrowing with robust issuance — below-normal credit stress, favorable conditions.
**Direction**: Credit spreads are below neutral (compressed/favorable) because spreads narrowed and issuance was strong. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes rose, on net, over the intermeeting period, as initial reports of better-than-expected first-quarter earnings lifted stock prices." "Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index was moderately lower, on net, ending the intermeeting period at the low end of its recent range."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising on earnings with low volatility; no valuation concern expressed. Roughly fair value / no alarm.
**Direction**: Equity valuation concern is at/slightly below neutral — no staff concern about over- or undervaluation.

### BANKING_STRESS: 48 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Bank lending standards and terms had eased somewhat further." But "core loans...continued to contract" and "the rates of serious delinquencies for subprime and prime mortgages...remained at elevated levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system improving — standards easing, consumer delinquencies declining — but residual stress from elevated mortgage delinquencies and contracting core loans.
**Direction**: Banking stress is near neutral — improving but not yet healthy, with lingering problem pockets.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined more than 2 percent over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear dollar depreciation over the period.
**Direction**: Dollar strength is below neutral because the dollar weakened. Bright-line satisfied: depreciation => < 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 62 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Increases in the prices of energy and food commodities continued to be passed through to the retail level." "Survey data through the middle of April pointed to additional increases in retail gasoline prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods inflation clearly elevated from commodity pass-through.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not specifically broken out.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 30 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of home prices slid further in February." Housing market "remained very weak" with depressed demand and excess supply.
**Center-of-gravity**: Shelter/housing costs declining amid weak market conditions.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Longer-term inflation expectations moved down in early April...and stayed within the range that has prevailed over the past several years." Staff models suggested increase in inflation compensation was "mostly attributable to increases in liquidity and inflation-risk premiums rather than higher expected inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored at target despite near-term elevation.
