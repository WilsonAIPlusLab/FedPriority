### Era Context
May 1995. The Fed had tightened aggressively in 1994 (fed funds from 3% to 6%). The economy is now showing a significant slowdown — industrial production falling, unemployment rising to 5.8%, motor vehicle sector weak. This is the classic 1995 "soft landing" period. Inflation had picked up somewhat but labor costs remained subdued.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE neutral. "Inflation had picked up somewhat in the early months of 1995." Consumer prices rose more rapidly, producer prices increased at a slightly faster pace then more briskly in April. Prices of intermediate materials "continued to increase rapidly." However, "trends in labor compensation costs remained subdued" and "gains in hourly compensation...slowed further." The staff forecast says "price inflation would likely moderate from its recently higher level." This is a mixed picture: goods/producer prices accelerating but labor costs contained. Direction: modestly above 50. "Somewhat" and "a little more rapidly" suggest 5-10 points above midpoint. Score ~55-58.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy as BELOW potential and moving further below. "The expansion of economic activity had slowed considerably further and rates of resource utilization had declined." The staff forecast: "Economic expansion would average less than the rate of increase in the economy's potential output for a number of months." This is explicit below-potential language. However, capacity utilization "remained at relatively high levels" and business investment "remained strong." The economy was recently above potential but is now falling below. Bright-line: "less than the rate of increase in the economy's potential output" = below potential = must be < 50. But also "operating rates in manufacturing remained at relatively high levels." Score ~44-47. The economy is crossing below potential but hasn't fallen far.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff reports: unemployment rate rose to 5.8% in April. Nonfarm payroll "changed little in April" after "reduced gains in first quarter." Initial claims "increased considerably." Hiring in services "down sharply." Manufacturing employment "decreased further." But labor compensation costs "remained subdued." UE at 5.8% anchors this in the 31-45 range per the rubric (5.5-7% range). The market is loosening from what was tighter. Score ~38-42.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had picked up somewhat in the early months of 1995. At the consumer level, prices rose a little more rapidly in the first quarter...In April, a surge in food prices and a rebound in energy prices contributed to a further step-up in consumer inflation."
- "At earlier stages of production, prices of intermediate materials continued to increase rapidly in April. By contrast, trends in labor compensation costs remained subdued."
- Staff forecast: "the prospects for some easing of pressures on resources suggested that price inflation would likely moderate from its recently higher level."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff reports a pickup in goods and producer prices but with subdued labor costs, placing inflation modestly above neutral — elevated now but expected to moderate.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because consumer and producer prices accelerated, intermediate materials prices rose rapidly, even though labor costs remained contained.
