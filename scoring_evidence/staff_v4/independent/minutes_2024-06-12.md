# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2024-06-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." No participant content needs to be filtered out.

**Era context**: June 2024. The Fed is in a holding pattern after aggressive rate hikes in 2022-23. Inflation has come down substantially from the 2022 peak but remains above the 2% target. The labor market is cooling gradually from very tight conditions. The economy is growing at a solid pace.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as above the midpoint. PCE at 2.7%, core PCE at 2.8%, CPI at 3.3%, core CPI at 3.4%. "Further progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective had been modest in recent months." This is clearly above target but down significantly from 2022 peaks. The staff projects inflation to decline further and reach close to 2% by 2026. Scoring the current level: clearly above target, "modest" progress. This maps to the 55-63 range. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Inflation is above target = more pressure. Score above 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as at or slightly above potential. "The economy was expected to maintain a high rate of resource utilization over the next few years" and "real GDP growth projected to be roughly similar to the staff's estimate of potential output growth." "U.S. economic activity had expanded at a solid pace." "High rate of resource utilization" suggests operating above potential but not dramatically so. Bright-line check: "high rate of resource utilization" implies above potential -> must be >50. Score ~53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as moderately tight but cooling. Unemployment ticked up to 4.0%. "Labor demand and supply continued to move into better balance." Job gains "strong" but vacancy-to-unemployment ratio at 1.2 (pre-pandemic level). Wage growth trending down. UE at 4.0% is below the typical NAIRU estimates. The rubric says UE below 4% -> almost certainly above 55. At exactly 4.0%, with language of "still low" unemployment and "strong" job gains, this is in the 53-58 range. Labor market is still tight but normalizing.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as solid. "PDFP—increased at a solid pace, similar to last year." "GDP and PDFP were increasing at solid rates in the second quarter." GDP rose "modestly" in Q1 but that was due to volatile components (inventories, net exports). Underlying demand is solid. This maps to the 50-55 range ("solid" = at or slightly above trend).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Mixed signals. Borrowing costs "remained elevated" across mortgages, credit cards, auto loans, C&I loans. But "financing was readily accessible for public corporations." Credit spreads at lowest decile of historical distribution. Stock prices up substantially. Short-term funding markets "stable." Credit generally available but some tightening for small firms and consumers. The overall picture: rates are high (tight), but capital markets are accommodative (loose). Net: somewhat tight due to elevated borrowing costs, but partially offset by easy capital market conditions. Score around 53-58.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No mention of market dysfunction. Yields moved down moderately. Market-based measures of interest rate uncertainty "remained elevated by historical standards" but this is about uncertainty, not dysfunction. No liquidity problems, no auction issues. Score in the normal range: 40-45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Yield spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds were little changed, remaining at about the lowest decile of their respective historical distributions." Lowest decile = very compressed = low stress. Bright-line check: compressed/narrow -> must be <50. Score ~18-22.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased substantially" with "positive investor outlook." One-month VIX "remained low by historical standards." No staff concern about overvaluation or undervaluation. Equities are up with low vol. This is modestly in the low-concern / elevated-valuation direction. Score ~35-40 (somewhat elevated valuations, no explicit concern but strong gains and low vol).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed as a standalone topic. Credit quality is mixed: solid for large firms and mortgages, deteriorating for CRE (CMBS delinquency rates rose to highest since 2021), rising credit card and auto delinquencies, small business delinquencies above pre-pandemic. But no mention of bank capital concerns, failures, or systemic stress. This is a mixed picture with pockets of deterioration but overall system is functioning. Score ~42-48 (neutral with some stress pockets). Bright-line check: no "well-capitalized" or "stressed" language directly about banks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated against most AFE currencies as differentials between U.S. and AFE yields narrowed. Nonetheless, the broad dollar index slightly increased as the dollar appreciated sharply against the Mexican peso." Net: broad dollar index slightly increased. This is essentially stable to very slightly stronger. Score ~52-54. Bright-line: the broad index "slightly increased" = slight appreciation -> slightly above 50.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not explicitly broken out as goods inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Core nonhousing services component remained elevated in several regions" — but this refers to foreign economies, not U.S. staff assessment. Not discussed for U.S. specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not explicitly discussed as a U.S. sub-component. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Some survey-based measures of short-term inflation expectations had moved up" but "longer-term expectations were little changed and stood at levels consistent with those that prevailed just before the pandemic." This is mostly anchored with a slight uptick in short-term. Score ~52-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (56)**: Close, both slightly above neutral. Coherent — economy at high resource utilization, labor market still tight but normalizing.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (53)**: Very close. Coherent — solid growth near potential.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (20)**: These diverge significantly. Explanation: Borrowing costs are elevated (tight conditions) but credit spreads are at historical lows (very easy credit market pricing). This is the 2024 dynamic where policy rates are high but risk appetite is strong. The divergence is justified by the text.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC somewhat tight, Treasury functioning normal. No contradiction — tight conditions from high rates, not from market dysfunction.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (58) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (~53). Consistent — overall inflation above target, expectations mostly anchored.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 2.7 percent in April, and core PCE price inflation...was 2.8 percent." "Further progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective had been modest in recent months."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is clearly above the 2% target with progress stalling, but well below 2022 peaks and projected to continue declining.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE and CPI readings are materially above the 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The economy was expected to maintain a high rate of resource utilization over the next few years." "U.S. economic activity had expanded at a solid pace so far this year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating at a high rate of resource utilization, with GDP growth roughly matching potential — slightly above potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff describes "high rate of resource utilization." Bright-line satisfied: above potential -> >50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate ticked up further to 4.0 percent." "The ratio of job vacancies to unemployment declined further to 1.2 in May, about the same as its pre-pandemic level." "Labor demand and supply continued to move into better balance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market still tight (UE at 4.0% with strong job gains) but clearly normalizing toward pre-pandemic balance. Vacancy-to-unemployment ratio back to pre-pandemic level suggests much of the excess tightness has been unwound.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE at 4.0% is still low historically, but only modestly above neutral given the normalization language.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Private domestic final purchases...increased at a solid pace, similar to last year." "Recent spending indicators suggested that GDP and PDFP were increasing at solid rates in the second quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and private investment at a solid pace, roughly at or slightly above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at/slightly above neutral because "solid pace" indicates healthy but not overheated demand.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Borrowing costs remained elevated despite declining modestly over the intermeeting period." "Financing was readily accessible for public corporations and large and middle-market private corporations."
**Center-of-gravity**: A mixed picture — elevated borrowing costs across most lending categories (somewhat tight) offset partially by strong capital market access and falling yields. Net slightly tight.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because borrowing costs are repeatedly described as "elevated" and "near recent high levels." Bright-line: "elevated" costs -> >50. Satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields at all maturities also moved down moderately." "Market-based measures of interest rate uncertainty ticked down but remained elevated by historical standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning appears normal with orderly yield declines. Elevated uncertainty is about rate path, not market dysfunction. No dysfunction signals.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral. No market dysfunction described. Yield movements were orderly. Bright-line satisfied: no dysfunction evidence, score below 55.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yield spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds were little changed, remaining at about the lowest decile of their respective historical distributions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads at the lowest decile of historical distributions — very compressed, indicating minimal credit stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are at historical lows. Bright-line satisfied: "lowest decile" / compressed -> <50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes increased substantially, on net, amid a positive investor outlook on corporate profits and economic activity." "The one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index remained low by historical standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose substantially with low volatility and positive outlook. No valuation concern expressed, but strong gains and low vol suggest elevated valuations. Modest overvaluation territory.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (lower = more overvaluation concern). Staff reports strong price increases with positive outlook and low vol — no alarm but elevated prices direction.

### BANKING_STRESS: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Credit quality continued to be solid for large and midsize firms, home mortgage borrowers, and municipalities but deteriorated further for other sectors." "In the CRE market, credit quality deteriorated further, as the average CMBS delinquency rate rose in April and May to the highest levels since 2021."
**Center-of-gravity**: No systemic banking stress, but notable deterioration in CRE, credit cards, auto loans, and small business. Overall system healthy with pockets of rising concern.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral — system is functioning but showing rising credit quality deterioration in specific sectors.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar depreciated against most AFE currencies as differentials between U.S. and AFE yields narrowed. Nonetheless, the broad dollar index slightly increased."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — down vs. AFE currencies but broad index slightly up due to sharp appreciation vs. Mexican peso. Net roughly stable to very slightly stronger.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is at/slightly above neutral. Broad index "slightly increased" -> slight appreciation -> slightly above 50. Bright-line satisfied.
