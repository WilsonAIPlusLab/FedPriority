# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2020-07-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains three clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views are included.

### Era Context
July 2020 — early stage of COVID-19 recovery. The economy suffered a catastrophic contraction in Q2 2020, with GDP declining at a "historically rapid rate." Labor markets saw massive job losses in March-April, with only partial recovery by June. Unemployment at 11.1%. Inflation very low. Financial markets still recovering from the March 2020 "dash for cash" but normalizing. Federal Reserve emergency facilities active.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as WELL BELOW midpoint. Total PCE at 0.5% over 12 months, core PCE at 1.0%. "Well below the rates that prevailed early in the year." Staff projects inflation to "remain subdued this year, reflecting the substantial amount of slack in resource utilization." This is clearly low inflation — the 1.0% core PCE is half the 2% target. Direction: well below 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as FAR BELOW potential. "Real GDP had decreased at a historically rapid rate in the second quarter." Activity picked up in May-June but remained far below pre-pandemic levels. Staff projects "substantial amount of slack in resource utilization." The staff outlook notes recovery won't be complete by year-end, and the pessimistic scenario is "no less plausible than the baseline." Bright-line check: "substantial slack" -> MUST be < 50. Direction: far below 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as VERY LOOSE. Unemployment at 11.1%, "far above its level at the beginning of the year." May-June gains offset "only about one-third of the jobs lost in March and April." Initial claims still elevated. Employment gains slowing since mid-June. Direction: far below 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending/investment as DEEPLY DEPRESSED but partially recovering. "Real consumer spending remained well below the levels that prevailed at the beginning of the year." Services spending (restaurants, hotels, air travel) "remained very subdued." Business fixed investment "had generally not begun to recover." Housing bounced back but still below pre-pandemic. Direction: well below 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes a MIXED picture — conditions easing from March crisis but still stressed. Corporate bond spreads "had retraced most of their pandemic-related surge." Capital market financing "eased somewhat further." BUT: bank lending standards "at the tighter end of their range since 2005." Small business financing "remained tight." Mortgage conditions tight for lower-credit borrowers. Consumer credit tightened further. Financial vulnerabilities characterized as "notable." Overall: conditions are still somewhat tight — market conditions improving but bank-intermediated credit quite tight. Direction: somewhat above 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields declined — normal price movement. Liquidity "continued to normalize but had not returned to their pre-pandemic levels in several markets." This is a mention of lingering liquidity issues but actively normalizing. No actual dysfunction described — just incomplete normalization. Direction: slightly above normal but not stressed. Score in the 42-48 range — normalizing from previous dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "narrowed" and "had retraced most of their pandemic-related surge." Yields on corporate bonds "near historical lows." BUT: bank lending standards very tight, defaults at highest since 2009, credit quality deteriorating, small business delinquencies at 2008 levels. The market-based spreads are narrowing but credit quality is poor. This is a split: market spreads compressing while underlying credit stress is high. Weighting both: still elevated overall despite market spread compression. Direction: above 50 but moderating.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes were roughly unchanged" over the period. VIX "remained elevated relative to its normal range." Staff's financial stability assessment: "asset valuation pressures were notable" — CRE prices increasing despite rising vacancy rates. Staff doesn't describe equities as distressed. The "notable" valuation pressures language suggests some concern but not extreme. Direction: roughly neutral to slightly concerned. Score near 45-50.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff describes notable stress. Banks tightened lending standards to "tighter end of their range since 2005." C&I loans contracted significantly. Financial leverage vulnerabilities increased from "low to moderate." Uncertainty about losses on business loans. Small business loan performance "deteriorated significantly; short-term delinquencies were comparable with levels seen in early 2008." CRE loan credit quality "continued to deteriorate." However, no bank failures mentioned, and the overall system vulnerability is "notable" not "severe." Direction: above 50 — meaningful stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad dollar index declined slightly, on net, with moderate depreciation against AFE currencies." Euro appreciated ~3% against dollar. Bright-line: depreciation -> MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Some recovery in goods prices. Prices "turned up after having fallen in March and April; this rebound was evident in many price categories." But overall inflation very low. Direction: below 50 but recovering.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Services spending "remained very subdued" — weak demand in services. No specific services inflation data but demand collapse implies low services price pressure. Direction: below 50.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity bouncing back but no specific housing inflation data cited. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10 year unchanged. NY Fed 3-year edged down but within recent range. TIPS-based inflation compensation rebounded from March lows but "remained below their pre-pandemic levels." Deflation risk "had abated somewhat." Direction: slightly below neutral — anchored but on the low side.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Total PCE price inflation was 0.5 percent over the 12 months ending in May, reflecting both weak aggregate demand and a considerable drop in consumer energy prices. Core PCE price inflation... was 1.0 percent over the same 12-month period."
- "Inflation was projected to remain subdued this year, reflecting the substantial amount of slack in resource utilization."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Staff reports PCE inflation at 0.5% and core at 1.0% — dramatically below the 2% target — with substantial slack keeping inflation subdued. This is well into the "meaningful deflation risk / well below target" range.

**Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is far below neutral because core PCE is half the target at 1.0% and staff projects inflation to "remain subdued."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** GDP declined at a historically rapid rate in Q2 2020, substantial slack in resource utilization, and complete recovery not expected by year-end. This is near the COVID trough anchor of 15-22.

**Direction confirmation:** OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because staff explicitly cites "substantial slack in resource utilization" and historically rapid GDP decline. Bright-line rule satisfied (slack -> < 50).
