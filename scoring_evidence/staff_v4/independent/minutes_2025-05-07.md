**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," "Staff Economic Outlook." Participant views begin at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions" and are excluded from scoring.

**Era context**: May 2025. The economy is navigating the aftermath of significant tariff announcements. GDP declined slightly in Q1 but largely due to measurement issues (import surge from front-loading). PDFP remained solid. Inflation is above target but easing. The unemployment rate is at 4.2%, stable. Tariffs are expected to drag on growth and boost inflation. This is a high-uncertainty environment with stagflationary cross-currents.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as above midpoint. PCE inflation at 2.3%, core PCE at 2.6% — both above the 2% target. Staff notes both total and core were "lower than their year-earlier levels" (decelerating), but the levels remain elevated. The staff projection says "tariffs were expected to boost inflation markedly this year." The word "somewhat elevated" is used in the participants section but the staff data clearly shows above-target readings. Score should be modestly above 50 — in the 55-62 range. The inflation is clearly above target but not severely so — 2.6% core is modestly elevated.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output gap as near potential currently but projecting widening. Real GDP declined slightly in Q1, but staff attributes this to measurement issues (import front-loading). PDFP posted "a solid gain in the first quarter that was similar to its average rate of increase over 2024." The staff projection says "the output gap was projected to widen significantly over the forecast period" and "the labor market was expected to weaken substantially." Currently the economy appears near potential (PDFP solid), but the staff's forward view is clearly below potential. Since the rubric says "score what the staff reports NOW" and the staff's current assessment is that PDFP is solid (near trend), but GDP declined and the outlook is for significant weakening — I'll weight both current and projected. Current is roughly at potential, outlook is clearly below. Score around 45-48: slightly below potential given the GDP decline and projected widening of the gap.

**Bright-line check**: The staff says the output gap "was projected to widen significantly" — this implies moving below potential. GDP declined. But PDFP was "solid." Mixed signals. The current state is roughly at potential with downward trajectory. Score 46.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff reports unemployment rate at 4.2% in March and April, "equal to its average over the second half of 2024." Participation rate rose 0.2pp, employment-to-population edged up. Payroll gains were "solid and roughly in line with the average pace seen over 2024." Job vacancies to unemployed workers ratio at 1.0. ECI rose 3.4%, "well below its year-earlier level." The labor market is solid but not particularly tight — UE at 4.2% with balanced V/U ratio at 1.0. This is near full employment. Score around 50-53. V/U at 1.0 (roughly balanced) and UE at 4.2% suggest near NAIRU. Wage growth moderating. Score 51.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff reports PDFP posted "a solid gain in the first quarter that was similar to its average rate of increase over 2024." Real GDP declined but due to import measurement issues. Foreign activity "moderate pace of expansion" but weakening momentum. Staff notes real imports soared from front-loading. The underlying picture is solid — at trend. Score around 48-52. "Solid" language in PDFP suggests at or modestly above trend. Score 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff mentions trade policies "were also expected to lead to slower productivity growth and therefore to reduce potential GDP growth over the next few years." This is a negative productivity outlook. Score below 50. "Slower productivity growth" = modestly below trend. Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff reports borrowing costs "increased notably in response to the tariff news." Yields increased from "already elevated levels" for an array of fixed-income securities. However, SLOOS reported "unchanged loan standards across all loan categories." Consumer credit "continued to be generally available." This is a mixed picture leaning toward somewhat tight. Borrowing costs elevated, availability reduced for spec-grade, but bank lending standards unchanged. Score 57-60. "Less available," "elevated" rates, "notably" increased costs all point to somewhat tight. Score 58.

**Bright-line check**: Evidence points to tight/tightening conditions. Score > 50. Check passes.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total consumer price inflation—as measured by the 12-month change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE)—was 2.3 percent in March. Core PCE price inflation...was 2.6 percent in March." Staff projection: "Tariffs were expected to boost inflation markedly this year."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Core PCE at 2.6% is modestly above the 2% target, with tariffs expected to push inflation higher; both total and core were decelerating from year-earlier levels but remain elevated.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because core PCE is 60bp above target and tariffs are projected to boost inflation further.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 46 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP declined slightly in the first quarter. However, this first-quarter estimate was likely affected by measurement issues." "Real private domestic final purchases (PDFP)...posted a solid gain in the first quarter that was similar to its average rate of increase over 2024." Staff outlook: "the output gap was projected to widen significantly over the forecast period."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Underlying economic momentum (PDFP) remains solid and near trend, but headline GDP declined and the staff projects significant output gap widening ahead, placing the current reading just slightly below potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is slightly below neutral; GDP declined and the gap is projected to widen, though PDFP remains solid. No explicit "above potential" language; the trajectory is clearly downward.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 4.2 percent in March and April, equal to its average over the second half of 2024." "The ratio of job vacancies to unemployed workers was 1.0 in April, slightly below its average over 2019." "The employment cost index...rose 3.4 percent over the 12 months ending in March, well below its year-earlier level."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is essentially in balance — UE at 4.2%, V/U ratio at 1.0, payroll gains solid, and wage growth moderating — placing it right at full employment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at neutral; the labor market is balanced with no significant slack or tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real private domestic final purchases (PDFP)...posted a solid gain in the first quarter that was similar to its average rate of increase over 2024." "Real imports of goods and services soared in the first quarter, likely driven by front-loading of imports."
