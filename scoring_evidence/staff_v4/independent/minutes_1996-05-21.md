### Era Context
Mid-1996: The U.S. economy is in the middle of its 1990s expansion. Unemployment is at 5.4%, which is near estimates of NAIRU at the time. The economy is growing moderately, with concerns about possible overheating. The fed funds rate is at 5.25%. This is the period just before the late-90s productivity boom became apparent.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as modestly above neutral. Evidence: "Upward pressures on food and energy prices accounted for somewhat larger increases in consumer prices." However, core measures showed restraint: "consumer prices rose only slightly in April" for nonfood/non-energy; twelve-month core CPI "increased a little less than the rise over the comparable year-earlier period." The staff forecast says "Inflation recently had been lifted by adverse developments in the energy market and was projected to remain above the levels of recent years, given the high level of resource utilization." This is a mixed picture — headline inflation elevated by food/energy, but core subdued. Staff projects inflation "above the levels of recent years" which implies modestly above recent trend but not alarming.
- Self-check: "somewhat larger increases" = modest move. Core inflation running slightly below year-earlier period suggests contained core. But staff forecast sees upside risks and projects inflation above recent levels. Net: modestly above neutral. Score ~53-55.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees food/energy lifting headline, core stable but forecast for higher inflation. Score above 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees the economy at or near potential. Key evidence: "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested that the economy would remain generally around its estimated potential." This is the clearest possible "at potential" language.
- Bright-line rule: "around its estimated potential" = at potential → score 48-52.
- Additional context: "economic activity had expanded moderately on balance"; capacity utilization "climbed in April to a rate slightly above that of the fourth quarter of 1995." The staff forecast notes "high level of resource utilization." This nudges slightly above pure neutral — the economy is AT potential with high resource utilization, not below it.
- Score: 51.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees the labor market as moderately tight. Unemployment fell to 5.4%, which in mid-1990s context is near NAIRU. "Nonfarm payroll employment was essentially unchanged in April after rising substantially in the first quarter." The staff forecast notes "high level of resource utilization" and mentions "still-favorable... employment and income conditions." Hourly compensation "expanded in the first quarter at the average rate for all of 1995" with "a sharp rise in wages and salaries" offset by declining benefit costs.
- At 5.4% unemployment in 1996, this is near the estimated NAIRU of the time (~5.5-6%). The rubric puts NAIRU/full employment at 46-52.
- Score: 50-52. The labor market is near full employment, not clearly tight, not clearly slack. Wage pressures are mixed (wages up but benefits down, net compensation stable).
- Score: 51.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees consumption and investment as moderately strong, near or slightly above trend. "Final demand, which had been quite robust early in the year, was showing some signs of slowing in recent data." Consumer spending "appeared to be growing at a moderate pace." "Business expenditures on durable equipment had registered further large gains." Housing "seemed to be holding up well." The staff forecast projects consumer spending growing "in line with disposable income" and business spending growing "less rapidly." Single-family starts "rose considerably in April."
- Net assessment: Q1 was strong ("quite robust"), recent data showing some moderation toward "moderate pace." Business investment was very strong in Q1 but decelerating. Housing strong. This is above-trend activity moderating toward trend.
- "Moderate pace" for consumption = ~50. "Further large gains" in equipment = above trend. Housing "well sustained" to strong. Net: slightly above trend.
- Score: 55.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not specifically discussed by staff. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff describes conditions as somewhat accommodative but tightening. Key staff-attributable evidence: Long-term rates rose, mortgage rates increased, dollar appreciated. But: "still-ample availability of credit," "equity and other financing remained available on relatively attractive terms" (this is from members section — ignore). From staff forecast: "still-ample availability of credit were expected to outweigh persisting consumer concerns." Stock prices rose. The staff forecast notes credit as "still-ample" and equity wealth as supportive.
- Financial conditions are somewhat accommodative despite recent tightening in bond yields. The staff forecast explicitly says "still-ample availability of credit" and notes favorable wealth effects from equities.
- Bright-line: "still-ample availability of credit" = accommodative → must be < 50.
- Score: 38. Conditions are somewhat loose/accommodative but have tightened from earlier in the year. Not extremely loose.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
The staff describes bond yield movements: "intermediate- and long-term rates came under upward pressure when incoming economic data were seen by market participants as pointing to stronger growth." This is normal yield adjustment from economic expectations — explicitly NOT dysfunction.
- Bright-line: Rising yields from economic expectations = normal (36-50).
- No mention of any market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues.
- Score: 42 (normal functioning with some yield volatility).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff describes "still-ample availability of credit" and "expansion in total domestic nonfinancial debt remained moderate." No mention of spread widening or credit stress. Credit appears freely available.
- This suggests below-normal spreads / favorable credit conditions.
- Score: 35.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff notes: "most indexes of stock prices rose on balance over the intermeeting period, apparently reflecting generally favorable first-quarter earnings reports and the improved economic outlook." The staff forecast mentions "favorable effect of higher equity prices on household wealth." No valuation concern expressed by staff.
- Stock prices rose, staff treats this as reflecting fundamentals (earnings + outlook). No concern language from staff.
- Score: 45 (roughly fair value, no particular concern, prices supported by earnings).

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Not specifically discussed by staff. N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: Dollar strengthened considerably. Staff reports: "the rise of U.S. interest rates contributed to a considerable appreciation of the trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the other G-10 currencies." "The dollar was particularly strong against the German mark." The directive states the dollar "has appreciated considerably."
- Bright-line: Dollar appreciation → score > 50. "Considerable appreciation" = significant strengthening.
- "Considerably" = 15-25 points from midpoint per magnitude rule → 65-75.
- Score: 67.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: "consumer prices rose only slightly in April" for nonfood/non-energy; producer prices of finished goods other than food/energy "recorded a third straight small increase." Core goods prices are modest. Score: 45.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out by staff. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff forecast mentions "Further risks of inflationary pressure" and the possibility that "higher food and energy prices might affect inflation expectations." Staff treats expectations as a risk but not yet realized. Score: 52 (near anchored but with acknowledged upside risk).

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (51) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51)**: Coherent. Both at approximately neutral, consistent with economy near potential and unemployment near NAIRU.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (51) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (55)**: Within 15 points. Final demand was robust early in the year but moderating. The slight divergence reflects strong spending against an economy assessed as being "around potential" — staff expects spending to moderate to be consistent with potential growth.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (35)**: Coherent. Both indicate somewhat accommodative/loose conditions, with ample credit and supportive financial conditions.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: Not contradictory. Loose financial conditions with normal Treasury market functioning is perfectly consistent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (54) ↔ Inflation sub-dimensions**: INFLATION_GOODS at 45 and INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS at 52. The headline inflation pressure is lifted by food and energy (not scored as sub-dimensions in goods since that covers core), which is consistent with a parent score modestly above 50.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Headline inflation pushed up by food and energy, but core inflation remains contained; staff projects inflation staying above recent (low) levels, placing overall pressure modestly above neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is slightly above neutral because headline inflation is elevated by food/energy while core remains subdued, and staff forecasts inflation above recent years.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 51 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested that the economy would remain generally around its estimated potential." "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested that economic activity had expanded moderately on balance in recent months."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff explicitly assesses the economy as operating around its estimated potential, with moderate overall growth.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is at neutral because staff says "around its estimated potential." Bright-line check: "around potential" → 48-52. Score of 51 satisfies this rule.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The civilian unemployment rate fell to 5.4 percent." "Nonfarm payroll employment was essentially unchanged in April after rising substantially in the first quarter." "Hourly compensation of private industry workers expanded in the first quarter at the average rate for all of 1995."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unemployment at 5.4% is near estimated NAIRU for this era; employment growth has been solid but flattened in April; compensation growth is steady, not accelerating — consistent with near-full-employment conditions.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because yield movements reflect normal economic expectations, not market dysfunction. Bright-line check: Rising yields from policy/economic expectations = normal (36-50). Satisfied.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit is freely available with no mention of spread widening, placing conditions in the somewhat-below-normal (favorable) range.
