### Era Context
December 2024: The Fed is in an easing cycle, having begun cutting rates. Inflation has come down substantially from its 2022 peak but remains somewhat above the 2% target. The labor market has cooled from its very tight 2022-2023 levels but remains solid. GDP growth has been resilient. The U.S. election recently occurred, introducing policy uncertainty.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint. Total PCE inflation was 2.3% (estimated 2.5% for November), core PCE at 2.8%, CPI at 2.7%, core CPI at 3.3%. All are below year-earlier rates (disinflation trend) but still "somewhat elevated." The staff forecast notes inflation in 2025 "was expected to remain at about the same rate as in 2024" with trade policy assumptions holding inflation up, and risks "tilted to the upside." This is modestly above target — "somewhat elevated" language with data points clearly above 2%. Direction: above 50. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" = 5-10 points above midpoint. Score ~57-60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as AT or NEAR potential. GDP "continued to expand at a solid pace," real GDP "posted a solid gain in the third quarter." The unemployment rate "was expected to be a bit higher but to remain near the staff's estimate of its natural rate." "Near natural rate" for unemployment and "solid" growth suggest the economy is operating roughly at potential. Bright-line: "near natural rate" maps to "near potential" => score 48-52. Score ~51.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as slightly above neutral but easing. "Labor market conditions had eased since early 2024" and "eased slightly further but remained solid." Unemployment at 4.2% in November. Job vacancies-to-unemployment ratio at 1.1, "slightly lower than its level just before the pandemic." Average hourly earnings rose 4%. The labor market is still solid but has normalized. UE at 4.2% is near the 4-4.5% range that maps to low-50s tightness. Wage growth at 4% is slightly above the ~3.5% that would be consistent with 2% inflation, suggesting modest remaining tightness. Score ~53.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as solid/above trend. "Real GDP growth remained solid," with "growth of real PDFP still outpacing that of real GDP" — PDFP (consumption + private fixed investment) is a strong signal. PDFP "rose faster than real GDP in the third quarter." "Economic conditions to stay solid." This is at-to-slightly-above trend. Score ~54.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff does not specifically discuss productivity growth. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff paints a mixed-to-somewhat-tight picture. "Borrowing costs for households, businesses, and municipalities remained elevated despite small declines in most credit segments." Mortgage rates "declined but stayed elevated." Credit card rates and auto loan rates "continued to be at historical highs." However, "financing in capital markets continued to be broadly available for large-to-midsize businesses and municipalities." For small businesses, "credit availability remained relatively tight." Conditions are still elevated/tight from the hiking cycle despite easing. Bright-line: "elevated" borrowing costs, "remained relatively tight" for small businesses => above 50. Score ~58.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Nominal Treasury yields across the maturity spectrum initially increased but then reversed those increases, ending the intermeeting period little changed." This is normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. Yields ended little changed. Score ~40 (normal functioning, no stress indicators).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "High-yield bond spreads narrowed." Yields on fixed-income securities "decreased slightly but remained elevated." The narrowing of high-yield spreads signals improving risk appetite. "Credit continued to be generally available for most households." But borrowing costs "remained elevated." The level of spreads is somewhat above normal given "elevated" language, but the direction is improving with narrowing HY spreads. Bright-line: spreads narrowing suggests moving toward lower stress, but "remained elevated" keeps it above neutral. Score ~48 — spreads are near-normal with mixed signals.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity prices rose, with more pronounced increases in stock prices in cyclical sectors amid increased investor optimism for corporate profits." The VIX "fell notably and remained well below pre-election levels." Staff describes rising prices and falling volatility — no valuation concern expressed. This is descriptive of price movements without concern. Default toward 45-50. Score ~45 (prices rising, low volatility, no concern expressed — slightly below neutral on the stress scale).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Credit quality discussion: "Credit quality remained solid for large-to-midsize firms, municipalities, and most home mortgage borrowers." "Credit performance of corporate bonds and leveraged loans was generally stable." C&I loan delinquencies "stable and remained within the range observed over the past decade." However, there are pockets of stress: small business loan delinquencies "continued to increase," CRE "credit performance deteriorated further as aggregate CMBS delinquency rates rose through October, driven by delinquencies on office loans," CRE loan delinquency rates at banks "ticked up from already elevated levels." FHA mortgage delinquencies "remained above pre-pandemic levels." Credit card delinquencies "continued to increase." This is a mixed picture: system broadly sound but with emerging stress in CRE, small business, and consumer credit. Bright-line: overall "solid" for large firms but notable deterioration in segments. Score ~45 (mostly healthy but emerging stress pockets keep it from being firmly below 40).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff reports "dollar appreciation against most foreign currencies" driven by foreign bond yields declining relative to U.S. counterparts and expectations of growth diverging. Bright-line: appreciation => score > 50. Score ~62 (broad-based appreciation, clearly stated).
