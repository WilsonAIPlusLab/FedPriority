# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-03-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: March 2012 — roughly 2.5 years into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment at 8.3%, still well above natural rate. The European sovereign debt crisis remains a background concern but has eased somewhat. Oil prices rising. Fed funds rate at the zero lower bound with forward guidance extending through late 2014.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key language: "overall consumer price inflation was relatively subdued," "consumer prices excluding food and energy also rose modestly," "nominal wage gains continued to be subdued." The staff projects inflation will be "subdued in 2012 and 2013." Oil and gasoline prices increased substantially, creating some near-term upward pressure, but the staff views this as temporary with energy prices expected to "level out." Long-run expectations "remained stable." This is clearly below-target inflation — in the 30-40 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Bright-line check: "wide margin of slack in product and labor markets," "the staff reduced its estimate of the level of potential output, yielding a measure of the current output gap that was a little narrower." Still clearly below potential. The gap is narrowing but remains wide. Score MUST be < 50. The language "wide margin of slack" suggests a meaningful gap — 33-38 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 8.3%, "remained elevated," "rate of long-duration unemployment" and "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons continued to be high." Conditions are improving (faster payroll growth, declining claims) but the level is very slack. UE at 8.3% maps to the 16-30 range per rubric (UE > 7%). Score ~28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend, mixed. Consumer spending: "real PCE were reported to have been flat in December and January," "spending for other consumer goods and services was weak," though motor vehicles were strong. Business equipment spending "rose at a notably slower pace," orders "declined in January." Housing "improved somewhat" but "continued to be restrained." Government spending declining. Net picture is weak/below trend. Score ~35.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as BELOW the midpoint (i.e., accommodative/loose). Bright-line: "U.S. financial conditions became somewhat more supportive of growth," "easing credit conditions," "strains in global financial markets eased." "Supportive" means score MUST be < 50. Multiple supportive indicators: equity prices rose significantly, credit spreads narrowed, funding conditions improved. Score ~32.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Yields on Treasury securities were little changed over the intermeeting period." No mention of any market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or stress. This is normal/calm market functioning. Score ~30 (normal, quiet).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as narrowing / below normal stress. "Reflecting a narrowing of spreads over yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds continued to decline over the period, moving toward the low end of their historical ranges." Spreads on ABCP "decreased significantly," LIBOR-OIS "narrowed." This is clearly below-normal credit stress. Score ~28.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes rose significantly." "Profit margins for large corporations remained wide by historical standards." No valuation concern expressed — just factual reporting of price rises. No "stretched" or "bubble" language. Default to roughly neutral. Score ~45.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff discusses banking in terms of improving conditions. "Aggregate credit quality of loans on banks' books continued to improve across most asset classes." C&I loan growth was "substantial and widespread." Bond issuance by financial firms was "strong." Equity prices of large banking organizations rose. But commercial real estate conditions "continued to be tight" and CRE loans "continued to decline." Mixed but generally improving — moderate-to-low stress. Score ~40.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar moved down modestly over the intermeeting period." Bright-line: depreciation = score < 50. "Modestly" = 5-10 points below midpoint. Score ~40.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Mixed. Energy prices "increased substantially," food prices "slowed." Core goods prices rose "modestly." The oil/gasoline surge is notable but goods ex-energy are subdued. Score ~45.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Home prices "were flat, on balance." Housing costs stable. Score ~45 (stable, slightly below neutral given flat prices in a period of general increase).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable." Near-term expectations "unchanged." Longer-term "remained in their recent range." Well-anchored at target. Score ~48.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28)**: Both well below 50, coherent. Labor is slightly lower reflecting UE at 8.3% — the output gap was narrowed by staff revision to potential, while labor slack remains very wide. Reasonable 7-point gap.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (35)**: Both at 35, coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (28)**: Both below 50, both indicating accommodative/favorable conditions. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs TREASURY_STRESS (30)**: Both low, coherent for a calm period with supportive financial conditions.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-dimensions**: Parent at ~35, goods ~45, housing ~45, expectations ~48. The parent is slightly lower, driven by the explicit "subdued" language and wage restraint that the sub-components don't fully capture. Reasonable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Overall consumer price inflation was relatively subdued in recent months." "The staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued in 2012 and 2013." "Nominal wage gains continued to be subdued."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees inflation running below target with muted wage pressures, though rising energy prices create some near-term upward risk.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff repeatedly characterizes inflation as "subdued" and "modest."

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The wide margin of slack in product and labor markets was expected to decrease gradually over the projection period." "The staff reduced its estimate of the level of potential output, yielding a measure of the current output gap that was a little narrower."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is clearly operating well below potential with a "wide margin of slack," though the gap has narrowed somewhat.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below 50 because "wide margin of slack" and "below potential" language. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate decreased to 8.3 percent in January and stayed at that level in February." "Both the rate of long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons continued to be high."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is very slack with UE at 8.3%, high long-term unemployment, and elevated underemployment, though conditions are improving at the margin.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.3% indicates substantial slack per the rubric (UE > 7% = 16-30 range).

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures were reported to have been flat in December and January." "Real business expenditures on equipment and software rose at a notably slower pace in the fourth quarter." "Housing market activity improved somewhat... but continued to be restrained."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spending is below trend — flat consumer spending, slowing business investment, and constrained housing, partially offset by strong motor vehicle sales and improving forward indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending is weak across most categories.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No explicit discussion of productivity trends in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. financial conditions became somewhat more supportive of growth over the intermeeting period, and strains in global financial markets eased." "Easing credit conditions" cited as a factor supporting the forecast.
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative and improving, with easing funding costs, narrowing spreads, and rising equity prices.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below 50 because "supportive" and "easing" language. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on Treasury securities were little changed over the intermeeting period." No mention of any market dysfunction or liquidity concerns.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets are functioning normally with little volatility — no stress indicators mentioned.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no dysfunction language, only calm, orderly markets.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds continued to decline over the period, moving toward the low end of their historical ranges." "The spread of the three-month LIBOR over the OIS rate narrowed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads are narrowing across the board and approaching historical lows, indicating very favorable credit conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below 50 because "narrowing," "low end of historical ranges." Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes rose significantly over the intermeeting period." "Profit margins for large corporations remained wide by historical standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose but staff expresses no valuation concern. Healthy margins noted. No stress or overvaluation language.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION near neutral — no staff concern about either overvaluation or distress.

### BANKING_STRESS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The aggregate credit quality of loans on banks' books continued to improve across most asset classes." "Financing conditions in the commercial real estate sector continued to be tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is improving with better credit quality and strong C&I lending, though CRE remains a pocket of concern. Moderately below neutral stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below 50 because credit quality is improving and banks are lending actively. Bright-line: improving credit quality = lower stress.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar moved down modestly over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar weakened modestly — straightforward modest depreciation.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below 50 because dollar depreciated. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices rose in January... gasoline prices moved up considerably in February and early March." "Increases in consumer food prices slowed in recent months." Core goods prices rose "modestly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Energy prices pushing up goods prices, but food slowing and core goods modest — net slightly below neutral.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No separate discussion of services inflation.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 45 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Home prices were flat, on balance, in December and January."
**Center-of-gravity**: Flat home prices suggest no shelter inflation pressure, slightly below neutral.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Thomson Reuters/University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers were unchanged in February, and longer-term inflation expectations in the survey remained in their recent range."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored and stable — essentially at target.
