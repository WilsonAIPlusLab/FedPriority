### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as BELOW the midpoint.
Evidence: "core price inflation had moderated a little recently after a pickup earlier in the year." "Core CPI rose relatively slowly in April and May." "Core producer price inflation for finished goods also was subdued." "upward pressures on energy prices had abated somewhat." The staff forecast says "sub-par expansion was expected to foster... some moderation in core price inflation."
However, "average hourly earnings... continued to rise... at the relatively brisk rate that had prevailed over the past year." This is a modest counterweight.
Magnitude: "moderated a little," "subdued," "no change on year-over-year basis" — this is modestly below 50. Core is not collapsing but has eased. Score ~40-42.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation has moderated, is subdued. Below 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy as BELOW potential.
Evidence: "economic activity continued to grow little, if at all, in the second quarter." "industrial output dropped sharply." The staff forecast says "after a period of very slow growth... the economic expansion would gradually regain strength... move back to a rate around the staff's current estimate of the growth of the economy's potential output." This explicitly says current growth is below potential. "The period of sub-par expansion was expected to foster an appreciable easing of pressures on resources."
Bright-line: "sub-par expansion," "appreciable easing of pressures on resources" = below potential. Score MUST be < 50.
Magnitude: Growth is "little if at all," industrial production contraction is "rapid," manufacturing capacity utilization at "lowest level since 1983." This is significant weakness. Score ~30-33.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Staff says well below potential. Low score is correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as softening but still at moderate levels.
Evidence: "Employment fell somewhat over the first two months of the quarter." "manufacturing recorded additional widespread job losses in May." "signs that weakness in employment was spreading to related sectors." Unemployment rate at 4.4% in May, but "initial unemployment insurance claims and other data suggested persisting softening." The staff forecast expects unemployment to rise to 4.75-5%.
At 4.4% UE, the labor market is still moderately tight by historical standards, but clearly loosening. The rubric says UE 4.5-5% corresponds to near NAIRU (46-52). UE at 4.4% is just below that threshold but falling.
Score: ~45-48. Labor market is softening toward neutral from above.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 4.4% and rising, jobs declining — this is at or just below the NAIRU zone, moving toward slack. 46 seems right.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees mixed picture — consumer spending slowing, business investment falling sharply.
Evidence: "Growth of consumer spending seemed to have slowed in the second quarter." "Business spending on equipment and software declined further early in the second quarter." "homebuilding had been well maintained." Consumer spending is soft but not collapsing; business investment is contracting significantly.
Net assessment: Consumer spending (slowing), business investment (declining appreciably), housing (stable/strong). The heavy emphasis is on business investment weakness and inventory liquidation. Net is clearly below trend.
Score: ~32-35. "Sluggish" consumer spending + "declined further" business investment + inventory liquidation, but housing holding up.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Staff sees weak spending/investment overall. Below 50 correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
The staff forecast mentions "continued elevated rates of technological progress" and members reviewed "staff reports that generally concluded that the growth of productivity in the years ahead was highly likely to remain appreciably stronger than it had been from the mid-1970s to the mid-1990s." However, there's also mention of "cyclical decline in productivity gains" (but that's in member discussion context).
The staff view is that structural productivity remains strong but current cyclical productivity is lower. The "staff reports" language on productivity is borderline — it says "members reviewed several staff reports." This is staff-produced analysis reviewed by members.
Score: ~55. Staff sees underlying productivity trend as strong, though acknowledging cyclical softness.
Polarity check: Higher = more productivity. Staff says trend productivity is strong. Above 50 is correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff sees financial conditions as mixed — accommodative in some respects, less so in others.
Staff-attributable: "short-term market rates declined somewhat." "Yields on long-term Treasury and investment-grade corporate securities fell appreciably." "rates on speculative-grade bonds rose sharply." "equity prices... edged lower." "broad monetary aggregates continued to grow rapidly."
The staff forecast notes "substantial monetary easing" already in place. Financial conditions are generally supportive for most borrowers but speculative-grade credit is tightening.
The member discussion (which I note but don't score) says "financial conditions, while generally supportive of greater spending, presented a mixed picture."
Net: Short rates down, monetary aggregates growing rapidly, credit available to most — but spec-grade stress and equity weakness. Conditions lean accommodative/loose but with pockets of tightness.
Score: ~42-45. Somewhat loose to neutral, acknowledging the mixed picture. The rate cuts and monetary growth push toward accommodative; spec-grade stress pulls slightly back.
Bright-line: Not clearly "accommodative" or "tight" in the staff's own words for overall conditions. Member language says "generally supportive" which would push below 50, but that's participant language. The staff-attributable data shows rate declines, monetary growth (loose), but spec-grade stress (tight). Score 43.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions are mostly accommodative but with spec-grade stress. Below 50 is correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

This is normal yield movement from policy expectations. Score: N/A or low normal. I'll score 40 — normal functioning, yields moving on macro expectations.
Actually, there's very little discussion of Treasury market functioning per se. Yield movements are noted but no discussion of dysfunction. Score N/A.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

Score: ~58. Speculative-grade spreads widening notably, but investment-grade fine.
Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress (wider spreads). Spec-grade stress is elevated. Above 50 is correct.
Bright-line: "rose sharply" on speculative-grade = widened. Score > 50 is correct.
