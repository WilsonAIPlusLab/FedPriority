## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The 12-month change in consumer prices...was close to the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent; excluding food and energy prices, inflation was little changed and continued to run somewhat below 2 percent." "Core PCE price inflation...was 1 3/4 percent." Staff projects inflation "slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Headline near target but core persistently below at 1.75%, with staff expecting gradual convergence but not reaching 2% by 2019.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because core inflation runs "somewhat below 2 percent" and the staff projects it staying below target through 2019.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." "The economy was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential." Unemployment rate projected to "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential with staff projecting it to remain so; the "modestly" qualifier keeps the score close to 50.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says economy is "operating above its longer-run potential." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged back down to 4.7 percent in February." "The labor market strengthened further in January and February." Payrolls increased at a "brisk pace," initial claims at a "very low level." UE projected to "edge down gradually" and "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment at 4.7% UE with staff projecting further tightening below the natural rate; compensation growing at a "moderate rate" suggests tightness not yet generating strong wage pressure.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at/slightly above neutral because UE at 4.7% is near NAIRU and projected to decline below it, but wage growth remains moderate.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be rising at a slower pace in the first quarter than in the fourth quarter." But fundamentals "consistent with moderate increases in real PCE." Business equipment "appeared to be rising," residential investment "increased at a solid pace." Business sentiment "upbeat."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture -- consumer spending decelerating from Q4 but supported by solid fundamentals; business investment rising; residential investment solid. Overall a moderate, at-trend pace.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral because spending and investment are moderate -- not weak, not strong.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses, households, and state and local governments remained generally accommodative in recent months." "Financial markets were generally quiet." "Credit flows to large firms remained solid." "Financing conditions for consumers remained accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes broadly accommodative financing conditions with quiet markets, solid credit flows, and favorable terms. This is moderately loose for the 2017 neutral-anchor period.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff repeatedly uses "accommodative" and "favorable" language. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financial markets were generally quiet." Treasury yields rose due to Fed communications -- normal price adjustment. No mention of liquidity issues, auction problems, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning; yield changes driven by policy expectations, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at normal levels because yield movements reflect policy expectations, not market stress. Bright-line rule satisfied (no dysfunction = 36-50).

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on bonds issued by nonfinancial corporations over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities were little changed." "Strong bond issuance by investment-grade corporations and brisk originations of leveraged loans." "Emerging market bond spreads narrowed." "Financing conditions...remained generally accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads stable at favorable levels, strong issuance, accommodative credit conditions -- below normal stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are stable/favorable with strong issuance and accommodative conditions.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Some measures of valuations, such as price-to-earnings ratios, rose further above historical norms." "A standard measure of the equity risk premium edged lower, declining into the lower quartile of its historical distribution of the previous three decades."

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (more overvaluation concern) because staff notes valuations "above historical norms" and risk premium in the "lower quartile." Per the rubric, lower scores = more overvaluation concern.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the banking system shows no meaningful stress -- credit is flowing and conditions are accommodative.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 49 +/-4 [High confidence]
