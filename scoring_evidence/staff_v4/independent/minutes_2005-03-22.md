# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — March 22, 2005 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary). It is marked as unsegmented pre-2009 minutes, meaning staff review content is mixed with participant discussion and policy action. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable language ("the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," staff-reported data sections) from participant-attributable language ("many participants said," "members noted," "some expressed").

### Era Context
March 2005: mid-expansion period. The Fed was in a "measured pace" tightening cycle, raising the funds rate by 25bp increments from June 2004. The economy was growing solidly, labor markets were firming, energy prices were rising, and core inflation was relatively contained but showing signs of uptick. Housing market was booming. This context should NOT override the text.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following paragraphs contain staff-attributable content:
- Paragraphs starting with "The information reviewed at this meeting..." through the trade deficit/international paragraph (lines 10-31) — these are factual staff review sections.
- "In the staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." (line 46) — explicitly staff forecast.
- Financial market developments paragraph (line 40) — describes market developments with some staff analysis attribution.
- The paragraphs beginning with "Many participants noted..." (lines 49+) are PARTICIPANT views and should be IGNORED for scoring.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff-attributable evidence:
- "Consumer prices edged higher in January after having been flat in December."
- "Core consumer price inflation over the same period was fairly subdued."
- "A survey measure of near-term inflation expectations moved up in early March, but long-term expectations had changed little."
- Staff forecast: "Measures of overall consumer price inflation were expected to be lower this year than last... Inflation in core consumer prices was seen as being boosted a bit by the effects of higher import and energy costs in the near term but still largely contained."

Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as modestly above neutral. Core inflation is "fairly subdued" but with some near-term upward pressure from energy and import costs. The staff forecast says "largely contained" but "boosted a bit."

Self-check: "Somewhat" / "a bit" = 5-10 points from midpoint. Staff sees current inflation as slightly above subdued with modest upward pressure. Score ~53.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says "edged higher," "boosted a bit" but "largely contained." Modestly above neutral is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff-attributable evidence:
- "The economy was expanding at a solid pace in the first quarter."
- Staff forecast: "the economy was seen as likely to expand at a rate above the growth of potential this year and next."
- Staff forecast also mentions "remaining slack in resource markets."

Direction: Staff sees the economy growing ABOVE potential but with REMAINING SLACK — meaning output is still BELOW potential but closing. The growth rate is above potential (closing the gap), but the level of output has not yet reached potential.

Bright-line check: "remaining slack in resource markets" = score MUST be < 50. The growth rate is above potential, but the OUTPUT_GAP dimension scores the LEVEL relative to potential, not the growth rate. Slack remaining = below potential.

Score: ~45. Economy is approaching potential but not there yet.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff-attributable evidence:
- "The labor market continued to improve in February. Private nonfarm payrolls grew at a solid pace."
- "the unemployment rate in February ticked back up to its December level" (unemployment was ~5.4% in early 2005)
- "the set of available information on the labor market suggested that resource slack was diminishing."
- Staff forecast: "labor markets were expected to continue to firm and the unemployment rate to decline gradually."

Direction: Labor market is improving but slack remains. Unemployment ~5.4% puts this in the "below full employment; slack remains" range (31-45). But it's diminishing, suggesting the upper end of that range.

Score: ~43. Improving but still with slack; UE around 5.4%.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff-attributable evidence:

- "consumer spending had increased at a robust rate in the last quarter of 2004 and appeared to be on track to post another strong advance."

- Staff forecast: "led by strong business demand for equipment and software."
- "Household spending... was projected to expand at a solid rate."

Direction: Both consumption and investment are clearly above trend. "Briskly," "robust," "sharply," "strong" — this is clearly above-trend language.

Magnitude: "Briskly" and "robust" are strong language = 10-15 points above midpoint. Score ~63.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**

Direction: Staff sees productivity as strong, above trend. Score ~60.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**

Bright-line check: "accommodative" / "supportive" / "easy" = score MUST be < 50. Also from participant section (which I note only for confirmation): "monetary conditions evidently were still quite accommodative."

Score: ~25. Conditions are loose. Era anchor for 2005-06 is 10-20. The description shows clearly accommodative conditions but Treasury yields are rising, so not at the extreme low end. Score 25.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core consumer price inflation over the same period was fairly subdued." Staff forecast: "Inflation in core consumer prices was seen as being boosted a bit by the effects of higher import and energy costs in the near term but still largely contained."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees core inflation as subdued but with modest near-term upward pressures from energy and import costs; overall picture is slightly above neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: Inflation pressure is slightly above neutral because energy and import cost pass-through is providing modest upward pressure, though core remains "largely contained."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Staff forecast: "the economy was seen as likely to expand at a rate above the growth of potential this year and next." Also: "remaining slack in resource markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is growing above potential (closing the gap) but slack still remains, placing output below potential.
**Direction confirmation**: Output gap is below neutral because "remaining slack in resource markets" — bright-line rule satisfied (slack remains = below 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "the set of available information on the labor market suggested that resource slack was diminishing." "the unemployment rate in February ticked back up to its December level" (approximately 5.4%).
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is improving with diminishing slack, but unemployment near 5.4% and "resource slack" still present keeps this below the full-employment threshold.
**Direction confirmation**: Labor tightness is below neutral because slack remains and unemployment is still above NAIRU estimates of the era.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer spending still appeared to be growing briskly." "Business spending on equipment and software increased sharply in the fourth quarter and... appeared to be growing briskly in the first quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are clearly above trend with "brisk" and "sharp" language, placing this well above the midpoint.
**Direction confirmation**: Consumption/investment is above neutral because of "briskly," "robust," and "sharply" language describing both consumption and investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: Staff forecast: "continued strong growth in underlying labor productivity." (Staff-attributable from the forecast paragraph.)
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff views productivity growth as strong and above trend, helping to contain unit labor costs.
**Direction confirmation**: Productivity is above neutral because staff describes "strong growth in underlying labor productivity."

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are clearly accommodative with narrow spreads, low interest rates, and easy credit — consistent with the 2005 pre-crisis ease era.
**Direction confirmation**: Financial conditions are well below neutral (loose/accommodative) because of narrow spreads, low rates, and "attractive cost of capital" — bright-line rule satisfied.

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yield movements were driven by normal policy expectation adjustments with no mention of market dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: Treasury stress is below neutral/normal because yield rises reflect orderly policy repricing, not dysfunction. Bright-line rule: no actual dysfunction described, so score stays in the 36-50 normal range.

**Evidence**: "Risk spreads on most corporate bonds narrowed on balance, significantly so for speculative-grade debt, amid generally strong corporate balance sheets and good credit performance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are narrow and narrowing further, especially in speculative-grade — classic 2005 compressed-spread environment.
**Direction confirmation**: Credit spreads are well below neutral (compressed) because "narrowed... significantly so for speculative-grade" — bright-line rule satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock market indexes edged up over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Only price movement described without valuation commentary; defaults to near-neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: Near neutral — no valuation concern expressed by staff.
