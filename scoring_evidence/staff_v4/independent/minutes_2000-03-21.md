# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — March 21, 2000

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff review content is mixed with participant discussion. I must score ONLY staff-attributable language (phrases like "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," and objective data descriptions in the review-of-conditions paragraphs) and IGNORE participant/member content ("members commented," "the Committee's discussion," "members expressed concern").

### Staff-Attributable Content Identified
- **Economic review paragraphs** (lines 46-70): Begin with "The information reviewed at this meeting..." — standard staff review language covering GDP, labor, production, spending, housing, investment, inventories, trade, and prices.
- **Financial market paragraphs** (lines 76-82): Market developments, interest rates, equity prices, FX, monetary aggregates.
- **Staff forecast** (line 85): "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..."

### Content to IGNORE
- Lines 88-110: "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective economic developments, members commented..." — this is participant content.

### Era Context
March 2000: Late-1990s boom. Dot-com bubble peak. Unemployment near 4%. Fed in tightening cycle (funds rate going to 6%). Strong productivity growth. Inflation moderate but risks rising.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as slightly above the midpoint but restrained. Key staff language: "Price inflation had remained moderate in recent months, with the exception of higher energy prices." Core CPI was stable year-over-year. However, the staff forecast projects "core price inflation was projected to increase somewhat over the forecast horizon, partly as a result of rising import prices and some firming of gains in nominal labor compensation in persistently tight labor markets." Also: "producer prices registered somewhat larger increases" at earlier stages of processing. The staff sees moderate current inflation but building pipeline pressures.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says moderate NOW but rising. Score should be modestly above 50.
- Score direction: Above midpoint. Staff sees current moderation but pipeline pressures and a forecast of "somewhat" higher core inflation. "Somewhat" = 5-10 points above 50.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees the economy running above potential. Staff language: "expansion of economic activity remained rapid," "the staff forecast...suggested that the economic expansion would moderate gradually from its currently elevated pace to a rate around, or perhaps a little below, the growth of the economy's estimated potential." The word "currently elevated pace" implies above potential now. Also: "bring the growth of aggregate demand into better alignment with the expansion of potential aggregate supply" (from the prior meeting directive context, but the staff forecast references this).
- Bright-line check: "Currently elevated pace" relative to potential growth implies above potential. Score MUST be > 50.
- Magnitude: "Rapid" expansion, demand exceeding supply. This is clearly above potential. Late 90s boom anchor: 57-63.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees very tight labor markets. "Labor markets continued to be very tight." Unemployment at 4.1% — "just above its 1999 low." "Initial claims for unemployment insurance were at an extremely low level." "Labor demand remained robust."
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Staff says "very tight." UE at 4.1% in 2000 (note: NAIRU estimates at the time were around 5-5.5%).
- Magnitude: "Very tight" = 15-25 points above midpoint. Anchor for 1999-2000: 65-75. UE at 4.1% with "extremely low" claims and "robust" labor demand supports 68-72.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees very strong spending. "Consumer spending and business fixed investment were still trending upward strongly." "Retail sales continued to increase rapidly." "Business spending on durable equipment and software and on nonresidential structures increased sharply in January." Housing "remained strong."
- Magnitude: "Strongly," "rapidly," "sharply" — these are strong descriptors. Well above trend.
- Score: 68-72 range. All three major components (consumption, business investment, housing) are described as strong to very strong.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: Staff discusses productivity explicitly in the forecast: "not be fully offset by productivity growth" and the review notes capacity utilization "stayed a little below its long-term average" despite strong manufacturing output (implying capacity/productivity growth). The staff forecast implies continued strong productivity but not enough to offset demand pressures.
- The minutes note "remarkable acceleration in productivity" in the member discussion, but the staff forecast itself references productivity growth as a factor. The staff review's observation that capacity utilization remained below average despite rapid production growth implies strong productivity/capacity expansion.
- Score: Above trend but discussed indirectly. ~58-62.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff describes market conditions. "Interest rates on private instruments registered small mixed changes while yields on longer-term Treasury securities declined significantly." "Most major indexes of equity prices moved up appreciably." The staff forecast notes expansion "held back by...higher interest rates" going forward, suggesting some tightening effect. But current conditions: equity prices up, private rates little changed, Treasury yields down.
- Bright-line check: The overall picture is mixed — rates have been rising (fed funds to 5.75%) but equity markets are buoyant and credit appears available. The staff doesn't describe conditions as "accommodative" or "tight" explicitly in the staff sections. Given rising equity prices and available credit but higher policy rates, conditions are roughly neutral to slightly accommodative.
- Score: ~43-47. Not clearly tight despite rate hikes; equity buoyancy and credit availability offset.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: No discussion of Treasury market dysfunction. Yields declined. "Yields on longer-term Treasury securities declined significantly." There is extensive discussion of SOMA portfolio management and Treasury debt paydowns, but this is about operations, not market stress.
- Score: N/A or low normal. The market is functioning — yields moved on fundamentals. No dysfunction language. Score ~38 if scored, but leaning N/A since there's no stress discussion. I'll score it as the yield discussion is present but shows normal functioning.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Staff mentions "interest rates on private instruments registered small mixed changes while yields on longer-term Treasury securities declined significantly." This implies private spreads widened somewhat relative to Treasuries (since Treasury yields fell more than private rates). But no explicit spread commentary.
- The staff doesn't directly discuss credit spreads or credit availability in staff-attributable sections. In the member section (which I ignore), there's mention of "ready availability of both debt and equity financing" but that's members.
- Score: Limited evidence. The implicit spread widening (private rates flat vs. Treasury yields down) suggests very modest widening but nothing alarming. ~48-52 with low confidence.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Direction: Staff notes "most major indexes of equity prices moved up appreciably on net over the intermeeting period" and mentions "surging prices of technology-related equities." The staff forecast references "waning of positive wealth effects associated with large earlier gains in equity prices." Staff doesn't make explicit valuation judgments in the staff sections.
- The staff mentions "greater volatility in equity prices" as a factor moving rates. But no explicit staff valuation concern language (the "historically elevated valuations" language is in the member section, which I ignore).
- Given it's March 2000 (dot-com peak) and staff notes surging tech equity prices and "greater volatility," there's implied concern but not explicit. Score around 25-30 (elevated valuations implied by the wealth effects discussion and tech surge).

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Direction: No discussion of banking system health or stress in staff-attributable content.
- Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: Staff says "the trade-weighted value of the dollar changed little over the intermeeting period against a basket of major currencies." Mixed movements: up against some, down against others.
- Bright-line check: "Changed little" = stable = ~50.
- Score: 50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

Staff forecast: "Core price inflation was projected to increase somewhat over the forecast horizon, partly as a result of rising import prices and some firming of gains in nominal labor compensation in persistently tight labor markets."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees currently moderate core inflation but with building pipeline pressures and a forecast of "somewhat" higher core inflation ahead.
- **Direction confirmation**: Inflation pressure is above neutral because staff projects rising core inflation from import prices and labor costs, even though current readings are moderate.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The expansion of economic activity remained rapid." Staff forecast: "the economic expansion would moderate gradually from its currently elevated pace to a rate around, or perhaps a little below, the growth of the economy's estimated potential."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes the economy as expanding at an "elevated pace" above potential, with gradual moderation expected.
- **Direction confirmation**: Output gap is above neutral because staff explicitly describes expansion as above potential growth rate. Bright-line rule satisfied (above potential = score > 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Labor markets continued to be very tight." "The civilian unemployment rate, at 4.1 percent in February, was just above its 1999 low, and initial claims for unemployment insurance were at an extremely low level."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly describes labor markets as "very tight" with unemployment near cycle lows and extremely low claims.
- **Direction confirmation**: Labor tightness is well above neutral. "Very tight" with 4.1% unemployment supports a score in the 65-75 anchor range.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending and business fixed investment were still trending upward strongly." "Retail sales continued to increase rapidly in January and February." "Business spending on durable equipment and software and on nonresidential structures increased sharply in January."
- **Center-of-gravity**: All major spending categories — consumer, business equipment, nonresidential structures, housing — described as strong to very strong, well above trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: Consumption and investment are well above neutral based on "strongly," "rapidly," and "sharply" language across all components.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning appears normal; yield movements reflect fundamentals (policy expectations, economic data), not dysfunction.
- **Direction confirmation**: Treasury stress is at/below neutral. No market dysfunction described. Normal functioning.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 50 +/-10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: Private instrument rates showed "small mixed changes" while Treasury yields "declined significantly" — implying modest relative widening of private-over-Treasury spreads. No explicit spread commentary in staff sections.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Very limited direct evidence. Implicit modest spread widening but no concern language. Scored at neutral with wide uncertainty.
- **Direction confirmation**: Credit spreads are at neutral — no explicit evidence of stress or compression.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 25 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

"Surging prices of technology-related equities might have spurred depositors to shift some of their M2 balances into equity mutual funds."
