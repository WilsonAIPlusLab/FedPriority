# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-01-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant views need filtering.

### Era Context
January 2013: The U.S. economy is in mid-recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.8%. The Fed is conducting large-scale asset purchases (QE3). Fiscal cliff concerns have partially resolved. Inflation is subdued, running below the 2% target. This is a period of below-potential growth with significant labor market slack.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key evidence: "Consumer price inflation was subdued"; "the rise in consumer prices excluding food and energy slowed"; "the staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2015"; "significant resource slack will persist." Wages also "remained relatively slow." This is clearly below-target inflation. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. The language "subdued" is consistent with the lower end of that range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Key evidence: "the expansion in economic activity was expected to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets"; "increases in real GDP would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output"; "significant resource slack will persist over the forecast period." Bright-line check: "slack remains" language → MUST be < 50. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as LOOSE. Key evidence: Unemployment at 7.8%, "still high"; "rate of long-duration unemployment... still elevated"; "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons... still elevated"; "hiring plans, was generally muted." With UE at 7.8%, the anchor table says UE > 7% → 16-30 range. However, there are some positive signals (moderate employment increases, UE declining from prior quarter), so the upper end of 16-30 or low 30s is appropriate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED, roughly below trend. Consumer spending "picked up somewhat" boosted by motor vehicles; equipment/software "rose briskly" after declining; but business nonresidential construction "declined somewhat"; inventory subtracted "considerable amount" from GDP; consumer sentiment "more downbeat"; forward indicators suggest "only modest" increases. Net position: some positive momentum but restrained, below trend. Anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate."

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. → N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as IMPROVING/ACCOMMODATIVE. "U.S. financial market conditions improved on net"; banks "continued to ease somewhat their lending standards"; credit spreads narrowed; equity prices rose; consumer credit "expanded briskly." Bright-line check: "improved" / easing conditions → score < 50. This is accommodative/supportive territory.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose, but this was attributed to improved sentiment and fiscal cliff resolution — normal market functioning. "Conditions in short-term dollar funding markets were generally little changed"; "year-end funding pressures were modest." No dysfunction language. Bright-line: yield movements from improved outlook are NOT stress. Score 36-45 range (normal functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as COMPRESSED/favorable. "Risk spreads on speculative-grade bonds narrowed substantially"; "spreads on investment-grade bonds decreased as well"; "net debt financing by nonfinancial firms increased"; consumer credit "expanded briskly." Bright-line: "narrowed" → score < 50. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 generally favorable.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities rose, implied volatility "dipped to its lowest level since early 2007." But "option-implied price of insurance against downside risk... at longer horizons remained elevated." Staff reports price moves without explicit valuation concern. Default to 45-50 range. The low volatility and rising prices suggest no stress — slightly below neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Indicators of the condition of domestic financial institutions generally improved"; "bank stock prices rose"; "median spread on credit default swaps of the largest banking organizations moved lower." Banks easing lending standards. Bright-line: improving conditions = LOW stress → MUST be < 50. Commercial real estate "continued to be strained" with "elevated vacancy and delinquency rates"; mortgage delinquencies "remained high." These add some stress pockets. Score in 35-45 range.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated against the euro and most emerging market currencies. Against the yen, however, the dollar appreciated substantially." Mixed but net depreciation against most currencies. Bright-line: depreciation → score < 50.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declined; food prices rose faster (drought effects). Mixed within goods. Energy decline is more prominent in overall narrative.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. → N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "increased further" and housing conditions "continued to improve" but this is about asset prices, not shelter inflation in CPI. Not explicitly discussed as inflation component. → N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"; "longer-term inflation expectations were unchanged." Anchored at or near target. Score ~48.

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Slack remains" = below potential. Score below 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE 7.8%, "still high." Score well below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. "Improved" / "easing standards." Score below 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. "Narrowed substantially." Score below 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. "Generally improved." Score below 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Net depreciation against most currencies. Score below 50. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (37) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30)**: Both well below 50, consistent. Gap of 7 points — reasonable since output may be recovering faster than labor market.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (37) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50, within 3 points. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (30)**: Both below 50, within 2 points. Coherent — both point to accommodative/easy conditions.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC loose while Treasury functioning normally. Consistent for non-crisis period.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Goods mixed (energy down, food up) → ~42; expectations stable → ~48. Parent at 38 reflects "subdued" overall, consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was subdued, and measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "The staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2015." "The rise in consumer prices excluding food and energy slowed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly characterizes inflation as subdued and below target, with expectations for it to remain so through 2015 due to persistent resource slack.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff uses "subdued" language and projects continued below-target inflation. Consistent with 2013 anchor of 38-42.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity was expected to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets over the projection period." "Significant resource slack will persist over the forecast period." "Increases in real GDP would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly operating below potential with significant slack, and only gradual closing expected. GDP growth slowed in Q4 due to transitory factors.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "significant resource slack" persisting. Bright-line satisfied: slack language → score < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 7.8 percent in December"; "the rate of long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons... were still elevated"; "the rate of private-sector hiring... was generally muted."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market remains very slack with high unemployment, elevated underemployment, and muted hiring — though gradually improving.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 7.8% indicates substantial slack. Anchor guidance: UE > 7% → 16-30 range. Score at top of that range given moderate improvement signals.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Private domestic final demand grew at a solid rate." But: "broader indicators... were at levels consistent with only modest increases in factory output"; "consumer sentiment was more downbeat"; inventory subtracted "a considerable amount" from GDP; "outlays for business equipment would rise only modestly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — private demand showing some strength (equipment/software rebound, consumer spending pickup) but overall GDP slowed, forward indicators tepid, and fiscal drag ahead.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral, consistent with 2011-13 "moderate" anchor of 35-42.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- **Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "U.S. financial market conditions improved on net"; "domestic banks continued to ease somewhat their lending standards and some loan terms"; "consumer credit expanded briskly"; spreads narrowed; equity prices rose.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly accommodative and improving — easing bank lending, narrowing spreads, rising equity prices, strong credit growth.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff describes broad improvement and easing. Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative"/"improved" → score < 50.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 40 ±4 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term dollar funding markets were generally little changed on balance; year-end funding pressures were modest overall." Treasury yields rose due to improved sentiment (normal adjustment).
- **Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning. Yield increases reflect improved outlook, not dysfunction. No liquidity concerns mentioned.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral. No dysfunction language. Bright-line satisfied: yield movement from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50).

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Risk spreads on speculative-grade bonds narrowed substantially, and spreads on investment-grade bonds decreased as well." "Net debt financing by nonfinancial firms increased." "Consumer credit expanded briskly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads notably compressed with strong issuance and narrowing across the board — clearly favorable credit conditions.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (compressed) because spreads "narrowed substantially." Bright-line satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 43 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased on net"; "option-implied volatility... dipped to its lowest level since early 2007." But: "option-implied price of insurance against downside risk at longer horizons remained elevated."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising with very low near-term volatility, suggesting no stress/concern. Staff does not offer valuation judgment. Low volatility since 2007 leans slightly toward "no concern" territory.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION slightly below neutral — no stress signals, equities performing well, though longer-term tail risk priced in.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 40 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Indicators of the condition of domestic financial institutions generally improved"; "bank stock prices rose"; "median spread on credit default swaps of the largest banking organizations moved lower." But: "Conditions in the commercial real estate sector continued to be strained amid elevated vacancy and delinquency rates"; "share of existing mortgages that were seriously delinquent... remained high."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system improving with lower CDS spreads and rising stock prices, but legacy stress pockets remain (CRE, mortgage delinquencies). Net: below neutral stress but not fully healthy.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because conditions "generally improved." Bright-line satisfied: improving indicators → score < 50.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The dollar depreciated against the euro and most emerging market currencies. Against the yen, however, the dollar appreciated substantially."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Mixed — net depreciation on a broad basis against most currencies, partially offset by substantial appreciation against the yen. Weight toward depreciation since it was against "most" currencies.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because net depreciation against most currencies. Bright-line satisfied: depreciation → score < 50.

### Inflation Sub-Components
