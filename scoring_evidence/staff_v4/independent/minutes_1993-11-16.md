### Era Context
November 1993: The U.S. economy is in the early-to-mid phase of recovery from the 1990-91 recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 6.8%. Inflation is low and declining. The fed funds rate is at 3%. This is a period of moderate expansion with significant remaining slack.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "inflation had remained muted, with consumer prices increasing moderately on balance in recent months and producer prices falling." Producer prices for nonfood/non-energy finished goods showed "the lowest yearly increase on record." Staff projection: "continued slack in labor and product markets...was expected to foster further reductions in wage and price inflation." However, labor compensation was not showing a "comparable downtrend" — hourly compensation "advanced slightly faster" year-over-year. Net: inflation clearly contained/muted but not deflationary.
- Direction: Below 50. Language is "muted," "moderately," "falling" — suggests 30-38 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says muted/falling. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees economy BELOW potential but closing. Staff projection explicitly states: activity "would expand moderately next year, about in line with the potential rate of economic growth over time, and thus would be associated with limited, if any, further reductions in margins of unemployed labor and capital." This means current margins of slack exist (below potential) but are expected to persist, not close further. "Some strengthening in the expansion" but unemployment at 6.8%, slack in labor and product markets.
- Direction: Below 50. "Margins of unemployed labor and capital" = slack remains.
- Bright-line check: Slack remains → MUST be < 50. Score in 38-44 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Slack remains = below potential. Below 50. Correct.

- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. UE 6.8% = significant slack. Below 50. Correct.

Retail sales "up substantially in October." Housing starts at highest in almost five years. BUT business capital spending "increased in the third quarter at a considerably slower pace than earlier." Mixed picture: consumer/housing strong, business investment slowing.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral to at neutral. Consumer spending strong, housing strong, but business investment slowing. "Some strengthening in the expansion" overall. Staff projection: "advancing relatively strongly in the fourth quarter" but then "expand moderately next year."
