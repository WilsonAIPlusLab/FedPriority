### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint.
Evidence: "Inflation remained low despite some firming of energy prices." Core PCE price inflation "declined appreciably" on a year-over-year basis. Employment cost index "rose moderately" and average hourly earnings "advanced only slightly." Staff forecast: "persistence of underutilized resources was expected to keep downward pressure on core price inflation."
Magnitude: "low," "declined appreciably," "downward pressure" — this is clearly below target. Language suggests contained/modest levels = 26-40 range. The staff explicitly forecasts continued downward pressure. Score: 30.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low and declining inflation. Score 30 (below midpoint) = correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy BELOW potential.
Evidence: "overall activity would remain below estimates of the economy's potential output for some time." Activity is "expanding briskly" but from a low base. Resource utilization would "rise somewhat more than anticipated earlier" but slack persists.
Bright-line check: "below...potential output" → MUST be < 50. Confirmed.
Magnitude: Economy is recovering ("expanding briskly") but "below potential for some time." This is the recovering-but-gap-persists range (31-40). The brisk expansion and improvement suggest the upper end. Score: 38.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential → score < 50. Correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE but improving.
Evidence: Unemployment rate at 5.5% and "edged down" in February. "Payroll employment appeared to be bottoming out." Initial claims "continued to drop." But the staff forecast notes "persistence of underutilized resources." Layoffs in manufacturing "slowed further."
Magnitude: UE at 5.5% = 31-45 range per anchors. Labor market is bottoming out, not tight. "Underutilized resources" persists. Score: 35.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Labor market is loose with 5.5% UE → score < 50. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees a MIXED picture — strong consumption, weak investment — but net improving.
Evidence: "Consumer spending remained strong in the early part of the year." "Residential construction had been very strong." BUT: "Business spending on durable equipment and software appeared to be turning upward after a marked moderation" from steep declines. "Nonresidential construction remained in a slump." Shipments of nondefense capital goods "were unexpectedly strong in January."
Net assessment: Consumer spending is strong (above trend), residential strong, but business investment is just beginning to turn from deep weakness, and nonresidential construction is in a slump. Staff forecast: "Elevated household spending and a shift from inventory liquidation to accumulation would provide significant impetus." Net position: above-trend consumption offset partly by still-weak investment. Score: 48.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Mixed picture nets to slightly below trend. Correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: Staff sees productivity as ABOVE trend.
Evidence: Staff forecast mentions "outlook for continued robust gains in productivity" and "higher projected growth in structural productivity." This is clearly positive productivity language.
Magnitude: "robust gains" and "continued" = above trend, 55-65 range. Score: 62.
Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Robust gains" → above midpoint. Correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**

Short-term rates increased slightly, longer-term rates rose more, but the overall policy environment is highly accommodative with fed funds at 1.75%. Speculative-grade bond yields "fell somewhat" on improved outlook. Equity prices "moved up sharply."
Bright-line check: "accommodative" → MUST be < 50. Confirmed.
Magnitude: "substantial monetary ease" is strong accommodative language. Rates did rise during intermeeting period, partially offsetting. But the overall characterization is clearly accommodative. Score: 30.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are accommodative/loose → below 50. Correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

Evidence: "Yields on longer-term Treasury instruments...rose" due to better-than-expected economic data. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.
Score: N/A — no specific discussion of Treasury market dysfunction. The yield movements described are normal price adjustment.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

Evidence: "Speculative-grade bond yields fell somewhat in reaction to the improved economic outlook and the perceived reduction of credit risk." "The debt of the domestic nonfinancial sectors was estimated to have increased at a relatively slow rate in January, reflecting weak demand for business debt financing."
Magnitude: Spreads are narrowing but from elevated levels (post-recession). Weak business debt demand suggests caution. The narrowing of spec-grade yields indicates improving but not yet fully normalized conditions. Score: 50 — roughly neutral, with improving direction from somewhat stressed levels.
Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress. Spreads are narrowing but business debt demand is weak — roughly neutral. Correct.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Direction: Staff notes equity prices rose but characterizes them as "still-depressed."
Evidence: "Most major indexes of equity prices moved up sharply on the bullish economic reports." But staff forecast: "still-depressed equity prices...would tend to hold down the pace of recovery."
Magnitude: "Still-depressed" despite recent gains indicates the staff views equities as significantly below fair/comfortable levels. Score: 62.
Polarity check: Higher = more equity market stress/concern. "Still-depressed" = elevated concern about equity valuations being low. Correct.
