# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2024-03-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. The staff content is clearly separated into three sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All scoring is based solely on these staff-attributable sections.

### Era Context
March 2024: The Fed is in a high-rate hold period following the 2022-2023 tightening cycle. Inflation has come down significantly from its 2022 peak but remains above the 2% target. The labor market has been gradually rebalancing but remains tight. GDP growth has been surprisingly resilient. Markets are pricing in eventual rate cuts.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as ABOVE neutral/target. Total PCE at 2.5%, core PCE at 2.9%, trimmed mean at 3.2%, core CPI at 3.8% — all above 2% target. However, all trending down from year-ago levels. "Consumer price inflation continued to decline, but recent progress was uneven." Month-over-month readings "firmed a bit." This is clearly above target but declining — not at peak levels. The "uneven" progress and the firming month-over-month readings add upside concern. Direction: above 50, in the 58-65 range. The anchor for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak is 82-88; this is well below that but still "above 2 percent" and "uneven." Score ~60.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees output as ABOVE neutral. "U.S. real GDP was expanding at a solid rate," "solid pace." The staff forecast says "policy effects... were still expected to hold output growth in 2024 below the staff's estimate of potential growth," meaning growth is below potential growth rate, but nothing says the level of output is below potential. The unemployment rate at 3.9% is low. Staff revised their projection upward. "Stronger than the January forecast." The economy is running solidly — no mention of slack, no mention of being below potential. The growth rate is positive and described as "solid." With UE at 3.9% and "solid" growth, the economy appears to be near or modestly above potential. Score ~53.

Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language regarding the level. The staff says growth will be "below potential growth" (referring to the growth rate, not the output level). This means the gap is closing (output converging toward potential from above) or staying near potential. With 3.9% UE and solid growth, slightly above 50 is appropriate.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor as TIGHT but gradually rebalancing. UE at 3.9% — below 4%. "Labor market conditions remained strong." "Labor demand and supply appeared to continue to move into better balance." Job openings and quits rates "far below their year-earlier levels." Wage growth declining. Payroll growth still solid. The UE of 3.9% anchors this — per the rubric, UE below 4% should be above 55. Despite rebalancing, the level is still tight. Score ~58.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending as MIXED, moderately positive. GDP "expanding at a solid rate" but "slower than its robust fourth-quarter pace." PCE "slowing" — expenditures declined in January, retail sales soft in February. Business fixed investment shows "some deceleration." Residential investment shows "modest pickup." Net: solid overall but decelerating with some soft spots. This is around trend — "solid" but with deceleration. Score ~52.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not discussed explicitly. N/A. The staff mentions higher population projections boosting potential, but that's not productivity per se.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees conditions as TIGHT/RESTRICTIVE. "Borrowing costs remained elevated." "Tight financial and credit conditions" (staff outlook section). Rates on mortgages, credit cards, auto loans all elevated or rising. However, credit "continued to be available to most businesses, households, and municipalities." Capital market financing "robust." So conditions are tight but not severely restrictive. The explicit "tight financial and credit conditions" language triggers the bright-line rule: must be > 50. Score ~60.

Bright-line check: "tight financial and credit conditions" -> score > 50. PASS.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields rose, but this was attributed to policy rate path expectations and inflation compensation — normal price adjustment. "Market-based measures of near-term interest rate uncertainty for shorter-term yields remained elevated by historical standards." No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, auction failures, or dysfunction. Per the rubric, yield movements from policy expectations = normal (score 45-55). The elevated uncertainty adds a touch. Score ~48.

Bright-line check: No actual dysfunction described. Yield moves are from policy expectations. Score in 36-50 range is appropriate. PASS.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff sees spreads as COMPRESSED/NARROW. "Yield spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were little changed, and those on speculative-grade bonds narrowed slightly." EME credit spreads also narrowed. Leveraged loan spreads narrowed. Credit is "available to most." Capital market financing "robust." This is below-normal spread territory — favorable conditions. Score ~32.

Bright-line check: "narrowed" -> score < 50. PASS.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff describes equities rising: "broad stock price indexes increased notably amid upbeat corporate earnings reports." Option-implied volatility "remained low by historical standards." No valuation concern expressed — just factual reporting of gains. Default to ~45-50 per rubric when no valuation commentary. Prices rising with low vol suggests no stress concern. Score ~40 (slightly below neutral — equities doing well, no concern expressed).

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff mentions: "The deterioration in CRE credit quality sparked investor concerns about the health of a few small U.S. and foreign banks." CRE delinquencies rising. Credit quality of small businesses deteriorated. Credit card delinquencies above pre-pandemic levels. However, large firm credit quality "remained solid." Banks' deposits edging up. No systemic concern — the stress is localized in CRE and a "few small" banks. Score ~45 — mixed with pockets of stress but no systemic concern.

Bright-line check: No "well-capitalized" or "sound" language, but also no systemic stress language. The concern is about "a few small" banks. Below 50 but near it. PASS.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"The broad dollar index were little changed on net." This is stable/neutral. Score ~48.

Bright-line check: "little changed" -> near 50. Score ~48. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58)**: Both above 50, close together. Labor slightly higher because UE is still quite low. Coherent.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52)**: Very close. Both modestly above neutral. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (32)**: These diverge significantly. Explanation: Financial conditions are tight due to high interest rates (policy transmission), but credit spreads themselves are narrow/compressed because risk appetite is strong and credit is available. This is consistent with a high-rate environment where the base rate is restrictive but corporate credit markets are functioning well with strong demand. This is a common late-tightening-cycle pattern.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs TREASURY_STRESS (48)**: FC tight but no Treasury dysfunction. Normal — Treasury stress is about dysfunction, not rate levels. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (48). The parent is higher, which makes sense — current inflation is elevated but expectations remain anchored. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE prices increased 2.5 percent over the 12 months ending in January, while core PCE price inflation... was 2.9 percent." "Consumer price inflation continued to decline, but recent progress was uneven." "The trimmed mean measure of 12-month PCE price inflation... was 3.2 percent." Month-over-month readings "had firmed a bit."
**Center of gravity**: Inflation is clearly above the 2% target across all measures (2.5%-3.8% depending on measure), trending down but with uneven recent progress and firming monthly readings.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because all price measures exceed 2% and recent monthly progress has been uneven/firming.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. real GDP was expanding at a solid rate in the first quarter." Staff forecast was "stronger than the January forecast." UE at 3.9%. Growth expected to be "below the staff's estimate of potential growth" (growth rate, not level).
**Center of gravity**: Economy is expanding solidly with low unemployment, suggesting output is at or modestly above potential, though growth is decelerating toward potential rate.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is modestly above neutral because growth is "solid" and UE is 3.9%, suggesting economy near or slightly above potential. No explicit "above/below potential" language for the level.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions remained strong." UE at 3.9%. "Labor demand and supply appeared to continue to move into better balance." Job openings and quits "far below their year-earlier levels." Wage growth declining.
**Center of gravity**: Labor market remains tight (UE 3.9%, strong conditions) but is gradually rebalancing with declining wage growth and normalizing turnover indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE at 3.9% is below 4%, conditions described as "strong," and the rubric anchors UE below 4% at above 55.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP was increasing at a solid pace in the first quarter, although more slowly than its robust fourth-quarter rate." "Incoming data pointed to some slowing in PCE growth." "Some deceleration in business fixed investment." But "modest pickup in residential investment."
**Center of gravity**: Spending is at a solid but decelerating pace — near trend with mixed signals across components.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at/slightly above neutral because growth is "solid" but decelerating, with offsetting signals across sectors.

"Interest rates on credit card offers increased." Mortgage rates rose. However, "credit continued to be available to most businesses, households, and municipalities."
**Center of gravity**: Conditions are explicitly described as tight with elevated borrowing costs across the board, though credit availability has not been severely impaired.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff explicitly describes "tight financial and credit conditions." Bright-line rule satisfied: "tight" -> > 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

"Most of the increase in short-term Treasury yields was attributed to a rise in near-term inflation compensation." "Market-based measures of near-term interest rate uncertainty... remained elevated."
**Center of gravity**: Treasury yields rose due to policy expectations and inflation compensation — normal price adjustment with no signs of market dysfunction. Elevated uncertainty but orderly functioning.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/near neutral because yield moves reflect normal policy repricing, not dysfunction. No liquidity or auction problems mentioned.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yield spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were little changed, and those on speculative-grade bonds narrowed slightly." "Credit continued to be available to most businesses, households, and municipalities." "Capital market financing was robust."
**Center of gravity**: Spreads are stable to narrowing, with robust credit availability and strong capital market issuance — clearly below-normal stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads "narrowed" and credit is "available" and "robust." Bright-line rule satisfied: "narrowed" -> < 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes increased notably amid upbeat corporate earnings reports." "The one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index... remained low by historical standards."
**Center of gravity**: Equities are rising with low volatility and no staff concern about valuations — slightly below-neutral stress.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is modestly below neutral (less stress) because equities are strong, earnings are upbeat, and volatility is low. No valuation concern expressed.

### BANKING_STRESS: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

"Credit quality for large firms and home mortgage borrowers remained solid but generally deteriorated further in sectors such as CRE and credit cards." Delinquency rates for nonfarm nonresidential loans rose to levels "last seen in late 2014."
**Center of gravity**: Pockets of stress in CRE and consumer credit (credit cards, small business), with concerns about a few small banks, but large firm credit quality solid and no systemic concern.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral because stress is localized ("a few small" banks, CRE sector) while the broader system remains functional.
