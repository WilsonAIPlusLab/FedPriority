# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

### Era Context
December 2010: The U.S. economy is roughly 18 months past the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The recovery is underway but uneven — unemployment remains near 10%, housing is deeply depressed, and the Fed has just launched QE2 ($600B in asset purchases). Core inflation is trending lower. European sovereign stress (Ireland, peripheral countries) is a significant backdrop.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key evidence: "core inflation continued to trend lower," "core PCE prices were unchanged for a second month," "broad-based deceleration in underlying inflation," and the staff forecast says "the persistent wide margin of economic slack in the projection was expected to sustain downward pressure on inflation." Labor cost pressures "still restrained." However, energy prices were rising and longer-run inflation expectations were "stable" — so not deflationary alarm, but clearly below target and decelerating. Direction: below midpoint. Score range: 25-30.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. "Economic activity was increasing at a moderate rate" but with "unemployment rate remained elevated," capacity utilization "remained significantly below its 1972-2009 average," housing "still quite depressed," and the outlook section references "persistent wide margin of economic slack." Bright-line: slack language = MUST be < 50. The recovery is underway (not deep recession), so not extreme low. Score range: 33-37.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees the labor market as very LOOSE. Unemployment at 9.8% (up from 9.6%), "labor force participation rate and the employment-population ratio remained depressed." Some improvement: private payrolls rising, initial claims trending lower, job openings improving. But the level is deeply slack. UE > 7% maps to 16-30 range per rubric. At 9.8%, this is severe. Score range: 20-25.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending as mixed, with improving consumer spending but weak housing and moderating business investment. Consumer spending "picked up" with "strong gain" in retail sales. E&S investment "appeared to be increasing, although the pace of spending seemed to have moderated." Nonresidential structures "appeared to be declining further." Housing "still quite depressed." Net: consumer spending improving but business investment mixed and housing drag. Below trend overall but with pockets of improvement. Score range: 38-42.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
The text briefly mentions: "the modest rise in hourly compensation in the nonfarm business sector was matched by a similar increase in productivity." This is minimal — productivity roughly matching compensation growth, suggesting trend-like performance. Score range: 45-50 with low confidence.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff describes a mixed picture. Credit terms easing per the Senior Credit Officer survey. "Net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations continued to be robust." Bond issuance "very heavy." But: "credit terms were still noticeably less favorable than in the past," mortgage rates "rose considerably," CRE financing "remained tight," C&I loan spreads "remained extremely wide." Residential mortgage delinquencies "extremely elevated." Some short-term funding stress from European concerns. Overall: conditions are accommodative in some areas (corporate bond issuance) but still tight in others (CRE, mortgage, small business). Net position: somewhat tight of neutral given the mixed legacy of crisis conditions persisting. Score range: 55-60.

Bright-line check: The text contains both "easing" language (dealer terms, corporate funding) and "tight" language (CRE, credit terms "less favorable than in the past," wide spreads). The net is modestly tight. Score > 50 is consistent.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields "increased significantly" but this was attributed to economic outlook revision, fiscal policy news, and reassessment of QE2 size — all normal price adjustment. However, the text also mentions "abrupt changes in investor positions, the effects of the approaching year-end on market liquidity, and hedging flows associated with investors' holdings of MBS as factors that may have amplified the rise in yields." This hints at some liquidity/positioning concerns amplifying moves but does not describe outright dysfunction. Per bright-line: yield movements from policy expectations are normal (score 45-55). The amplification language suggests modest concern above pure normal. Score range: 45-50.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
"Yields on investment-grade corporate bonds rose about in line with those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving risk spreads about unchanged; spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds moved down somewhat." C&I loan spreads "remained extremely wide." CRE financing "remained tight." Consumer credit stabilizing. Mixed: corporate bond spreads stable/narrowing but bank lending spreads still wide. Net level: somewhat elevated from the crisis overhang. Score range: 48-55.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
"Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased moderately." The equity risk premium "narrowed a bit, although it remained elevated relative to longer-run norms." Staff notes the equity risk premium is elevated — this means equities are priced with higher required returns (i.e., somewhat depressed valuations relative to norms, though improving). Score range: 52-56 (modestly depressed, but not severely).

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
"Bank profitability was little changed in the third quarter, remaining positive but well below pre-crisis levels." Revenues declining, supported by lower loan loss provisioning. "Banks continued to boost regulatory capital ratios." Delinquency rates on mortgages "extremely elevated." CRE delinquencies "increased further." C&I loans "edged up, ending a nearly two-year string of monthly declines" — signs of healing. Credit quality improving. Overall: improving but still stressed. Score range: 55-60.

Bright-line check: Staff does NOT say "well-capitalized" or "sound." They say profitability "well below pre-crisis levels," delinquencies "extremely elevated," capital ratios boosting (implying not yet adequate). This is above-neutral stress. Score > 50 is correct.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"On net, the dollar ended the intermeeting period up against most currencies, with particularly large gains against the euro." Also appreciated slightly against EM currencies. Bright-line: dollar appreciation = score > 50. Score range: 58-63.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: "core goods prices declined" — below neutral. Score ~30.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: "prices of non-energy services posted a small increase" — modest/low. Score ~40.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not specifically discussed as a component. House prices declining but that's asset prices not shelter CPI. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-run inflation expectations were stable," "survey measures of households' short- and long-term inflation expectations remained in the ranges that have prevailed since the spring of 2009," and "the ongoing stability in inflation expectations was anticipated to stem further disinflation." Anchored at or slightly below target. Score ~45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (22)**: Gap of 13 points. Both below 50, directionally aligned. The labor market was particularly weak relative to GDP recovery (typical of early recovery with productivity gains and slow hiring). Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Gap of 5 points. Both below 50. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (57) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (52)**: Gap of 5 points. Both slightly above 50. Coherent — both reflect legacy tightness with some improvement.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (57) vs TREASURY_STRESS (47)**: FC somewhat tight while Treasury stress near normal. This is expected in non-crisis periods. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (27) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (~30), Services (~40), Expectations (~45). The parent score of 27 is slightly below the sub-dimension average, but the parent reflects the "broad-based deceleration" and "trend lower" language which is more forceful than any single sub-component. Reasonable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

- Center-of-gravity: Staff describes a broad-based, ongoing deceleration in core inflation, running well below target with further downward pressure from slack. Energy prices rising but core measures declining.
- Direction: BELOW neutral — inflation pressure is low and falling, consistent with a score well under 50.

- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment near 10%, participation depressed, wages restrained — extremely loose labor market despite marginal improvement in payrolls and claims.
- Direction: BELOW neutral — deep slack in labor market. UE 9.8% maps to 16-30 range per anchor guidance.

- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending improving meaningfully but housing deeply depressed and business structures declining. E&S investment moderating. Net: below trend but improving.
- Direction: BELOW neutral — mixed with significant drags from housing and structures offsetting consumer strength.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS

- Center-of-gravity: Corporate bond spreads stable to narrowing, but bank lending spreads still wide and CRE tight. Level is near neutral with pockets of tightness.
- Direction: AT/slightly ABOVE neutral — mixed spread picture with persistent tightness in some segments.
