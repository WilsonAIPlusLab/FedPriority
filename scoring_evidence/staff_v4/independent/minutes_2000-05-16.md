### Document Type

- Paragraphs on employment (line 25), industrial production (line 28), consumer spending (line 31), housing (line 34), business fixed investment (line 37), inventories (line 40), trade (line 43), prices and labor costs (line 46) — all use passive/objective voice characteristic of staff review

### Era Context
May 2000 — the late-1990s/2000 boom. The dot-com bubble had started deflating (NASDAQ peaked March 2000). The economy was running hot with unemployment at 3.9%, and the Fed was in a tightening cycle (raising to 6.5% at this meeting with a 50bp hike). Inflation pressures were building.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

Staff language: "Recent information suggested that price inflation might be picking up slightly and only partly as a direct result of increases in energy prices." Core CPI "was up slightly in the March-April period compared with other recent months." ECI "registered a larger advance in the first quarter than in previous quarters" with acceleration in compensation over the year. Staff forecast: "Core price inflation was projected to rise noticeably over the forecast horizon."

Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as ABOVE neutral — picking up slightly, with labor cost acceleration, and projected to rise further. The language "picking up slightly" and "rise noticeably over the forecast horizon" suggests moderate above-midpoint. Not extreme, not "surging," but clearly above target and accelerating.

Score direction: Above 50. "Picking up slightly" = modest. "Rise noticeably" for forecast = somewhat stronger. Overall ~58-62.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff language: "economic growth had remained rapid through early spring," "industrial production accelerated in April," "capacity utilization in April was about equal to its long-term average." Staff forecast: "expansion would gradually moderate from its currently elevated pace to a rate around, or perhaps a little below, the growth of the economy's estimated potential."

The phrase "currently elevated pace" and "expansion of domestic final demand increasingly would be held back" implies the economy is running above potential now. "Aggregate demand was expanding more rapidly than potential supply" is from participant section but the staff forecast itself says growth is above potential pace.

Bright-line check: The staff language implies demand growing faster than potential supply — economy above potential. Score MUST be > 50.

Score direction: Above 50. "Rapid" growth, "elevated pace," demand exceeding supply growth, capacity utilization at long-term average. This is clearly above potential but not extreme overheating language. ~58-63.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff language: "Labor markets continued to be very tight." "The civilian unemployment rate dropped in April to 3.9 percent, a thirty-year low." "Employment surged in March and April." "Gains in private employment were very large."

Direction: VERY tight. UE at 3.9% (thirty-year low). The rubric says UE below 4% should be above 55. "Very tight" + 3.9% UE + "thirty-year low" = strongly above neutral. The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75.

Score: ~70. "Very tight," thirty-year low UE, surging employment.

BUT "underlying trend in spending remained strong." Business fixed investment "up sharply in the first quarter." Equipment and software surged. Orders pointed to further advances. Nonresidential structures rose rapidly. Housing at "elevated level."

Direction: Above trend. The deceleration in consumer spending is noted but from a very rapid pace, and the underlying trend is "strong." Business investment is surging. Housing elevated. Net: clearly above trend.

Score: ~62-66. Very strong investment, strong but slightly decelerating consumption.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**

Direction: Above trend implied by "increasingly rapid growth of potential supply" and business efforts to "improve the productivity of their operations." But this is thin and partly from participant section.

Score: ~58, Low-Moderate confidence. Limited direct staff discussion.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**

"Bond yields and other financial conditions had firmed to some extent recently" — but this is from participant section. From staff review: interest rates moved up, stock prices declined significantly. M2/M3 expanding above range upper ends (loose monetary aggregates counterbalancing).

Direction: Conditions have tightened — rates up appreciably, stocks declined. The tightening is from policy transmission. Higher = tighter. Score should be above 50.

Bright-line: Rising rates, tightening conditions → above 50.

Score: ~58. Rates up appreciably, stocks down, but no credit crisis language.

I'll score it as the discussion of portfolio management in declining debt environment is relevant but not stress. Score: 42 with moderate uncertainty.

Direction: Slight widening implied by differential rate moves, but credit still available. Modestly above neutral.

Score: ~52-55. Slight widening but no stress language.

The staff notes a valuation reassessment — investors revising down valuations of speculative tech stocks. This implies staff viewed prior valuations as stretched. The decline is "significant." Anchor for 2000 dot-com: 15-25.

But this is the beginning of the correction, not the bottom. Staff notes prices "began to level out" after the tumble. The language suggests overvaluation concerns being corrected.

Score: ~30-35. Valuations were stretched (speculative tech), now declining. Staff implicitly acknowledges prior overvaluation being corrected. Not full bubble-peak language (that was earlier) but still elevated valuation concern.
