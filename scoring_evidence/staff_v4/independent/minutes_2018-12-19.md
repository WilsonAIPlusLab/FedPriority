# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Late 2018. The U.S. economy is in a mature expansion with unemployment at 3.7%, GDP growth strong, and inflation near target. Financial markets are experiencing a notable selloff driven by trade tensions, slowing global growth, and concerns about the sustainability of corporate earnings. This is the meeting where the Fed raised rates for the fourth time in 2018 amid growing market anxiety.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff reports PCE inflation at 2% and core PCE at 1.8%. Staff forecasts core moving to 2% in 2019 and staying there. Inflation expectations "little changed on balance." Foreign inflation fell. This is essentially at-target, with core slightly below. Direction: at midpoint. Score range: 46-50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says "real GDP was forecast to rise at a rate above the staff's estimate of potential output growth." Also: "economy that was projected to move further above its potential." GDP growth is "strong." The staff explicitly identifies the economy as above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" -> MUST be > 50. Direction: above midpoint. Score range: 53-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 3.7% ("very low level"), projected to decline further below the natural rate. Job gains "strong." Job openings and quits at "high levels." Initial claims "close to historically low levels." "Labor market conditions already tight." With UE at 3.7%, the critical gotcha says score should be above 55. Direction: well above midpoint. Score range: 63-68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending continued to increase at a strong pace." Real PCE growth "brisk." Nominal retail sales "rose considerably." Business equipment spending "picking up solidly." However, residential investment "appeared to be declining further." Net position: strong consumption and business investment partially offset by weak residential. Direction: above midpoint. Score range: 55-60.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Financing conditions for businesses and households tightened a bit but generally remained supportive of economic growth." Corporate bond spreads widened to "near the middle of their historical distribution." CRE conditions "remained accommodative." Consumer credit "remained accommodative." Residential mortgage "remained accommodative." The net assessment is conditions tightened somewhat but are still broadly supportive/accommodative. Bright-line: "supportive"/"accommodative" -> MUST be < 50. But there's a tightening direction. This puts it in the upper-accommodative range. The 2017-19 neutral anchor is 46-54. With tightening noted but still accommodative, score around 48-52. Direction: near midpoint, slightly below. Score: 49.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Short-term funding markets functioned smoothly." Treasury yields fell due to risk sentiment — this is price adjustment, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. Direction: normal functioning. Score range: 38-42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "widened to near the middle of their historical distribution." CMBS spreads "widened slightly" but "remained near post-crisis lows." Consumer ABS "slightly higher spreads." The widening is notable but spreads are described as near historical midpoints. Direction: near midpoint, slightly above. Score range: 50-55.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Significant drop in U.S. equity prices." Declines in technology and retail sectors. VIX increased. Staff describes price declines driven by trade tensions and earnings concerns. No explicit valuation commentary (not calling them distressed or overvalued). Per the gotcha: price decline without valuation commentary defaults to 50-55. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score range: 55-60.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not specifically discussed in terms of bank health, capital, or stress. CRE loan growth "remained strong at banks" and credit card growth only "edged a bit lower." These suggest normal banking operations but no direct assessment of banking system health. Direction: not enough to score confidently. Score as N/A — no explicit banking system assessment.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated modestly." Bright-line: appreciation -> score > 50. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score range: 53-57.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not broken out separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment declining, mortgage rates rising, home sales mixed. But this is about housing activity, not housing inflation specifically. No shelter/rent inflation data cited. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan, SPF, Primary Dealers all "little changed." ### Polarity checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Staff says above potential. Score > 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Staff says "supportive" but "tightened a bit." Score ~49. Accommodative language keeps it < 50. Correct per bright-line.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Spreads widened to historical middle. Score ~52. Correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Markets functioning smoothly. Score ~40. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar appreciated. Score > 50. Correct.

1. OUTPUT_GAP (55) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Labor tighter than output gap by ~10 points. Reasonable — labor market can be tighter than the output gap measure when unemployment is at multi-decade lows while GDP growth is strong but not extreme.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (55) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (57): Coherent, within 2 points.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (49) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (52): Coherent, within 3 points. Both near neutral.

5. INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-dimensions: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (~48). Parent at 48. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer prices, as measured by the PCE price index, increased 2 percent over the 12 months ending in October. Core PCE price inflation...was 1.8 percent over that same period." Staff forecast: "both total and core PCE price inflation to be just a touch below 2 percent in 2018" with core "forecast to move up to 2 percent in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Headline at target, core slightly below, forecast to reach target — essentially neutral inflation pressure with a hair below target on core.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is at neutral because PCE is at 2% and core is 1.8%, very close to the 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "real GDP was forecast to rise at a rate above the staff's estimate of potential output growth"; "economy that was projected to move further above its potential"; GDP growth described as "strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly identifies the economy as operating above potential and expects it to move further above, placing this modestly above the midpoint.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says the economy is above potential. Bright-line rule satisfied (above potential -> > 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The national unemployment rate remained at a very low level of 3.7 percent"; "labor market conditions already tight"; "The unemployment rate was projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 3.7% (well below NAIRU), strong job gains, high openings and quits, claims at historic lows — a very tight labor market.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE at 3.7% with staff explicitly calling conditions "tight" and below natural rate.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Household spending continued to increase at a strong pace"; "Real PCE growth was brisk"; "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property looked to be picking up solidly." But: "Real residential investment appeared to be declining further in the fourth quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending and solid business investment are partially offset by declining residential investment, netting to an above-trend reading.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because consumer spending is "strong"/"brisk" and business investment is "picking up solidly," despite residential weakness.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for businesses and households tightened a bit but generally remained supportive of economic growth"; "Financing conditions in the residential mortgage market also remained accommodative"; corporate bond spreads widened to "near the middle of their historical distribution."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions tightened modestly from an accommodative starting point but remain broadly supportive — essentially at neutral with a slight lean toward still-accommodative.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is at/just below neutral because staff says conditions "tightened a bit" but remain "supportive" and "accommodative." Bright-line: "supportive"/"accommodative" -> < 50. Score of 49 satisfies this.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Short-term funding markets functioned smoothly over the intermeeting period"; Treasury yield declines attributed to risk sentiment and growth concerns (normal price adjustment), not market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: No signs of dysfunction; yields moved on fundamental reassessment. Funding markets smooth.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are described as functioning smoothly with no dysfunction indicators.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on nonfinancial corporate bonds widened to near the middle of their historical distribution"; "CMBS spreads widened slightly over the intermeeting period but remained near post-crisis lows"; "corporate credit spreads widened, consistent with the selloff in equities."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened from compressed levels to near historical midpoints — roughly at the normal/neutral level.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is at/slightly above neutral because spreads are described as near the middle of their historical distribution after widening.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "a significant drop in U.S. equity prices"; "The declines were largest in the technology and retail sectors"; "One-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index—the VIX—increased over the period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Significant equity price decline and rising volatility, but no staff commentary on valuation levels (not described as distressed or undervalued). Per scoring rules, price decline without valuation commentary scores 50-55, pushed slightly higher by "significant" language.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is modestly above neutral because equities experienced a "significant drop" with elevated volatility, indicating modest stress.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No direct discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or bank stress. Brief mentions of bank lending activity (CRE loan growth strong, credit card growth edging down) are not assessments of banking system soundness.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 55 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated modestly; although declines in U.S. yields weighed on the dollar, deteriorating global risk sentiment provided support."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest dollar appreciation driven by risk-off dynamics, partially offset by falling yields.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "appreciated modestly." Bright-line rule satisfied (appreciation -> > 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
No separate goods inflation discussion.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
No separate services inflation discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
No shelter/rent inflation data cited (housing activity discussed but not housing inflation).

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance"; "Measures of inflation compensation derived from TIPS also decreased notably over the period along with the declines in oil prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations well-anchored and stable; market-based compensation declined but attributed to oil prices. Slightly below neutral due to the TIPS decline.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is at/slightly below neutral because expectations are "little changed" (anchored) but market-based measures declined.
