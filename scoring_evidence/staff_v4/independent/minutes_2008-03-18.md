**Era context**: March 2008, early stages of the Global Financial Crisis. Bear Stearns was rescued days before this meeting. Housing contraction deepening, financial markets severely strained, economy decelerating rapidly but not yet in full-blown recession recognition. Oil and commodity prices surging.

### Directional Signals for Each Dimension

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE midpoint. Staff reports: "headline and core inflation as measured by the CPI dropped noticeably in February" but "prices of oil and other commodities rose sharply" in early March. The staff forecast raised core PCE inflation for first half of 2008 due to "elevated readings in recent months." Headline PCE was expected to "substantially exceed core PCE" due to energy prices. This is a mixed picture — recent CPI moderated but the staff revised UP their forecast and energy prices were surging. Net: modestly elevated, around 58-62.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy BELOW potential. Staff projection showed "a contraction of real GDP in the first half of 2008." The staff "substantially revised down its projection for the pace of real GDP throughout 2008." Resource utilization was "anticipated to follow a lower trajectory." The factory utilization rate "edged down to a level noticeably below its recent high." Bright-line check: contraction = below potential, must be < 50. Score around 30-35.

Private payroll employment declined for three consecutive months, with "widespread" losses. Unemployment at 4.8%, up from 4.5% a year earlier. This is loosening but UE at 4.8% is still not extreme. Score around 38-42.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending BELOW trend. "Real consumer spending appeared to have stalled." Real outlays for consumer goods "estimated to have declined." Equipment spending rose at a "sluggish rate" in Q4, and "the overall outlook for capital spending in the first quarter was weak." Nonresidential structures posted "an outright decline in January." Housing contraction intensified. Score around 28-33.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed by staff. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as TIGHT. "Conditions in some short-term funding markets worsened." Interbank spreads widened. Repo markets became "more difficult." "Tight financing conditions for jumbo and nonprime mortgages." Staff forecast noted "conditions in a broad range of debt markets became more restrictive." Multiple Fed emergency facilities created. Bear Stearns rescued. Bright-line: "tight"/"restrictive"/"strained" = must be > 50. Score around 78-82.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes yield movements but also significant dysfunction in related markets. Muni bond market dislocations with "ratios of municipal bond yields to those on comparable-maturity Treasuries climbed to historically high levels." Repo markets backed by MBS became difficult. The Fed created TSLF specifically to lend Treasuries. "Liquidity concerns" are mentioned multiple times. While not direct Treasury market breakdown, there are clear liquidity/functioning issues in closely related markets. Score around 62-67.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as significantly WIDE. "Spreads relative to Treasuries to the highest levels since 2002 or even earlier." Interbank spreads widened. Commercial paper spreads widened. Mortgage spreads "jumped." CDS premiums for GSEs "increased to record highs." Bright-line: "widened"/"elevated" = must be > 50. Score around 75-80.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff reports "Broad stock price indexes fell further over the intermeeting period on negative economic news as well as concerns about the outlook for many financial institutions." Also, "the downturn in equity prices since December further reduced household wealth." This is factual price decline reporting without explicit valuation commentary. Score around 62-66.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: Staff reports significant stress. "Many financial institutions experiencing significant balance sheet pressures." Concerns about "health of financial guarantors" caused market dislocations. Bear Stearns required emergency rescue. Bank credit decelerated. Multiple emergency lending facilities created. Bright-line: stressed/troubled = must be > 50. Score around 72-78.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff reports: "The trade-weighted foreign exchange value of the dollar against major currencies declined notably." Dollar depreciation also mentioned in context of boosting exports and raising import prices. Bright-line: depreciation = must be < 50. Score around 22-28.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The forecast for core PCE price inflation over the first half of 2008 was raised in response to elevated readings in recent months."
- "headline PCE price inflation was expected to substantially exceed core PCE price inflation in 2008" due to energy price increases.
- "Both headline and core inflation as measured by the CPI dropped noticeably in February" — but this was temporary; the staff raised their forecast overall.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff saw inflation as modestly elevated, with the forecast revised upward for near-term core and headline inflation driven by energy and commodities, though expected to recede in 2009.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because the staff raised their inflation forecast and described recent readings as "elevated," with energy prices adding significant upward pressure.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The staff projection showed a contraction of real GDP in the first half of 2008 followed by a slow rise in the second half."
- "The staff substantially revised down its projection for the pace of real GDP throughout 2008."
- "Resource utilization was anticipated to follow a lower trajectory than in the previous forecast."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff projected GDP contraction in H1 2008 with significantly downgraded growth forecasts and declining resource utilization, placing the economy well below potential.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff projects contraction and declining resource utilization. Bright-line satisfied (contraction = below potential → < 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Labor demand softened markedly in recent months. The decline in private payroll employment that began last December steepened through February."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market was clearly loosening with three consecutive months of payroll declines and rising unemployment, but the level (4.8% UE) was not yet severely slack.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because payroll employment was declining and unemployment rising, though still at a moderate level.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending stalled/declined, business investment was sluggish with a weak outlook, and housing construction contracted further — a broad-based weakness in spending and investment.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is well below neutral because staff described stalling consumer spending, weak capital spending outlook, and deepening housing contraction.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 80 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions were severely tight across multiple markets — interbank, repo, corporate credit, mortgages — prompting unprecedented Fed emergency interventions including the Bear Stearns rescue.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well above neutral because staff described worsening, tight, and restrictive credit conditions across virtually all markets. Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 65 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Concerns over the health of financial guarantors caused dislocations in the markets for municipal securities, and the ratios of municipal bond yields to those on comparable-maturity Treasuries climbed to historically high levels."
- The Fed created the TSLF to "lend Treasury securities to primary dealers for longer terms" based on "a broader range of collateral" — indicating Treasury collateral was in high demand and functioning was impaired.
- "Liquidity concerns reemerged subsequently."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: While Treasury yields moved in response to policy expectations (normal), the broader fixed-income market showed significant dislocations, liquidity impairment, and the Fed created specific facilities to address Treasury market functioning issues.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because staff described actual market dislocations, liquidity problems, and the Fed created the TSLF specifically to address functioning in the Treasury lending market.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit spreads were severely wide across corporate bonds, mortgages, and CDS markets, reaching multi-year or record highs in several categories.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well above neutral because spreads were at their widest levels in years across multiple credit markets. Bright-line satisfied.
