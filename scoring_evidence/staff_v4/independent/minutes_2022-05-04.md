# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-05-04

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
May 2022 — the U.S. is experiencing the highest inflation in four decades. The Fed has just begun its hiking cycle (March 2022 was the first hike). The labor market is extremely tight with unemployment at 3.6%. Russia's invasion of Ukraine (Feb 2022) is adding to commodity price pressures. GDP declined in Q1 2022 on net exports and inventories, but private domestic final demand remained strong. This is a period of significant overheating with the Fed pivoting to aggressive tightening.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as far above midpoint. Total PCE inflation at 6.6% (12-month), core at 5.2%, trimmed mean at 3.7%. "Consumer prices continued to rise rapidly." The ECI rose 4.8% — "largest 12-month increase since 1990." The staff's common inflation expectations index "moved up in the first quarter and was at the upper end of the range of values seen since 2005." The staff revised inflation projections up. Risks to inflation "skewed to the upside." This is clearly in the 76-90 range. The anchor for 2022 Q2 is 82-88.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output well above potential. Despite Q1 GDP decline, the staff explicitly states "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential over the projection period." The Q1 decline was attributed to volatile categories (inventories, net exports), while "continued strength in private domestic final demand, the labor market, and industrial production" provided a more accurate picture. Bright-line rule: "well above potential" -> must be > 50. Score should be 59-65 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very tight. Unemployment at 3.6%, "labor market conditions tightened further in March," ECI at 4.8% (largest since 1990), job openings rate "remained elevated," and the outlook states "labor market conditions were expected to remain very tight." With UE at 3.6% and "very tight" language, this aligns with the 66-80 anchor for 2022.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as solid/above trend. "Both real PCE and residential investment increased in the first quarter at rates similar to those seen in the fourth quarter of 2021." "Business fixed investment growth picked up sharply in the first quarter, with spending on equipment and intellectual property products posting a large increase." The staff expected GDP to "rebound in the second quarter and advance at a solid pace over the remainder of the year." Mixed by Q1 GDP decline, but staff attributes that to inventories and net exports, not demand weakness. Score in the 55-62 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions tightening but credit still widely available. "Borrowing costs increased in many sectors and were at or somewhat above pre-pandemic levels." Yet "credit remained widely available" and "credit quality continued to be strong overall." SLOOS showed "continued easing of lending terms." Mortgage rates increased substantially to levels not seen since 2010. This is a mixed picture — costs rising but availability still good. Conditions are somewhat tighter than neutral. Score 55-60.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "moved substantially higher" and "increased significantly on net." However, this is from policy expectations ("more aggressive tightening of monetary policy was likely"), NOT from market dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mentions of liquidity problems, auction failures, or settlement issues. Per the bright-line rule, rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 45-55). The financial system "had been resilient." Score around 45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "narrowed moderately earlier in the intermeeting period and then widened, ending the period only slightly narrower, on net, and below the median of their historical distribution." Municipal bond spreads "up modestly and stood at about the 90th percentile of their historical distribution." CP spreads narrowed. Investment-grade issuance "rebounded sharply." Credit "remained widely available." Spreads are generally contained with some mixed signals — corporate below median, muni elevated. Score around 48-53.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad domestic equity price indexes declined on net." VIX "remained elevated." The staff noted "valuations of many assets remained elevated" and "CRE valuations appeared somewhat elevated." For equities specifically, the decline was driven by rising rates and disappointing earnings. Staff doesn't describe equities as distressed but prices are falling. The "valuations remained elevated" language despite declines suggests a score around 40-45 (still elevated valuations, which means less equity stress).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff explicitly states "the banking sector continued to be well capitalized with strong liquidity." Credit quality of loans is strong, delinquency and default rates "both remained low." Household credit quality "remained strong." Leverage at NBFIs is "elevated" and NBFI reliance on bank credit lines is flagged as a moderate vulnerability. But core banking system is healthy. Bright-line: "well-capitalized" -> must be < 50. Score around 30-35.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The U.S. dollar generally strengthened, with a more pronounced dollar appreciation against AFE currencies." "The dollar appreciated significantly against the Chinese renminbi." Bright-line: appreciation -> must be > 50. Score 62-68. The 2022 Q3 anchor is 75-85; this is May 2022, somewhat earlier, with clear strengthening but not yet at the extreme Q3 levels.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out explicitly as a separate category, but the text notes "surging energy and food prices" globally, and large imports of consumer goods/capital goods. Supply chain disruptions and production constraints are mentioned. Total PCE at 6.6% is driven heavily by goods. Indirect evidence suggests very high goods inflation. Score ~78.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: The staff notes "wage increases would put more upward pressure on services prices than previously assumed." Core PCE at 5.2% includes services. Foreign inflation showed "some broadening of price pressures to core goods and services." Score ~68.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Residential house prices had risen rapidly." The staff continued to see differences from the pre-2008 boom but acknowledged rapid price increases. CRE valuations "somewhat elevated." Score ~70.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: The staff's common inflation expectations index "moved up in the first quarter and was at the upper end of the range of values seen since 2005." This is notable — at the upper end since 2005 = significantly above normal. Anchor for 2022 "showing signs of moving up" is 60-70. Score ~67.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**Score: 83 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.6 percent over the 12 months ending in March, and core PCE price inflation...was 5.2 percent." "Consumer prices continued to rise rapidly." "The employment cost index...rose 4.8 percent...the largest 12-month increase since 1990." Staff revised inflation projection upward; risks "skewed to the upside."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running at multi-decade highs across headline, core, and wage measures, with the staff revising its forecast up and seeing upside risks.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far above neutral because PCE is more than 3x the 2% target with broad-based pressures and rising wages.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential over the projection period." The staff viewed Q1 GDP decline as driven by volatile categories and "continued strength in private domestic final demand, the labor market, and industrial production as providing a more accurate picture."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Despite the Q1 GDP decline, staff explicitly judges the economy is operating well above potential and expects it to remain so.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff states GDP is "well above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 3.6 percent." "Labor market conditions tightened further in March." The ECI "rose 4.8 percent...the largest 12-month increase since 1990." "Labor market conditions were expected to remain very tight."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 3.6% with the largest wage growth in 30+ years and the staff using "very tight" language places this firmly in the high-tightness zone.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE is 3.6% (well below NAIRU) with record wage growth and explicit "very tight" characterization.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

"Business fixed investment growth picked up sharply in the first quarter, with spending on equipment and intellectual property products posting a large increase." - **Center-of-gravity**: Private domestic final demand was strong and accelerating despite the headline GDP decline, with broad-based gains across consumption and business investment.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because all major private spending categories showed strength.

- **Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed.

"Short-term funding markets were stable." "The financial system...had been resilient."

- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because yield movements reflect orderly policy repricing, not dysfunction.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Corporate bond spreads ended "only slightly narrower, on net, and below the median of their historical distribution." Municipal bond spreads "stood at about the 90th percentile of their historical distribution." "Credit remained widely available for most types of borrowers."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Corporate spreads are below median (favorable) while municipal spreads are elevated; credit availability is broad. Net position is approximately neutral.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is at neutral because the mixed signals (favorable corporate, elevated muni) net to roughly balanced conditions.

The staff flagged elevated NBFI leverage and reliance on bank credit lines as a moderate vulnerability.
- **Center-of-gravity**: The banking system is healthy and well-capitalized with strong credit quality, though NBFI-related vulnerabilities represent a moderate concern at the margin.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because staff describes banks as "well capitalized with strong liquidity." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
