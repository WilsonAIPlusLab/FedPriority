# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2015-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary), **post-2005 segmented** format with clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
December 2015 — the meeting at which the FOMC raised the federal funds rate for the first time since the GFC. The economy had been in a slow recovery; unemployment had fallen to 5.0%. Inflation remained well below 2%, weighed down by falling energy prices and dollar appreciation. Financial conditions were generally accommodative but high-yield bond markets were experiencing notable stress.

### Dimensional Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly below midpoint. PCE inflation was only 0.25% over 12 months; core PCE was 1.25%. Staff attributes this to energy price declines and non-energy import price declines. Staff projects inflation rising "gradually" to 2% by end of 2018 — but current readings are very low. Survey-based expectations showed "some hints of having edged slightly lower." This is solidly in the 26-40 range ("below target; contained; running below 2 percent"). Direction: below neutral. Score ~30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says "real GDP was increasing at a moderate pace." Staff projects GDP would "expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018," implying the economy is currently still below potential but closing the gap. The unemployment rate was "expected to decline gradually and to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" — meaning it hasn't gotten there yet. Risks to GDP were "tilted somewhat to the downside." The language suggests the economy is approaching but not yet at potential. Bright-line check: no explicit "above potential" language for the current period — the "faster than potential" language refers to the growth rate, not the level. The economy is approaching potential from below. Score ~45.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.0%, which fell 3/4 percentage point over the prior year. Payroll employment "expanded at a faster monthly rate." Part-time for economic reasons was flat but had "declined considerably" over the prior year. Job openings, hires, quits were "little changed." Compensation signals mixed: business sector compensation up 3.5% but ECI only 2%. Average hourly earnings up 2.25%. At 5.0% unemployment, the staff's longer-run natural rate estimate is likely around 4.9-5.0%, so we're approximately at NAIRU. The rubric says UE ~4.5-5% maps to 46-52 ("near NAIRU"). Wage signals are moderate but not screaming tightness. Score ~50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to be rising at a solid rate." Retail sales increased, auto sales "remained high," real disposable income showed "strong growth," consumer sentiment "improved a little." Housing was "mixed" — single-family starts lower, permits up, multi-family declined. Business equipment spending had been "solid" in Q3 but "looked to be slowing somewhat in Q4." Net exports were a "drag." Government purchases "about flat." The net picture: solid consumer spending offset by slowing business investment and drag from net exports. "Solid" consumer spending with "slowing" business investment nets to roughly at-trend. Score ~50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: This is complex. Investment-grade bond market was fine — spreads "little changed," issuance "remained solid." Consumer credit "remained accommodative for most borrowers." CRE financing conditions "tightened somewhat" but were "quite accommodative overall." Mortgage rates "continued to be quite low by historical standards" though credit was "tight for borrowers with low credit scores." However, there was notable stress in high-yield: spreads "widened significantly," a mutual fund closed, high-yield issuance slowed. Equity prices declined. The VIX climbed. Overall: conditions were broadly accommodative with pockets of tightening in high-yield. The staff describes mostly "accommodative" conditions. Bright-line: "accommodative" → must be < 50. But there are emerging stress signals. Score ~40.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose (2-year up 40bp, 10-year up 25bp) but this was driven by policy expectations (anticipated rate hike). No language about market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. Yield movements from policy expectations = normal market functioning. Score ~38. N/A could apply, but the Treasury market is discussed in terms of yield movements. I'll score it as normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Significant bifurcation. Investment-grade spreads "little changed." But high-yield spreads "widened significantly, on net, particularly for bonds rated triple-C or below." A mutual fund "suspended investor redemptions and closed." High-yield issuance slowed. CRE spreads "widened further." This is clearly above neutral for credit stress, though investment-grade remains calm. The high-yield stress is notable. Bright-line: "widened" → must be > 50. Score ~58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad measures of U.S. equity prices declined, with a steep selloff in energy-sector stocks." The VIX "climbed." But there's no valuation language — no "overvalued," "undervalued," "distressed." Just price movements. Per the rubric, price moves without valuation commentary → default 45-55. "Equities declined" alone → 50-55. Score ~53.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banking system is not specifically discussed as a system. C&I loan growth was "strong." There's no language about bank health, capital, or stress. The discussion is about credit markets, not banks per se. The mention of strong loan growth at large banks suggests health rather than stress, but it's indirect. Score: N/A — banking system health not specifically discussed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the dollar rose appreciably." Also: "the stronger dollar appeared to weigh on earnings growth across many sectors" and "declines in the prices of non-energy imported goods" (dollar effect). Staff notes risk that "the foreign exchange value of the dollar could rise substantially further." Bright-line: dollar appreciation → must be > 50. "Rose appreciably" is clear strengthening language. Score ~65.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Headline PCE at 0.25% and core at 1.25% represent clearly below-target inflation with persistent drags from energy and import prices.
