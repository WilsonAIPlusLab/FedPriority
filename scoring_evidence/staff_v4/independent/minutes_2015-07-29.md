# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2015-07-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Mid-2015. The economy is in a mature recovery from the GFC, with unemployment falling toward estimated natural rate. Inflation is running below the 2% target, held down by prior energy price declines and dollar appreciation. The Fed is approaching its first rate hike (which occurred in December 2015). Financial conditions are broadly accommodative. Greek crisis and Chinese equity volatility are notable international developments but spillovers to U.S. are limited.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Core PCE at 1.25% over 12 months, well below 2% target. Total PCE "only edged up." Restrained by energy price declines and non-energy import price decreases. Staff projects inflation "below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over 2016 and 2017." This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 26-40 range ("below target; contained; running below 2 percent"). Core PCE at 1.25% is meaningfully below target. Score ~32.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says GDP "rose moderately in the second quarter after edging down in the first quarter." The staff outlook says GDP is "expected to increase faster in the second half" and to "expand more rapidly than potential output in 2016 and 2017," implying the economy is currently BELOW potential but closing. The staff references "projected narrowing of the output gap" and "slack in labor and product markets projected to have waned" (future tense, implying slack exists now). Bright-line: slack remains --> score MUST be < 50. Score ~42 (approaching potential but gap persists).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.3%, "its lowest reading so far this year." Labor market "continued to improve." Job openings at "a high level." "Underutilization of labor resources had diminished since early this year" -- but "diminished" implies some slack remains. Participation rate and employment-to-population ratio "moved down." The staff lowered its estimate of the natural rate. UE at 5.3% is approaching NAIRU territory (estimated 4.5-5.0%). Using the anchor guidance: 46-52 for "near NAIRU." At 5.3%, still slightly above, with mixed signals from participation. Score ~44 (below full employment but approaching).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to have risen at a solid pace." Light vehicle sales "remained robust." Consumer sentiment near highest since before recession. Real disposable income rose. But business equipment spending rose at only a "modest rate." Nonresidential structures ex-drilling rose "solidly," but drilling/mining structures "fell sharply." Housing sector "improved somewhat" but "remained slow." Net: solid consumption offset by mixed investment. Score ~50 (at trend, "solid" consumption meets "modest" investment).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff "trimmed" projected rates of productivity gains and potential output growth over the medium term. This is a downward revision to an already-modest outlook. Score ~35 (modestly below trend, consistent with "sluggish" productivity that characterized this era).

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Financial conditions were affected by developments abroad over the intermeeting period but were little changed on balance." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms continued to be accommodative." CRE financing "remained broadly available." Residential mortgage conditions "stayed accommodative for many consumers." This is clearly accommodative/loose. Bright-line: "accommodative" --> score MUST be < 50. Score ~35 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields fell "somewhat on net." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. Market functioning appears normal. Score ~35 (normal, quiet market conditions for this era).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened somewhat over the period." But corporate bond issuance "remained strong," leveraged loan issuance "picked up noticeably," loan spreads tightened vs beginning of year. Municipal bond spreads "changed little." Net: generally favorable with modest widening. Bright-line check: conditions are still generally favorable, so < 50. Score ~40 (somewhat below normal, generally favorable with modest widening).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes were little changed." Earnings roughly unchanged or slightly higher. Staff notes "somewhat elevated valuations" in the financial stability section. This suggests mild overvaluation concern. Score ~35 (modestly above fair value, "somewhat elevated" language in stability report).

**BANKING_STRESS**: "The strong capital position of the U.S. banking sector" and "low to moderate use of leverage." Financial stresses from Greece/China had "limited" effect on largest U.S. firms reflecting "relatively strong financial positions." But also "rising debt burdens for riskier businesses" and "loosening lending standards" noted as concerns. Bright-line: "strong capital position" --> MUST be < 50. Score ~30 (healthy with some minor concerns about loosening standards).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the U.S. dollar increased during the intermeeting period against the currencies of major U.S. trading partners." Also "the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose modestly." Staff also notes "a slightly stronger forecast for the exchange value of the dollar" impacting the outlook, and "continuing effects of earlier increases in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" weighing on manufacturing. Dollar clearly appreciating. Bright-line: appreciation --> MUST be > 50. The anchor for 2015 H1 sharp appreciation is 60-70. By July, the bulk of appreciation has occurred; current move is "modest." Score ~60.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declined substantially. Non-energy import prices declining. Manufacturing output modest. Goods deflation evident. Score ~25.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI at 1.75% includes services. Not separately highlighted. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing sector "improved somewhat" but "remained slow." Home values advancing. Not specific enough on housing inflation. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures of longer-term inflation expectations "remained stable." Michigan survey and Primary Dealers survey "remained stable." Market-based measures "still low, although they were somewhat higher than early in the year." Mixed: surveys anchored, market-based low. Score ~43 (slightly below anchored-at-target, reflecting low market-based measures).

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (42) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44)**: Both below 50, close together. Coherent -- economy below potential, labor slack diminishing but present.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (42) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (50)**: Divergence of 8 points. Acceptable -- consumption is solid but investment mixed, and overall output gap reflects broader factors including net exports drag and first-half weakness.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (40)**: Both below 50, both in accommodative territory. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (35)**: Both low, coherent for a calm period with easy financial conditions.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods at 25, expectations at 43, no other subs scored. Parent at 32 is reasonable given substantial goods disinflation pulling things down.

### Bright-Line Verification

- OUTPUT_GAP: Slack remains --> 42 < 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Accommodative" --> 35 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Generally favorable --> 40 < 50. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: "Strong capital position" --> 30 < 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar appreciated --> 60 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Normal functioning, yield moves from fundamentals --> 35, no dysfunction. PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core PCE inflation, which excludes food and energy prices, was 1 1/4 percent over the same period, restrained in part by declines in the prices of non-energy imports." "The staff continued to project that inflation would be below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over 2016 and 2017."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees inflation running well below the 2% target, with core PCE at only 1.25% and the forecast showing below-target inflation persisting for years.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is well below target with no signs of imminent increase.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP was again expected to increase faster in the second half of this year than in the first half and to expand more rapidly than potential output in 2016 and 2017" (implying currently below potential). "Slack in labor and product markets projected to have waned" (future tense, slack exists now).
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is below potential but closing the gap; moderate growth returning after weak first half.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because slack remains in labor and product markets. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 5.3 percent, its lowest reading so far this year." "On balance, labor market indicators suggested that underutilization of labor resources had diminished since early this year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving with UE at 5.3%, but staff still sees underutilization and participation fell, suggesting remaining slack.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is slightly below neutral -- approaching but not yet at full employment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) appeared to have risen at a solid pace in the second quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to rise at a modest rate in the second quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending offset by only modest business investment and sharp declines in energy-related capital spending; net picture is roughly at trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral -- solid consumption balanced against mixed investment.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "The projected rates of productivity gains and potential output growth over the medium term were trimmed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff revised down already-modest productivity projections, suggesting a below-trend productivity outlook.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because the staff trimmed its already-modest estimates. Limited discussion warrants low confidence.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms continued to be accommodative." "Financing for commercial real estate (CRE) remained broadly available." "Residential mortgage lending conditions stayed accommodative for many consumers."
**Center-of-gravity**: Across corporate, CRE, and consumer lending, staff repeatedly describes conditions as accommodative with ample credit availability.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff uses "accommodative" and "broadly available" language. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on 5- and 10-year nominal Treasury securities fell somewhat on net." No mention of liquidity problems, auction difficulties, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally; yield movements reflect fundamentals (Greek developments, policy expectations), not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral -- normal/calm market conditions with no dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened somewhat over the period." But "corporate bond issuance remained strong" and "issuance of institutional leveraged loans picked up noticeably."
**Center-of-gravity**: Some modest spread widening but overall credit conditions remain favorable with strong issuance activity.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral -- spreads are generally at favorable levels despite modest widening.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes were little changed over the intermeeting period." "Somewhat elevated valuations and loosening lending standards for many asset classes pointed to some increasing concerns."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff flags "somewhat elevated valuations" in the financial stability assessment, indicating mild overvaluation concern.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (toward overvaluation concern) because staff explicitly notes "somewhat elevated valuations."

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The strong capital position of the U.S. banking sector, low to moderate use of leverage elsewhere in the financial system" were "factors supporting overall financial stability." "The effect of financial stresses related to Greece and China on the largest U.S. financial firms was limited."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking sector is healthy and well-capitalized with limited spillover from international stresses, though loosening lending standards are flagged as a minor concern.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral (low stress) because staff describes "strong capital position" and "limited" stress effects. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the U.S. dollar increased during the intermeeting period against the currencies of major U.S. trading partners." Staff also notes "continuing effects of earlier increases in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" weighing on manufacturing and trade, and "a slightly stronger forecast for the exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar modestly appreciated in the intermeeting period and the cumulative appreciation from earlier in the year continues to weigh on the economy.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 25 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Held down primarily by earlier large declines in energy prices." "Restrained in part by declines in the prices of non-energy imports."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices under clear downward pressure from energy and import price declines.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_GOODS is well below neutral reflecting goods disinflation/deflation.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Not separately discussed as an inflation component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of expected longer-run inflation from a variety of surveys...remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation were still low, although they were somewhat higher than early in the year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations stable but market-based measures running low, pulling the composite slightly below anchored-at-target.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is slightly below neutral because market-based measures are low despite stable surveys.
