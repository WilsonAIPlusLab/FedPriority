# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Only staff-attributable content is scored.

### Era Context
September 2022 — the Fed is in the midst of its aggressive tightening cycle. Inflation is at multi-decade highs (CPI at 8.3% YoY), the labor market is extremely tight (UE 3.7%), and the dollar is surging to multi-decade highs. The economy is sending mixed signals: GDP declined in H1 2022 but the labor market and spending remain resilient. Financial conditions are tightening rapidly due to rate hikes.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as very elevated. PCE at 6.3%, core PCE at 4.6%, CPI at 8.3%, core CPI at 6.3%. Wage growth at 5.2-5.5%. Staff notes inflation "remained elevated" repeatedly. Upside risks to inflation forecast are highlighted (wage pressure, expectations unanchoring, supply not improving). This is clearly well above target — high 70s to mid-80s range. The 2022 Q2-Q3 anchor is 82-88. Score ~83.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The staff revised the output gap "up considerably this year" due to lowered potential output estimates. The staff projection has "output slightly above potential at end of 2025." Current text says real GDP "increasing at a modest pace" after declining in H1. But the key bright-line language: the output gap was "revised up considerably" — meaning economy is operating above potential. Score must be > 50. Given "revised up considerably" and labor market tightness, this suggests modestly above potential. Score ~57.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE at 3.7%, "labor market continued to be very tight," "labor demand remained strong," "robust" payroll gains, wage growth at 5.2-5.5% (broad-based and rapid), job openings "remained at a high level." This is clearly very tight. Anchor for 2022 is 65-75. With UE at 3.7% and "very tight" language, score ~72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Real PCE "on track to post a modest gain." Residential investment sees "another sharp contraction." Business fixed investment "rising at a tepid pace." Net exports contributing positively. Overall GDP "increasing at a modest pace." The combination of modest consumption, sharp housing contraction, and tepid business investment nets to below trend. Score ~40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff explicitly notes "continued disappointing productivity growth" and "the staff's estimate of potential output in recent history was revised down significantly in response to continued disappointing productivity growth." This is clearly below trend. Score ~25.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Conditions are tightening notably. Borrowing costs "continued to rise," mortgage rates "highest levels since 2008," banks "tightened credit standards on C&I lending for the first time in two years," small business credit "tightening somewhat," consumers report harder to obtain credit. However, "credit remained widely available to most types of borrowers" and "credit remained generally available to businesses and households." This is somewhat tight but not severely restrictive. Score ~60.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "increased significantly across the maturity spectrum." Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. This is normal yield movement from policy expectations. Score ~42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate bond spreads narrowed slightly, on net, and remained roughly at the midpoints of their historical distributions." This is essentially neutral. Score ~50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad domestic equity price indexes decreased slightly, on net." VIX "increased somewhat" and "remained elevated by historical norms." No valuation commentary from staff — just price moves and elevated volatility. Default to 50-55 range for modest declines with elevated volatility. Score ~55.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Credit quality "remained generally strong" for nonfinancial corporations. C&I and CRE loan delinquency rates "remained at low levels." Banks "increased loan loss provisions somewhat." Some deterioration in subprime auto and credit card delinquencies. Overall banking system sounds healthy with minor emerging concerns. Score ~38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated further against most major currencies, reaching multi-decade highs against the euro, the British pound, and the Japanese yen." This is extreme dollar strength. The 2022 Q3 anchor is 75-85. Score ~80.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately broken out from services in staff text. PCE goods vs services not distinguished. Some mention of consumer goods imports declining. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "The latest housing market data pointed to another sharp contraction in residential investment" — but this is about housing activity, not housing/shelter inflation specifically. No shelter CPI/PCE component discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of short-run inflation expectations declined in recent weeks, while measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained roughly stable or moved lower." Also, staff notes upside risk that "inflation expectations could become unanchored given the large rise in inflation." Inflation compensation "declined substantially at short horizons and remained relatively little changed at medium- and longer-term horizons." Currently anchored but with noted risk of unanchoring. Score ~57.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says very elevated. Score 83 = correct direction.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Staff revised output gap up considerably. Score 57 > 50 = correct, bright-line satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Very tight" with 3.7% UE. Score 72 = correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Borrowing costs rising, standards tightening. Score 60 > 50 = correct, bright-line satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Spreads at "midpoints of historical distributions." Score 50 = correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. "Remained generally strong," "low levels" of delinquency. Score 38 < 50 = correct, bright-line satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. "Appreciated further," "multi-decade highs." Score 80 > 50 = correct, bright-line satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Rising yields but "stable" funding markets, no dysfunction. Score 42 < 50 = appropriate.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72)**: Gap of 15 points. Labor market is tighter than the output gap score suggests. This is consistent with the text: GDP was weak in H1 (declined) but the labor market remained very tight — a divergence the staff itself notes by revising down potential output. The output gap is above potential mainly because potential was revised down, not because output surged. Labor market tightness is more directly measured. Acceptable divergence.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Gap of 17 points. This needs explanation. The output gap is above potential (due to revised-down potential), but spending components are weak (sharp housing contraction, tepid business investment, only modest consumer spending). The divergence reflects that the output gap being above potential is driven by supply-side revisions (lower potential), not demand strength. This is coherent with the text.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (50)**: Gap of 10 points. Financial conditions are somewhat tight (rising borrowing costs, tightening standards) but credit spreads are at historical midpoints. This is consistent: tightening is driven by rate increases passing through, not by credit risk repricing. Acceptable.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: No crisis, so these can diverge. Treasury markets functioning normally even as financial conditions tighten. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (83) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (57). Expectations are better-anchored than realized inflation, which is consistent — expectations show some concern but are not yet unanchored, while realized inflation is very elevated.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 83 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.3 percent over the 12 months ending in July, and core PCE price inflation...was 4.6 percent." "The 12-month change in the consumer price index (CPI) was 8.3 percent, while core CPI inflation was 6.3 percent." "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid and broad based: Average hourly earnings rose 5.2 percent...the employment cost index...rose 5.5 percent." Staff also flags upside risks: "wage increases could put a greater-than-expected amount of upward pressure on price inflation" and "inflation expectations could become unanchored."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running at multi-decade highs across all measures, with broad-based wage pressures and upside risks flagged — squarely in the 2022 peak range.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far above neutral because PCE, CPI, and wages are all running at exceptionally elevated levels.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The staff's estimate of the output gap was revised up considerably this year." "The level of output was expected to be slightly above potential at the end of 2025." Real GDP "increasing at a modest pace in the third quarter after having declined over the first half of the year."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The output gap is above potential, but this is driven by downward revisions to potential output rather than demand strength. Actual growth is modest.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral (>50) because staff explicitly states the output gap was "revised up considerably" — bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The labor market continued to be very tight." "The unemployment rate...edged up, on net, from 3.6 percent in June to 3.7 percent in August." "Nominal wage growth continued to be rapid and broad based" at 5.2-5.5%. "The private-sector job openings rate...remained at a high level."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is very tight by any measure — low unemployment, strong payrolls, rapid wage growth, high job openings. "Very tight" is strong language.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE is 3.7% with "very tight" characterization and rapid wage growth.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real PCE was on track to post a modest gain in the third quarter." "The latest housing market data pointed to another sharp contraction in residential investment." "Business fixed investment appeared to be rising at a tepid pace."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is modestly positive, but residential investment is contracting sharply and business investment is tepid, netting to below-trend overall.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because only one of three major spending components is positive, and even that is merely "modest."

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 25 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The staff's estimate of potential output in recent history was revised down significantly in response to continued disappointing productivity growth." "The sluggish gains in labor force participation seen so far this year" also contributed.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly calls productivity growth "disappointing" and revised down potential output significantly as a result.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is well below neutral because staff characterizes productivity growth as "continued disappointing."

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Credit remained widely available to most types of borrowers, but increases in borrowing costs appeared to damp the demand for credit in some markets." "Banks tightened credit standards on C&I lending for the first time in two years." "Borrowing costs for residential mortgage loans increased and reached their highest levels since 2008."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are tightening — borrowing costs rising sharply, lending standards tightening, some credit demand suppressed — but credit is still generally available. Somewhat tight, not severely restrictive.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because borrowing costs are at multi-year highs and standards are tightening — bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields increased significantly across the maturity spectrum." "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yields rose significantly but this reflects policy expectations and inflation concerns — normal price adjustment. Short-term funding markets explicitly described as stable. No dysfunction mentioned.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral/slightly below because yield moves are orderly and funding markets are stable — no actual dysfunction described.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 50 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Corporate bond spreads narrowed slightly, on net, and remained roughly at the midpoints of their historical distributions." "Spreads on lower-rated short-term commercial paper changed little on net."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are at historical midpoints — the definition of neutral.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is at neutral because spreads are explicitly described as at "midpoints of their historical distributions."

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes decreased slightly, on net." "One-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500—the VIX—increased somewhat, on net, and remained elevated by historical norms."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities declined slightly with elevated volatility, but no staff valuation commentary. Default to modestly above neutral for mild stress signal.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral (modest stress) due to slight declines and elevated volatility, but no strong valuation signal.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The credit quality of nonfinancial corporations remained generally strong, with low default rates." "Credit quality for C&I and CRE loans on banks' balance sheets also remained sound, as delinquency rates remained at low levels." "However, banks increased loan loss provisions somewhat in the second quarter."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is fundamentally sound with low delinquencies and strong credit quality, but early warning signals (increased provisions, subprime deterioration) keep it from the lowest stress scores.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because credit quality is "generally strong" and "sound" with "low levels" of delinquency — bright-line rule satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 80 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated further against most major currencies, reaching multi-decade highs against the euro, the British pound, and the Japanese yen." "The dollar's strength largely reflected increasing investor concerns about the global growth outlook as well as widening interest rate differentials."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar at multi-decade highs against major currencies — this is near the extreme end of dollar strength.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is far above neutral because the dollar is appreciating to multi-decade highs — bright-line rule satisfied.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
