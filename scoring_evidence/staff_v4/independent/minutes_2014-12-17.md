# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2014-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Late 2014. The U.S. economy is in a mid-cycle recovery from the GFC. Unemployment has fallen to 5.8% but slack remains. Inflation is running below the 2% target, partly due to declining energy prices. The Fed is at the zero lower bound, with liftoff expected around mid-2015. Oil prices are declining sharply. The dollar is appreciating notably.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below the midpoint. PCE and core PCE both running at about 1.5%, well below the 2% target. Energy prices declining further. Staff projects inflation will "move up gradually toward, but run somewhat below" the 2% objective. Market-based inflation compensation moved lower. Wage growth only about 2%. This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 26-40 range ("below target; contained; running below 2 percent").

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as below potential. The staff forecast says GDP will "rise more quickly than potential output" -- implying the economy is still below potential. The staff mentions "resource slack" that would "slowly reduce" over the medium term. The unemployment rate was "expected to decline gradually and to temporarily move slightly below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" -- meaning it hasn't gotten there yet. Bright-line check: slack remains = MUST be < 50. Score in the 38-44 range (recovering but gap persists, approaching full capacity).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.8%, which is still above the longer-run natural rate (staff expects it to decline further). Payrolls expanding at a faster pace than Q3. Job openings at elevated levels, hiring and quits stepped up. Labor force participation and employment-to-population ratio rose slightly. Part-time for economic reasons declined. Wage growth modest at ~2%. This is a mixed picture: improving conditions but still some slack. UE 5.8% maps to the 31-45 range. Given the improving trend and the level, score around 40-42.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "rising robustly" -- strong retail sales, vehicle sales up, consumer sentiment at pre-recession highs. However, business equipment spending "appeared to be decelerating," nondefense capital goods orders declined, nonresidential structures edged down. Housing "remained slow." The net position: strong consumption offset by weak investment and slow housing. Score around 50-53 given the robust consumption emphasis but weak investment drag.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Mixed signals. Business financing "robust" -- strong bond issuance, brisk C&I loan growth, strong leveraged loan/CLO issuance. Consumer credit "accommodative." CRE financing "broadly available." However, mortgage conditions "remained tight for borrowers with less-than-pristine credit," and corporate spreads widened. On balance, conditions are mostly accommodative/supportive with some pockets of tightening. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language = MUST be < 50. Score around 35-40.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined significantly over the intermeeting period. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. This is normal market functioning. Score in the 30-40 range (normal but quiet to normal functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "widened over the period." Speculative-grade energy spreads "rose substantially." However, business financing was robust and issuance was strong. The widening is notable but concentrated in energy. On balance, spreads are widening from generally favorable levels. Score around 45-50 (moving from below-normal toward normal, with energy sector stress).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities "about unchanged" over the period. VIX rose sharply late in the period. No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 45-55 range. Score around 50 (neutral, no staff concern expressed).

**BANKING_STRESS**: No direct discussion of banking system health, capital, or stress. C&I loans expanding briskly and CRE loans expanding suggest healthy lending. Bright-line: no direct "well-capitalized" language but lending activity implies low stress. N/A is technically appropriate since banking system health is not directly discussed, but lending conditions are described positively. I'll score N/A since there's no explicit banking system discussion.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated substantially against most currencies over the intermeeting period." "The dollar moved up significantly against the yen." Staff also notes the projected path of the dollar was revised up and discusses its restraining effect on GDP. Bright-line: appreciation = MUST be > 50. "Substantially" = significant move. Score around 65-70.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declining; consumer food prices rose more than overall. Some goods-specific info but mostly energy-driven. Energy goods prices falling. Score around 25-30 (declining energy dragging goods prices down).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity slow, but no specific shelter/rent inflation discussion. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "remained stable." Market-based measures "moved lower." Mixed signal: surveys anchored, markets declining. Score around 42-45 (mostly anchored but market measures drifting down).

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Staff says below target. Low score. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Staff says slack remains. Score < 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE 5.8% with slack. Score < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Staff says accommodative. Score < 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress. Spreads widened. Score near 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar appreciated substantially. Score > 50. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (42) ~ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (41): Consistent. Both below 50, reflecting remaining slack.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (42) ~ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52): ~10 point divergence. Explained by robust consumer spending pushing C/I up while overall economy still has slack from weak investment/housing.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (37) ~ CREDIT_SPREADS (48): ~11 point divergence. FC reflects broadly accommodative lending; credit spreads widening driven by energy sector, but overall issuance still strong. Reasonable.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (37) ~ TREASURY_STRESS (35): Consistent. Both low, normal/calm environment.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (33) ~ sub-dimensions: Goods ~28, Expectations ~43. Parent at 33 is consistent with below-target readings and energy drag.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Both total U.S. consumer price inflation...and core inflation...were about 1-1/2 percent over the 12 months ending in October." "The staff continued to project that inflation would move up gradually toward, but run somewhat below, the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running meaningfully below the 2% target with energy prices declining further, and staff projects only a gradual return that stays somewhat below objective.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because both headline and core PCE are at 1.5%, well below the 2% target, with further downward pressure from energy.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity over the medium term was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack." "Real GDP would expand at a faster pace in 2015 and 2016...and...rise more quickly than potential output."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing at a moderate pace and recovering, but resource slack persists and GDP has not yet caught up to potential -- the gap is closing but still negative.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references remaining resource slack and forecasts GDP rising faster than potential (implying current output is below potential). Bright-line satisfied: slack remains = score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 41 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged down to 5.8 percent in October and remained at that level in November." "Total nonfarm payroll employment expanded in October and November at a faster pace than in the third quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving with strong payroll gains, elevated job openings, and declining part-time for economic reasons, but unemployment at 5.8% is still above the natural rate and wage growth remains muted at ~2%.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 5.8% is above the natural rate and wage growth is subdued, indicating remaining slack despite improvement.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be rising robustly in the fourth quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to be decelerating in the fourth quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Very strong consumer spending (robust PCE, high sentiment, strong vehicle sales) is partially offset by decelerating business investment and slow housing, netting to roughly at-trend or slightly above.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is roughly at neutral. Robust consumption pulls above; weak investment and slow housing pull below. Net is marginally above.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in the staff assessment.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Business financing flows were robust over the intermeeting period." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets generally stayed accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based accommodative conditions across corporate lending, consumer credit, and CRE, with only mortgage credit for weaker borrowers remaining tight. Overall financial conditions are clearly supportive.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose/accommodative) because staff describes robust financing, accommodative consumer credit, and broadly available CRE financing. Bright-line satisfied: accommodative = score < 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Longer-term nominal Treasury yields declined significantly, on balance, over the intermeeting period." No mention of market dysfunction or liquidity issues.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally with orderly yield movements driven by macro/policy factors. No stress indicators mentioned.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is zero mention of dysfunction; yield movements are orderly and explained by fundamentals.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond spreads widened over the period. Spreads on speculative-grade bonds for energy-related firms rose substantially." "Gross bond issuance by nonfinancial corporations was the strongest in more than a year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are widening, especially in energy, but overall credit availability remains strong with robust issuance. The level of spreads is near normal with emerging stress in one sector.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is roughly at neutral. Widening is occurring but from favorable levels, and issuance remains strong, suggesting spreads are around normal.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes were about unchanged over the intermeeting period." "Option-implied volatility...rose sharply late in the period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities flat with rising volatility late in the period. No staff valuation commentary. Default to neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is at neutral. No valuation concerns expressed; prices roughly unchanged.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No explicit discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or stress. Lending activity described as robust but this is captured in FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated substantially against most currencies over the intermeeting period." "The projected path of the dollar was revised up...the staff revised down its estimate of how much the appreciation of the dollar since last summer would restrain projected growth."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based, substantial dollar appreciation driven by divergent policy paths and oil price declines. Staff explicitly factoring dollar strength into GDP forecasts.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "appreciated substantially" -- a strong, clear directional signal. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation = score > 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 28 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices declined." "Total inflation as measured by the CPI was 1-1/4 percent, partly reflecting the further decline in energy prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Energy prices declining sharply, dragging goods inflation well below normal. Food prices rose modestly but overall goods price pressure is low.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral due to declining energy prices dominating goods inflation.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific services inflation discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific shelter/rent inflation discussion. Housing activity discussed but not housing-related inflation.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation moved lower."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations well-anchored but market-based measures declining, creating a mixed picture tilted slightly below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral because while surveys are stable, market-based compensation measures are declining, pulling the composite slightly below the anchored-at-target midpoint.
