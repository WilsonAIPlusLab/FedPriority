# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — August 22, 2000 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from a pre-2005 meeting. It is **unsegmented** — staff and participant content are mixed together. I must carefully separate staff-attributable language from participant/member discussion.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The key staff-attributable sections are:
- Paragraphs beginning with "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested..." through the trade/inventory/price data paragraphs (descriptive economic review)
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested..."
- References to what "the Manager" reported

Participant/member content (to be IGNORED for scoring):
- "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective economic conditions, the members agreed..."
- "In their review of the outlook for expenditures in key sectors..."
- "In the Committee's discussion of the outlook for inflation, members noted..."
- "In the Committee's discussion of policy..."
- "many members emphasized..."

### Era Context
August 2000 — the late-1990s boom is still running. The fed funds rate is at 6.5%. The economy is near the peak of the expansion, with the dot-com bubble beginning to deflate. The staff is seeing signs that growth is moderating from an unsustainably rapid pace, but labor markets remain extremely tight.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff language: "Recent data suggested that price inflation had picked up slightly." Core CPI "increased somewhat more than in the previous twelve-month period." PCE chain-price acceleration was "very small." Core producer prices "rose slightly more than in the previous twelve-month period." Labor compensation showed "an acceleration, on balance, over the past year" — four-quarter compensation change was "substantially larger than the change over the previous four-quarter period." However, "unit labor costs had changed little" due to productivity growth, and "measures of core price inflation had increased only mildly."
- Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as MODESTLY ABOVE neutral — there is a pickup, but it is described as "slightly," "mildly," "somewhat more." The energy component is stronger but core is contained.
- Magnitude: "Slightly" / "mildly" / "somewhat" = 5-10 points above midpoint. But labor compensation acceleration is "substantially larger," which pulls upward.
- Score reasoning: Core inflation rising modestly, energy prices surging, compensation accelerating. But unit labor costs contained by productivity. Net: slightly above neutral. Score ~57.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation picked up slightly with energy surge and comp acceleration but core contained. 57 = modestly above neutral. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff language: "economic activity was expanding at a more moderate pace than earlier in the year" — this implies it had been above potential and is now moderating. The staff forecast says expansion "would be sustained at a rate a little below that of the staff's upwardly revised estimate of the economy's potential output." This means the economy was running above potential and is expected to converge toward it. The language about "slowing appreciably from its elevated pace" and "unsustainably elevated pace" from the June meeting reference confirm above-potential operation.
- Direction: Staff sees output as ABOVE potential — "elevated pace," capacity utilization "around its long-term average," demand growth being described as needing to slow to sustainable levels.
- Bright-line check: "Above potential" / "elevated pace" / "unsustainably elevated" -> MUST be > 50. Score will be > 50.
- Magnitude: The economy is moderating but still above potential. "Elevated" but moderating = 55-60 range.
- Score: 57.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff language: "labor markets remained extremely tight by historical standards." Unemployment at 4.0%. "expansion in employment had slowed considerably in recent months" but the LEVEL remains extremely tight.
- Direction: ABOVE neutral — "extremely tight by historical standards" is unambiguous.
- Magnitude: "Extremely tight" = strong language. UE at 4.0% in 2000 was very low. Anchors say 65-75 for 1999-2000. "Extremely tight" justifies upper end.
- Score: 73. UE 4.0% with "extremely tight" language.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. "Extremely tight" -> high score. Matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff language: "Growth in consumer spending had slowed from the outsized gains seen earlier" — moderating from very strong. "business spending on equipment and software had continued to surge." Housing starts "fell further." Staff forecast: spending on consumer durables "well below that in recent quarters," housing to "stabilize at a level below recent highs," but business fixed investment "projected to remain robust."
- Direction: Mixed — consumer slowing, housing weakening, but business investment surging. Net is still above trend but moderating.
- The staff forecast says expansion of domestic final demand would be "held back to some extent" — suggesting it would be modestly above trend or near trend.
- Score: 55. Consumer spending moderating from "outsized" to more moderate (still positive), business investment "surging," housing declining. Net: modestly above trend.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff language: "productivity also continuing to accelerate" — staff revised up their estimate of potential output. The staff forecast notes "growth in productivity" would partially offset compensation gains. The descriptive paragraph says "unit labor costs had changed little" because of productivity acceleration.
- Direction: ABOVE trend — "continuing to accelerate" is strong language.
- Magnitude: "Continuing to accelerate" / "acceleration" = well above trend. This is the late-1990s productivity boom.
- Score: 70.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff-attributable language: "Other interest rates generally moved lower over the period" — rates declining. From the policy section (member-attributed but referencing staff analysis): "real interest rates for private borrowers were still at relatively elevated levels, banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans." The staff reports that mortgage rates are "well above their average for last year." The fed funds rate at 6.5%.
- The interest rate environment is restrictive (fed funds at 6.5%), mortgage rates elevated, banks tightening lending standards. But stock prices rose somewhat and long rates declined.
- Direction: ABOVE neutral (tighter than normal) — rates elevated, bank lending standards tightening.
- Bright-line: "Tightening standards" -> > 50.
- Score: 58. Conditions are somewhat tight — elevated real rates, tightening bank standards, but markets rallied and rates moved lower over the period, providing partial offset.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
No staff discussion of Treasury market dysfunction. Interest rates moved lower, which is normal market functioning. No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, etc.
- Score: N/A.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff language mentions "banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans" (member-attributed section referencing staff data). The descriptive staff section mentions "growth of domestic nonfinancial debt moderated slightly" and "private borrowing remained brisk" but may be "slowing somewhat."
- There is no direct discussion of corporate bond spreads or credit spread levels in the staff sections.
- Limited evidence. The tightening of bank lending standards suggests some credit tightening, but no spread-specific language.
- Score: 53 with low confidence. Some tightening in bank lending standards suggests modestly above neutral, but no direct spread evidence.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff language: "little net change in stock market valuations thus far this year." "Most broad indexes of stock market prices rose somewhat over the period since the June meeting." Staff forecast references "waning and eventual disappearance of positive wealth effects associated with outsized earlier gains in equity prices."
- The language about "outsized earlier gains" and wealth effects "waning" suggests the staff viewed prior valuations as elevated and now leveling off. No explicit valuation concern language, but the context of the dot-com era is relevant.
- Direction: The staff describes equity prices as having leveled out after outsized gains. This implies valuations were elevated. Score in the 30-40 range (modestly above fair value / somewhat elevated).
- Score: 38. Staff implicitly views equities as having been elevated (outsized gains) and now leveling, with wealth effects waning. Not expressing acute bubble concern but treating prior levels as above fair value.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff-attributable: Banks "continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans." In the lending context: "more cautious attitudes on the part of many business executives and especially their lending institutions."
- Direction: Banks tightening standards = moderate caution, not distress. This is precautionary tightening, not stress per se.
- Score: 42. Banks are healthy enough to be cautious, tightening standards proactively. This is modest concern, not stress. Below neutral on stress scale.
- Polarity check: Higher = more stress. Tightening lending standards from a position of strength is low-to-moderate stress. 42 fits.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff language: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar increased on net against an index of major currencies." "The dollar's appreciation reflected heightened market perceptions that economic growth in the United States...was likely to continue to exceed that in most other industrial nations."
- Direction: Dollar STRENGTHENED -> MUST be > 50.
- Bright-line: Appreciation -> > 50. Check.
- Magnitude: "Increased on net" / "appreciation" = modest strengthening. Score 57.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices surged; core producer prices roughly flat in June-July but up slightly year-over-year. Mixed — energy goods surging, core goods tame. Score: 55.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Growth in household expenditures for services eased somewhat." No specific services inflation discussion from staff. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff language: "stable and relatively favorable expectations with regard to the longer-run inflation outlook" (this is from member discussion but references staff view). The staff forecast projects core inflation to "rise somewhat." Expectations appear anchored. Score: 52. Anchored but with slight upside risk given energy.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (73)**: Gap of 16 points. The labor market is much tighter than the output gap score suggests. Explanation: The economy is moderating from above-potential growth, so the output gap is narrowing, but the labor market LEVEL remains extremely tight from the accumulated years of expansion. This divergence is expected in a late-cycle moderation where the flow (GDP growth) slows before the stock (employment level) adjusts. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (55)**: Within 2 points. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (53)**: Within 5 points. Coherent — both suggest modestly tight.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs TREASURY_STRESS (N/A)**: N/A for Treasury stress. Normal times, no dysfunction. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (57) vs sub-dimensions**: INFLATION_GOODS (55), INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (52). Parent at 57 is consistent — slightly above the sub-components, reflecting the labor compensation acceleration that affects the headline score.
