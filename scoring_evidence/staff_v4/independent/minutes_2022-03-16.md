# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: March 2022 — post-pandemic recovery with surging inflation (CPI at 7.9%), very tight labor market (UE 3.8%), Russia's invasion of Ukraine just weeks old creating commodity and geopolitical shocks. The Fed is about to begin its hiking cycle. This is early in the 2022 tightening episode.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as far above midpoint. PCE at 6.1%, core PCE at 5.2%, CPI at 7.9%, core CPI at 6.4%. Language: "continued to rise rapidly," "remained elevated," "persistently high and broad-based." Near-term projection "revised up considerably." Staff expects 4% PCE for 2022 and sees upside risks to inflation. This is clearly in the 76-90 range — substantially above target, broad-based, highest in decades. Anchor guidance: 82-88 for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak. This is Q1 2022, slightly before the peak, so ~80.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential over the projection period." This is unambiguous bright-line language: "well above potential" → MUST be > 50. GDP growth stepping down from rapid 2021 pace but level remains well above potential. Supply constraints binding. Score ~60-63. Anchor: 57-63 for 2022.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE at 3.8%, edging down. Job openings "well above pre-pandemic level," quits rate "remained elevated," average hourly earnings up 5.1% YoY, "widespread increases across industries." Staff outlook: "labor market conditions were expected to remain very tight." With UE at 3.8% and explicit "very tight" language, this is firmly in the 66-75 range. Anchor: 65-75 for 2022.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Real PCE appeared to be rising at a faster pace in the first quarter of 2022 than in the fourth quarter of 2021." Housing demand "remained strong." Business fixed investment "was picking up." Motor vehicle production declined sharply due to supply issues, but overall picture is above trend. Government purchases little changed. Trade deficit widened to record. Net picture: consumer spending strong, investment picking up, offset somewhat by supply constraints and government flat. Score ~58-62.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Financing conditions remained accommodative, although borrowing costs increased further." "Credit remained broadly available for most types of borrowers." Borrowing costs "remained low relative to their historical distributions." But there is also: "Financial markets were highly volatile over the intermeeting period, with strained liquidity in some markets." Spreads widened. The net assessment: conditions still accommodative but tightening. "Accommodative" triggers bright-line: MUST be < 50. But the tightening and volatility push it toward the upper end of the accommodative range. Score ~42-45.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Key language: "Liquidity conditions became strained in some financial markets." "Market depth—a gauge of the ability to transact in large volumes at quotes posted by market makers—deteriorated in U.S. Treasury, U.S. equity, and crude oil markets." "Investors reported that strained liquidity at times amplified the volatility of price moves." This is actual market dysfunction language — deteriorating market depth, strained liquidity amplifying volatility — not just yield movements. Score ~58-62.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds widened noticeably since the previous FOMC meeting and ended the period close to the medians of their historical distributions." Municipal spreads also "widened significantly." Spreads rose to "somewhat above their pre-pandemic levels." At median of historical distributions = roughly neutral. Score ~52-56.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity indexes declined modestly, on net, amid significant fluctuations." VIX surged briefly but ended slightly lower on net. No valuation commentary from staff — just price moves. Default to 50-55 per rubric: "Equities declined" alone → 50-55. Score ~53.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Credit quality of large nonfinancial corporations "remained strong." Upgrades outpaced downgrades. Default rates "remained very low." CRE showed "some signs of stress" (hotel/retail CMBS delinquencies above pre-pandemic, office sector increased somewhat). Household credit quality "remained fairly healthy." Small business credit access: "share...that reported it was more difficult to acquire credit...remained very low." The banking/financial system picture is mostly healthy with minor CRE pockets. Score ~35-38. Bright-line check: healthy/sound indicators → must be < 50. Satisfied.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "A moderate increase in the broad dollar index." Dollar appreciated. Bright-line: appreciation → must be > 50. Score ~57-60.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Supply bottlenecks "showed little sign of improvement," "materials inputs...remained in short supply," "industrial input prices remained elevated," motor vehicle production declined due to semiconductor shortages. Goods inflation clearly elevated. Score ~78.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Social distancing unwound further" boosting services spending. Services mentioned in context of Omicron effect internationally. Domestically, services implied rising via PCE composition but not specifically broken out by staff. Limited direct evidence. Score ~65, low confidence.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Housing demand remained strong, though activity...continued to be restrained by shortages." Mortgage rates increasing. Housing demand strong but no specific shelter/rent inflation data from staff. Limited. Score ~62, low confidence.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "The staff's common inflation expectations index...had largely leveled off over the fall and was close to its 2014 average." Near-term inflation compensation "rose sharply." But longer-term expectations anchored near 2014 levels. Staff sees "the possibility that continued high inflation would cause longer-term inflation expectations to become unanchored" as an upside risk. Net: near-term expectations elevated, but longer-term still anchored. Score ~55.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 80 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.1 percent...core PCE price inflation...was 5.2 percent"; "the 12-month change in the consumer price index (CPI) was 7.9 percent"; "The staff's near-term projection for PCE price inflation was revised up considerably...reflecting the staff's reaction to the persistently high and broad-based levels of domestic inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation running at multi-decade highs with broad-based pressures, upward forecast revisions, and upside risks — clearly substantially above target.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far above neutral because PCE inflation at 6.1%, CPI at 7.9%, and staff forecasts revised up considerably.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 61 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential over the projection period"; "Real GDP growth was expected to slow further in 2024 to a pace that was in line with potential growth."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly states GDP well above potential, though growth is decelerating from rapid 2021 pace. Level remains elevated.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says "well above potential." Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged down...to 3.8 percent"; "the private-sector job openings rate...remained well above its pre-pandemic level; the quits rate also remained elevated"; "Average hourly earnings rose 5.1 percent...with widespread increases across industries"; "labor market conditions were expected to remain very tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 3.8%, elevated quits and openings, 5.1% wage growth, and staff explicitly calls conditions "very tight."
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE 3.8% with "very tight" explicit assessment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE appeared to be rising at a faster pace in the first quarter of 2022 than in the fourth quarter of 2021"; "Housing demand remained strong"; "growth in business fixed investment was picking up in the first quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending accelerating, housing demand strong, business investment picking up — all above trend despite supply constraints.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because consumer spending, housing, and business investment all described positively.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity by staff.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 43 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions remained accommodative, although borrowing costs increased further"; "credit remained broadly available for most types of borrowers"; "Borrowing costs continued to increase in many sectors but remained low relative to their historical distributions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly calls conditions "accommodative" with credit broadly available, though noting that borrowing costs are rising and volatility elevated. Still on the easy side of neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose side) because staff says "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 59 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Liquidity conditions became strained in some financial markets"; "Market depth...deteriorated in U.S. Treasury, U.S. equity, and crude oil markets"; "investors reported that strained liquidity at times amplified the volatility of price moves."
**Center-of-gravity**: Actual market dysfunction — deteriorating market depth and strained liquidity in Treasuries — but short-term funding markets mostly stable and bid-ask spreads did not widen notably in most markets. Moderate stress, not severe.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because staff describes actual liquidity deterioration and strained conditions, not just yield movements.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 54 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds widened noticeably since the previous FOMC meeting and ended the period close to the medians of their historical distributions"; "Spreads in the corporate bond, municipal bond, and CMBS markets generally rose to somewhat above their pre-pandemic levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened to around historical medians — roughly normal levels after being compressed. Slightly above neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly above neutral because spreads "widened noticeably" to historical medians.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 53 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity indexes declined modestly, on net, amid significant fluctuations"; "Equity prices increased early in the period...but they retraced these gains as investor risk sentiment deteriorated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest decline with no staff valuation commentary. Default scoring for "equities declined" without valuation assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral, slightly elevated stress given modest declines amid volatility.

### BANKING_STRESS: 36 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The credit quality of large nonfinancial corporations and municipalities remained strong"; "Default rates on corporate bonds, municipal bonds, and leveraged loans remained very low"; "Credit quality in the commercial real estate sector continued to show some signs of stress."
**Center-of-gravity**: System broadly healthy with strong credit quality and low defaults. Minor CRE stress pockets. Low overall stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because credit quality strong, defaults very low. Bright-line satisfied (healthy → < 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "A moderate increase in the broad dollar index."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar appreciated moderately. One direct reference.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 78 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Motor vehicle production declined sharply in February as ongoing shortages of semiconductors...continued to restrain output"; "materials inputs such as electronic components and aluminum remained in short supply, while broad measures of industrial input prices remained elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Severe goods-sector supply constraints with elevated input prices driving goods inflation.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 65 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Social distancing unwound further" (boosting services demand); broad inflation described as "broad-based" which implies services included.
**Center-of-gravity**: Services inflation implied as elevated through broad-based inflation language, but limited direct services-specific detail from staff.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 62 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Housing demand remained strong, though activity in the residential housing sector continued to be restrained by shortages"; "Residential mortgage rates increased."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong housing demand with supply constraints implies upward pressure on shelter costs, though staff does not provide specific shelter inflation data.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff's common inflation expectations index...had largely leveled off over the fall and was close to its 2014 average"; "the possibility that continued high inflation would cause longer-term inflation expectations to become unanchored was seen as another upside risk."
**Center-of-gravity**: Longer-term expectations anchored near 2014 levels (roughly at target), but near-term compensation rose sharply and staff flags unanchoring as an upside risk. Modestly above neutral.
