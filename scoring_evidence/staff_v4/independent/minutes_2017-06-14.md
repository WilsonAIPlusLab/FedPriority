# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2017-06-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring is restricted to staff-attributable content only.

### Era Context
Mid-2017: The U.S. economy is in a mature expansion. The Fed has been gradually raising rates. Unemployment has fallen to historically low levels. Inflation has been persistently running below 2% despite a tightening labor market. Financial conditions remain broadly accommodative. The dollar has been weakening in 2017.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key evidence: "total consumer price inflation and core inflation...were both running somewhat below 2 percent." PCE at 1.75%, core PCE at 1.5%. The staff revised down inflation forecast for 2017 due to "weaker-than-expected incoming data." TIPS inflation compensation declined. However, the staff still projects inflation will be "close to the Committee's longer-run objective in 2018 and at 2 percent in 2019." Current readings are clearly below target. Direction: below neutral. Magnitude: "somewhat below" = modest distance. Score range: 36-42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as ABOVE potential. Bright-line check: "an economy that was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential." Also: "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." The unemployment rate "projected to decline gradually...and to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This clearly triggers the bright-line rule: must be > 50. But the language is "modestly" above — not dramatic overheating. Score range: 52-56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. UE at 4.3% — below estimated natural rate. "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." Job gains "solid." Initial claims "very low level." Job openings increased. However, wage growth is only moderate (2.25-2.5%), suggesting tightness hasn't fully translated to wage pressure. UE at 4.3% in 2017 is below typical NAIRU estimates (~4.6-4.7%). The rubric says UE below 4.5% should be 53-65. Score range: 55-60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MODERATELY STRONG. "Real PCE rose solidly in April." Business equipment spending "seemed to be increasing further after rising at a solid pace." GDP growth forecast was "a bit higher." But residential investment "appeared to be slowing." Government spending flat to declining. Net exports may be a "slight drag." Mixed but net positive. Score range: 53-58.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not directly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE. "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets remained generally accommodative." Bright-line: "accommodative" = must be < 50. But this is 2017 — the anchor neutral period is 46-54. "Generally accommodative" in 2017 context suggests slightly loose but not extremely so. Score range: 38-44.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved in orderly fashion — declined on CPI data, rose on FOMC communication, flattened on net. Short-term funding markets "stable." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. Balance sheet reduction was "not anticipated to have...adverse effects on market functioning." Normal market functioning. Score range: 35-42.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff describes accommodative financing conditions. "Issuance of both corporate debt and equity was strong." Leveraged loan issuance "solid" (near record levels previously). CRE loans grew robustly. Mortgage lending "remained near the upper end of its recent range." EME spreads "little changed" and flows "robust." Some tightening in subprime credit cards and auto, but overall very favorable. Score range: 28-35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased." VIX "near the lower end of its historical range." No valuation concern expressed by staff — no "stretched" or "elevated" language. Just factual reporting of gains. Default to near-neutral with slight lean toward no concern. Score range: 42-48.

**BANKING_STRESS**: CRE delinquencies on CMBS pools increasing but "mostly confined to loans originated during the period of weak underwriting before the financial crisis" and "generally been expected." Bank CRE delinquencies edged down. Some subprime auto and credit card deterioration, but lending standards tightening in response. Overall system sounds healthy with minor pockets. Bright-line: no "stressed" language; system appears sound with contained concerns. Score range: 35-42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad U.S. dollar depreciated about 1.75 percent over the intermeeting period, weakening against both AFE and EME currencies." Clear depreciation. Bright-line: depreciation = must be < 50. The depreciation is modest (1.75%). Score range: 32-38.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed in a way distinct from headline. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan survey median "unchanged." SPF "held steady." Desk surveys "essentially unchanged." TIPS inflation compensation declined. Mixed: surveys stable but market measures down. Anchored but with slight downward tilt. Score range: 44-48.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "total consumer price inflation and core inflation...were both running somewhat below 2 percent." "Core PCE price inflation was 1.5 percent over those same 12 months." Staff forecast for inflation "was revised down slightly for 2017 because of the weaker-than-expected incoming data."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running clearly below the 2% target with recent softening, but is expected to gradually return to target.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because both headline (1.75%) and core (1.5%) PCE are below the 2% target and the staff revised down its forecast.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." "an economy that was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential." Unemployment rate "projected to...continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential with unemployment below the natural rate, but the overshoot is not dramatic.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says the economy is operating above potential. Bright-line satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate moved down to 4.3 percent in May." "labor market conditions continued to strengthen." "initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits remained at a very low level." Wages rising at only "moderate rates" (2.25-2.5%).
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with UE at 4.3% (below natural rate), but wage growth remains moderate, suggesting tightness is not yet severe.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE is below the natural rate at 4.3% with continued strengthening.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE rose solidly in April." "real GDP growth was forecast to step up to a solid pace in the second quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property seemed to be increasing further after rising at a solid pace in the first quarter." But "Residential investment appeared to be slowing."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment are solid, modestly above trend, though residential investment is softening and government spending is flat.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because consumer spending and business investment are described as "solid" with an upgraded GDP forecast.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity in staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets remained generally accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative across multiple sectors — equity, credit, and consumer markets.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term funding markets were stable over the intermeeting period." Treasury yield movements were orderly responses to data and policy expectations. Balance sheet normalization plans seen as "mitigating the risk...of outsized movements in interest rates or adverse effects on market functioning."
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with orderly yield movements driven by fundamentals.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no mention of market dysfunction; all yield movements reflect normal market adjustment.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Issuance of both corporate debt and equity was strong." "Gross issuance of institutional leveraged loans was solid." "CRE loans on banks' books grew robustly." EME sovereign spreads "little changed" with "robust" fund flows. Some subprime tightening but overall conditions very favorable.
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit conditions are clearly favorable with strong issuance across corporate debt, equity, and CRE lending — well below normal spread levels.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because issuance is strong and credit is readily available across sectors.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased." "VIX...remained near the lower end of its historical range." No staff valuation commentary or concern expressed.
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose with very low volatility; staff reports factually without expressing concern about valuations.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral/slightly below because staff mentions no valuation concerns despite rising prices and low volatility.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "delinquency rates on CRE loans held by banks edged down further." CMBS delinquency increase "mostly confined to loans that were originated during the period of weak underwriting before the financial crisis" and "was not anticipated to have a material effect on credit availability." Subprime auto delinquencies "remained elevated" but lending standards had tightened in response.
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is broadly healthy with contained pockets of concern in subprime auto and legacy CMBS — manageable risks without systemic implications.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because delinquency concerns are contained and not expected to affect system stability.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad U.S. dollar depreciated about 1.75 percent over the intermeeting period, weakening against both AFE and EME currencies." Dollar weakened against Canadian dollar, euro; strengthened only against sterling.
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear but modest dollar depreciation over the period, broad-based against most currencies.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line satisfied.
