### Era Context
December 2002: The economy is in a sluggish recovery from the 2001 recession. The fed funds rate was cut to 1.25% at the November meeting. Unemployment is at 6%. This is a period of below-potential growth, excess capacity, and declining inflation — classic "jobless recovery" environment.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint.
- Evidence: "Core consumer price inflation...continued to trend lower in October." "Inflation...was down over the last twelve months when compared with the previous twelve-month period." "Core price inflation for finished goods...was at a very low rate." "Growth in those earnings over the last twelve months fell considerably." Staff forecast: "persistence of underutilized resources was expected to foster a slight moderation in core price inflation."
- This is clear below-target, trending-lower inflation. Not deflation yet, but clearly declining and low.
- Magnitude: "continued to trend lower," "very low rate," "fell considerably" — these are moderate-to-strong language pointing below 50. The staff forecast even expects further moderation. Score range: 25-32.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low and declining inflation. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential.
- Evidence: "economic growth had been sluggish on balance since midsummer." "business fixed investment was still in the doldrums." "industrial production still seemed to be on a downward trend." "capacity utilization in manufacturing fell again in October and remained substantially below its long-run average." Staff forecast: "expansion of economic activity would be relatively muted over the near term."
- Bright-line check: "substantially below its long-run average" (capacity utilization), "below the economy's potential" (from staff forecast context about underutilized resources). Score MUST be < 50.
- Magnitude: "sluggish," "doldrums," "substantially below" — this is meaningful slack. Not catastrophic (it's a recovery, not a deep recession), but clearly below potential with no sign of closing. Score range: 30-35.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE/slack.
- Evidence: "Private nonfarm payroll employment remained stagnant in the third quarter and edged down in October and November." "Job losses in manufacturing were again large." "The unemployment rate rose to 6 percent in November." "slack in labor markets" (explicitly stated re: wage growth).
- UE at 6% maps to score range 31-45 per rubric (UE 5.5-7% range).
- Magnitude: Stagnant employment, rising UE to 6%, explicit "slack in labor markets" — moderate slack. Score range: 30-35.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence says slack. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
