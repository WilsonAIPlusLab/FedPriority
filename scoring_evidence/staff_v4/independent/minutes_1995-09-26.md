### Era Context
Mid-1995: The US economy is in mid-expansion. The Fed had tightened in 1994-early 1995, then paused. Growth had slowed in early-mid 1995 but was rebounding. Unemployment around 5.6%, which was near estimates of NAIRU at the time. Inflation had moderated after elevated readings early in the year.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint — moderating. Key staff language: "consumer and producer prices had risen more slowly in recent months" after "elevated rates in the early part of the year." "Consumer prices rose more slowly in August than in the early months of the year." Core (nonfood, non-energy) CPI over 12 months was essentially unchanged from a year earlier. PPI of finished goods "edged lower in August." Staff forecast: "price inflation likely would not deviate significantly from recent trends."
- This is a picture of contained, modestly decelerating inflation. In the mid-1990s, CPI was running around 2.5-3%. The staff sees moderation but no significant further decline.
- Score direction: Slightly below midpoint. The staff acknowledges some price pressures earlier in the year have moderated, with prices "rising more slowly." Not deflation risk, not elevated — contained but not at target.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation is moderating and contained. Score should be below 50 but not dramatically so. Score: 42 (contained, modestly below what would be concerning).

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy "expanding at a moderate rate." Industrial production "moderately above its average for the second quarter" but capacity utilization "remained below the average rate for the first quarter." Staff forecast says growth would be "higher than the weak pace of the second quarter" but moderate. The staff notes "prospects for some further easing of pressures on labor and other resources."
- "Easing of pressures on resources" suggests the economy is near or modestly above potential but the gap is closing/narrowing. Capacity utilization still above historical average (per participant section line 346, but this is participant content). The staff's own language about "further easing of pressures" implies currently somewhat above potential or near it.
- However, the staff forecast mentions "less robust labor market conditions" ahead and moderate growth. The Q2 was weak and recovery is moderate.
- Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language from staff. The staff says "moderate rate" of expansion. The staff forecast mentions "easing of pressures on labor and other resources" which implies some current pressure (slightly above or near potential).
- Score: 50 — near potential. The economy is expanding moderately, not overheating, not in slack. The staff forecasts easing of resource pressures, suggesting currently near or slightly above potential. Score: 51.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Unemployment at 5.6%, which in 1995 was roughly near NAIRU estimates (5.5-6%). Payroll employment "increased considerably in August after changing little in July." The unemployment rate "edged down to 5.6 percent in August, remaining in the narrow range that had prevailed since late 1994." Staff forecast mentions "less robust labor market conditions" ahead.
- At 5.6% unemployment with NAIRU around 5.5-6%, this is roughly near full employment for the era. Not tight by modern standards but near balanced for 1995.
- Score: 48-52 range. The labor market is near NAIRU, not particularly tight, not slack. Score: 50.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Consumer spending "appeared to be advancing somewhat further after a sizable gain in the second quarter." Housing "had strengthened." Business investment "remained on a solid uptrend." However, inventory adjustment is a drag. Capital goods shipments "fell appreciably in July" though backlogs remain large. Retail sales "up slightly on balance over July and August."
- Mixed but generally positive: consumer spending advancing, housing strengthening, business investment solid but capital goods orders declined steeply in July. Staff forecast: consumer spending to grow "at a pace generally in line with incomes," business investment to "slow from the very rapid pace of the past few years."
- Net: moderate, around trend. Some components strong (housing, business investment), some soft (retail sales slightly up, capital goods orders down). Score: 50-52. Score: 51.
