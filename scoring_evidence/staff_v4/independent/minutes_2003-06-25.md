# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — June 25, 2003 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff views and participant/member views are mixed together. I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by cues such as "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," "the staff forecast prepared for this meeting."

Key staff-attributable passages:
- Paragraph starting "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested..." (line 17)
- Paragraphs on payrolls, industrial production, consumer outlays, housing, capital goods, inventories, trade, prices (lines 20-41) — these are factual staff review content
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." (line 56)
- Financial market developments paragraphs (lines 47-53) — reported by the Manager/staff

Participant/member content to IGNORE for scoring:
- "In the Committee's discussion..." passages
- "Members agreed..." / "members commented..." / "members believed..."
- Policy discussion paragraphs

### Era Context
Mid-2003: The U.S. economy was in the aftermath of the 2001 recession and the sluggish jobless recovery. The fed funds rate was at 1.25% (about to be cut to 1%). Deflation concerns were prominent. Unemployment was ~6.1%. This was a period of significant economic slack and very low inflation.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint.
- "Consumer price inflation remained at a very low level" (line 17)
- "core consumer price inflation eased noticeably" on a year-over-year basis (line 41)
- "Sharp declines in energy prices pulled down overall consumer prices" (line 41)
- Staff forecast: "ongoing slack in resource utilization, downward pressure on core price inflation was expected over the forecast period" (line 56)
- The balance of risks statement highlights "risk of an unwelcome substantial fall in inflation" and risk is "weighted toward the downside" for inflation (line 44, 113)

This is clearly low inflation with downside risk — disinflation territory. PCE forecasts of 1.25-1.5% for 2003 and 1-1.5% for 2004 (line 65), well below 2%. Core inflation easing noticeably. Staff expects further downward pressure. This fits the 11-25 range: meaningful deflation risk, well below target, "subdued," "very low."

Magnitude check: "very low level," "eased noticeably," "downward pressure expected" — these are strong below-target signals. Not quite deflation-spiral territory but meaningful deflation concern exists. Score: ~20.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very low and declining inflation. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy BELOW potential.
- "the economy continued to expand at a subpar pace" (line 17)
- "Overall capacity utilization remained very low, with manufacturing utilization near a twenty-year low" (line 23)
- Staff forecast: "ongoing slack in resource utilization" (line 56)
- "currently large margins of unemployed labor and other resources" (line 92 — this is Committee language echoing staff assessment of slack)

Bright-line check: Clear "below potential" / "slack remains" language. Score MUST be < 50. The slack is described as large and persistent with capacity utilization near 20-year lows. This is significant slack, not mild.

Magnitude: "subpar pace," "very low" capacity utilization, "near twenty-year low" in manufacturing — these are strong signals of significant slack. Not catastrophic (GDP still expanding, not contracting), but substantial below-potential operation. Score: ~30.

Anchor check: 2003 is in the sluggish recovery period. 30 fits between GFC trough (15-22) and 2010-14 recovery (35-42). The economy was expanding but at subpar pace with very large slack. 30 is appropriate.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE (below midpoint).
- "Private nonfarm payrolls changed little on balance in April and May after declines earlier in the year" (line 20)
- "employment in manufacturing continued to fall" (line 20)
- "Unemployment edged up further in May, to 6.1 percent" (line 20)
- "the number of both short-term job losers and longer-term unemployed increased" (line 20)
- "Initial claims for unemployment insurance remained high" (line 20)

Unemployment at 6.1% and rising, with payrolls flat after declines. Per the rubric, UE > 7% = 16-30, UE 5.5-7% = 31-45. At 6.1% with rising unemployment and high claims, this is in the 31-45 range but toward the lower end given the clearly deteriorating trend. Score: ~30.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/slack labor market. Score below 50. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend (mixed but net weak).
- Consumer spending: "increased moderately" (line 17), "Real consumer outlays, excluding cars and trucks, were flat in April but turned up in May" (line 26). "fundamentals underlying household spending became more favorable" — mixed.
- Housing: "held at a high level" (line 17), "reasonably well maintained" (line 29) — positive.
- Business investment: "still soft" (line 17), "orders and shipments of nondefense capital goods were lackluster" (line 32), "real outlays on equipment and software... first-quarter decline... more than reversed the fourth-quarter gains" (line 32), "Real investment in nonresidential structures dropped further" (line 32). Business investment is clearly weak.
- Government: "grew substantially" (line 17) — positive but not private sector.

Net assessment: Consumer spending moderate, housing strong, but business investment weak and described as the critical drag. The staff sees the expansion as "subpar" overall. "Sluggish" and "soft" language for investment. Score: ~35.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence is below-trend mixed with weakness. Score below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation remained at a very low level." "On a year-over-year basis, core consumer price inflation eased noticeably." Staff forecast: "ongoing slack in resource utilization, downward pressure on core price inflation was expected over the forecast period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes inflation as very low, declining, with further downward pressure expected — placing this firmly in the meaningful deflation-risk zone.
**Direction confirmation**: Inflation pressure is well below neutral because inflation is very low, declining, and staff expects further disinflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The economy continued to expand at a subpar pace in recent months." "Overall capacity utilization remained very low, with manufacturing utilization near a twenty-year low." Staff forecast: "ongoing slack in resource utilization."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy expanding but at subpar pace with very large slack and capacity utilization near multi-decade lows.
**Direction confirmation**: Output gap is well below neutral because the economy operates significantly below potential with large slack. Bright-line satisfied: "below potential" language, score < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Private nonfarm payrolls changed little on balance in April and May after declines earlier in the year." "Unemployment edged up further in May, to 6.1 percent, and the number of both short-term job losers and longer-term unemployed increased as well. Initial claims for unemployment insurance remained high."
**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 6.1% and rising with flat payrolls and high claims indicates significant labor market slack.
**Direction confirmation**: Labor tightness is well below neutral because unemployment is high and rising with no meaningful job creation.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
