# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2007-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary), marked as **unsegmented pre-2009** format. Staff and participant content are intermixed. I must carefully identify staff-attributable language and separate it from participant/member discussion.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following paragraphs contain staff-attributable content (data reviews, staff forecasts):
- Paragraphs on economic data: "The information reviewed at the March meeting indicated..." (standard staff review language)
- Employment, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, inventories, trade, international, prices — all staff data reviews
- Financial market developments paragraph beginning "The FOMC's decision at its January meeting..."
- Domestic nonfinancial sector debt paragraph
- Staff forecast paragraph: "In its forecast prepared for this meeting, the staff marked down..."

Participant content (to IGNORE for scoring): Paragraphs beginning with "In their discussion...", "Participants reported...", "Many participants...", "Most participants continued to expect...", "In the Committee's discussion..." etc.

### Era Context
March 2007: Pre-GFC period. Housing downturn underway, subprime concerns emerging, economy still growing but decelerating. Fed funds at 5.25%. Core inflation elevated. This is a transitional period — housing weakness is evident but broader economy still expanding modestly.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as above midpoint. Evidence: "the twelve-month increase in core consumer prices remained elevated relative to its pace one year earlier." Core CPI inflation "remained elevated." "core CPI inflation slowed slightly in February but remained elevated." PCE excluding food and energy "rose at a faster pace than in the previous two months." Average hourly earnings "rose at a noticeably faster pace." Staff forecast: "the staff continued to expect that core PCE inflation would edge down over the remainder of this year and next" — but current levels are elevated.
Direction: Above neutral. "Elevated" = roughly 10-15 points above midpoint. Score ~60-65.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation elevated. Score above 50 — correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as modestly below potential and decelerating. "the economy appeared to be expanding at a modest pace in the first quarter." Staff forecast: "real GDP growth was expected to pick up to a rate a little below that of the economy's long-run potential." The staff "marked down the projected increase in real GDP."
Bright-line: "a rate a little below that of the economy's long-run potential" = output gap slightly negative = below 50.
Direction: Below midpoint but not far. Score ~44-47.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff data: Unemployment rate at 4.5% in February (edged down from 4.6%). "Employment gains moderated in early 2007." Construction employment "contracted considerably." But "hiring in service-producing sectors remained solid."
UE at 4.5% is below the typical NAIRU estimate for this era (~5%). Per the rubric, UE 3.8-4.5% maps to 53-65 range. Despite some moderation in hiring, the level remains tight.
Direction: Above midpoint. Score ~55-58.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending: "Real consumer spending appeared on track to rise at a robust pace in the first quarter" — but partly weather/special factors. Business investment: "Business fixed investment had been sluggish in recent months. Real spending on equipment and software fell in the fourth quarter." "nominal orders for and shipments of nondefense capital goods excluding aircraft posted widespread declines in January."
Net: Consumer spending robust but business investment clearly weak. The staff explicitly notes the weakness in investment dragged down the GDP forecast. This is a mixed bag — robust consumption offset by weak investment.
Direction: Roughly at or slightly below midpoint. Score ~42-45.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not directly discussed by staff in a substantive way. The staff forecast doesn't provide productivity-specific assessment. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff reports: "funding in the bond and syndicated loan markets appeared to remain readily available." However, "Financial market volatility increased sharply in the second half of the intermeeting period amid an apparent pullback from risk-taking." Yields on Treasuries fell 30-40 basis points. "yields on speculative-grade bonds declined only modestly, leaving risk spreads noticeably wider, albeit still narrow by historical standards." Domestic nonfinancial sector debt "continuing to rise at a relatively brisk rate."
Overall: Funding still readily available, but some tightening at the margin (wider spec-grade spreads, volatility). Conditions still broadly accommodative but with emerging stress signals.
Bright-line: "readily available" funding suggests conditions are not tight. Spreads wider but "still narrow by historical standards." This is still in the loose-to-neutral range.
Direction: Below midpoint (accommodative) but moving toward neutral. Score ~38-42.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff notes yields fell 30-40 basis points. This is a yield movement, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, auction issues. The yield decline reflected flight-to-quality during volatility. Normal market functioning.
Direction: Normal. Score ~40-45. No dysfunction described.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff reports: "risk spreads noticeably wider, albeit still narrow by historical standards." Investment-grade spreads fell in line with Treasuries. Spec-grade spreads widened.
Bright-line: "still narrow by historical standards" = below normal = below 50. But "noticeably wider" suggests movement toward normal.
Direction: Below midpoint but rising. Score ~35-40.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff notes: "Broad stock price indexes dropped several percent on net over the period." "recent declines in equity prices." No valuation commentary from staff.
Per rubric: "Equities declined" alone -> 50-55. Score ~52.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff discusses subprime mortgage issues: "a tightening of standards for subprime borrowers in recent weeks seemed likely to restrain home sales." "increased strains in the subprime mortgage sector." "mounting concerns about the subprime mortgage sector." But the broader system: funding still "readily available," bond and syndicated loan markets functioning.
Bright-line: Emerging stress in subprime but broader system still functioning. This is in the 45-50 range — concerns building but system still sound overall.
Direction: Roughly at or slightly above neutral. Score ~48-52.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Not substantively discussed by staff. The trade deficit narrowed but that's trade data, not FX commentary. No direct discussion of dollar direction. N/A.

This is the typical lag between output slowdown and labor market response. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (45) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43)**: Close together (2 points). Coherent.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because staff explicitly says spreads are "still narrow by historical standards." Bright-line rule: "narrow" -> score < 50. Satisfied.
