# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2017-05-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with separate "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: May 2017. The economy is in the mature phase of the post-GFC recovery. Unemployment has fallen to 4.5%, near or modestly below estimates of the natural rate. Inflation running somewhat below 2%. The Fed raised rates in March 2017 and is contemplating balance sheet normalization. Financial conditions are generally accommodative. This is the 2016-2017 neutral-period anchor era.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as slightly below midpoint. Total PCE at 1.75% over 12 months, core PCE at ~1.5% -- both below the 2% target. March prices actually declined on a month-over-month basis. Staff projects inflation "marginally below" 2% even by 2019. The softness in March was noted but deemed "unlikely to be repeated." Score should be in the 36-42 range (below target, modest disinflation but not alarming).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff projects real GDP will "expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." The unemployment rate is projected to "run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This is explicit "above potential" language. BRIGHT-LINE: must be > 50. However, Q1 GDP was weak (attributed to transitory factors), so the economy is only modestly above potential. Score: 52-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 4.5%. Staff projects it to decline "gradually" and run "somewhat below" the natural rate. Part-time for economic reasons declined. Initial claims at "very low level." Compensation "accelerated modestly" -- ECI at 2.25%, AHE at 2.75%. These are moderate wage pressures at an unemployment rate suggesting near or slightly below full employment. Anchor for 2016-17 is ~50. With UE at 4.5% and modest wage acceleration, score: 52-55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Real PCE "rose only modestly in the first quarter" -- weak consumer spending, though improving late in quarter. However, residential investment increased at a "brisk pace." Business equipment and IP increased at a "solid pace." Nonresidential structures increased "briskly." Business inventory investment "slowed sharply" and "held down real GDP growth significantly." Government purchases declined. Net: consumer spending weak but expected to rebound; business investment solid to brisk. Overall Q1 GDP was soft but underlying demand ex-inventories was decent. Score: 48-52 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative." Financing conditions for large nonfinancial firms "stayed accommodative." Consumer credit markets "remained accommodative." BRIGHT-LINE: "accommodative" must be < 50. But this is the 2017 neutral anchor period (46-54). The conditions are described as accommodative but in a normal-era way, not excessively loose. Risky asset prices rose, Treasury yields declined, dollar depreciated. Score: 38-42 (somewhat loose -- "generally accommodative" is clear language).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined 20-35 basis points. This is normal price movement driven by policy expectations and economic data. No mention of liquidity issues, dysfunction, or market stress. Short-term funding markets "stable." The yield movement is orderly. Score: 40-45 (normal functioning, no stress).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds "narrowed a bit on net." CMBS spreads "remained near the lower end of the range seen since the financial crisis." Corporate bond issuance and leveraged loans "remained strong." BRIGHT-LINE: "narrowed" = compressed = must be < 50. Score: 30-35 (below normal, favorable credit conditions).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity indexes "increased slightly." Staff financial stability report notes "asset valuation pressures in some markets were notable" and "vulnerabilities appeared to have increased for asset valuation pressures, though not by enough to warrant raising the assessment of these vulnerabilities to elevated." This suggests staff sees valuations as somewhat elevated/concerning. VIX rose mid-period but ended slightly lower. Score: 32-38 (modestly above fair value, some concern about elevated valuations but not extreme).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff financial stability assessment: "leverage as well as vulnerabilities from maturity and liquidity transformation in the financial sector were low." Financial system vulnerabilities "moderate on balance." CRE loan standards being tightened. Banks reported tightening standards on auto loans. Some softness in C&I lending demand. BRIGHT-LINE: "low" vulnerabilities in financial sector = low stress. But there are some pockets of concern (CRE tightening, some credit quality issues). Score: 32-38 (healthy system with minor watchpoints).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated" over the intermeeting period. Dollar depreciated against both AFE and EME currencies. Staff forecast was revised partly due to "a somewhat lower assumed path for the exchange value of the dollar." BRIGHT-LINE: depreciation = < 50. Score: 35-40 (modest depreciation).

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Some information available. PCE declined in March partly reflecting "declines in some categories of prices that appeared unlikely to be repeated." Motor vehicle sales somewhat soft. Non-energy import prices turning up. Score: 38-42 (slightly below stable, soft goods prices in recent data).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately broken out enough to score. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment brisk, home sales up, but no specific shelter/rent inflation data from staff. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "little changed on balance." Michigan longer-run median "edged down a bit." Dealer and market participant surveys "little changed." Far-term TIPS inflation compensation "little changed on net." Near-term TIPS declined partly due to soft March CPI. Expectations appear well-anchored near target, maybe very slightly soft. Score: 46-48 (well-anchored, tiny hint of softness).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 39 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core PCE price inflation...was about 1 1/2 percent over those same 12 months." "The staff continued to project that inflation would increase gradually in 2018 and 2019 and that it would be marginally below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Core PCE at 1.5% is meaningfully below the 2% target, and staff projects inflation remaining below target through 2019, placing this firmly in the below-target range.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below the 2% target with no expectation of reaching it in the forecast horizon.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." "The unemployment rate was projected to...run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly sees the economy operating modestly above potential with the output gap expected to widen slightly over the forecast horizon. Q1 weakness was transitory.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff projects growth above potential and unemployment below the natural rate. Bright-line satisfied (above potential -> > 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate decreased to 4.5 percent in March." "Measures of labor compensation accelerated modestly...the employment cost index for private workers increased 2 1/4 percent...average hourly earnings...increased 2 3/4 percent...both increases were somewhat larger than those over the 12 months ending in March 2016."
**Center-of-gravity**: At 4.5% unemployment, the labor market is near or slightly below the natural rate. Wage growth is modest but accelerating. The market is tight but not extremely so -- consistent with the 2016-17 anchor of ~50, nudged up slightly by the declining UE and wage acceleration.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is slightly above neutral because unemployment is at/slightly below the natural rate with modest wage pressures building.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose only modestly in the first quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property increased at a solid pace...Real business expenditures for nonresidential structures increased briskly." "Business inventory investment slowed sharply last quarter and held down real GDP growth significantly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending was soft in Q1 (transitory) while business investment was solid to brisk. Residential investment was also brisk. The inventory drag was large but temporary. Net spending picture is roughly at trend when adjusting for transitory weakness.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is approximately at neutral -- consumer weakness offset by strong business and residential investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff review.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative over the intermeeting period." "Financing conditions for large nonfinancial firms stayed accommodative." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets remained accommodative, on balance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff uses "accommodative" three times across different sectors, placing conditions clearly on the loose side. However, this is normal-era accommodation (2017), not extreme pre-crisis looseness.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on intermediate- and longer-term nominal Treasury securities decreased 20 to 35 basis points over the intermeeting period." "Conditions in short-term funding markets were stable over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yield movements were orderly, driven by policy expectations and economic data. No dysfunction, liquidity issues, or market stress noted. Short-term funding was stable.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral -- normal market functioning with no signs of stress.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on investment- and speculative-grade nonfinancial corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed a bit on net." "CMBS spreads...remained near the lower end of the range seen since the financial crisis." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds and leveraged loans remained strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are narrowing and near post-crisis lows. Issuance is robust. Credit conditions are clearly favorable and below normal spread levels.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are compressed and narrowing. Bright-line satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased slightly." Staff financial stability assessment noted "asset valuation pressures in some markets were notable" and "vulnerabilities appeared to have increased for asset valuation pressures."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equity prices rose modestly and the staff flagged elevated valuation pressures as notable. This suggests equities are somewhat above fair value (low on the stress scale = overvaluation concern territory).
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (overvaluation side) because staff sees notable valuation pressures -- the market is elevated, not depressed.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Leverage as well as vulnerabilities from maturity and liquidity transformation in the financial sector were low." Overall financial vulnerabilities characterized as "moderate on balance." CRE lending standards tightened; some softness in C&I loan demand.
**Center-of-gravity**: The financial sector is healthy with low leverage and low maturity/liquidity transformation risk. Some pockets of caution (CRE standards tightening) but no systemic concerns.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the financial sector has low leverage and low vulnerabilities. Bright-line satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar depreciated." "A modest depreciation of the dollar against both the AFE and EME currencies." Staff forecast revised partly due to "a somewhat lower assumed path for the exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: The dollar depreciated modestly over the intermeeting period. Staff incorporated a lower dollar path into the forecast.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line satisfied.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Both the PCE price index and the CPI decreased in March, partly reflecting declines in some categories of prices that appeared unlikely to be repeated." Staff notes upturn in "non-energy imports" prices expected.
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices declined in the most recent month, though this was seen as transitory. Underlying goods inflation is soft but not deeply negative.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed with enough specificity to score.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
No specific shelter/rent inflation data from staff.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of inflation expectations were little changed on balance." "The median of longer-run inflation expectations from the Michigan survey edged down a bit, on balance." "Far-term inflation compensation was little changed on net."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are well-anchored near target with a very slight hint of softness (Michigan edging down). Essentially stable.
