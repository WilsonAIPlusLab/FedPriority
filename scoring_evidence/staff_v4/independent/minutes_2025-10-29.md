# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2025-10-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Participant content ("Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook") appears later and will be ignored for scoring purposes.

### Era Context
October 2025. The U.S. is navigating the aftermath of tariff increases implemented earlier in the year. A federal government shutdown is disrupting data availability. GDP growth has moderated from 2024 but remains positive. Inflation is somewhat elevated above 2%. The labor market is cooling gradually. The Fed has presumably been easing policy rates (foreign central banks are cutting too). This is a late-cycle moderation period with tariff-related uncertainty.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint. Total PCE inflation estimated at 2.8% in September (up 0.5 pp relative to a year ago). Core PCE also at 2.8%. Staff outlook: tariff increases expected to put upward pressure on inflation in 2025-2026, with inflation projected to return to disinflationary trend thereafter. Risks to inflation forecast "skewed to the upside" due to elevated expected inflation and four consecutive years above 2%. This is clearly above target — "somewhat elevated" language plus 2.8% readings. Score range: 58-63. Direction: Above midpoint because PCE at 2.8%, up from year ago, tariff upward pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. The staff outlook explicitly states "GDP growth after 2025 was expected to remain above potential until 2028." GDP posted a strong gain in Q2, moderated over H1 but PDFP rose faster than GDP. PDFP growth "continued at a solid pace in the third quarter." Staff projects GDP growth "modestly stronger, on balance, through 2028." BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language requires score > 50. Score: 53-56. The economy is above potential but moderating — not running hot, just modestly above.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW the tighter end but still relatively firm. "Continued gradual cooling in the labor market without any evidence of a sharp deterioration." Unemployment rate "edged up but remained low through August." Job gains slowed. Staff expects unemployment to "decline gradually after this year before flattening out at a level slightly below the staff's estimate of the natural rate." So unemployment is near the natural rate and expected to end slightly below it. This is near-NAIRU territory. Score: 48-52. The cooling is noted but unemployment remains low and near natural rate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as AT or modestly ABOVE trend. "PDFP growth continued at a solid pace in the third quarter." Real GDP posted strong Q2 gain though H1 average was slower than 2024. PDFP rose faster than GDP. The word "solid" anchors this in the 46-54 range, possibly slightly above. Score: 52-55.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff outlook mentions "stronger expected potential output growth" driving the upward revision to GDP forecast. The participants section mentions AI and productivity-enhancing technologies but that's participant views. The staff's stronger potential output growth projection implicitly suggests improved productivity assumptions. Limited direct staff discussion. Score: 55-58 with low confidence given indirect evidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes conditions as mixed. Borrowing costs "significantly lower than the highs observed in 2023 but elevated relative to their average post-Global Financial Crisis levels." Credit "generally available but relatively tight for small businesses." Bank lending standards easing on C&I loans. Financial conditions projected to become "a tailwind for spending" (from the outlook). Conditions were "somewhat supportive" per the outlook revision. The staff outlook says financial conditions are becoming supportive/providing greater support. This suggests moving toward accommodative. BRIGHT-LINE: If conditions are becoming supportive/accommodative, score should be < 50 or near 50. But borrowing costs still elevated vs post-GFC. Net assessment: slightly tight but easing. Score: 50-54.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Nominal Treasury yields were little changed on net." At short maturities, real yields rose somewhat, at longer maturities real yields and inflation compensation little changed. No mention of market dysfunction. Funding markets "tightened materially" but "remained orderly." EFFR-IORB spread narrowed; SRF take-up occurred on several days; ON RRP usage at lowest since 2021 — "these developments suggested that reserve balances were moving closer to ample levels." This describes reserve scarcity dynamics, not Treasury market dysfunction per se. The funding market tightening is notable but described as orderly. Score: 48-52. Normal functioning with some reserve-related tightening but orderly.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Credit spreads were little changed, on net, and remained very low by historical standards." BRIGHT-LINE: "very low" / historically compressed spreads → score MUST be < 50. Score: 18-25. Spreads are very compressed.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff financial stability assessment: "asset valuation pressures were elevated. For public equities, price-to-earnings ratios stood at the upper end of their historical distribution." "Broad equity price indexes increased moderately" over the intermeeting period. "High and broad-based investor demand for risky assets." This is overvaluation concern territory. Score: 20-28 (lower = more overvaluation concern). Staff sees elevated valuations — this is the "stretched" / "elevated" range.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff stability assessment: "banks remained resilient, with high regulatory capital ratios and improved funding structure, although their market-adjusted capital ratios remained depressed and sensitive to long-term interest rates." Also: hedge fund leverage "elevated and increased further." Credit quality "generally stable at levels somewhat weaker than during the pre-pandemic period." Delinquencies on small business loans "moderately above pre-pandemic levels." CMBS delinquencies "remained elevated." Mixed picture: banks themselves resilient but some stress indicators. BRIGHT-LINE: "resilient" / "high regulatory capital ratios" suggests below 50 for banking stress, but market-adjusted capital ratios depressed and credit quality somewhat weaker. Score: 38-44. Banks are fundamentally sound but with pockets of concern.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index increased modestly, primarily driven by the relative strength of U.S. economic data." BRIGHT-LINE: Dollar appreciation → score > 50. Score: 55-58. Modest appreciation.

Staff mentions "consumer food prices" and "energy prices" in the context of being excluded from core. Limited direct staff discussion of goods inflation specifically. The staff outlook notes "tariff increases expected to put upward pressure on inflation." Score: 58 with moderate confidence — tariffs are pushing goods prices up.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not directly broken out in staff sections. N/A.

Risks skewed to upside partly because of inflation expectations. This suggests some concern about expectations moving up. Score: 55-60.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation...was estimated to have been 2.8 percent in September"; "core PCE price inflation...was also estimated to have been 2.8 percent in September"; "total PCE inflation had risen 0.5 percentage point relative to a year ago"; staff outlook: "tariff increases expected to put upward pressure on inflation in 2025 and 2026"; inflation risks "skewed to the upside."
**Center of gravity**: PCE at 2.8% is meaningfully above the 2% target, with upward tariff pressures and upside risks, though staff projects eventual return to disinflationary trend.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE at 2.8% is well above target with upside risks.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "GDP growth after 2025 was expected to remain above potential until 2028"; "Real GDP posted a strong gain in the second quarter"; "PDFP growth continued at a solid pace in the third quarter"; "real GDP growth was projected to be modestly stronger, on balance, through 2028."
**Center of gravity**: Economy modestly above potential, with staff explicitly projecting above-potential growth through 2028, though the pace has moderated from 2024.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly states output is "above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "continued gradual cooling in the labor market without any evidence of a sharp deterioration"; "unemployment rate was expected to decline gradually after this year before flattening out at a level slightly below the staff's estimate of the natural rate."
**Center of gravity**: Labor market cooling but not weak. Unemployment near natural rate. Staff expects it to settle slightly below natural rate — essentially at full employment equilibrium.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at neutral because the labor market is near the natural rate, cooling gradually but not slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "PDFP growth continued at a solid pace in the third quarter"; "Real GDP posted a strong gain in the second quarter, although the average increase over the first half of the year was slower than the average pace seen over 2024"; staff forecast: "real GDP growth was projected to be modestly stronger, on balance, through 2028."
**Center of gravity**: Spending at a solid pace — at or modestly above trend. "Solid" in FOMC language typically connotes steady and adequate, not booming.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral — "solid pace" with modestly stronger growth projections.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 56 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: Staff outlook: "reflecting stronger expected potential output growth" as the primary driver of upward GDP revision. This implicitly suggests higher productivity assumptions (potential output growth = labor force growth + productivity growth).
**Center of gravity**: Staff revised up potential output growth, suggesting above-trend productivity assumptions, but the dimension receives limited direct discussion.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly above neutral because staff revised up potential output growth expectations.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "borrowing costs of businesses, households, and municipalities remained significantly lower than the highs observed in 2023 but elevated relative to their average post-Global Financial Crisis levels"; "Credit remained generally available but relatively tight for small businesses"; SLOOS: "easing in bank lending conditions on C&I loans"; staff outlook: "greater projected support from financial conditions" and financial conditions "becoming a tailwind for spending."
**Center of gravity**: Conditions are in transition — still somewhat elevated borrowing costs but easing, with the staff projecting them as increasingly supportive. Net position is near neutral, slightly on the tight side given still-elevated borrowing costs.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is near neutral — tight by post-GFC standards but easing and projected to become supportive.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 50 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "nominal Treasury yields were little changed on net"; short-term funding markets "tightened materially over the intermeeting period but remained orderly"; "reserve balances were moving closer to ample levels."
**Center of gravity**: No dysfunction in Treasury markets. Funding market tightening is a reserve-scarcity dynamic, described as orderly. Normal market functioning.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at neutral — no dysfunction described, just normal yield and reserve dynamics.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Credit spreads were little changed, on net, and remained very low by historical standards"; "Issuance of corporate bonds, leveraged loans, and private credit was robust."
**Center of gravity**: Spreads very compressed with robust issuance — well below normal levels, reflecting strong risk appetite.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are "very low by historical standards." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 24 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "asset valuation pressures were elevated. For public equities, price-to-earnings ratios stood at the upper end of their historical distribution"; "high and broad-based investor demand for risky assets"; "Broad equity price indexes increased moderately, boosted by technology firms with positive earnings news and AI-related investor optimism."
**Center of gravity**: Staff explicitly identifies elevated valuations with P/E ratios at upper end of historical distribution — overvaluation concern territory.

### BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral because banks are "resilient" with "high regulatory capital ratios" but some stress pockets pull the score toward neutral. Bright-line satisfied: "resilient" → below 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad dollar index increased modestly, primarily driven by the relative strength of U.S. economic data."
**Center of gravity**: Modest dollar appreciation driven by relative U.S. economic strength.
