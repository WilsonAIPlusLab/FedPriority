# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-06-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. The staff content is clearly separated into three labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
June 2010 — roughly one year after the official end of the Great Recession. The economy is in early-to-moderate recovery. Unemployment is still very high (~9.7%), the output gap is large, and inflation is subdued. The European sovereign debt crisis is intensifying, creating spillover financial stress. The fed funds rate is at 0-0.25%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation remained low," "core consumer price index rose only slightly," "year-over-year change in the index was lower than earlier this year," "core goods prices continued to decline," "prices of non-energy services remained soft," "core inflation was expected to continue at a subdued rate." Falling energy prices held down headline. Unit labor costs "continued to be restrained." The staff forecast was revised down slightly. This is clearly below-target, subdued inflation consistent with the 26-40 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation is low/subdued. Score should be well below 50. Confirmed.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Evidence: "moderate pace" of recovery, "anticipated decline in the unemployment rate was somewhat slower," "existing margins of economic slack," "greater amount of economic slack in the forecast," capacity utilization "still substantially below its longer-run average." The recovery is proceeding but slack persists and is large.
- Bright-line check: "margins of economic slack" → score MUST be < 50. Confirmed.
- Anchor check: 2010 recovery period anchor is 35-42. The economy is recovering ("moderate recovery") but slack remains substantial. Score ~35.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees the labor market as VERY LOOSE. Evidence: Unemployment at 9.7%, "remained elevated." Private employment posted "only a small increase" in May (after sizable gains in March-April). Initial claims "remaining at a still-elevated level." Proportion of long-term unemployed "continued to move up." Workers employed part-time for economic reasons "leveled off" but at high levels.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 9.7% is extremely loose → score must be very low. Anchor for 2009-10 is 15-22. Score ~20.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending: "rise in consumer spending slowed in recent months after a brisk increase in the first quarter," "nominal sales of non-auto consumer goods and food services were little changed." Equipment & software: "increased further," "brisk pace," "broadly based" increases. Nonresidential construction: "contracting further." Housing: starts "declined sharply in May." Net assessment: business equipment strong, consumer spending moderating, housing and nonresidential construction weak.
- Score: Mixed signals net to below trend overall. The strength in E&S is offset by weak housing, weak nonresidential construction, and slowing consumption. Around 38-42.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff mentions productivity directly: "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector, which rose rapidly in 2009, posted a more moderate but still-solid gain in the first quarter of 2010." Also: "further gains in productivity" restraining unit labor costs.
- Higher = more productivity growth. "Still-solid gain" after rapid 2009 increases → above historical trend but moderating. Score ~55.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff describes deteriorating conditions during the intermeeting period. Funding market conditions "deteriorated somewhat, particularly for European financial institutions." Libor-OIS spreads "widened noticeably." Credit terms "remained tight relative to those at the end of 2006" though some loosening noted. Equity prices fell. Corporate bond spreads widened. Consumer credit contracting. Bank credit declining. Commercial paper for European institutions saw reduced demand. Dollar liquidity swap lines had to be reestablished. Overall: conditions are tighter than neutral, reflecting European crisis spillovers.
- Bright-line check: "tight" / "deteriorated" → score MUST be > 50. The text says terms "remained tight relative to those at the end of 2006" and conditions "deteriorated somewhat." Score in the 58-65 range.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields fell considerably due to flight-to-quality flows. "2- and 10-year nominal yields fell considerably." "Market participants pointed to flight-to-quality flows." Swap spreads widened only "a small" amount. There is NO mention of actual Treasury market dysfunction — no liquidity problems, no auction failures, no settlement issues. Yield movements were orderly and driven by demand (flight to quality = increased demand for Treasuries).
- Bright-line: Yield movements alone are NOT dysfunction. Score should be 36-50 range. The small widening of swap spreads is notable but minor. Score ~42.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Corporate bond spreads "widened, although they remained within the range prevailing since last summer." High-yield bond mutual funds saw "substantial net outflows." Credit card spreads "remained extremely high." Syndicated leveraged loan bid prices fell and bid-ask spreads widened. But investment-grade issuance "remained solid," credit quality "generally continued to improve," and S&P 500 profits "jumped substantially."
- Higher = more credit stress. Spreads are widening but still within recent ranges and well below crisis levels. The level is above normal but not severely elevated. Score ~58.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
"Broad U.S. stock price indexes fell over the intermeeting period." VIX spiked to "more than double its value" in mid-May but largely reversed. Equity risk premium "increased from its already elevated level." Financial firms underperformed. Staff is noting depressed equity prices and elevated risk premiums — suggesting equities are somewhat distressed/undervalued relative to fundamentals.
- Higher = more equity stress/concern. Prices fell, risk premium elevated. Score ~58-62.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Bank credit declining. Consumer credit contracting. Commercial real estate "stayed weak," delinquency rates on securitized commercial mortgages "continued to climb." European bank funding strains reemerging. Credit terms "remained tight relative to end of 2006." But credit quality of nonfinancial firms improving, and "loosening of terms" for some client classes noted.
- Higher = more stress. Domestic banking shows ongoing stress with declining credit, tight terms, rising CRE delinquencies, though some improvement at the margin. European spillovers add concern. Score ~60.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"Broad appreciation of the dollar" driven by flight-to-quality. "Foreign exchange value of the dollar" increased. "Dollar ended the intermeeting period up."
- Bright-line: Dollar appreciation → score MUST be > 50. Score ~60.

### Inflation Sub-components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: "Core goods prices continued to decline, on net." → Falling goods prices. Score ~25.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Prices of non-energy services remained soft." → Below stable. Score ~38.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices declined somewhat in recent months." Housing costs mentioned only in context of prices, not shelter/rent CPI specifically. Limited discussion. Score ~35 with low confidence.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Inflation expectations had remained stable." "Survey measures of both short- and long-term inflation expectations remained relatively stable." TIPS-based compensation decreased recently. Stable expectations but TIPS declining suggests some downside drift. Score ~43.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (20)**: Both below 50, coherent. Gap of 15 points is at the boundary — labor market is even weaker than output gap because unemployment remains very elevated (9.7%) even as production recovers. This reflects the "jobless recovery" dynamic. Acceptable.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Both below 50, within 5 points. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (58)**: Both above 50, co-moving in the same direction. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC elevated but Treasury stress low — this is consistent because the stress is in credit/funding markets, not Treasuries. Treasuries actually benefited from flight-to-quality. Acceptable divergence.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Goods ~25, Services ~38, Housing ~35, Expectations ~43. Weighted average roughly 33-35, parent at 28 — parent is slightly lower, but headline pulled down by energy which isn't captured in sub-dimensions. Reasonable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation remained low in April and May. The core consumer price index rose only slightly over the period, and the year-over-year change in the index was lower than earlier this year." "Core inflation was expected to continue at a subdued rate over the projection period."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is clearly below target with core prices barely rising, goods prices declining, and the staff projecting continued subdued rates — placing this solidly in the "below target; disinflation" range.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes inflation as low, subdued, and declining in year-over-year terms.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff continued to anticipate a moderate recovery... sufficient to reduce the existing margins of economic slack." Capacity utilization was "still substantially below its longer-run average."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is recovering at a moderate pace but substantial slack remains, consistent with below-potential output with a slowly closing gap.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "margins of economic slack" and capacity utilization well below average. Bright-line rule satisfied (slack → below 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate... dropped back in May to 9.7 percent." "Initial claims for unemployment insurance were little changed over the intermeeting period, remaining at a still-elevated level." "The proportion of unemployed workers who were jobless for more than 26 weeks continued to move up."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: With unemployment at 9.7%, rising long-term unemployment, and elevated initial claims, the labor market is extremely loose — consistent with deep slack far from full employment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because 9.7% unemployment represents enormous labor market slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The rise in consumer spending slowed in recent months after a brisk increase in the first quarter." "Real spending on equipment and software increased further early in the second quarter" at a "brisk pace." "Business outlays for nonresidential construction appeared to be contracting further." "Starts of new single-family homes... declined sharply in May."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Strong equipment spending is offset by slowing consumption, contracting nonresidential construction, and declining housing starts, netting to below-trend aggregate demand.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the net picture across spending categories is below trend despite pockets of strength.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 55 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector, which rose rapidly in 2009, posted a more moderate but still-solid gain in the first quarter of 2010."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Productivity growth is moderating from rapid 2009 gains but remains solid — above historical trend.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is above neutral because "still-solid gain" suggests above-trend productivity growth.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 62 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term funding markets deteriorated somewhat, particularly for European financial institutions. Spreads of the term... Libor over rates on overnight index swaps widened noticeably." "Dealers generally reported that the terms on which they provided credit remained tight relative to those at the end of 2006."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly tight, with deteriorating funding markets, widening Libor-OIS spreads, and credit terms still tight from the crisis, compounded by European sovereign debt spillovers.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because staff describes deterioration, widening spreads, and tight credit terms. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "2- and 10-year nominal yields fell considerably over the intermeeting period. Market participants pointed to flight-to-quality flows and greater concern about the economic outlook as factors boosting the demand for Treasury securities." "The drop in Treasury yields was accompanied by a small widening of swap spreads."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market functioning appears orderly — yield declines reflect strong demand from flight-to-quality, not dysfunction. The only minor stress indicator is a small swap spread widening.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near/slightly below neutral because yield movements are orderly and demand-driven, with no dysfunction indicators.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on corporate bonds widened, although they remained within the range prevailing since last summer." "Credit card... spreads... remained extremely high." "High-yield bond mutual funds recorded substantial net outflows."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit spreads are somewhat elevated and widening from the European crisis, but remain within recent ranges and well below crisis peaks. Credit quality is improving.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral (wider/more stress) because spreads widened and high-yield saw outflows. Bright-line rule satisfied ("widened" → above 50).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes fell over the intermeeting period." "The spread between the staff's estimate of the expected real return on equities over the next 10 years and an estimate of the expected real return on a 10-year Treasury note—a measure of the equity risk premium—increased from its already elevated level."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equity markets are moderately stressed with declining prices, spiking volatility, and an elevated/rising equity risk premium — suggesting concern but not panic.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress) because prices fell and risk premiums rose from already elevated levels.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Bank credit declined, on average, in April and May at about the same pace as in the first quarter." "The delinquency rate for securitized commercial mortgages continued to climb in May." "Strains in euro-area bank funding markets reemerged during the period."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system shows ongoing moderate stress from declining credit, rising CRE delinquencies, and European contagion risks, though credit quality improvements provide partial offset.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral (more stress) because credit is contracting, CRE delinquencies are rising, and European bank stress is spilling over.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "This retreat led to a broad appreciation of the dollar." "The intensifying concerns among investors... contributed to an increase in the foreign exchange value of the dollar." "The dollar ended the intermeeting period up."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar appreciated notably due to flight-to-quality flows driven by the European sovereign debt crisis.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied (appreciation → above 50).

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 25 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
Evidence: "Core goods prices continued to decline, on net." Declining goods prices indicate deflationary pressure in goods.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: 38 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
Evidence: "Prices of non-energy services remained soft." Soft but not declining — below stable but not deflationary.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: 35 ±10 [Low confidence]**
Evidence: "House prices declined somewhat in recent months." Direct shelter/rent CPI not discussed in detail; housing prices declining.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 ±5 [High confidence]**
Evidence: "Survey measures of both short- and long-term inflation expectations remained relatively stable." "Inflation compensation based on Treasury inflation-protected securities decreased recently." Stable surveys but declining market-based measures suggest slight below-target drift.
