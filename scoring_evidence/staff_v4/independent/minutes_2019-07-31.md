# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-07-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. The staff content is clearly separated into three sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant views are included.

### Era Context
Mid-2019: The U.S. economy is in the late stages of a long expansion. The unemployment rate is historically low (~3.7%), GDP growth is moderate but slowing, and inflation is running below the 2% target. Trade tensions with China and global growth concerns are prominent downside risks. The Fed is on the cusp of a rate cut (25bp expected at this meeting). Financial conditions are broadly supportive/accommodative.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation is 1.4% (12-month), core PCE is 1.6% — both clearly below the 2% target. The staff notes some Q2 acceleration vs Q1 (suggesting some transitory softness), and the Dallas trimmed mean is near 2%, but the headline numbers are clearly below target. Inflation expectations are "little changed" — stable but at "relatively low level." This maps to "below target; running below 2 percent" range (26-40). Score around 38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as slightly ABOVE the midpoint. GDP is "forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." The unemployment rate is "below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Labor market conditions are "judged to be tight." This is "above potential" language — bright-line rule requires score > 50. However, the language is modest: "a little above" and growth is slowing. Score around 52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as ABOVE the midpoint (tight). Unemployment is 3.7% — historically low. Part-time for economic reasons below late-2007 lows. Initial claims near historically low levels. Quits at high level. Staff explicitly says "labor market conditions judged to be tight." With UE at 3.7%, the rubric says score should be above 55. Wage growth is 3.1% AHE (moderate). Score around 62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED. Consumer spending "rose briskly" in Q2 after sluggish Q1, but showed deceleration going into Q3. Fundamentals are "supportive of solid real PCE growth." But residential investment "declined again." Nonresidential fixed investment "edged down." Business investment is soft with trade concerns weighing. Manufacturing output declined in Q2. The net: solid consumption offset by weak investment. This maps to roughly neutral or slightly below trend. Score around 45-47.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as BELOW the midpoint (loose/accommodative). "Financing conditions for businesses and households were little changed over the intermeeting period and remained generally supportive of spending." Financing conditions "remained accommodative" across nonfinancial businesses, CRE, residential mortgage, and consumer credit. Banks easing standards on C&I loans. Bright-line: "accommodative"/"supportive" = MUST be < 50. Score around 35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "little changed on net." The yield curve is flat (bottom decile since 1971), but that's not dysfunction — it's a signal about rate expectations. Short-term funding markets "remained fairly stable" though repo rates were "somewhat elevated" around quarter-end. The EFFR "remained well within the target range." No description of actual market dysfunction. Score around 42 (normal functioning with minor repo note).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff says "corporate credit spreads narrowed." Corporate bond issuance "remained robust." Leveraged loan issuance at "moderate pace." Banks easing standards on C&I loans with "aggressive competition." CRE yield spreads "ticked down." This is clearly below-normal stress. Bright-line: narrowed/compressed = MUST be < 50. Score around 32.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased, on net." VIX decreased slightly. Staff's financial stability section: "asset valuation pressures to be notable in a number of markets." This is the staff flagging elevated valuations, which maps to the lower end of the scale (more overvaluation concern). Score around 30-35.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff says "financial institutions were viewed as resilient" and "risks associated with financial leverage and funding risk were still viewed as low despite some signs of rising leverage." Banks are easing lending standards (competition-driven). However, staff flags high business leverage, record share of loans to risky borrowers in leveraged loan market, and continued private equity credit growth as vulnerabilities. Bright-line: "resilient" = MUST be < 50. Score around 35 (healthy but with some noted concerns about business leverage/leveraged loans).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "On balance, the broad dollar index ended the period modestly lower." Bright-line: depreciation = MUST be < 50. "Modestly lower" = modest weakness. Score around 42.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not specifically broken out. Consumer food prices below core inflation. Energy prices declined. Goods implied to be weak. Score around 30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out beyond CPI vs core. N/A.

Dealers/market participants "little changed." TIPS inflation compensation "moved up modestly." This is anchored but at a low level — slightly below the target anchor. Score around 43.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62)**: Gap of 9 points. Labor market tighter than output gap suggests, which is consistent with late-cycle dynamics where unemployment can undershoot even as GDP growth moderates. Acceptable.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (46)**: Gap of 7 points. Slight divergence because overall GDP is above potential but spending/investment mix is uneven (strong consumption, weak investment). Acceptable.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (32)**: Co-move in the loose direction. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: Normal period — no crisis, so these can diverge somewhat. Treasury functioning is fine, financial conditions are easy. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (~30) pulling down, expectations (~43) slightly below target. Consistent with headline 38.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer price inflation, as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), was below 2 percent in June." "Core PCE price inflation...moved down to 1.6 percent." "The trimmed mean measure of 12-month PCE price inflation...remained at or near 2 percent."
- Center-of-gravity: Core PCE at 1.6% is clearly below the 2% target, though the trimmed mean near 2% and Q2 acceleration provide slight upward offset.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is BELOW neutral because both headline (1.4%) and core (1.6%) PCE are well below the 2% target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." "The unemployment rate was projected to...remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy operating slightly above potential with a tight labor market, but growth is moderating and risks are tilted to the downside.
- Direction: Output gap is ABOVE neutral because staff explicitly says growth is "a little above" potential. Bright-line satisfied: "above potential" = score > 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate edged up to 3.7 percent in June but was still at a historically low level." "With labor market conditions judged to be tight..." "Average hourly earnings for all employees rose 3.1 percent over the 12 months ending in June, somewhat faster than a year earlier."
- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment at 3.7% is well below NAIRU, job openings high, claims near historic lows, and staff explicitly calls conditions "tight." Wage growth moderate at 3.1%.
- Direction: Labor tightness is well ABOVE neutral given 3.7% unemployment and explicit "tight" characterization.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real consumer expenditures rose briskly in the second quarter" but "Real residential investment declined again in the second quarter" and "Real nonresidential private fixed investment edged down in the second quarter." "Manufacturing output declined."
- Center-of-gravity: Strong consumer spending is offset by weakness in business investment, residential investment, and manufacturing — netting out to slightly below trend.
- Direction: Consumption/investment is slightly BELOW neutral because investment weakness partially offsets strong consumption.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

- Center-of-gravity: Broadly accommodative conditions across all sectors — business, CRE, mortgage, and consumer credit. Banks actively easing standards due to competition.
- Direction: Financial conditions are BELOW neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative" and "supportive." Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

- Evidence: "The nominal U.S. Treasury yield curve was little changed, on net." "Conditions in domestic short-term funding markets remained fairly stable." Repo rates "somewhat elevated" around quarter-end but EFFR "remained well within the target range."
- Center-of-gravity: Normal market functioning. Minor repo rate elevation around quarter-end is transient and not indicative of dysfunction.
- Direction: Treasury stress is slightly BELOW neutral — no dysfunction, just normal functioning with minor quarter-end repo noise.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Corporate credit spreads narrowed." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds remained robust." "Agency and non-agency commercial MBS issuance was strong in the second quarter, as yield spreads ticked down."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads narrowing, issuance robust across corporate and CRE sectors, and banks easing standards — clearly below-normal credit stress.
- Direction: Credit spreads are BELOW neutral (less stress) given narrowing spreads and robust issuance. Bright-line satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad stock price indexes increased, on net." "The staff judged asset valuation pressures to be notable in a number of markets, supported in part by the low level of Treasury yields."
- Center-of-gravity: Equity prices rising further into territory where staff sees "notable" valuation pressures. This indicates overvaluation concern, not stress.
- Direction: Equity valuation concern is BELOW neutral in the "stress" direction — staff's concern is about overvaluation (prices too high), not about distressed prices.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financial institutions were viewed as resilient, as the risks associated with financial leverage and funding risk were still viewed as low." But "business leverage was high" and "the share of new loans to risky borrowers was at a record high."
- Center-of-gravity: Banks themselves are sound and resilient, but staff flags vulnerabilities in business leverage and leveraged lending that could amplify shocks. Low stress with watchpoints.
- Direction: Banking stress is BELOW neutral because banks are "resilient" with "low" risk. Bright-line satisfied.
