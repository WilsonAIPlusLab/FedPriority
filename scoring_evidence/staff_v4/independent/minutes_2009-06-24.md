### Era Context
June 2009 — deep in the Great Financial Crisis aftermath. The recession officially ended in June 2009 per NBER, but at the time the economy was still contracting, unemployment was at 9.4%, and the recovery was just barely beginning to emerge. Financial conditions were improving from crisis lows but still stressed. The anchors suggest OUTPUT_GAP ~15-22, LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~15-22, BANKING_STRESS ~70-85 for this era.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 25 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Headline consumer prices were little changed between March and May, held down by declines in the prices of food and energy." "Core inflation was slightly higher from March to May than during the preceding three months, although core prices posted fairly small increases." "The low level of resource utilization was projected to result in an appreciable deceleration in core consumer prices through 2010."
- **Center of gravity**: Inflation is quiescent — not actively deflating but running well below target with significant slack projected to push it lower.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is subdued with downside risks from slack, despite a modest uptick in energy prices and a slight markup in the forecast.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 20 ±4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The economy remained very weak, though declines in activity seemed to be lessening." "The staff projected that real GDP would decline at a substantially slower rate in the second quarter... and then increase in the second half of 2009, though less rapidly than potential output." "The low level of resource utilization."
- **Center of gravity**: The economy is deeply below potential — still contracting in Q2, with the output gap projected to widen further before slowly closing.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral. Bright-line: "low level of resource utilization" → below 50. ✓

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 ±4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate, however, rose further in May, to 9.4 percent." "The demand for labor weakened further in May, albeit less rapidly than in earlier months." "Nonfarm payrolls continued to shrink."
- **Center of gravity**: Massive labor market slack — UE at 9.4% and still rising, with payrolls still declining. Among the worst labor market conditions in the post-war era.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral — extremely loose labor market. UE at 9.4% places this firmly in the 16-30 range per anchors.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending appeared to be holding reasonably steady after shrinking in the second half of 2008." "Real investment in equipment and software (E&S) continued to contract; however, the decline in the second quarter appeared likely to be smaller than in either of the two preceding quarters." "Outside of the energy-related sectors, demand for nonresidential building remained extremely weak and financing difficult to obtain."
- **Center of gravity**: Consumer spending has stabilized near depressed levels while business investment continues to contract, albeit at a slower pace. Net position is still clearly below trend but no longer in freefall.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral — spending and investment remain weak despite deceleration in the rate of decline.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- **Evidence**: No explicit discussion of productivity trends or levels.
- **Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Pressures in short-term bank funding markets eased further, as evidenced by declines in London interbank offered rate (Libor) fixings and in spreads between one- and three-month Libor and comparable-maturity overnight index swap (OIS) rates. These spreads narrowed to levels not seen since early 2008." But also: "credit remained tight," the private-label nonprime mortgage market "remained closed," and the equity risk premium "remained high by historical standards."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are improving meaningfully from crisis extremes — funding markets normalizing, corporate spreads narrowing — but overall conditions remain tight with credit still restricted and risk premiums elevated.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter). Bright-line: "credit remained tight" → > 50. ✓

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Functioning in the market for Treasury securities generally improved and trading picked up, but some strains remained. The on-the-run/off-the-run premium narrowed considerably at the short end of the yield curve. Such spreads, however, remained somewhat wide for longer-dated issues."
- **Center of gravity**: Treasury market functioning is improving but residual strains persist in longer-duration segments and MBS-related hedging flows caused episodic stress.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral — actual dysfunction described (wide spreads for long-dated, MBS strains) but improving. This is not just yield movement.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Yields on speculative-grade and investment-grade corporate bonds dropped, and spreads over yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed considerably." "Estimates of bid-asked spreads in the secondary market for speculative-grade corporate bonds fell significantly to about their average levels in the few years before the summer of 2007." But CDS spreads for banking "remained well above historical norms."
- **Center of gravity**: Spreads are compressing rapidly from crisis peaks but remain elevated relative to historical norms, indicating ongoing credit stress even as conditions improve.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral — spreads still elevated despite significant narrowing. ✓

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes rose, on net, over the intermeeting period." "The spread between an estimate of the expected real equity return over the next 10 years for S&P 500 firms and an estimate of the real 10-year Treasury yield — a rough gauge of the equity risk premium — narrowed noticeably but remained high by historical standards. Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index declined but remained elevated."
- **Center of gravity**: Equities are recovering from crisis lows with prices rising, but risk premiums remain elevated and volatility is still high, indicating continued stress/concern about equity markets.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress) — risk premiums "high by historical standards" despite improvement.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Nearly all the BHCs evaluated had enough Tier 1 capital to absorb the higher losses envisioned under the hypothetical more adverse scenario; however, 10 institutions were required to enhance their capital structure." "CDS spreads for banking organizations declined considerably over the intermeeting period, although they remained well above historical norms." C&I loans dropped, consumer loans shrank, home equity loans edged down.
- **Center of gravity**: The banking system remains significantly stressed — 10 of 19 largest BHCs needed more capital, CDS spreads well above norms, credit contracting — though the successful capital raises and SCAP process represent meaningful improvement.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is well above neutral — significant stress with active recapitalization needs. ✓

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 25 ±4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The dollar depreciated substantially during the intermeeting period against all other major currencies. This decline appeared to be driven by a renewed sense of optimism about global growth prospects, leading investors to shift away from safe-haven assets."
- **Center of gravity**: Clear, substantial dollar weakness driven by risk-on sentiment and unwinding of safe-haven flows.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral. Bright-line: depreciation → < 50. "Substantially" → 20-25 points below midpoint. ✓
