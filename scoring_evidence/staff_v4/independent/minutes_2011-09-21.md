# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: September 2011 — two years after the GFC trough. The recovery is slow; unemployment is still 9.1%. European sovereign debt crisis intensifying (Greece). The Fed had just introduced calendar-based forward guidance ("at least through mid-2013"). GDP growth tepid, housing deeply depressed. Commodity-driven inflation pressures from earlier in the year fading.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Consumer price inflation "appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year." Core CPI running at about the same pace as Q2. Near-term inflation expectations "well below the elevated level seen in the spring." Longer-term expectations "remained stable." Staff projects inflation "would be subdued in 2012 and 2013" given "considerable slack in labor and product markets." Wage increases "continued to be restrained." This is clearly below-target but not deflation risk — around 35-40.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as well BELOW potential. "Economic activity continued to expand at a slow pace." Staff lowered its GDP projection. Unemployment at 9.1%. "Considerable slack in labor and product markets anticipated to persist." Unemployment "anticipated to remain elevated at the end of 2013." Bright-line: "slack remains" language is explicit — score MUST be < 50. Given 9.1% unemployment and persistent slack, this is deep negative output gap territory, around 28-32.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as very LOOSE. Unemployment 9.1%. "Labor market conditions remained weak." "Private nonfarm employment rose only slightly." Long-duration unemployment and part-time for economic reasons "still elevated." Initial claims "edged up." Hiring plans "deteriorated somewhat." Wage increases "restrained by the large margin of slack." With UE at 9.1%, this maps to 16-30 range per the rubric (UE > 7% = score 16-30).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but below trend. Consumer spending had a "solid gain in July" partly from motor vehicle rebound, but nominal retail sales "only inched up in August." Consumer sentiment "deteriorated significantly further." Housing "continued to be depressed." Business equipment spending "appeared to expand further" with orders running ahead of shipments, but survey measures of business conditions "remained at muted levels." Commercial building spending at "very low level." Net: some pockets of positive activity but mostly sluggish — around 36-40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as TIGHTENING / stressed. "Financial markets were volatile." "Financial conditions deteriorated since the time of the previous forecast as investors pulled back from riskier assets." Corporate bond spreads "rose significantly" to levels "last registered in late 2009." Speculative-grade bond issuance "nearly came to a halt." Equity IPOs "shelved." CRE financing "remained weak." Mortgage underwriting "tight." European bank dollar funding difficulties. However, some mitigating factors: investment-grade issuance "remained robust," consumer ABS spreads "remained low," C&I loans "grew strongly." Bright-line: "deteriorated" and "less hospitable" and widening spreads = conditions tighter than normal. Score should be > 50, around 62-68.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined reflecting flight to safety and policy expectations — this is normal price adjustment, NOT dysfunction. No language about liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues in Treasuries specifically. The strains mentioned are in repo for "lower-quality, nontraditional collateral" — that's not Treasury market dysfunction per se. Score around 42-48 (normal functioning with some repo market noise at the edges).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as clearly WIDE. Corporate bond spreads "rose significantly over the intermeeting period, reaching levels last registered in late 2009." Speculative-grade issuance "nearly came to a halt." Leveraged loan volume "appeared to drop sharply." CMBS "slowed further" with investors demanding "greater compensation for risk." Residential MBS spreads "increased." Bright-line: "widened" / "elevated" = score > 50. This is clearly stressed, around 68-72.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities were "volatile but increased, on net, since the August FOMC meeting, following sharp declines." IPOs shelved amid volatility. CDS spreads widened for financial institutions. This describes elevated stress/concern. Score around 58-62 (modestly depressed/stressed).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Some stress signals. Equity prices of "large U.S. financial institutions fell and their CDS spreads widened." European banks found it "increasingly difficult" to get dollar funding. Some strains in repo for nontraditional collateral. However, U.S. institutions' funding conditions "little changed." C&I lending growing strongly. Consumer delinquencies improving. This is moderate stress — not systemic but emerging concerns, especially from European spillover. Bright-line: mixed, but leaning toward some stress. Score around 55-60.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar increased over the intermeeting period, reflecting a flight to safety." Bright-line: dollar appreciation = score > 50. Score around 58-62.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out separately as goods vs. services in detail. Energy prices "stepped up in July and August but only partially retraced their decline." Food price increases "somewhat below the pace seen earlier." Import price pressures receding per outlook. This is modestly elevated but moderating — around 45-48.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not discussed as a price component (housing discussed only as activity/market conditions, not shelter inflation). N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Near-term expectations "well below the elevated level seen in the spring." Longer-term expectations "remained stable." Inflation compensation from TIPS "fell to the low end of their ranges for this year." Staff projects subdued inflation with "stable long-run inflation expectations." This is well-anchored, possibly slightly below target given TIPS decline — around 40-44.

### Polarity Checks

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year, and measures of long-run inflation expectations remained stable." Staff outlook: "inflation would be subdued in 2012 and 2013" with "considerable slack in labor and product markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation moderating from earlier elevated readings, with subdued outlook given persistent slack and stable expectations. Below target but not deflationary.
**Direction**: Below neutral because inflation is moderating and projected subdued. Bright-line: N/A for this dimension.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Economic activity continued to expand at a slow pace." "Considerable slack in labor and product markets anticipated to persist over the forecast period." Unemployment at 9.1%, "anticipated to remain elevated at the end of 2013."
**Center-of-gravity**: Deep negative output gap — economy growing slowly, far below potential, with staff explicitly citing persistent, considerable slack.
**Direction**: Well below neutral. Bright-line PASS: "slack remains" / "considerable slack" = must be < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 24 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions remained weak." Unemployment at 9.1%. "Wage increases continued to be restrained by the large margin of slack in the labor market." "Many indicators of firms' hiring plans deteriorated somewhat."
**Center-of-gravity**: Very loose labor market. 9.1% unemployment with weak hiring, restrained wages, and elevated long-duration unemployment.
**Direction**: Well below neutral. UE > 7% anchors this in the 16-30 range.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Consumer spending had a "solid gain in July" partly from motor vehicle rebound but "nominal retail sales...only inched up in August." "Consumer sentiment deteriorated significantly further." Business equipment spending "appeared to expand further" but "survey measures of business conditions and sentiment remained at muted levels." Housing "continued to be depressed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Below trend. Pockets of strength in equipment spending and auto rebound, but consumer sentiment very weak, housing deeply depressed, and commercial real estate at very low levels.
**Direction**: Below neutral — mixed but weighed down by housing depression and weak sentiment.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financial conditions deteriorated since the time of the previous forecast as investors pulled back from riskier assets." "Spreads of yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds...rose significantly over the intermeeting period, reaching levels last registered in late 2009." "Bond issuance by speculative-grade firms nearly came to a halt."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clearly tightened conditions. Credit markets hostile to lower-rated borrowers, significant spread widening, IPO market frozen, European spillover. Investment-grade access and bank lending provide partial offset.
**Direction**: Above neutral — conditions deteriorated. Bright-line PASS.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: Treasury yields declined reflecting "flight to safety" and forward guidance expectations. "Some strains emerged late in the intermeeting period in the market for repurchase agreements backed by lower-quality, nontraditional collateral."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market itself functioning normally — yields moving on fundamentals. Minor strain in nontraditional repo is at the periphery. No auction failures, no liquidity dysfunction in Treasuries.
**Direction**: Near neutral. Yield moves from policy/safety = normal. PASS per bright-line: no actual dysfunction described.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities rose significantly over the intermeeting period, reaching levels last registered in late 2009." "Bond issuance by speculative-grade firms nearly came to a halt, and the volume of new leveraged loans...appeared to drop sharply."
**Center-of-gravity**: Wide and widening spreads with reduced credit access for lower-rated borrowers. Clearly elevated credit stress, reaching late-2009 levels.
**Direction**: Above neutral — widened / elevated. Bright-line PASS.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 60 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes were volatile but increased, on net, since the August FOMC meeting, following sharp declines in the days just preceding that meeting." "Gross public equity issuance by nonfinancial firms weakened substantially...a large number of planned initial public offerings were shelved."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities modestly recovered from sharp declines but remained volatile. IPO market frozen. Financial institution equities down with CDS widening. Moderately stressed equity environment.
**Direction**: Above neutral (more stress/concern). No explicit valuation commentary, but IPO shelving and financial sector equity declines signal stress.

### BANKING_STRESS: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Equity prices of large U.S. financial institutions fell and their credit default swap spreads widened." "Many large European banks found it increasingly difficult...to get unsecured dollar funding beyond the very short term." For U.S. banks: "conditions faced by U.S. financial institutions in these markets were little changed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Emerging stress driven by European spillover. U.S. bank funding stable but market indicators (equity, CDS) deteriorating. Not systemic but concerning.
**Direction**: Above neutral — stress emerging. Bright-line: CDS widening and equity declines indicate stress > 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar increased over the intermeeting period, reflecting a flight to safety."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar strengthened on safe-haven flows amid European crisis and global risk aversion.
**Direction**: Above neutral — dollar appreciated. Bright-line PASS.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 47 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices stepped up in July and August but only partially retraced their decline over the previous two months." "Increases in food prices were somewhat below the pace seen early in the year." Import/commodity price pressures "expected to recede further."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods price pressures moderating from earlier elevated levels. Energy and food still contributing but fading. Near-neutral, slightly below.
**Direction**: Near neutral, modestly below — pressures moderating.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Not discussed as a price/inflation component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Near-term inflation expectations from the Thomson Reuters/University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers...stayed well below the elevated level seen in the spring, and longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." "Measures of inflation compensation for the next 5 years as well as 5 to 10 years ahead...each fell to the low end of their ranges for this year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored and if anything drifting down. TIPS-based measures fell to year lows. Stable long-run expectations but some downside drift in market-based measures.
**Direction**: Below neutral — anchored but with some downside movement in market measures.
