# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2021-12-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: December 2021 -- the economy is well into recovery from COVID-19. Inflation has surged well above target (PCE at 5.0%, CPI at 6.8%). The labor market has tightened considerably (UE at 4.2%, down from 4.8% in September). Supply bottlenecks persist. The Omicron variant has just emerged. The Fed is discussing tapering asset purchases and pulling forward rate hikes.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly ABOVE midpoint. Key evidence: "Inflation readings remained high, and various indicators suggested that inflationary pressures had broadened in recent months." Total PCE at 5.0%, core PCE at 4.1%, CPI at 6.8%. The staff revised the near-term inflation outlook UP. The word "broadened" is important -- it signals spreading pressures. The staff projects PCE to "move up further relative to October's pace and to end the year around 5 percent." Risks to inflation are "skewed to the upside." This is clearly elevated inflation -- the anchor range for 2021 Q3-Q4 is 65-72. Given broadening, 5% PCE, upward revisions, and upside risks, I would place this at the higher end: ~72.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. Bright-line check: "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period." This MUST be > 50. "Well above potential" is strong language. "Aggregate demand appeared to be rising sharply in the fourth quarter." GDP posting "sizable gain." The anchor for 2022 is 57-63 and this is late 2021 with "well above potential" language. Score: ~60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT and above midpoint. UE at 4.2% (down from 4.8%), job openings "well above pre-pandemic levels," quits rates "elevated," average hourly earnings up 4.8%, "sizable wage gains observed across most sectors," initial claims at pre-pandemic levels. Staff projects "labor market conditions were projected to remain very tight." UE at 4.2% with strong wage growth and "very tight" language puts this solidly above 55. Score: ~63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but overall moderately positive. Real PCE growth "appeared to be picking up in the fourth quarter." Services strengthened. But retail sales stepped down in November, motor vehicle sales constrained by low inventories. Business fixed investment "rising at a slow pace" due to supply bottlenecks. Housing demand "remained strong" but activity "generally little changed." The net is positive but supply-constrained. Score: ~54.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states "financing conditions for businesses and households remained accommodative except for small businesses and nonprime borrowers." Corporate bond financing "remained accommodative," CRE financing "remained accommodative," residential mortgage conditions "stayed accommodative," consumer credit "remained accommodative for most borrowers." Bright-line check: "accommodative" MUST be < 50. The repeated "accommodative" across nearly every credit channel is emphatic. Small business exception noted but overall strongly accommodative. Score: ~28.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes yield movements driven by policy expectations and pandemic fears -- these are normal market dynamics, not dysfunction. "Short-term funding markets were stable." No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, or market dysfunction. Yield movements from inflation/policy expectations = normal functioning. Score: ~40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads "widened moderately" for corporate bonds. However, default rates "declined to close to historical lows," upgrades outpaced downgrades, corporate bond issuance was "solid," leveraged loan issuance was "robust." CMBS spreads "at or below pre-pandemic levels." The moderate widening from very compressed levels still leaves spreads at accommodative levels. Score: ~35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes were little changed" over the intermeeting period. "Equity market volatility increased markedly." No explicit valuation commentary from staff. Staff notes strong earnings growth and earnings exceeding expectations. The volatility increase is notable but no distress or valuation concern language. Score: ~50.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not specifically discussed as a standalone topic. C&I loans grew for first time since beginning of year, credit quality described as "solid," default rates at historical lows, delinquency rates at pre-pandemic levels. This suggests healthy conditions. Bright-line check: healthy indicators = below 50. Score: ~30.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index increased modestly." Bright-line check: dollar appreciation = score > 50. "Modestly" = 5-10 points above midpoint. Score: ~55.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Supply bottlenecks driving goods inflation. Motor vehicle prices implicitly elevated (low inventories), manufacturing supply chain issues. "Faster-than-expected increases... for a broad array of consumer prices." Score: ~75.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Services expenditures "strengthened." No specific services inflation discussed separately. Limited evidence. Score: N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Housing demand remained strong" but housing inflation not specifically separated. Score: N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of medium- and longer-run inflation expectations... leveled off after having risen over the past year." Inflation compensation "declined moderately." Longer-run inflation "still assumed to remain anchored at 2 percent." The leveling off after rising suggests somewhat elevated but stabilizing. Score: ~55.

- OUTPUT_GAP (60): Higher = more above potential. "Well above potential." Correct. Bright-line satisfied (>50).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 72 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Inflation readings remained high, and various indicators suggested that inflationary pressures had broadened in recent months." "Total PCE price inflation was 5.0 percent... core PCE price inflation... was 4.1 percent." "The 12-month change in the consumer price index (CPI) was 6.8 percent." Staff revised inflation projection upward, with risks "skewed to the upside."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly describes elevated, broadening inflation well above target, with the projection revised up and upside risks flagged.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is well above neutral because PCE is at 5.0%, CPI at 6.8%, and pressures are described as broadening.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period." "Aggregate demand appeared to be rising sharply in the fourth quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly projects GDP well above potential with strong aggregate demand, though supply bottlenecks constrain output.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff says "well above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined from 4.8 percent in September to 4.2 percent in November." "Average hourly earnings rose 4.8 percent... with sizable wage gains observed across most sectors." "Labor market conditions were projected to remain very tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 4.2% with rapid wage growth and the staff's own characterization of "very tight" conditions point to a clearly tight labor market.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE is 4.2%, wages are rising sharply, and staff projects "very tight" conditions.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE growth appeared to be picking up in the fourth quarter." But "growth in business fixed investment appeared to be rising at a slow pace again in the fourth quarter, as supply bottlenecks continued to weigh." Motor vehicle sales constrained. Housing demand "remained strong" but activity "generally little changed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is solid and picking up, but business investment is slow and supply constraints limit activity across sectors, netting to slightly above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral -- demand is strong but supply-constrained output tempers the realized spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained accommodative except for small businesses and nonprime borrowers." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial corporations remained accommodative." "CRE... remained accommodative." "Residential mortgage... stayed accommodative." "Consumer credit remained accommodative for most borrowers."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes conditions as accommodative across virtually every credit channel, with only small business and nonprime as exceptions.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (less tight) because staff repeatedly uses "accommodative." Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Short-term funding markets were stable over the intermeeting period." Treasury yield movements described as driven by inflation expectations, policy expectations, and safe-haven flows -- all normal market dynamics. No mention of liquidity problems or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets are functioning normally with yield movements reflecting macro expectations, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because no market dysfunction is described. Yield movements from policy expectations are scored as normal (36-50 range per rubric).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds widened moderately." However, "trailing default rates on corporate bonds and leveraged loans declined to close to historical lows." "Gross corporate bond issuance by both investment- and speculative-grade borrowers was solid." CMBS spreads "at or below pre-pandemic levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate widening from compressed levels, but overall credit conditions remain very favorable with strong issuance and historically low defaults.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because despite moderate widening, the level of spreads remains favorable and credit is flowing freely.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity indexes were little changed, on net." "Equity market volatility increased markedly." "S&P 500 firms' earnings reports for the third quarter again exceeded analyst expectations."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities flat with increased volatility but no valuation commentary from staff. Strong earnings support but pandemic/policy concerns offset.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is at neutral -- no clear stress or overvaluation concern expressed by staff.

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The credit quality of large nonfinancial corporations remained solid amid strong earnings growth." "Trailing default rates on corporate bonds and leveraged loans declined to close to historical lows." C&I loans grew, delinquency rates at pre-pandemic levels, forbearance use at low levels.
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system indicators uniformly positive with solid credit quality, growing loans, and low delinquencies.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because all indicators point to healthy banking conditions. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50 for healthy/sound conditions).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 55 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad dollar index increased modestly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar appreciated modestly over the intermeeting period.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied (>50 for appreciation).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 75 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Supply chain bottlenecks were seen as continuing to put upward pressure on prices." "Faster-than-expected increases... for a broad array of consumer prices." Motor vehicle inventories "extremely low," constraining sales and implying price pressure. Manufacturing affected by semiconductor shortages.
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods inflation clearly elevated, driven by supply bottlenecks across multiple categories.
**Direction confirmation**: Above neutral -- supply-driven goods inflation is a major theme of this meeting.
