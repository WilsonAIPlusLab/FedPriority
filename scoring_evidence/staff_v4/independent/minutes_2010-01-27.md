# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-01-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains three clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views are included in these sections.

### Era Context
January 2010 — approximately 7 months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in the early stages of recovery with substantial slack remaining. Unemployment is near 10%, the fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound, and the Fed is conducting large-scale asset purchases. Conditions should show: deep slack (output gap well below 50), very loose labor market, subdued inflation, and financial conditions still showing some stress but improving from the crisis peak.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "core consumer price inflation remained subdued," "price increases for non-energy services slowed early last year and remained modest throughout 2009," "substantial slack in resource utilization was still expected to put downward pressure on costs," staff forecasts "some slowing of core and headline inflation over the next two years." Energy prices pushed up headline but core remained subdued. This is clearly below-target inflation with no upward pressure. Direction: below 50. Score range: 25-30 given "subdued" language and downward forecast.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output gap as WELL BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "resource slack would be taken up only gradually over the forecast period," "substantial slack in resource utilization." The economy is recovering — "economic activity continued to strengthen" — but from a very deep hole. Industrial production rising, inventory adjustment providing boost, but the staff explicitly projects gradual slack absorption. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language -> MUST be < 50. Direction: well below 50. Score range: 32-37 given early recovery but substantial remaining slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as VERY LOOSE. Evidence: "unemployment rate remained elevated," "hiring stayed weak," "the total number of individuals receiving unemployment insurance...continued to climb," "the average length of ongoing unemployment spells rose steeply," "joblessness became increasingly concentrated among the long-term unemployed." Layoffs subsiding but hiring weak. Unemployment ~10%. Direction: well below 50. Score range: 18-23. The unemployment rate near 10% anchors this firmly in the 15-22 range per the rubric anchors for 2009-10.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED but below trend. Consumer spending "continued to trend up" but "remained well below its pre-recession level." Equipment and software spending "rose robustly" in Q4. However, housing recovery slowed, nonresidential structures "continued to fall," consumer sentiment "remained low," and credit conditions for consumers "stayed very tight." The mix: robust E&S spending offset by weak housing, weak nonresidential structures, and below-pre-recession consumer spending. Direction: below midpoint but improving. Score range: 36-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed as a standalone topic. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as BELOW midpoint (loose/accommodative). Evidence: "Financial market conditions remained supportive of economic growth," "short-term funding markets were generally stable," Libor-OIS spreads "remained low," CP spreads "held steady at the low end." However, "standards and terms on all major loan types remained tight" and "demand for loans reportedly weakened further." Mixed signals: market-based conditions supportive, but bank lending still tight. Bright-line check: "supportive" -> MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50 but not extremely so given tight bank lending. Score range: 38-44.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff notes yields on Treasuries "were about unchanged on net." Repo market experienced "some year-end dislocations, with a few transactions reportedly occurring at negative interest rates" but these were described as modest. TIPS liquidity improving. No dysfunction language. Bright-line: no actual market dysfunction described, just minor year-end issues. Score range: 38-43 (normal with minor year-end friction).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed picture. Corporate bond yields edged down vs. Treasuries (spreads narrowing). CP spreads at low end of range since mid-2007. Leveraged loan bid prices rose. But: "financing conditions for commercial real estate...remained strained," consumer credit contracted for 10th consecutive month, credit card spreads widening, delinquency rates high, bank lending standards tight. Overall direction: some stress remaining but improving. Above neutral. Score range: 55-62.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices ended "little changed on balance" with "elevated volatility." The equity risk premium "remained well above its average level during the past decade" — meaning equities look cheap relative to bonds by historical standards. This implies modestly depressed valuations / elevated concern. Direction: above midpoint (more stress). Score range: 55-60.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Evidence of ongoing stress: "commercial bank credit continued to contract," C&I and CRE loans "fell markedly," "standards and terms on all major loan types remained tight," "demand for loans reportedly weakened further," delinquency rates high on consumer and CRE loans, "European financial stocks declined substantially" with concerns about banking health. Banks were repaying TARP (positive sign) but lending still contracting. Direction: above 50 (elevated stress). Score range: 62-68.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose" driven by perceptions of better U.S. growth prospects. Dollar appreciated against many currencies. Bright-line: appreciation -> MUST be > 50. Direction: above 50. Score range: 55-60 (modest appreciation).

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "Price increases for core goods were quite modest during the second half of 2009." Below midpoint. Score: ~30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Price increases for non-energy services slowed early last year and remained modest throughout 2009, reflecting declining prices for housing services and perhaps the deceleration in labor costs." Below midpoint. Score: ~28.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "declining prices for housing services" explicitly stated. Below midpoint. Score: ~22.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Households' expectations of near-term inflation increased in January; in addition, median longer-term inflation expectations edged up, though they remained near the lower end of the narrow range that has prevailed over the past few years." 5-year TIPS compensation increased partly from inflation risk premiums. 5-10 year compensation declined slightly. Longer-term expectations stable/anchored near lower end. Direction: near or slightly below midpoint. Score: ~45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (20)**: Gap of 15 points. Explainable: output is recovering faster than the labor market (typical in early recovery — productivity gains allow output recovery before hiring picks up). Coherent.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (39)**: Within 5 points. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (58)**: 18-point gap. Explainable: market-based financial conditions are supportive (loose), but bank credit conditions remain tight. The CREDIT_SPREADS dimension captures the bank/credit side which is lagging the market recovery. Acceptable divergence.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Aligned. Both near normal. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (27) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~30, Services ~28, Housing ~22, Expectations ~45. Weighted average (heavier on goods/services given discussion) ~30. Parent at 27 is consistent.

### Bright-Line Verification
- OUTPUT_GAP: "slack remains" -> score 35 < 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "supportive" -> score 40 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Spreads narrowing in corporate but still tight in bank/CRE -> score 58 > 50. Reflects that credit conditions are still stressed overall despite improvement. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks contracting credit, tight standards, high delinquencies -> score 65 > 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Dollar appreciated -> score 57 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction described -> score 40, within 36-50 normal range. PASS.

- Center-of-gravity: Corporate market spreads narrowing and CP spreads low, but bank credit still tight, CRE strained, consumer credit contracting, and delinquencies elevated. Overall credit conditions still somewhat stressed despite improvement.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because despite improvement in market-based spreads, bank lending standards remain tight, CRE is strained, and consumer credit is contracting.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 56 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "broad stock price indexes...ending the period little changed on balance"; "the equity risk premium...remained well above its average level during the past decade"
- Center-of-gravity: Equities roughly flat over the period with elevated volatility. The equity risk premium well above historical average suggests valuations are somewhat depressed (investors demanding higher compensation for risk). Modest stress/concern.
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral (more stress) because the elevated equity risk premium implies investors still see elevated risk in equities relative to historical norms.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS

- Center-of-gravity: Banking system showing significant ongoing stress — credit contracting, standards tight, delinquencies high across consumer and CRE, though some banks repaying TARP. Clearly elevated stress without being at crisis peak.
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because bank credit is contracting, lending standards are tight, and delinquency rates are elevated across multiple categories.
