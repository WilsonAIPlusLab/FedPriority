# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2014-07-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It has clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
Mid-2014: The U.S. economy is in a moderate recovery phase following the Great Recession. The Fed is still conducting QE (tapering) with the federal funds rate at the effective lower bound. Unemployment has been declining but remains above pre-crisis levels. Inflation has been running below the 2% target. This is the "2010-14 recovery" era per the rubric anchors.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. PCE prices were "about 1½ percent higher than a year earlier" — below the 2% target. Core PCE also at 1½ percent. However, there was a pickup: "Consumer price inflation rose somewhat in the second quarter" and the near-term forecast was "revised up a little." Still, inflation was "projected to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." Gains in nominal wages "remained modest." This is below-target inflation with a slight uptick — firmly in the 35-42 range per the 2013-19 anchor.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as below potential. Key evidence: "resource slack in this projection was anticipated to be somewhat narrower this year" and slack was expected "to be taken up slowly over the projection period." The unemployment rate was "now closer to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" but still above it. GDP growth was "only a modest pace, on balance, over the first half of the year." The staff lowered potential output growth, narrowing the gap, but slack persists. Bright-line: slack remains → must be < 50. The gap is narrowing, so closer to 50 than deep recession. Score ~42-45.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as below full employment but improving meaningfully. Unemployment at 6.1% in June — per the rubric, UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45 range. "Total nonfarm payroll employment increased strongly in June" and the "average monthly gain for the second quarter was the largest since the first quarter of 2012." But "long-duration unemployment" and "part time for economic reasons" both "remained elevated by historical standards." The "rate of hiring was unchanged and remained at a modest level." This is improving but still slack — score ~38-42.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as moderate/improving. "Real PCE rose more quickly in the second quarter than in the first." Business equipment spending increased. Residential investment "turned up in the second quarter after declining for two consecutive quarters." Business investment in nonresidential construction also increased. Key factors for household spending "remained positive." This is a moderate recovery picture — not sluggish, not robust. Score ~48-52, consistent with the "moderate pace" anchor of 50-55 for 2014-19.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff discusses productivity implicitly: they "lowered its assumed pace of potential output growth this year" to reconcile weak GDP with declining unemployment. This implies weaker productivity growth. But the discussion is indirect. Score ~35-38, below trend, with low confidence. Could also argue N/A given it's very indirect.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states: "Financial conditions eased somewhat, on balance." This means conditions became more accommodative/looser. Bright-line: "accommodative"/"easy" → must be < 50. Credit was flowing well: "Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations remained strong," C&I loans "continued to increase at a robust pace," banks easing lending standards. However, "residential mortgage markets generally remained tight." The overall picture is accommodative. Score ~32-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined ~20 bps, driven by geopolitical concerns and growth expectations — this is normal market functioning, not dysfunction. "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. Score ~30-35 (calm/well-functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Credit conditions are quite favorable. "Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations remained strong." "Gross issuance of investment- and speculative-grade bonds stayed brisk." Banks easing lending standards. CRE credit conditions "improved further." Consumer credit "continued to expand robustly." The staff even flagged "loosening of underwriting standards" and "low and declining risk premiums" as a risk. Only mortgage credit remained tight. This is below-normal spreads / ample credit. Score ~28-34.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: S&P 500 rose ~1½%. VIX "remained at low levels." The financial stability report noted "low and declining risk premiums" and "the risk of an eventual correction in asset valuations." This suggests staff sees valuations as somewhat elevated — a mild overvaluation concern. Score ~35-40 (modestly above fair value concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff financial stability report: "relatively strong capital positions of U.S. banks" is a key phrase. Banks are easing lending standards and expanding loans. This is healthy banking — low stress. Bright-line: "strong capital positions" → must be < 50. But there's the Portuguese bank concern in Europe, though that's foreign. For U.S. banking, score ~28-32.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad trade-weighted dollar was little changed, on net, over the intermeeting period." "The foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed." Bright-line: stable/little changed → 46-54 neutral range. Score ~48.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed in a way that distinguishes goods inflation clearly. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately identified. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Home prices "continued to rise through May, though the rate of increase was less rapid than earlier in the year." This is housing prices not shelter CPI per se. Limited. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-run measures of inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey were little changed, on net, in June and early July, while longer-term expectations declined." "Longer-run inflation expectations assumed to remain stable." "Measures of longer-horizon inflation compensation based on TIPS were about unchanged." This is well-anchored, consistent with 45-50 per the 2014-19 anchor. Score ~47.

- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Eased somewhat" = more accommodative → score < 50. My score: 35. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (43) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40)**: Both below 50, 3-point gap. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (43) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (50)**: 7-point gap. C&I slightly stronger than output gap because spending was picking up even with slack remaining. Acceptable — spending is recovering while slack persists.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (30)**: Both below 50, 5-point gap. Coherent — both indicate accommodative/loose conditions.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (32)**: Both below 50. Normal times with calm Treasury markets and easy financial conditions. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) ↔ INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored at 47. Expectations well-anchored near target while actual inflation running below target is coherent — expectations can be anchored even when current inflation is low.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "the PCE price index for items excluding food and energy...was 1½ percent higher than a year earlier"; "inflation was projected to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years"; "gains in nominal wages and employee benefits remained modest."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running at ~1.5% year-over-year, below the 2% target, with modest wage gains and a forecast to stay below target for years.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below target with a gradual projected return to 2%.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 43 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "resource slack in this projection was anticipated to be somewhat narrower this year" and slack expected "to be taken up slowly over the projection period"; unemployment rate "now closer to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" but still above it.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy below potential with slack narrowing. The gap is not large but persists, and the staff lowered potential output growth to reconcile data.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because slack remains; bright-line rule satisfied (< 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 6.1 percent in June"; "Total nonfarm payroll employment increased strongly in June, and the average monthly gain for the second quarter was the largest since the first quarter of 2012"; "the rate of long-duration unemployment...and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons...both measures remained elevated by historical standards."
- **Center-of-gravity**: UE at 6.1% is in the 5.5-7% range (rubric: 31-45). Strong payroll gains but residual slack indicators still elevated. Score at the upper end of that range given strong improvement.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because unemployment remains above full employment and slack indicators are elevated.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose more quickly in the second quarter than in the first"; "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products increased in the second quarter"; "Real expenditures for residential investment turned up in the second quarter after declining for two consecutive quarters."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spending broadly picking up across consumer, business equipment, and residential categories. Moderate pace, roughly at trend — consistent with the 2014-19 "solid pace" anchor of 50-55.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral — moderate and improving spending that is roughly at trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 37 ±10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "the staff lowered its assumed pace of potential output growth this year by more than it marked down GDP growth" — implying weaker trend productivity. No explicit productivity discussion.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Indirect evidence suggests below-trend productivity growth, as the staff reconciled weak GDP with falling unemployment by cutting potential output estimates.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because the staff reduced potential output growth estimates, implying weak productivity.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Financial conditions eased somewhat, on balance, between the June and July FOMC meetings"; "Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations remained strong"; banks "easing lending standards and terms as well as stronger loan demand."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions explicitly described as having eased, with strong credit flows and loosening underwriting standards. Mortgage credit remains tight, but overall picture is accommodative.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative/loose); bright-line rule satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period"; yields declined ~20 bps driven by geopolitical concerns and growth expectations — normal price adjustment.
- **Center-of-gravity**: No dysfunction described. Yields moved on fundamentals. Funding markets stable. This is calm/well-functioning Treasury markets.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral — no market dysfunction described; yield moves reflect normal repricing.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Gross issuance of investment- and speculative-grade bonds stayed brisk"; "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' balance sheets continued to increase at a robust pace"; staff flagged "low and declining risk premiums" and "loosening of underwriting standards."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit very freely available across corporate, CRE, and consumer segments. Risk premiums low and declining. Only mortgage credit is tight. Consistent with 2013-19 "generally favorable" anchor of 30-40.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (tight/compressed spreads, ample availability); bright-line rule satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 37 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "low and declining risk premiums, low levels of market volatility, and a loosening of underwriting standards in a number of markets raised somewhat the risk of an eventual correction in asset valuations"; S&P 500 rose ~1½%, VIX at low levels.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff expresses mild concern about elevated valuations / eventual correction risk. Not alarm, but a cautionary note. This is modestly above fair value concern.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (i.e., staff sees mild overvaluation concern rather than depressed markets). Higher = more stress/depression, so overvaluation concern = lower score.

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "relatively strong capital positions of U.S. banks"; "subdued use of maturity transformation and leverage within the broader financial sector."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banks described as well-capitalized with strong positions. Low stress. Some concern about European banks (Portuguese bank), but U.S. banking sector healthy.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are well-capitalized and healthy; bright-line rule satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "the broad trade-weighted dollar was little changed, on net, over the intermeeting period"; "the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar stable with no notable movement. Neutral.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is at neutral (little changed); bright-line rule satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Housing prices discussed but not shelter/housing inflation as a CPI component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "longer-run measures of inflation expectations remained stable"; "Near-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey were little changed...while longer-term expectations declined"; "Measures of longer-horizon inflation compensation based on TIPS were about unchanged."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored and stable, with longer-term survey measures edging down slightly. Consistent with the 2014-19 anchor of 45-55.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is at/slightly below neutral — well-anchored with a slight downward drift in longer-term survey expectations.
