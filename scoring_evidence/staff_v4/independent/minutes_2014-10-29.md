# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2014-10-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: October 2014 — mid-recovery period. The economy was expanding moderately but still below potential, with unemployment at 5.9% (declining but still above the staff's estimate of the natural rate). Inflation running below the 2% target. The Fed was about to end its asset purchase program (QE3 ended at this meeting). Dollar was strengthening, oil prices falling, and global growth concerns (especially Europe) were mounting.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at "about 1-1/2 percent" over 12 months, "below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent." CPI excluding food and energy at about 1-3/4 percent. Energy prices declining further. Staff forecast: inflation "projected to be lower in the second half of this year than in the first half and to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." Market-based inflation compensation declined. This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 30-40 range (below target, disinflation pressures from energy).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW potential. "Economic activity expanded at a moderate pace." Resource slack "anticipated to decline steadily" — implies slack still exists. Real GDP "expected to rise faster than potential output in 2015 and 2016" — meaning current output is below potential. Unemployment rate "expected to gradually improve and to be at the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate in 2017" — meaning it is currently above the natural rate. Bright-line check: slack remains / below potential → score MUST be < 50. Score in the 38-42 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW full employment but improving. Unemployment at 5.9%, which is above the natural rate (staff expects it to reach natural rate in 2017). "Labor market conditions improved over the intermeeting period." Payroll gains continued. But "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons decreased a little" (still elevated). Labor force participation "edged down." Per the rubric anchors, 2011-14 period should be 35-40. UE at 5.9% maps to ~31-45 range ("below full employment; slack remains; unemployment elevated; UE 5.5-7%"). Score around 38.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MODERATE — near but not quite at trend. Consumer spending "increased at a modest pace." Retail sales "little changed in September following solid gains in July and August." Motor vehicle sales "fell back." Housing "improving only slowly." Business equipment spending "risen at a moderate pace." Government purchases "risen modestly." Mixed but mostly moderate signals. Score around 46-48 (slightly below trend, "modest" consumer spending offset by "moderate" business investment).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat accommodative but with some tightening impulse. Credit conditions in consumer markets "remained accommodative." CRE lending conditions "continued to ease." C&I loans "continued to expand at a robust pace." Mortgage rates declined 25bp. However, equity prices edged down, corporate spreads widened modestly, and the staff noted "asset valuation pressures that were broadening, as well as a loosening of underwriting standards." The overall picture is still accommodative financial conditions despite the mid-October volatility spike. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language → score MUST be < 50. Score around 35-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes ACTUAL market dysfunction on October 15. "The Treasury market experienced significant volatility on October 15, with 5- and 10-year Treasury yields dropping as much as 30 basis points in about an hour." "Treasury market liquidity, as measured by bid-asked spreads, worsened significantly." This is actual liquidity impairment, not just yield movements. However, it was a single-day event that "subsequently fell back." On net over the period, markets functioned. Score should reflect the transient but real dysfunction — around 55-58.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as modestly wider but still relatively contained. "Corporate bond spreads widened modestly." Investment- and speculative-grade spreads "widened modestly relative to Treasury securities." But credit flows "picked up," issuance "rebounded." The level is still relatively contained — widened modestly from a generally favorable baseline. Bright-line check: "widened" → should push above neutral. Score around 45-48 (modestly wider from a below-normal starting point, but not at normal levels yet).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "S&P 500 index decreased about 1 percent, on net." VIX "increased moderately" but below long-run average. Earnings reports "generally viewed as positive." Staff's financial stability report noted "asset valuation pressures that were broadening." This suggests staff saw equities as somewhat elevated in valuation despite the modest decline. Score around 38-42 (modestly above fair value / somewhat elevated valuations per the staff stability report).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff explicitly describes banking system as resilient. "The U.S. financial system appeared resilient to shocks... due to the relatively strong capital and liquidity profiles of large domestic banking firms." ECB stress test results "broadly in line with expectations." However, staff noted "loosening of underwriting standards" as a risk. Bright-line check: "resilient" / "strong capital" → score MUST be < 50. Score around 28-32.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff sees dollar as appreciating. "The dollar appreciated moderately against most other currencies." "The broad nominal dollar index ended the period moderately higher." "A further rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" noted in the outlook as a factor revising down GDP growth. Bright-line check: appreciation → score MUST be > 50. "Moderately" = 5-10 points above midpoint. Score around 58-62.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent." PCE inflation "about 1-1/2 percent over the 12 months ending in August." "Consumer energy prices declined further in September." Staff forecast: inflation "projected to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation as persistently below target with energy-related disinflation adding downward pressure. No deflation concern, but clearly below 2%.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running about 1.5% — well below the 2% target — with declining energy prices adding further disinflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Economic activity expanded at a moderate pace." "Resource slack was anticipated to decline steadily" (implying slack currently exists). "Real GDP was still expected to rise faster than potential output in 2015 and 2016" (confirming output is below potential). Unemployment rate "expected to... be at the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate in 2017" (confirming currently above natural rate).
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing moderately but still below potential; slack remains but is declining. Consistent with mid-recovery 2014 conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references remaining resource slack and GDP below potential. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Unemployment rate declined to 5.9 percent." "Labor market conditions improved over the intermeeting period." But "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons decreased a little" (still elevated). "Labor force participation rate edged down." Staff expects natural rate to be reached in 2017, confirming current slack.
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving but still slack — UE at 5.9% is above the natural rate, with remaining part-time for economic reasons and declining participation. Solidly below full employment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because unemployment remains above the natural rate with continuing slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE appeared to have increased at a modest pace in the third quarter." "Real spending on business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to have risen at a moderate pace." "Housing market conditions seemed to be improving only slowly." Government purchases "risen modestly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is modest (below trend), business investment moderate, housing slow. The net picture is slightly below trend but not weak.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral — "modest" consumer spending and slow housing offset moderate business investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: Not discussed in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Conditions in most consumer credit markets remained accommodative." "Financing conditions in the commercial real estate market continued to ease." "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' books continued to expand at a robust pace." "Mortgage rates to qualified borrowers declined about 25 basis points." Staff also notes "loosening of underwriting standards in the speculative corporate debt and CRE markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative with easing credit availability, low rates, and robust lending — despite the transient mid-October volatility spike.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (i.e., loose) because staff describes widespread accommodative conditions. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 56 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The Treasury market experienced significant volatility on October 15, with 5- and 10-year Treasury yields dropping as much as 30 basis points in about an hour." "Treasury market liquidity, as measured by bid-asked spreads, worsened significantly." But these moves "subsequently fell back" and the episode was attributed to "technical factors related to investor positioning and trading strategies."
**Center-of-gravity**: A genuine but transient episode of Treasury market dysfunction occurred on Oct 15 — actual liquidity impairment (bid-ask spread widening, not just yield moves). However, it resolved quickly and was seen as technically driven.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral because staff describes actual (though temporary) liquidity dysfunction, not just normal yield movements.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds widened modestly relative to Treasury securities." But "credit flows to nonfinancial business picked up." "Gross issuance of investment- and speculative-grade bonds rebounded." "Commercial and industrial loans continued to expand at a robust pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened modestly from generally favorable (compressed) levels. The level is still below-normal/favorable, not at neutral. Credit remains ample.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is near but slightly below neutral — spreads widened modestly from a below-normal starting point, putting them in the 45-50 range.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The S&P 500 index decreased about 1 percent, on net." Staff financial stability report noted "asset valuation pressures that were broadening." Earnings reports were "generally viewed as positive" with "modest growth in earnings per share."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff expresses some concern about broadening valuation pressures (overvaluation concern) despite the modest decline. This places the score in the "modestly above fair value" / overvaluation-concern zone.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (score 35) because the staff's concern is about elevated/stretched valuations (overvaluation), which maps to the lower end of this scale where lower = more overvaluation concern.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The U.S. financial system appeared resilient to shocks of the magnitude seen recently due to the relatively strong capital and liquidity profiles of large domestic banking firms." Staff also noted "loosening of underwriting standards" as a monitoring concern but overall system is resilient.
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is healthy and well-capitalized, with low stress. Minor concerns about underwriting standards loosening.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because staff describes a resilient, well-capitalized system. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated moderately against most other currencies." "The broad nominal dollar index ended the period moderately higher." Staff revised down GDP growth projections partly due to "a further rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear moderate dollar appreciation over the intermeeting period, with the staff noting it as a headwind to growth.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### Inflation Sub-components:

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices declined further in September, largely reflecting continued declines in retail gasoline prices." "Consumer food prices rose solidly in recent months." Energy drag dominates goods inflation picture.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — energy prices declining, with some offset from food.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: Not separately discussed.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing discussed in terms of market activity, not shelter inflation.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Market-based measures of inflation compensation declined somewhat." "Survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey declined in September and early October, while longer-term inflation expectations in the survey were little changed." Staff notes the decline in compensation reflected "concerns about global growth and the risk of building disinflationary pressures."
**Direction confirmation**: Slightly below neutral — market-based measures declining but survey-based anchored. The mention of "risk of building disinflationary pressures" pulls this below 50.
