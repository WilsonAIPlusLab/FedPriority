# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-06-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered.

### Era Context
June 2011: Two years after the Great Recession trough. The recovery is underway but fragile. Unemployment remains very high (~9.1%). The Japan earthquake/tsunami has disrupted supply chains. Commodity prices have spiked but are beginning to ease. The Fed is completing QE2. European sovereign debt concerns (Greece, Ireland, Portugal) are intensifying.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as modestly above recent norms but not dangerously elevated. Headline inflation "edged down a bit" from Q1 levels. Core CPI "picked up in April and May" and the 12-month change is "above its level of a year earlier." However, upward pressures are attributed to commodities, imports, and supply-chain-driven motor vehicle prices — largely transitory factors. Labor cost pressures are "still subdued." Longer-run inflation expectations "remained stable." Staff raised their core inflation projection "slightly" but still expected core to "remain relatively subdued" due to "persistent slack." Direction: Modestly above the subdued levels of prior years, but not threatening. Above 50 but only slightly — the staff views pressures as temporary and expects inflation to recede. Score range: 52-58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says the "pace of the economic recovery slowed," manufacturing capacity utilization "was still well below its longer-run average," there is "persistent slack in labor and product markets," and the unemployment rate is projected to "remain elevated." The recovery is "softer than they had previously anticipated." This is clearly below potential — bright-line rule requires score < 50. The gap is significant but the economy is recovering, not in recession. Score range: 32-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment is 9.1% and rising. Payroll expansion was "markedly below" the prior pace. Initial claims rose. Long-duration unemployment and involuntary part-time work are "elevated." There is a "large amount of slack in the labor market." Wage increases are "restrained." UE at 9.1% anchors this firmly in the 16-30 range per the rubric. Score range: 22-28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending growth "declined in recent months from the already modest pace in the first quarter." Real PCE "only edged up in April." Real disposable income "remained flat." Consumer sentiment "stayed relatively low." Housing "remained depressed." Business investment in E&S was "rising a bit more slowly" than the solid Q1 pace. Commercial building was "depressed." Some positives: exports rose strongly, C&I loans increased at a brisk pace. But the net picture is clearly below trend. Score range: 33-39.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Mentioned briefly: "modest rise in hourly compensation in the nonfarm business sector was mostly offset by further gains in productivity." This suggests productivity is still growing but is only mentioned in passing in the context of unit labor costs. Not enough for a strong signal. Slightly positive mention — modest gains. Score range: 48-54 or borderline N/A. I'll score it given the mention.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Mixed picture. Dealer financing terms showed "continued gradual easing." Net debt financing was "strong." C&I loans grew briskly, banks eased some lending terms. Consumer credit conditions "continued to improve." But: later in the period, there was a "general pullback from risk-taking and a decline in liquidity." Residential mortgage markets "remained strained." Commercial real estate markets "remained weak." Credit risk spreads widened "a little." The balance suggests conditions are somewhat accommodative/loose on the corporate side but strained in real estate. Overall: modestly accommodative to neutral. Bright-line: the "easing" language and "accommodative" monetary policy context suggest < 50 but the real estate strains push it up. Score range: 42-48.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved in response to economic data and policy expectations — this is normal market functioning, not dysfunction. The debt ceiling "attracted attention" but investors "seemed to anticipate that the debt ceiling would be increased in time to avoid any significant market disruptions." No description of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction difficulties. Score range: 38-44.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads widened "a little" over the intermeeting period. But corporate bond issuance hit "record high in May." Consumer ABS issuance "remained robust." C&I lending was "brisk." Business credit quality "improved further." Spreads moved slightly wider but from a favorable base, and credit availability remained strong. Score range: 38-44.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices "declined, on net" in response to "downbeat economic data." Financial firms "underperformed." Option-adjusted volatility "rose somewhat." This is modest decline, not a severe correction. Staff reports it factually without valuation concern language. Score range: 52-56.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banks eased lending terms on C&I loans. Business credit quality "improved further." Consumer delinquency rates "edged down further" with some "moving back to pre-crisis levels." Commercial real estate delinquencies "edged down from their previous peaks." However, residential mortgage delinquencies were "at elevated levels." Financial firm stock prices underperformed due to "potential credit rating downgrades" and "heightened concerns about the anticipated capital surcharge." Overall: improving but with lingering stress pockets. Score range: 45-52.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Since the April FOMC meeting, the dollar rose modestly, on net, after depreciating over the preceding several months." Bright-line: appreciation → > 50. The appreciation was "modest." Score range: 52-56.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Consumer food and energy prices "decelerated." But core goods: "notable increases in motor vehicle prices" due to supply chain disruptions, "elevated prices of commodities and other imports." Retail gasoline prices declining. Mixed — goods inflation was elevated but easing. Score range: 55-62.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed as a separate category. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "fell further." Activity "remained depressed." This is about asset prices, not shelter CPI directly — but the direction is clearly deflationary for housing. Score range: 25-32.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Near-term expectations "moved down a little" from the "high level" in April. Longer-term expectations "remained within the range that has generally prevailed over the preceding few years." Well-anchored. Score range: 48-53.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modestly elevated core but subdued overall. Score ~55 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says well below potential. Score ~35 matches (< 50). Bright-line PASS.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE at 9.1% = very loose. Score ~25 matches.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says gradual easing but some strains. Score ~45 matches (slightly accommodative). Bright-line: mixed language, slightly accommodative overall → < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Spreads widened "a little" but overall credit conditions favorable. Score ~40. Bright-line: not clearly "compressed" or "widened" — slightly wider but from favorable levels. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Mixed — improving but elevated mortgage delinquencies. Score ~48. Not clearly "well-capitalized/sound" language so neutral is fine. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar "rose modestly." Score ~53. Bright-line PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction described. Score ~40. Bright-line PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25)**: Both well below 50. Gap of 10 points — labor market is even weaker than overall output gap, which makes sense given that UE at 9.1% is very elevated even for a slowly recovering economy. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (36)**: Very close. Both reflect a sluggish recovery. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (45) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (40)**: Both modestly below 50. Financial conditions slightly accommodative, spreads slightly below normal. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (45) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Both near neutral-low. No crisis conditions. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (55) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Goods ~58 (elevated commodities/autos), Housing ~28 (deflationary), Expectations ~50 (anchored). Weighted average tilts modestly above 50, consistent with 55. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "core consumer price inflation picked up in April and May, pushing the 12-month change in the core consumer price index through May above its level of a year earlier." "Upward pressures on core consumer prices appeared to reflect the elevated prices of commodities and other imports." But: "labor cost pressures were still subdued" and staff projected core inflation would "remain relatively subdued over the projection period."
- Center-of-gravity: Core inflation is rising modestly above recent levels due to commodity/import pass-through, but labor costs are contained and staff expects pressures to recede, putting this modestly above neutral.
- Direction: Inflation pressure is above neutral because core prices are picking up, though expected to be temporary.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the pace of the economic recovery slowed in recent months"; "the underlying pace of the recovery was softer than they had previously anticipated"; manufacturing capacity utilization "was still well below its longer-run average"; "persistent slack in labor and product markets."
- Center-of-gravity: The economy is well below potential with persistent slack, though a moderate recovery continues — consistent with a slowly closing but still large output gap.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff repeatedly references slack and below-potential operation. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate moved up in April and then rose further to 9.1 percent in May"; "Both long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons continued to be elevated"; "wage increases continued to be restrained by the large amount of slack in the labor market."
- Center-of-gravity: With unemployment at 9.1% and rising, massive labor slack persists. This is firmly in the high-unemployment, low-tightness zone.
- Direction: Labor tightness is far below neutral because UE is 9.1% with "large amount of slack."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 36 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Growth in consumer spending declined in recent months from the already modest pace in the first quarter"; "Total real personal consumption expenditures only edged up in April"; "Activity in the housing market remained depressed"; "real business investment in equipment and software was rising a bit more slowly."
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is weakening from already modest levels, housing is depressed, and business investment is decelerating — clearly below trend.
- Direction: Below neutral due to broad-based deceleration and depressed housing.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 52 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "the modest rise in hourly compensation in the nonfarm business sector was mostly offset by further gains in productivity."
- Center-of-gravity: Productivity is registering positive gains, enough to mostly offset compensation growth, but the mention is brief and incidental. Near trend.
- Direction: At or slightly above neutral — "further gains" suggests ongoing positive productivity growth.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 45 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "dealers pointed to a continued gradual easing over the previous three months in credit terms"; "Net debt financing by nonfinancial corporations was strong"; banks "eased some lending terms on C&I loans." But also: "market participants reported a general pullback from risk-taking and a decline in liquidity in a range of financial markets"; residential mortgage markets "remained strained."
- Center-of-gravity: Corporate credit conditions are broadly accommodative with easing terms and strong issuance, but mortgage markets remain strained and risk appetite pulled back late in the period. Net slightly accommodative.
- Direction: Below neutral (accommodative side) due to easing credit terms and strong issuance, though late-period pullback moderates this. Bright-line satisfied (< 50 for accommodative language).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 40 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Treasury yield movements reflected economic data and policy expectations — normal price discovery. "Investors seemed to anticipate that the debt ceiling would be increased in time to avoid any significant market disruptions." No language about liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.
- Center-of-gravity: Normal market functioning with yield movements driven by fundamentals. Debt ceiling mentioned but not causing stress.
- Direction: At or slightly below neutral — no dysfunction indicators.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "credit risk spreads a little wider"; but "Gross issuance of both investment- and speculative-grade bonds by nonfinancial corporations hit a record high in May"; "most indicators of business credit quality improved further"; consumer ABS "remained robust."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads widened slightly but from favorable levels, and credit availability/issuance remained very strong. Overall somewhat below-normal spread levels.
- Direction: Below neutral — credit conditions remain generally favorable despite minor widening.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 54 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes declined, on net, over the intermeeting period"; "Stock prices of financial firms underperformed the broader market"; "Option-adjusted volatility on the S&P 500 index rose somewhat."
- Center-of-gravity: Equities declined modestly in response to weak data, but no valuation distress language. A mild, orderly decline.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral (mild stress/concern) due to price declines and rising volatility, but no extreme language.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Banks eased C&I lending terms; "most indicators of business credit quality improved further"; consumer "delinquency rates on some categories moving back to pre-crisis levels." But: residential mortgage delinquencies "at elevated levels"; "Stock prices of financial firms underperformed" due to "potential credit rating downgrades" and "heightened concerns about the anticipated capital surcharge."
- Center-of-gravity: The banking system shows broad improvement in credit quality and lending, but residual stress persists in mortgage portfolios and regulatory concerns weigh on bank stocks. Near neutral with slight stress.
- Direction: Near neutral — improving fundamentals offset by lingering mortgage stress and regulatory headwinds.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Since the April FOMC meeting, the dollar rose modestly, on net, after depreciating over the preceding several months."
- Center-of-gravity: Modest appreciation over the intermeeting period, reversing part of prior depreciation.
- Direction: Above neutral because dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied (> 50 for appreciation).

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "notable increases in motor vehicle prices likely arising from the effects of recent supply chain disruptions"; "elevated prices of commodities and other imports." But: "prices of consumer food and energy decelerated" and gasoline prices declining.
- Center-of-gravity: Goods inflation elevated due to supply disruptions and commodity pass-through, though energy/food pressures easing.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: N/A**
- Not specifically discussed as a separate category.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: 28 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "measures of home prices fell further"; housing market "remained depressed."
- Center-of-gravity: Housing prices falling and market depressed — deflationary for shelter costs.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 50 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "longer-term inflation expectations remained within the range that has generally prevailed over the preceding few years"; "near-term inflation expectations... moved down a little in May and early June from the high level seen in April."
- Center-of-gravity: Expectations well-anchored at target, near-term expectations easing from a recent spike.
