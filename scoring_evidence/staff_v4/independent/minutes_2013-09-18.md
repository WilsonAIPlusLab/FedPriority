# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: September 2013 — four years into recovery from the GFC. The economy is growing at a moderate pace but substantial slack remains. Unemployment at 7.3% is well above NAIRU. Inflation running well below the 2% target. The "taper tantrum" period — markets reacting to expectations of reduced asset purchases. Fiscal sequestration weighing on growth.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Total PCE inflation at about 1.5% over 12 months, "continued to be modest." Core inflation "was modest." Wage increases "still subdued." Staff outlook projects inflation to be "subdued through 2016." This is clearly below the 2% target. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target period. Given "modest" and "subdued" language, score in 35-40 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy as BELOW potential. "Pace of expansion in real GDP this year would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output." "Expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets." "Resource slack persisting over most of the projection period." Bright-line: "slack remains" language means score MUST be < 50. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. Growth is moderate, slack persists but slowly closing — score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW full employment. Unemployment at 7.3% (well above NAIRU), "still elevated." Employment growing "at a somewhat slower pace than in the first half of the year." Mixed indicators — hiring plans up, but long-duration unemployment rose, job openings little changed. Wage increases "still subdued." With UE at 7.3%, anchor is 16-30 range (UE > 7%). Score ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. Real PCE "were flat in July." Business equipment spending "appeared to be subdued going into the third quarter." Nominal shipments of capital goods "declined again in July." Housing activity improvements "appeared to slow." Some positives: auto sales rose notably, forward indicators point to moderate gains. Net picture is below trend but not contracting. Score ~38.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "only slight gains in productivity." This suggests below trend. Score ~35.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as mixed but with some tightening. "Longer-term interest rates rose," mortgage rates increased, credit standards for mortgages "remained tight." But "credit flows to nonfinancial businesses remained solid," corporate bond and leveraged loan issuance "was robust," C&I loans "continued to expand moderately." "Conditions in short-term dollar funding markets were generally stable." Staff forecast references "further improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" (implying current conditions still somewhat restrictive). Also mentions "the recent tightening in U.S. financial market conditions" as a downside risk. The mix of rising rates, tight mortgage standards, BUT solid credit flows and stable funding markets suggests modestly tight — around 53-57. Bright-line: language leans toward "tightened" which requires > 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose ~25bp over the intermeeting period. Dealers reported that "liquidity and functioning had deteriorated in a number of fixed-income markets" during May-July volatility. Staff notes concerns about "dealer capacity and willingness to make markets in volatile conditions could again amplify price movements." This goes beyond normal yield movements — there is actual discussion of market functioning deterioration and liquidity concerns. Score ~52-55.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond yields "rose about in line with those on nominal Treasury securities" — meaning spreads were roughly unchanged. Credit flows "remained solid," issuance "was robust," CMBS "remained strong despite slightly wider spreads." CDS for largest banks "remained stable at levels near the bottom of their range over the past few years." Spreads are generally favorable/low. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 generally favorable. Score ~35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity prices "were fairly volatile but ended the period modestly higher." Financial-sector firms "underperformed the broad equity market somewhat." No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 45-55 range. Score ~48.

**BANKING_STRESS**: CDS for largest bank holding companies "remained stable at levels near the bottom of their range over the past few years." Staff: "loss-absorbing capital had increased and the reliance on short-term funding and the exposure of financial institutions to nonfinancial credit risk had decreased." System "vulnerability...appeared moderate." This is clearly low stress — healthy, well-capitalized. Bright-line: "well-capitalized" language means < 50. Score ~30.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar ended little changed on a trade-weighted basis against a broad set of currencies, but it appreciated notably against the currencies of India, Indonesia, and Turkey." Staff forecast was revised reflecting "higher projected paths for...the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Mixed — broadly stable but with some appreciation against select EME currencies. Bright-line: mixed, lean slightly toward strength. Score ~52.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Food prices "only edged up," energy "little changed," gasoline "moved down." Goods inflation running very low. Score ~30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "The pace of home price appreciation moderated a bit in July, although it was still strong." Home prices rising but this is asset prices not shelter CPI. Shelter inflation not directly discussed in inflation section. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Both near-term and longer-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey were little changed." Well-anchored. Score ~47.

### Polarity checks:

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total U.S. inflation, as measured by the PCE price index...was about 1-1/2 percent over the preceding 12-month period." "Core consumer price inflation...was modest in July and August." Staff outlook: "inflation was forecast to be subdued through 2016."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below the 2% target at about 1.5%, with staff expecting it to remain subdued for years — clearly below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running at 1.5%, well under 2% target, described as "modest" and "subdued."

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The pace of expansion in real GDP this year would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output." "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets." "Resource slack persisting over most of the projection period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing moderately but significant slack persists in both labor and product markets, with only slow closure expected through 2016.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff repeatedly references "slack" persisting. Bright-line satisfied: "slack remains" -> score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined further to 7.3 percent in August" but was "still elevated." "Increases in nominal wages were still subdued." "Increases in average hourly earnings for all employees were fairly slow on balance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 7.3% is well above full employment, with subdued wages confirming substantial labor market slack despite gradual improvement.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 7.3% signals significant slack. Rubric anchor for UE > 7% is 16-30; score of 30 reflects some improvement (declining UE, rising hiring plans) while respecting the elevated level.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures were flat in July." "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to be subdued going into the third quarter." "Improvements in housing-sector activity appeared to slow."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending flat, business investment subdued, and housing slowing — all below trend despite some bright spots in auto sales and forward-looking indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending and investment are described as flat-to-subdued across all major categories.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "There were only slight gains in productivity."
**Center-of-gravity**: A single sentence indicates below-trend productivity growth, but limited discussion.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because "only slight gains" implies below the historical trend of ~2%.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Staff outlook references "the recent tightening in U.S. financial market conditions" as a downside risk. "Longer-term interest rates rose over the intermeeting period." "Credit standards for mortgage loans remained tight." However, "credit flows to nonfinancial businesses remained solid" and "conditions in short-term dollar funding markets were generally stable."
**Center-of-gravity**: Overall conditions have tightened due to rising rates and tight mortgage credit, though credit availability for businesses remains solid. Modestly tight.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff explicitly identifies "tightening in U.S. financial market conditions." Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "During the period of heightened volatility beginning in May and extending into early July, liquidity and functioning had deteriorated in a number of fixed-income markets." Staff flagged concerns about "dealer capacity and willingness to make markets in volatile conditions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Beyond normal yield movements, staff identifies actual deterioration in market liquidity and functioning during the taper tantrum period, though conditions stabilized somewhat by September.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral because staff discusses actual market functioning deterioration, not just yield movements. The deterioration is retrospective (May-July) and not ongoing crisis-level.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on credit default swaps for the largest bank holding companies remained stable at levels near the bottom of their range over the past few years." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds and leveraged loans was robust in August." "Commercial mortgage-backed securities remained strong despite slightly wider spreads."
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads remain compressed by historical standards, with robust issuance across multiple markets. Slightly wider CMBS spreads are the only negative note.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are "near the bottom of their range" — clearly narrow/favorable.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 48 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Equity prices were fairly volatile but ended the period modestly higher." "Stock prices for financial-sector firms underperformed the broad equity market somewhat."
**Center-of-gravity**: No valuation commentary from staff; only price movements described. Slight volatility with net gains suggests near-neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because staff provides no valuation assessment, only price-level changes.

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Loss-absorbing capital had increased and the reliance on short-term funding and the exposure of financial institutions to nonfinancial credit risk had decreased." CDS for largest BHCs "remained stable at levels near the bottom of their range over the past few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system health is clearly positive — increased capital, reduced risk exposures, and low CDS spreads all point to low stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because capital has increased and risk metrics are near multi-year lows. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar ended little changed on a trade-weighted basis against a broad set of currencies, but it appreciated notably against the currencies of India, Indonesia, and Turkey." Staff forecast revised reflecting "higher projected paths for...the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar broadly stable with selective appreciation against EME currencies. Staff projects a stronger dollar path going forward.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is near neutral to slightly above because the dollar was "little changed" broadly but appreciated against select currencies and the projected path was revised higher.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer food prices only edged up in July and August, while energy prices were little changed, on net...and retail gasoline prices moved down in the first half of September."
**Center-of-gravity**: Food and energy prices showing minimal increases, with gasoline declining — well below typical goods inflation.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — goods prices showing very limited upward pressure.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Shelter/housing inflation component not discussed in the inflation section. Home price appreciation is mentioned in housing activity context but is asset prices, not CPI shelter.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Both near-term and longer-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey were little changed in August and early September."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations firmly anchored — stable and unchanging. Slightly below 50 because actual inflation is running below target, so "stable" expectations are anchored slightly below the 2% target level in practice.
**Direction confirmation**: Near neutral, well-anchored expectations.
