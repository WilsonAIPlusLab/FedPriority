# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2014-03-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Post-2005 segmented minutes. Three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: March 2014. The US economy is in the middle phase of the post-GFC recovery. Unemployment remains elevated at 6.7%, well above the natural rate. The Fed is in the process of tapering QE3 asset purchases. Inflation is running well below the 2% target. Winter weather is complicating the data picture.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at "about 1-1/4 percent" over 12 months, "continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." Core PCE "increased just a bit more than 1 percent." Wages "remained subdued." Staff forecasts inflation "would stay below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." This is clearly below target with no upward pressure. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. Given 1.25% headline and ~1% core, this is more like 30-35 range -- meaningfully below target.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low inflation. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. "Slow reduction in resource slack over the projection period," "unemployment rate was expected to decline gradually to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate," growth was "somewhat lower" than projected. Staff explicitly describes "slack in labor and product markets." GDP growth slowed early this year.
- Bright-line check: "slack remains" language present -> score MUST be < 50. Score in 35-42 range (2010-14 recovery anchor).
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy below potential. Score below 50. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE but improving. UE at 6.7% -- well above full employment. Payroll growth slowed. Part-time for economic reasons and long-duration unemployment "still high." Wage growth "subdued." Labor force participation ticked up.
- With UE at 6.7%, this falls in the 31-45 range per the rubric (UE 5.5-7%). Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor. Market is loose. Score below 50. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED/SOFT. PCE "increased a little, on net." Housing sector "appeared to soften" with starts lower. Business equipment spending "stepped up" in Q4 but softened in January. Forward indicators point to "modest increases." Nonresidential structures "essentially unchanged." Weather effects complicate the picture, but underlying tone is below-trend to moderate.
- Score in 38-44 range -- below trend but not severely weak.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Spending is soft. Score below 50. Correct.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "with some gains in labor productivity, unit labor costs declined a little." This is a passing reference, not a detailed discussion. The mention of "gains" suggests modestly positive but not elaborated on.
- Score around 48-52 or N/A. Given it's only one sentence, I'll score it with low confidence around 50.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states: "U.S. financial conditions remained supportive of growth in economic activity and employment." Treasury yields edged down, equity prices rose, corporate spreads narrowed, dollar depreciated slightly. Short-term funding "remained stable." Credit flows "robust."
- Bright-line check: "supportive" -> score MUST be < 50. This is accommodative/loose. Score in 30-38 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are supportive/loose. Score below 50. Correct.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields were "little changed" or "edged down." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in Treasuries. ON RRP exercise operated normally. This is calm, orderly functioning.
- Score in 25-35 range (2010-15 calm markets anchor).
- Bright-line check: No dysfunction mentioned. Normal yield movements only. Score 36-50 at most, but given explicit calm, 25-35 is appropriate.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Speculative-grade corporate bond spreads narrowed." Corporate bond issuance "rebounded" and was robust. Leveraged loan issuance at "brisk pace." CRE loans increasing. Municipal spreads "little changed." Credit flows "robust."
- Spreads narrowing, credit flowing freely. Score in 25-35 range (2013-19 generally favorable anchor, on the lower end given explicit narrowing and robust issuance).
- Bright-line check: "narrowed" -> score MUST be < 50. Correct.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose over the intermeeting period." Supported by earnings and risk appetite. No valuation concern language from staff.
- No valuation commentary, just price movement. Default to 45-50 per rubric. Prices rose, which if anything is less stress. Score ~45.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable." CRE loans increased. C&I loans growing. No mention of banking stress. Residential mortgage "stayed tight" but with "incremental signs of easing." Consumer credit "mixed" but auto loans "broadly available."

- Bright-line check: No stress language. Below 50. Correct.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar depreciated slightly." "Most foreign currencies appreciated against the dollar."
- Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation -> score MUST be < 50. "Slightly" = modest move. Score ~42-45.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar weakened. Score below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer price inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, was about 1-1/4 percent over the 12 months ending in January, continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Core PCE prices...increased just a bit more than 1 percent." "The staff continued to forecast that inflation would stay below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below 2% target at both headline and core, with subdued wage growth and a forecast to remain below target for years.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because both headline (~1.25%) and core (~1%) PCE are well below the 2% target with no upward pressures evident.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to lead to a slow reduction in resource slack over the projection period." "Slack in labor and product markets was anticipated to diminish slowly." "Real GDP growth in the first half of this year was somewhat lower than in the projection for the January meeting."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating below potential with significant slack remaining. Growth revised down but still expected to exceed potential going forward, implying gradual gap closure.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "resource slack" and "below potential" conditions. Bright-line rule satisfied (slack remains -> < 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 36 +-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 6.7 percent in February." "Both the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons and the rate of long-duration unemployment...were still high." "Increases in nominal wages remained subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has significant slack with UE at 6.7%, elevated part-time and long-duration unemployment, and no wage pressure. Improvement occurring but from a weak level.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 6.7% is well above full employment with broad labor market slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 41 +-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures increased a little, on net, in December and January." "The pace of activity in the housing sector appeared to soften." "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products stepped up in the fourth quarter."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending was tepid, housing softened, and business investment was mixed (Q4 pickup but January softening). Weather effects add uncertainty but underlying tone is below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending across major categories was soft to modest, with housing explicitly weakening.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 50 +-10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "With some gains in labor productivity, unit labor costs declined a little."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Only a brief, passing mention of "some gains" in productivity. Insufficient detail for a confident score. Scored at neutral.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is at neutral given only a single sentence mentioning modest gains without elaboration.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 33 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "U.S. financial conditions remained supportive of growth in economic activity and employment." "Equity prices rose, speculative-grade corporate bond spreads narrowed." "Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations remained robust."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly characterizes conditions as "supportive" with easing across multiple channels -- equities up, spreads narrowing, credit flowing freely.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose/accommodative) because staff explicitly says conditions are "supportive." Bright-line rule satisfied ("supportive" -> < 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Yields on short- and intermediate-term Treasury securities were little changed." "Yields on longer-term Treasury securities edged down." No mention of market dysfunction or liquidity issues.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning smoothly with orderly yield movements. No stress indicators.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because no dysfunction is mentioned; yields moved in small, orderly fashion.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Speculative-grade corporate bond spreads narrowed." "Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations remained robust." "Institutional issuance of leveraged loans continued at a brisk pace." "Issuance of commercial mortgage-backed securities was robust."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads narrowing with robust issuance across corporate bonds, leveraged loans, and CMBS. Credit conditions clearly favorable.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads narrowed and credit is flowing freely. Bright-line rule satisfied ("narrowed" -> < 50).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +-8 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes rose over the intermeeting period, apparently boosted by a solid finish to the corporate earnings season." "Equity prices were also supported by a broad increase in investors' willingness to take riskier positions."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose on earnings and risk appetite. No staff valuation concern language. Default near neutral with slight lean toward no-stress given rising prices.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral/slightly below because staff reports price increases without distress or valuation concern.

### BANKING_STRESS: 35 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable." "The growth of commercial and industrial loans on banks' balance sheets increased." "Financing conditions in residential mortgage markets stayed tight, even as further incremental signs of easing emerged."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system operating without stress. Lending growing across C&I and CRE. Mortgage market still tight but easing. No bank-specific stress mentioned. Minor concern from tight mortgage conditions prevents a lower score.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because system is stable and lending is expanding. Bright-line rule satisfied (no stress language -> < 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 43 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar depreciated slightly." "Most foreign currencies appreciated against the dollar as flight-to-safety flows reversed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar weakened modestly as EME concerns eased and risk appetite returned. "Slightly" indicates a small move.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied (depreciation -> < 50).
