# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 1995-02-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from the February 1, 1995 FOMC meeting. It is **unsegmented** (pre-2005), meaning staff and participant content are mixed together. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable language from member/participant discussion.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following sections contain staff-attributable content:
- Lines 164-272: "The information reviewed at this meeting..." -- This is the standard staff economic review section in pre-2005 minutes.
- Lines 275-297: Prior directive and open market operations -- factual/staff reporting.
- Lines 300-343: Market developments and monetary aggregates -- staff/manager reporting.
- Lines 346-367: "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." -- Explicit staff forecast.
- Line 768-770: "According to a staff analysis, the reserve conditions contemplated..."

Content from "In the Committee's discussion..." (line 370 onward) is **member discussion** and should NOT be scored, except where it directly attributes views to staff. However, lines 419-438 contain member commentary on financial conditions that I will note but not use as primary scoring evidence. The member discussion does contain references to the staff view (e.g., "the economy currently was operating beyond its long-run, noninflationary capacity" at line 365 is staff language).

### Era Context
February 1995: Late in the 1990s expansion. The Fed had been raising rates throughout 1994 (from 3% to 5.5%). The economy was growing strongly, unemployment was at 5.4%, capacity utilization was high. Inflation at the finished goods level was moderate but pipeline pressures (materials, intermediate goods) were building. The Mexican peso crisis was unfolding.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as modestly above neutral because: Consumer prices rose "moderately" overall, core CPI rose "significantly less in 1994 than in 1993," and labor costs "remained moderate." However, pipeline pressures are building: "price inflation at earlier stages of production picked up in 1994," crude materials "rose rapidly," and the staff forecast explicitly states "the economy currently was operating beyond its long-run, noninflationary capacity, and there remained a substantial risk that inflation could ratchet higher." The staff sees current headline inflation as contained but building risk.
- Direction: Modestly above neutral. Current inflation is moderate but pipeline pressures and the staff's own risk assessment of ratcheting higher put this above 50.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees moderate current inflation but significant pipeline/risk pressures. Score above 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff explicitly states: "the economy currently was operating beyond its long-run, noninflationary capacity" (line 365). This is a clear "above potential" statement.
- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "above potential" language --> score MUST be > 50.
- The staff forecast says growth would "slow substantially" and "for some period thereafter would average less than the rate of increase in the economy's potential output" -- but this is a forecast of future deceleration, not current state. Currently, the economy is ABOVE potential.
- Direction: Above potential. "Strong further rise in economic activity," capacity utilization "above already high levels."
- Magnitude: "beyond its long-run, noninflationary capacity" is a clear above-potential statement but not extreme. Late 90s anchor: 57-63. Score in the 57-62 range.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Unemployment at 5.4%, declining. "Nonfarm payroll employment advanced considerably further in December after a sharp rise in November." "Substantial job gains" in services, retail, manufacturing. Factory overtime "matching the highest level reached in the history of this series." Staff refers to "shrinking margins of unemployed resources."
- Direction: Above neutral -- labor market is tight. UE at 5.4% in 1995 is near or slightly below NAIRU estimates of that era (~5.5-6%).
- The rubric says UE 4.5-5% maps to ~46-52 (near NAIRU). At 5.4% with "shrinking margins" and record overtime, this is in the tight range.
- Score: 53-58. The labor market is tight but not extremely so -- 5.4% unemployment in 1995 with strong job gains and record overtime suggests moderately tight.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Consumer spending: "less buoyant," retail sales "changed little over November and December" -- this is softening. But business investment "grown at a very rapid pace in the fourth quarter." Exports remained brisk. Inventories brisk. Housing starts "sizable gains." Overall: "strong further rise in economic activity."
- Direction: Above trend. Business investment very strong, housing holding up, consumer spending merely flattening from strong levels.
- Magnitude: "strong further rise," "very rapid pace" for business investment. This is clearly above trend but consumer softening moderates the net.
- Score: 58-63.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff mentions "perhaps to some degree by various structural changes and productivity improvements in recent years that may have raised both the level and the rate of increase of the economy's potential" -- but this is in the member discussion section (line 556-558). The staff review section doesn't directly discuss productivity trends. Limited evidence.
- Direction: Slightly above trend based on the reference to productivity improvements, but this is weak/indirect evidence and partly from member discussion.
- Score: N/A -- insufficient staff-specific discussion.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
From staff/manager reporting: "Most other market interest rates declined slightly on balance." "Strong earning reports for the fourth quarter boosted major indexes of equity prices." Yields on tax-exempt instruments "declined considerably." The federal funds rate was at 5.5%.
From member discussion (which I note but weight less): "financial conditions remained broadly supportive of further economic expansion," "accommodative lending policies of banking institutions," "relatively narrow quality spreads."
- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "supportive" / "accommodative" --> MUST be < 50. While this is from member discussion, the staff data on declining rates, strong equity markets, and narrow spreads all confirm accommodative conditions.
- Direction: Below neutral (accommodative/loose).
- Magnitude: "broadly supportive," "accommodative lending" -- moderate language. Score in the 30-38 range.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff reports: "Most other market interest rates declined slightly on balance." No mention of any Treasury market dysfunction, liquidity issues, or settlement problems. Yields moved in orderly fashion responding to economic data.
- Direction: No stress. Normal market functioning.
- Score: N/A -- Treasury market functioning not specifically discussed beyond normal yield movements.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "narrow" spreads --> MUST be < 50.

- Magnitude: "relatively narrow" -- moderately compressed.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff reports: "Strong earning reports for the fourth quarter boosted major indexes of equity prices." No valuation concern expressed by staff.
- Direction: No particular concern. Equities rising on fundamentals (earnings).
- Score: 46-50. No staff valuation concern. Default neutral.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Member discussion mentions "accommodative lending policies of banking institutions" and concern that "a number of banks might have eased their lending standards unduly and thus assumed unwarranted risks." This is member content, but suggests banks are healthy/aggressive lenders -- low stress, possibly excessive risk-taking.
- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "accommodative" lending suggests healthy banks --> score MUST be < 50.
- Direction: Low stress. Banks are actively lending, perhaps too aggressively.
- Score: 25-35. Banks are healthy and lending aggressively -- low stress territory, possibly approaching excessive risk-taking territory.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff/manager reports: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the other G-10 currencies declined somewhat over the intermeeting period." "The dollar fell substantially against the mark." Mexican peso crisis was a factor.
- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: Dollar depreciation/weakening --> MUST be < 50.
- Direction: Below neutral (dollar weakening).
- Magnitude: "declined somewhat" for trade-weighted, "fell substantially" against mark. Mixed magnitude -- "somewhat" overall.
- Score: 35-40.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad indexes of prices for consumer goods and services had risen moderately on average over recent months despite shrinking margins of unemployed resources and further sizable increases in the prices of many materials." (staff review)
- "the economy currently was operating beyond its long-run, noninflationary capacity, and there remained a substantial risk that inflation could ratchet higher absent further monetary policy actions." (staff forecast)

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees current finished-goods inflation as moderate but identifies significant pipeline pressures and substantial risk of acceleration, placing overall inflation pressure modestly above neutral.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because the staff explicitly identifies the economy as beyond noninflationary capacity with risk of ratcheting higher, even though current broad price indexes are moderate.
