# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-11-08

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clear "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: November 2018 — late expansion. Unemployment at 3.7%, PCE inflation at 2.0% target, economy running above potential with gradual rate hikes underway. Trade tensions with China escalating, equity market volatility rising. This is a "goldilocks but aging" cycle environment.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff reports PCE at exactly 2.0% and core PCE at 2.0%. CPI 2.3%, core CPI 2.2%. Staff expects inflation to "remain close to 2 percent through the medium term." Inflation expectations "little changed on balance." This is textbook AT target — score should be right at 50. Direction: at midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says "output was forecast to rise at a rate above or at the staff's estimate of potential growth" over 2018-20. "Real GDP rose at a strong rate in the third quarter." Unemployment "projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Economy "projected to move further above its potential." Bright-line: "above potential" language present — score MUST be > 50. Direction: above midpoint. The language is moderate — "further above its potential" suggests modestly above. Score ~55-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 3.7%. "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." Job openings and quits "remained at high levels." Initial claims "close to historically low levels." Wage growth: ECI 2.9%, AHE 3.1%. Staff says "labor market conditions already tight." UE below 4% — score should be well above 55 per rubric. With 3.7% UE, historically low claims, high quits, and wage acceleration toward 3%, this is clearly tight. Direction: well above midpoint. Score ~63-67.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Real PCE continued to grow strongly in the third quarter." Light vehicle sales "robust." Consumer sentiment "upbeat." BUT "real residential investment declined further," business equipment spending "moderated," and nonresidential structures "declined." Mixed picture: strong consumption offset by weaker investment. Net position: moderately above trend for consumer side, below trend on investment side. Direction: roughly at or slightly above midpoint. Score ~52-55.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households remained supportive of economic activity on balance." SLOOS: banks eased standards on C&I loans. CRE conditions "remained accommodative." Consumer credit "remained supportive of growth." Mortgage conditions "accommodative" for most but "somewhat tight for borrowers with low credit scores." Bright-line: "supportive" and "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50. But not extremely loose — more like normally accommodative for late 2018. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~38-42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Nominal Treasury yields ended the period higher amid some moderate volatility." No mention of liquidity problems, settlement issues, auction failures. Yield moves are from policy expectations — normal price adjustment. Direction: at or slightly below midpoint (normal functioning). Score ~42-45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened only modestly." Despite considerable equity declines, spread widening was modest. CMBS spreads "near their post-crisis lows." Bright-line: overall spreads still relatively contained. The "modestly" widened language suggests slightly above the very compressed pre-intermeeting levels but still in favorable territory. Direction: slightly below midpoint. Score ~38-42.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Domestic stock prices declined considerably, on net." "Investors seemed to reassess equity valuations that appeared elevated." VIX increased but "remained below the levels seen in early February." Staff notes valuations "appeared elevated" even after decline, and financial stability report says "asset valuation pressures continued to be elevated." This is a valuation concern — staff sees valuations as elevated (overvaluation side). The "considerable" declines introduce some stress/concern element. The staff financial stability assessment says valuations are "elevated" — this points toward the overvaluation-concern end (lower score per rubric where lower = more overvaluation concern). Direction: below midpoint (elevated valuations/overvaluation concern). Score ~30-35.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Financial stability report: "vulnerabilities from financial-sector leverage and maturity and liquidity transformation remained low." Banks eased lending standards (competitive lending). "Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations remained solid, though there were some signs of modest deterioration." Nonfinancial business leverage "elevated" and "pickup in issuance of risky debt and continued deterioration in underwriting standards on leveraged loans." Bright-line: financial-sector leverage is "low" but nonfinancial leverage "elevated" — the banking system itself is healthy but there are concerns about credit quality. Overall banking stress: low. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~35-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index moved up." "The dollar appreciated against most advanced and emerging market currencies." Bright-line: appreciation → score MUST be > 50. Direction: above midpoint. Score ~57-60.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately broken out beyond "food and energy" exclusion mention. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment declining, mortgage rates rising, but no specific shelter/housing inflation discussion in price terms. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Inflation compensation "declined somewhat." Well-anchored at target. Score ~48-50.

### Polarity Checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Above potential" → score > 50. CHECK.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. "Supportive/accommodative" → score < 50. CHECK.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress/wider. "Widened only modestly" from low levels → still below normal stress → score < 50. CHECK.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Financial-sector leverage "low" → score < 50. CHECK.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. "Appreciated" → score > 50. CHECK.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Normal yield movements → score < 50 or around 40-45. CHECK.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (57) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap which is reasonable in a tight-labor late expansion. Gap of ~8 points — coherent.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (57) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (53): Both above 50, investment moderation keeping C_I lower. Gap of ~4 points — coherent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (40): Both below 50, accommodative conditions with contained spreads. Coherent.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (40) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (43): Both near/below midpoint. Coherent.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (50) ↔ INFLATION sub-dimensions: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (~49). Consistent with parent at 50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer prices, as measured by the PCE price index, increased 2.0 percent over the 12 months ending in September. Core PCE price inflation... also was 2.0 percent." Staff forecast: "both total and core PCE price inflation to remain close to 2 percent through the medium term."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation exactly at the 2% target with stable expectations and no change in the forecast — textbook neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is at neutral because both headline and core PCE are exactly at 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 57 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Over the 2018–20 period, output was forecast to rise at a rate above or at the staff's estimate of potential growth." "The unemployment rate was projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Economy "projected to move further above its potential."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly places the economy above potential with further above-potential growth expected, but language is moderate — not "significantly" or "substantially" above.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says economy is "above its potential." Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The national unemployment rate decreased to 3.7 percent." "The rates of private-sector job openings and quits both remained at high levels." "Average hourly earnings for all employees rose 3.1 percent." Staff says "labor market conditions already tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 3.7% well below NAIRU, multiple indicators of tightness, wage growth accelerating toward 3% — clearly tight but not unprecedented. Consistent with 2018-19 anchor of 55-62, pushed higher by 3.7% UE and explicit "tight" characterization.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE is 3.7%, below natural rate, with staff explicitly calling conditions "tight."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE continued to grow strongly in the third quarter." But "real residential investment declined further" and "growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property moderated." Nonresidential structures "declined."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending offset by weakening residential and moderating business investment. Net position slightly above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral — strong consumption dominates but investment weakness moderates the overall picture.

**Center-of-gravity**: Multiple sectors described as "supportive" or "accommodative." Banks actively easing lending standards. Some pockets of tightening (mortgage rates, credit card standards) but overall clearly accommodative.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff uses "supportive" and "accommodative" language. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 43 ±5 [Moderate confidence]

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because yield movements are normal price adjustment, not dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened only modestly." "CMBS spreads remained near their post-crisis lows." "Weak speculative-grade bond issuance was largely offset by rapid leveraged loan issuance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened modestly from very low levels — still in the generally favorable range. CMBS at post-crisis lows. Credit broadly available.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because despite modest widening, spreads remain in the favorable/accommodative range. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 32 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic stock prices declined considerably, on net." "Investors seemed to reassess equity valuations that appeared elevated." Financial stability report: "vulnerabilities associated with asset valuation pressures continued to be elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly flags valuations as "elevated" even after considerable declines, and the financial stability report identifies asset valuation pressures as a vulnerability. This is an overvaluation concern assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (overvaluation concern territory) because staff explicitly says valuations "appeared elevated" and flags valuation pressures as elevated vulnerability.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because financial-sector leverage is "low" — banking system is sound. Bright-line satisfied.
