### Era Context
August 1994: Mid-cycle tightening. The Fed had begun raising rates in February 1994. Economy expanding solidly, operating near potential. Unemployment at 6.1% (higher than later 1990s). Inflation moderate by broad measures but pipeline pressures building at intermediate/crude materials level.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff says: "Increases in broad indexes of consumer and producer prices had remained moderate in recent months, apart from the effects of short-run swings in the volatile food and energy components." Core CPI and core PPI showed little change year-over-year. However, intermediate materials prices posted "another sizable gain" and had increased at a faster rate than 1993. Employment cost index rose more rapidly in Q2 but four-quarter change was "little changed." Staff forecast: "trends in the core rate of inflation would not deviate significantly from recent experience."
Direction: Inflation is roughly AT the midpoint — broad measures moderate/stable, but pipeline pressures from intermediate goods are building. Slightly above neutral given the intermediate materials pressures.
Score range: 50-55.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff says: "the economy was operating close to its long-run capacity" and growth would "trend lower... to a rate generally in line with the increase in its potential." Also: "Resource utilization was at elevated levels, with factories operating at relatively high rates and labor markets evidencing very low levels of slack."
Bright-line check: "operating close to its long-run capacity" + "elevated levels" of resource utilization + "very low levels of slack" → at or slightly above potential → score 50-55.
Direction: Staff sees economy at or slightly above potential.
Score: 53.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff says: "labor markets evidencing very low levels of slack." Unemployment rate at 6.1% — edged up but "little changed from the average for the second quarter." Nonfarm payroll "continued to advance at a robust pace." Capacity utilization "at a high level, with most major industry groups operating at or near capacity."
In 1994, NAIRU was generally estimated around 5.5-6.0%. At 6.1%, the labor market was near but not yet clearly below NAIRU. "Very low levels of slack" is staff's characterization though. The hiring was robust.
Direction: Labor market is modestly tight — near full employment, robust hiring, "very low levels of slack."
Score: 53-55. UE at 6.1% in 1994 context with "very low levels of slack" language suggests near NAIRU.
Score: 54.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff says: Consumer spending "slowed in recent months" partly due to motor vehicle supply constraints. Retail sales "edged lower in July." BUT business fixed investment "expanded... at about the same brisk pace" with "strong expansion in spending on business equipment" ahead. Housing starts retraced part of decline but below Q4 1993 elevated rate. Staff forecast: consumer spending projected to slow, business investment "relatively brisk" but decelerating.
Net: Mixed — consumer slowing, business investment strong. Overall pace "still substantial" but moderating.
Direction: Modestly above trend. "Still substantial" expansion with strong business investment offsetting consumer softening.
Score: 55.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**

The member discussion references "ongoing efforts to improve productivity through business restructurings" but that is member content.
Direction: N/A — not discussed in staff-attributable sections with enough specificity.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**

"Total domestic nonfinancial debt continued to expand at a moderate pace." Interest rates had risen since start of year (policy tightening). Staff forecast notes "higher financing costs" and "adverse effects on household financial wealth of earlier increases in interest rates."
The member-attributable section says "financial climate remained supportive" and banks "increasingly aggressive" in lending — but this is member content.
From staff content: rates up, some restraint from higher rates on housing, but credit still flowing at moderate pace. The staff forecast projects higher financing costs dampening investment and housing.
Bright-line check: The staff doesn't use "accommodative" or "tight" explicitly in staff-attributable sections. The conditions appear modestly tightening from the rate increases but not restrictive.
Direction: Roughly neutral to slightly tight — rates have risen, some effect on housing, but credit still expanding.
Score: 52.
