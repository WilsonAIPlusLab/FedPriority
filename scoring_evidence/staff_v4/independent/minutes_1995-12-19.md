# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — December 19, 1995

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from a **pre-2005 unsegmented** meeting. Staff review content is mixed with participant discussion and policy action. I must score ONLY staff-attributable portions, identified by general information review language ("the information available," "the staff forecast," data descriptions without "members noted" framing) and explicitly marked staff forecast paragraphs. Committee discussion begins at "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective developments" and must be excluded.

### Era Context
December 1995: Mid-1990s expansion. The economy had grown strongly in Q3 1995 and was now slowing. Unemployment at 5.6%. The Fed was in a mild easing cycle (this meeting resulted in a slight easing). Inflation was relatively subdued. This is part of the long 1990s expansion that would eventually produce the late-1990s boom.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff says: "Consumer prices had risen relatively slowly in recent months" and "consumer prices excluding food and energy were up only slightly." Also: "increases in labor compensation had remained comparatively subdued." However, producer prices posted "a relatively large increase in November" and the ex-food-and-energy component "posted its largest rise since January." Consumers expected "less inflation over the year ahead and also over the next five to ten years." Staff forecast: "the underlying trend of price inflation was seen as unlikely to change significantly over the projection period."

Direction: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint — subdued, slow consumer price increases, subdued wages. The PPI blip is noted but does not dominate the narrative. Score should be below 50 but not dramatically so, since staff doesn't describe deflation risk. "Relatively slowly" and "subdued" = modestly below. Score ~38.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says subdued/slow. Score below 50 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff says: "expansion of economic activity had slowed substantially after a strong gain in such activity during the third quarter." Staff forecast: "the economy was still projected to expand at a pace that would keep activity close to the economy's potential." Also: "rates of utilization of labor and capital resources would remain relatively high."

Direction: Staff sees economy operating close to potential with high resource utilization. "Close to the economy's potential" with "relatively high" utilization rates suggests at or slightly above potential. Score ~52.

Bright-line check: "close to potential" with "high utilization" — this is near potential leaning slightly above. Score 50-54 is appropriate.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff data: Unemployment rate at 5.6% in November. "Nonfarm payroll employment continued to grow at a pace roughly in line with the expansion of the labor force." Some declines in factory and government jobs. Staff forecast: "rates of utilization of labor and capital resources would remain relatively high."

Direction: UE at 5.6% in 1995 — NAIRU estimates at the time were around 5.5-6%. This suggests roughly near full employment or slightly below. Payroll growth only matching labor force growth = not tightening further. The rubric says UE 5.5-7% = 31-45. But the staff says utilization is "relatively high," and the 5.6% rate in the 1990s context was near NAIRU estimates. I'll score ~46, near NAIRU/balanced.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence: near NAIRU, high utilization but not straining. Score near 50 matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Consumer spending: "somewhat reduced gains in recent months," retail sales "advancing at a slower pace than the average rate in the second and third quarters." Business fixed investment: "continuing to grow at a rapid pace" with "vigorous expansion in spending for office and computing equipment," though "recent gains in total business investment had moderated from the extraordinary pace." Nonresidential construction "continuing to advance briskly." Housing: "tended to stabilize after several months of considerable strengthening."

Direction: Mixed — consumer spending moderating/softer, but business investment still strong (though decelerating from extraordinary). Net position: still positive momentum but slowing. "Robust advances in business fixed investment continued to provide considerable impetus" + "somewhat reduced gains" in consumption. This nets to roughly at-trend or slightly above, but with a decelerating tone. Score ~48.

Direction: Declining interest rates, rising stock and bond prices, lower mortgage rates — these all point to accommodative/loose financial conditions. The bright-line rule says "accommodative"/"supportive"/"easy" must be < 50. Score ~35.

Bright-line check: Conditions are accommodative/supportive with declining rates and rising asset prices. Score < 50 confirmed.

This is an operational issue related to debt ceiling, not market dysfunction per se. Longer-term rates declined. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**Evidence**: "Consumer prices had risen relatively slowly in recent months"; "consumer prices excluding food and energy were up only slightly"; "increases in labor compensation had remained comparatively subdued."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes a picture of contained inflation with slow consumer price increases and subdued wages, modestly below the 2% trend anchor.
**Direction confirmation**: Inflation pressure is below neutral because staff uses "relatively slowly" and "subdued" language consistently across prices and wages.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "the economy was still projected to expand at a pace that would keep activity close to the economy's potential"; "rates of utilization of labor and capital resources would remain relatively high."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees the economy operating close to potential with high resource utilization — essentially at or marginally above potential.
**Direction confirmation**: Output gap is at/marginally above neutral because staff says "close to potential" with "relatively high" utilization. Bright-line: no explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language; "close to potential" = 48-52 range; high utilization nudges slightly above.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 46 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged up to 5.6 percent in November"; "Nonfarm payroll employment continued to grow at a pace roughly in line with the expansion of the labor force."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near NAIRU with payroll growth matching labor force growth — balanced conditions with UE at 5.6%, near estimated natural rate for this era.
**Direction confirmation**: Labor tightness is roughly at neutral because UE at 5.6% was near the era's NAIRU estimate (~5.5-6%), and employment growth was only keeping pace with labor force expansion.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "robust advances in business fixed investment continued to provide considerable impetus to the economy"; "the available information on consumer expenditures pointed to somewhat reduced gains in recent months."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong business investment partially offset by moderating consumer spending and stabilizing housing, netting to roughly trend-level activity with a slight cooling bias.
**Direction confirmation**: Consumption/investment is at/slightly below neutral because the strong investment component is offset by softening consumer spending, yielding a near-trend net assessment.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Most other short-term interest rates fell slightly and longer-term interest rates extended earlier declines"; "major indexes of stock prices continued to move higher in concert with the rise in bond prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Declining rates across the yield curve, rising equity and bond prices, and lower mortgage rates all indicate accommodative financial conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: Financial conditions are below neutral (loose/accommodative) because rates declined, asset prices rose, and mortgage rates fell — all supportive. Bright-line: accommodative conditions = must be < 50. Confirmed.

**Direction confirmation**: Treasury stress is at/slightly below neutral because there is no evidence of market dysfunction — only transitory settlement issues and normal yield movements.

**Direction confirmation**: Credit spreads are below neutral (favorable) because borrowers are accessing markets on attractive terms. Low confidence due to absence of explicit spread commentary.
