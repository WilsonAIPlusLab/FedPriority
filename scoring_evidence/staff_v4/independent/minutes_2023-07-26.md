### Era Context
July 2023: The Fed is deep into a tightening cycle. Inflation has been declining from the 2022 peaks but remains well above the 2% target. The labor market is still very tight with unemployment at 3.6%. The banking stress from SVB in March 2023 has partially resolved but left residual effects on credit conditions. The economy has proven more resilient than expected — staff dropped their mild recession forecast at this meeting.

The word "elevated" is used repeatedly. At 3.8-4.8% readings, this is meaningfully above target but substantially below the 2022 peaks (which anchored at 82-88). Direction: above 50, in the 65-72 range. The easing trajectory tempers slightly but the rubric says score the current level ("elevated"), not the forecast. Score: ~68.

However, private domestic final purchases "appeared to have decelerated." Staff dropped their mild recession call and upgraded the forecast as "stronger than the June projection." They project GDP growth below potential in 2024-2025, implying currently at or slightly above potential. The labor market being "very tight" suggests demand exceeds supply capacity. No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for current conditions. Score: ~52. The economy is growing moderately, slightly above potential given the tight labor market context.

**Bright-line check**: No explicit "above/below potential" language for current output. The tight labor market and dropped recession call suggest marginally above potential. Score 52 is consistent.

No language of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. This is normal market functioning. Score: ~38. N/A could also be justified, but there is enough discussion of Treasury market functioning to score.

**Bright-line check**: No actual dysfunction described. Yield movements from policy expectations are normal. Score 38 is appropriate.

The spread compression in capital markets vs. bank lending tightening creates a mixed picture. The narrowing spreads in markets suggest below midpoint for spreads, but the bank lending tightness is captured more in FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS and BANKING_STRESS. For CREDIT_SPREADS specifically (market-based), spreads narrowed. Score: ~40.

**Bright-line check**: "Narrowed" => should be < 50. Score 40 is consistent.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased." VIX at 25th percentile of historical distribution. Bank equity prices increased and outperformed. Staff's financial stability assessment noted "asset valuation pressures remained notable" with property markets "high relative to fundamentals." This is a mixed signal — equities rising (not stressed) but staff flags valuation concerns. The "notable" valuation pressures lean toward the lower end (overvaluation concern direction). Score: ~35. Staff sees valuations as somewhat elevated/notable, which maps to 31-45 range.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: Mixed picture. Largest bank stocks "fully recovered" from SVB. Deposit flows "stabilized." System "remained well capitalized." Funding risks characterized as "notable." Financial sector leverage characterized as "notable." Financial vulnerabilities overall characterized as "notable." This is post-acute-stress residual concern — not crisis but not fully healthy. Score: ~52. The system is well-capitalized (pushes below 50) but notable vulnerabilities and lingering effects from March turmoil push slightly above 50.

**Bright-line check**: "Well capitalized" => should be < 50. But "notable" vulnerabilities, deposit outflows, fair value declines push up. The net is right around the boundary. I'll set at 50 — neutral with mixed signals.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's trade-weighted broad dollar index declined moderately." Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation => must be < 50. Score: ~38.

However, the parent score is anchored by the explicit PCE/CPI data (3.8-4.8%) and the repeated "elevated" language. The sub-dimensions reflect more forward-looking elements.
