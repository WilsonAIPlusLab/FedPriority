# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2021-06-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: This is a post-2005 segmented minutes document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content within these sections.

**Era context**: June 2021 -- the U.S. economy is in the post-COVID reopening phase. Vaccines are rolling out, fiscal stimulus (American Rescue Plan) is in effect, supply-chain bottlenecks are emerging, and inflation is spiking above target. The labor market is recovering but still well below pre-pandemic levels. The Fed has not yet begun tapering.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly ABOVE the midpoint. PCE inflation at 3.6%, core PCE at 3.1%, CPI at 5%, core CPI at 3.8%. Staff attributes this to base effects, demand surge, bottlenecks, and supply constraints. Staff expects inflation to be "transitory" but per the rubric gotcha, I score the CURRENT level. Staff notes "PCE price inflation was forecast to still be well above 2 percent at the end of this year." The trimmed mean at 1.8% provides some offset, but the headline numbers dominate. The inflation expectations index returned to 2014 levels (modest). This is clearly elevated -- anchoring near 65-70.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential but recovering rapidly. Key evidence: "real GDP was expanding at a pace that was faster than in the first quarter," "total payroll employment had retraced two-thirds of the job losses," unemployment at 5.8% (still elevated). The staff projects GDP growth will "outpace that of potential over most of this period, leading to a decline in the unemployment rate to historically low levels" -- but this is a forecast, not current state. Currently there is still slack: employment only recovered two-thirds of pandemic losses, participation rate "recovered only partially." The bright-line rule: economy is below potential currently. Score MUST be < 50. I'll score around 40-43 given the rapid recovery underway.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment but tightening. Unemployment at 5.8%, participation "recovered only partially," initial claims "remained high relative to their pre-pandemic level." However, there are signs of emerging tightness: "pace of increases in several measures of labor compensation had moved up," average hourly earnings "jumped at a sizable monthly rate," ECI at 4% annualized. With UE at 5.8%, per the rubric (UE 5.5-7% = 31-45 range), but the strong wage pressure signals suggest the upper end. Score around 38-42.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as STRONG, above trend. "Real PCE increased substantially in March," "key factors...pointed to strong real PCE growth over the rest of the year," housing demand "robust," "construction of single-family homes and home sales remaining well above their pre-pandemic levels," equipment and intangibles investment "increasing solidly," manufacturing output "expanded solidly." Offset: nonresidential structures "weak," residential investment "temporarily held back." Net assessment is clearly above trend. Score around 62-66.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE (loose). "Overall financial conditions eased during the intermeeting period." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms through capital markets remained highly accommodative." "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained accommodative." Bright-line rule: "accommodative"/"easy" -> score MUST be < 50. Score around 22-28.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Treasury yield curve flattened a bit," "yields declined somewhat." "Short-term funding markets continued to function smoothly." There is mention of ON RRP surging to record levels, but staff frames this as functioning normally. No dysfunction described. Yields moved normally. Score around 35-40 (normal, quiet functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as COMPRESSED/favorable. "Corporate bond spreads narrowed a little," "spreads...narrowed a little for all credit ratings," "municipal bonds reached historical lows," "historically low corporate bond and leveraged loan yields," "credit rating upgrades outpaced downgrades," "defaults were low." Bright-line: "narrow"/"compressed" -> MUST be < 50. Score around 18-22.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff mentions "broad stock price indexes increased slightly," "high price-to-earnings ratios in the equity markets," VIX at lowest since Feb 2020. The mention of "high price-to-earnings ratios" suggests staff notes elevated valuations. This points to an overvaluation signal. Score around 25-30 (elevated valuations, modest concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff discusses banking conditions indirectly. C&I loans fell but due to PPP forgiveness and tepid demand, not stress. "Delinquency rates on bank C&I loans also remained low." "Loan performance and other indicators of the financial health of small businesses improved." Some stress in specific sectors: "accommodation and food services, small businesses remained stressed." CRE loan growth "weak" reflecting "tight underwriting standards." Overall, the banking system is mostly healthy with pockets of concern. Bright-line: mostly healthy signals -> MUST be < 50. Score around 35-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index decreased modestly." Bright-line: depreciation/weakening -> MUST be < 50. Score around 38-42.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Staff discusses goods inflation extensively. Supply bottlenecks, semiconductor shortages, "surge in demand...combined with production bottlenecks and supply constraints, contributed to the large recent monthly price increases." Goods inflation is clearly elevated. Score around 72-76.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately broken out from goods in a clear way. The reopening demand is mentioned generally. Services trade "remained depressed." Limited direct evidence. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Housing demand continued to be robust...house prices rising appreciably further." This is about housing prices/demand, not shelter CPI directly, but it signals housing-related inflation pressure. Score around 65-68.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "The staff's common inflation expectations index...had returned to the level that prevailed in 2014, a time when inflation was modest." At longer horizons, inflation compensation "were little changed." Shorter-horizon measures "moved down somewhat." This suggests expectations are modestly above the well-anchored baseline but not alarming. Score around 52-55 (slightly above anchored-at-target).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Total PCE price inflation was 3.6 percent over the 12 months ending in April. Core PCE price inflation...was 3.1 percent over the 12 months ending in April."
- "In May, the 12-month change in the CPI was 5 percent, while core CPI inflation was 3.8 percent."
- "A surge in demand as the economy reopened further, combined with production bottlenecks and supply constraints, contributed to the large recent monthly price increases."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff documents inflation running well above target across multiple measures, driven by base effects, reopening demand, and supply bottlenecks, though the trimmed mean PCE at 1.8% and expectations anchored at 2014 levels provide some moderation.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE and CPI are well above the 2% target, with multiple measures showing 3-5% inflation.

**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "The unemployment rate edged down and stood at 5.8 percent in May"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding rapidly but remains below potential, with significant remaining employment shortfalls and only partial recovery in participation -- though the gap is closing fast.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because the economy has not yet recovered to potential (one-third of job losses remain, participation only partially recovered). Bright-line satisfied: below potential -> score < 50.
