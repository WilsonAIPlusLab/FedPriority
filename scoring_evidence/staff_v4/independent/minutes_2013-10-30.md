# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2013-10-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). Staff content is clearly separated into "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. I will score only staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
October 2013: The economy is in a moderate recovery from the GFC. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.2%. The federal government experienced a partial shutdown in early October. The Fed is still conducting asset purchases (QE3/tapering discussions ongoing). Inflation is running below the 2% target. This is a period of gradual healing with significant remaining slack.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation continued to be modest," PCE and CPI "increased modestly, as did the core measures." Inflation "projected to run somewhat below the FOMC's longer-run inflation objective of 2 percent through 2016." Nominal average hourly earnings "increased slowly." This is clearly below-target inflation with no upward pressure. The anchor for 2013-19 below-target is 38-42. Staff language of "modest" and "somewhat below" the 2% objective fits squarely in the 26-40 range ("below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent"). Score: ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Evidence: "economic activity continued to rise at a moderate pace," "the pace of expansion in real GDP this year would be about the same as the growth rate of potential output" but "slack in labor and product markets persisting over most of the projection period." The staff projects GDP will "accelerate in 2014 and 2015" and growth will be "above potential output growth" starting then, meaning currently it is at or just below potential with substantial resource slack remaining. "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack over the projection period." Bright-line check: slack remains/persisting slack => MUST be < 50. The anchor for 2010-14 recovery is 35-42. GDP growth near potential but slack persisting suggests around 38-40. Score: ~39.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment (loose). Evidence: Unemployment at 7.2% ("still high"), mixed labor indicators, "share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was essentially unchanged and continued to be elevated," initial claims rose partly due to shutdown. UE at 7.2% maps to the 16-30 range per the rubric (UE > 7%). However, there are some positive signals: payrolls increased, hiring plans improved, job openings increased slightly, long-duration unemployment declined a little. The anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. With UE at 7.2%, I should be in the 31-35 range. Score: ~33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED, slightly below trend. Evidence: "Real PCE rose moderately in August," retail sales excluding autos "increased significantly" but auto sales declined, consumer sentiment declined. Business equipment spending was "tepid in the third quarter." Housing "recovery appeared to continue" but with mixed data (starts up, existing sales down). Federal government purchases "likely declined." The anchor for 2011-13 "moderate" is 35-42. The mix of moderate consumer spending and tepid business investment suggests around 40. Score: ~40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/LOOSE. Evidence: "longer-term interest rates declined and equity prices rose, largely in response to expectations for more-accommodative monetary policy." "access to credit generally remained ample for large firms." Banks "had eased standards on C&I and CRE loans." "Developments affecting financing for the household sector were generally favorable." Mortgage rates declined. However, there was temporary strain from the debt ceiling standoff. Overall, conditions are clearly accommodative. Bright-line check: "accommodative" / "ample" => MUST be < 50. The anchor for this era would be somewhere in the 30-40 range given ongoing QE and accommodative conditions but still some constraints from the recovery period. Score: ~35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes TEMPORARY stress in short-term Treasury markets. Evidence: "Short-term funding markets were adversely affected for a time by concerns about potential delays in raising the federal debt limit. The Treasury bill market was particularly affected as yields on bills maturing between mid-October and early November rose sharply and some bill auctions saw reduced demand. Conditions in other short-term markets, such as the market for repurchase agreements, were also strained." BUT "these effects eased quickly after an agreement to raise the debt limit was reached." This describes actual market dysfunction (reduced auction demand, strained repo markets), not just yield movements. However, it was temporary and resolved. Score: ~55 (elevated but transient).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees credit conditions as generally favorable with ample access. Evidence: "access to credit generally remained ample for large firms," "Gross issuance of nonfinancial corporate bonds... had been particularly strong in September," "leveraged loan issuance was also robust," C&I loans continued to advance, banks eased lending standards. Foreign yield spreads declined. This is clearly below-normal spreads territory. Bright-line check: ample credit availability => MUST be < 50. Anchor for 2013-19 generally favorable is 30-40. Score: ~32.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff mentions equity prices rose but provides no valuation commentary. Evidence: "equity prices rose, largely in response to expectations for more-accommodative monetary policy." No concern language, no valuation assessment. Default to 45-55 per the rubric. Score: ~47.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff provides some banking information. Evidence: "Third-quarter earnings reports for large banks generally met or exceeded analysts' modest expectations." Banks eased lending standards on C&I, CRE, and home-purchase loans. "Bank credit declined slightly during the third quarter" due to residential mortgage balance declines. Overall, banks appear healthy with easing standards. Bright-line check: easing standards and earnings meeting expectations => low stress => MUST be < 50. Anchor for 2014-19 well-capitalized is 25-35. Score: ~33.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff describes dollar DEPRECIATION. Evidence: "the dollar depreciated against most other currencies," "the value of the dollar fell further after the debt ceiling agreement was reached." The staff's medium-term forecast was revised partly due to "lower projected paths for the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Bright-line check: depreciation => MUST be < 50. Score: ~30.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices are discussed (rose), but shelter/housing inflation specifically is not scored as a separate inflation component. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Discussed. Evidence: "measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable," "near- and longer-term inflation expectations from the Thomson Reuters/University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers were little changed," "longer-run inflation expectations assumed to remain stable." Well-anchored at or near target. Score: ~48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (39) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33)**: Both below 50, consistent. Gap of 6 points is reasonable -- labor market slightly more depressed than overall output given the 7.2% UE rate vs. GDP growing near potential. OK.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (39) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Nearly identical, both below 50. Consistent. OK.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (32)**: Both below 50, consistent. Both indicate accommodative/ample conditions. OK.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (55)**: These diverge. Explanation: The Treasury stress reflects temporary debt-ceiling-related dysfunction in short-term funding markets, which is a distinct political/fiscal event, not a reflection of overall financial conditions which remained accommodative. This is coherent -- the debt ceiling created localized stress while broader financial conditions remained easy.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (35) <-> INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored at 48 (anchored). The overall inflation score of 35 (below target, modest) is consistent with well-anchored expectations during a period of below-target inflation. OK.
