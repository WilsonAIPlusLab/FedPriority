# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-08-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Mid-2018. The economy is in a mature expansion following the GFC recovery. Tax cuts enacted in late 2017 are providing fiscal stimulus. Unemployment is low (~4%), GDP growth is strong, and inflation has recently moved up near the 2% target after years of undershooting. Trade policy tensions are emerging but have not yet significantly impacted the domestic economy.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff reports PCE inflation at 2.2% (12-month), core PCE at 1.9%. This is essentially at the 2% target. Staff projects inflation to "remain near the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term." Direction: at the midpoint. Score near 50. The headline being slightly above 2% and core slightly below nets out to right around target.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff projects the economy to "expand at an above-trend pace" and output to "rise further above the staff's estimate of potential output." The unemployment rate is "projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of the longer-run natural rate." This is clearly above-potential language. BRIGHT-LINE: must be > 50. The language "further above" potential implies already above and going higher — this is a moderately positive output gap. Score in the 55-60 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment rate at 4.0% (moved up from prior month but due to higher participation). "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." "Labor market conditions already tight." Part-time for economic reasons at lowest since late 2007. Job openings elevated, quits rising, initial claims low. ECI at 2.9% (up from 2.4% year-ago). Staff assumes unemployment will decline further below the natural rate. This is a tight labor market. With UE at 4.0% and described as "already tight," score should be in the 58-63 range per anchors (2018-19: 55-62).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Real PCE rose briskly in the second quarter." "Real GDP rose at a strong rate in the first half." Business equipment spending at moderate pace (after strong Q1). Nonresidential structures "expanded at a solid pace." But residential investment "declined again." Government purchases rose at a faster rate. Consumer sentiment "remained upbeat." Net exports contributed positively. The overall picture is strong consumption, solid business investment, weak residential. Net assessment: above trend. Score 58-62.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households remained supportive of economic activity on balance." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial corporations continued to be favorable." CRE conditions "remained accommodative." Consumer credit conditions "remained largely supportive of growth." The word "supportive" and "accommodative" = below 50 per bright-line rule. But this is 2018, the neutral anchor period (46-54). Staff uses "supportive" and "accommodative" language, which points to the looser side. Score around 38-42 — wait, the rubric says 2017-19 is the neutral anchor at 46-54, and "accommodative"/"supportive" triggers the bright-line < 50. So somewhere in the 38-45 range. Given that conditions are described as broadly accommodative/supportive but not excessively so, and the 2017-19 anchor is 46-54, I'll score 42. The bright-line is satisfied (< 50 for supportive/accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Short-term funding markets functioned smoothly." Yields on medium- and longer-term Treasuries "were little changed." Yield curve "flattened somewhat." No discussion of any market dysfunction — just normal yield movements. Score in the 35-40 range (normal functioning, quiet).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of yields on nonfinancial corporate bonds over those of comparable-maturity Treasury securities were little changed, on net... These spreads remained low by historical standards." CMBS spreads "remained near their post-crisis lows." BRIGHT-LINE: "low by historical standards" = compressed/narrow, must be < 50. Anchor for 2013-19 is 30-40. Score around 28-32 given "low by historical standards."

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes displayed mixed results." VIX "little changed" and "only a bit above the very low levels." Staff's financial stability report: "vulnerabilities associated with asset valuation pressures continued to be elevated, with no major asset class exhibiting valuations below their historical midpoints." This is staff expressing elevated valuation concern — score toward the overvaluation side. Higher = more stress/concern, but staff is flagging elevated valuations (overvaluation concern), which maps to the low end of the scale (1-15 = extreme overvaluation concern, 16-30 = elevated valuations). Score around 25-30.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff judged "vulnerabilities from financial-sector leverage and maturity and liquidity transformation to be low." Banks eased standards on C&I loans (competitive environment). "Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations continued to be solid overall." However, "some signs of deterioration emerged" and "the ratio of aggregate debt to assets in this sector stayed near multidecade highs." Nonfinancial business sector leverage "elevated." Overall, banking system itself is low stress, but nonfinancial leverage is elevated. Score in the 30-38 range (healthy with minor concerns). BRIGHT-LINE satisfied: financial-sector leverage low = below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index moved up." "The dollar appreciated against most currencies." BRIGHT-LINE: appreciation = must be > 50. The language is moderate — "moved up," not dramatically. Score 57-62.
