# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-08-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains three clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member content is included.

### Era Context
August 2011: Two years after the Great Recession trough. Recovery was disappointing — GDP data revisions revealed the recession was deeper than thought. Unemployment remained around 9%. The debt ceiling crisis and S&P downgrade of U.S. sovereign debt dominated financial markets. Europe's sovereign debt crisis was intensifying. The Fed had just completed QE2.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key signals: "consumer price inflation moderated in recent months," "longer-term inflation expectations remained stable," core PCE posted only "a small increase" in June, the staff outlook says "prices to rise at a subdued pace in 2012" with "substantial slack expected to persist." Earlier commodity/energy pass-through was fading. Direction: below 50. Some residual upward pressure from commodity/import prices and motor vehicle prices, but the overall staff tone is of moderating inflation heading toward subdued levels. Score around 40-43.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as clearly BELOW potential. "The recent recession was deeper than previously thought," "the level of real GDP had not yet attained its pre-recession peak," "the recovery was quite sluggish," staff lowered estimate of potential GDP and marked down forecasts, "slack in the labor market" expected to reduce "only slowly." Bright-line check: "slack" language = must be < 50. Score around 30-33.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as very LOOSE. "Labor market conditions continued to be weak," private employment rose at "a considerably slower pace," unemployment "edged up, on net," "long-duration unemployment remained very high," participation rate "moved down further," initial claims "remained elevated," "indicators of hiring showed no improvement." Unemployment was ~9.1% at this time. Score around 22-25.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. "Real consumer spending was nearly unchanged in the second quarter," spending appeared "soft," consumer sentiment "weakened markedly" to "levels last seen in early 2009," housing "remained depressed," business spending on E&S rose only at "a modest pace," nonresidential structures "stabilized at a low level." Some positives: orders above shipments, net exports contributing. Overall quite weak. Score around 30-33.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "Productivity in the nonfarm business sector rose only slightly over the past four-quarter period." This is clearly below trend. Score around 30-33.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as MIXED but leaning toward TIGHTER. Positive: banks "eased lending standards to some degree," conditions in consumer credit "continued to improve," "improved credit availability" in the outlook. Negative: "Liquidity and funding in money markets deteriorated in the last week of July," interest rates on short-term funding "increased markedly," equity prices "fell considerably," lending standards remain "between moderate and relatively tight" compared to range since 2005, mortgage underwriting "tight," commercial real estate "weak." Overall, conditions tightened over the period though there was some easing in bank lending. Score around 58-62.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: There is evidence of actual market stress, not just yield movements. "Liquidity and funding in money markets deteriorated in the last week of July, and interest rates on a number of short-term funding instruments increased markedly" — this relates to the debt ceiling and potential Treasury payment delays. Strains eased after the debt ceiling was raised. Yields fell (not a stress signal). The S&P downgrade is mentioned. This is genuine but temporary dysfunction. Score around 55-60.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Yields on both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds fell a little less than those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving risk spreads wider." CP market for financial firms "experienced some strains." Leveraged loan issuance "remained strong." Business credit quality "continued to improve." Spreads widened but from a reasonably normal level, and the overall credit picture is mixed. Score around 55-58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes fell sharply, on net" and "option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index jumped late in the period." The decline was driven by weaker economic data and debt ceiling/downgrade concerns. Staff reports significant price declines but no valuation commentary per se. This is a notable decline. Score around 62-66.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Mixed signals. Positive: "Nearly all second-quarter earnings reports from large banking companies exceeded expectations," banks eased lending standards, credit quality improving, delinquencies declining. Negative: lending standards still "between moderate and relatively tight," residential mortgage delinquency "remained high," commercial real estate "weak," some European bank stress mentioned. The U.S. banking system itself is improving but still carrying legacy stress. Score around 45-48.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar fluctuated over the period... On net over the intermeeting period, the dollar rose modestly after having depreciated earlier this year." Bright-line: appreciation = > 50. Score around 53-56.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "Prices of nonfood, non-energy goods were apparently boosted by upward pressure from earlier increases in commodity and import prices, and motor vehicle prices rose further." This shows some goods inflation pressure, but the staff sees it as transitory pass-through. Score around 53-57.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "The PCE price index for non-energy services was essentially unchanged." Very low services inflation. Score around 35-38.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "The housing market remained depressed," "home prices continued to trend lower." Housing costs declining. Score around 30-33.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-term inflation expectations remained stable," "near-term expected inflation... moved down again in July from its elevated level in the spring," "long-run inflation expectations stable" in the outlook. Well-anchored. Score around 47-50.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (42): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderating/subdued. Below 50 is correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (32): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. Below 50 is correct. Bright-line PASS.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (23): Higher = tighter. Evidence says weak, high unemployment. Low score is correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60): Higher = tighter. Evidence says deterioration in funding markets, tight standards. Above 50 is correct. Bright-line PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS (56): Higher = wider/more stress. "Risk spreads wider," CP strains. Above 50 is correct. Bright-line PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS (46): Higher = more stress. Banks improving but legacy issues remain. Slightly below 50 is correct — earnings exceeded expectations, standards easing. Bright-line: not clearly "well-capitalized" language but improving. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH (54): Higher = stronger. "Rose modestly." Above 50 is correct. Bright-line PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS (57): Higher = more dysfunction. "Liquidity and funding deteriorated," short-term rates increased markedly. Above 50 is correct — this was actual market dysfunction, not just yield moves. Bright-line PASS.

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (32) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (23)**: Both well below 50. Gap of 9 points. Coherent — both reflect deep slack.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (32) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (31)**: Gap of 1 point. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Overall consumer price inflation moderated in recent months"; "the staff continued to expect prices to rise at a subdued pace in 2012"; "substantial slack expected to persist in labor and product markets."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation as modestly below target — temporary upward pressure from commodity pass-through is fading, with core measures soft and outlook subdued.
- Direction: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because current inflation was moderating and the outlook was subdued, though commodity pass-through provided some residual upward pressure.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The recent recession was deeper than previously thought and... the level of real GDP had not yet attained its pre-recession peak"; "the recovery was quite sluggish in the first half of this year"; "slack in the labor market only slowly" to be reduced.
- Center-of-gravity: Economy operating well below potential — GDP hadn't even recovered pre-recession peak, with substantial slack persisting and the staff marking down its growth forecast notably.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff explicitly describes persistent slack and a deeper-than-thought recession. Bright-line PASS.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 23 ±4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Labor market conditions continued to be weak"; "private nonfarm employment rose at a considerably slower pace in June and July"; "the unemployment rate edged up, on net... and long-duration unemployment remained very high."
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market is very slack — unemployment ~9.1%, participation declining, hiring indicators showing no improvement. Consistent with 2011 anchor of 35-40 but this text is more negative than typical 2011, reflecting the mid-year slowdown.
- Direction: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because the labor market is deeply weak with high unemployment and deteriorating conditions.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 31 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real consumer spending was nearly unchanged in the second quarter"; "consumer sentiment weakened markedly in July... to levels last seen in early 2009"; "the housing market remained depressed."
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending essentially flat, housing deeply depressed, business investment only modestly positive — overall spending picture is weak and below trend.
- Direction: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is well below neutral because spending was flat to soft across most categories.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 32 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Productivity in the nonfarm business sector rose only slightly over the past four-quarter period."
- Center-of-gravity: Productivity growth well below historical trend, mentioned only briefly.
- Direction: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because growth was only "slight" over the past year.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Liquidity and funding in money markets deteriorated in the last week of July, and interest rates on a number of short-term funding instruments increased markedly"; lending standards remain "between moderate and relatively tight when compared with the range of standards that had prevailed since 2005."
- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions tightened over the period with genuine funding market stress from the debt ceiling crisis, and bank lending standards remain elevated despite gradual easing.
- Direction: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because of funding market deterioration and still-tight lending standards. Bright-line PASS.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 57 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Liquidity and funding in money markets deteriorated in the last week of July"; "investor focus appeared to turn to the U.S. debt ceiling and the potential for delayed debt service payments by the Treasury Department."
- Center-of-gravity: Genuine but temporary market dysfunction from the debt ceiling standoff — not just yield moves, but actual liquidity impairment in money markets. Strains eased after resolution.
- Direction: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because of actual funding market dysfunction, though it was temporary.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 56 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Yields on both investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds fell a little less than those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving risk spreads wider"; "the market for CP issued by financial firms experienced some strains late in the period."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads widened modestly as risk appetite declined; credit conditions showed some strain but not severe — bond issuance remained solid and credit quality continued improving.
- Direction: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads widened. Bright-line PASS.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 64 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes fell sharply, on net, over the intermeeting period"; "option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index jumped late in the period."
- Center-of-gravity: Significant equity market decline driven by growth concerns and fiscal events; staff notes sharp declines and elevated volatility but provides no explicit valuation assessment.
- Direction: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress/concern) because of sharp declines and elevated volatility.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 46 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Nearly all second-quarter earnings reports from large banking companies exceeded expectations"; "respondents again eased lending standards to some degree"; but "current levels of their lending standards for all loan types were between moderate and relatively tight."
- Center-of-gravity: Banking system improving — strong earnings, easing standards, improving credit quality — but still carrying legacy stress from tight standards and elevated delinquencies. Net position just below neutral.
- Direction: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral because the system is healing (strong earnings, easing standards) though residual stress remains.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 54 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "On net over the intermeeting period, the dollar rose modestly after having depreciated earlier this year."
- Center-of-gravity: Modest net appreciation over the period, with significant fluctuation.
- Direction: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly above neutral because the dollar appreciated modestly. Bright-line PASS.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Prices of nonfood, non-energy goods were apparently boosted by upward pressure from earlier increases in commodity and import prices, and motor vehicle prices rose further."
- Center-of-gravity: Goods prices modestly elevated due to pass-through effects, but staff views this as temporary.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: 36 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

- Center-of-gravity: Services inflation very low, essentially zero in the most recent month.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: 31 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "The housing market remained depressed"; "home prices continued to trend lower."
- Center-of-gravity: Housing costs declining with a deeply depressed market and falling home prices.
