# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2025-01-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. Staff content is clearly separated into "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. The "Participants' Views" section is excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
January 2025: The Fed had eased over three consecutive meetings in late 2024 (cutting from 5.25-5.50% to 4.25-4.50%). Inflation had come down significantly from 2022 peaks but remained "somewhat elevated" above the 2% target. The labor market had cooled from extremely tight 2022-2023 levels but remained solid. GDP growth was solid. This is a late-cycle normalization environment with lingering above-target inflation.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint. Total PCE inflation was 2.4% (reported in November) / estimated 2.6% in December. Core PCE was 2.8%. CPI was 2.9%. These are clearly above the 2% target. The staff projection was "essentially unchanged" with inflation in 2025 "similar to 2024's rate" and projected to decline to 2% by 2027. Trade policy placeholder assumptions put "upward pressure on inflation." Risks around inflation were "skewed to the upside." This is modestly-to-clearly above target — score range 55-62. The language is factual rather than alarming: staff reports the numbers without words like "persistent" or "concerning," but the upside risk skew and the tariff pressure mention push this above simple "somewhat elevated."
- Direction: Above midpoint.
- Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" = 5-10 points above 50; but specific data (2.6-2.8% core) and upside risk skew push toward 58-62.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says above-target inflation, upside risks. Score above 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as modestly ABOVE potential or near potential. "Real GDP rose at a solid pace in the fourth quarter." The staff projection says "the output gap expected to narrow further until early next year and to remain roughly flat thereafter." This means the output gap is currently POSITIVE (above potential) and expected to narrow. "Solid pace" of GDP growth, strong consumer spending. The unemployment rate is "close to the staff's estimate of its natural rate."
- Direction: Above midpoint (positive output gap narrowing toward zero).
- Bright-line: The output gap is described as positive (needs to "narrow"), so score MUST be > 50. But it's expected to narrow, suggesting it's modestly above. Score 52-55.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Staff says gap is positive but narrowing. Score slightly above 50. Correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff reports "labor market conditions remained solid and that the labor market was not especially tight." Unemployment rate edged down to 4.1% in December. Payroll gains "slightly higher than their third-quarter pace." Job vacancies-to-unemployed ratio was 1.2 (its 2019 average). Quits rate fell to 1.9%, "well below its 2019 average." Average hourly earnings rose 3.9%, down from 4.3% a year ago. The labor market is solid but not overheating — the "not especially tight" language is key. UE at 4.1% is near NAIRU territory. The vacancy ratio at 2019 average and quits rate below 2019 suggest moderation.
- Direction: Near midpoint, perhaps slightly above. 4.1% UE suggests above the 46-52 range for "near NAIRU" but the explicit "not especially tight" language caps it.
- Score: 53-56. UE at 4.1% with "solid" conditions but "not especially tight" language.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. 4.1% UE is fairly low, but staff explicitly says "not especially tight." Score modestly above neutral. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff says "real GDP rose at a solid pace in the fourth quarter." Real private domestic final purchases "appeared to have risen faster than real GDP in the fourth quarter, led by continued strength in consumer spending." However, trade data "pointed to a step-down in both export and import growth." Monthly trade data were "volatile." Consumer spending is strong; investment data are more mixed (the text mentions volatility). Overall, "solid pace" with "continued strength in consumer spending."
- Direction: Above midpoint. "Solid pace," "continued strength in consumer spending."
- Magnitude: "Solid" = roughly at or slightly above trend = 50-55. "Continued strength" pushes a bit higher. Score 54-58.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says solid/strong. Score above 50. Correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed in staff sections. The staff sections don't mention productivity directly. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff reports: "market-implied path of the federal funds rate was little changed." Longer-term yields rose, "partly attributable to higher term premiums." "Broad equity price indexes were little changed." "Corporate bond spreads remained compressed by historical standards." Borrowing costs for households and businesses "remained elevated." Rates on 30-year mortgages rose. C&I loan rates remained elevated. Credit for smaller firms "remained relatively tight." Bank lending standards were "basically unchanged" for C&I but "tighter" for CRE. Credit availability for households was available but tightening for lower-credit-score borrowers. This is a mixed picture: elevated borrowing costs and tight standards for some sectors, but capital markets broadly available and equity prices steady. Financial conditions are somewhat restrictive overall given elevated rates and tight standards for CRE and smaller firms.
- Direction: Above midpoint (tighter than neutral). Elevated borrowing costs, tight CRE standards, tight conditions for smaller firms. But not severely tight — capital markets broadly available for large firms.
- Bright-line: "Tight" for some segments but not overall crisis. Score 55-62.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says elevated rates, tight for some. Score above 50. Correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff mentions shorter-term yields edged down, longer-term rates rose "partly attributable to higher term premiums." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. Yields moved for normal economic reasons. Participants (not staff) later discuss Treasury market vulnerabilities, but staff section shows normal market functioning. The staff financial stability section mentions "vulnerabilities in the Treasury market" and "dealer intermediation capacity" and "leveraged positions" — this is the staff's own stability assessment. This pushes slightly above baseline normal.
- Direction: Near midpoint. Yield movements are normal. Staff stability section flags some structural vulnerabilities but no acute stress.
- Bright-line: No actual dysfunction described. Score 40-50.
- Score: 45. Normal functioning with some structural vulnerability flags.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff explicitly says "corporate bond spreads remained compressed by historical standards." This is clear.
- Direction: Below midpoint. Compressed spreads = low credit stress.
- Bright-line: "Compressed" → score MUST be < 50. Score 20-30.
- Score: 22. "Compressed by historical standards" is strong language for narrow spreads.
- Polarity check: Higher = wider spreads/more stress. Compressed = low stress. Score well below 50. Correct.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff says "broad equity price indexes were little changed on net" and "measures of the equity risk premium remained low." The VIX "was also roughly unchanged on net and remained at a moderate level." In the financial stability section, "the staff judged that asset valuation pressures were elevated." This is a direct staff valuation assessment — elevated valuations = concern about overvaluation.
- Direction: Below midpoint (toward overvaluation concern). "Asset valuation pressures were elevated" and "equity risk premium remained low" both point to stretched valuations.
- Score: 25-32. Staff explicitly flags elevated valuation pressures.
- Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress/concern. But this dimension measures stress/concern. "Elevated valuation pressures" means concern about overvaluation, which maps to the LOW end of the scale (1-30 = overvaluation concern). Score 25-30. Correct.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: Staff characterizes "financial vulnerabilities as notable." "Leverage in the financial sector were characterized as notable." Banks have "high" regulatory capital ratios BUT "continued to hold large quantities of long-duration assets, leaving them more exposed than usual to an unexpected rise in longer-term interest rates." "Leverage at hedge funds remained high." Banks reduced reliance on uninsured deposits. The staff stability assessment shows mixed signals: strong capital but duration risk and notable vulnerabilities.
- Direction: Near midpoint, slightly above. Capital ratios high (low stress) but duration exposure and notable vulnerabilities push up.
- Bright-line: Not clearly "well-capitalized/sound" (which would be <50) — it's mixed with "notable vulnerabilities."
- Score: 42-48. Capital ratios high provides some comfort, but notable vulnerabilities and duration risk keep it from being clearly low-stress.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff says "the broad dollar index increased slightly on net." This is modest appreciation.
- Direction: Above midpoint. Dollar strengthened.
- Bright-line: Appreciation → score MUST be > 50. "Increased slightly" = modest.
- Score: 55-58. "Slightly" = 5-8 points above midpoint.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dollar strength. Dollar appreciated. Score above 50. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (54)**: Both modestly above midpoint. Coherent. Gap < 15.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (56)**: Both modestly above midpoint. Coherent. Gap < 15.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (22)**: These diverge significantly. - Center-of-gravity: Inflation is clearly above the 2% target with core PCE at 2.8%, and the staff sees upside risks from trade policy and stickier-than-expected disinflation.
