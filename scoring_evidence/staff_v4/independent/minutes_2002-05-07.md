# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — May 7, 2002

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from the May 7, 2002 FOMC meeting. It is **unsegmented** (pre-2005), meaning staff and participant content are intermixed. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable language from participant/member discussion.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following passages are clearly staff-attributable:
- Paragraph beginning "The information reviewed at this meeting..." (standard staff review language)
- Paragraphs on payroll employment, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, inventories, trade, and prices (all part of "the information reviewed")
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested that..."

Participant content (to be IGNORED for scoring): "In the Committee's discussion...", "members commented...", "members noted...", "the members generally viewed...", "In the Committee's discussion of policy..."

### Era Context
May 2002: Early recovery from the 2001 recession. The economy began recovering in late 2001/early 2002, but the recovery was uneven. Unemployment was still elevated (6.0% in April 2002). The fed funds rate was at 1.75%, highly accommodative. This was post-9/11, post-dot-com bust. Inflation was subdued and decelerating.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Key staff language: "core measures of inflation had trended lower over the past year"; "Both the overall CPI and the PCE chain-linked index decelerated significantly over the past year"; "both measures of inflation also fell over the past year" (excluding food/energy); "core finished goods inflation decelerated on a year-over-year basis"; "Labor cost growth...also appeared to have slowed." The staff forecast says "the persistence of underutilized resources was expected to contribute to damped core consumer price inflation." Energy prices pushed up headline but core was clearly declining. Direction: below 50, in the "contained/modest/running below" range. Score ~32.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation decelerated, core fell, costs slowed. This is low pressure. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees economy as BELOW potential. Key staff language: "economic activity expanded rapidly early in the year" BUT the staff forecast says "activity would remain below the economy's potential for a period ahead." Also: "the pressure on resources would rise somewhat despite the anticipated higher growth of structural productivity" and "continued sizable gap between actual and potential output." Capacity utilization "still substantially below its long-run average."

Bright-line check: "below the economy's potential" and "sizable gap" = MUST be < 50. The economy is recovering but still well below potential. Score ~35.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment rose to 6.0% in April. "Private nonfarm payroll employment turned up in April after having posted small declines in February and March and steep reductions earlier." Some firming, but UE at 6.0% is clearly elevated. With UE > 5.5% but some improvement, this falls in the 31-45 range per the rubric (UE 5.5-7%).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. UE at 6.0% with just starting to see job gains = loose. Score below 50. Score ~33.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff assessment is MIXED. Consumer spending: "well maintained," "supported by sizable gains in disposable income," "robust" motor vehicle demand, "briskly" expanding other goods/services. Housing: "surged," single-family starts at 23-year high. BUT business investment: "changed little thus far this year following the steep decline," communications equipment only "stabilized," aircraft/motor vehicle purchases "slowed sharply," nonresidential construction "slumped." Staff forecast: "expansion in economic activity was slowing substantially in the current quarter but would pick up in the second half."

Net assessment: Consumer side is solid-to-strong, business investment is weak-to-stabilizing. The staff forecast implies a slowing. Net position is modestly below trend given the drag from business investment and the slowing expansion. Score ~43.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff explicitly discusses productivity. "Available information suggested that labor productivity had risen substantially in the first quarter." Staff forecast mentions "vigorous anticipated growth in structural productivity." This is clearly above trend. Score ~65.

Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Substantially" risen and "vigorous" anticipated growth = well above trend. Score above 50. Correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff-attributable: "the current accommodative stance of policy" — but this is POLICY, not conditions. For conditions: "low mortgage rates" (supportive housing), "low financing rates" (auto sales), "yields on investment-grade bonds tended to edge higher" owing to accounting concerns, "most major indexes of equity prices moved down sharply." M2/M3 contracted. Mixed signals: low rates (accommodative) but equity declines and some bond yield pressure.

The overall tone from the staff review is that financial conditions are generally accommodative/supportive — low mortgage rates, low financing rates. The equity decline and bond concerns add some tightening. Net: somewhat accommodative/loose.

Bright-line check: "accommodative" → MUST be < 50. Score ~38.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff mentions: "declines in yields on short- to intermediate-term Treasury securities" driven by doubts about recovery. This is normal yield movement from expectations, not dysfunction. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. Score N/A or low-normal.

Given that Treasury yields are discussed but only in terms of normal movements, I'll score this as normal functioning ~40. Actually, there's no indication of any stress at all — just yield movements. I'll score N/A since there's no discussion of Treasury market functioning per se, only yield direction.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff mentions: "yields on investment-grade bonds tended to edge higher...in the wake of concerns about the transparency of the accounting statements of some firms." This is a modest widening signal from corporate accounting concerns (Enron/WorldCom era). Equity prices "moved down sharply." But no language about broad credit stress or spreads specifically. The language is limited. This suggests modestly elevated concern ~55.

Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress. Accounting concerns pushing yields higher, equity declines = some stress. Above 50. Correct.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff says: "Most major indexes of equity prices moved down sharply in response to the outlook for a weaker economic recovery and the adverse implications for corporate profits." This is a description of price declines, not valuation assessment. Per the gotcha rule, falling prices without valuation commentary → default 50-55. Score ~55.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
No specific discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or bank stress in the staff-attributable sections. Score N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff says: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the major foreign currencies eased somewhat over the intermeeting period." Also: "modest recent declines in the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Dollar weakened.

Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation → MUST be < 50. Score ~40.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices "boosted headline consumer inflation," "significant increases in energy costs," "upturn in some industrial prices." But core goods (ex food/energy) decelerated. Mixed but some upward energy pressure. Score ~45.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: "continuing upward pressures on medical costs," "sharp increases in many insurance costs." Some service-sector pressures noted. Score ~52.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity surged with "rising prices in many areas." But this is asset price, not shelter/rent inflation. No specific OER/shelter inflation discussion. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff forecast mentions expectations implicitly through the phrase "low and stable inflation expectations" (though this appears in the member discussion section). In the staff review section, the deceleration in prices and costs is noted. The staff forecast assumes subdued inflation. Limited direct staff discussion of expectations. N/A — the "low and stable inflation expectations" phrase appears in the participant section.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33)**: Both below 50, consistent with early recovery from recession. Gap of 2 points — coherent.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43)**: Gap of 8 points. Consumer spending is holding up better than the overall output gap suggests, which makes sense as the gap reflects business investment weakness and excess capacity. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (55)**: Divergence of 17 points. This reflects accommodative monetary policy keeping broad conditions loose while corporate accounting scandals (Enron era) pushed credit concerns higher. Explainable divergence.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs TREASURY_STRESS (N/A)**: N/A for Treasury, so no conflict.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~45, Services ~52, Housing N/A, Expectations N/A. The parent score of 32 may seem low relative to the scored sub-components, but the parent score reflects the OVERALL core inflation picture which was clearly decelerating. The energy-driven goods pressures and medical cost pressures are pockets, while the broad trend is downward. Acceptable.
