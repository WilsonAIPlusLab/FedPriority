# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-07-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly separated "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. I will score only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2013. The economy is in a slow recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment is still elevated at 7.6%. The "taper tantrum" is underway — the Fed has signaled potential reduction in asset purchases, causing a sharp rise in long-term yields. Inflation is running well below the 2% target. Fiscal austerity (sequestration) is a headwind.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly BELOW midpoint. "Consumer price inflation slowed markedly in the second quarter." PCE prices "unchanged from the first quarter to the second and were about 1 percent higher than a year earlier." Core PCE "rose at a subdued rate" and was "around 1-1/4 percent higher than a year earlier." Staff judges "much of the recent softness in consumer price inflation would be transitory" but "inflation was forecast to be subdued through 2015." This is well below the 2% target. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target, but this is particularly soft even for that era — closer to 30-35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy BELOW midpoint. "Economic activity expanded at a modest pace." "The rate of growth of real GDP would only slightly exceed that of potential output." "Significant resource slack persisting over the forecast period." "Slow reduction in the slack in labor and product markets." Bright-line: "slack remains" language → MUST be < 50. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW midpoint (loose). Unemployment at 7.6%, "still elevated." Long-duration unemployment and part-time for economic reasons "remained relatively high." Forward-looking indicators "mixed." Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. With UE at 7.6%, this fits the 16-30 range description (UE > 7%), though it's at the boundary. Score around 30-32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as BELOW trend but with mixed signals. Consumer spending "increased more slowly in the second quarter." However, residential investment "continued to expand briskly." Equipment investment growth was "greater in the second quarter than in the first." Home prices "continued to rise strongly." Business conditions surveys "were mixed and pointed to modest gains." Net assessment: modestly below trend with pockets of strength. Anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate."

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as mixed/somewhat tighter. Longer-term interest rates "rose significantly." Mortgage rates rose. However, banks "eased standards on both C&I and commercial real estate loans." C&I standards "somewhat easy compared with longer-term norms." Banks eased standards on household loans too. Credit "freely available" in some segments but tightening via rates. Bond redemptions were large in June. The SLOOS easing language suggests conditions are not very tight overall, but the rate rise is a tightening impulse. Staff notes "higher equity prices were seen as offsetting the restrictive effects of the increase in longer-term interest rates." Net: modestly accommodative to neutral. Score around 42-48.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields rose 30-45 bps. "Financial markets were volatile at times" and "market dynamics appeared to amplify some asset price moves." Bond mutual funds experienced "large net redemptions in June." MBS option-adjusted spread "widened somewhat." This is more than just normal yield movement — staff notes market dynamics amplifying moves and volatility. But there's no mention of actual market dysfunction (liquidity problems, settlement failures, auction failures). The amplification language pushes slightly above normal. Score around 48-52.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads between yields on 10-year nonfinancial corporate bonds and yields on Treasury securities narrowed somewhat on net." Banks easing standards. C&I standards "somewhat easy compared with longer-term norms." Bond issuance dropped due to reduced refinancing incentive, but leveraged loan issuance "continued to be strong." Overall spreads narrowing and credit conditions favorable. Bright-line: narrowing spreads → MUST be < 50. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 generally favorable.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes ended the period higher." "Gains in equity values... boosted household net worth." Stock prices of large banks outperformed. No valuation concern language. Default to 45-50 (no particular concern, equities rose). Anchor: 40-50 for 2013-19 typical.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Market sentiment toward large domestic banking organizations appeared to improve somewhat." Largest banks "reported second-quarter earnings that were above analysts' expectations." Stock prices of large banks outperformed. CDS spreads moved in line with broad indexes. Banks easing lending standards. Bright-line: improving sentiment, above-expectations earnings → low stress → MUST be < 50. Anchor: 25-35 for 2014-19 well-capitalized. Score around 32-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal dollar index moved up as the dollar appreciated against the currencies of the advanced foreign economies, consistent with the larger increase in U.S. interest rates." "The dollar was mixed against the EME currencies." Bright-line: dollar appreciation → MUST be > 50. Modest appreciation, mixed against EMEs. Score around 55-58.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "Consumer energy prices declined significantly." Commodity price changes "expected to be modest." Some transitory factors restraining prices. Below neutral — score around 30-35.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Home prices continued to rise strongly." But this is asset prices not shelter CPI. Housing activity restrained by tight mortgage credit standards. Not enough specific shelter inflation data. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey were little changed in June and July, as were longer-term inflation expectations, which remained within the narrow range seen in recent years." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation were about unchanged." Well-anchored at/near target. Score 47-50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation slowed markedly in the second quarter" and PCE prices "were about 1 percent higher than a year earlier"
- Core PCE "rose at a subdued rate in the second quarter and was around 1-1/4 percent higher than a year earlier"
- "Inflation was forecast to be subdued through 2015"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports headline PCE at 1% and core at 1.25% — well below 2% target with expectation of continued subdued inflation. This is meaningfully below target but not deflationary.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because both headline and core inflation are running well below the 2% target, and the forecast calls for continued subdued inflation.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity expanded at a modest pace in the first half of the year"
- "The rate of growth of real GDP would only slightly exceed that of potential output"
- "Significant resource slack persisting over the forecast period"

**Center-of-gravity**: Modest growth barely exceeding potential, with significant slack persisting. The economy is clearly below potential with only gradual closing.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies "significant resource slack" persisting. Bright-line satisfied: slack language → < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 31 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate was 7.6 percent in June, little changed from its level in the prior few months"
- "The rate of long-duration unemployment decreased somewhat, but the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons moved up; both of these measures remained relatively high"

**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 7.6% is well above NAIRU, with elevated long-duration unemployment and involuntary part-time work. The labor market has substantial slack.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because unemployment is elevated at 7.6% with multiple indicators of labor market slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Real personal consumption expenditures increased more slowly in the second quarter than in the first"
- "Real expenditures for residential investment continued to expand briskly in the second quarter" and equipment investment growth "was greater in the second quarter than in the first quarter"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending slowed, but residential and business investment showed improvement. Housing prices rising strongly. Net picture is modestly below trend with pockets of strength.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because consumer spending (the largest component) slowed, even though investment components showed improvement.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff review.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Longer-term interest rates rose significantly" and mortgage rates rose comparably
- "Banks generally indicated that they had eased standards on both C&I and commercial real estate loans" with C&I standards "somewhat easy compared with longer-term norms"
- Staff: "higher equity prices were seen as offsetting the restrictive effects of the increase in longer-term interest rates"

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — significant rate rise tightened one channel, but bank lending standards eased and equity prices rose. Staff explicitly notes these forces offset each other. Slightly below neutral given the easing bank standards.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is near neutral (slightly below) because tightening from rates is offset by easing lending standards and rising equity prices, per staff's own assessment.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 50 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial markets were volatile at times during the intermeeting period" and "market dynamics appeared to amplify some asset price moves"
- "Bond mutual funds and bond exchange-traded funds experienced large net redemptions in June"

**Center-of-gravity**: Yields rose 30-45 bps with some volatility and amplification dynamics, plus large fund redemptions. However, no actual market dysfunction (no liquidity failures, auction problems, or settlement issues). This is elevated volatility from policy signaling, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at neutral because while volatility was elevated and redemptions were large, staff describes no actual market dysfunction — yield moves reflected policy expectations.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Spreads between yields on 10-year nonfinancial corporate bonds and yields on Treasury securities narrowed somewhat on net"
- "Leveraged loan issuance also continued to be strong amid demand for floating-rate instruments"
- C&I loan standards "somewhat easy compared with longer-term norms"

**Center-of-gravity**: Corporate spreads narrowed, leveraged loan market strong, and bank lending standards easy relative to norms. Credit conditions clearly favorable.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads narrowed and credit availability was strong. Bright-line satisfied: "narrowed" → < 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 46 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad equity price indexes ended the period higher"
- "Gains in equity values and home prices boosted household net worth"

**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose with no staff valuation concern expressed. Default to roughly fair valued / no concern range.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because staff reports price increases without expressing valuation concern.

### BANKING_STRESS: 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Market sentiment toward large domestic banking organizations appeared to improve somewhat"
- "The largest banks reported second-quarter earnings that were above analysts' expectations"
- Banks easing lending standards across multiple categories

**Center-of-gravity**: Banking sector showing improving sentiment, strong earnings, and willingness to ease lending. Low stress environment.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are performing well with improving sentiment and above-expectations earnings. Bright-line satisfied: positive bank indicators → < 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The staff's broad nominal dollar index moved up as the dollar appreciated against the currencies of the advanced foreign economies"
- "The dollar was mixed against the EME currencies"

**Center-of-gravity**: Modest dollar appreciation driven by relative rate differentials, but not uniform across all currencies.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated against advanced economy currencies, pushing the broad index up. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation → > 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer energy prices declined significantly in the second quarter"
- PCE price index "unchanged from the first quarter to the second"

**Center-of-gravity**: Energy prices fell significantly, dragging goods inflation well below neutral.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Home prices discussed as rising strongly, but shelter/rent inflation not specifically addressed as a price index component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained stable" and "remained within the narrow range seen in recent years"
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation were about unchanged"

**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored and stable from multiple measures. Slightly below 50 given the context of below-target actual inflation (no upside pressure on expectations).
