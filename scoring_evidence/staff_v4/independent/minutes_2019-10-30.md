# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-10-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
- **Type**: Minutes (synthesized summary with staff-attributable language: "the staff reported," "the staff forecast," "the staff judged")
- **Segmentation**: Post-2005 segmented minutes with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook"
- **Scoring scope**: Staff-attributable content only

### Era Context
October 2019: Late-expansion period. The Fed had cut rates in July and September 2019 (and would cut again at this meeting). Unemployment at 50-year lows (3.5%). Trade tensions with China ongoing. Manufacturing weakness/global slowdown concerns. Inflation running below 2% target. This is a period of strong labor markets but emerging headwinds from trade uncertainty and soft business investment.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.4% (12-month), core PCE at 1.8% — both below the 2% target. Consumer energy prices declined. Food inflation "well below core." The trimmed mean PCE was at 2%, providing a partial offset, but the headline numbers dominate. Staff forecast: "both inflation measures were forecast to continue to run somewhat below 2 percent through 2022." Inflation expectations "little changed" but Michigan "ticked down to the low end of its recent range."
- Direction: Below neutral. Consistent with the 2013-19 anchor of 38-42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees low inflation. Score should be below 50. Confirmed.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees output as slightly ABOVE potential. Key language: "Output was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth in 2019 and 2020." GDP increased at a "moderate rate." But real GDP growth was revised down. Business investment declining. Manufacturing soft. Staff sees risks "tilted to the downside."
- Bright-line check: "a little above... potential rate of growth" — this means output is expanding faster than potential, implying it's currently at or slightly above potential. Score MUST be > 50 but only modestly so.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at "50-year low of 3.5 percent." Labor force participation held steady, employment-to-population ratio moved up. Job openings and quits at "relatively elevated levels." Initial claims "near historically low levels." Average hourly earnings at 2.9% (moderate wage growth). Part-time for economic reasons "below its level in late 2007." Staff projects unemployment "to remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
- With UE at 3.5%, per the rubric's critical note, score should be above 55. The 50-year low and "below natural rate" language supports a score in the 60-68 range. Wage growth at 2.9% is moderate, not signaling extreme tightness.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 3.5% = very tight. Score well above 50. Confirmed.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending: "Real PCE rose solidly," "sales of light motor vehicles... maintained their robust second-quarter pace," consumer sentiment favorable, fundamentals "supportive of solid real PCE growth." Residential investment "turned up solidly" after six quarters of contraction.
BUT: "Real nonresidential private fixed investment declined further." Equipment spending soft, orders below shipments, business sentiment deteriorated, structures "decreased markedly further," industrial production declined. Manufacturing "appeared likely to remain soft."
- Net: Solid consumer spending (~55) offset by weak business investment (~35). The text gives roughly equal weight to both. Net score around 45-48.
- Direction: Slightly below neutral due to the investment drag.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff says: "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained generally supportive of spending and economic activity." Repeated throughout: "generally accommodative" for nonfinancial businesses, CRE, residential mortgage market, consumer credit. Some tightening: banks tightened standards on subprime, credit cards, CRE loans. But overall tone is clearly accommodative/supportive.
- Bright-line check: "accommodative" / "supportive" → score MUST be < 50. Score in the 35-42 range.
- Wait — the 2017-19 neutral anchor is 46-54. The language says "generally supportive/accommodative" which per the bright-line rule must be < 50. But the anchor says 2017-19 should be 46-54. The bright-line rule overrides. However, "generally supportive" in a period where this was the norm could mean score of ~42-48. I'll score 42, acknowledging the accommodative language pushes below 50 per bright-line, but not far below since this is the normal state for the era.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Accommodative" = not tight. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
The mid-September repo market disruption is discussed: "Domestic short-term funding markets were volatile in mid-September and exhibited additional, albeit modest, pressures." The EFFR was "more volatile than usual." Some CP rates "remained elevated into October." The Desk's operations "alleviated" pressures. The October 11 announcement "appeared to improve expectations about funding market conditions."
- This describes actual market dysfunction (repo rate spikes, need for emergency operations), but staff characterizes the ongoing pressures as "modest" and notes they were "alleviated." This is above-normal stress but not severe. Score in the 52-58 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dysfunction. There was actual dysfunction that required Fed intervention. Score above 50. Confirmed.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
"On net, corporate credit spreads were little changed." Financing conditions "remained generally accommodative." Strong corporate bond issuance. Leveraged loan issuance "remained solid." Credit quality "deteriorated slightly... but remained solid on balance." Credit conditions for small businesses and municipalities "stayed accommodative."
- Spreads "little changed" in an era where they were generally favorable (2013-19 anchor: 30-40). No widening signal. Score around 35-40.
- Bright-line check: No "compressed/narrow" or "widened/elevated" language. "Little changed" from an accommodative baseline. Score in the generally favorable range.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Stock prices "fell by as much as 4 percent during the first half of the intermeeting period but recovered afterward, ending the period roughly unchanged." Option-implied volatility declined and was "below the middle of its historical distribution." Staff financial stability assessment: "valuation pressures eased over the past year" for many asset classes. This is staff commentary on valuations — they see less overvaluation concern than before, but no distress.
- "Valuation pressures eased" means less overvaluation concern, so moving toward neutral from the "stretched" end. Score around 42-48.
- Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress/concern. Valuations eased (less concerning), prices roughly flat. Neutral-to-slightly-low-concern. Confirmed.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
"The risks associated with leverage at financial institutions were viewed as being low, as they have been for some time, largely because of high capital ratios at large banks." However: "the resilience of financial institutions could be undermined by low interest rates and banks' announced plans to increase payouts to shareholders." Business leverage "at or near record-high levels" (though that's corporate not bank). Banks tightened some lending standards (CRE, subprime, credit cards).
- Bright-line check: "leverage... viewed as being low" and "high capital ratios" → score MUST be < 50. The caveats add some mild concern. Score around 30-35.
- Polarity check: Higher = more stress. Low leverage risk, high capital = low stress. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"Movements in the exchange value of the dollar against most currencies were relatively modest, and the broad dollar index declined slightly."
- Bright-line check: Dollar declined slightly → score should be < 50 but only marginally. Score around 45-47.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dollar strength. Dollar weakened slightly. Score slightly below 50. Confirmed.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Consumer food prices "well below core inflation," consumer energy prices "declined." Goods-price pressure appears low. Score ~30.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI at 2.4% includes services. Not separately broken out in detail. N/A — insufficient specific services discussion.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment turned up, home sales increased, mortgage rates decline "starting to show through to housing activity." But no specific shelter/rent inflation data cited. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "were little changed" but Michigan "ticked down to the low end of its recent range." TIPS-based inflation compensation "inched down and remained near multiyear low levels." This suggests expectations are anchored but with a slight downward drift. Score ~43.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63)**: Divergence of ~11 points. Explained by the fact that the labor market was very tight at a level basis (3.5% UE) while GDP growth was only modestly above potential and decelerating. This is consistent with late-cycle dynamics where labor tightness is a lagging indicator.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (46)**: Divergence of ~6 points. Within range. Consumer strength partly offset by investment weakness.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (37)**: Both below 50, co-moving in the accommodative direction. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) vs TREASURY_STRESS (55)**: Some divergence — financial conditions are accommodative overall but there was repo market stress. This is explained by the fact that the repo stress was a specific short-term funding market issue that did not broadly tighten financial conditions for businesses and households. Coherent with explanation.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods low (~30), expectations slightly below neutral (~43). Weighted average consistent with overall score of 38.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation, as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), remained below 2 percent in August."
- "both inflation measures were forecast to continue to run somewhat below 2 percent through 2022"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: PCE at 1.4%, core at 1.8%, both below the 2% target with the staff forecasting continued below-target inflation through 2022, placing this solidly in the "below target; contained" range.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because headline and core PCE are running below the 2% target and staff expects this to persist.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Output was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth in 2019 and 2020"
- "real gross domestic product (GDP) increased at a moderate rate in the third quarter"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff projects output growth "a little above" potential in 2019-2020, implying the economy is operating modestly above potential, though risks are tilted to the downside.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly states growth is "a little above" potential. Bright-line rule satisfied (above potential → score > 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate moved down to a 50-year low of 3.5 percent in September"
- "The unemployment rate was projected to be roughly flat through 2022 and to remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unemployment at a 50-year low of 3.5% with labor force participation steady, job openings and quits elevated, and initial claims at historically low levels — all point to a very tight labor market, moderated only by wage growth at a modest 2.9%.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE at 3.5% is far below the natural rate, with broad indicators of tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Real PCE rose solidly in the third quarter" and "key factors... were supportive of solid real PCE growth"
- "Real nonresidential private fixed investment declined further in the third quarter" and "business expenditures for nonresidential structures decreased markedly further"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Solid consumer spending and a housing rebound are offset by declining business fixed investment, falling industrial production, and soft manufacturing, netting to slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the meaningful drag from declining business investment and manufacturing partly offsets solid consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No staff discussion of productivity trends.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained generally supportive of spending and economic activity"
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained generally accommodative during the intermeeting period"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Repeatedly described as "accommodative" and "supportive" across business, CRE, mortgage, and consumer credit markets, with only minor tightening at the margins (subprime, some CRE lending standards).
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because "accommodative" and "supportive" language indicates conditions are loose. Bright-line rule satisfied (accommodative → score < 50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Domestic short-term funding markets were volatile in mid-September and exhibited additional, albeit modest, pressures around the September quarter-end and the mid-October Treasury settlement date"
- "The effective federal funds rate (EFFR) was more volatile than usual over the intermeeting period"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The September repo market disruption constituted genuine market dysfunction requiring Fed intervention (overnight and term repo operations), but staff characterizes ongoing pressures as "modest" and largely alleviated by Fed actions.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral because actual funding market dysfunction occurred, though it was contained by Fed intervention.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "On net, corporate credit spreads were little changed"
- "Credit conditions for both small businesses and municipalities stayed accommodative on net"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spreads unchanged, issuance strong, credit conditions accommodative across sectors, with only slight deterioration in corporate credit quality that remained "solid on balance."
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because conditions are described as accommodative with spreads little changed, consistent with the 2013-19 generally favorable range.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 44 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad stock price indexes... ending the period roughly unchanged"
- "the staff characterized the financial vulnerabilities of the U.S. financial system as moderate" and "valuation pressures eased over the past year"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equities roughly flat with low implied volatility, and staff notes valuation pressures have eased — suggesting modestly less concern about overvaluation compared to the prior year, settling near fair value.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly below neutral (less concern/stress) because staff sees easing valuation pressures and markets were calm.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The risks associated with leverage at financial institutions were viewed as being low, as they have been for some time, largely because of high capital ratios at large banks"
- "Nonetheless, the staff noted that the resilience of financial institutions could be undermined by low interest rates and banks' announced plans to increase payouts to shareholders"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system viewed as low-risk with high capital ratios, though staff flags potential vulnerabilities from low rates and shareholder payouts as forward-looking caveats, placing this in the healthy-with-minor-concerns range.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because "low" leverage risk and "high capital ratios" indicate a healthy banking system. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 46 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the broad dollar index declined slightly"
- "Movements in the exchange value of the dollar against most currencies were relatively modest"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar weakened marginally over the period with modest overall movements, placing it slightly below the neutral stable point.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly below neutral because the dollar "declined slightly." Bright-line rule satisfied (depreciation → score < 50).

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer food price inflation was well below core inflation, and consumer energy prices declined"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both food and energy prices exerting downward pressure on goods inflation.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific services inflation discussion separate from aggregate measures.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing activity discussed but shelter/rent inflation not specifically addressed.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations... were little changed, on balance, although the Michigan survey measure ticked down to the low end of its recent range"
- "Measures of inflation compensation over the next 5 years and 5 to 10 years ahead... inched down and remained near multiyear low levels"
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Expectations anchored but drifting slightly lower, with market-based measures near multiyear lows and the Michigan survey ticking down.
