# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2003-08-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary) from the August 12, 2003 FOMC meeting. This is an **unsegmented pre-2005** document, meaning staff and participant content are mixed together. I must carefully separate staff-attributable language from participant/member commentary.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following sections use staff-attributable language ("the information reviewed at this meeting suggested," "the staff forecast prepared for this meeting"):
- Paragraphs on economic data review (payrolls, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, inventories, trade, prices) — these are staff data reviews
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting continued to suggest..." — explicitly staff forecast

Participant/member content (to IGNORE for scoring):
- "In their review of current and prospective economic developments, members commented..."
- "Members commented that current financial conditions..."

- "In the Committee's discussion of policy..."
- All voting and directive language

### Era Context
August 2003: The US economy is in the aftermath of the 2001 recession and the jobless recovery period. The fed funds rate had just been cut to 1% in June 2003. Unemployment is at 6.2%, well above NAIRU. Deflation risk is a live concern. The economy is showing nascent signs of recovery but significant slack remains.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW neutral. Key staff language: "year-over-year consumer inflation remained at a very low level"; "core consumer prices...were unchanged for the month"; twelve-month core CPI and core PCE increase was "notably lower than the rise of the previous year"; core PPI "slightly below their levels twelve months prior"; ECI "rose less in the second quarter than in the first quarter." The staff forecast notes "some downward pressure on core price inflation was considered a risk." This is clearly low and declining inflation. Direction: well below 50. Magnitude: "very low level," "notably lower" — these are strong phrases suggesting 20-30 range. Score: 22.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very low, declining inflation. Low score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output BELOW potential. Key language: "economic activity was mixed"; the staff forecast references "substantial ongoing slack in resource utilization." The economy is growing but below potential. Bright-line: "substantial ongoing slack" = MUST be < 50. The staff forecast expects expansion to "strengthen substantially" but currently there is significant slack. Score: 33.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says substantial slack. Score below 50 is correct.
- Bright-line check: "slack in resource utilization" -> score < 50. PASS.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Key language: "The labor market remained weak"; "Private nonfarm payroll employment fell in both June and July"; unemployment at 6.2% (down from 6.4%); "the average workweek edged down." UE at 6.2% maps to the 16-30 range per the rubric (UE > 7% = 16-30, UE 5.5-7% = 31-45). At 6.2%, this is in the 31-45 band but near the lower end given falling payrolls and "weak" language. Score: 28.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence says weak, falling payrolls, 6.2% UE. Low score matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed but improving picture. Consumer spending: "Real personal consumption expenditures grew in June and in the second quarter as a whole" with "brisk rise in purchases of motor vehicles and other durable goods." Housing: "robust." Business investment: "jumped in the second quarter following a lackluster performance"; spending on computers/software "accelerated sharply"; communications equipment "increased considerably." But nonresidential structures weak with "record levels" of industrial vacancy. Inventories shrank. Net assessment: consumer spending solid, business equipment recovering sharply, structures weak, inventories negative drag. The staff forecast says accommodative conditions "would provide significant impetus to business and consumer spending." Overall this is a mixed-to-improving picture, somewhat below trend given the inventory drag and structural weakness. Score: 45.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence shows solid consumption, recovering investment but with structural drags. Score just below 50 is appropriate.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff explicitly mentions "robust gains in structural productivity" in the forecast paragraph. This is a clear positive signal. Score: 62.
- Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Robust gains in structural productivity" = above trend. Score above 50 matches.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff language: "Accommodative financial conditions" in the staff forecast. Long-term interest rates rose significantly since June but the staff still characterizes conditions as accommodative. Spreads on low-grade bonds "narrowed significantly." Equity indexes advanced. Bright-line: "accommodative" = MUST be < 50. Score: 30.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. "Accommodative" = loose = low score. Correct.
- Bright-line check: "accommodative" -> < 50. PASS.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff reports: "Ten-year Treasury yields rose dramatically over the following weeks." However, this is attributed to changed expectations about the economy and policy, not dysfunction. "The increase appeared to be based on a number of factors, including investors' interpretation of the Chairman's congressional testimony, the release of Committee members' relatively bullish economic projections, and incoming news regarding the economy and corporate earnings." This is normal market adjustment to improved outlook, not dysfunction. No liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues mentioned. Score: 42.
- Bright-line check: Rising yields from policy/economic expectations = normal functioning (36-50). PASS.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff language: "Yields on high-grade corporate bonds moved up roughly in line with those on Treasury securities, leaving spreads about unchanged. Rates on low-grade corporate bonds rose much less, and spreads over Treasuries on such obligations narrowed significantly." High-grade unchanged, low-grade narrowed significantly. This indicates improving credit conditions, below-neutral stress. Score: 35.
- Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress (wider spreads). Spreads narrowing = less stress = low score. Correct.
- Bright-line check: "narrowed" -> < 50. PASS.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff reports: "broad equity indexes advanced over the intermeeting period, supported by positive earning reports, strong profit forecasts, and increased confidence regarding economic prospects." No valuation concern expressed, just factual reporting of advances supported by fundamentals. Default to near-neutral. Score: 45.
- Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress. Markets rising on fundamentals = low stress. Score below 50 is appropriate.

**BANKING_STRESS**: No specific discussion of banking system health, capital, or lending conditions in the staff-attributable sections. Score: N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff reports: "The exchange value of the dollar, as measured by the major foreign currencies index, rose over the intermeeting period as long-term interest rate differentials moved sharply in favor of dollar-denominated assets." Dollar rose/strengthened. Bright-line: appreciation -> > 50. Score: 57.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dollar strength. Dollar rose = stronger. Score > 50 matches.
- Bright-line check: Dollar "rose" -> > 50. PASS.
