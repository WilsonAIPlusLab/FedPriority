# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-08-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format. Clear staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Only staff-attributable content will be scored.

**Era context**: August 2012 — three years into recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 8.2%. GDP growth has slowed. Europe is in fiscal/banking crisis. The Fed is running the maturity extension program (Operation Twist). Inflation is low. This is solidly in the "slow recovery with substantial slack" period.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Key evidence: "consumer price inflation was markedly lower than in the first quarter, mostly reflecting substantial declines in consumer energy prices." Core prices "increased more moderately in the second quarter than in the first." Staff outlook: "continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2014" with "substantial resource slack persisting." Wage gains "remained restrained." This is clearly low inflation — anchors suggest 30-38 range for this period.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as WELL BELOW potential. Key evidence: "substantial margin of slack in labor and product markets" that would be reduced "only slowly over the projection period." GDP growth "not significantly exceed the growth rate of potential output in 2013." Economic activity "increased at a slower pace in the second quarter." Bright-line check: "slack remains" language mandates score < 50. Anchor for 2010-14 recovery: 35-42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as WELL BELOW midpoint (very loose). UE at 8.2% is far above NAIRU. "Labor market conditions had improved little in recent months." "Long-duration unemployment stayed elevated." "Share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was still high." "Indicators of job openings and firms' hiring plans were generally subdued." Anchor: UE at 8.2% -> score ~25-30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend but mixed. Consumer spending: "increased at a slower rate in the second quarter than in the first quarter." Consumer sentiment "more downbeat." Business equipment spending "rose in the second quarter at a faster pace," but forward-looking indicators (orders, surveys) "suggested that increases in outlays for business equipment would slow." Housing "improved further" but "remained at a low level." Government spending contracting. Net: sluggish overall, below trend. Score ~38-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as MIXED — somewhat accommodative in some areas, strained in others. Corporate bond yields "approaching record lows," spreads "narrowed." C&I loans "advanced further." Lending standards "eased somewhat." But: mortgage underwriting "generally have not eased much from their tightest post-crisis levels." CRE "remained somewhat strained." Safe-haven flows depressing Treasury yields. Overall financial conditions are on the accommodative side but with pockets of tightness. Bright-line: corporate credit conditions are accommodative/supportive -> score should lean < 50. But mortgage/CRE still strained. Net: ~40-45.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined to "historically low level" driven by safe-haven demand and policy expectations. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues. Treasury GC repo rates "rose slightly on balance" — normal. This is normal functioning with yield movements. Score 35-40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds fell further... approaching record lows. Their spreads relative to comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed but were still above their average levels prior to the financial crisis." CDS spreads for banks "generally moved lower." Municipal CDS "roughly unchanged." Bright-line: "narrowed" spreads -> score < 50. But still above pre-crisis average -> not extremely compressed. Score ~35-40.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad indexes of U.S. equity prices rose somewhat, on net." Option-implied volatility "rose slightly." Bank stock prices "posted mixed changes." No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 45-55 per rubric. Score ~48.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not extensively discussed as a standalone topic, but some signals: bank credit "continued to expand modestly," C&I loans growing robustly. SLOOS showed "demand strengthened" and "lending standards eased somewhat." Bank CDS "moved lower." CRE "remained somewhat strained" with "tight underwriting." Mortgage underwriting at "tightest post-crisis levels." Bright-line: no "stressed" or "troubled" language, but no "well-capitalized" or "sound" either. Mixed: lending improving but mortgage/CRE still tight. Score ~45-50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index for the foreign exchange value of the dollar changed little, on net, over the intermeeting period, although the dollar appreciated against the euro." Bright-line: "changed little" net -> near 50. Appreciated vs euro but stable broadly. Score ~50-52.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "consumer price inflation was markedly lower than in the first quarter, mostly reflecting substantial declines in consumer energy prices"; "the staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2014"; "nominal wage gains remained restrained"
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation running clearly below target with subdued outlook and restrained wages.
- Direction: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation declined markedly and staff projects it will stay subdued.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the expansion in economic activity was anticipated to reduce the substantial margin of slack in labor and product markets only slowly over the projection period"; "economic activity increased at a slower pace in the second quarter"
- Center-of-gravity: Economy well below potential with substantial slack that the staff expects to persist for years.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff explicitly cites "substantial margin of slack." Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate was 8.2 percent in June"; "The rate of long-duration unemployment stayed elevated, and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was still high"; "Indicators of job openings and firms' hiring plans were generally subdued"
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market very loose — 8.2% unemployment, elevated long-term joblessness, subdued hiring indicators.
- Direction: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE 8.2% is far above NAIRU with broad labor slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real personal consumption expenditures increased at a slower rate in the second quarter than in the first quarter"; "Real business expenditures on equipment and software rose in the second quarter at a faster pace" but "forward-looking indicators...suggested that increases in outlays for business equipment would slow"
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending slowing, business investment up but expected to decelerate, housing low but improving, government contracting. Net below trend.
- Direction: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending growth has slowed and forward indicators are weakening.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS

- Center-of-gravity: Corporate credit broadly accommodative with strong issuance and easing bank lending standards, but mortgage and CRE segments remain tight. Net somewhat accommodative.
- Direction: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (looser) because credit is broadly available and spreads are compressing, though with pockets of continued tightness. Bright-line satisfied (accommodative -> < 50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

- Evidence: "The yield on nominal 10-year Treasury securities declined to a historically low level"; Treasury GC repo rates "rose slightly on balance" — no dysfunction language.
- Center-of-gravity: Treasury market functioning normally; yield declines driven by safe-haven flows and policy expectations, not dysfunction.
- Direction: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because no market dysfunction is described. Bright-line satisfied.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 37 +-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "spreads relative to comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed but were still above their average levels prior to the financial crisis"; "CDS spreads for those firms generally moved lower on net"
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads narrowing and approaching favorable levels but not yet back to pre-crisis compression. Below-normal stress.
- Direction: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads have narrowed. Bright-line satisfied (narrowed -> < 50).

- Evidence: "Broad indexes of U.S. equity prices rose somewhat, on net, over the intermeeting period"; "Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index rose slightly"

- Direction: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral; no valuation assessment from staff, just price movement description.
