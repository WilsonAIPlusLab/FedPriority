# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary with staff-attributable language: "the staff revised up," "the staff forecast," "the staff anticipated"). It is a **post-2005 segmented** minutes document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

### Era Context
April 2009 — deep in the Great Financial Crisis. The economy is in severe recession with rapidly rising unemployment (8.5%), collapsing industrial production, falling house prices, and impaired financial markets. However, this meeting shows some early tentative signs of stabilization (pace of decline slowing, some financial conditions improving from crisis extremes). The FOMC had already cut rates to near zero and was expanding large-scale asset purchases.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. "Headline and core consumer prices rose at a moderate pace over the first three months of the year." Energy prices rebounded somewhat after their late-2008 drop, and core prices picked up. However, the staff outlook anticipates "low level of resource utilization" leading to "deceleration in core PCE prices" and projects "overall and core PCE inflation would stay in a low range." Commodity prices "stayed far below their year-earlier values." Near-term expectations up but longer-term at "the low end of the range seen over the past few years." This is a picture of low inflation with some downside risk — consistent with the 2009 anchor of 15-20. The current readings are moderate, but the forecast is for continued low inflation. Score should be around 22-28.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy far below potential. "The pace of decline in some components of final demand appeared to have slowed recently" — this is describing a less negative rate of decline, not a return to potential. Industrial production "fell substantially," manufacturing capacity utilization "decreased to a very low level," light vehicle production "lowest level in more than three decades." The staff projects real GDP to only "edge higher in the second half" and expects unemployment to keep rising through early 2010. The staff says "low level of resource utilization" explicitly. Bright-line: "below potential" language → must be < 50. This is deep recession territory. Score ~18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff describes severe labor market deterioration. Unemployment at 8.5%, "fifth consecutive large monthly decrease" in payrolls, "losses widespread across industries," average workweek ticked down, claims "remained elevated," beneficiaries at "highest level since 1982." Staff expects unemployment to rise further through early 2010. With UE at 8.5% and rising, this is firmly in the 15-22 range per anchors. Score ~16.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but still very weak. Consumer spending "rose moderately in the first quarter after having fallen in the second half of last year" — some stabilization. But "fundamentals for this sector remained weak: Wages and salaries dropped, house prices were markedly lower." Equipment and software spending "dropped markedly in the first quarter, with declines about as steep and widespread as in the fourth quarter of 2008." Nonresidential structures contracted. Housing "remained depressed but seemed to have leveled off." Net picture: consumption stabilizing at depressed levels, investment still falling sharply. Score ~22.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Conditions improving from extreme stress but still very tight. "Conditions in short-term funding markets improved somewhat." Spreads "edged down" but "Libor fixings beyond the one-month maturity stayed elevated." Repo market showed "additional improvement" but "volumes were still low." Stock prices rose significantly. Credit availability was "still tight." The Senior Loan Officer Survey showed banks "continued to tighten their credit standards." Private borrowing rates moved lower and "some measures of financial stress eased." Overall: improving from crisis peaks but still very tight. Bright-line check: "tight" language → must be > 50. Score ~72.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff discusses Treasury market conditions directly. "Trading conditions in the secondary market for nominal Treasury securities also showed some signs of improvement. Premiums paid for on-the-run Treasury securities fell, and average bid-asked spreads for Treasury notes were relatively stable near their pre-crisis levels. Still, daily trading volumes for Treasury securities remained low." Also, "Poor liquidity in the market for Treasury inflation-protected securities continued." TSLF auctions were undersubscribed (sign that acute stress eased). This is a picture of improving but still somewhat impaired functioning — not normal yet. Score ~52.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate bond spreads remained extremely high by historical standards" even though they narrowed. Speculative-grade conditions "had become less strained since last autumn." Investment-grade bid-asked spreads at "moderately elevated levels." The basis between CDS and corporate spreads stayed at "high levels." "No issuance of commercial MBS occurred." Bright-line: "widened"/"elevated" → must be > 50. These are extremely elevated spreads though narrowing. Score ~78.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose significantly." "Financial stocks outperformed broader markets." But "the equity risk premium—narrowed during the intermeeting period but was still very high by historical standards." "Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index decreased but stayed well above historical norms." Equity prices still "down substantially from their levels of 12 months earlier." Staff describes elevated risk premium and volatility — suggestive of stressed/depressed equity conditions. Score ~65.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banks "continued to tighten their credit standards and terms on all major loan categories." Commercial bank credit "contracted in March and was estimated to have dropped again in April." The basis at high levels reflecting "ongoing lack of financing capacity at major financial institutions." However, there was "an easing of concern about financial institutions" and "relatively favorable first-quarter earnings reports from a few major firms." European countries took measures to "support their banking sectors." Still clearly stressed. Bright-line: stressed → must be > 50. Score ~72.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated against the other major currencies in an environment of seemingly increased investor appetite for risk." Bright-line: depreciation → must be < 50. Score ~35.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices "rebounded somewhat after their substantial late-year drop." Core intermediate materials prices fell "though at a noticeably slower pace than in late 2008." Commodity prices "rose in March but stayed far below their year-earlier values." Import prices of non-oil goods moved down. This is a picture of low goods inflation with some stabilization from the deflationary plunge. Score ~28.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed in enough detail. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: House prices "stayed on a downward trajectory." Rents are not explicitly discussed. The housing price discussion relates to asset prices, not shelter CPI. Insufficient direct discussion of shelter inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Near-term inflation expectations increased in early April but did not appear to influence longer-term expectations, whose levels in April were still at the low end of the range seen over the past few years." Staff outlook: "gradual decline in inflation expectations" anticipated, "inflation expectations would be relatively well anchored." This is anchored but at the low end — consistent with 2009 anchor of 25-35. Score ~32.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Headline and core consumer prices rose at a moderate pace over the first three months of the year." Staff outlook: "the low level of resource utilization and a gradual decline in inflation expectations would lead to a deceleration in core PCE prices" and "overall and core PCE inflation would stay in a low range."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Current inflation readings are moderate but the staff sees substantial slack pushing inflation lower, with the outlook firmly in a "low range" — consistent with meaningful disinflation risk though not outright deflation.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes moderate current readings with a weak outlook driven by massive resource slack, placing this in the 2009 deflation-fear anchor zone.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 18 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Manufacturing capacity utilization decreased to a very low level." "The staff anticipated that the low level of resource utilization...would lead to a deceleration in core PCE prices." Staff projects unemployment to "rise through the beginning of 2010."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is in deep recession with massive slack — industrial production collapsing, capacity utilization at historic lows, and the staff explicitly cites "low level of resource utilization."
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because the economy is deeply below potential with substantial slack. Bright-line satisfied: below potential → score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The civilian unemployment rate climbed to 8.5 percent." "Private nonfarm payroll employment registered its fifth consecutive large monthly decrease, with losses widespread across industries." "The number of individuals receiving unemployment benefits relative to the size of the labor force reached its highest level since 1982."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Severe labor market deterioration with unemployment at 8.5% and rising, massive ongoing job losses, and the staff expecting further increases in unemployment — one of the weakest labor markets in decades.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because the labor market is extremely slack with high and rising unemployment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Consumer spending "rose moderately in the first quarter after having fallen in the second half of last year" but "fundamentals for this sector remained weak." "Real spending on equipment and software dropped markedly in the first quarter, with declines about as steep and widespread as in the fourth quarter of 2008."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending showed some stabilization from its late-2008 collapse, but business investment continued to contract sharply — the net picture is deeply below trend with only tentative signs of bottoming on the consumer side.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is far below neutral because investment is still plunging and consumer spending, while stabilizing, is at depressed levels with weak fundamentals.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

**Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 72 ±6 [High confidence]

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions remain very tight despite meaningful improvement from crisis peaks — banks still tightening standards, credit contracting, though funding markets and some spreads showing improvement.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well above neutral because credit remains tight and banks continue to restrict lending. Bright-line satisfied: "tight" → score > 50.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market functioning is improving — bid-ask spreads near pre-crisis norms and on-the-run premiums declining — but residual stress persists in low volumes and poor TIPS liquidity.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral — meaningful improvement from crisis but not yet fully normal, with residual dysfunction in some segments.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 78 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Corporate bond spreads remained extremely high by historical standards." "The basis between an index of credit default swap spreads and measures of investment-grade corporate spreads...stayed at high levels, reportedly reflecting an ongoing lack of financing capacity at major financial institutions."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite some narrowing over the intermeeting period, credit spreads remain at historically extreme levels reflecting severe credit stress and impaired market functioning.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is far above neutral because spreads are "extremely high by historical standards." Bright-line satisfied: "elevated" → score > 50.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equity markets rallied significantly over the period but the risk premium and volatility remain elevated, indicating continued stress/concern despite improvement.

Banks "continued to tighten their credit standards and terms on all major loan categories." The CDS-corporate spread basis at "high levels, reportedly reflecting an ongoing lack of financing capacity at major financial institutions."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The banking system remains under significant stress — credit contracting, standards still tightening, and major institutions lacking financing capacity — though some easing of acute concerns with favorable earnings reports from a few firms.
