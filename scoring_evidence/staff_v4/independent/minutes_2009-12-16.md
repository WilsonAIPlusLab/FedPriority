# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format. Three clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

### Era Context
December 2009 — approximately 6 months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in early recovery but unemployment remains near its peak (~10%). The financial crisis has passed its acute phase but banking stress remains elevated. The Fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound. This is a period of massive slack, subdued inflation, and gradually improving but still-stressed financial conditions.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Core inflation "remained subdued," core consumer prices "increased modestly in October and were unchanged in November." Energy pushed up headline but staff views this as "largely transitory." Staff projects core inflation will "slow somewhat from its current pace." Inflation expectations moved down to "the lower end of the narrow range." Unit labor costs declined further. This is clearly low inflation pressure — anchor suggests 15-20 for 2009 deflation fears, but the text does not describe outright deflation risk as acute; rather inflation is subdued and expected to slow further. Score range: 20-25.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy as FAR BELOW potential. "Recovery in economic activity was gaining momentum" but "capacity utilization remained very low," "projected pace of real output growth... was expected to exceed that of potential output by only enough to produce a very gradual reduction in economic slack," and "projected degree of slack in resource utilization... was still quite substantial." Bright-line: "slack remains" language mandates score < 50. The slack is described as "quite substantial" and only gradually reducing. Score range: 22-28.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very LOOSE. "Unemployment rate remained quite elevated" (it was ~10%), "pace of job losses slowed noticeably" but still losing jobs, "average length of spells of unemployment continued to increase." The improvement is in the rate of deterioration, not the level. Unemployment at ~10% with ongoing job losses places this firmly in the 15-22 anchor range. Score: 18.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed but improving picture. Consumer spending "expanded solidly in October" with "broad-based advances." Motor vehicle sales bounced back. Home sales "increased significantly." However, investment in nonresidential structures "declined further," conditions in nonresidential real estate "remained quite poor," nonresidential structures "plunged in the third quarter." Equipment/software "continued to stabilize" with "slight" rise. This is a mixed picture: consumer spending improving from very weak levels, investment still contracting in key areas. Net assessment: below trend but improving. Score range: 35-40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff explicitly mentions "quite rapid productivity growth" and "further decline in unit labor costs." This is discussed and clearly above trend. Score: 62.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as still tight but improving. "Capital markets continued to become somewhat more supportive of business investment." But "banks continued to raise spreads on business loans," "continued tightening of credit terms by banks," C&I loan rate spread "rose for the fifth consecutive survey," "tight credit conditions persisted" in multifamily. Consumer credit "contracted for the ninth consecutive month." Short-term funding markets "little changed" with spreads "above pre-crisis levels." The net picture: conditions easing from crisis levels but still restrictive. Bright-line: language indicates tightening/restrictive conditions, score must be > 50. Score: 63.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes Treasury market functioning positively: "Indicators of functioning in the market for nominal Treasury securities—including trading volumes and liquidity premiums for the on-the-run 10-year note—were roughly stable." TIPS market showed "further improvement." Year-end pressures "remained modest." This is normal-to-good functioning. No dysfunction described. Score: 35.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads narrowing but still elevated. Corporate bond spreads "somewhat narrower." CP spreads "near their lows of the past two years." CDS spreads for banks "narrowed a bit." But bank loan spreads continue rising, Libor-OIS "remained above pre-crisis levels." Speculative- and investment-grade bond issuance was "robust." The picture is improving from crisis but spreads still above normal. Score: 55.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased further." Equity risk premium "remained... at a relatively high level" — meaning stocks are perceived as cheap/uncertain relative to bonds (high risk premium = depressed valuation or heightened uncertainty). Financial stocks "significantly underperformed." This suggests moderate equity concern. Score: 55.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Significant stress remains. "Commercial bank credit decreased further." C&I loans "continued to drop." "Credit quality appeared to worsen as delinquency and charge-off rates increased further for most major loan categories." Banks continue tightening lending. Unused loan commitments "shrank for the seventh consecutive quarter." Profitability "turned positive" but concentrated at a few large institutions. Banks raising equity and shrinking balance sheets. Financial stocks underperformed due to profitability concerns. This is substantial banking stress, consistent with the 2008-09 anchor of 70-85. The worst of the crisis is past but stress is very much ongoing. Score: 70.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated over much of the intermeeting period, but then reversed course... On balance, the dollar ended the period up slightly against the major foreign currencies and down a little relative to the currencies of other important trading partners." This is essentially mixed/flat. Score: 48.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not specifically broken out for goods vs services in detail. The text mentions energy prices pushed up headline, core was subdued. N/A — insufficient specific goods inflation discussion.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A — not separately discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices: "some house price indexes declined a little in September and October" but "remained above the troughs reached last spring." This is about asset prices, not shelter inflation per se. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff notes: "Median year-ahead inflation expectations in the Reuters/University of Michigan Survey of Consumers declined in early December, and the same survey's measure of longer-term inflation expectations moved down to the lower end of the narrow range that prevailed over the previous few years." Also, 5-year/5-year inflation compensation "rose" and was "near the upper end of its range in recent years." Mixed signals but expectations generally contained to slightly low. Score: 40.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "core consumer price inflation remained subdued"; "Core consumer prices increased modestly in October and were unchanged in November"; "the staff continued to project that core inflation would slow somewhat from its current pace over the next two years"
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation as subdued and expected to decline further, with substantial slack keeping a lid on prices. Not outright deflation risk but clearly well below target.
- Direction: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff reports subdued and decelerating core inflation with substantial resource slack.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "capacity utilization remained very low"; "projected degree of slack in resource utilization over the next two years was... still quite substantial"; "a very gradual reduction in economic slack"
- Center-of-gravity: The economy is far below potential with massive slack that will close only very gradually. Recovery is underway but the gap remains large.
- Direction: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff describes "quite substantial" slack persisting. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "unemployment rate remained quite elevated" (~10%); "pace of job losses slowed considerably... relative to the steep declines" earlier; "average length of spells of unemployment continued to increase"
- Center-of-gravity: Unemployment near 10%, still losing jobs (though more slowly), long-term unemployment worsening. Extreme labor market slack.
- Direction: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because of very high unemployment and ongoing (though slower) job losses.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending expanded solidly in October" with "broad-based advances"; but "investment in nonresidential structures declined further as conditions in nonresidential real estate markets remained poor"; "real outlays on structures outside of the drilling and mining sector plunged in the third quarter"
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is recovering with solid gains, but business investment is deeply mixed — equipment stabilizing while nonresidential structures are plunging. Net is below trend.
- Direction: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral reflecting the drag from weak business investment offsetting improving consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "quite rapid productivity growth and a further decline in unit labor costs"
- Center-of-gravity: Staff explicitly calls productivity growth "quite rapid," placing it clearly above trend.
- Direction: PRODUCTIVITY is above neutral because staff characterizes growth as "quite rapid."

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]**

- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions remain meaningfully tight, with banks actively tightening and credit contracting, though capital markets are slowly easing. The net picture is still restrictive.
- Direction: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because of ongoing bank credit tightening and elevated spreads. Bright-line satisfied (> 50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Corporate bond spreads "somewhat narrower"; CP spreads "near their lows of the past two years"; but bank C&I loan spreads "rose for the fifth consecutive survey"; Libor-OIS "remained above pre-crisis levels"
- Center-of-gravity: Market-based spreads are narrowing but still elevated relative to pre-crisis norms. Bank lending spreads continue widening. The overall credit environment is modestly stressed.
- Direction: CREDIT_SPREADS is modestly above neutral because while improving, spreads remain above pre-crisis levels and bank spreads are still rising.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "broad stock price indexes increased further"; "equity risk premium—remained about unchanged at a relatively high level"; "financial stocks significantly underperformed the market, as investors continued to express concerns about the future profitability of the banking industry"
- Center-of-gravity: Equities are rising but the high risk premium suggests continued investor caution/depressed risk appetite. Financial stocks remain a source of concern. Modest stress remains.
