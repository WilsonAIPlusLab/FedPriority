# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-08-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: August 2010 — about one year into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated (~9.5%), the recovery is losing momentum, fiscal stimulus is fading, and the housing market remains depressed. The euro area sovereign debt crisis emerged in spring 2010 but has stabilized somewhat. The Fed funds rate is at 0-0.25%. This is a period of significant slack with subdued inflation.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "inflation remained subdued," "headline consumer prices declined in May and June," "core PCE price index moved up only slightly," "year-over-year increase in the index in June was lower than earlier in the year," "prices of core consumer goods continued to decline." The staff outlook projects inflation to "remain subdued over the next year and a half" with "some slowing in core inflation in 2011." This is clearly below-target inflation with no pressure. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says subdued/declining. Score well below 50. Consistent.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as well BELOW potential. Evidence: "capacity utilization in manufacturing...was still substantially short of its longer-run average," "resource slack was still anticipated to remain quite elevated at the end of 2011," "the wide margin of economic slack," "the pace of the economic recovery slowed in recent months," "the recent recession was deeper than previously thought." Bright-line check: "slack remains" / "below potential" language clearly present => score MUST be < 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as very LOOSE. Evidence: "private nonfarm employment expanded slowly," "average monthly gain in private payroll employment...was small, considerably less than the average increase over the preceding three months," "unemployment rate moved down in June from its level earlier in the year" (but still around 9.5%), "initial claims for unemployment insurance remained at an elevated level," "employment in construction and financial activities fell further." The unemployment rate is ~9.5%, which is far above NAIRU. Score well below 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed signals but generally below trend. Consumer spending: "rose at a modest rate," "rose gradually during the second quarter," consumer confidence "fell back in July." Housing: "quite soft," "depressed level," starts at "lowest level since spring of 2009." Business investment in E&S: "rose strongly again in the second quarter" — a positive signal. Nonresidential structures still weak but turning up slightly. Net: modest consumer spending + weak housing + strong E&S investment. Mixed but below trend overall.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff discusses productivity directly: "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector declined in the second quarter after rising rapidly in the preceding three quarters." This is a notable decline. Score below midpoint.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat SUPPORTIVE/ACCOMMODATIVE. Evidence: "Financial conditions...became somewhat more supportive of economic growth," "interest rates on Treasury securities, corporate bonds, and mortgages moved down further," "conditions in short-term funding markets improved somewhat," spreads "declined moderately." Bright-line check: "supportive" = accommodative => score MUST be < 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes Treasury markets functioning well. "Treasury auctions were generally well received, with bid-to-cover ratios mostly exceeding historical averages." Yields fell on weaker data — normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. Score in the low/calm range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as improving/narrowing. Evidence: "spreads relative to yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities declined moderately," "credit default swap spreads for large financial institutions narrowed moderately," "secondary-market bid prices on syndicated leveraged loans rose a bit, while bid-asked spreads in that market edged down." However, "credit card interest rates...stayed elevated," consumer delinquency rates "remained elevated." Net: spreads narrowing but still somewhat elevated. Bright-line: narrowing/declining spreads => score < 50 direction, but still somewhat elevated levels.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased slightly," "option-implied volatility...declined but remained somewhat elevated by historical standards," "the equity risk premium—was little changed at an elevated level." An elevated equity risk premium suggests equities are relatively cheap/stressed, not overvalued. Score modestly above 50 (some stress/concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff describes ongoing stress but improvement. Evidence: "commercial banks' core loans...continued to contract," "commercial real estate loans continued to decline steeply," delinquency rates for consumer loans "remained elevated," but "a small net fraction of respondents had eased standards for C&I loans" (second straight quarter), consumer credit quality "continued to show improvement." Banking system still under stress but improving. Bright-line check: Higher = more stress. Evidence shows ongoing contraction in bank lending, elevated delinquencies. Score above 50 but not extreme.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff says "the value of the dollar declined on balance over the intermeeting period" and the staff outlook notes "the dollar reversed its April to June appreciation." Bright-line check: Dollar depreciation => score MUST be < 50.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "prices of core consumer goods continued to decline" — clearly below midpoint.
**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "prices of non-energy services rose moderately" — near or slightly below midpoint.
**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "were largely stable," mortgage rates fell to record lows, but no specific shelter/rent inflation discussion. Limited evidence, but house prices stable suggests low inflation pressure.
**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "survey measures of short- and long-term inflation expectations were largely stable" but "inflation compensation based on TIPS moved down further." Mixed but anchored near or slightly below target. Staff outlook: "stable inflation expectations" would temper disinflation.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (32) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (22)**: Both well below 50. Labor is even lower because unemployment is ~9.5%, very far from full employment. Divergence ~10 points — reasonable given that output is recovering (GDP growing) but labor market lagging.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (32) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38)**: Both below 50. Close together. Coherent — spending is weak but E&S investment provides some offset.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (42)**: Both below 50. Financial conditions somewhat accommodative, spreads narrowing but still somewhat elevated. Coherent.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (22)**: Both below midpoint. Treasury market functioning well, consistent with generally supportive financial conditions. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (22) <-> sub-dimensions**: Goods very low (~18), services moderate (~42), housing (~35), expectations (~40). Weighted average roughly 30-35, parent at 22 is somewhat lower, but the strong "subdued" and "declined" language in headline/core measures justifies a lower parent. Reasonable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "inflation remained subdued"; "headline consumer prices declined in May and June because of sizable drops in consumer energy prices"; "the core PCE price index moved up only slightly, and the year-over-year increase in the index in June was lower than earlier in the year"; staff outlook: "overall inflation was projected to remain subdued over the next year and a half."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation as subdued, with headline declining and core barely rising, well below target and projected to slow further.
- Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes inflation as subdued, declining in headline, barely rising in core, with slack projected to push core even lower.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "capacity utilization in manufacturing...was still substantially short of its longer-run average"; "resource slack was still anticipated to remain quite elevated at the end of 2011"; "the wide margin of economic slack."
- Center-of-gravity: Significant slack remains; the recovery has slowed and the recession was deeper than thought; GDP still well below potential with slow closing.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff repeatedly references substantial slack, below-potential output, and slow recovery. Bright-line satisfied: "slack remains" => score < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "private nonfarm employment expanded slowly"; "the average monthly gain in private payroll employment during the three months ending in July was small, considerably less than the average increase over the preceding three months"; "initial claims for unemployment insurance remained at an elevated level"; unemployment rate ~9.5%.
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market is very loose with high unemployment, slow payroll growth, and elevated claims — far from full employment.
- Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is ~9.5%, job gains are decelerating, and claims are elevated. The anchor for 2009-10 is 15-22; this fits the upper end as there is slow job growth.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "consumer spending continued to rise at a modest rate"; "housing activity dropped back" and was "quite soft"; "real business spending on equipment and software rose strongly again in the second quarter"; "nonresidential construction remained weak."
- Center-of-gravity: Below-trend spending overall — modest consumer growth, depressed housing, weak nonresidential construction, partially offset by strong business E&S spending.
- Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the balance of consumer, housing, and construction weakness outweighs strong E&S investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 35 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector declined in the second quarter after rising rapidly in the preceding three quarters"; "unit labor costs remained well below their level one year earlier."
- Center-of-gravity: Productivity declined in Q2 after strong prior gains; below-trend in the current period though the level of unit labor costs is still favorable.
- Direction confirmation: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because output per hour actually declined in Q2.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financial conditions...became somewhat more supportive of economic growth"; "interest rates on Treasury securities, corporate bonds, and mortgages moved down further"; "conditions in short-term funding markets improved somewhat"; "spreads of term Libor over rates on overnight index swaps moved down."
- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat accommodative/supportive, with declining rates and improving funding markets, though some residual stress remains.
- Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (= looser) because staff explicitly calls conditions "somewhat more supportive." Bright-line satisfied: "supportive" => score < 50.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Treasury auctions were generally well received, with bid-to-cover ratios mostly exceeding historical averages"; yields fell on weaker economic data (normal adjustment, not dysfunction).
- Center-of-gravity: Treasury market functioning well — strong auction demand, orderly yield declines. No dysfunction.
- Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is well below neutral because auctions were strong and there is no mention of any market dysfunction. Anchor for 2010-15 calm markets is 25-35; this fits the lower end given explicitly positive auction language.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "spreads relative to yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities declined moderately"; "credit default swap spreads for large financial institutions narrowed moderately"; but "spreads of credit card interest rates over those on Treasury securities stayed elevated"; consumer delinquency rates "remained elevated."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads are narrowing and generally favorable but some areas remain elevated, placing conditions somewhat below normal but not fully normalized.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (= less stress) because spreads are declining/narrowing across most markets. Bright-line satisfied: narrowing spreads => score < 50.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased slightly"; "option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index declined but remained somewhat elevated by historical standards"; "the equity risk premium—was little changed at an elevated level."
- Center-of-gravity: Equities edged higher but the elevated equity risk premium and somewhat elevated volatility suggest continued market wariness/mild stress. Not distressed, but not fully confident.
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral (= slightly more concern/stress than normal) because the equity risk premium is elevated despite modest price gains.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "commercial banks' core loans...continued to contract in June and July"; "commercial real estate loans continued to decline steeply"; delinquency and charge-off rates "remained elevated"; but "a small net fraction of respondents had eased standards for C&I loans" and consumer credit quality "continued to show improvement."
- Center-of-gravity: Banking system still under meaningful stress — contracting loan books, elevated delinquencies, steep CRE declines — but with emerging signs of improvement (easing standards, improving credit quality).
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because loan contraction, elevated delinquencies, and steep CRE declines indicate ongoing stress, despite early improvement signs. Bright-line satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the value of the dollar declined on balance over the intermeeting period, likely reflecting some reversal of flight-to-safety flows, better-than-expected European economic data, and the softer economic outlook for the United States"; staff outlook: "the dollar reversed its April to June appreciation."
- Center-of-gravity: The dollar weakened over the period, reversing prior appreciation.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line satisfied: dollar depreciation => score < 50.

- Goods prices are actively falling — clearly below midpoint.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

- Modest increases, somewhat below the 50 midpoint but not declining.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
