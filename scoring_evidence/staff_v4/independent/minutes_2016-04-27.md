# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-04-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Post-2005 segmented minutes. Three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: April 2016. The economy is in the mid-cycle recovery from the GFC. The Fed raised rates for the first time in December 2015 and is moving very gradually. Unemployment is around 5%, inflation is running below 2%, global growth is sluggish, and oil prices had declined sharply but were recovering. The dollar had appreciated notably in 2014-2015.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at 1% over 12 months, core PCE at 1-3/4%. "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." Inflation restrained by energy price declines and falling non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation "still low." Staff projects inflation to "increase over the next several years" but "still projected to be slightly below" 2% in 2018. Risks "weighted to the downside." This is clearly below-target inflation — score in the 30-38 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW midpoint but closing. GDP growth "appeared to have slowed" in Q1. Staff projects "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." Unemployment "expected to gradually decline further and to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Industrial production declined. The staff is saying the economy is still growing faster than potential (closing the gap), and unemployment is projected to go below natural rate. But current Q1 GDP was very weak. The phrase "modestly faster pace than potential output" suggests GDP is still near but perhaps slightly below potential given recent weakness, with the gap projected to close. Risks "tilted to the downside." Score around 45-48 — slightly below potential with the gap closing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as near midpoint, approaching tightness. Unemployment at 5.0%, "labor market conditions improved further," payroll employment expanded at "solid pace," participation and employment-to-population ratios rising, initial claims at "low level," job openings at "elevated level," hires and quits moved up. Wage growth modest at 2-1/4%. Unemployment projected to "run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." At 5.0% UE with the natural rate estimated around 4.8-5.0% at the time, this is near NAIRU. Score around 48-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as BELOW midpoint. Consumer spending growth "appeared to have slowed in the first quarter" though fundamentals support a pickup. "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to decline further." Forward-looking indicators "continued to be soft." Mining/drilling output contracting. Manufacturing output decreased. On the positive side: solid income gains, consumer sentiment "upbeat," housing showing "continued slow recovery." The net picture is weak — consumer spending slowing and business investment declining. Score around 37-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff mentions productivity directly: "Labor productivity growth appeared to have remained slow." This is clearly below trend. Score around 30-35.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat loose / accommodative. "Financial market conditions improved further, on balance." Risk sentiment improved, volatility declined, equity prices rose, VIX below historical median. "Financing conditions for U.S. nonfinancial businesses remained generally accommodative for investment-grade issuers." Speculative-grade conditions "improved somewhat after showing strains earlier in the year." However, banks tightened C&I lending standards, tightened CRE lending standards, and RRE credit "stayed tight" for lower-quality borrowers. There's a mixed picture — market-based conditions are quite accommodative but bank lending is tightening. Net: somewhat accommodative but with pockets of tightening. Score around 40-45. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language → must be < 50. Score 42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "declined slightly" — normal orderly price adjustment. No mention of any market dysfunction. Municipal bond markets "continued to be stable." Score around 35-40 (normal, calm). No dysfunction signals.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed picture. Corporate bond spreads "declined, on balance, but remained at levels near the high end of their ranges since 2012." CMBS spreads "narrowed markedly but remained elevated." ABS spreads "at levels that were a bit higher than usual." Banks tightened C&I and CRE lending standards, and banks expected increased delinquencies in energy sector. Credit conditions improving but still elevated from earlier stress. Score around 52-58 — somewhat above neutral given spreads near highs since 2012 and ongoing tightening.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity prices moved up. VIX below historical median. Corporate earnings "decreased markedly relative to the previous quarter." Staff financial stability report: "valuation pressures rose somewhat," "CRE valuations were elevated." The staff notes some valuation concern. Score around 35-40 — modestly above fair value / some elevated valuation concerns (lower score = more overvaluation concern). Actually, let me re-read the rubric: Higher = MORE equity market stress/concern. Low scores = overvaluation concern. Staff says "valuation pressures rose somewhat" — this is overvaluation concern direction (low score). But equities also rose and markets are functioning fine. Score around 38-42.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff financial stability report: "leverage and maturity transformation in the financial sector were subdued relative to historical levels." "The financial system was fairly resilient." "Vulnerabilities were moderate overall." Banks tightening lending standards, expecting energy-sector delinquencies. This is a mostly healthy system with some emerging concerns. Higher = more stress. Score around 38-42. Bright-line check: system described as "fairly resilient" and "subdued" leverage → must be < 50. Score 40.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Dollar "depreciated against most currencies" over the intermeeting period, "in part because higher oil prices supported the currencies of oil exporters." Staff also notes earlier dollar appreciation effects (manufacturing weakness from "earlier appreciation of the foreign exchange value of the dollar") and the staff outlook revised down the "assumed path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Bright-line check: depreciation → must be < 50. But the prior appreciation effects are still weighing on economy. Current direction is depreciation, so score < 50 but not extremely low since it's a modest decline. Score around 38-42.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and declining prices of non-energy imports." Core PCE at 1-3/4%, total PCE at 1%. "Inflation was still projected to be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation running well below 2% target with multiple restraining factors, and staff projects it to remain below target even through 2018.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running well below the 2% target at 1% headline and 1.75% core.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 46 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP growth in the first quarter of this year was estimated to have been much slower than in the forecast." "The staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." "The unemployment rate was expected to gradually decline further and to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Very weak Q1 GDP suggests the economy is slightly below potential, but staff projects growth above potential going forward, with the gap expected to close and reverse.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is slightly below neutral because current GDP growth was very weak in Q1 though the economy is close to potential and projected to grow above it.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate edged up to 5.0 percent, both the labor force participation rate and the employment-to-population ratio continued to increase." "Total nonfarm payroll employment expanded at a solid pace in March." "Average hourly earnings for all employees increased 2¼ percent over the 12 months ending in March."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unemployment near NAIRU at 5.0% with solid job growth and improving participation, but only modest wage gains — consistent with approximate full employment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at neutral because the labor market is near full employment with the unemployment rate near the estimated natural rate.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth in real personal consumption expenditures appeared to have slowed in the first quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to decline further in the first quarter." "Forward-looking indicators of equipment spending...continued to be soft."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment weakened in Q1, with business investment outright declining, though consumer fundamentals (income, sentiment) remain supportive of a near-term pickup.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because both major components weakened, with business investment declining further.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor productivity growth appeared to have remained slow over the four quarters ending in the first quarter of this year."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: A single sentence clearly characterizing productivity growth as slow and persistently so.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because staff explicitly characterizes productivity growth as having "remained slow."

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 42 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financial market conditions improved further, on balance." "Financing conditions for U.S. nonfinancial businesses remained generally accommodative for investment-grade issuers, and those for speculative-grade firms improved somewhat." However, "banks further tightened their lending standards on C&I loans" and "a significant number of SLOOS respondents reported tightening their lending standards on all major categories of CRE loans."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Market-based financial conditions are broadly accommodative and improving, but bank lending standards are tightening across C&I and CRE categories, creating a mixed but net-accommodative picture.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (looser) because overall conditions are described as "accommodative" and "improved further," despite some bank-level tightening.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 37 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities with maturities up to 10 years declined slightly over the period." "Credit conditions in municipal bond markets continued to be stable."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury markets functioning normally with orderly yield declines; no dysfunction indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are calm and orderly with no signs of dysfunction.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on 10-year corporate bond yields...for both triple-B-rated and speculative-grade issuers declined, on balance, but remained at levels near the high end of their ranges since 2012." "CMBS spreads narrowed markedly but remained elevated." Banks tightened C&I and CRE lending standards; banks "expected an increase this year in delinquencies and charge-offs" in energy sector.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spreads are narrowing but remain elevated relative to recent history, with tightening bank lending standards and energy-sector credit concerns adding to the picture of somewhat stressed credit conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads remain near the high end of their range since 2012 and banks are tightening standards.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes moved up, on net." "Valuation pressures rose somewhat. Term premiums remained very low, and CRE valuations were elevated." "Corporate earnings in the first quarter appeared to have decreased markedly."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equity prices rising with declining earnings and staff noting "valuation pressures rose somewhat" — suggests modest overvaluation concern rather than market stress.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (overvaluation concern direction) because staff explicitly flags rising valuation pressures alongside declining corporate earnings.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Leverage and maturity transformation in the financial sector were subdued relative to historical levels." "The financial system was fairly resilient." However, "banks expected an increase this year in delinquencies and charge-offs on existing loans to firms in the energy sector; banks also noted some deterioration in credit quality of loans to non-energy businesses."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Banking system broadly resilient with subdued leverage, but emerging pockets of concern around energy-sector credit quality and tightening lending standards.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the system is described as "fairly resilient" with "subdued" leverage, though minor energy-sector concerns push it above the lowest range.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 38 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar depreciated against most currencies, in part because higher oil prices supported the currencies of oil exporters." Staff also lowered "assumed paths for...the foreign exchange value of the dollar" in the outlook.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dollar depreciated over the intermeeting period, reversing some of its earlier strength, and the staff lowered its dollar path assumption.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated over the period. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and declining prices of non-energy imports." "Falling prices of non-energy imports and the pass-through of declines in energy prices to prices of other goods and services."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Goods prices are being restrained by declining energy and import prices, with pass-through effects dampening broader goods inflation.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not specifically discussed as a separate category.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Housing activity discussed but shelter/housing inflation not addressed.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance, in recent months, while market-based measures of inflation compensation were still low." "The reading from the Michigan survey in early April was at the low end of its historical range." Staff risks note "the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Expectations broadly anchored but with concerning downside signals — Michigan survey at low end of range, market-based measures still low, and staff flagging downside risk to expectations.
