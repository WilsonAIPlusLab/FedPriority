# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2015-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: April 2015 -- mid-recovery period. The economy had been improving since 2010 but Q1 2015 showed a notable slowdown (partly transitory). Unemployment at 5.5%, well down from crisis highs but still above pre-crisis lows. Dollar had appreciated substantially since mid-2014. Oil prices had collapsed. Inflation running well below 2% target. Fed had not yet begun liftoff (that would come in December 2015).

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. PCE prices only 1/4 percent higher than a year earlier. Core PCE at 1-1/4 percent -- well below 2% target. Energy price declines and import price declines restraining. Near-term forecast revised up slightly but medium-term still projected to "remain below" the 2% objective. This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 26-40 range ("below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent"). Core at 1.25% is meaningfully below target. Score ~32.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says real GDP "only edged up" in Q1, with growth held down by transitory factors. Staff projects GDP to "expand at a faster pace than potential output in 2015 and 2016" -- meaning the economy is currently BELOW potential and closing the gap. "Gradual reduction in resource slack" confirms slack remains. Unemployment projected to "decline slowly" toward natural rate. Bright-line: "resource slack" language means score MUST be < 50. The economy is recovering but still below potential. Industrial production fell. Score ~40 (below potential, recovering but gap persists).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.5%, "unchanged" over the intermeeting period. Job openings "well above pre-recession level." Payroll employment expanded at "solid pace" on average but March weaker. Hiring and quits "slightly above their levels of a year ago." The unemployment rate of 5.5% places this in the 31-45 range ("below full employment, slack remains, UE 5.5-7%"). However, 5.5% is at the boundary -- job openings strong. Score ~43.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Real PCE increased but "at a much slower pace than in Q4 2014." Real disposable income "rose strongly." Consumer sentiment near its highest level since pre-recession. BUT: residential investment "increased at a slow pace," housing indicators "remained weak." Business equipment spending rose "modestly," and forward-looking indicators "consistent with only small further gains." Nonresidential structures "fell considerably." Government purchases "moved down." Industrial production fell. Net: below trend but not dire. Score ~38 (below trend, sluggish, soft).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "Financial conditions eased, on balance, over the intermeeting period." Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms "remained accommodative." Corporate bond issuance "strong." C&I loans "expanded briskly." CRE financing "broadly available." Consumer credit "generally accommodative." Bright-line: "accommodative" / "eased" means score MUST be < 50. Multiple sectors described as accommodative. Score ~28 (loose; accommodative conditions; credit freely available).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields decreased over the period. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. Normal yield movements driven by policy expectations. The staff noted "changes in the structure of some fixed-income markets that could increase volatility" -- a mild forward-looking concern but not current stress. Score ~35 (normal, calm markets consistent with 2010-15 anchor of 25-35). Score ~33.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Speculative-grade corporate bond spreads "narrowed." Corporate bond issuance "strong." Leveraged loan issuance "stayed robust." CMBS issuance "continued to be robust." Auto and student loan balances "expanded robustly." This is clearly below-normal spread territory -- ample credit availability. Score ~28 (below normal, tight spreads, ample credit availability).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff says equity prices "rose somewhat" and volatility "declined." The financial stability section notes "valuations remained stretched for some asset classes," expected real return on equities "moved down" reflecting higher prices and lower earnings forecasts. This is mild overvaluation concern language -- "stretched" but not "bubble." Score ~30 (elevated valuations, "stretched").

**BANKING_STRESS**: Leverage in the banking system "remained relatively low." Household debt increases "modest" and "associated primarily with borrowers with strong credit scores." No mention of bank failures, NPL increases, or capital concerns. Bright-line: low leverage = healthy = score MUST be < 50. Score ~28 (very healthy, strong capital).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Dollar "depreciated during the intermeeting period" due to weaker data and lower rate expectations. BUT: "the cumulative appreciation of the dollar since mid-2014 remained substantial." Bright-line: depreciation over the period suggests < 50, but the overall level is described as substantially appreciated. The intermeeting movement was depreciation, but the staff notes the still-substantial cumulative appreciation. The staff outlook also notes that the "projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar was a little lower." Net: the dollar remains strong in level but weakened over the period. The 2015 H1 anchor is 60-70. Score ~58 (still notably strong in level despite intermeeting depreciation; net exports were "a substantial drag").

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Non-energy import prices declining. Energy prices had declined substantially (now reversing slightly). Goods inflation clearly depressed. Score ~25.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out from core. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing: "House prices continued to rise moderately." Rents/shelter not specifically discussed as an inflation component. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "stable" -- Michigan survey "still in the range seen over recent years," Primary Dealers "unchanged." Market-based measures "increased somewhat but were still low." Longer-run expectations "assumed to be consistent with the Committee's objective." This is anchored but perhaps slightly below target on the market-based side. Score ~44 (anchored but market-based measures still low).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer prices...were only 1/4 percent higher than a year earlier." "The core PCE price index...increased 1-1/4 percent over the same four-quarter period, partly restrained by the declines in prices of non-energy imported goods." Staff forecast: "inflation in 2016 and 2017 projected to move closer to, but remain below, the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation running well below the 2% target at 1.25% core, with energy and import price drags, and projects inflation to remain below target through the medium term.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because headline PCE is only 0.25% y/y and core is 1.25%, both substantially below the 2% objective.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real gross domestic product (GDP) only edged up in the first quarter, with growth likely held down, in part, by transitory factors." Staff outlook: "The expansion in economic activity over the medium term was expected to lead to a gradual reduction in resource slack."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly below potential with resource slack remaining, though the staff projects growth above potential going forward to close the gap gradually.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "resource slack" and projects its "gradual reduction" -- the gap has not yet closed. Bright-line satisfied: slack remains -> < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate remained at 5.5 percent in March." "The rate of job openings edged up in February and was well above its pre-recession level." "The unemployment rate was projected to decline slowly and to move a little below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate for a time."
**Center-of-gravity**: At 5.5% unemployment with improving but not yet tight conditions -- job openings are strong but hiring and quits only "slightly above" year-ago levels, and the participation rate edged down.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because unemployment at 5.5% is above the staff's estimated natural rate, with slack still being absorbed.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) increased in the first quarter, albeit at a much slower pace than in the fourth quarter of 2014." "Real spending for nonresidential structures fell considerably in the first quarter, as outlays for drilling and mining structures dropped sharply." "Real private expenditures on business equipment and intellectual property products rose modestly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending slowed materially, business investment was mixed (equipment modestly up, structures down sharply), residential investment was slow, and government purchases fell. Net picture is below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending and investment across nearly all categories showed weakness or only modest gains in Q1 2015.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financial conditions eased, on balance, over the intermeeting period." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained accommodative. Corporate bond issuance was strong in the first quarter." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets stayed generally accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes conditions as accommodative across multiple credit markets -- corporate, CRE, consumer, and residential -- with conditions easing further over the intermeeting period.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff explicitly uses "eased" and "accommodative" across multiple sectors. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 33 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "5- and 10-year nominal Treasury yields decreased" over the intermeeting period. "The staff also noted changes in the structure of some fixed-income markets that could increase volatility."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally with orderly yield declines. The only note of concern is forward-looking about structural changes that "could increase volatility" -- not current dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no indication of market dysfunction; yield movements were orderly and driven by policy expectations.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on 10-year speculative-grade corporate bonds over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed." "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' books again expanded briskly." "Issuance of collateralized loan obligations" remained strong; CRE loans "increased appreciably."
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads narrowed further from already-accommodative levels, with robust issuance across corporate bonds, leveraged loans, CMBS, and bank lending.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (compressed/accommodative) because spreads narrowed and credit availability was ample. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 30 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Some indicators suggested that valuations remained stretched for some asset classes. An estimate of the expected real return on equities moved down, reflecting an increase in stock prices and downward revisions to forecasts of corporate earnings."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff expresses mild overvaluation concern -- valuations "stretched," expected returns declining -- but stops short of "bubble" or "frothy" language.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (overvaluation concern territory, where lower scores = more overvaluation concern). Score of 30 reflects elevated valuations / stretched conditions.

### BANKING_STRESS: 28 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Leverage in the banking system remained relatively low, and increases in household debt stayed modest and continued to be associated primarily with borrowers with strong credit scores."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system described as healthy with low leverage and prudent lending -- low stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because "relatively low" leverage and sound lending practices indicate a healthy system. Bright-line satisfied: healthy = < 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 45 +-10 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The U.S. dollar depreciated during the intermeeting period, as U.S. macroeconomic data generally came in weaker than expected." "Nonetheless, the cumulative appreciation of the dollar since mid-2014 remained substantial."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar weakened over the intermeeting period (bright-line: depreciation -> < 50), but the overall level remains substantially appreciated from mid-2014. Score reflects the intermeeting depreciation per the bright-line rule while the wide uncertainty band captures the still-elevated level.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly below neutral for the intermeeting period per bright-line rule (depreciation -> < 50), though the level remains elevated.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 25 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent, partly restrained by earlier declines in energy prices along with further decreases in non-energy import prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Energy prices declining and import goods prices declining -- clear downward pressure on goods prices.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific breakout of services inflation in staff discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: "House prices continued to rise moderately" but shelter/rent inflation not specifically discussed as an inflation component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 44 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of expected long-run inflation were stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation at longer horizons increased somewhat but were still low."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey measures anchored and stable, but market-based measures "still low" despite recent increase -- slightly below the well-anchored-at-target midpoint.
