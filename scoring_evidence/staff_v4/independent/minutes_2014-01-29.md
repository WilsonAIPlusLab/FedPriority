# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2014-01-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). Staff content is clearly separated into "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. I will score only staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
January 2014: The U.S. economy is in mid-recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment has come down from its peak but remains elevated at 6.7%. Inflation is running well below the 2% target. The Fed has just begun tapering asset purchases (December 2013). Emerging market turbulence is beginning. This is a period of moderate growth with persistent slack and below-target inflation.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as clearly BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation was "a little under 1 percent" over 12 months, "well below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Core PCE "increased slightly more than 1 percent." Staff forecasts inflation to "run well below the Committee's 2 percent objective early this year." Inflation expectations "remained stable." This is clearly below-target inflation — in the 26-40 range. The language "well below" pushes toward the lower end of that range. Score: ~30.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. The staff projects "real GDP would rise faster than potential output" implying output is currently below potential. The staff anticipates "a slow reduction in resource slack over the projection period." Unemployment expected to reach the natural rate only in 2016. GDP growth "picked up in the second half of 2013" but slack remains. Bright-line check: "resource slack" / below potential language -> score MUST be < 50. Given ongoing recovery with growth accelerating but slack persisting, this is in the 36-42 range. Score: ~38.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees the labor market as BELOW full employment. Unemployment at 6.7% is still "elevated." Long-duration unemployment declined but "remained elevated." Part-time for economic reasons "remained elevated." Gross hiring "still low." However, there are signs of gradual improvement: job openings edging up, hard-to-fill positions trending up, claims moving down. With UE at 6.7% (well above NAIRU ~5%), and natural rate not expected until 2016, this is clearly slack territory. The rubric says UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45. At 6.7% with multiple indicators still elevated, Score: ~35.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees spending as modestly positive but mixed. Real PCE "rose at a faster pace in October and November than in the third quarter." Retail sales "increased strongly." Business equipment spending "appeared to strengthen." Manufacturing production "increased at a robust pace." Housing sector showed "tentative signs of stabilizing." However, real disposable income was "little changed," and federal government purchases "fell sharply." On net, private spending is picking up but not yet strong. This is below-trend to approaching-trend territory. Score: ~45.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
The staff "revised downward its view of the pace at which potential output had increased over recent years and would increase this year and next." This is an indirect reference to potential output growth, which includes productivity. A downward revision to potential output growth suggests weaker productivity assumptions. However, this is only one brief mention. Score: ~35, Low confidence.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE / supportive. "Financial conditions in the United States remained supportive of growth in economic activity and employment." Equity prices increased, longer-term rates declined. Banks "continued to ease their lending standards." Credit "remained widely available." Corporate bond spreads narrowed. CMBS spreads "continued to be tight." Bright-line check: "supportive" / "accommodative" -> score MUST be < 50. This is clearly loose/accommodative. Score: ~28.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: Treasury yields "declined about 10 basis points and 20 basis points" due to safe-haven demand and reduced uncertainty. No mention of any market dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "generally remained stable." Year-end pressures were "modest." This is normal-to-calm functioning. Score: ~30.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Staff sees credit conditions as very favorable. Corporate bond spreads "continued to narrow," with investment-grade "reaching their lowest levels in several years" and speculative-grade "approaching pre-crisis levels." CMBS spreads "continued to be tight." CDS spreads for banks "narrowed somewhat." Bright-line check: "narrow" / "tight" / "lowest levels" -> score MUST be < 50. This is well below normal — compressed spreads. Score: ~18.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Equities "edged higher, on net." Bank stock prices "rose notably." No valuation commentary from staff — just price movements described neutrally. Default to ~48. Score: ~48.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Direction: Staff sees banks as healthy/improving. Banks "continued to ease their lending standards." Demand increased. CDS spreads narrowed. Stock prices of bank holding companies "rose notably." More upgrades than downgrades for municipals (though that's not directly banks). Lending expanded. Bright-line check: easing standards, improved conditions -> low stress -> MUST be < 50. Score: ~30.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated against most other currencies over the period, with particularly large increases against the Argentine peso and the Turkish lira." Staff also notes dollar appreciation is a factor in the outlook, as the staff forecast was partly offset by "a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Bright-line check: appreciation -> score MUST be > 50. The appreciation was broad-based and notably large against some EM currencies. Score: ~60.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: "Consumer energy prices declined." Core prices low. Commodity prices muted. This suggests below-neutral goods inflation. Score: ~28.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices continued to increase in November, although not as quickly as earlier in 2013." Housing starts mixed. This is about house prices, not shelter CPI directly. The mention is limited. N/A (shelter inflation not specifically discussed as a component).

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." Michigan survey "little changed." TIPS-based 5-year "increased a little" while 5-to-10 year "decreased somewhat." This is well-anchored at or near target. Score: ~47.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35)**: Both below 50, both indicating slack. Difference of 3 points — coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45)**: Difference of 7 points. Spending is picking up but the output gap remains. This is consistent with an economy that has momentum but hasn't closed the gap yet. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (18)**: Both well below 50, indicating loose/favorable conditions. Coherent — conditions are accommodative and spreads are compressed.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (28) vs TREASURY_STRESS (30)**: Both below midpoint. No crisis, calm markets with supportive conditions. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~28, expectations ~47. The headline inflation of ~1% PCE is the dominant factor; expectations are anchored but near target. The headline score of 30 is consistent with well-below-target actual inflation tempered by anchored expectations.

### Bright-Line Verification

- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "supportive" -> score 28 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "narrowed," "lowest levels," "tight" -> score 18 < 50. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: easing standards, healthy indicators -> score 30 < 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "appreciated" -> score 60 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction described, yields declined normally -> score 30 (normal-to-calm). PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Total U.S. consumer price inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, was a little under 1 percent over the 12 months ending in November, well below the Committee's 2 percent longer-term objective." "Core PCE prices... increased slightly more than 1 percent."
- Center-of-gravity: Inflation running at roughly half the target, with staff forecasting it to remain "well below" the objective near-term.
- Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because actual inflation is roughly 1%, well below the 2% target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to lead to a slow reduction in resource slack over the projection period, and the unemployment rate was expected to decline gradually, reaching the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate in 2016."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy growing above potential but still well below potential output level, with meaningful slack expected to persist for two more years.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies "resource slack" that will only slowly diminish. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate declined to 6.7 percent in December." "The rate of long-duration unemployment declined, but the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was little changed, and both measures remained elevated."
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving but still with significant slack — elevated unemployment, elevated underemployment, low hiring rates. Natural rate not expected for two more years.
- Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 6.7% is well above NAIRU and multiple slack indicators remain elevated.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) rose at a faster pace in October and November than in the third quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to strengthen in the fourth quarter."
- Center-of-gravity: Private spending is accelerating and manufacturing is robust, but housing is only stabilizing, government spending is declining, and income growth is flat. Net picture is modestly below trend.
- Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral — spending is improving but restrained by multiple headwinds.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "The staff revised downward its view of the pace at which potential output had increased over recent years and would increase this year and next."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees potential output growth as weaker than previously thought, implying disappointing productivity trends.
- Direction confirmation: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because staff revised potential output growth downward.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financial conditions in the United States remained supportive of growth in economic activity and employment." "Domestic banks continued to ease their lending standards and some loan terms on balance."
- Center-of-gravity: Conditions are clearly accommodative — equity prices up, rates down, lending standards easing, credit widely available. Bright-line rule satisfied (supportive -> below 50).
- Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well below neutral (i.e., loose) because staff explicitly describes conditions as "supportive" with broad evidence of easy credit.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "10-and 30-year nominal Treasury yields declined about 10 basis points and 20 basis points, respectively." "Conditions in short-term dollar funding markets generally remained stable. Year-end funding pressures were modest."
- Center-of-gravity: Treasury markets functioning normally with orderly yield movements and no signs of dysfunction. Safe-haven flows were orderly.
- Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because no market dysfunction is described; yield changes reflect normal market dynamics.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Corporate bond spreads continued to narrow over the intermeeting period, with investment-grade bond spreads reaching their lowest levels in several years and those on speculative-grade corporate bonds approaching pre-crisis levels."
- Center-of-gravity: Spreads are at multi-year lows across the board — investment grade, speculative grade, CMBS, and CDS. This is clearly compressed/tight territory.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are described as at their "lowest levels in several years" and "approaching pre-crisis levels." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 48 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes edged higher, on net, over the intermeeting period." "Stock prices of bank holding companies rose notably."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff describes price movements but offers no valuation assessment. Default to near-neutral.
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is approximately neutral — no concern expressed about valuation levels.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Domestic banks continued to ease their lending standards and some loan terms on balance; they also experienced an increase in demand, on net, in most major loan categories." "Spreads on credit default swaps for the largest bank holding companies narrowed somewhat."
- Center-of-gravity: Banks are easing standards, demand is rising, CDS spreads narrowing, and stock prices rising. This indicates a healthy and improving banking system — low stress.
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because easing standards and narrowing CDS spreads indicate low stress. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated against most other currencies over the period, with particularly large increases against the Argentine peso and the Turkish lira." Staff forecast offset by "a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
- Center-of-gravity: Broad-based dollar appreciation, notably large against EM currencies, significant enough that staff incorporated it into the GDP forecast as a headwind.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated broadly. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 28 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer energy prices declined, consumer food prices rose modestly." "Changes in commodity and import prices expected to be muted."
- Center-of-gravity: Energy prices declining, food only modestly higher, commodity prices muted — goods inflation is clearly below target.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
