# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2015-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: March 2015. The U.S. was in the middle of a gradual recovery from the Great Recession, with the unemployment rate at 5.5%, inflation well below 2% due to energy price declines, and the FOMC preparing for the first rate hike ("liftoff") later in 2015. The dollar was appreciating notably as other central banks eased while the Fed anticipated tightening.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as well BELOW the midpoint. PCE price index edged up only 1/4 percent over 12 months, core PCE at 1-1/4 percent — significantly below the 2% target. Energy prices drove headline down. Market-based inflation compensation "still low." Staff projects inflation to "remain below" 2% through 2016-2017. Wage measures at 2-2.25% — modest. This is clearly in the 26-40 range ("below target, running below 2 percent").

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy BELOW potential. Key language: "the expansion in economic activity over the medium term was anticipated to gradually reduce resource slack" — meaning slack still exists. GDP growth moderated in Q1. Staff forecast GDP to expand "at a faster pace than potential output" (closing the gap from below). Unemployment expected to "decline slowly." Bright-line check: "resource slack" language → MUST be < 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market improving but still with some slack. Unemployment at 5.5% — getting close to NAIRU but not yet there. Payroll employment "continued to expand strongly." Job openings at "elevated level." But staff projects unemployment to "decline slowly" toward natural rate, implying still above it. The LFPR and EPOP rose only "slightly." With UE at 5.5%, the rubric anchor suggests 31-45 range. The strong payroll growth and elevated openings push toward the upper end.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending "appeared to decelerate somewhat" in Q1 after "rising markedly" in Q4. Retail sales declined slightly, housing sector "remained slow." Business equipment spending expanding at "about the same modest pace." However, fundamentals for future spending are positive (low energy prices, rising income, strong sentiment). Net assessment: below trend currently, with positive forward indicators. Score in the 40-45 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes conditions as generally accommodative to mixed. Credit for large nonfinancial firms "remained generally accommodative." Corporate bond issuance increased. C&I loans "continued to expand strongly." CRE financing "stayed broadly available." CMBS issuance "still robust" and "expected to be the strongest since the financial crisis." Consumer credit "rose further." However, mortgage credit "remained tight for riskier borrowers." Overall: accommodative conditions. Bright-line: "accommodative" → MUST be < 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields increased across the maturity spectrum, yield curve steepened. But this is described as normal price adjustment to data and policy expectations — no mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. Per the bright-line rule, yield movements alone are NOT dysfunction. Score in the 36-45 range (normal functioning).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on BBB and speculative-grade bonds "narrowed notably" — reflecting "increased appetite for riskier investments." Tightening was "broad based." CMBS spreads "continued to be low." Leveraged loan spreads were "elevated" but overall credit conditions favorable. Bright-line: narrowing/tight spreads → MUST be < 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equity prices "moved up, on balance" and implied volatility "moved down." No staff valuation commentary — just price direction. Default to 45-55 per the rubric. Slight positive price movement suggests no concern → ~47.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not specifically discussed as a topic. C&I loans expanding strongly, CRE loans growing solidly — these suggest healthy bank lending. CMBS issuance strongest since crisis. No mention of bank stress, NPLs, capital concerns. Bright-line: healthy lending activity → below 50. But discussion is indirect, so N/A is also defensible. I'll score it based on the indirect evidence of healthy lending.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated against most other currencies over the intermeeting period" due to policy divergence. Staff economic outlook also notes "a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a factor reducing the GDP growth forecast. This is clear appreciation. Bright-line: appreciation → MUST be > 50. Anchor: 2015 H1 sharp appreciation → 60-70.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices "declined significantly." Non-oil import prices expected to "bottom out." PCE headline at 1/4% driven by energy. Goods price pressure is clearly very low.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out from core. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing sector "remained slow" but this is activity, not prices. Home values "advanced further" is mentioned in the consumption context. Not enough on shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "remained stable." Market-based measures "still low." Staff assumes longer-run expectations "consistent with the Committee's objective." Survey stable = anchored at target. Market-based low = slightly below. Score around 43-47.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total U.S. consumer prices, as measured by the PCE price index, edged up only ¼ percent over the 12 months ending in January, as energy prices declined significantly. The core PCE price index...rose 1¼ percent over the same 12-month period." Staff projects inflation to "remain below, the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below 2% target on both headline and core, with energy declines dominating headline and limited wage pressure.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because both headline (0.25%) and core (1.25%) PCE are well below the 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 42 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity over the medium term was anticipated to gradually reduce resource slack." "Projected real GDP growth in the first half of this year was lower than in the forecast prepared for the January meeting." Staff forecasts GDP growth "at a faster pace than potential output" — meaning economy is still below potential.
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing but still below potential with resource slack that is expected to close gradually.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references remaining "resource slack." Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 5.5 percent in February." "Nonfarm payroll employment continued to expand strongly." "The rate of private-sector job openings moved up in January and was at an elevated level." Staff expects unemployment to "decline slowly" toward natural rate.
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving with strong job gains and 5.5% UE, but still above the natural rate with some remaining slack (participation only slightly up, part-time for economic reasons only edging down).
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 5.5% is still above the staff's estimate of the natural rate, with slack still being absorbed.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to decelerate somewhat going into the first quarter after rising markedly in the fourth quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to be expanding...at about the same modest pace as in the previous quarter." Housing sector "remained slow."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending decelerating from strong Q4, business investment at a modest pace, housing slow — overall below trend with positive forward indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending decelerated and investment was only at a "modest pace," with housing slow.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Credit conditions faced by large nonfinancial firms remained generally accommodative." "CRE financing stayed broadly available." "Issuance in the first quarter of 2015 was expected to be the strongest since the financial crisis." CMBS spreads "continued to be low."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based accommodative conditions across corporate, CRE, and consumer credit, with strong issuance volumes. Mortgage credit tight only for riskier borrowers.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative" and "broadly available." Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities increased across the maturity spectrum, and the Treasury yield curve steepened." No mention of liquidity problems, auction stress, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with orderly yield adjustments reflecting changing policy expectations.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because yield movements reflect normal market adjustment, not dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of 10-year corporate bond yields over those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities for both BBB-rated and speculative-grade issuers narrowed notably, likely reflecting increased appetite for riskier investments." CMBS spreads "continued to be low."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads narrowing across the board, with strong risk appetite and robust issuance — well below normal stress levels.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads "narrowed notably" and are described as "low." Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 ±8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes moved up, on balance, over the intermeeting period, and one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index moved down on net."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose modestly with declining volatility. No staff valuation commentary. Default to near-neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because staff reports price movement without valuation assessment.

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' books continued to expand strongly." "Growth of CRE loans on banks' books remained solid." CMBS issuance "expected to be the strongest since the financial crisis."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system appears healthy with strong lending growth, but no direct staff discussion of bank capital or health.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because strong lending activity and robust issuance indicate a healthy banking system. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated against most other currencies over the intermeeting period." Staff lowered GDP growth forecast "mostly because of the effects of a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based dollar appreciation driven by policy divergence, significant enough to materially affect the staff's GDP forecast.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "appreciated against most other currencies" and this appreciation was significant enough to lower the GDP forecast. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Energy prices declined significantly." PCE headline at only 0.25% over 12 months. Non-oil import prices expected to "bottom out."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices deeply depressed by energy declines, with broader goods disinflation from import prices.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not specifically broken out in staff discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Shelter/rent inflation not specifically discussed in the staff sections.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of expected long-run inflation from a variety of surveys...remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation were still low."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations stable (anchored near target), but market-based measures running low, pulling slightly below the midpoint.
