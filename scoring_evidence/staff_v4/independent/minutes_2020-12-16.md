# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2020-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: December 2020 — roughly 9 months after the COVID-19 pandemic shock. The economy is in recovery but still well below pre-pandemic levels. Vaccines have just been announced but not yet widely deployed. The pandemic is resurging. Unemployment is 6.7%, inflation is running well below 2%, and the Fed funds rate is at the effective lower bound.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as notably below midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.2%, core PCE at 1.4%, trimmed mean at 1.7%. Staff attributes weakness to "relatively weak aggregate demand" and earlier energy price declines. Inflation projected to "finish the year at a relatively subdued level, reflecting substantial margins of labor- and product-market slack." This is clearly below target — in the 26-35 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy well below potential. "The level of real GDP remained well below its level at the start of 2020." Staff projects growth "well above the rate of potential output growth" in medium term, implying economy is still far below potential. Staff describes "substantial margins of labor- and product-market slack." Recovery is occurring but economy is well below potential. Bright-line check: "below" language present, score must be < 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very loose. Unemployment at 6.7%. Employment "continued to be well below its level at the beginning of the year." Payroll gains are "more moderate" than Q3. Only "somewhat more than half of the losses" recovered. Wage increases are explicitly not "indicative of tight labor market conditions" — compositional effects. Initial claims "only slightly lower." This is clearly loose labor market — score in 25-32 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Real PCE "rose strongly in October, though at a more moderate pace than in the third quarter." But November data "stepped down, possibly reflecting... renewed social-distancing measures and concerns about the resurgent pandemic." Business fixed investment "appeared to be expanding further." Industrial production "rose strongly" but "still below its February pre-pandemic level." Housing "advanced further." Government purchases "declining moderately." Net picture: recovering but below pre-pandemic levels and slowing. Score around 40-45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes a split: "Financing conditions for businesses able to access capital markets and households possessing high credit scores remained accommodative and eased a bit further in some sectors, but conditions for borrowers dependent on bank financing remained tight." Overall: "Financing conditions in capital markets continued to be broadly accommodative, supported by low interest rates and high equity valuations." Short-term funding "remained stable." Mortgage conditions "highly accommodative" for conforming borrowers. Small business conditions "remained tight." Net: predominantly accommodative with pockets of tightness. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language dominant, score must be < 50. Score around 30-35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No dysfunction described. Yields rose "moderately" driven by "greater optimism about the economic outlook." Option-implied volatility on 10-year Treasury futures "declined to historic lows." This is explicitly orderly, normal market functioning. Score around 25-30.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields narrowed markedly across the credit spectrum." Agency CMBS spreads "remained narrow." Non-agency CMBS spreads "ticked down." CP spreads "remained around pre-pandemic levels." Some elevated levels for lower-quality borrowers (leveraged loan defaults "at somewhat elevated levels," triple-B non-agency CMBS "remained elevated relative to pre-pandemic levels"). Net: narrowing/below normal with some residual pandemic-era elevation. Score around 32-38.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased over the intermeeting period." "High equity valuations" mentioned as supporting financing conditions. VIX declined. Staff mentions "high equity valuations" without expressing concern — more as a factual description supporting accommodative conditions. No "stretched" or "bubble" language. Score around 35-40 (modestly elevated valuations, no alarm).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed as a standalone topic. C&I loans contracting. Small business lending standards tightened. CRE bank loan growth "remained weak, consistent with tightened bank lending standards." Some tightness in bank lending, but no discussion of bank health, capital adequacy, or systemic concerns. This suggests mild stress from the lending standards side. Score around 48-52. Bright-line check: no "well-capitalized" or "stressed" language, so neutral zone is appropriate.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Broad dollar depreciation" and "the dollar depreciated against most currencies." Bright-line check: depreciation = score must be < 50. Score around 25-30.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not separately broken out (housing activity discussed but not housing inflation specifically). N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "edged up, though each remained within the range in which it has fluctuated in recent years." TIPS-based inflation compensation "rose above their pre-pandemic levels." This suggests expectations are anchored but edging up from recent lows — roughly at or slightly below target. Score around 45-48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (30) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28)**: Both well below 50, consistent with slack economy. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (30) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42)**: Gap of 12 points. C/I is higher because spending is recovering (strongly in some sectors) even though GDP level remains well below potential. Reasonable divergence — activity flow vs level gap.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (35)**: Both below 50, indicating accommodative/narrow. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs TREASURY_STRESS (27)**: Both low, consistent in non-crisis period. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (28) vs INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Only expectations scored (46). Parent is lower because actual inflation readings are well below target while expectations are roughly anchored. Consistent — expectations anchored while realized inflation is low.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 1.2 percent over the 12 months ending in October, and it continued to be held down by relatively weak aggregate demand." "Core PCE price inflation... was 1.4 percent." Staff projects inflation to "finish the year at a relatively subdued level, reflecting substantial margins of labor- and product-market slack."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees inflation running well below the 2% target, held down by slack and weak demand. Multiple data points confirm.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because realized inflation is substantially below target at 1.2-1.4% and staff characterizes it as "subdued."

### OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The level of real GDP remained well below its level at the start of 2020." "Substantial margins of labor- and product-market slack in the economy." Staff projects medium-term growth "well above the rate of potential output growth," confirming economy is currently well below potential.
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is recovering but remains well below potential with substantial slack.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies "substantial" slack and GDP "well below" prior level. Bright-line satisfied: below potential = score < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate moved down further and stood at 6.7 percent in November." "Employment continued to be well below its level at the beginning of the year." "Outsized increases in these average measures of earnings and compensation that were not indicative of tight labor market conditions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 6.7% with large remaining job losses; staff explicitly flags that wage data do not indicate tightness. Significant slack remains.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is elevated at 6.7% with only half of pandemic losses recovered.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE rose strongly in October, though at a more moderate pace than in the third quarter." "The components of the nominal retail sales data... along with the rate of light motor vehicle sales, stepped down." "Business fixed investment appeared to be expanding further." "Industrial production rose strongly over October and November... but production was still below its February pre-pandemic level."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are recovering with strength in some sectors, but levels remain below pre-pandemic, and November data show deceleration from the pandemic resurgence. Below trend overall but not collapsing.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because despite recovery momentum, levels remain below pre-pandemic and recent data show slowing.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions in capital markets continued to be broadly accommodative, supported by low interest rates and high equity valuations." "Financing conditions for businesses able to access capital markets and households possessing high credit scores remained accommodative and eased a bit further in some sectors, but conditions for borrowers dependent on bank financing remained tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: Predominantly accommodative conditions with low rates, strong issuance, and narrow spreads, offset somewhat by tight conditions for small businesses and bank-dependent borrowers.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly uses "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative" = score < 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 27 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "10- and 30-year yields rose moderately... Market participants attributed the increases in longer-term yields primarily to greater optimism about the economic outlook." "Near-dated option-implied volatility on the 10-year Treasury futures contract declined to historic lows."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning well with orderly yield movements driven by fundamentals. Volatility at historic lows. No dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because yield moves are driven by improving outlook, not dysfunction, and volatility is at historic lows.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields narrowed markedly across the credit spectrum, most notably for debt securities of the lowest credit quality firms." "Market indicators of future default expectations for corporate bonds fell slightly but remained above their pre-pandemic levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads have narrowed markedly and conditions are accommodative, though some residual elevation from pandemic remains in lower-quality segments.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and conditions are "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied: "narrowed" = score < 50.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 35 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes increased over the intermeeting period, led by steep stock price gains in cyclical sectors." Staff references "high equity valuations" as supporting accommodative financing conditions.
**Center-of-gravity**: Equity prices rising with declining volatility. Staff notes "high equity valuations" but without alarm — descriptive rather than cautionary. Suggests modestly elevated valuations.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (low stress) because equities are rising, valuations are high (suggesting no distress), and VIX is declining. "High valuations" = low equity stress.

### BANKING_STRESS: 50 +-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Conditions for borrowers dependent on bank financing remained tight." "Small business loan originations ticked up in October to a level near that seen in the same period last year." "CRE bank loan growth in October and November remained weak, consistent with tightened bank lending standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Bank lending standards are tight and loan growth is weak, but no discussion of bank health, capital, or systemic concerns. The stress is on the lending channel rather than bank solvency.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is at neutral — tightened lending standards suggest some stress on the lending side, but absence of discussion about bank health/capital makes this ambiguous.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 25 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad dollar depreciation." "The dollar depreciated against most currencies."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear, broad-based dollar weakness over the intermeeting period.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because staff explicitly reports depreciation. Bright-line satisfied: depreciation = score < 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
Not separately broken out in staff discussion.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately broken out in staff discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Housing activity discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 46 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The latest readings on survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations edged up, though each remained within the range in which it has fluctuated in recent years." "The 5-year and 5-to-10-year measures of inflation compensation based on TIPS rose above their pre-pandemic levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are anchored, edging up but within normal ranges. Market-based measures recovering to pre-pandemic levels, suggesting roughly at-target anchoring.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is slightly below neutral — expectations anchored within recent ranges, not elevated, but edging up from lows. Consistent with near-target anchoring.
