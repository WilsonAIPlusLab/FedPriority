### Era Context
Late 1997 -- the U.S. is in the midst of a strong expansion with tight labor markets, low inflation, rising productivity, and the Asian financial crisis is unfolding. Unemployment is at 4.6%, well below estimates of NAIRU at the time. The fed funds rate is at 5.5%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "Price inflation remained subdued despite tight labor markets and some pickup in the rate of wage increases" (line 54-56). "Consumer price inflation had remained at a low level" (line 141). CPI and core CPI "increased appreciably less than in the year-earlier period" (lines 145-147). Producer prices "edged lower in November and the index was down somewhat on balance over the past year" (lines 147-149). There IS a note on wage increases picking up, but the overall tone is that price inflation is contained and declining. This suggests below-target inflation -- around 30-38 range given "subdued," "low level," and declining producer prices, but with some wage pressure noted.
- Self-check: "Subdued" / "low level" = 5-10 points below midpoint. "Appreciably less than year-earlier" suggests meaningful disinflation. But wages are picking up -- this slightly moderates the low reading.
- Score direction: Below 50. Magnitude: "subdued" and "low level" with declining PPI = roughly 35.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees the economy as operating ABOVE potential. Evidence: "economic activity had continued to grow at a rapid pace" (line 48). "The large rise in production boosted the rate of utilization of manufacturing capacity to its highest level in more than two years" (lines 74-76). The staff forecast notes the need for "moderation" and mentions "reduced pressure on resources" (lines 214-217), implying current pressure on resources exists. The directive summary says "economic activity continued to grow rapidly" (line 432).
- Bright-line check: Capacity utilization at highest in 2+ years and "rapid pace" of growth with labor tightness suggests above potential. Score MUST be > 50.
- The staff forecast projects slowing but the current assessment is of rapid, above-trend growth. Late 90s boom anchor: 57-63.
- Score: 58.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as VERY TIGHT. Evidence: "tight labor markets" (line 55), unemployment fell to 4.6% -- "its low for the current expansion" (lines 64-65). Payroll employment "rose sharply further" (line 59), gains "widespread across sectors" (line 60). Manufacturing hours showed "a particularly large advance" (line 63).
- UE at 4.6% in 1997 was below most NAIRU estimates (typically 5-5.5% at that time). This is clearly tight.
- Anchor: 1999-2000 at 65-75. Late 1997 with UE at 4.6% is tight but not at the extremes of 1999-2000 (when UE dropped below 4%).
- Score: 65.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
