### Era Context
Mid-2017: The economy is in a mature expansion phase. The Fed raised rates in June 2017 to 1-1.25%. Unemployment is at 4.4%, near or slightly below estimates of the natural rate. Inflation has been persistently running below the 2% target despite tightening labor markets — the so-called "missing inflation" puzzle. Financial conditions remain broadly accommodative. The dollar had been weakening after its late-2016/early-2017 strength.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation is 1.5% (12-month), core PCE 1.5% — both well below the 2% target. CPI at 1.5%, core CPI at 1.75%. Staff revised down the forecast for 2017. The language says inflation "slowed again," "running below 2 percent." However, inflation expectations are "little changed on balance" and the staff views recent weakness as "transitory," projecting inflation to be "close to the Committee's longer-run objective in 2018 and at 2 percent in 2019." The current reading is clearly below target. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "running below 2 percent" with 1.5% readings = modestly below. Score range: 33-38.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. The staff outlook explicitly states: "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019" and "projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential." The unemployment rate is "projected to decline gradually...and to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language requires score > 50. The word "modestly" suggests not far above potential. Score range: 52-56.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as moderately tight. Unemployment at 4.4%, "labor market conditions continued to strengthen," payroll employment "expanded solidly," initial claims at "very low level." The unemployment rate is projected to run "below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Wage growth at 2.5% is moderate — "about the same as over the comparable period a year earlier." With UE at 4.4% and below the natural rate, this is tight but not extremely so. Score range: 53-58.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending as moderate/at trend. Real PCE "appeared to have rebounded in the second quarter" after modest Q1. Key factors "pointed to solid growth in total real PCE in the near term." Business equipment spending "increased moderately." However, residential investment "seemed to have declined," nonresidential structures investment "risen at a markedly slower pace," and C&I lending was "quite weak." Mixed picture nets to roughly at-trend. Score range: 48-53.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
No explicit discussion of productivity growth. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff explicitly states: "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households generally remained supportive of growth in spending." "Financing for large nonfinancial firms remained readily available." BRIGHT-LINE: "accommodative" / "supportive" requires score < 50. This is the neutral-era anchor period (2017), so "accommodative" here means somewhat below neutral tightness. The financial stability report notes "asset valuation pressures had edged up from notable to elevated." Score range: 32-38.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields "increased slightly." Short-term funding markets were "stable." There is no discussion of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. Yields remained "very low by historical standards." This is normal, calm functioning. Score range: 30-35.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds "narrowed a bit on net." "Risk spreads narrowed." BRIGHT-LINE: "narrowed" requires score < 50. CRE financing "remained accommodative" but some tightening in standards on CRE loans. Consumer credit: banks "tightened standards and widened spreads for credit card and auto loans." Mixed signals — corporate spreads narrow, some consumer/CRE tightening. Overall still favorable. Score range: 33-40.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff explicitly flags concern: "vulnerabilities associated with asset valuation pressures had edged up from notable to elevated, as asset prices remained high or climbed further." VIX "remained at historically low levels." Equity prices rose. This is staff expressing valuation concern — elevated valuations. Score range: 25-32 (more overvaluation concern = lower score on the "more stress" scale... wait, let me re-check polarity).

Polarity check: Higher EQUITY_VALUATION = MORE equity market stress/concern. The rubric says: 1-15 = "Extreme overvaluation concern; bubble; frothy." 16-30 = "Elevated valuations; stretched; rich by historical standards." Staff says asset valuation pressures are "elevated." This maps to the 16-30 range — elevated overvaluation concern. Score range: 25-32.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
No direct discussion of banking system health/capital. The SLOOS responses discuss lending standards (some tightening for CRE and consumer) but this is about credit conditions, not bank stress per se. The financial stability assessment mentions leverage vulnerabilities are "low." BRIGHT-LINE: low leverage = low stress. But banking not directly discussed as a separate topic. Closest: "vulnerabilities stemming from financial leverage... as low." Score: 30-35 (low stress, limited discussion).

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff explicitly states: "the broad dollar depreciated 2 percent." BRIGHT-LINE: depreciation requires score < 50. The depreciation was driven by foreign central bank hawkishness and weak US CPI. The staff also notes "lower assumed paths for the exchange value of the dollar" in the outlook. Score range: 30-36.
