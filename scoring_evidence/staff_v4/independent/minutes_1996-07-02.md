### Era Context
Mid-1996: The U.S. economy is in a mature expansion. Growth has been surprisingly strong, unemployment is around 5.5-5.6%, and core inflation has been moderate despite relatively high resource utilization. The Fed funds rate is at 5.25%. This is the mid-90s expansion period before the late-90s productivity boom became fully apparent.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as modestly above neutral. Evidence: "Upward pressures on food and energy prices had led to somewhat larger increases in the consumer price index over recent months." However, core inflation was well-behaved: "price increases for nonfood, non-energy items remained small" and "the increase in core consumer prices was appreciably smaller than in the previous twelve-month period." The staff forecast: "Inflation recently had been lifted by adverse developments in energy markets and was projected to remain above the levels of recent years, given the still-high level of resource utilization and the effects of tight grain supplies on food prices." Also: "average hourly earnings...had increased by a somewhat larger amount." Overall, headline inflation is being pushed up by food/energy, but core is contained. Staff sees modestly elevated inflation picture.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral — food/energy pushing up headline, core contained but wage pressures emerging.
- Magnitude: "somewhat larger increases" = modest, 5-10 pts from midpoint. Score ~55.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff reports some upward pressure. 55 is correct direction.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy operating at or slightly above potential. Evidence: "economic activity advanced considerably further in the second quarter" and "the still-high level of resource utilization." Staff forecast says growth "would moderate and the economy would expand around or perhaps a little above its estimated potential." Utilization of industrial capacity was "somewhat above the average rate." The staff projects the economy near potential going forward with "reduced rate of utilization of production capacity now prevailing."
- Bright-line check: "a little above its estimated potential" and "still-high level of resource utilization" → must be > 50. "perhaps a little above" = modestly above.
- Magnitude: "a little above" = 5 pts from midpoint. Score ~53.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slightly above. 53 is correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as moderately tight. Evidence: Unemployment rate at 5.6% (line 47-48). Nonfarm payroll employment "continued to expand briskly." The staff notes "still-high level of resource utilization." Average hourly earnings rising somewhat faster. UE at 5.6% in 1996 context — NAIRU was estimated around 5.5-6% at this time. The rubric says UE 5.5-7% → score 31-45, but that seems too low given the brisk employment growth and the staff describing "high resource utilization." The 5.6% rate was considered at or near full employment in mid-1996. Staff forecast notes "still-high level of resource utilization." Employment was "up substantially."
- With UE at 5.6% and brisk job growth in a period where NAIRU was thought to be near 5.5-6%, this is approximately at full employment.
- Score: ~50-52 (near NAIRU, balanced).
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. At approximately full employment with brisk job growth → 52.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees spending as above trend but moderating. Evidence: "Consumer spending continued to post sizable gains" — "real consumer spending on goods had risen substantially." But business investment "was rising less vigorously" and "Growth in business expenditures on durable equipment and nonresidential structures appeared to be slowing." Housing starts fell considerably. Staff forecast: "Consumer spending was projected to expand at a more moderate pace." "Business spending on equipment and structures was projected to grow less rapidly."
- Net position: Consumer spending strong (above trend), business investment decelerating from rapid pace but still positive, housing softening. Current quarter was strong but forward momentum is mixed.
- Score: ~57. Above trend currently with the surge in Q2, but moderation expected.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. "Sizable gains" in consumption + "slowing" in business investment nets to moderately above trend. 57 fits.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
No explicit staff discussion of productivity trends. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff describes conditions as roughly neutral to slightly loose. Evidence: "still-ample availability of credit" (staff forecast, line 185). Market interest rates "were up a little over the intermeeting period" — modest tightening. Fed funds at 5.25% with "the current stance of monetary policy could not be described in this view as clearly accommodative" (but this is participant language — must ignore). From staff content: credit is ample, rates rose modestly. The staff notes favorable cash-flow affordability for housing.
- "Still-ample availability of credit" suggests conditions are somewhat accommodative/loose.
- Bright-line: "ample availability of credit" → accommodative → must be < 50.
- Score: ~40. Somewhat accommodative.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Ample credit = loose conditions = lower score. 40 is correct.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: No discussion of Treasury market dysfunction. Yields moved modestly. "Most market interest rates have edged higher" — normal price adjustment.
- No evidence of dysfunction. Normal yield movements only.
- Score: N/A (no Treasury-specific dysfunction discussed).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Staff mentions "still-ample availability of credit" but no specific discussion of credit spreads, corporate bond markets, or spread levels.
- Very limited evidence. The "ample credit" language suggests spreads are not elevated.
- Score: ~35. Below normal stress given ample credit availability, but low confidence due to minimal direct discussion.
- Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress. Ample credit = low stress. 35 is correct direction.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Direction: Staff notes "Major indexes of stock prices were down on balance over the period." Staff forecast mentions "the favorable effect of higher equity prices on household wealth." No valuation commentary from staff.
- Equities declined modestly but no staff valuation concern expressed. Default to near-neutral.
- Score: ~50. Price decline mentioned without valuation commentary.
- Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress. Modest decline with no concern expressed → 50 neutral.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Direction: No staff discussion of banking system health or stress. N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: Staff reports "the trade-weighted value of the dollar in terms of the other G-10 currencies depreciated slightly over the intermeeting period." Dollar declined vs European currencies, rose vs yen.
- Bright-line: Dollar depreciation → must be < 50. "Slightly" depreciated = modest.
- Score: ~45. Slight depreciation.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dollar strength. Dollar weakened slightly. 45 is correct.

**Evidence**: "Upward pressures on food and energy prices had led to somewhat larger increases in the consumer price index over recent months." Staff forecast: "Inflation recently had been lifted by adverse developments in energy markets and was projected to remain above the levels of recent years, given the still-high level of resource utilization."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees headline inflation modestly elevated due to food/energy pressures and rising labor costs, though core inflation measures remain well-contained.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because food/energy push up headline and wage costs are accelerating modestly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "economic activity advanced considerably further in the second quarter." Staff forecast: "growth would moderate and the economy would expand around or perhaps a little above its estimated potential."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff views the economy as running slightly above potential in Q2 with moderation expected to bring it near potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says "a little above its estimated potential." Bright-line rule satisfied (above potential → > 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 52 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The civilian unemployment rate rose in May to 5.6 percent, which was the average rate for the year to date." "Nonfarm payroll employment continued to expand briskly over April and May." "still-high level of resource utilization."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market at approximately full employment (5.6% UE near estimated NAIRU) with brisk job growth, indicating near-balanced but slightly tight conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at/slightly above neutral because UE is near NAIRU and employment is expanding briskly.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]

But "business investment in equipment and structures apparently was rising less vigorously" and "Growth in business expenditures on durable equipment and nonresidential structures appeared to be slowing."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending running well above trend in Q2, while business investment decelerates from a rapid pace; net position is moderately above trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because consumer spending posted "sizable gains" and "substantial" increases despite some investment softening.
