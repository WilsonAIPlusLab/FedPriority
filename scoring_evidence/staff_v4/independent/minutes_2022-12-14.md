# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type:** Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context:** December 2022 — the Fed is deep into its aggressive tightening cycle. Inflation remains well above target but has begun to show signs of moderation. The labor market is very tight with unemployment at 3.7%. The economy is slowing but not yet in recession. The strong dollar has begun to reverse somewhat. Financial conditions have eased modestly over the intermeeting period.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:** Staff sees inflation as well above neutral. PCE at 6.0%, core PCE at 5.0%, core CPI at 6.0%. "Consumer price inflation remained elevated but had eased in recent months." Wage growth at 5.1% is "above the pace judged to be consistent with the FOMC's 2 percent inflation objective." Inflation is still very high but past its peak and moderating. The rubric anchor for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak is 82-88. By December 2022, inflation has stepped down notably from peak. Score should be in the 73-78 range — still "substantially above target" but clearly declining from peak.

**OUTPUT_GAP:** Staff says real GDP is "increasing at a modest pace" in Q4 after "expanding strongly" in Q3. The staff forecast says "output was expected to move below the staff's estimate of potential near the end of 2024." This implies output is currently ABOVE potential (since it needs to move below later). The staff also notes "a slower pace of decline in the natural rate of unemployment" and "job-matching efficiency was not improving as fast as previously anticipated." Growth is slowing but the economy is still above potential. Bright-line rule: above potential → must be > 50. Score ~55, reflecting modestly above potential but with momentum clearly decelerating.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:** Staff says labor market "eased somewhat" but "remained quite tight." Unemployment at 3.7% — well below natural rate. Wage growth elevated at 5.1%. Job openings "remained high." With UE at 3.7%, the rubric says score should almost certainly be above 55. "Quite tight" + 3.7% UE + elevated wage growth → score ~68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:** Mixed picture. "Real PCE growth appeared to have picked up in the fourth quarter." But "residential investment looked to be contracting sharply further" and "growth in business fixed investment seemed to be slowing markedly" with "tepid gains in equipment and intangibles" and "continued declines in nonresidential structures." Manufacturing output modest/declining. Net position: consumer spending solid but investment weak. Net to ~42-45.

**PRODUCTIVITY:** Staff mentions "slightly lowered its outlook for potential output" and discusses labor force participation and job-matching efficiency. There is no direct discussion of productivity growth levels. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:** Staff says "broad financial conditions were projected to be somewhat less restrictive than previously assumed." Borrowing costs "remained above levels observed at the end of the previous tightening cycle." Credit "continued to be generally available to businesses and households, but high borrowing costs appeared to weigh on financing volumes." Credit availability for small businesses "tightened further." "Persistently tight financial conditions" mentioned in the risk section. Bright-line check: "restrictive" / "tight" → must be > 50. Score ~62 — conditions are tight but eased somewhat over the intermeeting period.

**TREASURY_STRESS:** Treasury yields "declined substantially over the intermeeting period." Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. Yield movements are from policy/inflation expectations — normal functioning. Score ~40 (normal functioning, orderly).

**CREDIT_SPREADS:** "Spreads of interest rates on corporate debt, mortgage-backed securities, and municipal bonds to comparable-duration Treasury yields all narrowed over the intermeeting period." Credit quality "remained solid." Default rates "remained at very low levels." But borrowing costs still elevated, speculative-grade issuance "remained soft," and expected default probabilities had increased. Spreads are narrowing but still somewhat above normal. Score ~52 — roughly normal, slightly elevated.

**EQUITY_VALUATION:** "Broad stock price indexes increased" over the period. VIX "decreased notably and was around the middle of its range since mid-2020." Staff attributes the rise to "reduced concerns about the inflation outlook." No valuation commentary — just price direction. Per rubric, default to 45-55. Stock prices rose → less stress → score ~47.

**BANKING_STRESS:** Staff describes C&I loan delinquencies as "stayed flat," small business loan delinquencies "continued to edge up" but "remained low relative to historical levels." CRE lending shows some caution (rising office vacancies), CMBS delinquencies "ticking up" in office and retail. Credit quality of businesses borrowing from banks "remained sound on balance." Auto loan delinquencies "surpassed their pre-pandemic peak." Overall: mostly healthy with emerging pockets of concern. Bright-line check: "sound" → must be < 50. Score ~40.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:** "The foreign exchange value of the dollar depreciated against most foreign currencies, particularly against those of advanced foreign economies." Bright-line rule: depreciation → must be < 50. The staff outlook notes "a lower path for the dollar." Score ~35 — notable depreciation over the intermeeting period.

**INFLATION_GOODS:** Staff mentions "core goods inflation was anticipated to slow further" and "supply–demand imbalances in goods markets expected to unwind further." Manufacturing orders declining. Goods price pressures are still elevated but clearly easing. Score ~60.

**INFLATION_SERVICES:** "Housing services inflation was expected to peak in 2023 and then move down" and "core non-housing services inflation was forecast to move down as wage growth eased." Services inflation still very elevated with wage growth at 5.1%. Score ~72.

**INFLATION_HOUSING:** "Residential investment looked to be contracting sharply" (reflecting housing market cooling). Staff says "housing services inflation was expected to peak in 2023." This implies housing/shelter inflation is still rising toward its peak. Score ~73.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:** "A preliminary estimate of the staff's common inflation expectations index... edged down in the fourth quarter but continued to be above pre-pandemic levels." Inflation compensation "declined notably." Expectations still somewhat elevated but coming down. Score ~58.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68):** Gap of 13 points. Coherent — the labor market is tighter than the output gap would suggest because the economy is decelerating (output slowing) while labor market tightness is a lagging condition.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (55) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43):** Gap of 12 points. Coherent — consumer spending is holding up (supporting GDP above potential) but investment is contracting, pulling the spending/investment composite below neutral while GDP remains modestly above potential partly due to trade/other factors.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (52):** Gap of 10 points. Coherent — financial conditions are tight due to high rates/policy, but credit spreads themselves have narrowed, meaning the tightness is rate-driven rather than credit-risk-driven.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40):** Coherent — tight financial conditions from policy, but Treasury markets functioning normally.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (75) ↔ Sub-dimensions:** Goods ~60, Services ~72, Housing ~73, Expectations ~58. Weighted toward services/housing (which are stickier and more discussed), average of sub-dimensions around 65-66. Parent score of 75 is somewhat higher, reflecting the overall headline numbers (PCE 6%, core 5%) which capture the full breadth. Reasonable.

### Bright-Line Verification

- **OUTPUT_GAP:** Above potential → score 55 > 50. PASS.
- **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:** "Tight"/"restrictive" → score 62 > 50. PASS.
- **CREDIT_SPREADS:** Spreads narrowed but from elevated levels, roughly normal → score 52. Reasonable.
- **BANKING_STRESS:** "Sound" → score 40 < 50. PASS.
- **DOLLAR_STRENGTH:** Depreciation → score 35 < 50. PASS.
- **TREASURY_STRESS:** Normal functioning, yields declined → score 40 ≤ 50. PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 75 ±5 [High]
**Evidence:**

- "Nominal wage growth continued to be elevated and remained above the pace judged to be consistent with the FOMC's 2 percent inflation objective. Average hourly earnings rose 5.1 percent."

**Center-of-gravity:** Inflation is substantially above target at 6% PCE / 5% core, with elevated wage growth, but has clearly stepped down from its mid-2022 peak, placing this below the 82-88 peak anchor.

**Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is well above neutral because PCE inflation at 6% is triple the 2% target and wage growth remains inconsistent with price stability.

**Center-of-gravity:** The economy is still modestly above potential (implied by output needing to move below potential in the future), but growth is decelerating with a "subdued" forecast ahead.
