# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — May 4, 2004

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff review content is mixed with participant discussion and policy action. I must score ONLY portions attributable to the staff using language cues such as "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," "the staff anticipated," etc., and ignore participant/member content ("members noted," "Committee members," "several members," etc.).

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The key staff-attributable sections are:
- **Paragraphs starting with "The information reviewed at this meeting..."** through the economic data review paragraphs (labor market, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, inventories, international trade, prices). These are standard staff review of economic conditions.
- **"The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..."** — the explicit staff forecast paragraph.
- **Financial market paragraphs** starting with "The Committee's decision at its March meeting..." through the M2 discussion — these describe market developments reported by staff.

Participant content begins with "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective economic developments, a number of members noted..." and continues through the policy discussion. I will IGNORE this for scoring.

### Era Context
May 2004: The economy is in the recovery phase following the 2001 recession and the "jobless recovery" period. The fed funds rate is at 1% (very accommodative). Growth has picked up strongly, but the labor market is only now beginning to improve meaningfully. Inflation had been very low (deflation concerns in 2003) but is now showing signs of picking up. The Committee is on the cusp of beginning to tighten — the famous "measured pace" language is introduced at this meeting.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff language: "core consumer price index advanced at a faster rate in the first quarter than it had in the fourth quarter, reflecting the pass-through of higher energy prices and a leveling off of goods prices after sizable declines last year." "The price index for core personal consumption expenditures also rose at a faster rate in the first quarter." However: "the cumulative increase in the overall consumer price index for the year ending in March was somewhat less than the advance for the twelve months ending in March 2003." Staff forecast: "the staff anticipated a transitory rise in the pace of core inflation in the near term. However, it was expected that the remaining slack in resource utilization and strong productivity growth would keep core inflation at a low level over the forecast period."

Direction: Staff sees inflation as picking up from very low levels but still low overall. The quarterly acceleration is noted but the year-over-year CPI was still moderate. Staff explicitly expects core inflation to remain "at a low level." This places inflation below target — still in the "contained/modest" range but moving up. Score should be in the 38-45 range. "Faster rate" but "low level" — I'll place this around 42.

Self-check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees a pickup but from very low levels, still low overall. 42 is below 50, consistent with "low" inflation. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff language: "the economy expanded at a rapid pace in the first quarter." Staff forecast: "the economy would continue to expand briskly for the rest of 2004." Critically: "the remaining slack in resource utilization" and "resource use slack" (from the statement language the staff helped prepare). Manufacturing capacity utilization "rose for the second consecutive quarter, but to a rate well below its long-run average."

Direction: Staff clearly sees the economy as BELOW potential — "remaining slack in resource utilization," capacity utilization "well below its long-run average." Growth is rapid but hasn't closed the gap yet. Bright-line rule: "slack remains" → score MUST be < 50. Given rapid growth closing the gap, I'll score this around 42-43. The economy is recovering strongly but slack persists.

Self-check: Higher = more above potential. Staff says slack remains. Score 42 < 50. Bright-line satisfied.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff language: "The labor market showed renewed vigor during the first quarter." "Growth in payroll employment during March pushed the average monthly gain for the first quarter as a whole well above that of the fourth quarter." But: "the unemployment rate ticked up to 5.7 percent in March, and the labor force participation rate remained low." Also: "considerable slack seemed to remain in the labor market overall" (from staff forecast context) and "remaining slack in resource utilization."

Direction: UE at 5.7% — using the anchor guidance, UE 5.5-7% corresponds to score 31-45. Labor market is improving but still slack. Hiring is picking up from a low base. Score around 37-38.

Self-check: Higher = tighter labor market. UE at 5.7% with slack remaining = loose. Score 37 < 50. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff language: "Consumer spending and the housing market continued to exhibit strength. Business fixed investment grew smartly." "Real consumer spending grew at a somewhat faster pace in the first quarter." "Retail sales rose briskly." "Outlays for equipment and software expanded at a vigorous pace." "Residential housing activity remained high." But: "investment in nonresidential structures fell considerably."

Direction: Staff sees consumption and investment as clearly above trend — "strength," "briskly," "vigorous," "smartly." The one weak spot is nonresidential structures. Net assessment is solidly above trend. Score around 62-65.

Self-check: Higher = stronger spending. Staff uses "strength," "briskly," "vigorous." Score 63 > 50. Correct.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff language: "still-strong advances in structural productivity" and "strong productivity growth" (in the staff forecast paragraph).

Direction: Staff explicitly references "strong" productivity growth as a key feature. This is above trend. Score around 62-65.

Self-check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Strong" = above trend. Score 63 > 50. Correct.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff language: "the appreciable rise in real long-term interest rates over the intermeeting period implied that financial market conditions had already tightened on balance." Also the staff-reported market developments: Treasury yields "climbed significantly," federal funds futures suggested tightening sooner. However, risk spreads narrowed, equity prices roughly unchanged. The fed funds rate itself was at 1% (very accommodative).

Direction: The text explicitly says "financial market conditions had already tightened on balance." But the starting point was extremely accommodative (1% fed funds). Even with tightening, conditions remain accommodative overall. Staff notes "accommodative financial conditions" supporting household spending. Score should reflect accommodative but tightening — around 33-38.

Bright-line: "accommodative" → must be < 50. Score 35 < 50. Satisfied.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**

Direction: Normal market functioning with yield movements driven by economic data and policy expectations. Score around 40-45 (normal functioning, some volatility but orderly).

Bright-line: No actual dysfunction described. Score 42. Satisfied.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

Direction: Spreads are narrowing — credit conditions improving. "Narrowed significantly" for high-yield. This means less credit stress. Score around 28-32.

Bright-line: "Narrowed" → must be < 50. Score 30 < 50. Satisfied.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

Direction: No valuation concern expressed. Equities roughly flat with balanced forces. Default to 45-50 for no particular concern. Score 48.

Direction: Dollar is appreciating. Bright-line: appreciation → score > 50. Score around 58-60.
