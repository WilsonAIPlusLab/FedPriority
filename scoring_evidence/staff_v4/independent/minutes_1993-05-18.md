## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes**. Staff review content is mixed with participant discussion and policy action. I must carefully separate staff-attributable language from member/participant views.

**Staff-attributable sections** are identified by:
- "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested..." (paras starting ~line 39)
- Factual data summaries on employment, industrial production, retail sales, business investment, trade, prices (lines 39-146)
- "The staff projection prepared for this meeting suggested..." (lines 220-238)
- Interest rate/financial market factual descriptions (lines 163-217)

**Participant/member sections** to IGNORE for scoring:
- "In the Committee's discussion..." (line 241 onward)
- "members focused...", "members commented...", "members continued to report..."
- All dissent discussions, policy directive discussions

### Era Context
May 1993: Early expansion following the 1990-91 recession. Unemployment at 7.0% -- still elevated, well above NAIRU. Economy growing but at a pace that has recently slowed. Inflation had been declining but recent readings showed a pickup. Fed funds rate at 3% -- very low/accommodative. Dollar weakening. This is a recovery period with significant remaining slack.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as ABOVE recent trend but still in context of considerable slack. Key staff language: "increases in wages and prices had been appreciably larger thus far in 1993 than in the second half of last year" (line 47-49). Producer prices "rose more rapidly in March and April" (line 134-135). Excluding food and energy, "producer prices advanced...at a faster pace than in 1992" (line 137-139). Consumer prices: "the rate of increase in consumer prices was higher than in 1992" (line 140-141). Labor costs: "deceleration of labor costs also appeared to have stalled" (line 141-142). Average hourly earnings "grown more rapidly thus far this year" (line 143). However, the staff projection says "persisting slack in resource utilization was expected to be associated with a return to more subdued price increases after a spurt earlier in the year" (line 236-238).

Direction: Staff sees inflation as modestly above prior trend, a pickup from low levels. Not alarming but clearly accelerating from the subdued pace of 1992. This is not high inflation -- it's a modest acceleration from low levels during a slack economy. Score range: 48-55. The "appreciably larger" language and "faster pace" push above 50, but the staff expects it to be temporary ("spurt"). I'll score the current readings, not the forecast. Score: 53.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says prices accelerating. Score above 50 matches.
