# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clear staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: December 2012. The U.S. economy is in a slow recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.7%. The Fed is conducting QE3 ($85B/month in asset purchases). The "fiscal cliff" is a dominant concern. Europe remains in recession. Inflation is running below the 2% target.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation slowed as consumer energy costs fell"; "Increases in consumer prices excluding food and energy remained subdued"; "the staff projected that inflation would be subdued through 2015"; "considerable resource slack persisting." Inflation expectations "remained stable." This is clearly below-target inflation with no pressure. Magnitude: "subdued" = moderate distance below 50. Anchors suggest 38-42 for 2013-19 below-target. Late 2012 is similar. Score ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy BELOW potential. Bright-line check: "considerable resource slack persisting over the forecast period"; "only a gradual decline in slack in labor and product markets"; GDP growth "not materially exceed the growth rate of potential output in 2013." Clear "below potential" language -- score MUST be < 50. The economy is growing at a moderate pace but remains well below potential with substantial slack. This is recovery but gap persists. Score ~37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 7.7% (well above NAIRU of ~5-5.5%); "still elevated"; "relatively large share of workers employed part time for economic reasons trended up"; "share of long-duration unemployment... remained elevated." With UE at 7.7%, anchors suggest 16-30 range (UE > 7%). Employment is expanding but the level is still very slack. Score ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. Consumer spending: "rose at a modest pace in the third quarter, but spending declined in October" (partly hurricane); "factors that tend to influence household spending became less supportive"; real disposable income "declined in October"; consumer sentiment "fell back." Business investment: "Real business expenditures on equipment and software decreased in the third quarter"; forward-looking indicators "were still subdued"; nonresidential structures "also decreased." Housing improving gradually but "still at a low level." This is clearly below trend. Score ~35.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "a moderate rise in productivity" over the year ending Q3. This is a passing reference suggesting roughly normal productivity. Score ~48. Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat ACCOMMODATIVE/LOOSE. Bright-line check: "U.S. financial conditions were little changed, on balance"; "Conditions in unsecured and secured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable"; speculative-grade yields "fell to historical lows"; bond issuance "increased further... rising robustly"; C&I loans "expanded notably"; consumer credit "continued to expand briskly." The staff outlook says "financial conditions were anticipated to be a little less supportive than expected." The word "supportive" implies accommodative. Accommodative = score < 50. But also CRE sector "still generally strained" and mortgage delinquencies "remained elevated." Net: conditions are accommodative but not extremely so, with some pockets of strain. Score ~35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No mention of Treasury market dysfunction. Yields declined due to safe-haven demand and fiscal cliff concerns -- this is normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "remained stable" with "only limited disruptions" from Sandy. Score ~35 (normal, quiet). Moderate confidence.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees credit conditions as somewhat favorable. Investment-grade spreads "widened modestly" but speculative-grade spreads "decreased slightly" with yields at "historical lows." Bond issuance "increased further... rising robustly"; C&I loans "expanded notably"; leveraged loan issuance "surged." Consumer ABS issuance "continued to rise at a strong pace." Lending survey showed "some easing in loan pricing and terms." CRE sector "still generally strained" but improving. Overall credit is available and spreads are relatively compressed. Score ~32.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes edged up, on net." Staff financial stability review: "Valuations for broad asset classes did not appear stretched, or supported by excessive leverage." This is roughly fair-valued assessment. Score ~47.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank equity prices decreased slightly but CDS spreads continued to move lower. Bank credit was "about flat." CRE delinquency "elevated," mortgage delinquency "remained elevated." But the financial stability review says "Indicators of risk-taking and leverage... remained notably below their levels before the financial crisis." The overall picture is moderate -- some lingering stress from the crisis (delinquencies) but improving. Score ~48. The system isn't described as "well-capitalized/sound" (which would be <40) nor as stressed. Mixed signals suggest neutral-ish with some residual stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar edged lower on balance." Dollar weakened modestly. Bright-line: depreciation = score < 50. "Edged lower" = modest. Score ~42.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declined; food prices rose "a little faster" due to drought. Net goods inflation is low/mixed. Score ~38.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed as a category. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing market improving, home prices "advanced further," but not discussed as shelter/housing inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"; "longer-term inflation expectations in the survey were little changed and continued to run within the relatively narrow range that has prevailed for some time"; near-term expectations "edged up." Staff assumes "long-run inflation expectations... to remain stable." Well-anchored at target. Score ~48.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued. Score 35 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slack remains. Score 37 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE 7.7%, elevated. Score 30 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says supportive/accommodative. Score 35 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress. Evidence says spreads compressed, strong issuance. Score 32 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence is mixed -- some elevated delinquencies but improving. Score 48 ~ neutral. Acceptable.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says dollar edged lower. Score 42 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction noted, stable. Score 35 < 50. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (37) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30): Both well below 50. Gap = 7 points. Coherent -- both reflect substantial slack.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (37) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (35): Both below 50. Gap = 2 points. Coherent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (32): Both below 50. Gap = 3 points. Coherent -- both accommodative.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (35): Both below 50. Coherent -- calm markets with accommodative conditions.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (35) vs sub-dimensions: INFLATION_GOODS (38), INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (48). Goods slightly below neutral, expectations near target. Parent score of 35 is reasonable given the overall "subdued" characterization.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Increases in consumer prices excluding food and energy remained subdued." "The staff projected that inflation would be subdued through 2015" with "considerable resource slack persisting."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees inflation running below target with no upward pressure, projected to stay subdued.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes inflation as subdued with slack keeping it low.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 37 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "considerable resource slack persisting over the forecast period"; "progress in reducing unemployment was expected to be relatively slow"; "the increase in real GDP would not materially exceed the growth rate of potential output in 2013."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing moderately but clearly below potential with persistent slack expected through the forecast horizon.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly describes persistent slack. Bright-line rule satisfied (slack remains -> < 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 7.7 percent in November" but was "still elevated." "The relatively large share of workers employed part time for economic reasons trended up a bit" and "share of long-duration unemployment... remained elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly loose with UE at 7.7%, elevated part-time-for-economic-reasons, and high long-duration unemployment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE of 7.7% signals substantial slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose at a modest pace in the third quarter, but spending declined in October." "Real business expenditures on equipment and software decreased in the third quarter" and forward-looking indicators "were still subdued."
**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are below trend, with consumer spending modest and business investment actually contracting.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because both major components are weak.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 48 +-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "a moderate rise in productivity" mentioned in passing in the context of labor compensation.
**Center-of-gravity**: Brief mention of moderate productivity growth suggests roughly normal levels.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is near neutral based on limited evidence.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. financial conditions were little changed, on balance." Speculative-grade bond yields "fell to historical lows"; bond issuance "increased further... rising robustly"; the staff outlook notes conditions expected to be "a little less supportive than expected" (implying conditions are supportive/accommodative).
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative -- strong credit issuance, low yields, stable funding markets -- with some pockets of residual strain in CRE/mortgages.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative/supportive). Bright-line rule satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Conditions in unsecured and secured short-term dollar funding markets remained stable, on net, over the intermeeting period, with reports of only limited disruptions to trading or operations following Hurricane Sandy." Treasury yield movements reflect safe-haven flows and fiscal cliff concerns -- normal price adjustment.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally with no signs of dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are described as stable and orderly.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 32 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on speculative-grade corporate bonds fell to historical lows, and their spreads decreased slightly." "The pace of bond issuance by nonfinancial firms increased further... rising robustly"; C&I loans "expanded notably"; leveraged loan issuance "surged." November lending survey showed "some easing in loan pricing and terms."
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit conditions are clearly favorable with compressed spreads, strong issuance, and easing terms.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (compressed/favorable). Bright-line rule satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes edged up, on net." "Valuations for broad asset classes did not appear stretched, or supported by excessive leverage."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly assesses equity valuations as not stretched -- roughly fair value with no particular concern.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral, consistent with staff's "not stretched" characterization.

### BANKING_STRESS: 48 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: CDS spreads of large bank holding companies "continued to move lower" (positive). But CRE sector "still generally strained amid elevated vacancy and delinquency rates"; mortgage delinquencies "remained elevated." "Indicators of risk-taking and leverage... remained notably below their levels before the financial crisis."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture -- improving bank health indicators but persistent elevated delinquencies in real estate. Not described as healthy/sound nor as stressed. Neutral with lingering legacy concerns.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is near neutral -- neither clearly healthy (which would be < 40) nor clearly stressed (which would be > 55).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 42 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar edged lower on balance. However, the dollar appreciated against the Brazilian real and the Japanese yen."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar weakened modestly on a trade-weighted basis despite appreciating against selected currencies.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar edged lower on balance. Bright-line rule satisfied (depreciation -> < 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 38 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer prices moved up more slowly in October... primarily because of a small decline in energy prices." "Consumer food prices rose a little faster in October, as the effects of last summer's drought started to show through." Core goods prices subdued.
**Center-of-gravity**: Energy declining, food up modestly from drought, core goods subdued. Net goods inflation is below normal.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not specifically discussed as a distinct category.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Housing market discussed in terms of activity and prices, not shelter/housing inflation as a price measure.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "longer-term inflation expectations in the survey were little changed and continued to run within the relatively narrow range that has prevailed for some time"; staff assumes "long-run inflation expectations assumed to remain stable."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are well-anchored and stable, consistent with target. Near-term expectations edged up slightly but long-term firmly anchored.
