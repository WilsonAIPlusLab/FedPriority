### Era Context
January 2021: The U.S. economy is roughly 10 months into the COVID-19 pandemic recovery. Vaccines are beginning to roll out. The economy rebounded strongly in Q3 2020 but slowed markedly in Q4 as a winter wave of COVID hit. Unemployment is at 6.7%, well above pre-pandemic levels. Inflation is running well below 2%. Congress recently passed the CAA and additional fiscal stimulus is anticipated. Financial markets are buoyant on fiscal stimulus expectations.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as clearly BELOW midpoint. Total PCE at 1.1%, core PCE at 1.4%, trimmed mean at 1.7% — all well below 2% target. Staff says inflation "continued to be held down by relatively weak aggregate demand." The outlook projects inflation briefly above 2% in Q2 2021 due to base effects, then finishing the year "at just below 2 percent" and only gradually edging up to 2% by end of medium term. Survey-based expectations "ticked higher" but this is a modest move. Current inflation is clearly subdued.
Score direction: Below 50, in the 26-40 range ("below target, disinflation, contained").
Magnitude: "considerably lower" and specific readings of 1.1-1.7% suggest 28-33.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy clearly BELOW potential. "The level of real GDP had not yet returned to the level seen before the onset of the pandemic." GDP growth slowed markedly in Q4. Staff projects GDP growth will "outpace that of potential" going forward, "leading to a considerable further decline in the unemployment rate" — implying significant slack remains. Unemployment at 6.7%. Industrial production "had not yet overtaken its pre-pandemic level."
Bright-line check: Economy below pre-pandemic level = below potential. Score MUST be < 50.
Score direction: Below 50, in the 31-40 range. The economy is recovering (not in deep recession) but substantial slack remains.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as clearly LOOSE. Employment "continued to be well below its level at the start of 2020." Payrolls fell in December. Unemployment at 6.7%. Payrolls had only "retraced a little more than half of the losses." Initial claims rising in mid-January. Staff explicitly notes that wage gains "were not indicative of tight labor market conditions" and the median wage measure "remained well below its pre-pandemic pace."
Score direction: Below 50, in the 25-35 range. UE at 6.7% with ongoing job losses maps to the 16-30 range per rubric (UE > 7% = 16-30, but 6.7% is slightly below that threshold). I'll score in the 28-33 range.

Score direction: Around 42-48. Banks themselves are healthy, but lending behavior reflects caution/some stress. The overall "notable" financial vulnerabilities assessment pulls slightly toward neutral.
