# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2024-07-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2024. The Fed has been holding rates at restrictive levels after the 2022-2023 hiking cycle. Inflation has come down significantly from its 2022 peak but remains somewhat above the 2% target. The labor market has been gradually cooling from its historically tight 2022-2023 levels. GDP growth has been solid. Markets are pricing in rate cuts by early 2025.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as somewhat above neutral. Total PCE was 2.5%, core PCE was 2.6% — both above the 2% target but well below their peaks. The staff notes inflation was "well below its year-earlier pace but remained somewhat elevated." Month-over-month changes in May and June were smaller than earlier in the year, indicating progress. The staff forecast expects further declines, with total and core reaching around 2% by 2026. Direction: above midpoint — inflation is still elevated but moderately so.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The staff's economic forecast "implied a lower rate of resource utilization" and "the output gap at the start of 2025 was somewhat narrower than had been previously projected, although still not fully closed." The phrase "not fully closed" means the gap is negative (output below potential). GDP growth was "noticeably slower" than its 2023 average pace. BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: "not fully closed" = below potential = score MUST be < 50. Over 2025-2026, GDP was expected to rise "about in line with potential, leaving the output gap roughly flat." So the gap is slightly negative and expected to stay that way. Direction: slightly below midpoint.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor market conditions "continued to ease." Unemployment rose to 4.1%. Job gains moderated — second quarter gains were smaller than Q1 and smaller than the previous year's average. The vacancy-to-unemployment ratio was 1.2, "about the same as its pre-pandemic level." Wage growth decelerated: AHE at 3.9% (down 0.8pp from a year earlier), ECI at 3.9% (down 0.6pp). The labor force participation rate ticked up. The unemployment rate was expected to "edge up slightly" further. At 4.1% UE, the labor market is near but slightly above what many estimates place NAIRU. The vacancy ratio at pre-pandemic levels suggests the extreme tightness is gone. Direction: roughly near neutral, maybe slightly above given UE is still historically moderate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: GDP "rose solidly in the second quarter" but at a "markedly slower pace" than H2 2023. Real PDFP "posted a solid second-quarter increase that was in line with its first-quarter pace and only moderately slower than its average rate of increase in 2023." The word "solid" appears repeatedly. Net exports subtracted from growth. Imports rose at a "brisk pace." Direction: at or slightly above trend — "solid" maps to 50-55 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: This requires careful parsing. Borrowing costs "remained elevated" despite "modest declines in some markets." Mortgage rates stayed "near recent high levels." Small business loan rates "remained elevated." Credit card rates increased slightly. For large firms, financing was "readily accessible." For smaller firms, credit availability "remained moderately tight." SLOOS showed "modestly tighter standards" for C&I loans, and "tightening standards for all loan categories" in CRE. The staff characterized financial vulnerabilities as "notable." Overall, conditions are elevated/tight but not crisis-level. BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: "elevated" borrowing costs + "moderately tight" for small firms + tightening lending standards = tight = score > 50. Direction: above midpoint, moderately tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Nominal Treasury yields "moved down, on net." Conditions in "U.S. short-term funding markets remained stable, with typical dynamics observed surrounding quarter-end." There's no mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement failures. The French election caused some spread widening but effects on Treasury markets "were short lived." This is normal market functioning. Direction: around neutral to slightly below — stable, orderly.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds "were little changed and remained at about the lowest decile of their respective historical distributions." BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: "lowest decile" = extremely compressed = score MUST be < 50. Direction: well below midpoint.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose slightly on net." The one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 "rose moderately and was somewhat elevated by historical standards, suggesting that investors perceived some, but not outsized, risks." The staff judged "asset valuation pressures remained elevated, with estimates of risk premiums across key markets low compared with historical standards." This suggests staff sees valuations as stretched/elevated — which maps to low scores (overvaluation concern). Direction: below midpoint (elevated valuations = concern about overvaluation).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Regulatory capital ratios "remained high" (i.e., banks well-capitalized). But bank fair value of assets "remained low." Hedge fund leverage "at its highest recorded level based on data since 2013." Life insurer leverage "somewhat elevated." CRE credit quality deteriorated further — delinquency rates in CMBS rising, nonperforming CRE loans rising. Consumer delinquencies increasing, particularly among nonprime borrowers. The staff characterized financial vulnerabilities as "notable" and leverage in the financial sector as "notable." Funding risks also "notable." BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: Mixed — banks well-capitalized but notable vulnerabilities and deteriorating CRE credit. Direction: slightly above neutral — pockets of stress amid generally sound capital positions.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index was little changed over the intermeeting period." BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: "little changed" = stable = score around 46-54. Direction: at midpoint.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately broken out in detail beyond the total/core split and a mention that energy and food price changes were excluded from core. The text mentions "declines in energy prices and many consumer food prices" contributed to the gap between total and core. This suggests goods-related categories (energy, food) are softening. Direction: below midpoint.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices remained elevated relative to fundamentals." CRE prices "continued to decline, especially in the multifamily and office sectors." Housing inflation is not explicitly discussed as a price index component. N/A for the inflation sub-component (this is about asset prices, not shelter CPI).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Not discussed in staff sections. N/A.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (47) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52)**: Within 5 points. Coherent — both near neutral with slight divergence reflecting that the labor market is still relatively firm while output gap hasn't fully closed.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (47) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (53)**: Within 6 points. Coherent — "solid" spending with a slightly negative output gap is consistent with GDP being dragged by net exports.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (18)**: Diverge by 40 points. Explanation: Credit spreads are historically compressed (risk-on in markets), but borrowing costs remain elevated due to the high policy rate environment. Lending standards are tightening. 4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (58) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: 16-point gap. Normal — Treasury markets functioning fine while overall financial conditions are tight from elevated rates.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_GOODS scored (40). Core PCE at 2.6% suggests services inflation is the remaining sticking point. The parent score (56) is consistent with goods easing while services remain elevated.
