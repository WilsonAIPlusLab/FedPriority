# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-03-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Participant discussion sections are also present but will be ignored for scoring purposes.

### Era Context
March 2019: Late-cycle expansion. Unemployment near 3.8%, inflation running slightly below 2%, Fed had paused its hiking cycle and adopted a "patient" stance. Trade tensions with China ongoing; global growth slowing. Financial conditions had eased significantly after the Q4 2018 tightening episode.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Staff sees inflation as slightly below midpoint. PCE inflation was 1.7% over 12 months (below 2% target). Core PCE at 1.9%, "somewhat higher than a year earlier" but still below target. Energy prices declining. Staff revised inflation forecast down slightly. Core PCE expected to remain at 1.9% this year, edging to 2% over medium term. Survey-based longer-run expectations "little changed."
- Direction: Below target but not far below. Language: "somewhat below 2 percent," "softer-than-expected readings." This is moderate below-target inflation, not deflation risk.
- Score range: 38-42 per anchors for 2013-19 "below target."

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Staff says economy "still projected to be operating notably above potential for an extended period." Staff forecast GDP growth "at a rate similar to the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020" and "below potential output growth in 2021." Resource utilization was "a little less tight than in the previous forecast." Unemployment projected to "decline a little further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
- BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language present — score MUST be > 50.
- However, growth is slowing markedly in Q1, and the gap is narrowing. Staff revised GDP path down. "Resource utilization was a little less tight." This is modestly above potential, not strongly above.
- Score: 53-55. Above potential but narrowing.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Unemployment at 3.8% (well below 4%). "Labor market conditions remained strong." Part-time for economic reasons below late 2007 lows. Job openings "remained elevated." Quits edged up. Initial claims "near historically low levels." Average hourly earnings up 3.4% YoY ("significantly faster pace than a year earlier"). ECI up 3% ("somewhat faster than a year earlier"). Staff projects UE to decline further below natural rate.
- With UE at 3.8%, score should be above 55. Multiple indicators of tightness. Wage growth accelerating.
- Score: 62. Tight labor market with strong wage growth, UE at 3.8%.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Real PCE "decreased markedly in December" with only partial January rebound. Residential investment "appeared to be softening further." Business equipment spending "looked to be slowing." Housing starts mixed; home sales decreased. Forward-looking indicators for business equipment "pointed to sluggish increases in the near term."
- However, staff judged the Q1 slowdown would be "transitory" and GDP would "bounce back solidly." Government spending moving sideways. Net exports were a drag.
- Net picture: Current spending is weak — Q1 clearly soft — but staff sees it as temporary. The score should reflect the current readings, which are below trend.
- Score: 38-42. Below trend currently despite supportive fundamentals.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Not directly discussed in staff sections. One reference in participant section to "pickup in productivity growth last year" and labor compensation "2.9 percent over the four quarters of 2018, about the same rate as a year earlier." No explicit staff productivity assessment.
- Score: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Staff says "financing conditions for businesses and households improved slightly and generally remained supportive of economic activity." Corporate bond spreads narrowed. Equity prices increased notably. VIX declined. Mortgage conditions "accommodative on balance." Consumer credit "generally supportive." CRE financing "generally accommodative." Short-term funding "generally remained stable."
- BRIGHT-LINE: "supportive" / "accommodative" → score MUST be < 50.
- This is a period of easing from the Q4 2018 tightening. Conditions are now described as accommodative/supportive, but the 2017-19 neutral anchor is 46-54. Given the explicit "supportive" and "accommodative" language and the improvement described, this is slightly below neutral.
- Score: 40-44. Accommodative/supportive conditions.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Yields on nominal Treasuries "declined a bit across the yield curve." Yield curve "quite flat" with spread at lower end of historical range. Funding markets "generally remained stable." EFFR at IOER. No language about dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress.
- Normal market functioning. Yield moves from policy expectations = normal.
- Score: 40. Normal functioning, orderly yield movements.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
- Investment- and speculative-grade spreads "narrowed." CMBS spreads "declined." Leveraged loan issuance "recovered." CP/CD yield spreads "narrowed further from their elevated year-end levels." Spreads "still wider than levels observed last fall." Gross issuance "strong." Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations "continued to show signs of deterioration, although actual defaults remained low."
- Spreads narrowing, generally favorable. Some residual wideness from Q4 2018 but trending toward normal. Anchors for 2013-19: 30-40.
- Score: 38. Generally favorable, narrowing from Q4 widening, approaching normal.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- "Major U.S. equity price indexes increased over the intermeeting period, with broad-based gains across sectors." VIX declined to lowest this year. Improved trade deal prospects and accommodative policy cited as drivers. Earnings projections revised down but prices rose anyway. No staff valuation concern language.
- Equities rising, no valuation concerns expressed by staff. Default to typical 2013-19 range.
- Score: 45. No particular concern; prices rising.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
- Not explicitly discussed in staff sections. CRE lending "grew at a strong pace." C&I lending "showed continued strength." No mention of bank capital, stress, or health issues in staff review. One participant mention of corporate debt/leverage "warranted continued monitoring."
- Limited direct evidence. The lending indicators suggest health. But not enough staff-specific banking system assessment.
- Score: N/A (banking system not specifically discussed as a system in staff sections).

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
- "The broad dollar index appreciated slightly as the extension of accommodative policies and revised guidance by major foreign central banks weighed on AFE currencies."
- BRIGHT-LINE: Appreciation → score MUST be > 50.
- "Slightly" = modest, 5-10 points from midpoint.
- Score: 55. Slight appreciation.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: Gap of 14 points. Borderline but explainable — Q1 spending weakness is seen as transitory; the economy overall is still above potential even as current-quarter spending is soft.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Both below 50, co-moving. Consistent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Both near 40. Consistent. Normal times, no dysfunction.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (40) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (46). Consistent — headline inflation slightly below target, expectations near target.
