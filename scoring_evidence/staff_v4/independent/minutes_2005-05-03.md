### Era Context
May 2005: Mid-expansion period. Fed funds rate at 2.75%, moving to 3%. Housing boom underway. Oil prices rising. Economy had grown rapidly in 2004H2, moderating in 2005Q1. Unemployment at 5.2%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as somewhat above midpoint. "Sharp increases in energy prices pushed up headline inflation, and core measures were also somewhat elevated." Core CPI "rose a bit more rapidly recently" though 12-month change was little different from year-earlier. ECI "decelerated over the first quarter from an already moderate pace." Staff forecast: "Inflation was projected to edge lower over the rest of the year and into 2006."
- Headline pushed up by energy, core "somewhat elevated," but labor costs moderate and staff expects inflation to edge lower. This is modestly above neutral.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says somewhat elevated headline and core. Score above 50 matches.
- Score: 55 — "somewhat elevated" core with moderating labor costs and expected easing.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy modestly above potential. "Economic growth was projected to run a bit above the staff's estimate of the economy's potential." However, "remaining slack in resource markets" is also mentioned. Capacity utilization "remained a bit below its thirty-year average."
- Bright-line check: "a bit above the staff's estimate of the economy's potential" → MUST be > 50. But also "remaining slack in resource markets" → suggests < 50. These are contradictory. The staff forecast says growth is projected to run above potential going forward, but currently there is "remaining slack." The current state appears to be slightly below potential with slack remaining, but growth trajectory is above potential rate. The output gap refers to the LEVEL, not the growth rate. "Remaining slack in resource markets" and capacity utilization below average indicate the economy is still below potential.
- Wait — re-reading: "economic growth was projected to run a bit above the staff's estimate of the economy's potential" — this is about growth rate exceeding potential growth rate, meaning the gap is closing. "Remaining slack in resource markets" confirms currently below potential.
- Bright-line: "slack remains" → MUST be < 50.
- Score: 46 — slightly below potential, gap closing, growth above potential rate but slack still present.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as moderately tight, approaching full employment. Unemployment at 5.2% in March. "A gradual erosion of slack in labor markets" — surveys indicating "some employers were finding some jobs requiring special skills harder to fill" and "households were experiencing increases in job availability." However, "survey measures of expected conditions in labor markets softened somewhat" and "participation rate remained low." ECI "decelerated over the first quarter."
- UE at 5.2% maps to the 46-52 range (near NAIRU anchor). Staff says slack is eroding gradually but still present. Moderate labor costs suggest remaining slack.
- Score: 48 — near NAIRU but still some slack; labor market tightening gradually.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Mixed but moderately positive. Consumer spending "advanced solidly in the first quarter" but "much of that strength was registered early in the quarter, and spending in March was subdued." "Consumers appeared to have turned somewhat cautious." Business spending on E&S "moderated substantially in the first quarter from the very high rates of last year, but appeared to retain considerable momentum; strong gains occurred in all major categories except motor vehicles." Nonresidential structures "remained quite subdued." Housing remained very strong.
- Net: Consumer spending solid but weakening; business investment strong but decelerating; housing very strong; nonresidential structures weak. Staff marked down growth forecast but still sees "considerable momentum."
- Score: 52 — roughly at trend; solid but moderating from above-trend prior pace.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: Not explicitly discussed by staff in dedicated terms. Participant section mentions "robust underlying productivity growth" but that is participant language. No staff-specific productivity assessment.
- Score: N/A

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff describes "favorable borrowing conditions in the form of low interest rates and narrow risk spreads in bond and loan markets." Policy stance described as "accommodative." Interest rates rose then fell. Equity indexes "moved down considerably."
- "Low interest rates and narrow risk spreads" + "favorable borrowing conditions" = accommodative/loose conditions.
- Bright-line: "accommodative" / "favorable" → MUST be < 50.
- Score: 28 — clearly accommodative with low rates and narrow spreads; 2005 era anchor is 10-20, but the equity decline and some tightening from rate hikes push it slightly higher.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: Staff describes interest rate movements as responses to data and policy expectations — "Interest rates, however, rose, reportedly in response to the statement's references to increased price pressures." Then "these increases were more than reversed by weaker-than-expected data." This is normal market functioning, not dysfunction.
- No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.
- Score: 40 — normal market functioning with some volatility.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Staff explicitly notes "narrow risk spreads in bond and loan markets" and "favorable borrowing conditions."
- Bright-line: "narrow" → MUST be < 50.
- 2005 anchor: 10-20 (extremely compressed).
- Score: 15 — narrow/compressed spreads, consistent with 2005-06 era anchor.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**

- Per the gotcha rule: price moves without valuation commentary → default 45-55. "Declined considerably" pushes toward 55.
- Score: 53 — modest decline noted but no distress or valuation assessment.
