# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-08-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). Staff sections are clearly delineated: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring is based solely on staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
August 2009 — deep in the Great Financial Crisis aftermath. The economy is near the trough of the recession; GDP has been contracting severely since late 2008. Unemployment is at 9.4%. The economy is stabilizing but not yet growing. Financial conditions are improving from crisis levels but remain stressed.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. Gasoline prices pushed up headline in June, but "core consumer price inflation remained relatively stable." For Q2, "core inflation picked up from the pace in the first quarter," but this was partly due to measurement revisions. Staff expects "core PCE inflation to slow substantially over the forecast period." Labor costs "decelerated markedly." Direction: below neutral. The staff sees muted inflation with downside risks going forward. Magnitude: "relatively stable" core with expectation of "substantial" slowing — this is moderately below midpoint, around 25-30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output gap as far below midpoint. "Overall economic activity was stabilizing after a contraction in real GDP during 2008 and early 2009 that...was greater than previously estimated." Factory utilization "recorded a new low in June." Staff projects GDP to "start to increase in the second half of 2009" and grow "somewhat above its potential rate in 2010" — meaning output is currently far below potential. Bright-line check: "below potential" language implicit throughout → must be < 50. Score around 18-22. The economy is stabilizing at the bottom, not recovering yet.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very loose. Unemployment at 9.4%. "Job losses continued to abate" but "labor markets still quite slack." "Year-over-year growth in average hourly earnings...slowed further." "Considerable deterioration in labor market conditions." Unemployment expected to "increase somewhat during the remainder of 2009." Score around 15-18.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as very weak but stabilizing. Consumer spending "dropped only a little further," housing "beginning to turn up," E&S investment contracting but "rate of decline was substantially smaller." But "fundamental determinants of investment in E&S remained weak." Nonresidential construction "generally remained quite poor." This is deep contraction territory with early signs of leveling off. Score around 25-28.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions improving from crisis but still stressed. Short-term funding markets "showed further improvement." Libor-OIS spreads "declined a bit further" but "still somewhat elevated by historical standards." Corporate bond spreads "narrowed further" but context is improving from extreme. Commercial bank credit "contracted further." Lending standards still tightening. CRE lending contraction "accelerated." This is improving from crisis but still tight. Bright-line check: conditions still strained/elevated → must be > 50. Score around 62-67.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Explicitly discussed. "Indicators of Treasury market functioning were little changed over the intermeeting period, and functioning continued to be somewhat impaired. Bid-asked spreads held roughly steady, and trading volumes remained low. The on-the-run liquidity premium for the 10-year Treasury note was little changed at elevated levels, although it was well below its peak last fall." This is actual dysfunction language (impaired functioning, low trading volumes, elevated liquidity premium). Score around 58-62.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff reports corporate bond spreads "narrowed further and dropped below the previous peak levels reached in 2002." Libor-OIS spreads declined but "still somewhat elevated." Commercial paper spreads at "low ends of their ranges over the past two years." Leveraged loan market "continued to improve." But bank lending standards still tightening, CRE credit tightening further, "small businesses reporting increased difficulty in obtaining credit remained high." This is a mixed picture: market-based spreads improving notably, but bank credit conditions still very tight. Score around 60-65 — still elevated but clearly improving.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose, on net." Equity risk premium "narrowed a bit more but remained high by recent historical standards." Option-implied volatility "dropped a bit further." Staff does not express overvaluation concern; equities recovering from lows. Risk premium still high = still somewhat depressed relative to fair value. Score around 58-62.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Significant stress evident. "Commercial bank credit contracted further in June and July. All major loan categories declined." CRE lending contraction "accelerated." Banks still tightening standards. CIT Group "worsened sharply." Large consumer loan losses reported. However, larger banking institutions reported "better-than-expected second-quarter earnings," bank equity prices rose, CDS spreads declined. Mixed but still stressed. Bright-line check: stress indicators dominate → must be > 50. Score around 68-72.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar depreciated mildly on a trade-weighted basis since late June." Staff outlook mentions "a lower dollar" as supporting the forecast. Bright-line check: depreciation → must be < 50. "Mildly" depreciated = modestly below midpoint. Score around 38-42.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Gasoline prices pushed up headline; "a jump in gasoline prices pushed up overall consumer price inflation in June." PPI for core intermediate materials "turned up in June following a string of monthly declines." Some goods price pressure returning from low levels. Score around 35-40.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Restaurant meals reclassified. "Nonmarket components" had "unusually low readings." Limited direct evidence on services inflation. N/A — not enough specific service-sector inflation evidence.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not directly discussed as an inflation component. Housing activity improving but no discussion of shelter/rent inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Median year-ahead inflation expectations...held relatively steady in July." "Longer-term inflation expectations were about the same as the average over 2008." "Survey measures of inflation expectations showed no significant change." Anchored, no particular concern. Score around 42-45 — stable but in a period where downside risk is the concern.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Core consumer price inflation remained relatively stable in recent months."
- "The staff still expected core PCE inflation to slow substantially over the forecast period; the very low readings on hourly compensation lately suggested that such a process might already be in train."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff sees inflation as contained with meaningful downside risk; core is stable but expected to slow substantially given enormous labor market slack and decelerating wages.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff sees subdued current inflation and expects substantial further slowing.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 20 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall economic activity was stabilizing after a contraction in real GDP during 2008 and early 2009 that...was greater than it had previously estimated."
- "The factory utilization rate recorded a new low in June."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is at or near the trough of a severe recession; output is far below potential with massive resource slack, though stabilization signs are emerging.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because output is deeply below potential with utilization at record lows. Bright-line: below potential → < 50. PASS.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 16 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate edged down in July to 9.4 percent."
- "With labor markets still quite slack, year-over-year growth in average hourly earnings of production and nonsupervisory workers slowed further in July."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Extreme labor market slack with 9.4% unemployment, decelerating wages, and the staff expecting unemployment to rise further — very loose labor conditions.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because UE is 9.4%, "quite slack," and expected to worsen.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 27 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending dropped only a little further in the first half of this year, on balance, after falling sharply in the second half of last year."
- "Real investment in E&S continued to contract in the second quarter; however, the estimated rate of decline was substantially smaller than in the previous two quarters."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumption and investment are still contracting but the pace of decline has moderated significantly; housing is beginning to turn up, but nonresidential construction remains "quite poor" and most determinants of spending remain weak.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is well below neutral because spending and investment are still declining, though the pace of contraction is moderating.

**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends or levels in staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Libor-OIS spreads—while still somewhat elevated by historical standards—declined a bit further."
- "Commercial bank credit contracted further in June and July. All major loan categories declined, apparently reflecting the combined effects of weaker demand for most types of loans, some substitution from bank loans to other funding sources, and an ongoing tightening of lending standards and terms."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly improving from crisis extremes — spreads narrowing, short-term funding improving — but bank credit is still contracting, standards remain tight, and multiple indicators are "still somewhat elevated by historical standards."

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because conditions remain tight despite improvement. Bright-line: "elevated" / still tightening → > 50. PASS.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Indicators of Treasury market functioning were little changed over the intermeeting period, and functioning continued to be somewhat impaired."
- "The on-the-run liquidity premium for the 10-year Treasury note was little changed at elevated levels, although it was well below its peak last fall."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Treasury market functioning is explicitly described as "somewhat impaired" with low trading volumes and elevated liquidity premiums — genuine dysfunction, though much improved from peak crisis.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because of explicit "impaired" functioning and elevated liquidity premiums. This is actual market dysfunction, not mere yield movements. PASS.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Corporate bond spreads narrowed further and dropped below the previous peak levels reached in 2002 following the 2001 recession."
- "The contraction in commercial real estate (CRE) lending accelerated...Large fractions of respondents to the July survey again noted that they had tightened standards."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Market-based credit spreads have narrowed significantly from crisis peaks but remain historically wide (only now dropping below 2002 peaks). Bank credit conditions remain very tight with accelerating CRE contraction and continued tightening of standards.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads are still historically elevated and bank credit conditions remain restrictive. Bright-line: "elevated" → > 50. PASS.

---

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad stock price indexes rose, on net, over the intermeeting period."
- "The spread between an estimate of the expected real equity return over the next 10 years for S&P 500 firms and an estimate of the real 10-year Treasury yield—a rough gauge of the equity risk premium—narrowed a bit more but remained high by recent historical standards."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Equity markets are recovering with improved sentiment, but the equity risk premium remains elevated, suggesting markets still reflect significant stress relative to historical norms.

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral because the risk premium "remained high by recent historical standards," indicating continued market stress/concern despite recovery.

---

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Commercial bank credit contracted further in June and July. All major loan categories declined."
- "The financial condition of CIT Group, Inc., one of the largest lenders to middle-market firms, worsened sharply over the period."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The banking system remains under significant stress — credit contracting across all categories, CRE lending contraction accelerating, large consumer loan losses, CIT Group near failure — though large bank earnings surprised positively and bank equity prices rose.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is well above neutral because of broad credit contraction, accelerating CRE stress, and continued loan losses. Bright-line: "stressed" indicators dominate → > 50. PASS.
