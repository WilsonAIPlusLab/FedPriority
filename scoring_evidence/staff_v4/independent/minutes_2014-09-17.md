## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary) from the September 16-17, 2014 FOMC meeting. I will score ONLY staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
September 2014 falls in the mid-recovery period following the Global Financial Crisis. The economy was growing moderately but still below potential, with unemployment at 6.1% (above the natural rate), and inflation running below the 2% target. The Fed was still in its asset purchase program (tapering phase), with liftoff expectations forming for mid-2015. Anchors suggest: OUTPUT_GAP ~35-42, LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~35-40, INFLATION_PRESSURE ~38-42 for this era.

### Dimensional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation was running below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent." PCE price index was "about 1-1/2 percent" over 12 months. Core inflation "essentially the same as total inflation." Staff projected inflation "to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." Near-term forecast revised lower. This is clearly below-target inflation. Score should be in the 35-42 range per era anchors. Language is "running below" and "lower than anticipated" -- consistent with ~35-38.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Evidence: "economic activity was expanding at a moderate pace" and the staff projected GDP growth would "exceed the growth rate of potential output" in 2015-2016, meaning currently it had not yet closed the gap. The expansion was "anticipated to steadily reduce resource slack." "Resource slack projected to diminish slowly." Bright-line check: "resource slack" / below potential language --> score MUST be < 50. The gap is closing but still present. Score ~40-42.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW full employment but improving. Unemployment rate was 6.1% in August. Per rubric anchors, UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45. "Labor market conditions improved a little further." The unemployment rate was "essentially unchanged." Long-duration unemployment and part-time for economic reasons "declined a little." Job openings and hiring moved up. But 6.1% is still significantly above NAIRU (~5%). Score ~37-40.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as moderate/solid. "Real PCE appeared to be rising at a moderate pace." Retail sales "increased at a solid rate." Light motor vehicle sales "surged in August." Business equipment spending "appeared to rise further." Orders above shipments pointing to future gains. Housing "seemed to be picking up." Government purchases "roughly flat." Net assessment is moderate-to-solid, consistent with the 2014 anchor of ~48-52. Score ~50.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned. "Modest gains in labor productivity" -- compensation per hour rose 2-3/4% but unit labor costs advanced "more slowly than compensation per hour" due to productivity gains, but described as "modest." This suggests below-trend productivity. Score ~38-40.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as generally accommodative/favorable. "Credit conditions for domestic businesses remained favorable." Corporate bond issuance "rebounded strongly." C&I loans "expanded briskly." CRE conditions "continued to ease." Leveraged loan issuance "continued apace." Short-term funding "little changed." However, household financing "remained mixed" -- auto loans widely available but mortgage access limited. Bright-line check: "favorable" / "accommodative" --> score MUST be < 50. Score ~30-35.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**: 10-year yields "moved up about 15 basis points" due to Fed communications. No mention of any dysfunction, liquidity issues, or market stress. This is normal yield movement from policy expectations. Per bright-line rule, rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50). No dysfunction described. Score ~38-40. Could also be N/A since no specific dysfunction discussed, but yields are discussed, so I'll score it low-stress.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**: High-yield spreads "widened noticeably" early in the period but "returned to their initial levels over subsequent weeks." Corporate bond yields rose "about in line with" Treasuries. Liquidity "remained stable." Credit conditions "remained favorable." Net: spreads had a temporary wobble but returned to prior levels. Bright-line: overall favorable/narrow --> score < 50. Score ~35-38.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad measures of domestic equity prices were up modestly." Investors interpreting data as implying "economic recovery was strengthening." No valuation concern language. This is a neutral-to-no-concern description. Score ~45-48.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**: No specific discussion of banking system health, capital levels, or stress. C&I loans expanding, CRE loans solid -- these are indirect indicators of healthy banking. But no explicit banking health assessment. I'll score N/A since banking system is not specifically discussed as a separate topic.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated against most currencies over the intermeeting period, including large appreciations against the euro, the yen, and the pound sterling." Bright-line: dollar appreciation --> score MUST be > 50. "Large appreciations" suggests notable strength. Score ~62-67.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was running below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Total U.S. consumer price inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, was about 1-1/2 percent over the 12 months ending in July." Staff projected inflation "to remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly describes inflation as persistently below the 2% target at roughly 1.5%, with a near-term forecast revised lower and no expectation of reaching target for several years.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running about 1/2 percentage point below the 2% target with no near-term expectation of reaching it.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 41 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate pace." Staff anticipated GDP growth would "exceed the growth rate of potential output" in 2015-2016, implying current output is below potential. "The expansion in economic activity over the projection period was anticipated to steadily reduce resource slack."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing moderately but still below potential, with resource slack expected to diminish slowly over the projection period.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references remaining "resource slack" that will only gradually diminish. Bright-line satisfied: slack remains --> below 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 6.1 percent in August." "Labor market conditions improved a little further." "The unemployment rate was expected to decline gradually and temporarily move slightly below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate toward the end of the period" -- implying currently well above the natural rate.
**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is improving but remains significantly below full employment with unemployment at 6.1%, well above the natural rate.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 6.1% indicates meaningful labor market slack. Per rubric, UE 5.5-7% maps to 31-45.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be rising at a moderate pace." "Sales of light motor vehicles surged in August." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to rise further." "The pace of activity in the housing sector seemed to be picking up."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is moderate, auto sales surged, business investment is rising, and housing is picking up. The overall tone is solid but not exceptional -- at trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral because staff describes a moderate, sustained pace of spending growth consistent with trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "With modest gains in labor productivity, unit labor costs advanced more slowly than compensation per hour."
**Center-of-gravity**: Productivity is mentioned only briefly as "modest," suggesting below-trend gains.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because "modest gains" suggests productivity growth below historical trend.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Credit conditions for domestic businesses remained favorable." "Commercial paper outstanding and commercial and industrial loans at banks expanded briskly." "Credit conditions in the commercial real estate sector continued to ease." "Issuance of institutional leveraged loans continued apace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes credit conditions as broadly favorable with strong issuance, brisk loan growth, and easing CRE conditions. Only household mortgage access remains limited.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (accommodative) because staff explicitly says conditions are "favorable" with "brisk" expansion and continued "easing." Bright-line satisfied: favorable/accommodative --> below 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The yield on 10-year nominal Treasury securities moved up about 15 basis points, on net, since the FOMC met in July, likely boosted in part by Federal Reserve communications." No mention of liquidity issues, auction problems, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury yields rose modestly due to policy expectations -- entirely normal market functioning with no stress indicators.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because yield movements reflect normal policy-expectation repricing with no dysfunction. Bright-line satisfied: normal yield movement from policy expectations = score 36-50.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on [high-yield] bonds widened noticeably; however, these spreads returned to their initial levels over subsequent weeks." "Measures of liquidity in the corporate bond market remained stable." "Credit conditions for domestic businesses remained favorable."
**Center-of-gravity**: A temporary widening in high-yield spreads fully reversed, and overall credit market conditions remained favorable with stable liquidity.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads returned to prior (favorable) levels and credit conditions remained broadly supportive. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad measures of domestic equity prices were up modestly over the intermeeting period, with some reports suggesting that investors were interpreting incoming economic data as implying that the economic recovery was strengthening."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rose modestly with no valuation concern expressed by staff. This is a neutral, no-concern description.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because staff describes modest gains with no valuation commentary -- default to 45-55 per rubric.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or stress. Loan growth data is mentioned in the financial conditions context but not as a banking system assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A -- not specifically discussed.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The U.S. dollar appreciated against most currencies over the intermeeting period, including large appreciations against the euro, the yen, and the pound sterling." The staff also noted "a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a factor revising down the GDP forecast.
**Center-of-gravity**: The dollar strengthened broadly and significantly, with "large appreciations" against major currencies, and this was material enough to affect the GDP forecast.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because staff describes broad, large dollar appreciation. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation --> above 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 35 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer energy prices declined in both July and August, while consumer food prices rose."
**Center-of-gravity**: Energy prices declining, food prices rising -- mixed but net downward pressure on goods prices.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral -- energy price declines pulling goods inflation down.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
No specific services inflation discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
No specific shelter/rent inflation discussion. Housing activity is discussed but not housing inflation.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Michigan survey moved down a bit in August and early September, while longer-term inflation expectations in the survey were little changed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are well-anchored with longer-term measures stable, though near-term expectations edged down slightly.
**Direction confirmation**: Slightly below neutral because near-term expectations moved down while longer-term remained stable -- essentially anchored at/near target.
