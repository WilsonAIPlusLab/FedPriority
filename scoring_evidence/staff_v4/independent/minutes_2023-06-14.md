# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2023-06-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document from the June 13-14, 2023 FOMC meeting. It contains three clearly delineated staff sections: Staff Review of the Economic Situation, Staff Review of the Financial Situation, and Staff Economic Outlook. I will score ONLY staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
Mid-2023: The Fed has been aggressively hiking rates since March 2022. Inflation remains elevated but is declining from peak. The labor market is still very tight. The banking stress episode from SVB (March 2023) is recent but conditions have stabilized somewhat. The staff is forecasting a mild recession.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly above midpoint. "Consumer price inflation remained elevated." Total PCE at 4.4%, core PCE at 4.7%, core CPI at 5.3%. The trimmed mean PCE at 4.8%. These are all well above the 2% target. Staff projects total PCE at 3.0% and core at 3.7% for this year, only reaching close to 2% by 2025. Risks tilted to the upside. This is clearly elevated — score should be in the 70-78 range. Not at the 82-88 peak of 2022 Q2-Q3 because inflation has been easing from highs, but still well above target. Direction: above midpoint. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff describes "real GDP was expanding at a modest pace." Staff outlook explicitly forecasts a mild recession starting later this year. However, the CURRENT assessment says "current tight resource utilization in both product and labor markets." The key phrase is "tight resource utilization" which implies currently above potential. Staff projects output moving below potential in 2025 and unemployment rising above the natural rate. But RIGHT NOW, resource utilization is tight. Bright-line rule: "tight resource utilization" implies above potential, so score MUST be > 50. But growth is "modest" and decelerating, and staff expects recession. Score modestly above 50 — around 53-55 to capture the tension between currently tight but rapidly decelerating. Direction: slightly above midpoint.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff says "labor market conditions remained tight." Unemployment at 3.7% — well below 4%. Job gains "robust." Job openings rate "relatively high." Wage growth "continued to be elevated" at 4.3% AHE. The rubric says UE below 4% should almost certainly be above 55. Anchors for 2022-23: 65-75. With UE at 3.7%, robust job gains, elevated wages, and explicit "tight" characterization, this should be around 67-70. Direction: well above midpoint.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff says real GDP expanding at "modest pace." PDFP "expanding more slowly in the second quarter than its robust first-quarter pace" but "more resilient than in the second half of last year." Trade deficit widened in April. Overall: modest/moderate growth, not strong but not weak. The language "modest" maps to 46-50 range. But PDFP was "robust" in Q1 and is slowing; for Q2 it's more "modest." Net assessment: around 48-50. Direction: roughly at midpoint.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: No explicit discussion of productivity growth. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff says "Financing conditions remained moderately restrictive." Borrowing costs increased notably. Interest rates on bank loans "rose further above their peaks from the previous tightening cycle." Credit card and auto loan rates "at, or near, their highest levels since the Global Financial Crisis." But also "credit availability generally remained solid" and consumer credit "generally accommodative." Bright-line: "moderately restrictive" means score MUST be > 50. The language "moderately restrictive" with still solid credit availability suggests around 60-63. Direction: above midpoint.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose significantly over the intermeeting period, but this was driven by policy rate expectations and economic resilience — normal market functioning. T-bill yields spiked around debt limit concerns but "largely retracing." No mentions of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. The debt limit created temporary stress but resolved. Conditions in overnight funding and repo markets "remained generally stable." Score in the 42-48 range for normal functioning with a brief debt-limit blip. Direction: roughly at midpoint — no dysfunction.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on corporate bonds moved roughly in line with Treasury benchmarks — meaning spreads themselves didn't widen much. "Credit spreads...were little changed over the intermeeting period" (in foreign markets context, but domestic spreads also moved in line with Treasuries). Leveraged loan and CMBS markets "somewhat more strained." Speculative-grade issuance "remained subdued." Small business credit "continued to show signs of tightening." Investment-grade issuance was robust. Mixed picture: some areas strained, others functioning. Score around 50-55 — modestly above neutral given the leveraged loan and CMBS strain. Direction: slightly above midpoint.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: S&P 500 "increased sizably." VIX "near the 30th percentile of its historical distribution" (low volatility). Bank equities moved up. No staff valuation commentary — just price movement. Per rubric, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55. Equities rising is good from a stress perspective (lower stress). Score around 42-45 — modestly below neutral (less stress). Direction: slightly below midpoint.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank equity prices improved but regional banks "remained below their levels in early March." Banks' funding ability "appeared to have stabilized" but "remained somewhat strained relative to before the closure of SVB." Deposit outflows moderated. CMBS delinquency rate increased, especially for office buildings. Credit quality "remained solid" but market participants expected "some deterioration." This is a post-SVB environment with residual stress but improving conditions. Bright-line: not "well-capitalized/sound/healthy" — instead "somewhat strained" and "stabilized" — so above 50. Score around 55-58. Direction: above midpoint (moderate stress). Anchor for March 2023 SVB: 55-65; this is June, conditions improving, so lower end.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's trade-weighted broad dollar index was also little changed, on net." Bright-line: "little changed" = stable = around 46-54. Dollar did appreciate against the renminbi specifically, but the broad index was little changed. Score: 50. Direction: at midpoint.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Core goods inflation "was forecast to move down further this year and then remain subdued." Declines in energy prices and softening food prices. Goods prices are disinflating. Score around 40-45 — below midpoint, as goods prices are easing but not yet fully normalized.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Housing services inflation "about peaked" and expected to move down. Core nonhousing services inflation "projected to slow gradually as nominal wage growth eased further." Services inflation is still elevated — housing peaked, nonhousing slow to decline. Score around 65-68.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing services inflation "considered to have about peaked and was expected to move down over the rest of the year." At the peak implies still elevated. Score around 62-65.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Longer-term survey measures "remained within the range of their values reported in the decade before the pandemic." Near-term measures "moved down" and "continued to be below their peaks." But staff sees upside risk that "inflation expectations could become unanchored." Well-anchored currently but with risk. Score around 52-55 — at target anchor with slight upside tilt from risk language.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68)**: Divergence of ~14 points. This is explainable: the labor market is a lagging indicator — GDP growth is modest/decelerating but the labor market hasn't yet loosened. Staff explicitly notes both "tight resource utilization" and "modest" GDP growth. The gap reflects the tension in the staff's own assessment.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (49)**: Divergence of ~5 points. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (53)**: Divergence of ~9 points. Explainable: financial conditions are moderately restrictive driven by high interest rates (rate levels), while credit spreads themselves haven't widened as much — the tightening is through the rate channel not the spread channel.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (62) vs TREASURY_STRESS (45)**: Non-crisis period — Treasury stress near neutral is appropriate regardless of FC level. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (73) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (~42), Services (~66), Housing (~63), Expectations (~53). Weighted toward services and housing given discussion emphasis, and current readings (core PCE 4.7%) are clearly elevated. The parent score of 73 is consistent — it reflects the actual current inflation readings which are being driven heavily by services while goods have eased.

### Bright-Line Verification
- OUTPUT_GAP: "tight resource utilization" = above potential. Score 54 > 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "moderately restrictive" = tight. Score 62 > 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Mixed — some strained areas, some accommodative. Score 53 slightly above 50. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: "somewhat strained" = stressed side. Score 57 > 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "little changed" = stable. Score 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Normal yield movements from policy expectations. No dysfunction. Score 45 < 55. PASS.

**Score: 73 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "the staff saw the risks around the baseline inflation forecast as tilted to the upside"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation remains well above the 2% target with core PCE at 4.7%, though easing from peak; staff sees upside risks to inflation and projects only a gradual return toward target.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because current inflation readings are more than double the 2% target, easing only slowly.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff describes current resource utilization as tight (above potential) but growth is decelerating rapidly and staff forecasts a mild recession, keeping the score only modestly above neutral.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly describes "tight resource utilization" in both product and labor markets. Bright-line rule satisfied: "tight resource utilization" > 50.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is very tight with unemployment at 3.7%, robust job gains, and elevated wage growth, though some metrics are easing from peaks.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE is 3.7% (below 4%), job gains are robust, and staff explicitly calls conditions "tight."
