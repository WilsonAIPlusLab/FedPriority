# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2024-05-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: May 2024. The Fed is at peak rates (5.25-5.50%) following the 2022-2023 hiking cycle. Inflation has come down substantially from 2022 peaks but remains above 2%. Labor market is strong but gradually rebalancing. GDP growth has been resilient. No recession. This is a "last mile" disinflation period.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE midpoint. PCE at 2.7%, core PCE at 2.8% — "considerably lower than their year-earlier levels but had surprised to the upside." Inflation "remained above 2 percent." The staff outlook notes "more persistence in inflation in coming months" and upside risks to inflation. This is clearly above target but moderating — in the 55-65 range. "Somewhat elevated" + "surprised to the upside" + still declining from highs = ~60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE midpoint. "The economy was expected to maintain its high rate of resource utilization over the next few years." GDP growth "stepped down" from "robust" but PDFP "posted an increase that was similar to the solid gains seen over the second half of 2023." The staff projects "output growth roughly similar to the staff's estimate of potential growth" — implying the economy is AT or slightly above potential and expected to stay there. "High rate of resource utilization" = above potential. Bright-line: "high rate of resource utilization" → must be > 50. Score ~53-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as ABOVE midpoint (tight). "Labor market conditions remained strong." Unemployment at 3.8% — below historical NAIRU estimates. "Labor demand and supply continued to move into better balance, though the speed of this realignment appeared to have slowed." Payrolls increasing at a "faster average monthly pace." ECI 3-month "stepped up noticeably." With UE at 3.8%, per the rubric guidance (UE < 3.8% → 66-80; UE 3.8-4.5% → 53-65), and the language of "strong" conditions, score should be ~58-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as AT or slightly above midpoint. GDP "rose modestly" but PDFP increase "was similar to the solid gains seen over the second half of 2023." "Solid gains" suggests at-trend to slightly above. Net exports were a drag. Real imports "rose at a brisk pace." Consumer credit card balances "continued to grow at a robust pace." Score ~53-55 ("solid" = at-trend to slightly above).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ABOVE midpoint (tighter). Borrowing costs "generally rose somewhat over the intermeeting period from already elevated levels." Mortgage rates "increased and remained near recent high levels." Small business loan rates "remained at elevated levels." Banks tightened lending standards across CRE categories. Smaller banks "continued to tighten standards and terms." However, "financing through capital markets and nonbank lenders was readily accessible for public corporations." C&I price terms "little changed." So a mixed picture: rates elevated and rising, bank standards tightening, but capital market access still good. This is somewhat tight — score ~58-62.

Bright-line check: The language describes elevated borrowing costs and tightening standards → score > 50. Satisfied.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "rose substantially" but this is attributed to reassessment of inflation persistence and policy implications — normal price adjustment, NOT dysfunction. "Market-based measures of interest rate uncertainty remained elevated by historical standards." Short-term funding markets "remained stable." Basis trade by hedge funds "appeared to have declined from its peak but remained elevated." No language of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction failures. Per the bright-line rule, rising yields from policy expectations = normal (45-50). The elevated uncertainty and basis trade concerns nudge slightly higher. Score ~48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Yield spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were about unchanged, and those on speculative-grade corporate bonds increased moderately." C&I loan terms "little changed." Capital market financing "readily accessible." But CRE delinquencies rising, credit card and auto delinquencies "notably above their levels just before the pandemic." Spreads stable-to-slightly-widening with some credit quality deterioration. Score ~48-52. The spreads themselves are described as roughly stable with modest widening in spec-grade. Score ~50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff raised assessment of asset valuation vulnerabilities to "elevated" — "valuations across a range of markets appeared high relative to risk-adjusted cash flows." Stock prices "declined moderately" over intermeeting period. VIX "increased significantly" but "remained at moderate levels by historical standards." The staff explicitly flags elevated valuations — this is concern about overvaluation. Per the rubric, overvaluation concern → lower scores. "Elevated" valuations = stretched → score ~25-30.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Regulatory capital ratios "remained high." But "the fair value of bank assets was estimated to have fallen further in the first quarter, reflecting the substantial duration risk on bank balance sheets." CRE nonperforming loans "rose further through March, especially for loans secured by office buildings." Financial vulnerabilities characterized as "notable." Leverage in financial sector "notable." This is a mixed picture: well-capitalized but with emerging stress from duration risk and CRE. Score ~45-50. The capital ratios push below 50 but the duration risk and CRE NPLs push toward 50. Score ~46.

Bright-line check: Not purely "well-capitalized/sound" — there are notable vulnerabilities. Score near 50 is appropriate.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Wider U.S.–AFE yield differentials and, to a lesser extent, heightened geopolitical tensions in the Middle East contributed to a moderate increase in the broad dollar index." Dollar appreciated → score must be > 50. "Moderate increase" = modest strength. Score ~57.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not specifically broken out. Imports of goods rose; exports of goods mixed. No direct goods inflation discussion. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices remained elevated relative to fundamentals such as rents and Treasury yields." CRE prices "continued to decline, especially in the multifamily and office sectors." Housing inflation not directly discussed as a price index component. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Some measures of short-term inflation expectations had moved up" but "longer-term expectations were little changed and stood at levels consistent with those that prevailed before the pandemic." This is anchored with slight short-term upside. Score ~53.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60)**: Both above 50, labor slightly higher — consistent. Labor market is strong while output is near potential. The 6-point gap is well within tolerance.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (54) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (54)**: Both at 54. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (50)**: 10-point gap. FC tighter due to elevated rates and bank standard tightening, while market spreads remain roughly normal. This is consistent with a rate-driven tightening rather than a credit-stress-driven one. Explainable.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (60) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (48)**: FC tight but Treasury market functioning normally. Consistent — this is not a crisis, just elevated rates.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (53). Headline at 60 reflects above-target readings with upside surprises; expectations anchored. Consistent — current inflation elevated but expectations stable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "total PCE prices increased 2.7 percent...core PCE price inflation...was 2.8 percent...Both inflation measures were considerably lower than their year-earlier levels but had surprised to the upside." Staff outlook: "the expected pace of disinflation was slower than in the March projection, as incoming data pointed to more persistence in inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is clearly above 2% target, has surprised to the upside, and disinflation is proceeding more slowly than expected — but is still declining from much higher levels.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE is 2.7-2.8%, above target, with upside surprises and persistence concerns.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The economy was expected to maintain its high rate of resource utilization over the next few years, with projected output growth roughly similar to the staff's estimate of potential growth." PDFP "posted an increase that was similar to the solid gains seen over the second half of 2023."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is at a high level of resource utilization (above potential) but growth is settling to potential pace, implying the gap is positive but stable.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff says "high rate of resource utilization." Bright-line satisfied: above potential → > 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions remained strong." "The unemployment rate ticked down to 3.8 percent in March." "The 3-month change in the ECI stepped up noticeably from the average pace that prevailed over the second half of 2023."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight with UE at 3.8%, strong payrolls, and accelerating short-term wage growth, though gradually rebalancing.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE is 3.8% (at the boundary of 53-65 range), conditions described as "strong," and wage measures are still elevated.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "real GDP rose modestly in the first quarter" but PDFP "posted an increase that was similar to the solid gains seen over the second half of 2023." "Credit card balances continued to grow at a robust pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Headline GDP slowed but underlying private demand remains solid — at-trend to slightly above.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral; "solid" gains in PDFP signal continued healthy spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No discussion of productivity trends in the staff sections.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "borrowing costs generally rose somewhat over the intermeeting period from already elevated levels." "Rates on 30-year conforming residential mortgages increased and remained near recent high levels." "Banks reported tightening standards further in the April SLOOS for all loan categories of commercial real estate loans."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are somewhat tight — rates elevated and rising, bank lending standards tightening — though capital market access remains available for large firms.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because borrowing costs are elevated and standards are tightening. Bright-line satisfied: tight/restrictive language → > 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "nominal Treasury yields at all maturities also rose substantially as investors appeared to reassess the persistence of inflation." "Conditions in U.S. short-term funding markets remained stable." "Market-based measures of interest rate uncertainty remained elevated by historical standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Yields rose substantially but this reflects normal repricing of policy expectations, not dysfunction. Funding markets stable. Elevated uncertainty is a mild concern but no actual market dysfunction described.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral because yield moves are policy-driven (normal functioning), not dysfunction. Bright-line satisfied: no actual dysfunction → 45-55 range.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 50 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yield spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were about unchanged, and those on speculative-grade corporate bonds increased moderately." "Financing through capital markets and nonbank lenders was readily accessible for public corporations."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are roughly normal — IG unchanged, HY slightly wider, capital market access good. Delinquencies rising in some consumer and CRE segments but not translating to broad spread stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is at neutral; spreads are not notably tight or wide.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "the staff...raised the assessment of vulnerabilities in asset valuations to elevated, as valuations across a range of markets appeared high relative to risk-adjusted cash flows." "Broad stock price indexes declined moderately, on net, over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly flags elevated/stretched valuations across markets — this is overvaluation concern. The moderate price decline does not change the valuation assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION lower score = more overvaluation concern. Staff says valuations are "elevated" and "high relative to risk-adjusted cash flows" → score in the 25-35 overvaluation concern range.

### BANKING_STRESS: 46 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Regulatory capital ratios in the banking sector remained high." But "the fair value of bank assets was estimated to have fallen further in the first quarter, reflecting the substantial duration risk on bank balance sheets." "The share of nonperforming CRE loans at banks...rose further through March, especially for loans secured by office buildings."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is well-capitalized on a regulatory basis but faces notable duration risk and rising CRE credit quality concerns — mixed picture, slightly below neutral stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral because capital ratios are high (low stress) despite emerging pockets of concern (duration risk, CRE NPLs). Bright-line: "capital ratios remained high" → below 50.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Wider U.S.–AFE yield differentials and, to a lesser extent, heightened geopolitical tensions in the Middle East contributed to a moderate increase in the broad dollar index."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar appreciated moderately driven by yield differentials.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because dollar strengthened. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation → > 50.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
Not specifically discussed as a separate inflation component.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not specifically discussed as a separate inflation component.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
House prices discussed in financial stability context but not as an inflation sub-component with price index data.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 53 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "some measures of short-term inflation expectations had moved up" but "longer-term expectations were little changed and stood at levels consistent with those that prevailed before the pandemic."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations largely anchored at target with slight short-term upside movement — very close to neutral with a small upward tilt.
**Direction confirmation**: Above neutral because short-term expectations moved up, though long-term remain anchored.
