# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-01-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly separated "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: January 2016. The Fed just raised rates for the first time in December 2015 (to 0.25-0.50%). The economy was in a moderate expansion with unemployment at 5.0%. Global financial markets were in turmoil due to Chinese financial market disruptions and collapsing oil prices. The dollar had been strengthening. Inflation was running well below target due to energy and import price declines.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at ~0.5% over 12 months, core PCE at 1.25% — well below the 2% target. Restrained by energy price declines and non-energy import price decreases. Staff projects inflation will "increase gradually" and reach 2% by end of 2018 — but current readings are clearly low. Risks to inflation "weighted to the downside." This maps to the 26-40 range: "below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent." I'll score around 30. Higher = more pressure; low readings = low score. Consistent.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff says real GDP growth "appeared to slow" in Q4. However, the staff projects that "real GDP would expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." The unemployment rate was "expected to gradually decline further and to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This is a mixed signal: current GDP growth slowed, but the economy is near potential and projected to move above it. The staff's medium-term forecast suggests the economy is near or slightly below potential currently but closing. Industrial production decreased. Net exports weighed on growth. Bright-line check: Staff says GDP was slower than expected but projects expansion "somewhat faster than potential" — the current state appears to be near potential but not yet above it given the slowdown. I'll score 45 — slightly below potential, approaching full capacity.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.0%, which is near the staff's estimate of the natural rate. Payroll employment increased "substantially" in December. Q4 job gains faster than Q3. Participation rate and employment-to-population ratio increased slightly. Average hourly earnings up 2.5% (modest). The staff projects unemployment will "run somewhat below" the natural rate. With UE at 5.0%, this maps to the 46-52 range ("near NAIRU; full employment; balanced"). Anchors suggest 50 for 2016-17. I'll score 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending appeared to increase at a "slower rate" in Q4. Real PCE "rose solidly in November" but "spending had been flat in October" and retail sales "edged down" in December. Key factors "generally favorable" — solid income growth, elevated consumer sentiment. Housing in "continued gradual recovery." Business equipment spending growth "looked to be slower," with indicators pointing to "soft business equipment spending in the coming months." Drilling/mining continued to fall. Inventory investment decreased. Government purchases "about flat." This is below trend overall — consumer spending decelerating, business investment weak, mining declining. I'll score 40 — "below trend; sluggish; cautious."

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "Domestic financial conditions tightened over the intermeeting period." Turmoil in Chinese markets, lower oil prices, "pullback from risky assets," "sharp decline in equity prices," "notable widening in risk spreads." However, financing conditions "remained accommodative for firms of higher credit quality but tightened somewhat for riskier firms." Consumer credit "remained accommodative on balance." Financial vulnerabilities judged "moderate on balance." This is a mixed but tightening picture — conditions were accommodative but actively tightening. Bright-line check: "tightened" language means > 50. But "remained accommodative" for investment-grade and consumer credit pushes it back. Net: I'll score 55 — somewhat tight, reflecting the active tightening but still accommodative base.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined reflecting lower expected rate path and "some increase in safe-haven demands amid the market turbulence." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction failures. This is normal market functioning — yields moved on fundamentals. Score 40 — normal market functioning with some volatility context.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened over the intermeeting period." "Notable widening in risk spreads on corporate bonds." "Increased concerns about corporate credit quality, particularly in the energy sector." Speculative-grade issuance weak. CMBS spreads continued to widen. EME credit spreads widened. But investment-grade issuance "stayed robust." This is clearly above neutral — notable widening. Bright-line check: "widened" = must be > 50. I'll score 62 — somewhat to notably wide, reflecting significant concern especially in energy/HY but investment-grade still functioning.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes declined sharply." "Quite volatile in January." Option-implied volatility "climbed to the upper end of its range of the past few years." High correlation with oil prices and foreign indexes. No explicit valuation commentary from staff. Per the gotcha rule, "equities declined" alone maps to 50-55. Given "sharply" and high volatility, I'll score 58 — modestly depressed/stressed.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff "judged the financial vulnerabilities of the U.S. financial system as moderate on balance." "Strong capital and liquidity positions at banks." "Moderate leverage in the nonbank financial sector." "Subdued borrowing by households." But "leverage continued to increase in the nonfinancial business sector" and "high leverage of nonfinancial corporations and the liquidity mismatch at high-yield bond mutual funds suggested some elevated risks." Bright-line check: "strong capital and liquidity positions at banks" = must be < 50. The concerns are about non-bank entities, not banks themselves. I'll score 35 — healthy banking system with minor concerns about non-bank risks.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated against most foreign currencies." "The foreign exchange value of the U.S. dollar appreciated further against most currencies, with larger increases relative to the currencies of commodity-exporting countries." The staff also notes "higher assumed trajectory for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" in their forecast and risks include "the foreign exchange value of the dollar could rise substantially further." Bright-line check: appreciation = must be > 50. Strong appreciation language. I'll score 65 — notable strength with ongoing appreciation.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declining substantially. Non-energy import prices decreasing. Motor vehicle sales at high level but declining. Goods price pressure is clearly low. Score 25.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core CPI at ~2% includes services. Not separately discussed with much specificity beyond the overall core figure. Limited evidence. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing mentioned in activity context (gradual recovery, home values with "further strong gains") but housing/shelter inflation not specifically discussed. Score N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10 year "ticked up but continued to run near the low end of its typical range of the past 15 years." Survey of Primary Dealers/Market Participants "essentially unchanged." But market-based measures of inflation compensation "declined further" and "fell further." Staff notes risk that "longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down." This is mixed — surveys stable but low-end, market-based declining. I'll score 40 — modestly below the well-anchored midpoint.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (45) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (50)**: 5-point gap. Consistent — economy near potential with labor market at roughly full employment. Small divergence explained by GDP slowing while labor market remains solid (labor is a lagging indicator).

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (45) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (40)**: 5-point gap. Consistent — both slightly below neutral, reflecting the Q4 slowdown.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (62)**: 7-point gap. Consistent — both above 50 reflecting tightening. Spreads slightly higher because the widening is more pronounced than overall conditions (which still have accommodative base for IG/consumer).

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Divergence of 15 points. Explained: Treasury market functioning normally (yields moving on fundamentals), while broader financial conditions tightened via risk assets. This is expected — Treasuries served as safe haven.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (25), Expectations (40). Parent at 30 is consistent — goods dragging down, expectations anchored but with downside risk.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by decreases in both energy prices and the prices of non-energy imports." "Total U.S. consumer prices...increased about 1/2 percent over the 12 months ending in November...Core PCE price inflation...was 1-1/4 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes inflation running well below target at 0.5% headline / 1.25% core, with downside risks — classic below-target disinflation.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because headline PCE is 0.5% and core is 1.25%, both well below the 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth in real gross domestic product (GDP) appeared to slow." Staff projects "real GDP would expand at a somewhat faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018" and unemployment "to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential with recent slowing; staff sees it as slightly below but closing, projected to move above potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is slightly below neutral because GDP growth slowed while the economy is near but not yet above potential. Bright-line: no explicit "above potential" for current quarter — projected for future.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate remained at 5.0 percent in December." "Total nonfarm payroll employment increased substantially in December, and the monthly pace of job gains in the fourth quarter as a whole was faster than in the third quarter." "Average hourly earnings for all employees increased 2-1/2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market at roughly full employment with solid job gains and modest wage growth — balanced, near NAIRU.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at neutral because UE at 5.0% is near the natural rate, with moderate wage growth and strong payrolls.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) appeared to have increased at a slower rate in the fourth quarter." "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products looked to be slower in the fourth quarter...generally pointed to soft business equipment spending in the coming months."
**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment decelerating, with mining/drilling in continued decline — below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because both consumer and business spending slowed with forward indicators pointing to continued softness.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial conditions tightened over the intermeeting period, as turmoil in Chinese financial markets and lower oil prices contributed to concerns about prospects for global economic growth and a pullback from risky assets." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained accommodative for firms of higher credit quality but tightened somewhat for riskier firms."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions actively tightening from an accommodative base — net result is somewhat tight, with bifurcation between IG (still accommodative) and HY/risky (tightening).
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff explicitly says conditions "tightened." Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities moved lower over the intermeeting period...Part of the decline likely also reflected an increase in safe-haven demands." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally — yields moved on expected policy path and safe-haven flows, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because no market dysfunction described. Yields moving on fundamentals is normal.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened over the intermeeting period, reportedly reflecting increased concerns about corporate credit quality, particularly in the energy sector, and a decline in investors' willingness to assume risk." "Speculative-grade bond issuance was weak." "Credit spreads in CMBS markets continued to widen."
**Center-of-gravity**: Notable spread widening across corporate and CMBS markets, concentrated in energy and lower-rated credits, with reduced risk appetite.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads "widened" across multiple markets. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes declined sharply over the intermeeting period." "Domestic equity indexes were quite volatile in January, and one-month-ahead option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index climbed to the upper end of its range of the past few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Sharp equity declines with elevated volatility, but no explicit staff valuation commentary — scored as modestly depressed per the rubric's default for "equities declined sharply."
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress/concern) because equities declined sharply with high volatility.

### BANKING_STRESS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Strong capital and liquidity positions at banks, moderate leverage in the nonbank financial sector, and subdued borrowing by households." "Financial vulnerabilities of the U.S. financial system as moderate on balance."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system healthy with strong capital; some non-bank risks from corporate leverage and HY bond fund liquidity mismatch, but system-level stress low.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are "strong" in capital and liquidity. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated against most foreign currencies." "The foreign exchange value of the U.S. dollar appreciated further against most currencies, with larger increases relative to the currencies of commodity-exporting countries." Also in economic review: "ongoing effects of the appreciation of the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based dollar appreciation continuing from 2015, weighing on exports and industrial production — notable strength.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "appreciated further." Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 25 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Substantial declines in consumer energy prices." "Decreases in the prices of non-energy imports." PCE headline at 0.5% driven largely by goods deflation (energy, imports).
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices falling due to energy and import price declines — meaningful downward pressure.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
**Evidence**: Not separately discussed with sufficient specificity.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
**Evidence**: Housing activity discussed but shelter/rent inflation not specifically addressed.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Recent survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance." "Michigan survey measure...continued to run near the low end of its typical range of the past 15 years." "Measures of forward inflation compensation based on TIPS and inflation swaps fell further."
**Center-of-gravity**: Surveys anchored but at the low end of historical range; market-based measures declining further. Modestly below the well-anchored midpoint.
