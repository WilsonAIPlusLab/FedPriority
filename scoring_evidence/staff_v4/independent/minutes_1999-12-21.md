# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — December 21, 1999

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
- **Type**: Minutes (synthesized summary)
- **Segmentation**: Unsegmented pre-2005 minutes. Staff review content is mixed with participant discussion. I will score ONLY staff-attributable language ("the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," data descriptions in the review-of-conditions paragraphs).
- **Staff-attributable sections**: Paragraphs describing economic data reviewed at the meeting (lines 19-58), the staff forecast paragraph (line 58). Everything beginning with "In the Committee's discussion" or referencing "members" is participant content and excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
Late 1999 — the economy is in the midst of the late-1990s expansion/boom. Unemployment is at 4.1%, GDP growth is strong, dot-com equity markets are surging. The Fed has been tightening. Y2K concerns are present. Inflation remains remarkably subdued despite tight labor markets, partly due to the productivity boom.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint — subdued/contained.
- Evidence: "Aggregate price increases had been smaller in recent months, reflecting a flattening in energy prices after a rapid run-up." "Excluding the volatile food and energy components, consumer prices rose slightly less in the twelve months ended in November than in the previous twelve-month period." "labor compensation rates have been rising more slowly than last year." "the rise in compensation per hour... was down considerably from the advance in the preceding four-quarter period."
- Staff forecast: "Core price inflation was projected to rise somewhat over the forecast horizon" — so currently low but expected to rise.
- Magnitude: Inflation is "subdued," "smaller increases," core CPI slightly less than prior year. This is clearly below target/neutral, but not deflationary. The staff notes some upside risks (non-oil import prices, labor cost firming). Language is "subdued" / "contained" — fits 38-42 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Staff says pressure is low/subdued. Score below 50. Correct.
- Score: 40 ±5

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees economy as ABOVE potential.
- Evidence: "continued strong expansion of economic activity." Staff forecast says "the expansion would gradually moderate from its currently elevated pace to a rate around or perhaps a little below the growth of the economy's estimated potential." This explicitly says current pace is ABOVE potential. Also: "the increase in aggregate demand had been exceeding even the now-higher sustainable rate of growth in aggregate supply" — but this is participant language, so I won't use it directly. However, the staff forecast itself states growth is above potential.
- Bright-line check: "currently elevated pace" above "economy's estimated potential" → score MUST be > 50.
- Magnitude: "continued strong expansion," growth above potential but expected to moderate. The late 90s anchor is 57-63. Staff describes strong but not extreme overheating. "Elevated pace" relative to potential is moderately above.
- Score: 60 ±5

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as VERY TIGHT.
- Evidence: "Despite very tight labor markets, labor compensation had been climbing more slowly than last year." "The civilian unemployment rate fell to 4.1 percent in October, its low for the year." "Nonfarm payroll employment rose substantially further."
- Staff forecast: "persistently tight labor markets that would increasingly outpace even continued rapid productivity growth."
- Magnitude: UE at 4.1% — very low for this era. Staff explicitly uses "very tight labor markets." The late 90s anchor is 65-75. UE at 4.1% with "very tight" language.
- Critical note: UE below 4.5% with explicit "very tight" language → 65-75 range.
- Score: 70 ±5

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Direction: Staff sees spending as ABOVE trend — strong/robust.
- Evidence: "Consumer demand was particularly robust and business fixed investment remained on a strong upward trend." "Total nominal retail sales rose appreciably." "Sales gains were widespread, but purchases of durable goods, especially light vehicles, were particularly strong." Capital equipment: "some slowing in the very rapid growth" but still growing.
- Housing: "somewhat softer" but "still at an elevated level."
- Magnitude: "Particularly robust" consumer demand + "strong upward trend" in business investment. Some slowing in capex growth rate but from very rapid levels. Net assessment is clearly above trend.
- Score: 65 ±5

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
- Direction: Staff sees productivity as strong and above trend.
- Evidence: Staff forecast mentions "continued rapid productivity growth." "labor compensation had been climbing more slowly... even continued rapid productivity growth." The staff forecast explicitly anticipates that labor cost firming would "increasingly outpace even continued rapid productivity growth" — implying productivity growth is rapid.
- This is the late-90s productivity boom era.
- Score: 68 ±7

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
- Direction: Staff sees conditions as somewhat mixed — rates have risen but equities are up.
- Evidence: "most market interest rates rose somewhat in the period after the November 16 meeting." "most broad stock market indexes advanced further during the intermeeting period." "M3 surged in November." "Total domestic nonfinancial debt continued to expand at a pace in the upper portion of its range."
- Interest rates up (tightening direction), but equities surging and credit expanding freely. Net: conditions are not particularly tight. Credit is freely available, equity wealth is rising. This is a late-90s environment with generally accommodative/supportive financial conditions despite rate hikes.
- Bright-line check: No explicit "accommodative" or "tight" staff language about conditions overall. Rates are up somewhat but credit is expanding. Score near neutral to slightly loose.
- Score: 42 ±8

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
- Direction: No market dysfunction discussed.
- Evidence: "most market interest rates rose somewhat" — this is normal yield movement from policy expectations, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market stress.
- Bright-line: Rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50). But no specific discussion of Treasury market functioning beyond yield changes.
- Score: N/A (no specific discussion of Treasury market functioning/dysfunction)

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**

- Evidence: No staff language about corporate bond spreads, credit availability, or spread levels. There is mention of "total domestic nonfinancial debt continued to expand at a pace in the upper portion of its range" — suggesting easy credit conditions, but no explicit spread language.
- Score: N/A (not specifically discussed in staff-attributable language)

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
- Direction: Staff notes equity prices rising.
- Evidence: "most broad stock market indexes advanced further during the intermeeting period." Staff forecast references "positive wealth effects associated with large earlier gains in equity prices" and "anticipated waning of positive wealth effects."
- The staff forecast discusses large equity gains as a factor expected to moderate. This is December 1999 — dot-com peak. But staff doesn't explicitly comment on valuation levels or bubble concerns in the staff-attributable sections.
- The mention of "large earlier gains" and "wealth effects" suggests awareness of elevated valuations but no explicit valuation judgment.
- Score: 30 ±10, Moderate confidence. The staff clearly sees equity prices as elevated (building wealth effects that are expected to wane), suggesting some implicit concern about stretched valuations, but no explicit bubble/frothy language in staff sections.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**

- Score: N/A

The dollar appreciated against the euro and the Canadian dollar, but those movements were largely counterbalanced by declines against the Japanese yen."
