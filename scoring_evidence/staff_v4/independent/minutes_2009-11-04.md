# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2009-11-04

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: November 2009. The U.S. is in the early stages of recovery from the Great Financial Crisis. GDP has turned positive but the labor market remains deeply impaired. Unemployment near 10%. Financial conditions improving from crisis levels but still strained. Inflation subdued with significant slack.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. "Consumer price inflation remained subdued." "PCE price index moved up only a bit." Core PCE "edged up." Staff projects "core inflation would slow somewhat further over the next two years" due to "significant underutilization of resources." Longer-term expectations "about unchanged." This is clearly below-target, subdued inflation with risk of further disinflation. Score range: 20-28.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as FAR BELOW potential. "Considerable slack remained in the manufacturing sector." Factory utilization "up only a bit from its historical low." Staff "revised up its estimate of the degree of slack in the economy." "Significant underutilization of resources expected to persist for several years." Bright-line check: "slack remains" language present -> score MUST be < 50. Score range: 20-28.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very LOOSE. Unemployment at 9.8%. "The labor market continued to weaken." "Job losses remained widespread across industries." Staff "boosted its projection for the unemployment rate over the next several years." Labor force participation "fell to its lowest level of the year." With UE at 9.8%, anchor is 15-22 range. Score range: 16-22.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as mixed — nascent recovery but from very weak levels. Q3 real PCE "rose at a solid rate" but September declined. E&S spending "appeared to have stabilized." Nonresidential construction "remained quite poor." Housing recovering from "extremely low level." Consumer credit contracting. Real disposable income fell for fourth consecutive month. Net: some signs of stabilization/recovery but still below trend. Score range: 33-40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff directly mentions productivity. "Output per hour rose sharply in the second and third quarters, contributing to a sizable decline in unit labor costs." This is above-trend productivity growth. Score range: 62-70.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as still tight but improving. Short-term funding "eased a bit further." Libor-OIS spreads "near their pre-crisis levels." But bank lending standards "tightened further," bank loans "continued to contract rapidly," consumer credit contracting, nonresidential financing "very difficult to obtain." Mixed picture: money markets improving, credit channels still impaired. Bright-line check: overall still restrictive/tight -> score MUST be > 50. Score range: 60-68.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff specifically discusses Treasury market functioning. "Indicators of Treasury market functioning... showed some signs of improvement over the intermeeting period, but trading conditions remained somewhat impaired." This is actual market functioning language, not just yield movements. Score range: 55-62.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as improving but still elevated. "Corporate bond spreads narrowed further." CP spreads "at the lower ends of their respective ranges." But bank lending still contracting, private sector debt declining, CRE credit very tight. Spreads narrowing from extreme levels but still above normal. Score range: 55-63.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "broad stock price indexes were about unchanged." Equity risk premium "remained elevated." Option-implied volatility "moved up slightly." Stock prices flat, elevated risk premium suggests some lingering concern. Score range: 55-62.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff sees significant banking stress. "Investor sentiment toward the banking sector appeared to deteriorate." Bank share prices fell. CDS spreads widened for regional banks. "Market participants reportedly remained concerned about the earnings prospects for banks... weak economic activity and rising loan losses." Bank lending standards tightening further. Allowance for loan losses rising. Bright-line check: stressed/concerned language -> score MUST be > 50. Score range: 68-75.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The trade-weighted index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed over the intermeeting period." Little changed = near neutral. Score range: 45-52.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices rose, food prices declined. Core goods edged up. Mixed but subdued. Score: 30-35.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Some house price indexes rose in recent months but "remained below year-earlier levels." This is about housing prices not shelter CPI specifically. Limited direct discussion. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Median year-ahead inflation expectations... increased, remaining higher than at the turn of the year, but longer-term inflation expectations... were about unchanged." Near-term up slightly, long-term anchored. "Five-year inflation compensation five years ahead was little changed." Score: 42-48.

### Polarity Checks

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "consumer price inflation remained subdued in recent months"; "the staff continued to project that core inflation would slow somewhat further over the next two years" due to "significant underutilization of resources expected to persist for several years"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation currently subdued and staff forecasting further disinflation — solidly below target with slack-driven downward pressure.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because current inflation is subdued and staff projects further slowing.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "considerable slack remained in the manufacturing sector, as the factory utilization rate for September was up only a bit from its historical low"; "the staff also revised up its estimate of the degree of slack in the economy"; "significant underutilization of resources expected to persist for several years"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy far below potential with enormous slack; recovery just beginning from near-Depression lows.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff repeatedly emphasizes significant slack and underutilization. Bright-line: "slack remains" -> < 50. PASS.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 18 +/-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "the unemployment rate rose in September to 9.8 percent"; "The labor market continued to weaken... job losses remained widespread across industries"; staff "boosted its projection for the unemployment rate over the next several years"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market deeply slack with UE near 10%, still weakening, and staff projecting elevated unemployment persisting.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral. UE at 9.8% anchors near 15-22 range per rubric.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "real personal consumption expenditures rose at a solid rate" in Q3 but "real outlays declined in September"; "Real spending on E&S appeared to have stabilized... conditions in the nonresidential construction sector generally remained quite poor"; "financing for new construction projects was very difficult for businesses to obtain"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showing some recovery (boosted by one-time programs), but business investment weak, nonresidential construction very poor, and credit channels impaired. Below trend but no longer collapsing.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral — mixed with nascent recovery but still well below trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 65 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Output per hour rose sharply in the second and third quarters, contributing to a sizable decline in unit labor costs so far this year"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Productivity growing sharply above trend, driven by firms cutting labor faster than output. One sentence but very clear language.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is above neutral because "rose sharply" indicates well-above-trend growth.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Overall conditions in short-term funding markets eased a bit further" and Libor-OIS "near their pre-crisis levels," BUT "bank lending standards and terms tightened further and demand continued to decline... for most types of loans"; "Bank loans continued to contract rapidly"; "financing for new construction projects was very difficult for businesses to obtain"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Money markets normalizing but bank credit channels still significantly impaired; net conditions remain restrictive.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because despite money market improvement, broad credit conditions remain restrictive. Bright-line: tight/restrictive -> > 50. PASS.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Indicators of Treasury market functioning... showed some signs of improvement over the intermeeting period, but trading conditions remained somewhat impaired"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets improving but still not fully normal — residual dysfunction from crisis.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because staff explicitly notes "somewhat impaired" trading conditions, which is actual market functioning language.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Corporate bond spreads narrowed further"; "Spreads on A2/P2-rated commercial paper and AA-rated asset-backed CP remained at the lower ends of their respective ranges"; but "Debt of the private domestic nonfinancial sector appeared to have declined again"; "Bank loans continued to contract rapidly"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Market-based spreads narrowing significantly but still above normal; bank credit still contracting. Net level still somewhat elevated.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral — narrowing from crisis levels but still elevated overall.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes were about unchanged"; "the equity risk premium—remained elevated"; "Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index moved up slightly"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities flat with elevated risk premium, suggesting modest lingering stress/concern but not acute distress.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is modestly above neutral — elevated risk premium signals some ongoing concern.

### BANKING_STRESS: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Investor sentiment toward the banking sector appeared to deteriorate... Bank share prices fell"; "Market participants reportedly remained concerned about the earnings prospects for banks in an environment of weak economic activity and rising loan losses"; "The allowance for loan and lease losses rose further at large banks"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system under significant stress — deteriorating sentiment, rising loan losses, tightening lending, contracting credit.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is well above neutral because of deteriorating sentiment, rising losses, and continued credit contraction. Bright-line: stressed language -> > 50. PASS.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The trade-weighted index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed over the intermeeting period"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar essentially stable — no significant movement in either direction.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is near neutral because dollar was "little changed."

### INFLATION_GOODS: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "increases in energy prices were largely offset by declines in food prices"; "Gasoline prices rose again in October"; core PCE "edged up"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Energy up but food down; net goods price pressure low, consistent with broad disinflation.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
- Not specifically broken out in the staff discussion.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- Housing prices mentioned but shelter/rent inflation not specifically discussed by staff.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "longer-term inflation expectations from this survey were about unchanged"; "five-year inflation compensation five years ahead was little changed"; but "Median year-ahead inflation expectations... increased"
- **Center-of-gravity**: Long-term expectations well anchored near target; short-term edging up slightly. Net: anchored but perhaps slightly below target given deflationary backdrop.
