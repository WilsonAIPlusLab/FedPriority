### Era Context
May 1997 — mid-to-late 1990s expansion. The economy had surged in late 1996/early 1997 and was now moderating. Unemployment at 4.9%, which was considered low. This is the pre-dot-com-bubble period with strong growth and surprisingly benign inflation. The Fed had just tightened slightly in March 1997.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint — subdued/contained despite tight labor.
- Evidence: "underlying price inflation was still subdued"; "price inflation remained moderate despite a gradual acceleration of labor costs"; consumer prices ex food/energy "increased by the same amount as in the previous twelve months"; producer prices "fell in both March and April"; core PPI "increased considerably less over the twelve months ended in April than over the previous twelve months." However, labor costs show "upward creep" — compensation trending higher.
- Staff forecast: "core consumer price inflation was forecast to drift slightly higher."
- Net: Current inflation subdued but labor costs creeping up and staff expects slight drift higher. This is below-target/contained territory. Score in the 38-44 range. The word "subdued" with "drift slightly higher" in the forecast suggests mild upside risk from a low base.
- Magnitude: "subdued" = meaningfully below midpoint, ~8-12 points. "Drift slightly higher" moderates the distance slightly.
- Score: 42 — inflation contained now with minor upward drift expected.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says subdued now, slightly higher later. 42 < 50 = less pressure. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees the economy as operating ABOVE potential.
- Evidence: Staff forecast says "the economy would expand in the second half of the year at a rate a little above that of its estimated potential and then would increase at a slower and more sustainable rate in 1998." Also: "With resource utilization high and labor compensation gradually accelerating..."
- Bright-line: "a rate a little above that of its estimated potential" and "resource utilization high" = MUST be > 50.
- Magnitude: "a little above" = modest, 5-8 points above midpoint. "Resource utilization high" reinforces.
- Score: 57 — modestly above potential with high resource utilization.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. "Little above potential" and "high resource utilization" = above. 57 > 50. Correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor markets as TIGHT.
- Evidence: "labor markets remained tight"; unemployment fell to 4.9%; "worker compensation appeared to be accelerating gradually"; "labor force participation rate edged down from the record high."
- The unemployment rate of 4.9% in 1997 was considered below NAIRU estimates of the time (~5.5%). Staff explicitly says "tight."
- Magnitude: "tight" is clear language. UE at 4.9% — the rubric says UE 3.8-4.5% = 66-80, UE ~4.5-5% = 46-52. But the staff explicitly says "tight" which pulls it above the pure UE-based range. Also, "growth of labor demand had moderated somewhat" tempers it.
- Score: 58 — tight labor market with UE at 4.9% and explicit staff "tight" language, plus gradual wage acceleration.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Tight" = above 50. 58 > 50. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Direction: Staff sees a MIXED picture — consumption slowing but investment strong.
- Evidence: Consumer spending "appeared to be increasing at a considerably slower pace after the spurt in the first quarter"; retail sales "unchanged in March and declined in April." But: "business fixed investment remained on a strong uptrend"; "expanded briskly in the first quarter"; "Available indicators pointed to further sizable gains." Housing "well maintained."
- Staff forecast: Consumer spending "expected to remain fairly brisk"; business spending "anticipated to continue to outpace the overall expansion."
- Net: Recent consumer data soft but temporary (after a surge); business investment very strong; housing solid. The forecast is for continued solid growth. This is above-trend territory.
- Score: 57 — consumer spending moderating from a surge but still supported; business investment brisk; net position above trend.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Evidence is for solid-to-strong net. 57 > 50. Correct.
