### Era Context
January 2024: The Fed is at or near the end of its aggressive tightening cycle that began in March 2022. Inflation has declined markedly from its 2022 peaks but remains above 2%. The labor market has been resilient, with unemployment historically low. The economy has shown surprising strength through 2023. Financial conditions have eased somewhat from their tightest point but remain restrictive. The SVB crisis of March 2023 is nearly a year past.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint but declining. PCE at 2.6%, core PCE at 2.9%, trimmed mean at 3.3% — all above the 2% target but "well below their year-earlier levels." Inflation "continued to slow." This is clearly above target but moderating. The current readings (~2.6-3.3%) place this in the "modestly above target" to "clearly above target" range. Given "continued to slow" and "declined markedly over the course of the year," this is upper end of 51-60 range. Score direction: above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as AT or SLIGHTLY ABOVE potential. GDP growth was "solid" in Q4 but "stepped down from the third quarter's strong pace." The staff forecast says "output growth in 2024 and 2025 below the staff's estimate of potential growth" and "in 2026, output was expected to rise in line with potential." This implies output is currently slightly above potential (since growth below potential is needed to bring it back to potential by 2026). The language "solid" growth supports output being near or modestly above potential. Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for the current level, but the forecast structure implies currently near or slightly above potential. Score ~52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT but EASING. "Labor market conditions continued to be tight but showed further signs of easing." Unemployment at 3.7% — well below 4%, which per the rubric should place this above 55. Job openings rate and quits rate declining, wage growth moderating. The level is still tight (3.7% unemployment) even though the direction is easing. Score: above neutral, in the 58-63 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as SOLID. "Real GDP rose at a solid pace in the fourth quarter." Private domestic final purchases "also rose solidly." Real exports "grew robustly." This is at or modestly above trend — "solid" maps to the 46-54 range per the rubric, upper end. Score ~54.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as RESTRICTIVE but easing. "Indicators of broad financial conditions eased over the period, but the staff's Financial Conditions Impulse on Growth index remained restrictive." "Financing conditions for households and businesses continued to be moderately restrictive, as borrowing costs remained elevated." Bright-line check: "restrictive" → score MUST be > 50. "Moderately restrictive" maps to ~58-62. Score: above neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined, the implied policy rate path shifted downward. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or stress. "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable." Market-based measures of interest rate uncertainty "remained highly elevated by historical standards" — but this is uncertainty, not dysfunction. No actual dysfunction described. Score: normal market functioning, ~40-45. No bright-line issue.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade bonds narrowed modestly." Yields on corporate bonds declined partly driven by "narrower spreads." However, SLOOS shows "tightening standards and terms on C&I loans to firms of all sizes" and for CRE across "all loan categories." Credit generally available but "credit availability for smaller firms continued to tighten." This is mixed: market-based spreads are narrowing (favorable) but bank lending standards are tightening. Net assessment: slightly below to near neutral. Bright-line: "narrowed" spreads → should be < 50 or near 50. Given the tightening lending standards, I'll net this at ~42.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased." Staff's financial stability assessment: "asset valuation pressures remained notable, as valuations across a range of markets appeared high relative to fundamentals." This is explicit overvaluation concern. Higher score = more stress/concern, but the rubric says overvaluation concern scores LOW (1-30 range). "Notable" valuation pressures, "high relative to fundamentals" → score in the 25-35 range. Score ~28.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff reports: "regulatory risk-based capital ratios continued to increase and indicated ample loss-bearing capacity in the banking system." However, "banks remained vulnerable to significant increases in longer-term interest rates," uninsured deposits "remained high for some banks," funding risks "characterized as notable," leverage "characterized as notable." This is a mixed picture — system sound but vulnerabilities noted. Bright-line: "ample loss-bearing capacity" suggests below 50 but the "notable" vulnerabilities push it toward neutral. Score ~42.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The exchange value of the dollar little changed." Bright-line: stable/unchanged → near 50. Score ~48.
