April 2021: The U.S. economy is in the early-to-mid recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic. Vaccines are rolling out, fiscal stimulus (American Rescue Plan) was recently enacted. GDP is recovering but has not yet returned to pre-pandemic levels. Unemployment is still elevated at 6%. Inflation is still below 2% on a 12-month basis but base effects and supply bottlenecks are expected to push it above 2% in coming months.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: Total PCE inflation 1.6% over 12 months ending February, core PCE 1.4%, both "well below 2 percent." CPI rose 2.6% in March but this was "boosted by increases in consumer energy prices." Current readings are below target, but the forward-looking language is notable — staff expects above 2% for the rest of 2021. This creates a mixed signal: current data below target, but imminent upward pressure expected. The gotcha rule says "transitory" language does NOT reduce the score — score what staff reports NOW. Current readings are below target, but the staff is clearly flagging emerging pressures. I'll weight both: current data pulls below 50, expected near-term pressures pull modestly above. Net: around 45-50.

Direction check: "Somewhat" below target currently, but "expected to move above 2 percent" with supply bottlenecks and import prices. Score ~48. This captures the transitional moment.

Bright-line: GDP has not returned to pre-pandemic level = below potential = score MUST be < 50. Score ~38-42, reflecting rapid recovery but still below potential.

At 6% UE, the rubric anchor suggests 31-45 range (UE 5.5-7%). Score ~33.

Net picture: strong consumer spending momentum (stimulus-driven), mixed investment. Score ~55 — above trend given the strong March rebound and robust consumer spending.
