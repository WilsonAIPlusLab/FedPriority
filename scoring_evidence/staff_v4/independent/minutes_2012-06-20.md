# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2012-06-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: Mid-2012. The U.S. was in a slow recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remained elevated at 8.2%. The European sovereign debt crisis was intensifying, particularly around Spain and Greece. Oil prices were declining. The Fed was conducting its maturity extension program (Operation Twist).

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Consumer prices were "flat in April and then fell in May," energy prices "declined considerably," core CPI "increased moderately," wages "subdued," unit labor costs "rose only slightly," and the staff projects "inflation would be subdued through 2014." Near-term forecast revised down. This is clearly below-target inflation with no pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. "Wide margin of slack in labor and product markets," "growth rate of real GDP would not materially exceed that of potential output until 2014," slack narrowing "only slowly." Unemployment "expected to still be elevated at the end of 2014." Bright-line: "slack remains" language requires score < 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 8.2%, "long-duration unemployment remained very high," part-time for economic reasons "little changed," job gains slowing. With UE at 8.2%, this is far from full employment. Anchor: 16-30 range for UE > 7%.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. PCE "increased solidly in the first quarter" but "nominal retail sales excluding purchases of motor vehicles declined" in April/May. Business equipment spending "increased moderately" but shipments and orders "decreased" in April. Consumer sentiment "subdued." Housing "still restrained." Government spending falling. Net assessment is below trend but not contracting.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "small gains in productivity." This suggests below-trend productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as SOMEWHAT TIGHT. "Tighter domestic financial conditions" noted in the outlook. Equity prices declined, mortgage markets "continued to be tight," CRE "remained strained." But corporate bond yields "remained low by historical standards," C&I loans increased, consumer credit expanded at "solid pace," bank lending conditions "eased slightly." Mixed but leaning tight due to European spillovers and equity declines. Bright-line: "tighter" language pushes above 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields fell to "historically low level" driven by safe-haven demand. No dysfunction described — this is orderly flight-to-quality. Repo rate "edged higher" but explained by technical supply factors, not stress. Score should be in normal range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate spreads "widened a bit" but yields "remained low by historical standards." CRE "remained strained," mortgage markets "continued to be tight." Municipal spreads rose. Some widening but from low levels — modestly above neutral.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes declined," bank stocks "significantly underperformed." No valuation commentary beyond price moves. Default to modest stress range.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Domestic banks: bank credit expanded, lending conditions "eased slightly," but bank equity prices underperformed, a "large trading loss at a major U.S. bank," expected credit rating downgrades for five large banks. European bank stress was severe but question is about U.S. banking. Risks from Europe "appeared to increase." Mixed — some stress signals but system still functioning. Bright-line: not described as "well-capitalized" or "stressed" explicitly. Moderate stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal dollar index ended the intermeeting period up moderately." Bright-line: appreciation/strengthening requires score > 50.

### Polarity verification:
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Staff says conditions tightened. Score > 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Mixed signals with some stress. Score near 50-55. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar "up moderately." Score > 50. Correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction described. Score < 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider spreads. Spreads "widened a bit" but still low. Score near 50. Correct.

### Bright-line checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP: "wide margin of slack" -> must be < 50. Will score ~30. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "tighter domestic financial conditions" -> must be > 50. Will score ~55. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "widened a bit" -> score modestly > 50. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: Not clearly "healthy" or "stressed" — mixed signals. Score ~52. Acceptable.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "up moderately" -> must be > 50. Will score ~58. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction described, just yield moves from safe-haven flows. Score ~38. PASS.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (30) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25)**: Both well below 50 — consistent with slow recovery, high unemployment, significant slack. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (30) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (38)**: Both below 50, within 10 points. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (52)**: Both modestly above 50. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (55) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (38)**: FC somewhat tight but Treasury market functioning normally (safe-haven flows, not dysfunction). This is expected in a European-spillover environment. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (30) <-> sub-dimensions**: Goods deflating (energy), services moderate, expectations stable. Consistent with ~30.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation declined, primarily reflecting reductions in the prices of crude oil and gasoline"
- "the staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2014"
- Core prices "increased moderately," wages "subdued," unit labor costs "rose only slightly"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes inflation as declining and projects it to remain subdued through 2014, with energy prices falling, stable expectations, and substantial slack keeping pressures muted.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff sees declining headline inflation, moderate core, subdued wages, and projects continued subdued inflation.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the wide margin of slack in labor and product markets" to narrow "only slowly over the projection period"
- "economic activity was expanding at a somewhat more modest pace than earlier in the year"
- GDP growth "would not materially exceed that of potential output until 2014"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees a wide output gap with the economy well below potential, growth decelerating, and slack expected to persist through 2014.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff explicitly identifies "wide margin of slack." Bright-line rule satisfied (slack = below 50).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate stood at 8.2 percent in May"
- "The rate of long-duration unemployment remained very high, and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was little changed"
- "the unemployment rate was expected to still be elevated at the end of 2014"

**Center-of-gravity**: At 8.2% unemployment with very high long-duration unemployment, the labor market has significant slack. Job gains are slowing. This is firmly in the "High unemployment; significant slack" range.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.2% with high long-duration unemployment signals substantial labor market slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "nominal retail sales excluding purchases of motor vehicles declined" in April/May
- "Real business expenditures on equipment and software increased moderately in the first quarter" but "nominal shipments and orders of nondefense capital goods excluding aircraft decreased" in April
- "Consumer sentiment was lower in early June than earlier in the year, and it continued to be subdued"

**Center-of-gravity**: First-quarter PCE was solid but recent data show weakening consumer spending, flat business investment trends, continued government drag, and subdued sentiment. Below trend but not contracting.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending momentum is slowing with declining retail sales, softening investment orders, and subdued consumer sentiment.

---

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 38 +/-3 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "with small gains in productivity, unit labor costs rose only slightly"

**Center-of-gravity**: Only a brief mention indicating modest/below-trend productivity gains. Limited textual basis.

**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly below neutral because "small gains" suggests below-trend growth.

---

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "tighter domestic financial conditions led the staff to revise down somewhat the medium-term forecast for real GDP growth"
- "Credit conditions in residential mortgage markets continued to be tight"; CRE "remained strained"
- But: corporate yields "remained low by historical standards," bank lending conditions "eased slightly," consumer credit expanded at "solid pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly characterizes conditions as having tightened, with tight mortgage and CRE markets, but corporate credit remains accessible and bank lending is easing. Net assessment is somewhat tight.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff explicitly states "tighter domestic financial conditions." Bright-line rule satisfied.

---

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Yields on longer-dated nominal and inflation-protected Treasury securities moved down substantially" — driven by safe-haven demand, not dysfunction
- "The yield on nominal 10-year Treasury securities reached a historically low level" — orderly price movement
- Repo rate "edged higher" but explained by technical supply factors

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally with orderly safe-haven flows pushing yields to record lows. No dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues described. Slightly below normal stress given the very smooth functioning implied by strong safe-haven demand.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no market dysfunction described — only orderly yield movements from safe-haven flows.

---

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate debt remained low by historical standards, but their spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened a bit"
- "Financing conditions for the commercial real estate sector remained strained"
- "credit default swap spreads for state governments generally moved higher"

**Center-of-gravity**: Corporate spreads widened modestly but remain low historically. CRE and municipal spreads are elevated. Overall a slight widening from favorable levels — near neutral with modest stress.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is approximately at neutral because spreads widened a bit but remain low by historical standards. The "widened" direction pushes slightly above 50.

---

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Broad U.S. stock price indexes declined, and option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index rose"
- "Equity prices for large domestic banks significantly underperformed the broad indexes"

**Center-of-gravity**: Equity prices declined with rising volatility, but no valuation commentary is provided. Default to modest stress for price declines without extreme language.

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral because prices declined and volatility rose, but no distressed or extreme language is used.

---

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "Investors' expectation that five large U.S. banks would have their credit ratings downgraded"
- But: "Bank credit expanded," "lending conditions again eased slightly," "signs of strains in short-term funding markets were muted"

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed signals. Some idiosyncratic stress (trading loss, expected downgrades, European exposure risks) offset by continued credit expansion and easing lending conditions. System functioning but with emerging vulnerabilities.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is approximately at neutral because negative signals (trading losses, downgrade expectations, European risks) are balanced by positive signals (credit expansion, easing lending terms, muted funding strains).
