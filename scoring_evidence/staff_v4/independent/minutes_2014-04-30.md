# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2014-04-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: April 2014 — midway through the post-GFC recovery. The Fed is tapering asset purchases. Unemployment is still elevated (~6.7%), inflation running below target (~1% PCE year-over-year), and Q1 GDP was weak partly due to severe winter weather. The economy is growing but with significant slack remaining.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE prices rose "at a slow rate" in Q1, about 1% higher than a year earlier. Core PCE around 1.25% year-over-year. Staff forecasts inflation "would remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." Longer-run inflation expectations "remained stable." This is clearly below target — score in the 30-38 range per the rubric ("below target; disinflation; running below 2 percent").

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy BELOW potential. GDP growth "paused in the first quarter," though partly weather-related. Staff projects GDP "would rise more quickly than the growth rate of potential output" — meaning currently below potential. "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack over the projection period." The phrase "resource slack" is a bright-line trigger: MUST be < 50. Score in the 35-42 range (recovery era, below potential but closing).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW full employment. Unemployment at 6.7%, "still elevated." Long-duration unemployment and part-time for economic reasons "well above pre-recession levels." Hiring rate "continued to be subdued." With UE at 6.7% and explicit slack language, score in the 31-38 range per anchors (UE 5.5-7% = 31-45).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. Real PCE "expanded slightly less rapidly." Business equipment spending "declined in the first quarter." Housing "remained soft" with residential investment decreasing. Forward-looking indicators are modestly positive (new orders above shipments, surveys consistent with increased outlays). Net: weak current data but some forward momentum. Score in the 37-43 range ("sluggish," "soft").

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees financial conditions as roughly UNCHANGED and generally accommodative. "Financial conditions did not change appreciably, on net." Credit flows "remained robust." CRE conditions "improved further." Leveraged loans at "brisk pace" with "ongoing gradual easing of credit terms." Mortgage credit "remained tight" but easing. The staff's stability report says "vulnerability of the financial system to adverse shocks remained at moderate levels." Overall, conditions are accommodative/supportive — bright-line rule: "accommodative" / "supportive" → score MUST be < 50. Score in the 30-38 range.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Short- and medium-term yields rose, long-term declined. Short-term funding markets "remained fairly stable." No mention of any dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. This is normal market functioning. Score in the 30-35 range (calm markets).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "narrowed." Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations "remained robust." Leveraged loans at "brisk pace" with easing terms. Consumer ABS conditions "remained favorable." Peripheral euro-area spreads "continued to narrow." Bright-line: "narrowed" = compressed = MUST be < 50. Score in the 25-32 range.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: S&P 500 "increased a bit." Social media and biotech stocks "fell sharply." The staff stability report "highlighted valuation pressures in some segments of the equity market." This valuation concern language pushes toward the lower end (more concern about overvaluation). Score in the 35-42 range (modestly above fair value with some concern in specific segments).

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Relatively strong capital profiles of large domestic banking firms" is a key phrase from the stability report. First-quarter earnings for large banks were "mixed." Bright-line: "strong capital" → MUST be < 50. But there are also highlighted risks (corporate debt demand, underwriting standards pressures). Score in the 30-38 range (healthy with minor concerns).

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed." The broad nominal index "was about unchanged" — dollar appreciated against some currencies, depreciated against others. Bright-line: mixed/stable → score near 50. Score in the 46-50 range.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 34 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective"
- PCE prices "were about 1 percent higher than a year earlier"; core PCE "around 1¼ percent higher than four quarters earlier"
- Staff forecast: "inflation would remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation running well below the 2% target at roughly 1-1.25% core, with staff projecting a slow return to target over several years.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because headline and core inflation are substantially below the 2% objective.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 ±5 [High confidence]

- "Growth in economic activity paused in the first quarter" (partly weather)
- "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack over the projection period"
- Staff projects GDP "would rise more quickly than the growth rate of potential output" — confirming currently below potential

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is operating below potential with significant remaining resource slack, though the staff expects gradual closing of the gap.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "resource slack" and projects growth above potential to close the gap. Bright-line rule satisfied (< 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 ±4 [High confidence]

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market shows significant remaining slack with unemployment at 6.7%, elevated underemployment, and subdued hiring, though payrolls continue to expand.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because unemployment is elevated at 6.7% with multiple indicators of labor market slack.
