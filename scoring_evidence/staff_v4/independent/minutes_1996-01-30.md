# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — January 30, 1996 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Era Context
January 1996: Mid-1990s expansion, post the 1994-95 rate hiking cycle. The Fed had just begun easing (December 1995 cut). Unemployment at 5.6%, moderate growth, contained inflation. The economy was experiencing a temporary soft patch due to government shutdowns, severe weather, and inventory correction. This is a mid-cycle period with growth near potential.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

#### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Direction**: Staff sees inflation as roughly stable, slightly below what would be concerning — at midpoint or modestly below.
**Evidence**: "Recent data suggested little change in underlying inflation trends." Consumer prices "increased slightly in December," core CPI for 1995 "advanced slightly more than in 1994." PPI of finished goods other than food and energy "rose at a subdued pace." Average hourly earnings "had been trending up over the past several years." The staff forecast says "rates of utilization of labor and capital resources and of inflation were not expected to change materially."
**Magnitude**: "No clear change in underlying inflation trends" = neutral. "Subdued pace" on core PPI = modestly below. Hourly earnings "trending up" = mild upward pressure. CPI was running roughly 2.5-3% (CPI basis, not PCE), which was considered contained for the era. Net: roughly neutral with contained tone.
**Self-check**: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff language is "little change," "subdued," "no firm evidence of a change." This is slightly below the alarm zone — near neutral or modestly contained. Score: 42.
**Polarity check**: 42 < 50 = below-neutral inflation pressure. Consistent with "subdued," "contained," "no clear change." Confirmed.

#### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Direction**: Staff sees the economy slightly below potential in the near term, then returning to potential.
**Evidence**: Staff forecast: "economic activity would expand at a relatively slow pace over the near term" with "slightly weaker outlook for the current quarter" due to inventory correction and weather. "Over the remainder of the two-year forecast horizon, the economy was expected to grow generally along its estimated potential." "Utilization of total industrial capacity fell slightly but remained at a moderately elevated level."
**Bright-line check**: "grow generally along its estimated potential" = at potential over the medium term. Near-term weakness due to transitory factors. Capacity utilization "remained at a moderately elevated level" suggests near or slightly above potential for the broader economy. But the near-term soft patch with inventory correction and weather pulls it slightly below. Net: near potential with slight near-term weakness.
**Score**: 47. Slightly below potential in the near term but capacity utilization still elevated.
**Polarity check**: 47 < 50 = slightly below potential. Consistent with "relatively slow pace" near term but "along its estimated potential" over the forecast. Confirmed.

#### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Direction**: Staff sees a moderately tight labor market — not extreme but at relatively low unemployment.
**Evidence**: "The demand for labor was still growing at a moderate pace" and "the unemployment rate remained relatively low" at 5.6%. "Nonfarm payroll employment continued to expand moderately in December." The staff forecast projected "rates of utilization of labor and capital resources... were not expected to change materially."
**Magnitude**: UE at 5.6% in 1996 — NAIRU was estimated around 5.5-6.0% at the time. This suggests near full employment. "Remained relatively low" confirms the staff saw the labor market as reasonably tight. Employment growth "moderate."
**Score**: 50. Near NAIRU, balanced labor market. 5.6% unemployment was considered roughly at the natural rate for this era.
**Polarity check**: 50 = balanced, near NAIRU. UE at 5.6% with "relatively low" characterization. This fits the 46-52 range. Confirmed.

#### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Direction**: Staff sees spending as below trend in the near term.
**Evidence**: "Consumer spending had expanded modestly on balance." "Growth of consumer spending was modest." "Retail sales continued to grow at a relatively modest rate in December, and the fourth-quarter increase was considerably smaller than those of the previous two quarters." "Business fixed investment... suggested a moderation recently in the expansion of business spending on capital goods." "Housing starts rebounded in November." Staff forecast: "Consumer spending was anticipated to keep pace with the growth of disposable income." "Business investment in new equipment and structures was expected to expand at a more moderate rate." "Utilization of existing capacity having eased considerably."
**Magnitude**: "Modest" consumer spending + "moderation" in business investment + inventory correction = below trend. But not weak — housing favorable, consumer fundamentals supported. Score: 40.
**Polarity check**: 40 < 50 = below trend. Consistent with "modest," "moderation," "relatively slow pace." Confirmed.

#### 5. PRODUCTIVITY

**Score**: N/A. No specific staff discussion of productivity trends.

#### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Direction**: Staff sees conditions as accommodative/supportive.
**Evidence**: "Most market interest rates had declined somewhat further over the period." "Bond yields down on balance, and occasionally approaching two-year lows." "Major indexes of equity prices advanced sharply further." Staff forecast: "still-ample availability of credit and the substantial rise in the value of household equity holdings would support further increases in consumption." "The further decline in mortgage rates recently from already-favorable levels would help to sustain homebuilding." "Conditions in mortgage markets remained quite favorable."
**Bright-line check**: "ample availability of credit," "favorable" mortgage conditions, declining rates, rising equities = accommodative/supportive. Score MUST be < 50.
**Score**: 30. Clearly accommodative — declining rates, ample credit, rising equity prices, favorable mortgage conditions.
**Polarity check**: 30 < 50 = loose conditions. Consistent with "ample," "favorable," "declined." Confirmed.

#### 7. TREASURY_STRESS

**Evidence**: "Most market interest rates had declined somewhat further." Bond yields approaching two-year lows. This is orderly market behavior.
**Score**: N/A. No dysfunction described. Yield movements are normal.

#### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Direction**: Staff sees credit conditions as favorable/easy.
**Evidence**: "Still-ample availability of credit." "Conditions in mortgage markets remained quite favorable." "Growth of total domestic nonfinancial debt had been moderate." "Indications that lenders were no longer easing their terms and conditions for granting credit to consumers and businesses" (from the staff projection on debt) — this suggests the easing had been happening and was just leveling off, meaning conditions remained loose. Consumer debt burdens rising and "rising delinquency rates on some types of loans" are data points, but staff characterizes credit as "ample."
**Bright-line check**: "ample availability of credit" = favorable. Score MUST be < 50.
**Score**: 35. Credit conditions generally favorable and accommodative, though there's a hint of lenders starting to tighten at the margin.
**Polarity check**: 35 < 50 = below-normal stress. Consistent with "ample" credit. Confirmed.

#### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION

**Evidence**: "Major indexes of equity prices advanced sharply further." No staff language on valuation levels, overvaluation, or concern.
**Score**: N/A. Staff reports price movement only, no valuation assessment. Per the rubric, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55, but since there is no staff valuation discussion at all, N/A is more appropriate for this pre-2005 document.

#### 10. BANKING_STRESS

**Score**: N/A. No specific staff discussion of banking system health/stress.

**Polarity check**: 60 > 50 = dollar strength. Consistent with "continued to rise," "appreciation." Confirmed.
