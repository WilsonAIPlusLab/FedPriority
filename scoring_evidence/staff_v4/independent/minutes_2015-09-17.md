# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2015-09-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: September 2015. The economy is in the later stage of the post-GFC recovery. Unemployment has fallen to 5.1%, near estimates of the natural rate. Inflation remains persistently below 2%, pulled down by energy prices and dollar appreciation. The key question is the timing of the first rate hike ("liftoff"). Global concerns centered on China are creating financial market volatility.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Core PCE at 1.25% over 12 months, total PCE "edged up" (near zero). Energy price declines and dollar appreciation are restraining inflation. Staff projects inflation rising "gradually" but still "slightly below" 2% at end of 2018. This is clearly below target — around 30-35 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the output gap as slightly BELOW potential now but CLOSING. Key language: "real GDP was expanding at a moderate pace." The staff outlook says "the level of GDP was anticipated to rise above its potential next year" — meaning it is currently below potential. Bright-line rule: below potential → must be < 50. But it's close to potential and closing, so 44-47 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor as moderately TIGHT. Unemployment at 5.1% in August, down from 5.3% in July. Job openings at "a high level." Payroll employment expanding at "a solid pace." But: "labor compensation gains were modest," part-time for economic reasons "remained elevated." With UE at 5.1%, near NAIRU estimates, and staff projecting UE to run "a little below" the natural rate, this is near full employment. Score around 48-52. The rubric says UE ~4.5-5% is near NAIRU (46-52). At 5.1% with modest wage growth, score ~48.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as moderate/mixed. Consumer spending "rising at a moderate pace" with "strong rate" in retail sales. Housing on "gradual upward trend." Business equipment "rising moderately." But: manufacturing output "little changed," drilling/mining spending fell "steeply." Government purchases "declining slightly." Net assessment is moderate — around 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff "further trimmed its assumptions for the rates of increase in productivity and potential output over the medium term." This indicates below-trend productivity. Score ~35.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "domestic financial conditions tightened modestly" due to global growth concerns centered on China. "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses tightened modestly over the summer." Consumer credit "remained generally accommodative." Mortgage conditions "continued to improve slowly" but tight for some borrowers. Net: modestly tightened from an accommodative baseline. Bright-line: "tightened" → should be > 50 or at least near 50. The language is "tightened modestly" from what was presumably accommodative, so conditions are now closer to neutral. Score ~50-53.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved up "modestly" with some noting foreign government sales of Treasuries. No language about dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in the Treasury market itself. Normal functioning. Score ~40-45. Actually, the broader equity volatility reached "levels last seen in 2011" and there was general "turbulence in financial markets," but Treasury-specific stress is not indicated — yields moved orderly. Score ~40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened slightly." CMBS spreads "widened noticeably." C&I loan growth slowed. But consumer credit remains accommodative, CRE financing "broadly available." Net: modest widening from generally favorable levels. Score ~45-50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes... posted net declines." "Investors may also have reassessed valuations and risk in equity markets." Volatility reached levels "last seen in 2011." Stock indexes "sharply lower." Staff notes possible valuation reassessment. Score ~55-58 (modest stress/concern, not extreme).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed. C&I loan growth slowed concentrated in oil/gas exposed banks, but no discussion of bank health, capital, or systemic stress. Score N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The exchange value of the U.S. dollar rose notably over the period against the currencies of most major U.S. trading partners." "Strongly appreciated against the currencies of most emerging market economies." Dollar appreciation is also cited as restraining inflation and import prices. Bright-line: appreciation → > 50. Score ~65-70.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declining, non-energy import prices declining. Goods prices clearly subdued. Score ~25-30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity on "gradual upward trend" but no specific discussion of shelter/rent inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "remained stable." Market-based measures "fell to near their historical lows." Mixed signals — surveys anchored, market measures very low. Score ~40.

### Bright-line checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP: Below potential → score < 50. ✓ (46)
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "Tightened modestly" → score ≥ 50. ✓ (52)
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "Widened slightly" → score ≥ 50. ✓ (50)
- BANKING_STRESS: N/A ✓
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "Rose notably" / "appreciated" → score > 50. ✓ (67)
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction language → score 36-50. ✓ (40)

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (46) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Close together, both near neutral. Coherent — economy near potential with labor near full employment. ✓
2. OUTPUT_GAP (46) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (50): Within 5 points. Coherent. ✓
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (52) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (50): Aligned, both near neutral with slight tightening. ✓
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (52) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (40): FC slightly tight, Treasury normal. Coherent for non-crisis period. ✓

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation remained below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent and was restrained by further declines in energy prices and non-energy import prices." "Core PCE prices... increased 1¼ percent over the same period." Staff projects inflation "would still be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent at the end of 2018."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running well below target at 1.25% core, with downward pressures from energy and dollar, and staff not projecting return to 2% until after 2018.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is persistently below target with multiple restraining forces.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 46 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real gross domestic product (GDP) was expanding at a moderate pace in the third quarter." "The level of GDP was anticipated to rise above its potential next year" (implying currently below). Staff "lowered its projection for economic growth over the next several years."
**Center-of-gravity**: GDP is slightly below potential and closing, with moderate growth. Staff expects crossover to above potential next year.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because GDP has not yet reached potential. Bright-line satisfied: below potential → < 50.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate... fell to 5.1 percent in August." "Labor market conditions continued to improve, but labor compensation gains were modest." "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons remained elevated." "The unemployment rate was projected to run a little below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment at 5.1% UE, but residual slack (elevated part-time, modest wages) keeps score just below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is approximately at the midpoint — UE near NAIRU with mixed signals on slack vs. tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) appeared to be rising at a moderate pace." Retail sales "increased at a strong rate." But manufacturing output "would be little changed," drilling spending "fell steeply," and government purchases "appeared to be declining slightly."
**Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending offset by weak manufacturing, declining energy investment, and flat government spending nets to roughly trend growth.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral — moderate overall with offsetting sectors.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff also further trimmed its assumptions for the rates of increase in productivity and potential output over the medium term."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff is revising productivity assumptions downward, indicating below-trend productivity growth.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because staff is actively lowering its productivity estimates.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial conditions tightened modestly as concerns about prospects for global economic growth, centered on China, prompted an increase in financial market volatility and a deterioration in risk sentiment." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses tightened modestly over the summer."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions tightened modestly from a generally accommodative baseline, landing near neutral with a slight tilt toward tighter.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is slightly above neutral because staff explicitly uses "tightened" language. Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities moved up modestly" with some citing "purported sales of Treasury securities by foreign government authorities." No language about dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in Treasuries.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning normally; yield movements reflect policy expectations and foreign flows, not dysfunction.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral — no dysfunction indicators. Yield movements alone are not stress per bright-line rule.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated and speculative-grade corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened slightly." "Spreads on investment-grade CMBS widened noticeably in August." But consumer credit "remained generally accommodative" and CRE financing "broadly available."
**Center-of-gravity**: Slight widening in corporate and CMBS spreads from previously favorable levels, partially offset by continued consumer credit ease. Roughly normal levels.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is at neutral — slight widening brings spreads closer to normal from previously favorable.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 57 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes... posted net declines." "Investors may also have reassessed valuations and risk in equity markets." "Domestic equity indexes were quite volatile in late August and early September, and one-month-ahead option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index reached levels last seen in 2011."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equity prices declined with notable volatility and possible valuation reassessment, indicating modestly elevated concern — beyond routine fluctuation but not distressed.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral because staff describes sharp declines, high volatility, and possible reassessment of valuations.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No direct discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or systemic stress. C&I loan growth slowdown at oil/gas-exposed banks is a lending conditions observation, not a banking stress assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A — not discussed.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 67 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The exchange value of the U.S. dollar rose notably over the period against the currencies of most major U.S. trading partners." "The dollar also strongly appreciated against the currencies of most emerging market economies." Staff outlook cites "the foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated further" as a factor weighing on growth.
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad, notable dollar appreciation against both advanced and emerging market currencies, weighing on exports and import prices.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated notably. Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 27 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Restrained by further declines in energy prices and non-energy import prices." "Crude oil prices declined further." "The effects of recent dollar appreciation and lower commodity prices were anticipated to push down non-oil import prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices declining due to energy, commodity, and import price pressures — well below stable.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — goods prices actively falling.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
No separate discussion of services inflation.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
No discussion of shelter/rent inflation specifically.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation fell to near their historical lows."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations anchored, but market-based measures at historical lows create a below-neutral composite — some risk of downward drift.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — market measures at historical lows outweigh stable surveys for a modestly below-midpoint score.
