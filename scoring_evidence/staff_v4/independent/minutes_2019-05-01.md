# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-05-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly delineated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." I will score only staff-attributable content from these sections.

### Era Context
May 2019: Late-expansion period. The Fed had paused its hiking cycle and adopted a "patient" approach. Unemployment was near 50-year lows (~3.8%), but inflation was persistently below 2%. Trade tensions with China were a background risk. Financial conditions had eased substantially after the Q4 2018 tightening episode.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.5% over 12 months, core PCE at 1.6% — both below the 2% target. The staff describes this as "somewhat below 2 percent." The trimmed mean was 2.0%, providing some offset. Staff forecast was revised down slightly and core PCE expected to "run just below 2 percent over the medium term." Longer-run expectations "little changed." This is classic 2013-19 below-target territory. Direction: below 50.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. Critical language: "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020" and the economy was "operating notably above potential for an extended period." Unemployment projected to "decline a little further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Bright-line rule: "above potential" -> MUST be > 50. Direction: above 50.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 3.8% (well below 4%), "labor market conditions remained strong," "strong gain" in payrolls, job openings at "elevated level," quits at "high level," initial claims "near historically low levels." Wage growth at 3.2% AHE (somewhat faster than a year earlier). Staff explicitly says "labor market conditions still judged to be tight." With UE at 3.8%, this should be in the 58-65 range per anchors (55-62 for 2018-19). Direction: well above 50.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending as MIXED — consumer spending slowed but with improvement toward end of quarter; business investment slowed; residential investment still declining but at a slower rate. Key: "household spending and business fixed investment rose more slowly in the first quarter than in the fourth quarter." But fundamentals are "supportive of solid near-term gains." Forward-looking business indicators "pointed to sluggish increases." GDP growth was "solid" overall but partly driven by inventories and net exports. This nets out to roughly at-trend, maybe slightly below. Direction: near 50, slightly below.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not specifically discussed. Direction: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/LOOSE. "Financing conditions for businesses and households also eased." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained generally accommodative." CRE conditions "remained accommodative." Residential mortgage market "remained supportive." Consumer credit "broadly supportive." SLOOS reported easing of C&I loan terms. Bright-line rule: "accommodative"/"supportive"/"eased" -> MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields "declined modestly, on net." Short-term funding markets "remained stable." The EFFR rose above IOER after tax date but this is described as a normal seasonal dynamic. No language about dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress. This is normal functioning. Direction: near 40-45 (normal, no dysfunction).

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff sees spreads as COMPRESSED/FAVORABLE. "Corporate bond spreads narrowed," "spreads over yields of comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds was strong against a backdrop of narrower corporate spreads." SLOOS reported "narrowing loan rate spreads." In leveraged loans, "spreads remained somewhat elevated." But overall direction is clearly compressed. Bright-line: "narrowed" -> MUST be < 50. Direction: below 50.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff explicitly discusses valuations: "asset valuation pressures in equity and corporate debt markets to have increased significantly this year, though not quite to the elevated levels that prevailed for much of last year." S&P 500 returned to pre-Q4 2018 decline levels. VIX at low level. This is staff expressing some valuation concern (elevated but not extreme). "Valuation pressures increased significantly" leans toward overvaluation concern. Direction: below 50 (more overvaluation concern = lower score per rubric where higher = more equity stress/distress). Wait — let me re-check polarity. Higher = MORE equity market stress/concern. The rubric says 1-15 = extreme overvaluation concern ("bubble"), 16-30 = elevated valuations ("stretched"). Staff says valuation pressures "increased significantly" — this is overvaluation concern, which maps to the LOW end of the scale (15-30). Direction: below 50, in the 25-35 range.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff notes bank stock prices declined on yield curve concerns but retraced on strong earnings. Lending standards eased for C&I loans. CRE standards tightened but this was specific. Financial leverage risks viewed as low. Vulnerabilities from maturity transformation "low." But staff notes nonfinancial business debt near historical highs and growing bank lending to nonbank firms as interconnectedness risk. Overall: system healthy with some pockets of concern. Bright-line: no "stressed" or "troubled" language; lending is growing, conditions accommodative. Direction: below 50 (low stress).

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"The broad dollar index increased modestly." Bright-line rule: appreciation -> MUST be > 50. Direction: modestly above 50.
