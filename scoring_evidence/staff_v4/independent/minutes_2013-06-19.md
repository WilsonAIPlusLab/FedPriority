# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-06-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2013. The economy is in a slow recovery from the GFC. Unemployment is still elevated at 7.6%. The "taper tantrum" is underway — markets are reacting to Fed communications about potentially reducing asset purchases. Inflation is running below target. Fiscal policy (sequestration) is acting as a drag.

### Directional signals for each dimension:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Evidence: "Consumer price inflation was subdued, partly reflecting transitory influences." PCE price index "edged down in April," CPI and PCE "increased at a subdued rate over the most recent 12-month period." Core prices "only edged up." Staff forecast revised down, inflation "projected to still be relatively subdued through 2015." Wage gains "remained modest." This is clearly below-target inflation. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. Score ~35, given "subdued" language and downward revision.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW potential. Evidence: "economic activity continued to increase at a moderate rate," "pace of expansion in real GDP would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output," "slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets over the projection period." Bright-line: slack remains = MUST be < 50. The economy is growing but still below potential with slack. Score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as LOOSE. Unemployment at 7.6% (anchor: 16-30 for UE > 7%). Long-duration unemployment and part-time for economic reasons "remained well above their prerecession levels." Forward indicators "mixed." The labor force participation rate was still declining. This is firmly in the slack territory. Score ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed but moderately positive. Consumer spending: real PCE rose in April, retail sales "increased briskly," motor vehicle sales "moved up solidly," consumer sentiment "most upbeat level since the onset of the recession." But business investment: "appeared to slow somewhat," forward indicators "suggested that outlays for business equipment would continue to rise at only a modest pace." Manufacturing "at subdued levels." Net: consumer spending improving, business investment sluggish. Anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate." Score ~42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "a small rise in productivity" in nonfarm business sector, "unit labor costs advanced only a little." Very limited discussion. This suggests modest/low productivity growth. Score ~40, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes volatile markets with tightening pressures. Treasury yields "rose sharply," MBS spreads "notably wider," corporate bond yields "rose significantly," mortgage rates increased. But funding markets "generally little changed," credit flows "remained strong," leveraged loan issuance "remained robust." Overall financial stability risks "roughly unchanged since March." This is a mixed picture — conditions were accommodative but tightening from the taper tantrum. The staff notes "further improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" expected to support growth — implying conditions are still broadly supportive but less so. Score ~45 — slightly below neutral, still somewhat accommodative but tightening.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields rose sharply (35-55 bps on 5-30yr), but this was driven by policy expectations and term premium reassessment — NOT market dysfunction. Funding markets were "generally little changed." No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues. Per bright-line rule: rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 45-55). But there was "increased uncertainty" and volatility. Score ~48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "edged higher on net." But credit flows "remained strong," speculative-grade issuance was "particularly elevated" early in period (slowed later), leveraged loans "remained robust," consumer credit "continued to expand at a solid pace." Spreads edged wider but from favorable levels. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 generally favorable. Score ~38.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "Signs of upward pressures on the valuations of some risky assets were also noted." Banks outperformed. No mention of equity market stress or sharp corrections (except Japan, which is foreign). Staff seems mildly concerned about reach-for-yield/overvaluation. Score ~38 — modestly below neutral, tilted toward elevated valuations/mild concern about overvaluation.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff is quite positive: "market-based measures of the health of the banking sector... pointed to an improvement in the stability of the banking sector," "rising levels of liquidity and capital," "diminished concerns about downside risks," "further reductions in nonperforming loans." But notes "modest increase in risk-taking and leverage" through shadow banking. Bright-line: healthy/improvement = MUST be < 50. Score ~30.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on average, relative to the currencies of the advanced foreign economies, but appreciated against EME currencies." Staff outlook includes "higher projected paths for... the foreign exchange value of the dollar." Mixed but slight appreciation. Bright-line: mixed with some appreciation = slightly above 50. Score ~52.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed in a way that distinguishes goods vs services. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "continued to rise rapidly through April" (5-12% over 12 months), but this is asset prices not shelter CPI. The Medicare price reduction is mentioned as transitory. Not enough to score shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." Michigan survey near-term "little changed," longer-term "essentially flat and remained within the narrow range." Market-based inflation compensation "fell notably but ended... within their ranges over the past few years." Well-anchored. Score ~45 — anchored but slightly below target given compensation decline.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence = subdued. Score 35 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence = slack remains. Score 38 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = 7.6% UE, elevated slack. Score 30 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = still supportive but tightening. Score 45 < 50. Correct. Bright-line: conditions still broadly accommodative/supportive = below 50. Satisfied.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress. Evidence = edged wider but still favorable/strong flows. Score 38 < 50. Correct. Bright-line: strong issuance, generally favorable = below 50. Satisfied.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence = improving, well-capitalized. Score 30 < 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence = little changed vs advanced, appreciated vs EME. Score 52 > 50 marginally. Bright-line: mixed with slight appreciation. Satisfied.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Evidence = yields rose but no dysfunction. Score 48. Bright-line: normal price adjustment = 45-55. Satisfied.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
1. OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30): 8-point gap, both below 50, directionally aligned. Labor slightly lower reflecting the 7.6% UE rate anchoring it further down. Coherent.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (38) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42): 4-point gap. Very close, both below 50. Coherent.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (45) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (38): 7-point gap. Both below 50. FC slightly higher reflecting taper tantrum tightening while credit flows still strong. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation was subdued, partly reflecting transitory influences." "Both the CPI and the PCE price index increased at a subdued rate over the most recent 12-month period." Staff forecast: "inflation was projected to still be relatively subdued through 2015."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly sees inflation running below target with subdued readings and only gradual pickup expected.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is subdued and below target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "the pace of expansion in real GDP would only moderately exceed the growth rate of potential output"; "slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets over the projection period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing modestly above potential growth rate but still has meaningful slack in labor and product markets.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because slack remains. Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 7.6 percent in May"; "The rate of long-duration unemployment declined slightly, while the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was little changed; both of these measures remained well above their prerecession levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has significant slack with unemployment well above full employment and multiple measures of underemployment still elevated.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because of elevated unemployment and labor slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: Consumer side: "nominal retail sales, excluding those at motor vehicle and parts outlets, increased briskly, while light motor vehicle sales moved up solidly." Business side: "Real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to slow somewhat"; "outlays for business equipment would continue to rise at only a modest pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showing solid improvement but business investment sluggish; net assessment is below trend but improving.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral given the mixed consumer strength vs business investment weakness.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 40 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "with a small rise in productivity, unit labor costs advanced only a little."
**Center-of-gravity**: Brief mention suggests modest/below-trend productivity growth.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly below neutral — "small rise" suggests below-trend growth.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal yields on Treasury securities rose sharply over the intermeeting period"; "credit flows to nonfinancial businesses remained strong in May, especially through bond issuance." Staff outlook references "further improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" supporting growth.
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions were tightening due to taper tantrum but still broadly accommodative — credit flows strong, funding markets stable.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is slightly below neutral (still accommodative/supportive). Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 48 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal yields on 5- to 30-year Treasury securities increased about 35 to 55 basis points." "Conditions in domestic and offshore dollar funding markets were generally little changed, on balance." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or settlement issues.
**Center-of-gravity**: Sharp yield increases driven by policy expectations — normal market repricing, not dysfunction. Some elevated volatility but orderly.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near neutral — yield moves are policy-driven, not dysfunction. Bright-line satisfied.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "spreads relative to comparable-maturity Treasury yields edged higher on net"; "Credit flows to nonfinancial businesses remained strong"; "issuance of syndicated leveraged loans remained robust in April and May."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened marginally but remained at favorable levels with strong credit flows.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads only edged wider from favorable levels and credit availability remained strong. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Signs of upward pressures on the valuations of some risky assets were also noted." Bank equities outperformed broad indexes.
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff expresses mild concern about elevated valuations/reach-for-yield rather than equity market stress.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (toward overvaluation concern side) — staff flags "upward pressures on valuations."

### BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Most market-based measures of the health of the banking sector... pointed to an improvement in the stability of the banking sector, in part because of rising levels of liquidity and capital as well as diminished concerns about downside risks." "further reductions in nonperforming loans."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking sector clearly improving with better capital, liquidity, and declining NPLs, though some shadow banking risk-taking noted.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are healthier and more stable. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed, on average, relative to the currencies of the advanced foreign economies, but appreciated against EME currencies." Staff outlook includes "higher projected paths for... the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar broadly stable vs. advanced economies but strengthening against EMEs; staff projects further appreciation.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly above neutral given mixed-to-appreciating signal. Bright-line: slight appreciation = above 50. Satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Home prices discussed as asset prices, not shelter CPI component.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"; "longer-term inflation expectations in the survey also were essentially flat and remained within the narrow range." "Measures of inflation compensation... fell notably but ended the intermeeting period within their ranges over the past few years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored but market-based compensation declined notably, suggesting slight downside drift.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is slightly below neutral — anchored but with some downward pressure from declining compensation measures.
