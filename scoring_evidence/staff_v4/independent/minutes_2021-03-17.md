# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2021-03-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains clearly delineated sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
March 2021: The U.S. economy is recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic recession. Vaccines are rolling out, the American Rescue Plan (ARP) was just enacted providing major fiscal stimulus. The economy is still below pre-pandemic levels but recovering rapidly. Inflation is still below 2% but base effects and supply constraints are expected to push it temporarily above 2%. Treasury yields are rising sharply on improved outlook and fiscal expectations.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.5%, core PCE at 1.5%, well below 2% target. Staff expects transitory moves above 2% from base effects and bottlenecks but currently "well below 2 percent." However, staff notes upside inflation risks have increased and are now "balanced." Current reading is clearly below target, but near-term trajectory is upward. Score should reflect the current below-target reading with the acknowledged upward pressures. Direction: below neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW midpoint. "The level of real GDP had likely not yet returned to the level seen before the onset of the pandemic." Employment "still well below its level at the start of 2020." There is explicit "slack in resource utilization." However, the recovery is accelerating — GDP growth is "substantially" stronger, projection is "considerably stronger." Bright-line: slack remains → must be < 50. The economy is recovering fast but still below pre-pandemic output. Score in the 35-42 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW midpoint. Unemployment at 6.2%, employment "still well below its level at the start of 2020," payroll employment "retraced somewhat more than half of the losses." Wage measures (ECI 2.6%, median wage 3.2%) are "rising more slowly than their pre-pandemic pace." The compositional effects on average hourly earnings are "not indicative of tight labor market conditions." With UE at 6.2%, this is clearly in the slack range (16-30 per rubric for UE > 7%, 31-45 for 5.5-7%).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as ABOVE midpoint. Consumer spending "increasing in the first quarter at a pace considerably faster" than Q4. Business equipment investment "continuing to increase." Housing "well above pre-pandemic levels." However, nonresidential structures "remain weak." Net: strong consumer spending + solid equipment investment + strong housing - weak structures = above trend.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes conditions as mixed but broadly accommodative. "Financing conditions for businesses with access to capital markets and households with high credit scores remained broadly accommodative, but conditions stayed tight for other borrowers." Capital markets accommodative, small business conditions tight. Bright-line: "accommodative" → must be < 50. Score in the 30-40 range reflecting broadly accommodative but with pockets of tightness.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes a specific episode of stress: "On February 25, yields rose especially sharply. Market depth became thin, and bid-ask spreads widened, but Treasury market liquidity gradually recovered over the following days." This is actual market dysfunction (thin market depth, widened bid-ask spreads) but it was temporary and recovered. This is above normal but not severe. Score in the 52-58 range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as below normal (low stress). "Spreads on high-yield corporate bonds narrowed." "Corporate bond defaults declined... substantially below their 2019 average." Leveraged loan "primary-market spreads compressed notably." Investment-grade spreads roughly unchanged, speculative-grade narrowed. Bright-line: narrowed/compressed → must be < 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff mentions "high equity valuations" and "broad equity price indexes rose on net." "Stocks in cyclically sensitive industries... recorded sizable gains." Staff references "high equity valuations" as supporting financing conditions — this is more of a factual statement about elevated valuations. Score in the 25-35 range (elevated valuations, modestly stretched).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed in terms of banking system health/stress. There are mentions of bank lending (C&I loans declining, CRE loan growth weak, tight bank lending standards), but these reflect demand-side weakness and some caution rather than banking stress per se. The banking sector stocks recorded "sizable gains." Limited direct evidence. Score in the 35-42 range — some tightness in bank lending but not indicative of stress. Bright-line: no "well-capitalized" or "stressed" language specifically, but tight lending standards suggest mild caution, not stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index rose modestly, with more notable appreciation against currencies of emerging market economies." Bright-line: dollar appreciation → must be > 50. "Rose modestly" = modest strength. Score 53-57.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Some discussion of goods prices via supply constraints and bottlenecks ("production bottlenecks and supply constraints," semiconductor shortage). Rising energy and oil prices mentioned. Some upward pressure but current inflation still low. Score around 45-50 — pressures building but not yet manifested in data.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Services not specifically broken out. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Home prices continued to rise briskly." This is asset prices, not directly shelter CPI, but it's the closest reference. Score around 55-60 given brisk home price increases.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10 year same as previous months. NY Fed 3-year "essentially unchanged." SPF "edged up... returning to its pre-pandemic level." Staff index "about the same level." These are well-anchored near target. Score around 48-52.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32)**: Both below 50, directionally coherent. 6-point gap is reasonable — labor recovery lagging output recovery.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (38) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (58)**: 20-point divergence. Explanation: Spending is surging (fiscal stimulus) but output level hasn't yet returned to pre-pandemic — the flow is strong but the stock/level is still below. This divergence is justified by the rapid recovery trajectory.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (28)**: Both below 50, coherent. Spreads more compressed than overall conditions because of pockets of tightness for small business/nonprime borrowers.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (54)**: Slight divergence — financial conditions accommodative but Treasury market had a brief stress episode. This is consistent with the rubric note that in normal times Treasury stress can diverge from FC.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (37) ↔ sub-dimensions**: Goods ~48, Housing ~57, Expectations ~50. Parent at 37 reflects the currently-measured below-target inflation. Sub-dimensions capture some forward-looking pressures. The parent score reflects the dominant staff message that inflation is "well below 2 percent."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation through January—as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE)—remained well below 2 percent." "Total PCE price inflation was 1.5 percent over the 12 months ending in January." Staff projects inflation will "transitorily move above 2 percent in coming months" due to base effects and bottlenecks.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running well below target at 1.5% PCE, but upward pressures from base effects, bottlenecks, and fiscal stimulus are building.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because current readings are well below the 2% target, consistent with the 26-40 range for "below target; contained."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The level of real GDP had likely not yet returned to the level seen before the onset of the pandemic." "Continued to be held down by slack in resource utilization." However, "Real GDP growth was projected to be substantial this year."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy is still below pre-pandemic output with slack in resource utilization, but recovering rapidly with a considerably stronger projection.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because GDP has not returned to pre-pandemic levels and slack remains. Bright-line satisfied: "slack" → < 50. ✓

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 ±4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate fell to 6.2 percent in February." "Employment was still well below its level at the start of 2020." Wage measures "not indicative of tight labor market conditions" and "rising more slowly than their pre-pandemic pace."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has substantial slack with UE at 6.2%, employment well below pre-pandemic, and wage growth subdued when adjusted for composition effects.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because of high unemployment and explicit statement that conditions are "not indicative of tight labor market conditions."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending appeared to be increasing in the first quarter at a pace considerably faster, on balance, than in the fourth quarter." "Business investment in equipment and intangibles was continuing to increase." "Construction of single-family homes and home sales remained well above their pre-pandemic levels."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending surging on fiscal stimulus, business equipment investment growing, housing strong, though nonresidential structures remain weak.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because spending is accelerating sharply, supported by fiscal stimulus and improving fundamentals.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- **Evidence**: No specific discussion of productivity trends.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financing conditions for businesses with access to capital markets and households with high credit scores remained broadly accommodative, but conditions stayed tight for other borrowers." "Financing conditions for small businesses remained tight."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Overall financial conditions broadly accommodative, with low rates and supportive capital markets, but tighter for small businesses and lower-credit borrowers.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (= looser) because "broadly accommodative" is the dominant characterization. Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative" → < 50. ✓

**Score: 54 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "On February 25, yields rose especially sharply. Market depth became thin, and bid-ask spreads widened, but Treasury market liquidity gradually recovered over the following days." "Swaption-implied volatilities of longer-term interest rates rose notably."
- **Center-of-gravity**: A brief episode of actual market dysfunction (thin depth, widened bid-ask) occurred on Feb 25 but resolved quickly. This is above normal functioning but not sustained stress.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral because of documented market dysfunction (thin depth, widened bid-ask spreads), though it was temporary. This is genuine dysfunction, not mere yield movements. ✓

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 28 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads on high-yield corporate bonds narrowed." "Primary-market spreads compressed notably since the end of last year." "Corporate bond defaults declined in December and January and reached levels substantially below their 2019 average."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads are narrowing/compressed across speculative-grade and leveraged loan markets, with improving credit quality and declining defaults.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (= less stress) because spreads are narrowing and compressing. Bright-line satisfied: "narrowed"/"compressed" → < 50. ✓

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes increased, on net." Staff references "high equity valuations" as a feature of the current environment. VIX "moved lower on balance."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities are rising with elevated valuations noted by staff, suggesting stretched but not bubble-level concern.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION higher = more stress/concern. Staff notes "high equity valuations" as a backdrop but not in an alarmed tone. Score in the elevated-valuation range (below 50 = overvaluation side of scale). ✓

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 38 ±8 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Commercial and industrial loans outstanding on banks' balance sheets continued to decline through February as loan paydowns persisted and demand for new bank credit reportedly remained subdued." "CRE loan growth at banks was weak, on net, through February, consistent with tight bank lending standards."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Bank lending is weak with tight standards in some areas, but this appears demand-driven rather than indicative of banking system stress. No mention of capital adequacy concerns.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because there is no indication of systemic banking stress; tight lending standards appear precautionary, and banking stocks recorded "sizable gains." ✓

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 55 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

- **Center-of-gravity**: The dollar strengthened modestly over the intermeeting period, more so against EME currencies.
