### Era Context
June 2008 — the U.S. economy is in the early-to-middle stages of the Great Financial Crisis. The housing market is in steep decline, labor markets are weakening, financial markets are under strain but not yet at the September 2008 peak crisis. Oil and commodity prices are surging, creating a stagflationary environment. The fed funds rate is at 2%.

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
Key staff sections:
- "The information reviewed at the June meeting indicated..." through the data review paragraphs (economic activity, labor, industrial production, consumer spending, housing, business investment, trade, inflation) — these are staff data presentations.
- "In the forecast prepared for the meeting, the staff raised its projection..." — explicit staff forecast.
- "The staff's projection for price inflation in core PCE..." — explicit staff forecast.
- Financial market conditions paragraphs ("Conditions eased somewhat in some U.S. financial markets...") — staff reporting on market developments.

Participant content to EXCLUDE: "In their discussion of the economic situation and outlook, FOMC participants noted...", "members generally agreed...", "Mr. Fisher dissented..."

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as ABOVE midpoint. Evidence: "Headline consumer price inflation in the United States remained elevated in April and May, mostly because of large increases in food and energy prices." Core prices "rose at a relatively subdued rate." Staff forecast: core PCE projection unchanged for 2008, but second half marked up for "pass-through of recent jumps in prices of energy and other commodities." Headline inflation "expected to exceed core inflation by a considerable margin this year." The mix is elevated headline but subdued core — net position is above neutral given the staff marked up their forecast. Score: ~62. Direction: above midpoint because headline is elevated and staff marked up the second-half forecast.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff reports elevated headline, rising pass-through risk. Score > 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees economy BELOW potential. Evidence: "economic activity had remained soft in recent months," "the trajectory of economic growth anticipated through 2009 implied noticeable slack in resource utilization." Staff raised their GDP projection slightly but still sees "modest expansion in real GDP in the first half of 2008 followed by a slight slowdown in growth in the second half." Manufacturing deteriorated, housing in steep descent.
- Bright-line check: "noticeable slack in resource utilization" = MUST be < 50. Score: ~35. The economy is below potential but not in catastrophic collapse — soft activity, not freefall.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as LOOSENING/SLACK. Evidence: "Labor demand continued to weaken in April and May." Unemployment "jumped from 5.0 percent in April to 5.5 percent in May" — about a percentage point above a year ago. Private payroll employment fell. Aggregate hours fell.
- UE at 5.5% with weakening trend fits the 31-45 range ("slack remains; unemployment elevated; UE 5.5-7%"). Score: ~35.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees mixed but below-trend. Evidence: "The growth of real consumer spending appeared to have picked up moderately from its sluggish pace in the first quarter" — some resilience. But "real spending on equipment and software appeared to move down a bit further." Housing "remained very weak." Business outlays on transportation "continued to fall sharply." Consumer spending picked up somewhat but investment weak.
- Net: consumer spending slightly better than expected but still sluggish overall, investment declining. Score: ~37 — below trend, "sluggish" to "soft."

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not specifically discussed by staff. Score: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees conditions as TIGHT/STRAINED. Evidence: "Conditions eased somewhat in some U.S. financial markets over the intermeeting period but nonetheless remained strained." "Functioning of short-term funding markets showed some improvement" but "liquidity in the market for interbank loans at maturities beyond three months remained thin." "Conditions were still poor for lower-quality collateral." Credit availability tight for nonprime and nonconforming mortgages. Tighter credit standards and terms at banks.
- Bright-line: "strained" = MUST be > 50. Score: ~70. Conditions improved from worst but remain clearly strained/tight.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff discusses Treasury markets: "Nominal Treasury yields also rose noticeably, and the Treasury yield curve flattened." Treasury repo markets "appeared to improve somewhat, but conditions were still poor for lower-quality collateral." "Haircuts" on non-Treasury collateral remained elevated. TSLF auctions "generally undersubscribed."
- The repo market stress and elevated haircuts indicate some dysfunction beyond normal yield movements. But TSLF undersubscription suggests pressure was easing. Score: ~55 — elevated concern, some dysfunction in collateral markets but not severe.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff reports: "yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds had risen significantly since the end of April but by slightly less than yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, implying a further modest narrowing of credit spreads." But also: "business lending by banks decelerated, partly reflecting continued low issuance of leveraged loans as well as tighter credit standards and terms at banks." Spreads on jumbo mortgages widened. Mortgage credit markets tight.
- Spreads modestly narrowed from elevated levels but conditions remain tight. The level is still elevated. Score: ~63 — wide but modestly improving.
- Bright-line: "narrowing" direction but from an elevated level — score the LEVEL, not direction per the gotcha rule. Still clearly elevated.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff reports: "Broad stock prices were somewhat volatile but declined modestly, on net, over the intermeeting period. The surge in oil prices weighed on equity prices outside of the energy sector, and a more pessimistic outlook for future earnings in the financial sector caused stocks of financial institutions to decline significantly."
- Modest decline in broad equities, significant decline in financials. No valuation commentary per se. Per the gotcha: "Equities declined" alone = 50-55. Financial sector decline is more concerning. Score: ~57.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff reports: Credit outstanding through PDCF declined (positive sign). But: "tighter credit standards and terms at banks," bank lending decelerated. "Some regional banks that had experienced substantial credit losses were expected to adopt a significantly more conservative lending posture" — wait, this is participant content. Staff-attributable: depository institutions' use of primary credit borrowing increased. Financial institutions' equity prices declined reflecting "increased concern about asset quality and the outlook for profits" — this appears in the market conditions review (staff-reported). "Deteriorating condition of some financial guarantors and mortgage insurers" — participant section.
- Staff-attributable banking signals: tighter lending standards, deceleration in business lending, increased primary credit borrowing. Score: ~62 — emerging to significant stress.
- Bright-line: "tighter lending standards" = stress > 50. Correct.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff reports: "The trade-weighted foreign exchange value of the dollar against major currencies rose."
- Dollar appreciated. Bright-line: appreciation = MUST be > 50. Score: ~55 — modest strengthening noted briefly.
