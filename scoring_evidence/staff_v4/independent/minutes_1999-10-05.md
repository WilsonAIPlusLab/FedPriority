# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — October 5, 1999 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff content and participant/member discussion are interleaved. I must carefully separate staff-attributable language from participant views.

**Staff-attributable sections** are identified by:
- "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested that..." (paragraphs on economic data)
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested that..."
- Factual reporting paragraphs on employment, production, trade, inflation, financial markets

**Participant-attributable sections** (to IGNORE for scoring):
- "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective economic conditions, members commented..."
- "members reported continued high levels of activity..."
- "In the Committee's discussion of policy..."
- "members indicated that they favored..."

### Era Context
October 1999 — late 1990s expansion/boom. Unemployment very low (~4.2%), strong GDP growth, productivity acceleration underway, equity markets high but beginning to wobble. Fed funds at 5.25% after an August rate hike. Energy prices rebounding sharply. Core inflation still subdued despite tight labor markets.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as MODESTLY ABOVE the low levels of 1998 but still moderate overall. Key staff language: "Inflation was moderate, but somewhat above that in 1998, owing to a sharp rebound in energy prices." And: "the core CPI rose less than in the previous twelve-month period." Also: "Core price inflation was projected to rise somewhat over the forecast horizon." The staff forecast sees a SLIGHT pickup ahead but from a low base. Core is subdued; headline lifted by energy.
- Direction: Slightly below to at the midpoint. Energy pushes headline up but core remains contained.
- Staff says "moderate" and "relatively subdued" for core, with "sharp rebound in energy prices" and forecast of "rise somewhat."
- Score: ~48. Core is running below prior year, but energy rebound and staff forecast of "rise somewhat" keeps it near neutral. The word "moderate" maps to near-midpoint.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence of moderate/subdued core but energy rebound and forecast of slight rise = near-neutral with a slight upward lean. 48 fits.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees: "the expansion of economic activity was substantial in the quarter just ended." Staff forecast: "expansion would gradually moderate to a rate around or perhaps a little below the growth of the economy's estimated potential." The staff says growth is currently above potential but expected to moderate TO potential. This means current output is ABOVE potential.
- Direction: Above midpoint. "Substantial" expansion, above-potential growth currently.
- Bright-line check: The staff forecast refers to moderating TO potential, implying currently ABOVE potential. Score MUST be > 50.
- Magnitude: "Substantial" is strong language. Late 1990s boom anchor is 57-63.
- Score: 58. Strong expansion, above potential, but staff expects moderation.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff reports: "civilian unemployment rate dropped back to 4.2 percent in August, matching its low for the year." "Employment gains continued to be relatively robust, keeping labor markets taut." Staff forecast: "persistently tight labor markets."
- Direction: Well above midpoint. 4.2% unemployment is very low. "Taut" and "tight" are explicit.
- The rubric anchors for 1999-2000 are 65-75. UE at 4.2% with "taut" language is clearly tight.
- Score: 68. "Taut," 4.2% UE, "persistently tight labor markets" — this is clearly tight. The anchor for this era is 65-75.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. 68 matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff reports: "Consumer spending and business investment in durable equipment remained strong." "Total retail sales posted strong gains over July and August." "business capital spending continued to climb rapidly." "new orders for durable equipment turned up sharply."
- Direction: Well above midpoint. Multiple "strong" and "rapidly" descriptors.
- Housing shows "some signs of deceleration" but at high level. This is a minor offset.
- Score: 65. Strong consumer spending + rapid business investment = well above trend. "Rapidly" and "strong" warrant 15 points above neutral. Slight housing offset.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says strong/rapid. 65 matches.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff forecast: "not be fully offset by rising productivity growth" and mentions "rising productivity growth" and "accelerating productivity." The staff clearly sees productivity growth as elevated and possibly accelerating.
- Direction: Above midpoint. Staff perceives strong and rising productivity.
- Score: 63. "Rising productivity growth" / "accelerating productivity" suggests notably above trend.
- Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. Evidence says rising/accelerating. 63 matches.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff-attributable language on financial markets: "longer-term yields rose somewhat over the intermeeting period." "Most measures of share prices in equity markets registered sizable declines." Staff forecast mentions: "higher intermediate- and longer-term interest rates" as restraining demand; "somewhat less accommodative conditions in financial markets" (this phrase is in a paragraph starting with "In the Committee's discussion" — this is participant language, but it describes market conditions that staff data supports).
- The staff data shows: short rates little changed, longer yields up somewhat, equity prices down sizably. The staff forecast references higher rates restraining demand.
- Direction: Slightly above midpoint — conditions have tightened modestly from prior easy stance due to rate hikes and rising longer-term yields.
- Bright-line: No explicit "accommodative" or "tight" label from staff specifically. The data shows some tightening (higher rates, equity declines). But overall conditions are not described as restrictive.
- Score: 52. Rates up somewhat, equities declined, but no language of "tight" or "restrictive" from staff. Modestly tightening from a relatively neutral stance.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Rising yields and equity declines suggest slightly above neutral. 52 fits.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Staff reports: "longer-term yields rose somewhat over the intermeeting period." No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction failures. This is normal yield movement from policy expectations and data.
- Direction: At or below midpoint. Normal market functioning with some yield movement.
- Bright-line: Rising yields from policy/data expectations = normal (45-55 range). No dysfunction described.
- Score: 45. Normal functioning, orderly yield movements.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
No specific discussion of credit spreads, corporate bond markets, or credit availability from staff. "Total domestic nonfinancial debt has continued to expand at a pace somewhat above the middle of its range" suggests credit is flowing normally.
- Direction: Near or slightly below midpoint. Debt expanding normally suggests no stress.
- Score: 42. No mention of spread widening; debt expanding normally. Late 1990s generally favorable credit conditions.
- Confidence: Low — inferred from indirect evidence.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff reports: "Most measures of share prices in equity markets registered sizable declines over the intermeeting period, apparently reflecting not only higher interest rates but also concerns that U.S. stocks might be overvalued." The staff explicitly mentions overvaluation concerns.
- Direction: Below midpoint (toward overvaluation concern). Staff notes "overvalued" concern.
- The rubric: lower scores = more overvaluation concern. 1999 dot-com era anchor: 15-25.
- Score: 28. Staff explicitly cites overvaluation concerns. This is the dot-com era. "Sizable declines" plus overvaluation language. Not full bubble language but explicit concern.
- Polarity check: Higher = more equity stress/concern (depressed markets). Lower = overvaluation concern. The staff mentions overvaluation, which maps to the LOW end (15-30). 28 fits.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
No specific discussion of banking system health or stress from staff.
- Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff reports: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar changed little over the period." "The dollar depreciated against the currencies of the major foreign industrial countries, especially the Japanese yen." But "the dollar rose against the currencies of the other important trading partners."
- Direction: Near midpoint. Net "changed little" on trade-weighted basis. Mixed movements.
- Bright-line: Trade-weighted "changed little" = near neutral. Score ~48-52.
- Score: 48. Net little changed, with depreciation vs. majors offset by appreciation vs. emerging markets. Slight lean toward weakness given emphasis on depreciation vs. majors.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Inflation was moderate, but somewhat above that in 1998, owing to a sharp rebound in energy prices." "Core consumer inflation continued to be relatively subdued over the two months. In the past twelve months, the core CPI rose less than in the previous twelve-month period."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff sees inflation as moderate overall — core is subdued and actually decelerating year-over-year, but energy prices have rebounded sharply and the staff forecast projects core to "rise somewhat" ahead.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is slightly below neutral because core inflation is subdued and decelerating even though energy is up and some pipeline pressures are building.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The expansion of economic activity was substantial in the quarter just ended." Staff forecast: "the expansion would gradually moderate to a rate around or perhaps a little below the growth of the economy's estimated potential."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is running above potential with "substantial" expansion, but staff expects moderation toward potential growth ahead.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because the economy's "substantial" expansion implies above-potential output, and the staff forecast projects moderation TO potential (implying currently above). Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +/-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The civilian unemployment rate dropped back to 4.2 percent in August, matching its low for the year." "Employment gains continued to be relatively robust, keeping labor markets taut." Staff forecast: "persistently tight labor markets."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: At 4.2% unemployment with "taut" labor markets and "persistently tight" conditions in the forecast, the labor market is clearly tight by any standard.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral. UE at 4.2% with explicit "taut" and "tight" descriptors. Consistent with 1999-2000 anchor of 65-75.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending and business investment in durable equipment remained strong." "Business capital spending continued to climb rapidly." "Total retail sales posted strong gains over July and August."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment are running well above trend, with "strong" and "rapidly" characterizing the pace. Housing deceleration provides modest offset.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because spending and investment are described with strong/rapid language across multiple categories.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 63 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Staff forecast references "rising productivity growth" and "not be fully offset by rising productivity growth." Also: "Productivity and economic potential seemed to have been growing at an increasingly rapid rate in recent years" (participant language but echoing staff assessment).
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff perceives productivity growth as above trend and accelerating, a key factor allowing strong growth without proportionate inflation.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is above neutral because staff sees rising/accelerating productivity growth.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Longer-term yields rose somewhat over the intermeeting period." "Most measures of share prices in equity markets registered sizable declines." Staff forecast: "higher intermediate- and longer-term interest rates" restraining demand.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral to very slightly tight — rates have risen and equities declined, providing some restraint, but no language of restrictive or stressed conditions.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is at/slightly above neutral. No "accommodative" or "tight" staff language; market data shows modest tightening.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 45 +/-8 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Longer-term yields rose somewhat over the intermeeting period." "Most short-term interest rates have posted small mixed changes."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Normal Treasury market functioning with orderly yield increases driven by economic data and policy expectations. No dysfunction described.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral. Yield moves are normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +/-10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Total domestic nonfinancial debt has continued to expand at a pace somewhat above the middle of its range." No mention of spread widening or credit stress.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Credit appears to be flowing normally with no mention of spread stress, consistent with generally favorable late-1990s credit conditions.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly below neutral, consistent with a period of normal/favorable credit availability.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 28 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Most measures of share prices in equity markets registered sizable declines over the intermeeting period, apparently reflecting not only higher interest rates but also concerns that U.S. stocks might be overvalued."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff explicitly notes overvaluation concerns as a driver of equity declines, placing this in the elevated-valuation/concern zone consistent with the late-1990s dot-com era.
