# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2012-04-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly delineated sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered.

### Era Context
April 2012: The U.S. economy is in the middle of a slow recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 8.2%. The Fed is maintaining highly accommodative policy (zero lower bound, prior QE rounds). Euro-area sovereign debt stress is re-emerging (Spain, Italy). Inflation is near or slightly below target with energy prices providing temporary upward pressure.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as modestly below neutral. Overall consumer price inflation "increased somewhat" due to higher oil/gasoline, but core prices rose only "moderately," wage gains were "muted," and the staff forecast inflation to be "subdued through 2014." Long-run expectations "remained stable." The temporary energy boost does not change the underlying subdued picture. Direction: below midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff clearly sees the economy as operating BELOW potential. Key phrases: "wide margin of slack in the labor market," "substantial resource slack persisting over the projection period," unemployment rate "still elevated" and expected to remain so through 2014. Economic activity "expanding moderately" but not closing the gap. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language --> MUST be < 50. Direction: well below midpoint.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.2% -- very elevated. "Long-duration unemployment remained elevated." Private payroll employment "rose at a slower pace in March." "Wide margin of slack in the labor market." With UE at 8.2%, the anchors point to 16-30 range. Direction: well below midpoint.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Real PCE "rose briskly in February." Retail sales excluding autos "increased solidly." But consumer sentiment "remained subdued." Business equipment spending "appeared to rise modestly." Nonresidential construction "declined." Housing "continued to be held down." Net assessment: consumer spending is reasonably solid but investment is mixed/modest. Direction: slightly below to at midpoint.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: "Broad financial market conditions changed little, on balance." Businesses "continued to raise substantial amounts of funds." Bond issuance "robust," leveraged loan issuance "brisk," banks "eased slightly their lending standards." However, CRE sector "remained strained," residential mortgage markets "continued to be weak." Overall conditions are somewhat accommodative/loose given the easing standards and robust issuance, though with pockets of weakness. Bright-line check: language suggests accommodative/supportive conditions --> score MUST be < 50. Direction: below midpoint (somewhat accommodative).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields moved in an orderly fashion in response to economic news and policy expectations. Funding markets were "stable." The overnight GC repo rate fluctuations were normal. No dysfunction described. Direction: low stress, normal functioning.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Investment-grade spreads "about unchanged." Speculative-grade spreads "increased somewhat." Bond issuance was robust/strong. C&I loan growth was robust. Banks eased lending standards. CRE was strained. Net: credit conditions are generally favorable with mild concerns at the edges. Direction: slightly below midpoint (modest stress but mostly favorable).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices ended "little changed on net." Financial equity prices increased on better-than-expected earnings and CCAR. No valuation commentary from staff. Direction: roughly neutral, no particular concern. Default to 45-55.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Banks eased lending standards. CCAR results were "better than expected." C&I lending expanding. However, CRE sector "remained strained," home equity loans and CRE loans "continued to decline." Euro-area bank stress was discussed but that's foreign. Domestically, banks appear in decent shape with pockets of stress in CRE/mortgage. Bright-line check: domestic banking appears sound with some CRE issues --> below 50 but not deeply. Direction: modestly below midpoint.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was about unchanged." Dollar appreciated against EM currencies but depreciated against yen and sterling. Bright-line check: "about unchanged" --> neutral, around 46-54. Direction: at midpoint.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices "climbed markedly." Food prices "relatively subdued." Core goods prices rose "moderately." Mixed but with energy pass-through. Slightly above neutral for goods as a category due to energy.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "rose in January and February" but the sector is weak. Not discussed as an inflation component per se. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Near-term expectations "increased in March but then fell back in early April." Longer-term expectations "remained stable." Well-anchored. Direction: at/near target.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the staff continued to forecast that inflation would be subdued through 2014"
- "Consumer prices excluding food and energy rose moderately in February and March"; "nominal wage gains continued to be muted"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation as contained and below target, with temporary energy-driven headline increases but subdued core and muted wages.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff forecasts subdued inflation with muted wages and stable expectations, despite transitory energy effects.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "decrease the wide margin of slack in the labor market slowly over the projection period"
- "substantial resource slack persisting over the projection period, and...the unemployment rate was anticipated to still be elevated at the end of 2014"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly identifies substantial slack with only gradual closing expected. Economy expanding but far below potential.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff explicitly cites "wide margin of slack" and "substantial resource slack." Bright-line: "slack remains" --> score < 50. SATISFIED.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate declined to 8.2 percent in March"
- "the rate of long-duration unemployment remained elevated"; "Private nonfarm employment rose at a slower pace in March"

**Center-of-gravity**: With UE at 8.2% and long-duration unemployment elevated, the labor market has significant slack. The anchor for UE > 7% is 16-30.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.2% represents substantial labor market slack with elevated long-duration unemployment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) rose briskly in February"; "nominal retail sales excluding purchases of motor vehicles increased solidly"
- "Real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to rise modestly in the first quarter"; "nominal business spending for nonresidential construction declined"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is solid/brisk, but business investment is only modest and construction declining. Housing weak. Net is slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the brisk consumer spending is offset by modest/weak business investment and declining construction.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the document.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Businesses continued to raise substantial amounts of funds in credit and capital markets"; "Domestic nonfinancial firms' bond issuance and growth in commercial and industrial (C&I) loans were robust"
- "domestic banks eased slightly their lending standards on core loans"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative with robust issuance, brisk leveraged lending, and easing bank standards, though with pockets of strain in CRE and residential mortgage markets.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (more accommodative) because banks are easing standards and credit issuance is robust/strong. Bright-line: "eased" lending standards --> score < 50. SATISFIED.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in unsecured short-term dollar funding markets were stable over most of the intermeeting period"
- Yield movements were orderly, driven by economic news and policy expectations -- normal functioning.

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally. Yield movements are driven by macro outlook changes, not dysfunction. Funding markets stable.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are functioning normally with stable funding conditions. No dysfunction described. Score 35 is appropriate for normal/quiet conditions.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Yields and spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were about unchanged, but yields and spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds increased somewhat"
- "financial conditions in the commercial real estate (CRE) sector remained strained amid weak fundamentals and tight underwriting conditions"

**Center-of-gravity**: IG spreads unchanged, spec-grade widened somewhat. Issuance robust in most sectors but CRE strained. Overall slightly below normal stress levels but with some areas of concern.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly below neutral because conditions are generally favorable (robust issuance, unchanged IG spreads) with minor widening in spec-grade. The CRE strain is a pocket issue, not broad.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Broad U.S. stock price indexes...end the period little changed on net"
- "Equity prices of financial institutions increased, reportedly as investors interpreted the first-quarter earnings...and the results of the CCAR as better than expected"

**Center-of-gravity**: Stock prices roughly flat; no staff valuation commentary. Financial sector equity rose on better-than-expected results. No distress or overvaluation language.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is at neutral because staff reports factual price movements without valuation concern. Default to ~50 per rubric.

### BANKING_STRESS: 40 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "domestic banks eased slightly their lending standards on core loans"; CCAR results "better than expected"
- "CRE loans continued to decline"; "financial conditions in the commercial real estate (CRE) sector remained strained"

**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system generally improving -- easing standards, expanding C&I lending, positive CCAR results. But CRE and home equity loan weakness persists. Overall modestly below neutral stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are easing standards and CCAR results were positive, indicating reduced stress. Bright-line: banks improving --> score < 50. SATISFIED.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The staff's broad nominal index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar was about unchanged over the intermeeting period"
- "the dollar appreciated against most emerging market currencies but depreciated moderately against the yen and sterling"

**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar broadly unchanged on a trade-weighted basis. Mixed movements against individual currencies. Neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is approximately at neutral because the broad dollar index was "about unchanged." Bright-line: no clear direction --> score near 50. SATISFIED.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
