# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2011-11-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: November 2011 -- two years after the Great Recession trough. The economy is in a slow recovery with persistently high unemployment (9.1%). The European sovereign debt crisis is a dominant theme in financial markets. The Fed has just launched "Operation Twist" (maturity extension program). Inflation had spiked earlier in 2011 on commodities but was now moderating.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. "Overall consumer price inflation was more moderate than earlier in the year"; "consumer prices excluding food and energy rose a bit less than in the preceding quarter"; "longer-term inflation expectations remained stable"; "wage increases continued to be subdued"; staff forecast: "prices to rise at a subdued pace in 2012 and 2013." Inflation was coming down from an earlier commodity-driven spike. Current inflation was moderating but not deflationary -- this is a "contained/modest" picture. Score range: 35-42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output BELOW potential. "The pace of economic activity strengthened somewhat in the third quarter, reflecting in part a reversal of temporary factors." But: "slack in product and labor markets" persists; "unemployment rate was expected to remain elevated"; "many of the factors that have been restraining the recovery... remained in place." The word "slack" is used explicitly. Bright-line: "slack remains" -> score MUST be < 50. Given UE at 9.1% and only gradual recovery projected, this is well below potential. Score range: 30-35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very LOOSE. UE at 9.1%; "labor market conditions continued to be weak"; "long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still high"; "wage increases continued to be subdued"; "initial claims... remained at a level consistent with only modest employment growth." With UE at 9.1%, the anchor range is 16-30. Score range: 22-28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees mixed signals -- slightly below trend. Consumer spending: "Real PCE rose briskly in September but posted a more moderate gain for the third quarter as a whole"; motor vehicle purchases increased significantly; but "real disposable income declined" and "consumer sentiment continued to be downbeat." Business investment: "Real business purchases of equipment and software expanded appreciably"; nonresidential construction also rose but from low levels. Housing: "very weak." Net: moderate consumption gains + appreciable business investment offset by very weak housing and declining real income. Score range: 38-44.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes a mixed but somewhat tight picture. "Financial markets were quite volatile"; "tight credit conditions" mentioned multiple times; "tight underwriting standards for mortgage loans"; CRE financing "deteriorated in some respects"; "elevated risk premiums remained in place." But also: "C&I loans accelerated"; investment-grade bond financing remained active. The overall tone is conditions that are somewhat restrictive but not crisis-level. Score range: 58-65.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Longer-term yields moved in response to policy and European developments. There is NO discussion of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction failures. Yield movements are described as orderly responses to policy and economic news. This is normal market functioning. Score range: 40-48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds edged lower, on net, over the period, leaving their spreads to Treasury securities slightly narrower." But "bond issuance by speculative-grade firms was limited"; "issuance financed by institutional investors slowed significantly"; "CMBS spreads widened"; CRE financing deteriorated. Mixed picture: investment-grade spreads slightly narrowed, but speculative-grade access limited and CMBS widened. Score range: 52-58.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes ended the intermeeting period little changed." "Gross public equity issuance by nonfinancial firms continued to be very weak... with a large number of firms shelving planned initial public offerings amid the volatility." Markets volatile but flat. IPOs shelved suggests some concern. Modest stress. Score range: 53-58.

**BANKING_STRESS**: U.S. banks: "Third-quarter U.S. bank earnings reports generally met investors' expectations"; "equity prices for U.S. banking firms were not much changed"; "their CDS spreads were a bit higher." European banks: "CDS spreads remained elevated" and "continued to face somewhat strained conditions in short-term bank funding markets." U.S. bank lending: "less net easing of lending standards." There is moderate stress -- U.S. banks are functioning but CDS spreads rising slightly; European bank stress is spilling over as a concern. Score range: 50-58.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Some reversal of safe-haven flows in October reportedly led the dollar to give back most of the gains it registered in late September, leaving the broad nominal foreign exchange value of the dollar little changed, on balance." Bright-line: dollar "little changed" -> near 50. Score: 48-52.

### Inflation sub-components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "consumer prices for food and energy increased last quarter at a slower pace than earlier in the year" -- moderating from elevated levels. Score range: 40-45.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "consumer prices excluding food and energy rose a bit less than in the preceding quarter" -- this includes services but is not services-specific. Limited info. Score range: 38-42.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "home prices trended lower"; "Indicators of home prices remained weak." Housing deflation. Score range: 20-25.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "longer-run inflation expectations in the survey remained stable"; "near-term inflation expectations... continued to be well below the elevated level seen in the spring." Well-anchored. Score: 45-50.

- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says "little changed." Score near 50. Correct. Bright-line satisfied.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (33) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (41)**: Both below 50. C&I a bit higher because business investment was "appreciable" even in a slack economy. Gap of 8 points -- coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Overall consumer price inflation was more moderate than earlier in the year, as prices of energy and some commodities declined from their recent peaks." "The staff continued to expect prices to rise at a subdued pace in 2012 and 2013."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation moderating from a commodity-driven spike, running below target on a forward basis, with subdued wages and stable expectations.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes moderating, subdued inflation with downward momentum.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The increase in real GDP was projected to be sufficient to reduce the slack in product and labor markets only slowly, and the unemployment rate was expected to remain elevated at the end of 2013." "Many of the factors that have been restraining the recovery... remained in place."
**Center-of-gravity**: Significant slack persists in both product and labor markets, with only slow closing projected.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly identifies persistent slack. Bright-line satisfied (slack remains -> below 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate held at 9.1 percent in September, and both long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons were still high." "Wage increases continued to be subdued."
**Center-of-gravity**: Very loose labor market with 9.1% unemployment, weak job gains, subdued wages, and no improvement in hiring plans.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 9.1% with significant slack signals a very loose labor market.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 41 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose briskly in September but posted a more moderate gain for the third quarter as a whole." "Real business purchases of equipment and software expanded appreciably in the third quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate consumer spending and appreciable business investment, but weighed down by very weak housing, declining real income, and downbeat sentiment.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because despite some bright spots in business investment, housing is depressed and the overall picture is below-trend.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Many of the factors that have been restraining the recovery, such as... tight credit conditions, and elevated risk premiums, remained in place." "Financing conditions for commercial real estate markets appeared to have deteriorated in some respects." Also: "tight underwriting standards for mortgage loans."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broadly tight financial conditions with elevated risk premiums, tight mortgage and CRE credit, though some improvement in C&I lending.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff describes tight credit conditions and elevated risk premiums. Bright-line satisfied (tight -> above 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 44 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Longer-term Treasury yields declined appreciably following the release of the September FOMC statement." Yield movements described as responses to policy and European news -- no dysfunction language.
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning with yield movements driven by policy expectations and European developments. No liquidity or market dysfunction described.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no evidence of market dysfunction. Yields moved in orderly fashion.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads to Treasury securities [were] slightly narrower" for corporate bonds. But "bond issuance by speculative-grade firms was limited"; "issuance financed by institutional investors slowed significantly in the third quarter"; "CMBS spreads [widened]."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed -- investment-grade spreads slightly narrowed but speculative-grade access limited and CRE/CMBS conditions deteriorating. Net: modestly elevated stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly above neutral given limited spec-grade access and CRE spread widening, even though IG spreads narrowed slightly.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes ended the intermeeting period little changed." "Gross public equity issuance by nonfinancial firms continued to be very weak in September and October, with a large number of firms shelving planned initial public offerings amid the volatility in equity markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equity prices flat but high volatility and shelved IPOs indicate modest concern. No valuation commentary from staff.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral reflecting volatility and weak issuance activity, though prices were flat.

### BANKING_STRESS: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "CDS spreads [of large U.S. banking organizations] were a bit higher." "European bank CDS spreads remained elevated, and these institutions continued to face somewhat strained conditions in short-term bank funding markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: U.S. banks generally stable but with slightly rising CDS spreads; European bank stress creating spillover concerns. Lending standards stopped easing.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly above neutral because CDS spreads edged higher and European banking stress created ambient risk. Bright-line: not described as "well-capitalized" or "sound," so above-50 is not contradicted.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 49 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad nominal foreign exchange value of the dollar [was] little changed, on balance, relative to its level at the time of the September FOMC meeting."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar essentially flat over the intermeeting period after reversing earlier safe-haven gains.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is at neutral because the dollar was "little changed." Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer prices for food and energy increased last quarter at a slower pace than earlier in the year." Commodity prices declined from peaks.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 40 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer prices excluding food and energy rose a bit less than in the preceding quarter." This captures services but is not services-specific.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 22 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Home prices trended lower." "Indicators of home prices remained weak, reflecting a large inventory of unsold properties and modest demand." Active price deflation in housing.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Longer-term inflation expectations in the survey remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations... continued to be well below the elevated level seen in the spring."
