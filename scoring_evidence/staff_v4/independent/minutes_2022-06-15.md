# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2022-06-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member content is included.

### Era Context
June 2022 — peak inflation era. CPI at 8.6%, PCE at 6.3%. The Fed had begun its tightening cycle (50bp hike in May). Unemployment at 3.6% — historically tight labor market. The economy was rebounding from a Q1 GDP decline but real GDP remained "well above potential." This is the period of maximum inflation pressure with tightening financial conditions beginning to bite.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as FAR ABOVE midpoint. PCE inflation at 6.3%, core PCE at 4.9%, CPI at 8.6%, core CPI at 6.0%. Staff revised UP its inflation projection. "Consumer price inflation remained elevated." Wage growth at 5.2% "widespread across industries." Supply-demand imbalances "more persistent than previously assumed." This is clearly in the 80-88 anchor range for 2022 Q2 peak.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy ABOVE potential. "The level of real GDP was still expected to remain well above potential over the projection period." BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language mandates score > 50. "Well above potential" suggests significantly above — in the 58-65 range. GDP rebounding from Q1 decline, "moderate rate of increase."

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as VERY TIGHT. "The labor market remained very tight." Unemployment at 3.6%. Job openings "remained at a high level." Wage growth elevated at 5.2%, "widespread across industries." Some signs momentum slowing but LEVEL remains extremely tight. With UE at 3.6%, this should be 70+.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED but still moderately strong. "Production and spending indicators were mixed but generally remained strong." Consumer spending and IP posted "sizable gains" in April, but retail sales declined in May, housing starts down, manufacturing weakened, consumer sentiment "decreased noticeably." "Private domestic final purchases were increasing at a slower pace." GDP rebounding. Net assessment: moderately positive but decelerating — around 48-53.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states "financing conditions in domestic credit markets remained generally accommodative" and "Credit remained widely available." BRIGHT-LINE: "accommodative" language mandates score < 50. However, there's significant nuance — borrowing costs increasing across many sectors, mortgage rates at highest since 2010, corporate bond yields "well above pre-pandemic levels." The staff's own summary says "generally accommodative" but with clear tightening trend. I'll score this in the 40-45 range — still accommodative per staff language but notably less so.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "increased considerably" — but this is from policy expectations, not dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues. Yields moved on macro expectations — normal market functioning. Score 40-45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: IG and speculative-grade spreads "widened notably, on net, reaching levels comparable with those at the end of 2018." CP spreads narrowed. Municipal spreads narrowed. Default volumes historically low but downgrades exceeding upgrades. "Future default expectations of businesses deteriorated to some extent." Spreads widening but still at end-of-2018 levels (moderate, not crisis). Score 55-60.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad domestic equity price indexes declined considerably." "Stock price declines" associated with earnings news and growth outlook concerns. VIX "increased moderately" and "remained elevated relative to its historical distribution." Staff describes significant price declines but no valuation commentary (no "distressed" or "undervalued"). Default to modest stress range. Score 58-63.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Credit quality on bank books "remained sound." C&I and CRE delinquency rates "continuing to be low." Household credit quality "remained solid." Banks allocated net positive loan loss provisions (first time after releasing reserves) — "reflected concerns about the credit quality outlook." System fundamentally healthy with emerging caution. BRIGHT-LINE: "sound" language mandates < 50. Score 35-40.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar edged up." BRIGHT-LINE: appreciation mandates > 50. "Edged up" is modest. Score 53-57.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Supply disruptions "improved in some sectors" but "deteriorated in others." Goods inflation clearly elevated as part of the overall 6.3% PCE. Supply-demand imbalances expected to be "more persistent." Score 75-80.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Core PCE at 4.9% includes services. "Some additional broadening of price pressures to core goods and services" (international context, but domestic mirrors). Score 70-75.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Data on home sales and single-family housing starts moved down in April." Mortgage rates at highest since 2010. Home equity lines posted "first significant increase in more than a decade." Housing activity is slowing but no direct discussion of shelter/housing inflation per se. Limited evidence — the housing discussion is about activity, not price inflation specifically. Score N/A (housing inflation not directly discussed; housing activity is captured in CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of inflation expectations derived from surveys of professional forecasters and of consumers generally suggested that inflation was expected to remain high in the short run but then fall back toward levels consistent with a longer-run rate of 2 percent." TIPS inflation compensation declined. This suggests expectations remain broadly anchored for the long run despite high short-run expectations. Score 58-62.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (60) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72)**: Gap of 12 points. Acceptable — labor market can be tighter than output gap suggests given structural factors and the Q1 GDP decline. The output gap reflects "well above potential" but moderated by recent mixed data, while labor tightness at 3.6% UE is unambiguously very tight.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (60) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (50)**: Gap of 10 points. Acceptable — spending indicators are "mixed" and decelerating even though overall GDP remains above potential. Staff notes "slower pace" of private domestic final purchases.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (43) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (58)**: Gap of 15 points in opposite direction from midpoint. This needs explanation: Staff explicitly calls financing conditions "generally accommodative" with credit "widely available" — but spreads have widened. The staff seems to view the overall availability of credit as still easy despite spread widening. This is coherent because spread widening from very compressed levels can still leave conditions accommodative overall.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (43) vs TREASURY_STRESS (43)**: Coherent — both near-normal/slightly below neutral.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (84) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~78, Services ~72, Expectations ~60. Weighted average consistent with parent score of 84 given that the headline measures (CPI 8.6%, PCE 6.3%) include energy and food which are driving the extreme readings.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 84 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was 6.3 percent over the 12 months ending in April"; "the 12-month change in the CPI was 8.6 percent"; "the staff revised up its projection for the second half of 2022 in response to stronger-than-expected wage growth and the staff's assessment that the boost to inflation from supply-demand imbalances in the economy...would be more persistent than previously assumed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation far above target on all measures, with upward revisions and broad-based pressures including elevated wage growth. This is peak 2022 inflation.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far above neutral because PCE is 6.3%, CPI is 8.6%, and staff revised projections upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The level of real GDP was still expected to remain well above potential over the projection period, though the gap was projected to narrow significantly this year"; "GDP growth appeared to be rebounding after having declined in the first quarter."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff projects GDP "well above potential" but with a narrowing gap — clearly above potential but moderating.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says "well above potential." Bright-line satisfied (>50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The labor market remained very tight"; "the unemployment rate remained unchanged at 3.6 percent"; "Nominal wage growth remained elevated, with average hourly earnings having risen 5.2 percent over the 12 months ending in May, and the increases were widespread across industries."
- **Center-of-gravity**: UE at 3.6% with "very tight" language and elevated, widespread wage growth. Some slowing in momentum but level is emphatically tight.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because UE at 3.6% and staff uses "very tight" language.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 50 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Production and spending indicators were mixed but generally remained strong"; "private domestic final purchases were increasing at a slower pace in the second quarter than in the first quarter"; "retail sales declined in May, data on home sales and single-family housing starts moved down in April, some indicators of manufacturing activity weakened in May."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Mixed signals — April was strong but May showed deceleration across multiple categories. Net position is roughly at trend — solid but decelerating.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is approximately at neutral, reflecting the mix of strong April data and weakening May indicators.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- Not discussed in any staff section.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Despite further increases in borrowing costs, financing conditions in domestic credit markets remained generally accommodative"; "Credit remained widely available, particularly to higher-rated firms and consumers with higher credit scores."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly characterizes conditions as "generally accommodative" despite rising borrowing costs and tightening trend. Conditions are tightening but still on the easy side.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (less tight) because staff says "generally accommodative." Bright-line satisfied (<50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 43 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period"; Treasury yield increases driven by policy expectations ("economic data releases largely being interpreted as highlighting the possibility of a more aggressive tightening of monetary policy").
- **Center-of-gravity**: Yield movements are policy-driven, not dysfunction-driven. Funding markets stable. No mention of liquidity problems or market breakdown. Normal market functioning.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral because yield movements reflect normal price adjustment to policy expectations with stable funding markets.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads on investment-grade and, to a greater extent, speculative-grade corporate bonds widened notably, on net, reaching levels comparable with those at the end of 2018"; "market indicators of future default expectations of businesses deteriorated to some extent."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads have widened notably to late-2018 levels — moderately above normal but far from crisis levels. Upgrades exceeded by downgrades in leveraged loans.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads "widened notably." Consistent with 2022 mild widening anchor (55-65).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 60 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad domestic equity price indexes declined considerably, on balance, amid elevated market volatility"; "the VIX—increased moderately, on balance, remaining elevated relative to its historical distribution and significantly above average pre-pandemic levels."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Considerable equity declines with elevated volatility, driven by growth concerns and tightening. No valuation-level commentary from staff (no "distressed" or "overvalued" language), so moderate stress level.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress) because equities "declined considerably" with elevated volatility.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Credit quality of business loans on banks' books remained sound, with C&I and CRE delinquency rates continuing to be low"; "Household credit quality remained solid"; "banks allocated net positive loan loss provisions in the first quarter...reflected concerns about the credit quality outlook."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system is sound with low delinquencies and solid credit quality, but banks are starting to provision for potential future deterioration — healthy system with early-stage caution.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because credit quality is "sound" and "solid." Bright-line satisfied (<50).

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 55 +/-5 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "the broad dollar edged up."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Only brief mention — the dollar "edged up," indicating modest appreciation. Limited discussion.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied (>50).

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 78 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Supply disruptions appeared to have improved in some sectors (such as general merchandise retailers) but to have deteriorated in others (such as materials for home construction)"; supply-demand imbalances "more persistent than previously assumed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Goods inflation remains very elevated as part of the broad inflation picture, with persistent supply-demand imbalances despite some improvement in specific sectors.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: 72 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Core PCE at 4.9% captures services inflation; "some additional broadening of price pressures to core goods and services"; wage growth at 5.2% "widespread across industries" — a key services inflation driver.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Services inflation clearly above target with broadening pressures and elevated wage growth feeding through.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: N/A**
- Housing is discussed in terms of activity (home sales, housing starts declining) and mortgage markets, but housing/shelter inflation as a price measure is not directly addressed by staff.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Measures of inflation expectations derived from surveys of professional forecasters and of consumers generally suggested that inflation was expected to remain high in the short run but then fall back toward levels consistent with a longer-run rate of 2 percent"; "inflation compensation implied by Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities declined."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Short-run expectations elevated but long-run expectations remain anchored near 2%. TIPS compensation actually declined. Modestly above neutral — some upward drift in short-run but not unanchored.
