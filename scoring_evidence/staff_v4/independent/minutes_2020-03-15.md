# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2020-03-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: March 15, 2020 -- this is the emergency intermeeting action at the very onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in the United States. The economy had been strong through February data, but the coronavirus was rapidly disrupting activity. Financial markets were in turmoil. The staff provided two scenarios, both involving GDP declines and rising unemployment. This is an emergency meeting, so some dimensions may have limited coverage.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff reports PCE inflation at 1.7%, core PCE at 1.6% -- both below the 2% target. The trimmed mean was 2.1%. Survey-based longer-run expectations were "little changed." The staff outlook projects inflation to "weaken" further reflecting "deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices." Direction: below midpoint. The current reading is modestly below target, and the forward outlook is for further weakening. Score range: 30-38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Through February, the economy was growing at a "moderate pace" with very strong labor markets. However, the staff forecast was "downgraded significantly" -- "Real GDP was forecast to decline and the unemployment rate to rise, on net, in the first half of this year." One scenario has recession. The staff describes a situation where the economy WAS near or above potential but is NOW expected to fall sharply below. The data in hand still reflects the pre-COVID strength, but the staff's assessment and outlook clearly indicate a sharp move below potential. This is a transitional moment. The bright-line rule: the staff projects GDP to decline and unemployment to rise -- this implies moving below potential. I score this reflecting the staff's NET assessment at the time of the meeting, which is that the economy is rapidly deteriorating from a position of strength. Score range: 28-35. Below 50 because staff forecasts GDP decline and rising unemployment.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 3.5% (50-year low), strong payroll expansion, labor force participation edging up. Initial claims still near historic lows through early March. However, the staff outlook expects unemployment to rise. The DATA shows extremely tight labor markets, but the forward trajectory is sharply negative. The score should reflect the assessed level at the time, which is still very tight by the data but with acknowledged imminent deterioration. Score range: 55-62. The data in hand says 3.5% UE which anchors above 55, but the acknowledgment of imminent deterioration pulls it down somewhat from what would otherwise be a 65+ score.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Real PCE growth was "moderate" in January. Retail sales edged down in February. Auto sales above Q4 average. Housing starts and permits increased. Capital goods shipments and orders "increased solidly." But consumer sentiment "started to decline notably in early March" and other sentiment measures were "deteriorating." The staff outlook forecasts GDP decline. Net: the hard data through Jan-Feb was solid/moderate, but forward indicators were collapsing. Score range: 32-40. Below trend reflecting the sharp deterioration overlay on still-moderate hard data.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: This is the March 2020 "dash for cash." Staff describes: equity prices "plummeted," short-term funding markets showed "signs of stress," trading conditions for Treasuries and MBS "impaired," corporate bond issuance came to "near standstill," speculative-grade issuance "virtually stopped," "tightening credit conditions," financing conditions "strained," consumer ABS spreads "widened sharply." Earlier in the period conditions had been "supportive" but by late in the period conditions were severely tight. Bright-line: "strained" / "tightened" = must be > 50. This is crisis-level tightening. Score range: 85-92.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Explicit dysfunction language: "Treasury market functioning was severely impaired late in the intermeeting period, with some dealers reportedly unwilling to make markets for clients and the normal linkage between cash and futures markets broken. Market depth was extremely thin, and bid-ask spreads widened sharply." This is textbook severe market dysfunction -- not just yield movements. Score range: 78-85.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate bond spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened significantly," speculative-grade energy bonds "widened especially sharply," municipal spreads "widened substantially," CRE spreads "widened notably," consumer ABS spreads "widened sharply," investment-grade issuance "sporadic," speculative-grade "virtually stopped," default expectations rose. Bright-line: "widened" = must be > 50. This is severe widening approaching crisis levels. Score range: 80-88.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes plummeted because of a flight to safety." Volatility reached "levels not seen since the fall of 2008." Declines were "broad based." Earnings outlook "deteriorated." This describes significant market stress/depression. Score range: 75-82.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank sector was "among the worst performers" in equity declines. Default expectations rose. But there is no specific discussion of bank capital, bank health, or banking system soundness. The mentions are about bank stock performance and lending conditions rather than solvency/stress per se. C&I loan growth was "modest," credit card and auto loan balances grew solidly through February. There are signs of emerging stress (lending conditions tightening, some firms developing borrower assistance programs) but the banking system itself is not described as stressed. Score range: 52-60. Some emerging stress signals but no systemic banking stress language.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index strengthened notably over the period, boosted by safe-haven demand, predominantly against EME currencies." EME currencies "depreciated sharply." Bright-line: dollar strengthening = must be > 50. "Notably" = 15-20 points above midpoint. Score range: 63-70.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation, as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), remained below 2 percent in January."
- "Core PCE price inflation...was 1.6 percent over that same 12-month period."
- Staff outlook: "inflation was projected to weaken, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports current inflation running modestly below the 2% target at 1.6-1.7% on PCE measures, with the outlook for further weakening due to resource slack and falling energy prices.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because current inflation is below target and projected to weaken further.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Real gross domestic product (GDP) appeared to have been increasing at a moderate pace in the first two months of the year."
- "Real GDP was forecast to decline and the unemployment rate to rise, on net, in the first half of this year."
- "In a more adverse scenario, the economy entered recession this year."

**Center-of-gravity**: While hard data through February showed moderate growth, the staff's assessment at the meeting was that the economy was heading into significant contraction. The uncertainty is wide because the staff offered two scenarios, but both involve GDP decline.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is well below neutral because staff forecasts GDP decline and rising unemployment, implying the economy is moving below potential. Bright-line rule satisfied (below potential = score < 50).

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Total nonfarm payroll employment expanded strongly in January and February, and the unemployment rate was at its 50-year low of 3.5 percent in February."
- "Initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits...remained near historically low levels through early March."
- Staff outlook: "the unemployment rate [was forecast] to rise."

**Center-of-gravity**: The data in hand through February shows an extremely tight labor market (3.5% UE, 50-year low). However, the staff acknowledges imminent deterioration. The score reflects the tension between the still-tight data and the forward outlook. The 3.5% UE anchors above 55, but the anticipated sharp loosening pulls the score down from what would otherwise be 65+.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because the most recent data shows 3.5% unemployment -- well below NAIRU -- despite the anticipated deterioration ahead.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Real PCE growth was moderate in January" and capital goods shipments "increased solidly in January."
- "The consumer sentiment measure from the Michigan survey started to decline notably in early March" and other sentiment measures "were also deteriorating."
- Staff outlook: "Real GDP was forecast to decline...in the first half of this year."

**Center-of-gravity**: Hard data through January-February showed moderate-to-solid spending and investment, but forward indicators were collapsing and the staff forecast called for GDP decline. The net assessment is below trend, reflecting the forward-looking deterioration dominating the backward-looking data.

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because while recent hard data was moderate, the staff's forward assessment is for sharp decline.

---

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A

Not discussed in the document.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 88 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Trading conditions for Treasury securities and MBS were impaired. Moreover, primary issuance of investment-grade corporate bonds was sporadic, and that of speculative-grade corporate bonds and leveraged loans virtually stopped."
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms were strained over the late part of the intermeeting period."
- "Short-term funding markets showed signs of stress, with elevated demand for repo funding and increased short-term spreads."

**Center-of-gravity**: The staff describes crisis-level tightening across virtually every financial market -- Treasuries, MBS, corporate bonds, CP, repo, municipal bonds, CRE, and consumer ABS. Issuance halted in multiple markets. This is consistent with the March 2020 "dash for cash" crisis.

**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is far above neutral because staff describes "strained," "impaired," and crisis-level conditions across all segments. Bright-line rule satisfied.

---

### TREASURY_STRESS: 82 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Treasury market functioning was severely impaired late in the intermeeting period, with some dealers reportedly unwilling to make markets for clients and the normal linkage between cash and futures markets broken."
- "Market depth was extremely thin, and bid-ask spreads widened sharply."

**Center-of-gravity**: This is explicit, severe market dysfunction -- not mere yield movement. Dealers unwilling to make markets, broken cash-futures linkage, extremely thin depth, and wide bid-ask spreads constitute genuine Treasury market stress.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is far above neutral because of explicit market dysfunction language (dealers refusing to make markets, broken linkages). This is NOT just yield volatility. Bright-line rule satisfied.

---

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 83 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Corporate bond spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened significantly, and spreads on speculative-grade energy bonds widened especially sharply."
- "Corporate bond issuance came to a near standstill around late February...speculative-grade issuance and leveraged loan issuance virtually stopped."
- Municipal spreads "widened substantially," CRE spreads "widened notably," consumer ABS spreads "widened sharply."

**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened severely across every credit segment -- corporate IG/HY, municipal, CRE, consumer ABS. Multiple markets saw issuance halt. This is near-crisis level spread widening.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is far above neutral because spreads "widened significantly" across all segments and issuance stopped in several markets. Bright-line rule satisfied.

---

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 78 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Broad stock price indexes plummeted because of a flight to safety amid escalating concerns about global economic activity."
- "The one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index soared, sometimes reaching levels not seen since the fall of 2008."

**Center-of-gravity**: Equities experienced sharp, broad-based declines with volatility reaching GFC-era levels. The staff describes this in the context of flight to safety and deep economic concern, indicating genuine market distress rather than a routine correction.

**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is well above neutral (more stress/concern) because of plummeting prices, GFC-level volatility, and flight to safety.

---

### BANKING_STRESS: 55 +/-10 [Low confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "The airline, energy, and bank sectors were among the worst performers" in equity declines.
- "Measures of the year-ahead expected default rate increased in March to levels slightly under those observed during the oil price plunge in early 2016."
- Lending conditions were tightening: "developments late in the intermeeting period pointed to tightening credit conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: There is no explicit discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or bank-specific stress. The signals are indirect -- bank stocks falling, default expectations rising, and credit conditions tightening. This suggests emerging stress rather than systemic banking problems. Confidence is low because banking stress is inferred rather than directly assessed.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly above neutral because of indirect stress signals (falling bank stocks, rising default expectations, tightening lending). Bright-line: no explicit "well-capitalized" or "sound" language, but no explicit "stressed" or "troubled" language either.

---

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 66 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "The broad dollar index strengthened notably over the period, boosted by safe-haven demand, predominantly against EME currencies."
- "Other EME currencies, such as the Brazilian real and Mexican peso, depreciated sharply."

**Center-of-gravity**: The dollar strengthened notably on safe-haven flows, with broad-based appreciation especially against EME currencies. "Notably" suggests a meaningful move.

**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "strengthened notably." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed."
- "The University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers measure for the next 5 to 10 years edged down in February and remained in the lower part of its prevailing range."

**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based measures were "little changed" (near anchored), but market-based inflation compensation "fell sharply" to historical lows. The net signal is that expectations were drifting below target -- not unanchored, but leaning low.

**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral (50 = anchored at target) because market-based measures fell to historical lows and survey measures were in the "lower part of prevailing range."
