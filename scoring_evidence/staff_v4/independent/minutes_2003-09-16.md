### Era context
September 2003: The U.S. economy is emerging from the 2001 recession and the subsequent "jobless recovery." The fed funds rate is at 1% — historically low. Unemployment remains elevated (~6.1%). The dominant concern is the risk of unwelcome further disinflation. GDP growth is picking up but labor markets remain weak.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis

Core producer prices "posted only a slight increase over the twelve-month period." The staff forecast anticipates "some slight downward pressure on core consumer price inflation" given "substantial ongoing slack." Direction: clearly below neutral. The language "very low level" and projected further downward pressure suggests 20-30 range. "Very low" is stronger than "subdued/muted" (which maps to 11-25) but the staff doesn't describe outright deflation risk — just very low and declining. I'll place this at 22.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation is very low and may decline further. Low score is correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. The staff forecast references "substantial ongoing slack in resource utilization." Economic activity is "picking up" but the staff forecast describes needing stronger expansion than has occurred. The language "substantial slack" maps clearly to below-50 territory.
- Bright-line check: "Substantial slack" → MUST be < 50. Score should be in the 31-40 range. The economy is recovering — "economic activity had been picking up," "substantially stronger economic expansion" ahead — but slack is still "substantial." This is recovery-with-persistent-gap territory. Score: 35.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Substantial slack = well below potential. Score 35 is correct.
