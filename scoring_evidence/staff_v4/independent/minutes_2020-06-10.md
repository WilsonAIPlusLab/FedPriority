# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2020-06-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: June 2020 — the acute COVID-19 pandemic period. The economy is in the immediate aftermath of the sharpest downturn in modern history (March-April 2020). By June, there are early signs of partial recovery (May employment surprise), but conditions remain severely depressed relative to pre-pandemic levels. The Fed had already cut to the ELB and launched extensive emergency lending facilities.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as far BELOW midpoint. Total PCE inflation was only 0.5% over 12 months ending April. Core PCE was 1.0%. CPI was 0.1%. Prices fell in many categories affected by social distancing. Staff projects inflation to "weaken this year, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices." This is clearly very low inflation — well below target. Score should be in the 15-25 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as massively BELOW potential. "U.S. real GDP would likely post a historically large decline in the second quarter." Industrial production "plunged," business investment "continued to tumble," residential investment "weakening significantly." Staff projects the economy to recover but "a complete recovery was not expected by year-end." The language "deterioration in resource utilization" directly signals enormous slack. Bright-line: below potential language mandates score < 50. Given "historically large decline," this should be in the 10-18 range (anchor: 15-22 for COVID Q2 2020).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as extremely SLACK. Unemployment at 13.3% (likely understated by ~3 percentage points, so effective ~16%). "Historic job losses" in April. Initial claims still "at a historically elevated level." Even with May improvement, conditions are catastrophically weak. Score should be in the 8-15 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as severely depressed. "Real PCE slumped in April, with declines widespread." Business fixed investment "continued to tumble." Residential investment "weakening significantly." Some high-frequency indicators turned up in May but "levels of these indicators were mostly still below their levels early in the year." Score should be in the 15-22 range.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as still somewhat tight but improving. "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms eased somewhat...though they remained moderately strained for lower-rated borrowers." Liquidity "continued to improve in general, but some stress was still evident in several markets." Spreads narrowed notably but remained at stress levels. Small business financing tightened. Mortgage conditions tight for lower-credit borrowers. This is clearly tighter than neutral — conditions are strained. Bright-line: "strained" language mandates > 50. But conditions are improving from crisis levels and Fed facilities are helping. Score around 60-65.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff discusses Treasury market conditions. "Liquidity measures improved in the Treasury market, but off-the-run Treasury securities of all tenors and longer-maturity on-the-run securities remained less liquid than before the onset of the pandemic." The 20-year offering had "solid demand." Yield curve steepened due to normal factors (issuance expectations, unwinding safe-haven demand). There is residual dysfunction (less liquid off-the-runs) but improving. Score around 52-58 — some remaining stress above normal but improving.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as still elevated but narrowing. "Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds...narrowed considerably but remained at levels similar to those in other periods of notable economic or bond market stress, though well below financial crisis levels." CRE spreads "remained elevated relative to before the pandemic." Bright-line: "elevated" and "stress" levels mandate > 50. Score around 62-68.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "equity prices rose" and "broad stock price indexes moved higher." Implied volatility at 85th percentile since 1990. No valuation commentary per se, but the market is recovering from March lows with still-elevated volatility. Given the recovery but residual stress signals (high vol), score around 52-55 — modestly stressed.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff discusses banks primarily in terms of lending. C&I loans surged (driven by PPP). Banks tightened lending standards on small business loans or "discontinued lending to such borrowers altogether." Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations "continued to deteriorate sharply." Corporate bond downgrades "very high." Defaults increasing. CRE showing "early signs of credit repayment difficulties." This signals meaningful stress in the banking/credit system. Bright-line: tightening lending standards = stress > 50. Score around 62-68.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff says "the staff's broad dollar index fell." The euro appreciated, Canadian dollar/Brazilian real/Mexican peso appreciated. Bright-line: dollar depreciation/weakening mandates score < 50. Score around 35-40.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Implied from discussion of low energy prices, falling prices in many categories. Consumer energy prices saw "sizable expected declines" and "considerable drop." Motor vehicle output collapsed. Score around 15-20 (deflationary pressure in goods).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Prices fell "in many categories that were affected the most by social-distancing measures, such as the prices for air travel and hotel accommodations." Score around 18-25.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not specifically discussed separately from broader inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Recent readings on survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10 year edged up, NY Fed 3-year unchanged, SPF 10-year PCE ticked down. "All of these measures...continued to be near their recent ranges." TIPS-based inflation compensation was "roughly halfway between their mid-March lows and typical levels seen in recent years." Expectations are holding near recent ranges but the TIPS measures are below normal. Score around 40-45 — slightly below anchored-at-target.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation was only 0.5 percent over the 12 months ending in April." "Core PCE price inflation...was 1.0 percent over the 12 months ending in April." "Inflation was projected to weaken this year, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation dramatically below target with further weakening expected — meaningful deflation risk territory with core PCE at half the target.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is far below neutral because core PCE at 1.0% and total PCE at 0.5% are well below the 2% target with further declines expected.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "U.S. real gross domestic product (GDP) would likely post a historically large decline in the second quarter." "A complete recovery was not expected by year-end."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The economy is in the deepest contraction since the Great Depression with massive resource slack; even the recovery path leaves large gaps.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because staff describes a "historically large decline" in GDP with enormous resource underutilization. Bright-line: below potential, score < 50. PASS.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 12 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate moved down to 13.3 percent in May after soaring to 14.7 percent in April." "Accounting for the unusually large number of workers who reported themselves as employed but absent from their jobs would have raised the unemployment rate by...3 percentage points in May."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Effective unemployment around 16% represents near-Depression levels of labor market slack, even accounting for modest May improvements.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because unemployment at 13.3% (effectively ~16%) represents catastrophic slack in the labor market.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real PCE slumped in April, with declines widespread across most spending categories." "Real business fixed investment continued to tumble in the second quarter." "Real residential investment appeared to be weakening significantly in the second quarter."
- **Center-of-gravity**: All major spending categories — consumer, business, and residential — are in severe decline, with only nascent signs of recovery from collapse levels.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is far below neutral because spending and investment are contracting sharply across all categories.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- Not discussed in the staff assessment.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms eased somewhat over the intermeeting period, though they remained moderately strained for lower-rated borrowers." "Financing conditions for small businesses tightened amid widespread continued pandemic-related closures."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are improving from crisis peaks thanks to massive Fed intervention, but remain moderately strained especially for lower-rated and smaller borrowers.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral (tighter) because staff explicitly describes conditions as "moderately strained" and "tightened" for key segments. Bright-line: strained = > 50. PASS.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Liquidity measures improved in the Treasury market, but off-the-run Treasury securities of all tenors and longer-maturity on-the-run securities remained less liquid than before the onset of the pandemic." "The Treasury's first 20-year bond offering since 1986 was met with solid demand."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market dysfunction has largely abated thanks to Fed purchases, but residual liquidity impairments persist in off-the-run segments — modestly above normal stress.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly above neutral because there is actual residual market dysfunction (liquidity impairment in off-the-runs), not just yield moves.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds...narrowed considerably but remained at levels similar to those in other periods of notable economic or bond market stress, though well below financial crisis levels." "CRE...spreads...remained elevated relative to before the pandemic."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads have improved meaningfully from March crisis peaks but remain at levels consistent with notable economic stress — still well above normal.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because staff describes spreads at "notable... stress" levels. Bright-line: elevated/widened = > 50. PASS.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 53 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes moved higher." "One-month implied volatility on the S&P 500 index declined somewhat but still stood at the 85th percentile of its distribution since 1990."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities have recovered substantially but implied volatility remains very elevated, suggesting residual stress and uncertainty. No staff valuation commentary beyond price/vol description.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — prices recovering (less stress) but extreme volatility (more stress) roughly offset.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 64 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The credit quality of nonfinancial corporations continued to deteriorate sharply during the intermeeting period. The volume of nonfinancial corporate bond and leveraged loan downgrades remained very high." "Lenders indicated that they had tightened loan standards on small business loans or discontinued lending to such borrowers altogether."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Credit quality is deteriorating sharply, banks are tightening or cutting off lending to small businesses, and defaults are rising — significant stress in the credit/banking system.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because staff describes tightening lending standards, sharply deteriorating credit quality, and rising defaults. Bright-line: tightening = stress > 50. PASS.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The staff's broad dollar index fell." "The euro appreciated notably over the period...the Canadian dollar, the Brazilian real, and the Mexican peso" also appreciated.
- **Center-of-gravity**: The dollar weakened broadly against both advanced and emerging market currencies, driven by improving risk sentiment and foreign policy support.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line: dollar weakening = < 50. PASS.
