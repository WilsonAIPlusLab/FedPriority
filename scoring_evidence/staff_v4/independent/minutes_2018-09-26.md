# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-09-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
- **Type**: Minutes (synthesized summary with staff-attributable language: "the staff reported," "the staff projected," "the staff's assessment")
- **Segmentation**: Post-2005 segmented minutes with clearly labeled sections: staff_economic_review, staff_financial_review, staff_outlook
- **Scoring scope**: Staff-attributable content only

### Era Context
September 2018: Late-cycle expansion. Unemployment at 3.9%, well below estimates of the natural rate. Tax cuts (TCJA) enacted late 2017 providing fiscal stimulus. Trade tensions with China escalating. Core PCE at 2.0%. Fed in gradual tightening cycle. This is a period of above-potential growth with a tight labor market but contained inflation.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as AT the midpoint. Core PCE at 2.0% over 12 months ending July — exactly at target. Total PCE at 2.3%, slightly above but driven by energy. Survey-based longer-run expectations "little changed on balance." Staff projects total PCE to "remain near the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term." This is essentially neutral — at target, stable expectations, no sense of acceleration or concern.
- Direction: At midpoint.
- Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Staff says at target, not accelerating. Score ~50.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees output as ABOVE the midpoint. Key language: "output was projected to rise at a rate above or at the staff's estimate of potential growth" and "economy that was projected to move further above its potential." The unemployment rate was "projected to decline further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Real GDP "rising at a strong rate." The staff explicitly references above-potential output.
- Direction: Above midpoint.
- Bright-line check: "above potential" language present -> MUST be > 50. PASS.
- Magnitude: "further above its potential" suggests clearly above, not just modestly. Score ~57.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as ABOVE the midpoint (tight). Unemployment at 3.9% — well below natural rate. "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." Job openings "elevated," quits "moved higher," initial claims "historically low." Part-time for economic reasons "below its level in late 2007." Compensation rising 3.3%. "With labor market conditions already tight." Unemployment rates for minorities below previous expansion levels. UE at 3.9% -> per rubric guidance, should be ~60-65.
- Direction: Above midpoint (tight).
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score ~63.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees this as ABOVE the midpoint. "Real PCE appeared to be rising strongly in the third quarter." Consumer sentiment "remained upbeat." Key factors "continued to be supportive of solid real PCE growth." BUT residential investment "looked to be declining further." Business equipment spending "moderating a little" from strong gains. Business equipment forward indicators "pointed to robust gains." Net exports a "notable drag." Government purchases "rising further."
- Net assessment: Strong consumption, weakening residential, moderating but still solid business investment. Net is above trend but not booming.
- Direction: Above midpoint.
- Score ~58.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not specifically discussed. Staff mentions "supply constraints might restrain output growth somewhat in the medium term" and assumed "larger-than-usual upward pressure on the labor force participation rate," but no direct productivity discussion.
- Score: N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff sees conditions as BELOW the midpoint (accommodative/loose). Key language: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households remained supportive of economic activity." "Financing conditions for large nonfinancial firms remained accommodative." "Financing conditions for small businesses remained favorable." CRE "financing conditions also remained accommodative." Residential mortgage "financing conditions remained accommodative on balance." Consumer credit "largely supportive of growth."
- Direction: Below midpoint (accommodative = loose = low score).
- Bright-line check: "accommodative"/"supportive" -> MUST be < 50. PASS.
- Magnitude: Pervasive accommodative language across all sectors. Score ~35.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields rose (10-year above 3%), but this was driven by stronger-than-expected data and policy expectations — normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. "Short-term funding markets functioned smoothly." Spreads of unsecured funding rates "continued to retrace the rise." No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction.
- Direction: At or slightly below midpoint. Normal functioning.
- Bright-line check: No actual dysfunction described. Score must be < 55. Rising yields from data = normal.
- Score ~38.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff sees spreads as BELOW the midpoint (compressed). "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields narrowed a bit on net." CMBS spreads "little changed" and "near their post-crisis lows." This is compressed/narrow spread territory.
- Direction: Below midpoint (narrow spreads = low stress).
- Bright-line check: "narrowed" / "near post-crisis lows" -> MUST be < 50. PASS.
- Score ~28.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
"Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased about 4 percent." Positive earnings news. VIX "moved down but remained somewhat above the extremely low levels seen in late 2017." No valuation concern expressed by staff — prices rising on fundamentals (earnings). No "stretched" or "elevated valuations" language.
- Direction: Near midpoint, no particular concern. Prices rising but no valuation commentary.
- Score ~43 (typical 2013-19 range, slightly below neutral as equities rising without concern).

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Not specifically discussed. No mention of bank health, capital levels, lending standards changes, NPLs, or bank failures.
- Score: N/A.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"The broad dollar index moved up." Against AFE currencies "little changed" but "appreciated against EME currencies." EME stress (Turkey, Argentina) drove EME depreciation. Net: modest dollar strength, particularly against EMEs.
- Direction: Above midpoint (dollar appreciated).
- Bright-line check: Dollar appreciation -> MUST be > 50. PASS.
- Magnitude: "Little changed" vs AFEs, appreciated vs EMEs. Net modest. Score ~55.

**Inflation Sub-Components:**

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed in a way that distinguishes goods from services. N/A.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment declining, housing starts and permits stepping down, sales declining. But this is activity, not housing inflation/shelter costs. No shelter CPI/PCE discussion. N/A.

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance" — Michigan, SPF, Desk surveys all stable. Inflation compensation from TIPS "ticked up" 5-year, "little changed" 5-10 year ahead. Well-anchored at target.
- Score ~50.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63)**: Both above 50, consistent. Labor somewhat higher than output gap — reasonable given UE at 3.9% is a strong signal of tightness while output is only modestly above potential. Gap of 6 points, within tolerance.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (57) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (58)**: Very consistent. Both modestly above trend. PASS.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (28)**: Both below 50, consistent direction (loose/compressed). Gap of 7 points, reasonable. PASS.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (38)**: Both below 50. In normal times, Treasury stress near 40 is fine. PASS.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (50) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored at 50. Consistent. PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Core PCE price inflation, which excludes changes in consumer food and energy prices, was 2.0 percent over that same period." "The staff continued to project that total PCE inflation would remain near the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation exactly at the 2% target with stable expectations and a staff forecast of continued near-target inflation.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is at neutral because core PCE is exactly 2.0% and the staff projects it staying near target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Over the 2018-20 period, output was projected to rise at a rate above or at the staff's estimate of potential growth." "The upside risk that inflation could increase more than expected in an economy that was projected to move further above its potential."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating above potential with strong GDP growth and the staff projects it will move further above potential before slowing.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because the staff explicitly states the economy is above potential and projected to move further above it. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The national unemployment rate decreased to 3.9 percent in July and remained at that level in August." "With labor market conditions already tight, the staff continued to assume that projected employment gains would manifest in smaller-than-usual downward pressure on the unemployment rate."
- **Center-of-gravity**: UE at 3.9%, well below natural rate, with multiple indicators of tightness (elevated openings, rising quits, historically low claims, rising compensation). Staff explicitly describes conditions as "tight."
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because unemployment is well below NAIRU with broad-based indicators of tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real PCE appeared to be rising strongly in the third quarter." "Real residential investment looked to be declining further in the third quarter." "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to be moderating a little."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending is the dominant signal, partially offset by declining residential investment and moderating business equipment spending. Net is above trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is above neutral because strong consumption dominates despite some weakness in residential and moderation in business investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY

"Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses and households remained supportive of economic activity on balance." Repeated "accommodative" language across CRE, residential, consumer credit.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Broad-based accommodative financial conditions across all major sectors — corporate, small business, CRE, residential mortgage, and consumer credit.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because the staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative," "supportive," and "favorable." Bright-line rule satisfied.

- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally. Yield increases reflect stronger data and policy expectations, not market stress. Short-term funding smooth.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no dysfunction — yield movements are normal price adjustment. Bright-line rule satisfied (no dysfunction = no score above 55).

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Spreads of investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields narrowed a bit on net." "CMBS spreads were little changed over the intermeeting period and stayed near their post-crisis lows."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Corporate and CRE spreads compressed/near post-crisis lows, with further narrowing during the period. Low credit stress.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrow and near post-crisis lows. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased about 4 percent since the August FOMC meeting, as positive news about corporate earnings and the domestic economy outweighed negative international developments."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising on strong earnings fundamentals. No staff valuation concern expressed. VIX slightly elevated but declining. No "stretched" or "bubble" language.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly below neutral (low concern) because equities are rising on fundamentals with no valuation worry expressed.
