# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — June 28, 2000 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff content is mixed with participant/member discussion and policy action. I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by language cues such as "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast prepared for this meeting," and factual data descriptions in the early paragraphs.

### Staff-Attributable vs. Participant Content

**Staff-attributable content** (score these):
- "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested that the economic expansion was moderating somewhat from a very rapid pace..."
- Paragraphs describing nonfarm payrolls, industrial production, consumer spending data, housing, business fixed investment, inventories, trade, and prices — these are staff data reviews.
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting continued to suggest that the economic expansion would moderate gradually from its currently elevated pace..."
- Market developments paragraphs (intermeeting financial market review).
- M2/M3 discussion.

**Participant content** (ignore for scoring):
- "In the Committee's discussion of current and prospective economic developments, members cited..."
- "In their assessment of business conditions..."
- "In their comments on developments in key sectors..."
- "With regard to the outlook for inflation, members gave considerable attention..."
- All policy discussion paragraphs.

### Era Context
June 2000 — late-1990s boom era. The economy has been expanding rapidly, the dot-com bubble is near its peak (NASDAQ had already declined sharply from its March 2000 peak). Fed funds rate is at 6.5% after a 50bp hike in May. Unemployment is around 4%. This is a period of strong growth, tight labor markets, and emerging inflation concerns. The anchor for this era suggests OUTPUT_GAP 57-63, LABOR_TIGHTNESS 65-75.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as ABOVE neutral. Key staff language: "Core consumer prices continued to evidence some acceleration, to an important extent reflecting some indirect effects of the sharp increase in oil prices over the past year." Also: "consumer prices rose moderately further in May... both total and core consumer prices increased somewhat more than in the previous twelve-month period." Staff forecast: "Core price inflation was projected to rise noticeably over the forecast horizon, partly as a result of higher import prices and some firming of gains in nominal labor compensation in persistently tight labor markets that would not be fully offset by productivity growth."

Direction: Above 50. The language "some acceleration," "increased somewhat more," and forecast of "rise noticeably" suggests moderate above-target pressure. Not extreme — language is "some" and "somewhat" — but the staff forecast projects further increases. Score: 60 ±5. "Somewhat" = 5-10 from midpoint for current conditions, but the forward projection of "noticeably" rising inflation pushes it higher. Score: 62.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says acceleration in core prices with more projected. Score 62 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. Key staff language: "the economic expansion was moderating somewhat from a very rapid pace in the first quarter." Staff forecast: "the economic expansion would moderate gradually from its currently elevated pace to a rate around or perhaps a little below the growth of the economy's estimated potential." The language "currently elevated pace" and "very rapid pace" strongly indicate output above potential. Capacity utilization "lifted a little further, bringing it still closer to its long-term average" is less clear — this suggests capacity utilization is near but not yet at its long-term average.

However, the growth rate is described as "very rapid" and moderating. The staff expects it to come down to "around or perhaps a little below" potential growth. This implies the LEVEL of output is above potential and the gap is positive but expected to narrow.

Bright-line check: "elevated pace" and "very rapid pace" = above potential. Score MUST be > 50.

Score: 58 ±5. The economy is running above potential but moderating. The language is not "overheating" or "severe" — it's "elevated" and "moderating somewhat."

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. Key staff language: "The civilian unemployment rate averaged 4.0 percent over April and May." Staff forecast references "persistently tight labor markets." Average hourly earnings "registered only a slight increase in May" — wage growth is contained.

With UE at 4.0%, per the rubric anchor for 1999-2000 (65-75), this should be scored high. The staff forecast explicitly says "persistently tight labor markets." The fact that wage growth is contained does not reduce tightness — it reflects productivity offsetting labor costs.

Score: 70 ±5. UE at 4.0% with "persistently tight labor markets" language. The rubric says if UE is below 4%, score should almost certainly be above 55. At exactly 4.0%, with explicit "tight" language, 70 is appropriate for this era.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. UE at 4% with "persistently tight" = tight. Score 70 matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees spending as mixed but still solid overall. Consumer spending: "Growth of consumer spending apparently slowed considerably in the second quarter after outsized gains in several previous quarters." Housing: "Total private housing starts fell in May to their lowest level since the middle of last year." But business investment: "Business fixed investment appeared to be on track for another rapid increase in the second quarter." And "Shipments of nondefense capital goods, notably computing and communications equipment, continued on a strong uptrend."

Net position: Consumer spending slowing considerably, housing weakening, but business investment still "rapid" and "strong uptrend." Staff forecast: "business fixed investment, notably purchases of equipment and software, was projected to remain robust." The net is modestly above trend — consumer spending is decelerating from very strong to moderate, business investment remains strong.

Score: 55 ±7. The mix of slowing consumer/housing with strong business investment nets to modestly above trend.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Staff discusses productivity in the forecast: "not be fully offset by productivity growth." Also, from the participant section (which I normally would ignore), members note "rising productivity growth had contained labor cost pressures." The staff forecast implicitly acknowledges strong productivity by noting costs "would not be fully offset by productivity growth" — implying productivity growth is substantial but not enough to fully offset wage pressures. This is indirect but suggests above-trend productivity.

Score: 62 ±8 (Moderate confidence). Staff implicitly treats productivity as strong. The late-1990s/2000 era was the productivity boom period.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff-attributable market review: "yields on longer-term Treasury securities and investment-grade corporate bonds declined appreciably" over the intermeeting period. "Most broad stock price indexes ended the period little changed." Earlier: sharp declines in equity prices and widening risk spreads, then rebound. Also: "rising levels of... interest rates" and fed funds at 6.5%. Staff forecast notes "higher interest rates" restraining demand. Regional presidents note "cumulative effects of the firming in financial conditions this year."

The staff describes conditions that have tightened due to rate hikes — fed funds at 6.5%, mortgage rates restraining housing. But market conditions (longer-term yields declining, equities roughly flat) suggest conditions are not severely tight. The phrase "firming in financial conditions" from the regional overview is staff-adjacent.

Bright-line check: No explicit "accommodative" or "tight" language from staff proper, but "firming in financial conditions" and "higher interest rates" restraining spending = conditions tightening. Score should be > 50.

Score: 58 ±7. Conditions have tightened somewhat (rate hikes feeding through) but are not severely restrictive. Long-term yields actually declined over the intermeeting period.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
The staff describes normal market functioning: "yields on longer-term Treasury securities... declined appreciably." The yield movements are driven by data releases and policy expectations — this is normal price adjustment. No mention of liquidity problems, auction failures, or dysfunction.

Score: N/A or near-normal. The staff describes orderly yield movements. Score: 40 ±5 — normal market functioning with some volatility.

Actually, there is enough discussion of Treasury markets to score this rather than N/A. Yields moved on data and policy expectations — this is textbook normal functioning.

Score: 40 ±5.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Staff notes: "widening risk spreads on corporate bonds" during the intermeeting period, but then "debt and equity markets rebounded." On net, "yields on... investment-grade corporate bonds declined appreciably." This suggests spreads may have narrowed on net after the initial widening. The staff also notes "strong demands for bank credit." The description is of a temporary widening that reversed.

Score: 48 ±8. Spreads widened temporarily then reversed. Net position is roughly neutral. "Strong demands for bank credit" suggests credit is available.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Staff describes: "sharp further declines in equity prices" followed by a rebound, with "most broad stock price indexes ended the period little changed." No explicit valuation commentary from staff. The staff forecast mentions "anticipated waning of positive wealth effects associated with earlier large gains in equity prices." This is a factual description of price movements, not valuation assessment.

Given the dot-com era context and no specific staff valuation commentary, default to near-neutral. The "large gains in equity prices" language and wealth effects suggest elevated prior valuations but staff doesn't make a judgment call.

Score: 40 ±10 (Low confidence). Staff doesn't explicitly comment on valuations. The implicit reference to "large gains" and wealth effects suggests some elevation concern, pushing slightly below neutral (remember: lower = more overvaluation concern). Actually — let me re-check polarity. Higher = MORE equity market stress/concern. "Large gains" and leveling off suggests mild concern about valuation levels, but no "bubble" or "stretched" language. Price movements were roughly flat over the period. Score: 48 ±10.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Staff notes "strong demands for bank credit" and M3 acceleration driven by bank credit growth. No mention of banking system stress, NPLs, or capital concerns. The banking system appears healthy.

Bright-line check: No explicit "well-capitalized" language, but "strong demands for bank credit" funded normally suggests a healthy system. Score should be < 50.

Score: 35 ±10 (Low confidence). Limited direct discussion but what exists suggests healthy banking.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Staff reports: "the trade-weighted value of the dollar depreciated somewhat over the intermeeting period against an index of major currencies." Dollar weakened against euro and yen but "strengthened a little against the currencies of a group of other important trading partners."

Bright-line check: Net depreciation → score MUST be < 50.

Score: 42 ±5. Modest net depreciation ("somewhat") = modestly below 50.

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (58) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70)**: Gap of 12 points. 5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (62) ↔ Inflation sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_GOODS scored (48). The parent score is higher because the staff forecast projects rising core inflation and notes energy passthrough effects, which are broader than just goods prices. Coherent.
