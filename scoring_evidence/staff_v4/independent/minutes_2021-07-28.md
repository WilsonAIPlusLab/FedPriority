# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2021-07-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: July 2021 — the economy is in the reopening boom phase following COVID. Vaccinations are widespread in the US, the Delta variant is emerging as a concern, fiscal stimulus is still in effect. Inflation is running well above 2% but staff views it as largely transitory. Unemployment is at 5.9%, still elevated but declining rapidly. Supply chain bottlenecks are a dominant theme.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly ABOVE midpoint. Total PCE at 3.9%, core PCE at 3.4%, CPI at 5.4%, core CPI at 4.5%. Staff attributes much of this to transitory factors (base effects, reopening surge, supply bottlenecks), but per the rubric's gotcha rule, "transitory" language does NOT reduce the score — I score the current level. The trimmed mean at 1.9% provides some offset. Near-term outlook revised up further. Risks around inflation tilted to the upside. This is clearly elevated — anchor for 2021 Q3 is 65-72.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees GDP as having "increased at a faster pace" in Q2. Staff projects "real GDP was projected to post a substantial increase" for the year, with "a correspondingly large decline in the unemployment rate." Staff expects GDP growth to "outpace growth in potential output over most of this period." However, unemployment is still 5.9% — clearly above pre-pandemic levels. Staff describes supply constraints holding back production. The economy is recovering rapidly but still below potential given the high unemployment rate and the gap from pandemic losses ("total payroll employment had retraced more than two-thirds of the losses"). The language about growth outpacing potential suggests the gap is closing but hasn't closed yet. Score should be below 50 — slack remains, though rapidly diminishing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Unemployment at 5.9% — elevated. But job openings at highest recorded level since 2000. "Strong labor demand and increased difficulties in hiring." Average hourly earnings rising. Payrolls only retraced "more than two-thirds" of pandemic losses. LFPR unchanged. This is a bifurcated labor market: tight in some dimensions (openings, hiring difficulty, wage pressure) but still with significant slack (unemployment at 5.9%, participation not recovered). The level of UE at 5.9% per the rubric anchors to 31-45 range, but the hiring difficulties and record job openings push it up somewhat. Net: modestly below full employment but with notable tightness signals.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Real PCE rose in Q2 at a pace "similar to that seen in the first quarter" — the first quarter was strong given stimulus payments. Consumer spending "supported by previous rounds of federal stimulus payments and reductions in social distancing" but "held back some as producers struggled to meet demand." Residential investment "declined in the second quarter" due to materials shortages. Business fixed investment growth "slowed sharply in the second quarter" due to supply disruptions. Government purchases "moved lower." Net: consumer spending solid but held back; investment weak due to supply constraints. Overall somewhat below trend given the investment weakness.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states: "Market-based financing conditions were accommodative, and bank lending standards eased for most loan categories." "Financing conditions faced by nonfinancial firms in capital markets continued to be broadly accommodative." This triggers the bright-line rule: "accommodative" must be < 50. Multiple categories described as accommodative: consumer credit, residential mortgage, CRE (capital markets), municipal bonds. Small business conditions remain "relatively tight" — a notable exception. Overall clearly accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields fell over the intermeeting period. No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in Treasury markets. Short-term funding markets described as "stable." The ON RRP facility increase is about excess liquidity, not stress. No dysfunction signals. Score should be in the normal/calm range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "remained near the low end of their historical ranges." High-yield corporate bond spreads "tightened further and were near the low end of their historical range." CMBS spreads "generally at or below pre-pandemic levels." Municipal bond spreads increased moderately but "remained below their pre-pandemic levels." Bright-line: "near the low end of their historical range" = compressed/narrow, score MUST be < 50. Very compressed spreads.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff explicitly states: "asset valuation pressures were elevated." "Forward price-to-earnings ratio for the S&P 500 index stood at the upper end of its historical distribution." House prices "continued to increase rapidly, leaving valuation measures stretched." Equity prices "rose moderately." Staff characterizes financial vulnerabilities as "notable" partly due to elevated valuations. This is clearly an overvaluation concern — score should be low (more concern about frothy valuations).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff says "aggregate common equity tier 1 capital ratio of the largest banks significantly exceeded regulatory requirements." Domestic banks held "significant quantities of high-quality liquid assets" with "only limited reliance on short-term wholesale funding." However, some hedge fund leverage elevated, and data gaps at nonbank financial institutions. Credit quality stable, upgrades outpacing downgrades, defaults low. Banks are well-capitalized and healthy. Bright-line: "well-capitalized" must be < 50. Some concerns about nonbank entities but banking system itself is sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar broadly appreciated" over the intermeeting period. Bright-line: appreciation = score > 50.

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Surge in goods prices driven by "production bottlenecks and supply constraints." Motor vehicle production disrupted, supply chain issues. PCE/CPI elevated largely from goods categories. Clearly elevated.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Services sector reopening contributing to inflation. Leisure and hospitality strong job growth (implying reopening). Housing costs discussed separately. Services inflation present but the text focuses more on goods/supply chain.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices continued to increase rapidly, leaving valuation measures stretched." "Very strong demand for housing" but residential investment declined due to materials shortages and limited stocks. This is about house prices not shelter CPI directly, but clearly elevated housing cost pressures.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff's common inflation expectations index "had more than reversed the moderate decline recorded in the middle of last year and had returned to the level that prevailed in 2014, when actual inflation was relatively modest." Longer-term forward measures of inflation compensation "fell notably." This suggests expectations roughly back to normal/anchored levels — the 2014 level was a time of modest inflation, and longer-term measures declining. Near anchored at target.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Total PCE price inflation was 3.9 percent over the 12 months ending in May, and core PCE price inflation...was 3.4 percent over the 12 months ending in May."
- "The 12-month change in the CPI was 5.4 percent, while the core CPI rose 4.5 percent over the same period."
- "The staff's near-term outlook for inflation was revised up further...PCE price inflation was projected to be running well above 2 percent at the end of the year."
- "The staff judged that the risks around the inflation projection were now tilted to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is running well above target across all measures, staff has revised up the near-term outlook, and risks are tilted to the upside — though staff expects moderation over time and the trimmed mean is near 2%.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE is at 3.9%, core PCE at 3.4%, CPI at 5.4%, and the staff sees upside risks to inflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Total payroll employment had retraced more than two-thirds of the losses seen at the onset of the pandemic."
- "The unemployment rate...stood at 5.9 percent in June."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is growing rapidly and closing the output gap, but with unemployment at 5.9% and one-third of pandemic job losses not recovered, significant slack remains. Staff projects above-potential growth will eventually close this gap.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because unemployment remains at 5.9% and payrolls have not fully recovered, indicating remaining slack. Bright-line rule satisfied: slack remains -> score < 50.
