# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Post-2005 segmented minutes. Three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: December 2016 — late-cycle expansion. Unemployment had fallen to 4.6%, near or slightly below the natural rate. Inflation still below 2% but trending up. Post-U.S.-election period with expectations of fiscal stimulus. The Committee was raising rates (this was the second rate hike of the cycle).

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation is "almost 1.5 percent" over 12 months, core PCE "about 1.75 percent" — both below the 2% target. Staff says inflation "increased further above its pace early in the year but was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." The forecast projects inflation "marginally below" 2% even in 2019. This is clearly below target but trending up — consistent with 38-42 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. The staff outlook explicitly states "real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019" and "the economy was projected to continue operating above its long-run potential." Unemployment was "forecast to continue to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires score > 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 4.6% (below natural rate per staff), "labor market conditions had tightened further," "solid pace" of payroll gains, job openings and quits at levels "above those seen during much of the current economic expansion." With UE at 4.6% and projected to go lower, and staff explicitly saying it's below the natural rate, this is clearly above neutral. The rubric says UE ~4.5-5% maps to 46-52 (near NAIRU), but since UE is already at 4.6% and projected to decline further below the natural rate, this is slightly above that range — around 53-55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees a MIXED picture, roughly at trend. Consumer spending "rising at a moderate pace," residential investment "picking up," but business equipment spending "seemed to be soft," and industrial production "flat." Net exports expected to be a drag. Government purchases "rising modestly." The "moderate pace" language for the dominant consumption component, offset by soft business investment, puts this near 48-50.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff EXPLICITLY mentions productivity: "Labor productivity in the business sector was flat over the four quarters ending in the third quarter." Flat productivity is below trend — maps to 30-35 range.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE. "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms and households remained broadly accommodative," "financing conditions for commercial real estate also remained largely accommodative," "consumer credit continued to be readily available." Bright-line rule: "accommodative" requires score < 50. Given "broadly accommodative" across multiple sectors, this is 30-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Nominal Treasury yields moved up considerably." But this was driven by policy expectations and fiscal stimulus expectations — normal market functioning. No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues. Money market flows "continued to stabilize." Bright-line rule: rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 45-55). No dysfunction described, so score in the 40-45 range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff says "credit spreads on corporate bonds narrowed," "yield spreads of nonfinancial corporate bonds... narrowed for both investment- and speculative-grade firms," "spreads on commercial mortgage-backed securities narrowed a little." Bright-line rule: narrow/compressed spreads must be < 50. Strong narrowing and accommodative credit = 28-35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff reports equity prices "rose over the intermeeting period," driven by "expectations of stronger earnings growth and improved risk sentiment." Implied volatility decreased. No valuation concern language. "Earnings... increased... broad based across sectors." No staff concern about valuations — default to 45-50 range.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not directly discussed as a separate topic. There is mention of "growth in commercial and industrial loans from banks picked up," CRE loan growth "continued to be strong," and subprime auto lending "edged down." No stress language. Italy banking concerns mentioned but as foreign development. Bright-line: no domestic banking stress discussed, system functioning normally. Score 30-35 (healthy, low stress). Confidence low because limited direct discussion.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff says "the dollar appreciated notably against both AFE and EME currencies," "the dollar strengthened about 10 percent against the Japanese yen and 5 percent against the Mexican peso." Bright-line rule: appreciation requires > 50. "Notable" appreciation = 63-68.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Non-energy import prices declining, "decreases in the prices of non-energy imports." Food prices also declining. This is below neutral — 35-38.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed in a way that allows clean scoring. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing discussed in terms of activity (starts, permits), not housing/shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Median longer-run inflation expectations edged up, on net." Survey of Professional Forecasters "unchanged." Primary Dealer and Market Participant surveys "also unchanged." Market-based measures of inflation compensation "continued to move up." This is roughly anchored with slight upward drift — around 50-52.

### Polarity Checks

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Staff says below target. Score below 50. CORRECT.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Staff says above potential. Score above 50. CORRECT.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE at 4.6% below natural rate. Score above 50. CORRECT.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Staff says accommodative. Score below 50. CORRECT.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Staff says narrowing. Score below 50. CORRECT.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. No stress described, healthy lending. Score below 50. CORRECT.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar appreciated notably. Score above 50. CORRECT.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction described. Score near or below 50. CORRECT.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (54)**: Both slightly above 50, coherent. Economy modestly above potential with labor market slightly tight.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (52) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (48)**: Close together (4 points apart), coherent. Moderate growth near trend with economy modestly above potential from cumulative prior growth.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (30)**: Both below 50, coherent. Accommodative conditions with narrow spreads.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: FC loose while Treasury markets functioning normally. Coherent.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (40) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods below neutral (~37), expectations at neutral (~51). Parent at 40 is consistent given goods drag and energy/import effects holding down headline.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation increased further above its pace early in the year but was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." PCE "increased almost 1.5 percent over the 12 months ending in October." Core PCE "rose about 1.75 percent." Staff projects inflation "marginally below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation trending up but still meaningfully below the 2% target, with staff projecting it to remain below target for years.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below target at ~1.5% headline / ~1.75% core.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff projected that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 through 2019." "The unemployment rate was forecast to... continue to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "The economy was projected to continue operating above its long-run potential."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential with unemployment below the natural rate — a small positive output gap.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says economy is "operating above its long-run potential." Bright-line satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined, reaching 4.6 percent in November." "Labor market conditions had tightened further." UE "forecast to continue to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "Compensation per hour in the business sector rose 3 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market at or slightly beyond full employment with UE at 4.6%, below the natural rate, with moderate but not strong wage growth.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE is below the natural rate and conditions described as having "tightened further."

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be rising at a moderate pace." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property seemed to be soft early in the fourth quarter." "Real residential investment was picking up." "Real net exports would make a negative contribution."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate consumer spending and housing recovery offset by soft business investment and negative net exports — roughly at trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is near/slightly below neutral due to the mixed signals across components.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor productivity in the business sector was flat over the four quarters ending in the third quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Flat productivity is well below historical trend, indicating poor productivity performance.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because flat productivity is clearly below the ~2% historical trend.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms and households remained broadly accommodative." "Financing conditions for commercial real estate also remained largely accommodative." "Consumer credit continued to be readily available for most borrowers." "Credit spreads on corporate bonds narrowed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broadly accommodative conditions across all sectors despite some rise in rates.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (looser) because staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields moved up considerably since the November FOMC meeting." But increases driven by "expectations of more expansionary fiscal policy" and "inflation compensation." Money market flows "continued to stabilize."
**Center-of-gravity**: Yields rose significantly but this reflected normal repricing of fiscal/inflation expectations, not dysfunction. No liquidity or functioning concerns.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral — yield movements from policy expectations are normal market functioning, not stress.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yield spreads of nonfinancial corporate bonds over those of comparable-maturity Treasury securities narrowed for both investment- and speculative-grade firms." "Spreads on commercial mortgage-backed securities narrowed a little." "The decrease in corporate bond spreads after the U.S. elections suggests that the lower issuance did not reflect a tightening of financial conditions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads actively narrowing across corporate and CMBS markets — below normal, favorable credit conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and described in the context of accommodative conditions. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes rose over the intermeeting period, apparently boosted by investors' expectations of stronger earnings growth and improved risk sentiment." "Implied volatility in equity markets decreased." "Earnings for firms in the S&P 500 index increased... broad based across sectors."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising on improved fundamentals with no valuation concern expressed. Slightly below neutral stress — market optimism.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near/slightly below neutral — no stress concern expressed, rising on fundamentals.

### BANKING_STRESS: 33 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth in commercial and industrial loans from banks picked up." "The average rate of growth of CRE loans at banks continued to be strong." Consumer credit "readily available." No discussion of banking system stress or capital concerns.
**Center-of-gravity**: Banks lending actively across C&I, CRE, and consumer segments with no stress signals — healthy system.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because lending growth is strong with no stress indicators. Bright-line satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "On a trade-weighted basis, the dollar appreciated notably against both AFE and EME currencies." "The dollar strengthened about 10 percent against the Japanese yen and 5 percent against the Mexican peso."
**Center-of-gravity**: Notable, broad-based dollar appreciation driven by higher U.S. yields and fiscal expectations.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "appreciated notably." Bright-line satisfied.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 37 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Decreases in the prices of non-energy imports." "Recent decreases in consumer food prices." "Earlier declines in consumer energy prices" (though oil prices now rising).
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices depressed by import prices and food prices, though energy beginning to turn.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 51 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The Michigan survey measure of median longer-run inflation expectations edged up, on net." Survey of Professional Forecasters "unchanged." Primary Dealer and Market Participant surveys "also unchanged in December." "Measures of inflation compensation extended an upward trajectory."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey-based expectations stable/anchored; market-based measures drifting up. Roughly at target.
