# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2021-11-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

### Era Context
November 2021. The U.S. economy is in recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic. Inflation has surged well above 2% due to supply bottlenecks, energy prices, and reopening demand. The labor market is improving rapidly but has not fully recovered. Supply chain disruptions are a dominant theme. The Fed is about to begin tapering asset purchases.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly ABOVE midpoint. PCE at 4.4%, core PCE at 3.6%, both well above 2%. Staff revised near-term inflation outlook UP. Food and energy prices rose faster than expected. Production bottlenecks and wage gains putting "greater upward pressure on prices." Inflation expectations at highest level since 2014. The "transitory" framing (expected to step down to 2% in 2022) does NOT reduce the score per the rubric — score the current elevated reading. Anchor: 65-72 for 2021 Q3-Q4 "elevated."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff explicitly states "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "above potential" language requires score > 50. However, Q3 GDP growth slowed markedly due to supply constraints, delta variant, waning fiscal stimulus. Staff projection was "slightly weaker" than September. Still, the LEVEL is well above potential even if the GROWTH rate slowed. Score should reflect the level being above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment fell to 4.8% in September. Employment retraced three-fourths of pandemic losses. Job openings "well above pre-pandemic levels." ECI rose 4.1% over 12 months — fastest since 2001. Initial claims approaching pre-pandemic levels. However, labor force participation edged lower, and employment growth slowed. The labor market is tightening meaningfully but UE at 4.8% with participation still depressed suggests not yet at full employment. Staff expects UE to "decline to historically low levels." Score: tightening but not yet very tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Real PCE posted only "a modest increase" in Q3 after "having risen sharply" in H1. Residential investment "dropped further." Business fixed investment growth "slowed sharply." Motor vehicle purchases plunged. Manufacturing output declined. These are clearly below-trend readings for Q3, though driven by supply constraints rather than demand weakness. Demand for housing was "strong" but supply constrained. Net: spending activity is below trend in Q3.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "market-based financing conditions were accommodative" and "bank lending standards eased for most loan categories." Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms "remained highly accommodative." Residential mortgage conditions "remained accommodative." Consumer credit "remained accommodative." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "accommodative" requires score < 50. This is clearly loose/accommodative.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose notably over the intermeeting period due to "increase in perceived inflation risks" and upward revision in expected policy path. However, this is normal yield adjustment from policy/inflation expectations — NOT dysfunction. Short-term funding markets were "stable." No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction issues. Per rubric, rising yields from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of corporate bonds were little changed overall" and "remained at low levels." Municipal bond spreads "narrowed slightly." Bond and leveraged loan spreads "remained narrow." CMBS spreads "at or below pre-pandemic levels." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "narrow" spreads require score < 50. This is clearly low-stress credit.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff notes "asset valuations remained generally high relative to historical norms." Equity prices "continued to increase, supported by strong earnings expectations, still-low Treasury yields, and high risk appetite." VIX "declined notably to near pre-pandemic levels." This is high-valuation language — staff sees elevated valuations but not distress. Higher score = more stress/concern. Staff language is about elevated valuations (overvaluation concern), not depressed prices.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff notes "banks were strongly capitalized, with high levels of stable funding and high-quality liquid assets." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "strongly capitalized" requires score < 50. Low stress. Some mention of hedge fund leverage being "noteworthy" and stablecoin risks, but banking system itself is healthy.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar appreciated a touch." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: appreciation = score > 50. But "a touch" is minimal — just slightly above neutral.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Strong signal. Motor vehicle prices surging due to semiconductor shortages. Supply bottlenecks heavily affecting goods. PCE at 4.4% driven substantially by goods price pressures.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed in detail. Housing discussed separately. Services activity is reopening. Limited direct service-price language.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "House prices rose rapidly, outpacing rents." Demand for housing was "strong." But residential investment dropped due to supply constraints. Housing prices are clearly elevated.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff's common inflation expectations index "was little changed relative to the second quarter and remained at its highest level since 2014." This is modestly above neutral — expectations somewhat elevated but not unanchored.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence

Explanation: Q3 spending was weak due to supply constraints, but the LEVEL of GDP remains above potential because of strong H1 momentum. ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**Evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation well above target with upward revisions, broad-based pressures from supply bottlenecks, energy, food, and wages, though expected to subside. Per the rubric, score the current elevated state.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE is more than double the 2% target and the staff revised the outlook upward.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 57 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite a slowdown in Q3 growth, the staff explicitly states GDP is well above potential. The growth slowdown is from supply constraints, not demand weakness.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff says GDP level is "well above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is slightly above neutral — market is clearly tightening with strong wage pressure signals, but UE at 4.8% and incomplete employment recovery keep it moderate.
