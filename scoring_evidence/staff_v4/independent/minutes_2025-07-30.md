# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2025-07-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (post-2005, segmented). It contains clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Participant views follow in a separate section. I will score only staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
July 2025: Post-tariff-hike environment. The economy is slowing from tariff effects, inflation is somewhat above target with tariff pass-through adding upward pressure, the labor market remains solid but showing some signs of softening, and financial conditions are relatively supportive. The federal funds rate is at 4.25-4.50%.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE the midpoint. Key evidence: PCE estimated at 2.5% in June, core PCE at 2.7%—both above the 2% target. "Disinflation appeared to have stalled, with tariffs putting upward pressure on goods price inflation." Staff projects tariffs to "raise inflation this year and to provide some further upward pressure on inflation in 2026." Risks to inflation skewed to the upside. This is modestly above target — "somewhat elevated" language. Score should be in the 57-63 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as AT or SLIGHTLY BELOW potential. "Real GDP expanded at a tepid pace" in the first half. "Real GDP rose in the second quarter after declining in the first quarter." Staff expects the unemployment rate "to move above the staff's estimate of its natural rate around the end of this year and to remain above the natural rate through 2027." This means the economy is currently near potential but projected to slip below. Growth was "tepid" and the staff projects weakening. Score should be around 45-48 (slightly below potential, heading lower).

**Bright-line check**: Staff expects unemployment to move ABOVE the natural rate, implying output will be BELOW potential soon. Current GDP growth is "tepid." Score < 50 is appropriate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as SOLID but with signs of softening. Unemployment at 4.1% in June (down 0.1pp from May). Payroll gains were "solid" but private payroll gains "stepped down noticeably." Job vacancies-to-unemployed ratio at 1.1. Average hourly earnings at 3.7% (slightly lower than year-earlier). Staff expects the labor market to weaken. At 4.1% unemployment the market is still modestly tight — score around 53-55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend. GDP growth was "tepid." PDFP growth "slowed in the second quarter, as a step-down in investment growth offset faster PCE growth." Activity "slowed in the first half of the year, driven in large part by slower consumption growth and a decline in residential investment." Score around 40-43.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed by staff. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes conditions as easing/supportive. "Credit spreads tightened" (narrowed), "broad equity price indexes increased," borrowing costs "eased moderately but remained elevated relative to post-GFC average levels." "Credit remained generally available." Financing through capital markets and nonbank lenders was "readily accessible." The staff outlook notes "financial conditions were projected to be slightly more supportive of output growth." Several participants noted the current policy rate "may not be far above its neutral level" and "broader financial conditions were either neutral or supportive of stronger economic activity." This points to conditions that are roughly neutral to slightly accommodative. Score around 43-47.

**Bright-line check**: Language leans toward "supportive"/"generally available"/"eased" — this suggests conditions are slightly accommodative, so score should be < 50 or just at 50. Score 45 fits.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Conditions in U.S. short-term funding markets remained orderly." "Nominal Treasury yields were little changed, on net." SRF usage was $11.1 billion at quarter-end (highest since inception), but this was described as a normal quarter-end dynamic. Staff financial stability section notes "vulnerabilities in the market for Treasury securities, raising concerns about dealer intermediation capacity, the increasing presence of hedge funds in the market, and the fragility associated with low market depth." This is an important structural concern but not acute dysfunction. Score around 45-48 — normal functioning with noted structural vulnerabilities.

**Bright-line check**: No actual market dysfunction described, just structural vulnerabilities. Yields little changed. Score in 36-50 range is appropriate.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff clearly states spreads NARROWED. "Credit spreads tightened" (narrowed). Spreads "for all rating categories except the lowest-quality bonds narrowed to exceptionally low levels relative to their historical distribution." High-yield spreads "narrowed notably and were low relative to their historical distribution." This is clearly below normal — compressed spreads. Score around 18-22.

**Bright-line check**: "Narrowed"/"exceptionally low" = score MUST be < 50. Score 20 fits.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff's financial stability assessment: "Asset valuation pressures were elevated. In equity markets, price-to-earnings ratios stood at the upper end of their historical distribution." "Broad equity price indexes increased." Staff sees elevated valuations — overvaluation concern. Score around 22-28 (elevated valuations = lower scores per rubric, where lower = more overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff characterizes financial vulnerabilities as "notable." "Leverage in the financial sector were characterized as notable." "Regulatory capital ratios in the banking sector remained high" and "all participants stayed above minimum capital requirements even under stress conditions." However, "banks were still seen as more exposed to interest rate risk than had been historically typical." "Some banks continued to be vulnerable to a rise in longer-term yields and the associated unrealized losses on bank assets." Credit performance was "generally stable but somewhat weaker than pre-pandemic levels." CRE delinquencies elevated. This is a mixed picture — well-capitalized but with notable vulnerabilities. Score around 40-45.

**Bright-line check**: Capital levels are strong ("remained high"), but interest rate risk exposure and CRE delinquencies add stress. Score just below 50 is appropriate.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index was little changed on net." This is neutral/stable. Score around 48-50.

**Bright-line check**: "Little changed" = stable = score near 50. Score 49 fits.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Housing valuations edged down but remained elevated." Residential investment declined. Not much direct discussion of shelter/housing inflation by staff specifically. Score N/A (staff doesn't discuss housing inflation as a price metric directly).

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (54)**: Gap of 8 points. Reasonable — labor market is a lagging indicator, still tight while output is already softening.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (46) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (42)**: Gap of 4 points. Coherent — both below trend.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (45) vs TREASURY_STRESS (46)**: Consistent — both near neutral.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (59) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~63, Services ~49, Expectations ~54. Weighted average roughly consistent with 59 overall given goods inflation is the dominant discussion topic.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 59 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Disinflation appeared to have stalled, with tariffs putting upward pressure on goods price inflation." PCE estimated at 2.5%, core PCE at 2.7% in June. "Both inflation rates were similar to their year-earlier levels." Staff projects tariffs to "raise inflation this year and to provide some further upward pressure on inflation in 2026."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is modestly above the 2% target at 2.5-2.7%, stalled in its progress, with tariff-related upside risks — placing this clearly above neutral but not dramatically so.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is above neutral because PCE is running 2.5-2.7% (above 2% target) and disinflation has stalled.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP expanded at a tepid pace." GDP "rose in the second quarter after declining in the first quarter." Staff expects "the unemployment rate to move above the staff's estimate of its natural rate around the end of this year and to remain above the natural rate through 2027."
**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing tepidly and is projected to slip below potential, with unemployment expected to exceed the natural rate by year-end.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because growth is "tepid" and unemployment is projected to move above the natural rate — bright-line rule satisfied.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 4.1 percent in June, down 0.1 percentage point from May." "Total nonfarm payroll gains were solid in June, though the pace of private payroll gains stepped down noticeably." "The ratio of job vacancies to unemployed workers was 1.1 in June." Average hourly earnings rose 3.7%.
**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market remains modestly tight with 4.1% unemployment and solid payrolls, but private payroll gains are slowing and the staff expects weakening ahead.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is slightly above neutral — 4.1% unemployment is below most NAIRU estimates, supporting a score modestly above 50.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP expanded at a tepid pace." "Growth of real private domestic final purchases... slowed in the second quarter, as a step-down in investment growth offset faster PCE growth." Activity "slowed in the first half of the year, driven in large part by slower consumption growth and a decline in residential investment."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are below trend — PDFP slowed, residential investment declined, and overall activity was tepid.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because growth is "tepid," investment declined, and consumption slowed.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No specific staff discussion of productivity trends.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Borrowing costs facing businesses, households, and municipalities eased moderately but remained elevated relative to post-GFC average levels." "Credit remained generally available." "Financial conditions were projected to be slightly more supportive of output growth." Equity prices increased and credit spreads tightened.
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral to slightly accommodative — credit is available, spreads are compressed, equities rose, but borrowing costs remain elevated relative to historical norms.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is at or slightly below neutral — "supportive" and "eased" language points to < 50. Bright-line rule satisfied.

Staff notes structural vulnerabilities: "concerns about dealer intermediation capacity, the increasing presence of hedge funds in the market, and the fragility associated with low market depth."
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets are functioning normally with no acute stress, but the staff flags structural vulnerabilities around dealer capacity and hedge fund concentration.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at neutral — orderly conditions with noted structural concerns. No actual dysfunction described. Bright-line rule satisfied (no dysfunction = score 36-50).

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Credit spreads tightened... for all rating categories except the lowest-quality bonds narrowed to exceptionally low levels relative to their historical distribution." "High-yield corporate bonds narrowed notably and were low relative to their historical distribution."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are compressed to exceptional lows across the quality spectrum — clearly below normal, signaling very low credit stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are at "exceptionally low levels." Bright-line rule satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Asset valuation pressures were elevated. In equity markets, price-to-earnings ratios stood at the upper end of their historical distribution." "Broad equity price indexes increased."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees elevated equity valuations with P/E ratios at the upper end of historical range — overvaluation concern.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is below neutral (indicating overvaluation concern, not stress/depression). P/E at upper end of distribution = elevated valuations = score 20-30.

**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is slightly below neutral — capital is strong (low stress) but interest rate risk and CRE issues add modest stress. Bright-line rule: "remained high" capital = below 50. Satisfied.
