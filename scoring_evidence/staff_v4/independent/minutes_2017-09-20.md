# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2017-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: September 2017 — mid-expansion, labor market near full employment with unemployment at 4.3-4.4%, inflation persistently running below 2% target. Balance sheet normalization beginning. Hurricanes Harvey and Irma affecting near-term data. This is the 2016-19 "near potential" era.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation "continued to run below 2 percent in July and was lower than at the start of the year." Core PCE at about 1.5%. Staff projects inflation will "edge higher" and "reach the Committee's longer-run objective in 2019" — so below target now, expected to eventually converge. The "lower than at the start of the year" language indicates recent deterioration. This places it in the 26-40 range ("below target; running below 2 percent"). Score ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential. The staff outlook states: "the staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output through 2019" and "the economy was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential." The unemployment rate was "projected to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language mandates score > 50. However, "modestly faster" is not extreme. Real GDP "rising at a moderate pace." Score ~53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 4.3-4.4% is below the staff's estimate of the natural rate (they revised down their natural rate estimate). "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." Job openings increased, hiring ticked up. However, wage measures are mixed — compensation per hour rose just 1.25%, ECI at 2.5%, average hourly earnings at 2.5%. The unemployment rate places this solidly in the 53-65 range. Score ~55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED but roughly at trend. Consumer spending "likely increasing at a slower rate in the third quarter than in the second" — retail sales declined, motor vehicle sales moved lower. But "key factors that influence consumer spending... remained supportive of solid growth." Business equipment and IP spending "increasing at a solid rate." However, residential investment was "decreasing," and nonresidential structures were "poised to decline." Housing was weak. Net of hurricane effects, the staff outlook says "Real GDP was expected to rise at a solid pace." Net assessment: roughly at trend, maybe slightly below given the mixed signals. Score ~48.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states conditions are "generally accommodative." Financing conditions "continued to provide support for growth in spending and investment." Consumer credit "readily available for most borrowers." CRE financing "broadly available." BRIGHT-LINE: "accommodative" mandates score < 50. The language is clear and repeated. Score ~35. Wait — the rubric anchor for 2017-19 neutral is 46-54, and the CRITICAL note says the 2017-19 period was underscored at 34-42. But the staff explicitly says "accommodative" and "supportive," which per the bright-line rule must be < 50. The staff is describing conditions as loose/supportive, not neutral. I'll score 38 — accommodative but not extremely so.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined modestly, attributed to normal factors (FOMC communications, geopolitical tensions, fiscal policy expectations). Short-dated T-bill yields were "elevated for a time" due to debt ceiling concerns, but this resolved ("retraced their entire increase"). "Conditions in other domestic short-term funding markets were stable." No dysfunction described. Normal market functioning. Score ~40.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporates "widened a bit." CMBS spreads "narrowed a little... near the bottom of their ranges of the past several years." Municipal bond spreads "increased a bit." Corporate debt issuance was "strong." Leveraged loan issuance "robust." Overall, spreads near the favorable end — "widened a bit" from compressed levels is still below normal. Score ~35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased over the intermeeting period." VIX "remained at historically low levels." No valuation concern language from staff. Equities rising with low vol — no distress, no explicit bubble language. Default to 45-55 range. Score ~45.

**BANKING_STRESS**: No explicit banking stress discussion. CRE loan growth "continued to moderate." C&I loan growth "ticked up" but remained "significantly lower than over recent years." Credit conditions tight for subprime borrowers, rising delinquencies on subprime auto and credit card. Credit quality of state/local governments "improved overall." Some pockets of concern (subprime auto, CRE moderation) but no systemic stress language. Score ~35 — low stress with minor pockets.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar decreased 1.5 percent; the decline was widespread." BRIGHT-LINE: depreciation mandates score < 50. Clear weakening. Score ~30.

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Above potential" = > 50. Score 53 > 50. Correct.

- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. No systemic stress, minor subprime pockets. Score 35 < 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar depreciated 1.5%. Score 30 < 50. Correct.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Coherent. Both low/calm.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total consumer price inflation... continued to run below 2 percent in July and was lower than at the start of the year." "Core PCE price inflation... also was about 1.5 percent." Staff projects inflation will "reach the Committee's longer-run objective in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation clearly below target at ~1.5% and deteriorating from start of year, with staff expecting slow convergence.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — inflation running well below 2% target with downward trajectory from early 2017.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff continued to project that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output through 2019." "The economy was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential with staff projecting unemployment below natural rate.
**Direction confirmation**: Above neutral because staff explicitly says economy operating above longer-run potential. Bright-line satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen." "The unemployment rate dipped to 4.3 percent in July and edged back up to 4.4 percent in August." Unemployment "projected to continue running below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Wage growth mixed: ECI at 2.5%, compensation per hour at just 1.25%.
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market tight by unemployment metric (below natural rate), but mixed wage signals temper the score.
**Direction confirmation**: Above neutral — unemployment below natural rate indicates tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE was likely increasing at a slower rate in the third quarter." "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to be increasing at a solid rate." "Real residential investment spending was decreasing in the third quarter after declining in the second quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — consumer spending slowing, business equipment solid, housing declining, hurricanes causing temporary drag. Net slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: Slightly below neutral due to slowing consumption, declining residential investment, and hurricane effects, despite solid business equipment spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial market conditions remained generally accommodative." "Financing conditions for households and nonfinancial businesses continued to provide support for growth in spending and investment." "Financing conditions for U.S. nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff repeatedly characterizes conditions as accommodative and supportive. Clear signal of loose conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — "accommodative" means loose/easy, which means low score on the tightness scale. Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Yields on intermediate- and longer-term nominal Treasury securities decreased modestly over the intermeeting period." "Conditions in other domestic short-term funding markets were stable." Short-dated T-bill yields were elevated briefly due to debt ceiling concerns but "retraced their entire increase."
**Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning. Yield movements driven by standard macro factors. Brief debt-ceiling concern fully resolved.
**Direction confirmation**: Near neutral-to-low — no dysfunction described. The brief T-bill episode resolved cleanly.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on investment- and speculative-grade nonfinancial corporate bonds... widened a bit." CMBS spreads "narrowed a little over the intermeeting period and were near the bottom of their ranges of the past several years." "Issuance of corporate debt and equity was strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads generally favorable with strong issuance — slightly below normal consistent with accommodative financial conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — spreads near the bottom of multi-year ranges indicates favorable credit conditions (low stress).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes increased over the intermeeting period." "Option-implied volatility of the S&P 500 index—the VIX—remained at historically low levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities rising with historically low volatility. No valuation concern or distress language from staff. Roughly neutral to slightly favorable.
**Direction confirmation**: Near neutral — no explicit staff valuation commentary; equities rising but no concern expressed.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer credit continued to be readily available for most borrowers." "Standards and terms on auto and credit card loans were tighter for subprime borrowers, likely in response to rising delinquencies on such loans." CRE loan growth "continued to moderate" but CRE financing "appeared to remain broadly available."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system functioning well with some targeted tightening in subprime segments. No systemic concerns.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — credit broadly available, banking system sound with only minor pockets of caution. Low stress.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 30 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad index of the foreign exchange value of the dollar decreased 1.5 percent; the decline was widespread, led by the strengthening of the euro and the Chinese renminbi."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear, broad-based dollar depreciation of 1.5%.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral — dollar weakened. Bright-line satisfied (depreciation = score < 50).

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." Michigan 5-10 year "edged back up," SPF 10-year "edged down," Primary Dealers "relatively little changed."
**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations well-anchored and stable, though in an environment of below-target actual inflation, suggesting slight downside lean.
**Direction confirmation**: Near neutral, slightly below — anchored but in a below-target inflation environment.
