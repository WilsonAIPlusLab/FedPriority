# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2018-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: March 2018 — late-cycle expansion. Unemployment at 4.1%, below most estimates of NAIRU. Tax cuts recently enacted. Inflation still running below 2% target but expected to reach it. Fed in gradual tightening mode. Recent equity volatility (VIX spike February 5).

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Core PCE at 1.5%, total PCE at 1.75% — both below the 2% target. However, staff projects inflation will reach 2% in 2019 and notes "slightly tighter resource utilization" pushing the medium-term projection up. This is classic 2018 "below target but approaching" language. Per rubric anchors, early 2018 "at target" = 50, but the actual readings here are still below target, so slightly below 50. Score range: 41-46.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as ABOVE potential (score MUST be > 50 per bright-line rule). Key evidence: "Real GDP was projected to increase at a faster pace than potential output through 2020." "The unemployment rate was projected to...continue to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "The economy was already operating above its potential level and resource utilization continued to tighten, as the staff projected." This is clear "above potential" language. The staff projects it will move "further above its potential." This is modestly above potential — not extreme overheating. Score range: 54-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT (above midpoint). Unemployment at 4.1%, "labor market conditions continued to strengthen," job openings and quits increased, initial claims low. However, wage growth is "modest" (2.5-2.75%), and participation rate "stepped up markedly." The UE rate of 4.1% is below most NAIRU estimates (~4.5%), and staff projects it to "decline further" and "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Per the rubric, UE below 4.5% with modest wage pressure = 53-65 range. At 4.1% with modest wages, score ~58-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MODERATELY POSITIVE but mixed. Consumer expenditures "likely to rise at a modest pace in the first quarter following a strong gain in the preceding quarter" — the softness is seen as transitory (delayed tax refunds). Business equipment spending "moderating" but forward-looking indicators "pointed to further solid gains." Residential investment "slowing." Government purchases "flattening out." Net exports drag lessening. Overall: near trend, slightly positive. The language of "moderate pace," "solid gains" ahead, and transitory softness supports a score around 48-53.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "financing conditions for nonfinancial corporations and households remained accommodative" and "continued to support further expansion of economic activity." The word "accommodative" appears multiple times (credit conditions, CMBS, municipal bonds, residential mortgage). Per bright-line rule, "accommodative" = MUST be < 50. Despite some turbulence and volatility, overall conditions are described as accommodative. Score range: 35-42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields rose, but the staff describes this as driven by "monetary policy communications, higher-than-expected domestic price data, and expectations for increases in the supply of Treasury securities." This is normal price adjustment, NOT dysfunction. There is mention of increased T-bill issuance pushing bill yields above OIS rates and some money market rate pressure, but no language of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market breakdown. "Reductions in the size of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet continued as scheduled without a notable effect on markets." Per the bright-line rule, yield movements from policy expectations = normal (score 36-50). The money market pressures are noteworthy but not described as stress. Score: 42-48.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened but remained near the lower end of their historical ranges." Widened = some upward pressure, but "near the lower end of their historical ranges" = still below normal. CMBS spreads "continued to be at low levels." EME sovereign spreads "widened modestly." Per rubric, spreads that widened but remain near historical lows = still below normal. Score range: 35-42.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff discusses a substantial decline in equity prices, VIX spike to highest since 2015, "broader concerns about the level of stock market valuations." Prices recovered about half of the decline. The mention of valuation concerns suggests staff sees some elevated valuation concern, and the decline is partially attributed to those concerns. This is modest stress/concern — prices declined but partially recovered. Score range: 48-55. The valuation concern language plus the decline pushes slightly above neutral.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff mentions "provision of bank-intermediated credit to businesses slowed further, likely reflecting weak loan demand rather than tight supply." Subprime auto underwriting "continued to tighten" but "few signs of significant restriction." For subprime borrowers, conditions "remained tight." But overall, no mention of bank stress, capital concerns, or system-level issues. The slowdown in lending is attributed to weak demand, not supply constraints. Banks appear healthy. Score range: 30-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad nominal dollar index appreciated moderately over the period." Per bright-line rule, dollar appreciation = score MUST be > 50. "Appreciated moderately" = modest strength. Score range: 55-60.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." "Measures of inflation compensation derived from TIPS were little changed on net." This is well-anchored, stable expectations language. Score: 48-50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Core PCE price inflation...was 1½ percent over that same period." "Total U.S. consumer prices...increased 1¾ percent over the 12 months ending in January." Staff projects inflation will "reach the Committee's 2 percent objective in 2019."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running meaningfully below the 2% target (core at 1.5%) but is expected to rise; slightly below neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because current readings are below target, though the trajectory is upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 56 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP was projected to increase at a faster pace than potential output through 2020." "The economy was already operating above its potential level and resource utilization continued to tighten, as the staff projected."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly projects the economy as above potential and moving further above. Modestly above potential — not overheating.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff says "above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The national unemployment rate remained at 4.1 percent." "The unemployment rate was projected to decline further over the next few years and to continue to run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "Gains in total nonfarm payroll employment were strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 4.1% (below NAIRU), strong payrolls, projected to tighten further. Wage growth modest at 2.5-2.75%, keeping this from scoring higher.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because unemployment is below the natural rate and declining.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer expenditures appeared likely to rise at a modest pace in the first quarter following a strong gain in the preceding quarter." "Forward-looking indicators of business equipment spending...pointed to further solid gains in equipment spending in the near term." "Real residential investment looked to be slowing."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — consumer spending temporarily modest (Q1 softness seen as transitory), business investment moderating but outlook solid, residential investment slowing. Net: at trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is at neutral — modest current pace offset by solid forward-looking indicators and supportive fundamentals.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial corporations and households remained accommodative over the intermeeting period and continued to support further expansion of economic activity." "Credit conditions in municipal bond markets remained accommodative." "CMBS markets remained accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Multiple uses of "accommodative" for credit conditions across sectors despite recent market volatility.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because "accommodative" = loose. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 45 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The nominal Treasury yield curve shifted up and flattened a bit" due to "monetary policy communications, higher-than-expected domestic price data, and expectations for increases in the supply of Treasury securities." "Reductions in the size of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet continued as scheduled without a notable effect on markets." Increased T-bill issuance "lifted Treasury bill yields above comparable-maturity OIS rates for the first time in almost a decade."
**Center-of-gravity**: Yield movements are from fundamental factors (policy expectations, supply), not dysfunction. Money market rate pressures from T-bill issuance are notable but not described as stress. Normal functioning.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is near/below neutral — no dysfunction language. Yield changes are normal price adjustment.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bond yields over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened but remained near the lower end of their historical ranges." "CMBS spreads continued to be at low levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened slightly from very compressed levels but remain historically narrow — still accommodative.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads remain "near the lower end of their historical ranges." Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes decreased over the intermeeting period." "The decline may have been exacerbated by broader concerns about the level of stock market valuations." "Subsequently, equity prices recovered about half of their decline."
**Center-of-gravity**: Prices declined with explicit mention of valuation concerns but partially recovered. Slightly above neutral stress — some concern about valuations but not extreme.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — modest concern/stress from the decline and valuation worries, partially resolved by recovery.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 34 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The provision of bank-intermediated credit to businesses slowed further, likely reflecting weak loan demand rather than tight supply." "Small business owners continued to report accommodative credit supply conditions but also weak demand for credit."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system appears healthy with accommodative supply conditions. Lending slowdown attributed to demand, not stress. Subprime tightening is localized.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because conditions are accommodative and slowdown is demand-driven. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad nominal dollar index appreciated moderately over the period, largely reflecting an outsized depreciation of the Canadian dollar and a massive devaluation of the Venezuelan bolivar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate broad dollar appreciation, though partly driven by idiosyncratic factors (Canada, Venezuela). Japanese yen appreciated against the dollar.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the broad dollar index appreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).
