# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: September 2019 — late-expansion period. Trade war escalation with China, global manufacturing slowdown, inverted yield curve. Fed had just cut rates in July 2019 for the first time since 2008. Unemployment very low at 3.7%, inflation running below 2% target.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.4% (12-month), core PCE at 1.6% — both well below the 2% target. The trimmed mean was at 2%, providing some offset. Staff revised inflation forecast down and projected inflation to "continue to run below 2 percent through 2022." Recent monthly readings were faster, suggesting earlier softness was transitory, but the level remains clearly below target. Score should be in the 35-42 range (below target, "running below 2 percent" anchor).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as modestly ABOVE potential. "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." The unemployment rate was "below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." However, staff also revised up potential output, meaning "estimates of current and projected resource utilization were less tight than the staff previously assumed." Risks tilted to the downside. Bright-line check: "above potential" language present, so score MUST be > 50. But the language is hedged — "a little above" and resource utilization "less tight than previously assumed." Score ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 3.7%, below NAIRU estimate. "Labor market conditions remained strong." Payrolls expanding at "solid pace" though slower than H1. Job openings and quits at "relatively high levels," initial claims "near historically low levels." Labor compensation up 4.4% (faster than year ago). However, the upcoming benchmark revision would show "notably slower" job gains. With UE at 3.7%, the anchor guidance says score should be above 55 — likely in the 58-63 range given strong but modestly decelerating conditions.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED — solid consumption but weak investment. Consumer spending "rising solidly" with "brisk" July PCE growth, though some slowing from Q2. Residential investment "picking up a little" after declining. But business investment is "declining further" — shipments decreased, "continued softness," trade policy weighing on decisions. Industrial production "remained notably lower than at the beginning of the year." Net: solid consumer side but clearly weak business side. This nets to around 43-47.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff revised up trend productivity: "the staff revised up its estimate of the level of trend productivity in recent years after incorporating the BEA's recent annual revisions." This is discussed but briefly. Score modestly above neutral ~53-55.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/SUPPORTIVE. "Financing conditions for businesses and households were largely unaffected by the intermeeting turbulence in financial markets and remained generally supportive of spending and economic activity." Corporate financing "remained accommodative," CRE "generally accommodative," consumer credit "generally supportive." Residential mortgage conditions "eased." Bright-line check: "supportive"/"accommodative" means score MUST be < 50. Score ~35-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: There was a notable short-term funding stress event. "Flows associated with a combination of corporate tax payments and Treasury coupon securities settlements led to significant tightening of conditions, particularly for overnight funding. The EFFR rose to the top of the target range on September 16 and exceeded it by 5 basis points on September 17." This is actual market dysfunction in short-term funding, not just yield movements. The yield curve inverted (10yr-2yr negative for first time since 2007) but that alone isn't dysfunction. The repo stress is genuine dysfunction, though brief and resolved by Desk operations. Score ~55-60.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as contained/slightly mixed. "Spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds widened modestly, while those on speculative-grade corporate bonds were little changed on net." Consumer ABS "spreads remained at relatively low levels, though somewhat above their post-crisis averages." "Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations weakened slightly." Overall, spreads are close to normal but with modest widening. Score ~45-48.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Staff describes equity prices as "decreased slightly, on net" with "heightened volatility." VIX "remained at the lower end of its historical distribution." No valuation commentary per se — just price movements. Default to 50-55 range per guidance. Score ~52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank stocks underperformed, "downward revisions to banks' earnings forecasts." But no discussion of capital adequacy, NPLs, or systemic concerns. C&I loan growth "ticked up." Lending conditions remained accommodative. This implies low stress. Score ~35-38.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated against emerging market currencies but was little changed, on balance, against AFE currencies, leaving the broad dollar index slightly higher." Also, staff outlook mentions "a higher projected path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a headwind. Bright-line: appreciation means > 50. Score ~55.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not specifically broken out as a category. Energy prices declined, food prices below core. Some downward pressure from goods side. N/A (no explicit goods inflation discussion separate from headline).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed." Michigan dipped but "remained within its recent range." Dealer and market participant surveys "little changed." TIPS-based measures "declined on net." Anchored but with slight downside drift. Score ~45.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence: below target. Low score = correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence: slightly above. Score slightly above 50 = correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence: UE 3.7%, strong conditions. Score above 55 = correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence: "supportive"/"accommodative." Score below 50 = correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress. Evidence: "modestly widened" IG, "little changed" HY, levels still low. Near neutral = correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence: no stress signals, accommodative lending. Score below 50 = correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence: "slightly higher" broad index. Score slightly above 50 = correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Evidence: repo market stress episode. Score above 50 = correct.

### Bright-Line Verification
- OUTPUT_GAP: "above potential" -> score 53 > 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "supportive"/"accommodative" -> score 36 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: modestly widened but still at low levels -> score 46, near neutral. OK.
- BANKING_STRESS: no stress language, accommodative lending -> score 35 < 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "slightly higher" broad index -> score 55 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Actual repo stress event -> score 57 > 50 but not extreme. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60)**: Gap of 7. Labor market tighter than output gap suggests — consistent with late-cycle dynamics where labor market lags and business investment is softening while employment remains strong. Acceptable.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45)**: Gap of 8. C&I is lower because business investment is actively declining while consumption holds up. GDP is still slightly above potential on net. Acceptable divergence explained by split between consumer and business sectors.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (46)**: Gap of 10. FC is more accommodative than spreads suggest, but spreads only widened modestly. FC captures broader conditions including mortgage easing and accommodative lending. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (36) vs TREASURY_STRESS (57)**: Divergence of 21. This is the repo stress episode — a short-lived funding market event that did not spill over into broader financial conditions. Staff explicitly notes financing conditions "were largely unaffected by the intermeeting turbulence." Explained.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) vs sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (45). Headline below target at 38 is consistent with expectations slightly below anchor at 45. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Consumer price inflation, as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), was below 2 percent in July."
- "core PCE price inflation... moved down to 1.6 percent"
- "both inflation measures were forecast to continue to run below 2 percent through 2022"

Center-of-gravity: Staff reports headline PCE at 1.4% and core at 1.6%, both meaningfully below the 2% target, with the forecast projecting persistent below-target inflation through 2022. Some offset from trimmed mean at 2% and faster recent monthly readings.

Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running well below the 2% target with downside risks.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020"
- "The unemployment rate was projected to... remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate"
- "estimates of current and projected resource utilization were less tight than the staff previously assumed"

Center-of-gravity: Staff sees the economy as modestly above potential, with the output gap positive but narrowing. Downside risks are prominent but the central estimate is still slightly above potential.

Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff forecasts GDP growth "a little above" potential and unemployment below the natural rate. Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "labor market conditions remained strong"; unemployment at 3.7%
- "the unemployment rate... remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate"
- "Total labor compensation per hour in the business sector increased 4.4 percent... a faster rate than a year earlier"
- Job openings and quits at "relatively high levels"; initial claims "near historically low levels"

Center-of-gravity: Very low unemployment (3.7%), accelerating compensation, strong job openings, and historically low claims indicate a tight labor market, though the benchmark revision suggests payroll growth was somewhat overstated.

Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because unemployment is well below NAIRU with strong labor demand indicators and rising compensation.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Real consumer expenditures appeared to be rising solidly in the third quarter"
- "Real nonresidential private fixed investment looked to be declining further in the third quarter"
- "forward-looking indicators generally pointed to continued softness in business equipment spending"

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is solid but decelerating from Q2's strong pace, residential investment is picking up modestly, but business investment is clearly declining with poor forward indicators. The split between a solid consumer and weak business sector nets to modestly below trend.

Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the weakness in business investment and manufacturing offsets solid but decelerating consumer spending.

---

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 54 +/-8 [Low confidence]**

Evidence:
- "the staff revised up its estimate of the level of trend productivity in recent years after incorporating the BEA's recent annual revisions to the national income and product accounts"

Center-of-gravity: Staff revised trend productivity upward based on data revisions, implying somewhat better productivity performance than previously estimated. Brief mention only.

Direction confirmation: PRODUCTIVITY is slightly above neutral because the staff revised productivity estimates upward.

---

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Financing conditions for businesses and households... remained generally supportive of spending and economic activity"
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained accommodative"
- "Financing conditions in the residential mortgage market eased over the intermeeting period"
- "Credit conditions for both small businesses and municipalities remained accommodative on balance"

Center-of-gravity: Across all sectors — corporate, CRE, residential, consumer — staff describes conditions as accommodative or supportive. Even the intermeeting turbulence did not impair broader financing conditions.

Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (conditions are loose/accommodative) because staff uses "supportive" and "accommodative" language throughout. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

---

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "flows associated with a combination of corporate tax payments and Treasury coupon securities settlements led to significant tightening of conditions, particularly for overnight funding"
- "The EFFR rose to the top of the target range on September 16 and exceeded it by 5 basis points on September 17"

Center-of-gravity: A genuine short-term funding market stress event occurred just before the meeting, with the effective federal funds rate exceeding the target range. This represents actual market dysfunction (not just yield movements), though it was brief and addressed by Desk operations. Yield curve inversion is also noted but is not dysfunction per se.

Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because of the actual repo market stress episode, though the dysfunction was short-lived and contained.

---

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds widened modestly, while those on speculative-grade corporate bonds were little changed on net"
- "credit quality of nonfinancial corporations weakened slightly, with the volume of nonfinancial corporate bond downgrades modestly outpacing that of upgrades"
- Consumer ABS "spreads remained at relatively low levels"

Center-of-gravity: Spreads are near normal levels with only modest widening in IG and stable HY. Credit quality has softened marginally. Overall, conditions are close to neutral, perhaps very slightly below given accommodative overall tone.

Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is approximately at neutral — modest widening in IG but levels remain contained and HY unchanged. Consistent with late 2019 anchor of 30-40 range; modest widening pushes slightly higher to ~46.

---

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION

Center-of-gravity: Equities declined slightly with heightened volatility, but no valuation commentary from staff. VIX low. Per scoring guidance, price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55; slight decline points to ~52.

Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is approximately at neutral — slight decline but no distress language and VIX remained low.

Center-of-gravity: No direct discussion of banking system health or capital adequacy. Lending conditions are accommodative and loan growth positive, suggesting a healthy banking system with low stress. Bank stock underperformance is a market signal, not a staff assessment of banking stress.

Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because lending remains accommodative and loan growth is positive, indicating low banking system stress.

Center-of-gravity: The dollar strengthened modestly on net, with a slightly higher broad index. Staff incorporated a stronger dollar path into the medium-term forecast as a drag on growth.

Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the broad dollar index was "slightly higher." Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).
