### Era context
February 1998: Late 1990s economic boom. Asian financial crisis underway. U.S. economy growing rapidly with tight labor markets and low inflation, partly due to falling import/energy prices. Fed funds rate at 5.5%. Unemployment ~4.7%.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint.
Evidence: "Consumer price inflation remained low in December, damped by a sizable further drop in energy prices and a small decline in food prices." "For the year as a whole, prices of core consumer items rose considerably less than in 1996." "At the producer level, prices of all finished goods and of the core finished goods component declined further in December." "For the year 1997, the core producer price index was little changed." "Prices also remained subdued at earlier stages of processing in 1997, with prices of crude materials falling substantially."
However, staff also notes: "Labor costs, as measured by the hourly compensation of private industry workers, increased at appreciably faster rates in the fourth quarter and for the year." And the staff forecast says "inflation would increase to some degree over the ensuing period through 1999."
Net assessment: Current inflation is low/subdued, but labor costs are rising and staff expects some increase. The word "subdued" and "low" place this below 50. The fact that core CPI rose "considerably less than in 1996" and PPI was declining reinforces this. But labor cost acceleration and staff forecast of "increase to some degree" prevent going too low.
Score direction: Below 50. "Subdued" / "low" = roughly 35-40 range. The labor cost pressure and forward expectation of some increase nudge it up slightly.
Magnitude: "low," "subdued," "considerably less" = about 10-15 points below midpoint.
Score: 38 +/-5

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is low/subdued. Score below 50 = less pressure. Matches.

"As output growth slowed, pressures on resources would be expected to diminish somewhat" — implying current resource pressures exist (above potential).
Bright-line check: "robust pace," "pressures on resources," capacity utilization at "relatively high rate" — all imply above potential. Score MUST be > 50.
Magnitude: "robust" is strong language. But staff also notes "few bottlenecks" which tempers it. The staff expects slowdown to "vicinity of current estimates of the economy's long-run growth potential" — confirming currently above potential.
Era anchor: Late 90s boom = 57-63.
Score: 58 +/-5

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy expanding robustly above trend. Score > 50. Matches.

"Tighter labor markets brought some acceleration in wages."

With UE at 4.7% and language of "tighter labor markets" and wage acceleration, this is moderately tight — above NAIRU.
Era anchor: 1999-2000 peak = 65-75. In early 1998, not yet at peak tightness but clearly tight.
Score: 60 +/-5

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence: tight labor market, low UE, wage acceleration. Score > 50. Matches.
