# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-11-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
November 2010 — roughly 16 months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in a slow recovery with unemployment still near 9.6%, well above pre-crisis levels. The Fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound (0-0.25%), and the FOMC is on the cusp of launching QE2. Inflation is running below the 2% target. This is a classic "grinding recovery with substantial slack" period.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW neutral. Key phrases: "continued low rate of inflation," "headline consumer price inflation was subdued," "core PCE price index was unchanged in September, and the 12-month increase in this index continued to trend down," forecast shows "subdued rates of headline and core inflation during 2011 and 2012." Commodity prices accelerating is noted but not translating to core. Score should be well below 50 — in the 20-28 range (below target, trending down, but not outright deflation).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Key phrases: "economic recovery proceeded at a modest rate," "capacity utilization leveled off at a rate still well below its longer-run average," "the margin of slack at the end of 2011 was still expected to be substantial." Bright-line check: "slack" language → must be < 50. Unemployment at 9.6% indicates enormous slack. Score around 30-35 (below potential, recovering but gap persists).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very LOOSE. Unemployment at 9.6%, "only a gradual improvement in labor market conditions," "private businesses continued to increase their demand for labor only modestly," "long-duration unemployment continued to recede somewhat but was still very high," "indicators of layoffs remained elevated." UE 9.6% anchors this firmly in the 16-30 range. Score ~22.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture but below trend overall. Consumer spending "rose at a moderate rate," business investment in E&S posted "a smaller, but still solid, gain," but housing "remained exceptionally weak," nonresidential structures "about flat," consumer credit contracting. Net: moderate consumption growth offset by depressed construction, tight credit. Score ~38-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed as a separate topic. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Mixed signals. Short-term funding markets "generally stable," risk spreads "slightly lower," corporate bond issuance "very strong," but "bank lending standards were still relatively tight," "commercial real estate markets remained strained," consumer credit contracting, mortgage purchase applications "anemic." Overall conditions are somewhat loose from capital market side but still tight from bank lending side. The staff describes conditions that are improving but still constrained. Score ~50-55 (neutral to slightly tight due to bank lending tightness). Bright-line check: The text has mixed language — corporate credit is accommodative but bank lending is tight. Net effect is roughly neutral to slightly tight.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No indication of market dysfunction. Yields moved in response to policy expectations (normal). "Conditions in short-term funding markets were generally stable." Repo spreads declined. This is normal market functioning. Score ~30-35 (calm/normal).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Risk spreads slightly lower," corporate bond issuance "very strong" and "sizable gross corporate bond issuance across the credit spectrum," syndicated loan prices moving up, bid-asked spreads narrowing. But CRE remains strained, consumer credit quality improving but delinquency "remained elevated." Corporate credit is clearly favorable; consumer/CRE less so. Net: somewhat below normal stress. Score ~35-40.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes rose," equity risk premium "narrowed a bit but remained at an elevated level." Elevated equity risk premium means equities are cheap relative to bonds — modestly depressed valuation. Bank stocks underperformed. No explicit valuation concern language. Score ~52-55 (modestly depressed due to elevated risk premium, bank stock underperformance).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank stocks underperformed "amid concerns about the handling of mortgage foreclosure documents and possible lack of compliance with securitization agreements." Bank lending standards still relatively tight. CRE loans contracting. Consumer delinquency rates "remained elevated." But credit quality of nonfinancial corporations "remained solid," and banks were easing C&I standards. Mixed but still showing stress elements. Score ~55-60 (emerging stress from foreclosure concerns, tight lending, elevated delinquencies). Bright-line check: stress indicators present → above 50 is correct.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar declined about 3 percent against a broad array of other currencies during the intermeeting period, depreciating even more against the euro and the yen." Also noted: "lower foreign exchange value of the dollar" in the staff outlook. Clear depreciation. Bright-line check: depreciation → must be < 50. Score ~25.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Commodity prices "accelerated considerably in October" for industrial and agricultural commodities, but core goods prices muted (core PCE unchanged). Mixed — upstream pressure but not passing through. Score ~40.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing market "exceptionally weak" but this is activity, not housing inflation specifically. Not enough direct evidence on shelter inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey measures of households' short- and long-term expectations for inflation remained in the ranges that have prevailed since the spring of 2009." Market-based: "Five-year inflation compensation increased over the intermeeting period, and forward inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead also rose." Surveys stable, market-based rising. Score ~48 (anchored but with slight upward drift in market measures; overall well-anchored).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Headline consumer price inflation was subdued in recent months, despite a rise in energy prices, as core consumer price inflation trended lower." "The core PCE price index was unchanged in September, and the 12-month increase in this index continued to trend down." Staff forecast: "subdued rates of headline and core inflation during 2011 and 2012."
- **Center of gravity**: Core inflation trending down, below target, with subdued forecast — meaningful disinflation but not outright deflation fear.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is subdued, trending lower, and forecast to remain subdued.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The economic recovery proceeded at a modest rate." "Capacity utilization leveled off at a rate still well below its longer-run average." "The margin of slack at the end of 2011 was still expected to be substantial."
- **Center of gravity**: Economy recovering but far below potential with substantial and persistent slack.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly describes substantial slack and below-average capacity utilization. Bright-line: "slack remains" → below 50. PASS.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate remained at 9.6 percent in September." "Private businesses continued to increase their demand for labor only modestly." "Long-duration unemployment continued to recede somewhat but was still very high."
- **Center of gravity**: Extremely loose labor market with 9.6% unemployment, minimal hiring, and persistent long-term unemployment.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 9.6% with only modest labor demand growth indicates very substantial slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose at a moderate rate in the third quarter." "Business investment in equipment and software posted a smaller, but still solid, gain in the third quarter." But: "Activity in the housing market remained exceptionally weak." "Construction activity in both the residential and nonresidential sectors remained depressed."
- **Center of gravity**: Moderate consumption and solid E&S investment offset by exceptionally weak housing and depressed construction — net below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the positive components (moderate consumption, solid E&S) are offset by depressed construction and tight credit.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- Not discussed as a separate topic in the staff review.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Bank lending standards were still relatively tight, and household borrowing remained low." "Conditions in short-term funding markets were generally stable." "Risk spreads slightly lower." "Net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations was very strong in September."
- **Center of gravity**: Cross-currents — capital markets accommodative with strong corporate issuance and narrowing spreads, but bank lending still tight and household credit contracting. Net: roughly neutral with slight tightness from bank side.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is approximately at neutral, slightly above due to persistent tightness in bank lending and housing credit despite accommodative capital markets.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term funding markets were generally stable over the intermeeting period." "Bid-asked spreads in most repo transactions generally declined." Yields moved in response to policy expectations (normal functioning).
- **Center of gravity**: No signs of market dysfunction. Treasury market functioning normally with orderly yield movements driven by policy expectations.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets are calm and functioning well.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds declined somewhat more than those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving risk spreads slightly lower." "In the secondary market for syndicated leveraged loans, prices of loans continued to move up and bid-asked spreads narrowed a bit further." "Measures of the credit quality of nonfinancial corporations remained solid."
- **Center of gravity**: Credit spreads narrowing with strong corporate issuance, though CRE and consumer delinquencies remain elevated. Somewhat below normal stress.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and issuance is strong. Bright-line: narrowing spreads → below 50. PASS.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 53 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes rose, on balance, over the intermeeting period." "The spread between the staff's estimate of the expected real return on equities over the next 10 years and an estimate of the expected real return on a 10-year Treasury note... narrowed a bit but remained at an elevated level." "Bank stocks generally underperformed the broader market."
- **Center of gravity**: Equity prices rising but risk premium still elevated (suggesting valuations are not stretched); bank stocks weak. Near-neutral with a slight lean toward modestly depressed.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral (more stress/concern) because the elevated equity risk premium suggests equities are not fully valued, and bank stocks underperformed.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Bank stocks generally underperformed the broader market amid concerns about the handling of mortgage foreclosure documents and possible lack of compliance with securitization agreements." "Commercial real estate markets remained strained." "Consumer credit quality generally continued to improve, though delinquency rates remained elevated."
- **Center of gravity**: Emerging stress from foreclosure/securitization concerns, strained CRE, and elevated delinquency rates, though some easing in C&I lending standards.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because of active foreclosure concerns, CRE strain, and elevated delinquencies. Bright-line: stress indicators → above 50. PASS.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH

- **Center of gravity**: Clear and broad dollar depreciation.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is well below neutral because the dollar is depreciating. Bright-line: depreciation → below 50. PASS.
