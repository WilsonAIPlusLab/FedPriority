# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2010-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with separate "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: September 2010 — roughly 15 months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in a slow recovery with the unemployment rate still near 9.6%, substantial slack, very low inflation, and the federal funds rate at the zero lower bound. The European sovereign debt crisis is ongoing. This is firmly in the "sluggish post-GFC recovery" period.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. Key evidence: "Inflation remained subdued," "prices for core goods and services moved up slightly," "producer prices of core intermediate materials moved down," "unit labor costs remained below their level one year earlier," and the outlook projects inflation to "remain subdued" with "wide margins of economic slack" contributing to "a small slowing in core inflation in 2011." Inflation expectations "essentially unchanged." This is clearly below-target, subdued inflation — not deflationary spiral, but well below normal. Score range: 20-30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as clearly BELOW potential. "The pace of the economic expansion slowed in recent months," "resource slack was anticipated to still remain elevated at the end of 2012," "current and projected wide margins of economic slack," "considerable economic slack." Capacity utilization "still substantially below its longer-run average." Bright-line: "slack remains" language mandates score < 50. The language of "wide margins" and "considerable" slack suggests deep below-potential. Score range: 30-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as very LOOSE. "The labor market situation continued to improve only slowly," unemployment rate "remained elevated" (~9.6%), "average monthly increase in private payroll employment... was small and was less than the average gain earlier in the year," "initial claims for unemployment insurance remained at an elevated level," "other indicators of labor demand... did not improve." With UE near 9.6%, this is far from full employment. Score range: 20-28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend but not collapsing. Consumer spending "continued to increase at a moderate rate," real PCE "rose modestly." Business E&S spending "appeared to have slowed" after "expanding rapidly." Housing "weakened further" and "remained at a depressed level." Nonresidential construction "remained depressed." Mixed picture: moderate consumer spending, slowing business investment, very weak housing. Net assessment: below trend. Score range: 33-40.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff discusses productivity explicitly. "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector declined in the second quarter following large increases in the preceding three quarters, productivity was still well above its level one year earlier." Also: "labor productivity stayed near its recent high level." This is mixed — recent decline but level still high. Score range: 48-55.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat accommodative but with pockets of tightness. Short-term funding markets "continued to improve," dealers "loosened credit terms," corporate bond issuance "remained robust," mortgage rates at "historically low level." However, CRE markets face "difficult financial conditions," consumer credit "declined," bank loans "continued to contract." The overall tone is improving/accommodative with lingering stress in specific sectors. Bright-line: "accommodative" and "improve" language → score < 50, but not extremely loose given CRE/housing stress. Score range: 38-45.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury auctions "were generally well received." Yields were "volatile" but this was due to policy expectations and economic data — normal price adjustment, NOT dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, settlement issues, or auction failures. Bright-line: no actual dysfunction described. Score range: 28-35.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Risk spreads little changed." Corporate bond spreads "moved roughly in line with those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities." Syndicated loan market improving (bid price moved up, bid-asked spreads edged down). ABCP spreads "remained narrow." Consumer loan spreads "little changed." Issuance robust. Bright-line: stable to slightly improved spreads. Score range: 38-45.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. stock price indexes edged up, on balance." The equity risk premium "remained at an elevated level" — this suggests equities may be somewhat cheap relative to bonds, implying modest stress/concern. Bank stocks "underperformed" and were "more volatile." No explicit valuation commentary beyond the elevated ERP. Score range: 50-58.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Bank stocks underperformed, CDS spreads for large banks "edged up," bank loans "continued to contract," CRE delinquency rates increasing, consumer delinquency/charge-off rates "remained elevated" despite improving. Commercial mortgage debt "continued to contract amid further increases in delinquency rates." However, credit quality of nonfinancial corporations "remained solid," and bank credit expanded via securities purchases. Mixed but still elevated stress signals. Bright-line: stress indicators still present but improving. Score range: 55-63.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated broadly on safe-haven demands" in August, but "in September... the dollar retraced its earlier appreciation." Net: roughly flat over the period, maybe slightly weaker on net. The yen was at "15-year high against the dollar." Bright-line: net roughly flat to slightly weak. Score range: 40-48.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: "Prices for core goods... edged up slightly," "producer prices of core intermediate materials moved down." Very low goods inflation. Score range: 28-35.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Prices for core... services edged up slightly." Very muted. Score range: 30-38.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "declined modestly." Rents not specifically discussed. Housing-specific inflation signal is weak/declining. Score range: 28-35.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey measures of short- and long-term inflation expectations were essentially unchanged." Forward TIPS compensation "edged up" but "remained at a lower level than in the spring." Staff outlook: "stable inflation expectations." Anchored but at somewhat low levels. Score range: 42-48.

### Polarity Verification

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued/low. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says wide slack. Score below 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very loose, high UE. Score well below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says improving/accommodative with some pockets of tightness. Score below 50. Correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Evidence says stable, narrow spreads. Score below 50. Correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Evidence says ongoing contraction in loans, rising CRE delinquencies, but improving. Score modestly above 50. Correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says net roughly flat/slightly weaker. Score at or slightly below 50. Correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Evidence says well-functioning. Score below 50. Correct.

### Bright-Line Checks

- OUTPUT_GAP: "slack remains," "wide margins of slack" → MUST be < 50. Score ~35. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative," "improve" → MUST be < 50. Score ~42. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Stable/narrow → should be < 50. Score ~42. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: Mixed but delinquencies elevated, loans contracting → above 50. Score ~58. Consistent.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Net flat/slightly weaker → at or slightly below 50. Score ~45. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: No dysfunction → below 50. Score ~30. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (24)**: Both well below 50. Gap of ~11 points. Reasonable — labor market lagging output recovery somewhat, consistent with jobless recovery narrative.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) ↔ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (37)**: Within 2 points. Coherent.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) ↔ CREDIT_SPREADS (42)**: Identical. Coherent.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) ↔ TREASURY_STRESS (30)**: Both below 50, Treasury functioning well. Coherent.

Parent at 25 is somewhat lower, reflecting the outlook projection of further slowing and "wide margins of slack" contributing to subdued inflation. Reasonable given strong forward-looking language reinforcing the low-inflation narrative.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation remained subdued in recent months." "The current and projected wide margins of economic slack were expected to contribute to a small slowing in core inflation in 2011."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff clearly views inflation as well below target and expects it to slow further — meaningful below-target reading consistent with the 2010 post-GFC environment.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff describes inflation as subdued with further slowing projected due to wide slack.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Resource slack was anticipated to still remain elevated at the end of 2012." "The current and projected wide margins of economic slack." Capacity utilization "still substantially below its longer-run average."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy recovering but far below potential, with "wide" and "considerable" slack language indicating a significant negative output gap that would persist for years.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff repeatedly emphasizes wide/considerable/elevated slack. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate ticked up in August and remained close to the level that has prevailed since the beginning of this year" (~9.6%). "The labor market situation continued to improve only slowly." "Other indicators of labor demand, such as measures of hiring and job vacancies, did not improve."
- **Center-of-gravity**: With unemployment near 9.6% and no improvement in hiring indicators, the labor market is far from tight — deep slack consistent with the 2010 anchor of 15-22, though slight improvement from absolute trough pushes it slightly above.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is very elevated and labor demand indicators are not improving.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending continued to increase at a moderate rate." "The rise in business outlays for equipment and software looked to have moderated recently following outsized gains." "Housing activity weakened further, and nonresidential construction remained depressed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Moderate consumer spending offset by slowing business investment, depressed housing, and depressed nonresidential construction — net below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because housing and nonresidential investment are depressed and business investment is moderating.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Productivity was still well above its level one year earlier." "Output per hour in the nonfarm business sector declined in the second quarter following large increases in the preceding three quarters."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Recent quarter showed a decline, but the level remains high relative to a year ago. Mixed near-term signal at roughly trend level.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is near neutral — recent decline offset by high level.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term funding markets continued to improve." Dealers "loosened credit terms applicable to several important classes of counterparties." However, "commercial real estate markets continued to face difficult financial conditions" and "consumer credit declined."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Overall conditions improving and broadly accommodative, but with pockets of tightness remaining in CRE and household credit — somewhat loose but not extremely so.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (i.e., conditions are loose/accommodative) because funding markets improving, dealers loosening terms. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Treasury auctions over the intermeeting period were generally well received." Yields were volatile but driven by policy/economic news — normal price adjustment.
- **Center-of-gravity**: No dysfunction described; well-functioning market consistent with the 2010-15 calm markets anchor.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because the market is functioning normally with well-received auctions.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Yields on investment- and speculative-grade corporate bonds moved roughly in line with those on comparable-maturity Treasury securities, leaving risk spreads little changed." "Spreads on asset-backed commercial paper remained narrow."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads stable and generally favorable, with some improving pockets (leveraged loans). Below-normal stress but not extremely compressed.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are stable/narrow. Bright-line satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The spread between the staff's estimate of the expected real return on equities over the next 10 years and an estimate of the expected real return on a 10-year Treasury note—a rough measure of the equity risk premium—remained at an elevated level." "Bank stocks underperformed the broader equity market."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Elevated equity risk premium suggests equities are somewhat cheap/stressed relative to historical norms, and bank stocks are underperforming. Modest stress signal.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly above neutral (modest stress) because the elevated ERP suggests equities are not richly valued and bank stocks are weak.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Credit default swap spreads for large banking organizations edged up." "Bank loans continued to contract." "Total commercial mortgage debt continued to contract amid further increases in delinquency rates on commercial mortgages." Delinquency and charge-off rates "remained elevated."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking sector still showing stress — contracting loans, rising CRE delinquencies, elevated consumer delinquencies — though with signs of stabilization. Moderately stressed.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because of contracting loans, rising delinquencies, and elevated CDS spreads. Bright-line: stress indicators → above 50.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated broadly on safe-haven demands" in August, but "the dollar retraced its earlier appreciation" in September. The yen was at a "15-year high against the dollar."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Net roughly flat to slightly weaker over the full intermeeting period, with the dollar giving back August gains.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is slightly below neutral because the dollar retraced its appreciation and the yen was at a 15-year high against the dollar.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Prices for core goods... edged up slightly." "Producer prices of core intermediate materials moved down, on net."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Very low goods price pressure with pipeline prices declining.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: 33 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Prices for core... services edged up slightly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Minimal services inflation. Only brief mention — low confidence.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "House prices declined modestly in July after changing little, on net, in recent months."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Housing prices declining modestly — below-normal shelter inflation.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Survey measures of short- and long-term inflation expectations were essentially unchanged." "Forward inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead edged up... but remained at a lower level than in the spring."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations anchored but at levels slightly below target — stable but on the low side.
