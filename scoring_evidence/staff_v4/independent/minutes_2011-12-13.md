# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2011-12-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with clearly delineated "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. Only staff-attributable content will be scored.

**Era context**: December 2011 -- roughly 2.5 years after the GFC trough. The U.S. economy is in a slow recovery with unemployment still very high (8.6%). The European sovereign debt crisis is intensifying, creating global financial market stress. The Fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound and the Fed has completed QE2.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Inflation continued to decrease relative to earlier in the year." PCE price index "edged down in October." Food prices rising at "substantially slower" pace. Core prices rising at "more modest pace." Longer-term expectations "remained stable." Staff projects inflation will be "subdued in 2012 and 2013" with "substantial slack" persisting. This is clearly below-target inflation with ongoing disinflation. Score range: 30-38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as BELOW potential (below midpoint). "The gains in real GDP were anticipated to be sufficient to reduce the slack in product and labor markets only slowly." Economy "expanded moderately" but housing "continued to be depressed," business equipment spending "seemed to be decelerating," and the unemployment rate was "expected to remain elevated at the end of 2013." Bright-line check: "slack in product and labor markets" = must be < 50. Score range: 32-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as LOOSE (below midpoint). Unemployment at 8.6% -- well above any plausible NAIRU. "Long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons remained elevated." Initial claims "still at a level consistent with only modest employment gains." Nominal wage gains "continued to be subdued." With UE at 8.6%, anchors suggest 16-30 range. Some improvement noted ("conditions seemed to have improved somewhat"), so upper end of that range. Score range: 26-32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as BELOW trend (below midpoint). Consumer spending mixed: "real PCE rose modestly" with "significant gains in the previous month" and retail sales "expanded further." But household income declined, net wealth decreased, consumer sentiment "still at a subdued level." Business investment: equipment spending "seemed to be decelerating," "muted" outlook. Housing "continued to be depressed." Nonresidential construction "still at a low level." Government sector declining. Net: below trend with consumer spending offering some support. Score range: 36-42.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed as a standalone topic. Mention of "unit labor costs edged up over the past four quarters" is tangential. Score: N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as TIGHTER than neutral. "Strains in global financial markets." Short-term funding markets "deteriorated somewhat." Libor-OIS spread "widened noticeably." Dealers reported "moderate tightening of credit terms." CRE financing "appeared to remain strained." Tight underwriting standards for mortgages. CDS spreads for banks widened. However, secured funding markets were "relatively steady" and some consumer credit improved. Bright-line check: "tightened" / "strained" = must be > 50. European contagion stress is clearly present. Score range: 62-70.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields were "little changed" and "essentially unchanged, on balance." No description of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction failures. Normal market functioning with yields driven by policy expectations and European developments. Score range: 38-44. No dysfunction signals.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as WIDER than normal. Corporate bond spreads "somewhat wider." CDS spreads for banks "widened." European bank CDS "remained elevated." CRE financing "strained." Leveraged loan market "sluggish." However, corporate bond issuance was "particularly robust" and consumer credit quality "improved further." Net: moderately elevated stress. Bright-line check: "widened" = must be > 50. Score range: 58-64.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: S&P 500 "ended the period slightly higher, on net." Banking firm equity prices "were lower." "Equity prices in both advanced and emerging market economies fluctuated widely." No valuation commentary -- just price movements. Default to near-neutral with slight concern given banking declines and volatility. Score range: 52-58.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff reports notable banking system stress. U.S. banking CDS spreads widened, equity prices for banks lower. European banks faced "increasingly strained conditions." MF Global bankruptcy raised "renewed concerns about securities dealers." Dealers reported "moderate tightening" of credit terms. CRE delinquency rates "close to their recent highs." Residential mortgage delinquency rates "remained elevated." However, consumer credit quality improving, delinquency on credit cards "declining nearly to historical lows." Mixed but leaning stressed. Bright-line check: stress signals dominate. Score range: 56-64.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated, on balance, over the intermeeting period." Bright-line check: appreciation = must be > 50. This is a moderate, safe-haven driven appreciation amid European crisis. Score range: 55-62.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices "decreased," food prices rising at "substantially slower" pace. Consumer goods prices rising but decelerating. Score range: 32-38.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: "Outlays for consumer services were roughly flat." No direct services price discussion beyond the overall core reading being "more modest." Very limited evidence. Score: N/A (insufficient specific services price discussion).

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing market "depressed," home prices "moved down further." No direct shelter/rent inflation discussion. Score: N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." Near-term expectations "declined." Inflation-protected Treasury-derived measures "remained at the low end of their recent ranges." Anchored but on the low side. Score range: 40-46.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28)**: Both well below 50, consistent. Labor somewhat lower reflecting the very high 8.6% UE rate while GDP growth is at least "moderate." Divergence ~7 points -- acceptable given jobless recovery dynamics.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (39)**: Both below 50, very close. Coherent.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (65) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (61)**: Both above 50, consistent. Financial conditions slightly tighter reflecting funding market stress beyond just credit spreads.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (65) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: FC elevated due to European contagion but Treasury market itself functioning normally. Divergence explained by the fact that Treasury stress is about dysfunction, not general tightness. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (34) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~35, expectations ~43. Weighted toward goods price deceleration and subdued overall readings. Parent score of 34 is consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Inflation continued to decrease relative to earlier in the year. Indeed, the PCE price index edged down in October." "The staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued in 2012 and 2013."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation as clearly below target and decelerating, with ongoing commodity/import price pressures fading and substantial slack expected to keep inflation subdued.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is actively decelerating, core measures are moderating, and the staff forecasts subdued inflation ahead.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The gains in real GDP were anticipated to be sufficient to reduce the slack in product and labor markets only slowly, and the unemployment rate was expected to remain elevated at the end of 2013." Housing "continued to be depressed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy is well below potential with persistent slack in both product and labor markets, improving only slowly.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly describes persistent "slack" in product and labor markets. Bright-line rule satisfied (slack = < 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate dropped to 8.6 percent in November." "Long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons remained elevated." "Nominal wage gains continued to be subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Despite some improvement, unemployment is very high at 8.6% with elevated underemployment and subdued wages, indicating a very slack labor market.
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because UE at 8.6% is far above NAIRU, with significant slack and no wage pressure.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 39 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Real business spending on equipment and software seemed to be decelerating." "Overall real personal consumption expenditures rose modestly in October following significant gains in the previous month." "Activity in the housing market continued to be depressed."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showed moderate gains but business investment is decelerating, housing is depressed, and government spending is contracting. Net assessment is below trend.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because the overall picture is below-trend spending with decelerating business investment and depressed housing.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
- **Evidence**: No substantive discussion of productivity trends.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Conditions in short-term wholesale funding markets appeared to deteriorate somewhat." "The spread of the three-month Libor over the OIS rate widened noticeably." "Dealers reported a moderate tightening of credit terms."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are notably tighter than neutral due to European contagion, funding market stress, and tightening credit terms, though some sectors (secured funding, consumer credit) remain more stable.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is above neutral because staff describes tightening, deterioration, and strain. Bright-line rule satisfied (tightened/strained = > 50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities were little changed." "Short-term nominal Treasury yields and the expected path of the federal funds rate were essentially unchanged, on balance."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning appears normal with orderly yield movements and no signs of dysfunction.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no description of market dysfunction. Normal functioning.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 61 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Spreads over yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities were somewhat wider." "CDS spreads for European banks remained elevated as these institutions faced increasingly strained conditions."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Corporate and bank credit spreads have widened, reflecting European contagion and general caution, though U.S. corporate issuance remains robust.
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads widened and conditions are described as strained. Bright-line rule satisfied (widened = > 50).

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 54 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The S&P 500 index ended the period slightly higher, on net." "Equity prices for most major U.S. banking firms were lower." "Equity prices in both advanced and emerging market economies fluctuated widely."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Mixed equity performance with broad index slightly higher but banking stocks lower and significant volatility. No valuation commentary, so near neutral with slight stress tilt given banking declines.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral -- no valuation assessment given, just price movements with mixed signals.

### BANKING_STRESS: 60 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "CDS spreads for European banks remained elevated as these institutions faced increasingly strained conditions." "Market participants also expressed renewed concerns about securities dealers that rely heavily on short-term wholesale funding markets" (MF Global). Dealers reported "moderate tightening of credit terms."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system shows emerging stress with European contagion affecting U.S. bank CDS and equity prices, CRE delinquencies elevated, and MF Global fallout, though some consumer credit metrics are improving.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is above neutral because stress signals (CDS widening, funding strain, MF Global concerns, elevated delinquencies) outweigh pockets of improvement. Bright-line rule satisfied.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar appreciated, on balance, over the intermeeting period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Dollar strengthened modestly, consistent with safe-haven flows during European crisis.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line rule satisfied (appreciation = > 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer prices for energy decreased." "Increases in consumer food prices in October were substantially slower than the average pace in the preceding months."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Goods price pressures are receding with energy prices declining and food price increases slowing markedly.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
- **Evidence**: No specific services price discussion beyond the aggregate core reading.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
- **Evidence**: Housing market discussed in activity terms (depressed, prices falling) but no specific shelter/rent inflation discussion.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 43 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Longer-term inflation expectations remained stable." "Near-term inflation expectations from the Thomson Reuters/University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers declined in early December." Inflation compensation measures "remained at the low end of their recent ranges."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are anchored but on the soft side, with near-term expectations declining and market-based measures at the low end of recent ranges.
