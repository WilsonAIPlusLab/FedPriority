# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2013-03-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
March 2013: The economy is roughly four years into recovery from the GFC. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.7%. The Fed is conducting large-scale asset purchases (QE3). Fiscal headwinds are prominent (sequestration began March 1, 2013). Inflation is running below target. Europe remains a source of downside risk (Italy elections, Cyprus deposit tax proposal).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key evidence: "Consumer price inflation...was subdued"; "staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2015"; "significant resource slack persisting over the forecast period"; core prices "increased moderately"; wage gains "remained relatively slow." This is clearly below-target inflation with no upward pressure. The "subdued" language and "significant resource slack" anchoring inflation down points to the 30-38 range. Anchor for 2013 is 38-42 for "below target."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Key evidence: "expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets"; "significant resource slack persisting"; GDP growth "would only modestly exceed the growth rate of potential output"; risks "skewed to the downside." The bright-line rule requires < 50 for "slack remains" language. With "significant resource slack" and only modest growth above potential, the gap is substantial but narrowing. Anchors: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as well BELOW full employment. Unemployment at 7.7% (anchor: UE > 7% = 16-30 range); "still elevated"; long-duration unemployment and involuntary part-time "remained high"; hiring rate and job openings "generally subdued"; wage gains "relatively slow" and "subdued." This is firmly in the slack territory.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as BELOW trend but mixed. Consumer spending "rose modestly"; retail sales ex-autos "increased at a strong rate"; business equipment spending "appeared to slow somewhat" after brisk Q4; government spending decreasing; housing "improved further" but "still at a relatively low level." The net picture is modest/moderate growth, with mixed signals. Anchor for 2011-13: 35-42 "moderate."

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Briefly mentioned: "small increases in productivity" over 2012. This is minimal discussion but suggests below-trend productivity growth.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as BELOW neutral (i.e., accommodative/loose). Key evidence: "improved sentiment in domestic financial markets"; "accommodative monetary policy"; "further improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" expected; equity prices rose; credit issuance "robust"; funding markets "generally little changed." The staff financial stability review notes "low interest rates likely have supported gains in asset prices and encouraged the flow of credit" but "not accompanied by significant financial imbalances." This is clearly accommodative/supportive conditions. Bright-line: "accommodative"/"supportive" must be < 50.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "modestly lower, on net" with some volatility around Italian elections but "mostly retraced." No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress in Treasuries. Normal market functioning. Score in 30-40 range.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Spreads "a little wider" but corporate bond issuance "fairly robust"; CLO issuance at "post-financial-crisis high"; C&I loans expanding; consumer credit in "moderate expansion"; CRE conditions "improved somewhat." Spreads are near normal to slightly below normal. Bright-line: robust issuance and generally favorable credit conditions should be < 50.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad equity price indexes increased"; implied volatility "remained low, at levels last seen in early 2007"; earnings "increased modestly." The staff stability section notes gains in asset prices but "not accompanied by significant financial imbalances" though "trends in a few specific markets bore watching." No explicit overvaluation concern, but the mention of low vol (levels of 2007) and watching certain markets hints at mild concern. Anchor for 2013: 40-50.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Stress tests had "limited" market reaction; bank equity prices "rose, on net"; CDS spreads for large banks "edged down"; bank credit "expanded." The stability review found no "significant financial imbalances." CRE delinquency rates "remained near historically high levels" is a residual concern. Overall healthy with minor legacy concerns. Bright-line: healthy banks = below 50.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "Dollar appreciated against the euro, sterling, and the Canadian dollar"; staff outlook incorporated "a higher assumed path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar"; EME currencies "generally small" moves. The dollar is strengthening. Bright-line: appreciation = > 50. But it's modest/selective, not broad-based.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not specifically broken out beyond food (flat/edged up) and energy (temporary fluctuations, gasoline price rise). Limited discussion.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed as a category.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices "increased further" but this is asset prices, not shelter/rent inflation. No direct shelter CPI discussion. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"; near-term expectations "unchanged"; longer-term "little changed and remained within the narrow range." Well-anchored at or slightly below target.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation, excluding some temporary fluctuations in energy prices, was subdued"; "the staff continued to project that inflation would be subdued through 2015" with "significant resource slack persisting over the forecast period."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes current inflation as subdued and below target, with slack expected to keep it subdued through 2015. Wage growth also slow.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff explicitly characterizes inflation as "subdued" with slack holding it down. Consistent with 2013 anchor of 38-42; scored slightly below anchor due to repeated "subdued" language and projected persistence through 2015.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 37 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce the slack in labor and product markets"; "significant resource slack persisting over the forecast period"; GDP growth "would only modestly exceed the growth rate of potential output."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The economy is expanding at a moderate pace but remains well below potential, with significant slack in both labor and product markets. Growth only modestly above potential means the gap is closing very slowly.
- **Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references "significant resource slack." Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 7.7 percent in February"; "the rate of long-duration unemployment and the share of workers employed part time for economic reasons...remained high"; "the rate of private-sector hiring...were generally subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: With UE at 7.7%, high long-duration unemployment, elevated involuntary part-time work, subdued hiring, and slow wage growth, the labor market has substantial slack. Anchors: UE > 7% = 16-30 range; scored 28 (upper end given some improvement).
- **Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral because unemployment is far above full employment and slack indicators are elevated.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures rose modestly in January"; "real business expenditures on equipment and software appeared to slow somewhat early this year"; "real federal government purchases appeared to decrease further."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending modest with mixed signals (strong retail sales but declining real disposable income); business investment slowing; government spending contracting. Housing improving but from low levels. Net: below trend but not severely weak.
- **Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because spending is described as "modest" and investment is slowing, with fiscal drag adding headwinds.

### PRODUCTIVITY: 35 +-10 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "With small increases in productivity, unit labor costs also advanced only modestly."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Only one mention, indicating small/below-trend productivity growth in 2012. Limited basis for scoring.
- **Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because "small increases" suggests below historical trend growth.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Generally favorable U.S. economic data releases...appeared to contribute to improved sentiment in domestic financial markets"; "further improvements in credit availability and financial conditions" expected; the stability review notes "low interest rates likely have supported gains in asset prices and encouraged the flow of credit."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative -- equity prices rising, credit flowing, issuance robust, funding markets stable, monetary policy accommodation continuing. Some European concerns but limited spillover to U.S.
- **Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose/accommodative). Bright-line satisfied: "accommodative" -> < 50.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Yields on nominal Treasury securities were modestly lower, on net, over the intermeeting period"; yields declined on Italian election news "but mostly retraced this decline as economic data releases...exceeded expectations."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Normal Treasury market functioning. Yield movements were orderly and driven by fundamental news (economic data, European politics). No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, or market dysfunction.
- **Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at or slightly below neutral because there is no dysfunction -- just normal yield movements. Score in the 36-50 normal range.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Risk spreads a little wider"; "Corporate bond issuance by nonfinancial firms remained fairly robust"; "issuance of collateralized loan obligations reached a post-financial-crisis high."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spreads are approximately normal to slightly favorable. While risk spreads widened slightly, robust issuance across corporate bonds, CLOs, and C&I loans indicates generally favorable credit conditions. Anchor: 30-40 for 2013-19 "generally favorable."
- **Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (favorable) because credit is flowing freely with robust issuance despite marginally wider spreads. Bright-line satisfied.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 42 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Broad equity price indexes increased over the intermeeting period"; "option-implied volatility...remained low, at levels last seen in early 2007"; staff stability review found no "significant financial imbalances" but noted "trends in a few specific markets bore watching."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Equities are rising with low volatility, and while staff is not flagging a bubble, the implied vol comparison to 2007 and "bore watching" language suggests mild watchfulness. Roughly fair-valued with a slight lean toward elevated. Anchor: 40-50 for 2013-19.
- **Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral to slightly below (mild overvaluation concern direction) because valuations are not distressed; low vol and rising prices with no explicit concern places this near fair value.

### BANKING_STRESS: 38 +-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "A broad index of U.S. bank equity prices rose, on net"; "credit default swap spreads for most large domestic banks edged down"; "CRE...delinquency rates on loans underlying existing CMBS remained near historically high levels."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Banking system appears generally healthy -- stress tests passed without market disruption, bank equity up, CDS tighter. However, legacy CRE delinquencies remain high, keeping this from a fully clean bill of health. Bright-line: healthy = below 50.
- **Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because bank health indicators are positive. Bright-line satisfied (< 50).

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 56 +-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated against the euro, sterling, and the Canadian dollar"; staff outlook assumed "a higher assumed path for the foreign exchange value of the dollar"; EME currency moves "generally small."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The dollar is modestly appreciating, driven mainly by weakness in European and Canadian economies. Not a broad-based surge, but clearly strengthening against major currencies.
- **Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied (> 50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: N/A

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 47 +-4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"; "longer-term inflation expectations in the survey were also little changed and remained within the narrow range that they have occupied for some time"; inflation compensation "edged down."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Expectations firmly anchored. If anything, the edging down of market-based inflation compensation suggests anchored slightly below target rather than at target.
- **Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS is at or slightly below neutral because expectations are well-anchored and stable, with market measures edging down.
