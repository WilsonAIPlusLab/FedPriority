# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2011-01-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type:** Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context:** January 2011 — roughly 18 months into the recovery from the Great Recession. Unemployment remains very high (~9.4%), the Fed is conducting QE2 (large-scale asset purchases of longer-term Treasuries), and the federal funds rate is at 0-0.25%. The recovery is firming but substantial slack persists. Euro-area peripheral sovereign debt concerns are ongoing.

### Dimensional Direction Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:** Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Key language: "measures of underlying inflation remained subdued," "core CPI edged up" but "12-month change in the core CPI remained near the very low readings," "labor cost pressures were still restrained," "increases in core PCE prices would remain subdued in 2011 and 2012." Energy and commodity prices are rising, but the staff clearly distinguishes between headline pressures (energy/food) and underlying inflation which is subdued. The "persistent wide margin of economic slack" is expected to maintain downward pressure. Score should be in the 28-35 range — below target, subdued, but not deflation risk. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says subdued/low. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP:** Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. Key: "the expansion had not yet been sufficient to bring about a significant improvement in labor market conditions," "persistent wide margin of economic slack in the forecast," "the jobless rate was anticipated to remain elevated at the end of 2012," "capacity utilization in manufacturing... remained below its long-run average." The recovery is "firming" and near-term forecast was revised up, but there's clearly a large negative output gap. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language -> must be < 50. Score in the 33-38 range — below potential, recovering, gap persists but narrowing. Anchor check: 2010-14 recovery anchor is 35-42. Fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:** Staff sees labor market as WELL BELOW tight. Key: "unemployment rate decreased to 9.4 percent," "long-duration unemployment remained elevated," "employment-to-population ratio was still at a very low level," "modest gains in employment continued." UE at 9.4% is far from full employment. Anchor: 2011-14 range is 35-40. With 9.4% UE, this should be in the 25-30 range (UE > 7% -> 16-30 range per rubric). The labor market is improving gradually but remains very slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:** Staff sees spending as MIXED but modestly improving. Consumer spending "rose strongly late last year," "picked up in the fourth quarter." Business investment in E&S "increased further" but at a "more moderate rate." However, residential and nonresidential construction "remained weak," housing market "remained weak." Net position: consumer spending is solid, business E&S moderate, but construction/housing very weak. Weighted net: below trend to at-trend, around 42-47.

**PRODUCTIVITY:** Staff mentions "productivity gains appeared to remain substantial" in the context of labor costs. This is a brief mention but directional — above trend. Score ~55-58. Confidence low given limited discussion.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS:** Staff describes a MIXED picture leaning somewhat accommodative/supportive. Short-term funding "remained stable," spreads on commercial paper "remained low," equity prices rose, corporate bond spreads narrowed somewhat. However: "tight credit conditions for construction loans and mortgages," CRE financing "remained tight," residential mortgage lending standards tight. Accommodative monetary policy is supporting conditions. Net: somewhat loose but with pockets of tightness. Score around 40-45 — somewhat loose to neutral. Bright-line check: Staff language leans "supportive/accommodative" for broad markets but "tight" for real estate credit. Net score slightly below 50 is appropriate.

**TREASURY_STRESS:** Staff explicitly states "The purchases of longer-term Treasury securities by the Desk during the intermeeting period reportedly had no significant effects on measures of day-to-day Treasury market functioning." Yields rose modestly on economic data, which is normal. Market-based measures of uncertainty "declined on balance." No dysfunction described. Score ~30-35 — normal, well-functioning. Bright-line: no dysfunction = 36-50 per rubric. I'll score 35 given "no significant effects on functioning" is slightly positive.

**CREDIT_SPREADS:** Staff notes investment- and speculative-grade spreads "somewhat narrower," secondary market for leveraged loans "bid price moved up further," "net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations was robust," bond issuance "particularly strong." Spreads are narrowing and credit is available. However, municipal bonds show "atypically high level of default risk." CRE credit "remained tight." Net: below-normal stress in corporate credit but pockets of stress in muni/CRE. Score ~35-40. Bright-line check: "narrower" spreads -> should be < 50. Correct.

**EQUITY_VALUATION:** Staff notes "Broad U.S. stock price indexes rose, on net," "equity risk premium... narrowed further over the period but remained elevated relative to longer-run norms." The elevated risk premium suggests equities are not overvalued — if anything, still somewhat stressed/undervalued relative to norms. Score ~45-50. No strong valuation concern expressed.

**BANKING_STRESS:** Staff describes: core loans "edged down again, but the rate of contraction appeared to be abating," C&I loans "expanded at a robust pace," some banks easing lending standards for C&I. CRE loans and delinquencies "stayed elevated." Serious delinquency rates on mortgages "flattened out" after improving earlier. Mixed: improving but residual stress. Leverage "remained well below pre-crisis levels." Score ~50-55 — neutral with some remaining stress from CRE delinquencies and mortgage issues. Bright-line: not described as "well-capitalized/sound" nor as "stressed/failing." Neutral-to-slightly-elevated stress.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH:** Staff explicitly states: "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined more than 1 percent over the intermeeting period, depreciating by roughly similar amounts... against the currencies of the AFEs and the EMEs." Clear depreciation. Bright-line: depreciation -> score < 50. Score ~30-35. "Declined more than 1 percent" is notable but not extreme. Staff forecast assumptions include "a lower foreign exchange value of the dollar."

### Inflation Sub-components

**INFLATION_GOODS:** "goods prices were unchanged" in core CPI; nonfuel industrial commodity prices rising but not passing through to consumer goods yet. Score ~40 — slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_SERVICES:** "prices of non-energy services rose slightly." Score ~42 — just below neutral, very modest.

**INFLATION_HOUSING:** "measures of house prices declined further in recent months," housing market "remained weak," "soft demand." This is about asset prices more than shelter inflation, but the direction is clearly down. Score ~30 — below neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:** "longer-run inflation expectations were stable," "survey measures of households' long-term inflation expectations stayed in the range that has prevailed for some time." 5-year TIPS compensation moved up (commodities/outlook), but 5-to-10-year ahead "edged down slightly." Well-anchored. Score ~48 — at/near target, stable.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (27):** Both well below 50, consistent with large slack. Gap of 8 is reasonable — labor market lagging the output recovery (typical).
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (35) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (44):** Gap of 9. Spending is improving faster than the overall gap is closing, which is consistent with early recovery dynamics. Acceptable.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (37):** Both below 50, consistent. FC slightly higher due to pockets of real estate tightness.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (42) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (35):** Both below 50, consistent. No crisis, Treasury market functioning well.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) <-> sub-dimensions:** Goods ~40, Services ~42, Housing ~30, Expectations ~48. Weighted toward the low end given housing drag and the staff's clear "subdued" assessment. Parent score of 32 is slightly below the average of sub-components, but the staff's strong "subdued" language and "wide margin of slack" keeping downward pressure justifies the parent being weighted toward the lower end.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Measures of underlying consumer price inflation remained low." "The 12-month change in the core CPI remained near the very low readings of the previous two months." "The staff continued to project that increases in core PCE prices would remain subdued in 2011 and 2012." "The persistent wide margin of economic slack in the forecast was expected to maintain downward pressure on inflation."
- **Center of gravity:** Core inflation running well below target with substantial slack expected to keep it subdued; energy/commodity pass-through limited.
- **Direction confirmation:** INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because staff repeatedly characterizes underlying inflation as "subdued," "low," and "restrained."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "The expansion had not yet been sufficient to bring about a significant improvement in labor market conditions." "The persistent wide margin of economic slack in the forecast." "Capacity utilization in manufacturing increased further, although it remained below its long-run average."
- **Center of gravity:** Economy recovering and near-term forecast revised up, but large output gap persists with slack expected to remain through 2012.
- **Direction confirmation:** OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because "wide margin of slack" and "below long-run average" capacity utilization. Bright-line satisfied: slack remains -> < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "The unemployment rate decreased to 9.4 percent in December, but this decline in part reflected a further drop in the labor force participation rate." "Long-duration unemployment remained elevated, and the employment-to-population ratio was still at a very low level." "Wage increases slowed along with inflation... labor cost pressures were still restrained."
- **Center of gravity:** UE at 9.4% is far from full employment; long-duration unemployment elevated; employment-to-population ratio very low. Significant labor slack.
- **Direction confirmation:** LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well below neutral. UE > 7% puts this squarely in the 16-30 range per rubric.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Consumer spending rose strongly late last year." "Real business investment in equipment and software appeared to have increased further in the fourth quarter, although likely at a more moderate rate." "Activity in the housing market remained weak." "Business outlays for nonresidential structures stayed weak."
- **Center of gravity:** Consumer spending and business E&S solid and improving, but housing and nonresidential construction remain very weak. Net: slightly below trend.
- **Direction confirmation:** CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral due to the drag from weak construction offsetting solid consumer spending.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 57 +/-10 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Productivity gains appeared to remain substantial." (In context of restraining labor costs.)
- **Center of gravity:** Brief mention suggests above-trend productivity, but very limited discussion.
- **Direction confirmation:** PRODUCTIVITY is above neutral — "substantial" gains.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable." "Spreads on unsecured nonfinancial commercial paper remained low." "Net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations was robust." But also: "tight credit conditions for construction loans and mortgages," "financing conditions for most types of commercial real estate remained tight."
- **Center of gravity:** Broad financial conditions somewhat accommodative/supportive — robust corporate issuance, stable funding markets, rising equities — but real estate credit remains tight.
- **Direction confirmation:** FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (less tight/more accommodative) because supportive corporate credit, low funding spreads, and rising equity prices dominate, despite CRE/mortgage tightness. Bright-line: "accommodative"/"supportive" language -> < 50. Satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "The purchases of longer-term Treasury securities by the Desk during the intermeeting period reportedly had no significant effects on measures of day-to-day Treasury market functioning." "Market-based measures of uncertainty about longer-term Treasury yields... declined on balance."
- **Center of gravity:** Treasury market functioning normally with declining uncertainty — no dysfunction.
- **Direction confirmation:** TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral — no dysfunction, market functioning well. Bright-line: no actual dysfunction described, score < 50. Satisfied.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Both investment- and speculative-grade spreads over yields on comparable-maturity Treasury securities somewhat narrower." "Net debt financing by U.S. nonfinancial corporations was robust... Net issuance of bonds was particularly strong."
- **Center of gravity:** Corporate credit spreads narrowing with strong issuance; credit broadly available for corporations. Municipal market shows stress but is a secondary factor.
- **Direction confirmation:** CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral (less stress) because spreads are narrowing and issuance is robust. Bright-line: "narrower" -> < 50. Satisfied.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 48 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Broad U.S. stock price indexes rose, on net, over the intermeeting period, extending their recent strong performance." "The spread between the staff's estimate of the expected real equity return for S&P 500 firms and the real 10-year Treasury yield... narrowed further over the period but remained elevated relative to longer-run norms."
- **Center of gravity:** Equity prices rising but risk premium still elevated relative to norms — no overvaluation concern, roughly fair-valued to slightly undervalued.
- **Direction confirmation:** EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — no valuation alarm, risk premium still elevated (slightly favoring equities as undervalued).

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "Core loans on banks' books... edged down again, but the rate of contraction appeared to be abating." "Modest net fractions of banks continued to ease standards for C&I loans." But: "Delinquency rates for broad categories of commercial real estate loans stayed elevated." "Serious delinquency rates on prime and subprime mortgages flattened out."
- **Center of gravity:** Banking sector improving — easing lending standards, C&I loans expanding — but residual stress from elevated CRE delinquencies and mortgage issues. Roughly neutral with slight remaining stress.
- **Direction confirmation:** BANKING_STRESS is near neutral — improving conditions but still-elevated delinquencies keep it from falling below 50.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 32 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence:** "The broad nominal index of the U.S. dollar declined more than 1 percent over the intermeeting period, depreciating by roughly similar amounts, on average, against the currencies of the AFEs and the EMEs."
- **Center of gravity:** Clear dollar depreciation driven by stronger foreign activity, foreign policy tightening, and commodity price increases.
- **Direction confirmation:** DOLLAR_STRENGTH is below neutral because the dollar depreciated. Bright-line: depreciation -> < 50. Satisfied.

### Inflation Sub-components

**INFLATION_GOODS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Goods prices were unchanged" in core CPI. Commodity input prices rising but not yet passing through. Slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Prices of non-energy services rose slightly." Very modest increases — slightly below neutral.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Measures of house prices declined further in recent months." Housing activity "remained weak" with "soft demand." Below neutral — declining shelter/house prices.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Longer-run inflation expectations were stable." "Survey measures of households' long-term inflation expectations stayed in the range that has prevailed for some time." 5-to-10-year TIPS "edged down slightly." Well-anchored near target.
