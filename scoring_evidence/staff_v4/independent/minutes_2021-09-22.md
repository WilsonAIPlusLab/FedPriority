# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2021-09-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Identification
- **Type**: Minutes (synthesized summary with staff-attributable language: "the staff reported," "the staff's near-term outlook," "the staff continued to judge")
- **Segmentation**: Post-2005 segmented minutes with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook"
- **Scoring scope**: Staff-attributable content only

### Era Context
September 2021: The U.S. economy is in a strong but uneven recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic. The Delta variant is creating headwinds. Inflation has surged well above 2% driven by supply-demand mismatches, supply chain bottlenecks, and reopening effects. Unemployment is falling rapidly but remains elevated relative to pre-pandemic levels. The Fed has not yet begun tapering asset purchases. This is a period of high inflation, recovering but still-below-potential labor markets, very accommodative financial conditions, and significant supply chain disruptions.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Staff sees inflation as WELL ABOVE the midpoint. Evidence: Total PCE inflation was 4.2% (12-month), core PCE 3.6%, CPI 5.3%, core CPI 4.0%. Staff notes "surge in demand," "production bottlenecks and supply constraints" putting "a larger amount of upward pressure on prices than previously anticipated." The near-term outlook was "revised up further." Staff expected inflation to "remain high" and "end the year well above 2 percent." Risks around inflation were "tilted to the upside." The "transitory" framing does not reduce the current score per rubric instructions.
- Direction: Well above neutral. Anchor suggests 65-72 for 2021 Q3.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says high and rising inflation. Score matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Staff sees the economy as operating with output ABOVE potential. Critical evidence: "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period." GDP increasing in Q3 at a slower pace than Q2 but still posting "sizable gains." Growth projected to "step down noticeably in 2023 and to be roughly equal to potential output growth in 2023 and 2024."
- Bright-line check: "well above potential" language mandates score > 50.
- Direction: Above neutral. The "well above potential" language combined with ongoing strong growth suggests a score in the 55-62 range.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Staff sees labor markets as mixed but tightening rapidly. UE rate at 5.2% in August, down from 5.9% in June — "very rapid" pace of improvement in July but "slowed sharply in August." Total payroll employment had "retraced three-fourths" of pandemic losses. Job openings suggest "labor demand was extraordinarily high." Average hourly earnings "rose strongly," with "continued strong labor demand and increased difficulties in hiring." Wage growth "well above the growth rates seen before the pandemic." ECI rose at 3.6% annual rate. However, UE at 5.2% is still above pre-pandemic levels (~3.5%), and the labor force participation rate only "edged up."
- At 5.2% UE, the rubric suggests range 31-45 ("slack remains; unemployment elevated; UE 5.5-7%") to 46-52 ("near NAIRU"). But the extraordinary labor demand and wage pressures push this higher. The tension is between the still-elevated unemployment rate and the very tight demand-side indicators (job openings, wages, hiring difficulties).
- Direction: Modestly below full employment but rapidly tightening with strong demand-side signals. Score around 48-53.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Mixed evidence but strong demand signals pull this toward tighter.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Staff sees mixed picture. Real PCE declined in July but retail sales "rose strongly in August." Consumer services spending slowing due to Delta variant concerns. Consumer confidence moved lower. Housing demand "remained very strong" but supply constraints restrained building. Business fixed investment growth "slowing somewhat" due to supply bottlenecks. Manufacturing output "rose strongly in July."
- Net: Consumer spending choppy but still solid overall, business investment decelerating somewhat due to supply constraints, housing demand very strong. This is roughly at-trend to modestly above with cross-currents.
- Direction: Around neutral to slightly above. Score 48-55.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Not directly discussed. N/A.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Staff describes very accommodative conditions. "Market-based financing conditions were robust." "Credit availability improved for riskier borrowers." Mortgage financing "remained accommodative." Consumer credit "remained accommodative for most borrowers." CRE financing conditions "improved." Municipal bond market "remained accommodative." Small business loans "above pre-pandemic levels." Corporate bond issuance "remained solid," leveraged loan issuance "strong," equity funding "robust."
- Bright-line check: "accommodative" / "robust" language mandates score < 50 (meaning loose/supportive conditions, low tightness).
- Direction: Well below neutral (very accommodative). Score around 20-28.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very accommodative/loose. Low score matches.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Treasury yields "increased modestly" amid mixed data. No mention of market dysfunction, liquidity problems, auction failures, or settlement issues. Short-term funding markets "were stable." This is normal market functioning.
- Bright-line check: Yield movements alone are NOT dysfunction. Normal functioning.
- Direction: Normal to quiet. Score around 35-42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction mentioned. Low-to-neutral score matches.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Speculative-grade spreads "narrowed modestly." Investment-grade spreads "little changed." Municipal bond spreads "remained well below pre-pandemic levels." Agency CMBS spreads "generally at or below pre-pandemic levels." Credit quality "remained strong with a positive outlook." Upgrades outpaced downgrades. Default rates "remained low."
- Bright-line check: "narrowed" = compressed = score < 50.
- Direction: Well below neutral (compressed/favorable). Score around 20-28.
- Polarity check: Higher = more credit stress. Evidence says tight/compressed spreads, strong credit quality. Low score matches.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
"Broad stock market prices were about unchanged, on net." Concerns about Delta variant and Chinese property developer caused temporary fluctuations but were "mostly reversed." No valuation commentary from staff.
- No valuation language, just price direction (unchanged). Default to 45-55 per rubric.
- Direction: Neutral. Score around 48.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Not directly discussed as a system-level topic. CRE loan growth "strengthened." No mention of bank capital, stress, NPLs, or lending conditions from a system health perspective (beyond credit availability which falls under financial conditions/credit spreads). Some mention of PPP loan forgiveness affecting C&I balances, but this is mechanical not stress-related.
- N/A — banking system health not specifically discussed.

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
"The broad dollar appreciated a touch." Brief mention, minimal discussion.
- Bright-line check: Appreciation = score > 50.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral. Score around 52-55.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger dollar. "Appreciated a touch" = slightly strong. Matches.

**Inflation Sub-Components:**

**S1. INFLATION_GOODS**
Staff notes "surge in demand," "production bottlenecks and supply constraints" especially for motor vehicles. Supply chain issues across industries. This is strongly goods-price inflationary.
- Direction: Well above neutral. Score 72-78.

**S2. INFLATION_SERVICES**
Demand for "services sensitive to social distancing" was slowing. No specific services inflation discussion separate from overall PCE. Limited evidence.
- Direction: Modestly above neutral given overall high inflation environment, but Delta was suppressing services demand. Score around 55-60.

**S3. INFLATION_HOUSING**
"Demand for housing appeared to have remained very strong" but this is about housing demand/activity, not specifically shelter/rent inflation. Limited direct discussion of housing inflation.
- N/A — housing inflation not specifically discussed (housing demand discussed but not in inflation terms).

**S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**
"The staff's common inflation expectations index... was little changed relative to the second quarter and was near its average over the decade before the pandemic." However, inflation compensation "declined modestly." Staff flagged "a risk that longer-run inflation expectations would move appreciably higher."
- Direction: Near neutral/anchored. The expectations index near pre-pandemic average is essentially at-target anchoring. Score around 50-53.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (58) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (52)**: 6-point divergence — coherent. Both modestly above neutral.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (24) vs TREASURY_STRESS (38)**: FC very loose while Treasury stress normal — coherent for non-crisis period.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Total PCE price inflation was 4.2 percent over the 12 months ending in July, and core PCE price inflation... was 3.6 percent"
- "The staff's near-term outlook for inflation was revised up further in response to incoming data" and "supply constraints were putting a larger amount of upward pressure on prices than previously anticipated"
- Risks "tilted to the upside, with the possibility of more severe and persistent supply issues viewed as especially salient"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation well above target with upside risks, driven by supply-demand imbalances and reopening effects; near-term outlook revised further upward.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is well above neutral because PCE inflation is running more than double the 2% target with upside risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period"
- "real GDP was expected to post a sizable gain over the second half of 2021"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly states GDP is well above potential, though growth is decelerating from Q2's rapid pace.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says GDP is "well above potential." Bright-line rule satisfied (>50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate had declined from 5.9 percent in June to 5.2 percent in August"
- "labor demand was extraordinarily high" and "Average hourly earnings for all employees rose strongly... with gains that were widespread across industries"
- "continued strong labor demand and increased difficulties in hiring"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market shows extreme demand-side tightness (extraordinary job openings, strong wage growth, hiring difficulties) but supply-side slack persists (UE 5.2%, participation only edging up, employment still 25% below pre-pandemic peak). The two sides roughly balance to near-NAIRU conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is approximately at neutral — demand-side signals suggest tightness but UE rate of 5.2% still implies some slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "U.S. real GDP was increasing in the third quarter at a slower pace than in the second quarter"
- "real PCE declined in July" but "retail sales... rose strongly in August"
- "growth in business fixed investment was slowing somewhat in the third quarter as supply bottlenecks... weighed on business equipment spending"
- "Demand for housing appeared to have remained very strong"

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed signals with choppy consumer spending, decelerating business investment due to supply constraints, and very strong housing demand. Net picture is roughly at trend with some upside from demand strength offset by supply-side constraints.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is approximately at neutral — demand is solid but supply disruptions and Delta variant are restraining actual spending and investment.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: Not discussed in the document.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Market-based financing conditions were robust, and credit availability improved for riskier borrowers"
- "Financing conditions in commercial real estate improved" / "residential mortgage market, financing conditions remained accommodative" / "Financing conditions for consumer credit remained accommodative for most borrowers"

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff describes pervasively accommodative financial conditions across all major markets — corporate, mortgage, consumer, CRE, and municipal.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well below neutral (meaning very loose) because staff uses "accommodative" and "robust" repeatedly across all segments. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Yields on intermediate-maturity Treasury securities increased modestly, on net, amid mixed news"
- "Short-term funding markets were stable over the intermeeting period"

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally with modest yield moves and stable short-term funding. No dysfunction mentioned.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because there is no evidence of market dysfunction — only normal yield adjustments.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 23 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "spreads of yields on speculative-grade corporate bonds... narrowed slightly" / "Investment-grade corporate bond spreads were little changed"
- "The credit quality of large nonfinancial corporations remained strong with a positive outlook. The volume of credit rating upgrades... outpaced downgrades noticeably"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads compressed/narrowing with strong credit quality, low defaults, and positive rating momentum. Conditions are clearly favorable/easy.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is well below neutral because spreads are narrow and narrowing further. Bright-line rule satisfied (<50).
