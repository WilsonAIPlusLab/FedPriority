# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-11-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document with clearly separated staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member language needs to be excluded.

### Era Context
November 2016. The U.S. economy is in a mature expansion following the GFC. Unemployment at 5.0% — near estimates of NAIRU. Inflation running below 2% target but gradually firming. The Fed has raised rates once (December 2015) and is widely expected to raise again in December 2016. Dollar has been strong. Financial conditions are accommodative.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at ~1.25% over 12 months, core PCE at ~1.75%. "Still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." Restrained by earlier energy and import price declines. Michigan longer-run expectations moved to "a new historical low." Staff projects inflation "marginally below" 2% even in 2019. This is clearly below target — range 35-42.
- Direction: Below 50. "Running below 2 percent" with near-term forecast "somewhat higher" but still below target.
- Magnitude: "Below" target but not dramatically — "restrained in part" suggests moderate shortfall. ~38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy operating ABOVE potential. "The staff projected that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 and 2018." "An economy that was projected to continue operating above its long-run potential." GDP growth faster in H2 than H1. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires score > 50.
- Direction: Above 50. Economy projected to operate above potential.
- Magnitude: "Modestly faster pace than potential" — modest language = 5-10 points above midpoint. ~53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as near full employment, modestly tight. UE at 5.0%, projected to "edge down gradually" and "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Part-time for economic reasons "still slightly elevated." Compensation rising at "moderate pace" — ECI 2.25%, AHE 2.5%. The unemployment rate at 5.0% places this around full employment per the rubric (46-52 for near NAIRU). But the projection of running below natural rate in future suggests modestly tight. Anchor: 50 for 2016-17.
- Direction: At/slightly above 50. Near NAIRU now, projected below natural rate.
- Magnitude: ~50-52. Currently at 5.0%, which is near NAIRU. Wage growth moderate. Part-time still slightly elevated.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "increased at a moderate pace" with "continued gains in employment, real disposable personal income, and households' net worth." Consumer sentiment "relatively upbeat." But housing was "weak" — residential investment decreased. Business equipment spending "about flat." Business structures increased. Inventory investment positive after drag. Net: moderate consumer spending offset by weak housing and flat business investment.
- Direction: Near midpoint. "Moderate" consumer spending but weakness elsewhere.
- Magnitude: ~46. Below-trend overall due to weakness in housing and flat business investment offsetting moderate consumption.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff repeatedly describes conditions as "accommodative." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms and households remained accommodative, on balance." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained generally accommodative." CRE "largely accommodative but showed some signs of tightening." Residential mortgage "credit remained readily available." Consumer credit "little changed" with "largely stable credit quality." Bright-line: "accommodative" requires score < 50.
- Direction: Below 50. Accommodative = loose = low score.
- Magnitude: Clearly accommodative with multiple confirmations but some CRE tightening. ~35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Domestic financial markets were relatively calm." "Asset prices were little changed, and volatility was mostly low." "No significant market disruptions were observed around the October 14 compliance deadline for MMF reform." Nominal Treasury yields "edged up on net." No dysfunction described. Bright-line: normal yield movements, no dysfunction.
- Direction: Below 50. Calm, orderly markets.
- Magnitude: ~30. Explicitly calm with low volatility.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads of yields on nonfinancial investment-grade and speculative-grade corporate bonds over those of comparable-maturity Treasury securities declined a bit, with both spreads finishing the period at levels close to their medians during the economic expansions of the past two decades." CMBS spreads "little changed." Consumer ABS spreads "little changed." Default rates "edged down, although they remained elevated."
- Direction: Below 50. Spreads at median historical level and declining a bit.
- Magnitude: Spreads near historical medians — roughly average. ~42. "Close to medians" suggests near-normal but slightly favorable given the decline.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes were little changed." "Realized and implied volatility in equity markets remained relatively low." Valuations for domestic corporate equity "on balance, in the middle of their historical ranges." No overvaluation or undervaluation concern expressed.
- Direction: Near 50. Fair valued, no concern.
- Magnitude: ~47. "Middle of their historical ranges" = roughly fair valued.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "Vulnerabilities emanating from leverage in the financial sector remained low, as the largest U.S. banks had strong regulatory capital and liquidity positions." "Valuation pressures across major asset categories remained at a moderate level." CRE lending standards tightened somewhat. Bright-line: "strong regulatory capital and liquidity positions" = low stress, must be < 50.
- Direction: Below 50. Banks healthy, low stress.
- Magnitude: ~30. Strong capital, low leverage vulnerabilities. Some CRE valuation pressures noted but system sound.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The dollar appreciated against most AFE currencies; the appreciation against the pound was particularly pronounced." "The dollar depreciated against the currencies of most commodity-exporting countries." Also, staff outlook mentions "higher assumed paths for the dollar" weighing on GDP forecast. Net: dollar appreciation against AFEs, depreciation against some EM. Bright-line: appreciation = > 50.
- Direction: Above 50. Net appreciation.
- Magnitude: Appreciation against most AFEs but depreciation against commodity exporters. Mixed but net appreciation. ~57.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: PCE held down by "decreases in the prices of non-energy imports" and "pass-through of earlier declines in energy prices into the prices of other goods and services." Manufacturing "relatively flat." Consumer food prices decreased. Goods prices clearly soft.
- Direction: Below 50. Goods deflation/disinflation.
- Magnitude: ~32. Active declines in import and food prices restraining overall inflation.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately broken out. Core CPI at 2.25% while core PCE at 1.75% — the wedge often reflects services (shelter). Not enough to score independently.
- N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Housing market activity was weak in the third quarter." But housing inflation (shelter component) is not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Most survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed, on balance, while market-based measures of inflation compensation moved up but remained low." Michigan survey median longer-run expectations "moved down in October to a new historical low." Blue Chip "also declined slightly." Desk surveys unchanged. Market-based inflation compensation moved up (helped by oil prices, reduced deflation tail risk) but from low levels.
- Direction: Below 50. Expectations anchored but showing some downside drift in surveys.
- Magnitude: ~42. Michigan at new historical low is concerning. Market-based moved up but "remained low." Net slightly below neutral.

### Bright-Line Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP: "above potential" -> score 53 > 50. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" -> score 35 < 50. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: at medians, declining a bit -> score 42 < 50. Consistent (near-normal to favorable). PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: "strong capital and liquidity" -> score 30 < 50. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "appreciated against most AFE currencies" -> score 57 > 50. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: "relatively calm," no dysfunction -> score 30, normal. PASS.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51): Close, coherent. Both near potential/NAIRU.
2. OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (46): 7-point gap. Economy modestly above potential driven partly by earlier momentum, but current spending mixed. Acceptable — GDP above potential while current-quarter spending data is mixed.
3. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (42): Both below 50, FC more accommodative than spreads suggest. Spreads at historical medians while conditions described as accommodative. Coherent — accommodative conditions reflected in multiple credit channels, spreads near normal.
4. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (35) vs TREASURY_STRESS (30): Both calm. Coherent.
5. INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) vs sub-dimensions: INFLATION_GOODS (32), INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (42). Weighted average consistent with 38.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation increased further above its pace early in the year but was still running below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." PCE inflation ~1.25% over 12 months; core PCE ~1.75%. Staff projects inflation "marginally below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2019."
**Center of gravity**: Inflation clearly below target, restrained by energy and import price pass-through, but gradually firming.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below the 2% target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff projected that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2017 and 2018." "An economy that was projected to continue operating above its long-run potential."
**Center of gravity**: Economy modestly above potential with GDP growth exceeding potential growth.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly states economy operating above potential. Bright-line satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was little changed at 5.0 percent." UE projected to "run below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons was still slightly elevated." Compensation rising at "moderate pace" — ECI 2.25%, AHE 2.5%.
**Center of gravity**: Labor market near full employment — at NAIRU now, projected below it. Moderate wage growth and slightly elevated part-time work temper tightness assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at/slightly above neutral because UE is at NAIRU (5.0%) with projections of going below it, but residual slack indicators persist.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE) increased at a moderate pace in the third quarter." But "housing market activity was weak in the third quarter" and "real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property were about flat." Staff forecast for H2 "lower than in the September projection."
**Center of gravity**: Moderate consumer spending offset by weak housing and flat business investment yields a slightly below-trend composite.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the mix of moderate consumption with weak housing and flat business capex nets to slightly below trend.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the document.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms and households remained accommodative, on balance." "Credit remained readily available for most borrowers." "Gross issuance of corporate bonds was robust." Some CRE tightening noted.
**Center of gravity**: Broadly accommodative financing conditions across corporate, household, and mortgage markets, with only minor CRE tightening.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because staff repeatedly characterizes conditions as "accommodative." Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial markets were relatively calm." "Asset prices were little changed, and volatility was mostly low." "No significant market disruptions were observed around the October 14 compliance deadline for MMF reform."
**Center of gravity**: Treasury markets functioning normally with low volatility and calm conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because markets were explicitly calm with no dysfunction.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of yields on nonfinancial investment-grade and speculative-grade corporate bonds...declined a bit, with both spreads finishing the period at levels close to their medians during the economic expansions of the past two decades." Default rates "edged down, although they remained elevated."
**Center of gravity**: Spreads near historical expansion medians and declining — slightly favorable credit conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is slightly below neutral because spreads are at/near historical medians and narrowing.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 47 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes were little changed." "Valuations for domestic corporate equity and bonds were, on balance, in the middle of their historical ranges." "Realized and implied volatility in equity markets remained relatively low."
**Center of gravity**: Equities roughly fair valued with no staff concern about over- or undervaluation.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral because valuations are in the "middle of their historical ranges."

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Vulnerabilities emanating from leverage in the financial sector remained low, as the largest U.S. banks had strong regulatory capital and liquidity positions." "Overall vulnerabilities remained moderate."
**Center of gravity**: Banking system healthy with strong capital — low stress. Some moderate vulnerabilities from CRE and corporate leverage but system-level stress is low.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because banks are well-capitalized with low leverage vulnerabilities. Bright-line satisfied.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The dollar appreciated against most AFE currencies; the appreciation against the pound was particularly pronounced." "The dollar depreciated against the currencies of most commodity-exporting countries." Staff forecast reflects "higher assumed paths for the dollar."
**Center of gravity**: Net dollar appreciation, especially against AFE currencies, partly offset by depreciation against commodity exporters.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated against most AFE currencies. Bright-line satisfied.
