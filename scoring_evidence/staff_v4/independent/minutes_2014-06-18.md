# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2014-06-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Mid-2014 -- the U.S. economy is in the gradual recovery phase following the GFC. The Fed is still tapering QE3. Unemployment has come down from its crisis peaks but remains elevated. Inflation is running below the 2% target. This is a period of moderate growth with remaining slack.

### Dimensional Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at ~1.5% over 12 months ending in April, below the 2% objective. Core PCE also ~1.5%. CPI stepped up more recently (total and core CPI ~2% over 12 months ending in May). Near-term inflation expectations declined slightly; longer-term expectations little changed. Staff projects inflation to "remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years." Compensation measures "remained modest" (~2%). The near-term forecast was revised up slightly, but medium-term revised down. Overall: below target, gradually approaching -- this is clearly below-midpoint territory. Score range: 36-42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as BELOW potential. GDP "dropped significantly early in the year" but "bounced back." Staff expects GDP to "rise more quickly than potential output" going forward. "The expansion in economic activity was anticipated to slowly reduce resource slack over the projection period." Unemployment rate expected to "decline gradually to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate in the medium term." Key phrase: "resource slack" -- this confirms below potential. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language present --> score MUST be < 50. Score range: 38-42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as BELOW full employment but improving. Unemployment at 6.3% (down markedly), but "still elevated." Participation rate declined. Part-time for economic reasons and long-duration unemployment "still high." Rate of hiring "remained at a modest level." Compensation growth modest at ~2%. At 6.3% unemployment, the anchors suggest 31-45 range ("slack remains"; unemployment elevated; UE 5.5-7%). Score range: 36-40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as MIXED, below trend but with forward-looking positive signals. Real PCE "declined a little in April following strong gains in February and March." Housing sector "remained subdued." Business equipment spending "increased slowly." But forward-looking indicators are positive: rising disposable income, equity/home value gains, new orders above shipments. GDP growth expected to "rebound briskly in the second quarter." This is a mixed picture -- recent data soft but outlook improving. Score range: 40-45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Staff mentions productivity briefly: "small gains in labor productivity" and lowered assumed pace of potential output growth. This suggests below-trend productivity. Score range: 35-40.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly says "financial conditions in the United States remained supportive of growth in economic activity and employment." Treasury yields moved down modestly, equity prices rose, corporate bond spreads narrowed, dollar little changed. VIX near historical lows. Credit flows strong. This is clearly accommodative/supportive. Bright-line check: "supportive" --> score MUST be < 50. Score range: 28-35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: No dysfunction described. Yields moved normally. Short-term funding markets "remained stable." ON RRP exercise functioning. No liquidity problems, no auction issues. This is normal/calm market functioning. Score range: 25-32.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff says "corporate bond spreads narrowed." Credit flows to nonfinancial corporations "remained strong." Bond issuance "rebounded." C&I loans increased, leveraged loan issuance strong. CRE loans increasing "amid some further easing of underwriting standards." Consumer credit "solid." This is clearly below-normal stress / compressed spreads. Bright-line check: "narrowed" --> MUST be < 50. Score range: 22-30.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes rose." VIX "near its historical lows." Staff notes markets "boosted by a more optimistic assessment of near-term economic prospects and likely supported by continued low interest rates." Staff cites possible "complacency on the part of market participants about potential risks." This hints at some mild overvaluation concern but is not strongly worded. Score range: 35-42.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed specifically as a standalone topic. Lending is expanding, underwriting standards easing somewhat on CRE. Mortgage credit conditions "generally remained tight" -- but this is about credit availability, not banking system stress per se. The overall picture from lending/credit data suggests healthy banks. But no explicit "well-capitalized" or "sound" language. Score: N/A (banking system health not explicitly discussed).

Wait -- there is indirect evidence. CRE loans increasing, C&I loans increasing, easing underwriting standards, robust ABS issuance. This paints a picture of active, healthy banking. But it's indirect. I'll score this as N/A since there's no direct discussion of banking system conditions.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff says "the foreign exchange value of the dollar was little changed" (mentioned twice). Dollar appreciated against the euro but declined against Canadian dollar and emerging market currencies. Net: "little changed." Bright-line: stable = 46-54. Score: 48-50.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not broken out separately (healthcare mentioned only in context of GDP). N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Not discussed as inflation sub-component. Housing activity discussed but not shelter inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Staff reports: "measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." Near-term Michigan expectations "declined slightly." TIPS-based inflation compensation "remained about steady." This is well-anchored. Score range: 45-48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (40) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38)**: Both below 50, both reflect remaining slack. Coherent, divergence ~2 points.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP (40) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43)**: Both below 50, reflecting below-trend activity. Coherent, divergence ~3 points.

3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) <-> CREDIT_SPREADS (25)**: Both below 50, both show accommodative/loose conditions. Coherent, both in the accommodative range.

4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) <-> TREASURY_STRESS (28)**: Both low -- calm financial conditions and no Treasury dysfunction. Coherent.

5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) <-> INFLATION sub-dimensions**: Only INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS scored (47). Headline inflation below target but expectations anchored near target -- this is coherent (expectations anchored even as actual inflation runs below).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer price inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, was about 1-1/2 percent over the 12 months ending in April, below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent"
- "The staff continued to forecast that inflation would remain below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over the next few years"

**Center-of-gravity**: PCE inflation running at 1.5%, well below the 2% target, with staff projecting it to remain below target for years. Recent CPI readings stepped up but PCE remains below target.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running below the 2% target and projected to stay there.
