## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type

I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by cues like "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," "the staff's judgment," and factual data summaries. Participant content ("members noted," "members described," "members generally viewed") must be excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
August 1995: The economy had slowed in Q2 1995 after the 1994 tightening cycle. The Fed eased slightly in July 1995. The economy was transitioning from a mid-cycle soft patch back toward moderate growth. Unemployment at 5.7%. Inflation had been moderately elevated early in the year but was decelerating. This is a mid-cycle adjustment period.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Assessment

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as slightly above neutral. Key evidence: "After increasing at elevated rates in the early part of the year, consumer and producer prices had risen more slowly in recent months." "Advances in labor compensation costs remained subdued." However, "Excluding food and energy, producer prices rose by more over the year ended in July than over the preceding year." "Average hourly earnings grew faster in July than in June; for the year ending in July, earnings rose somewhat more than in the preceding year." Staff forecast: "price inflation likely would not deviate significantly from recent trends" with "some further easing of pressure on labor and other resources."

The picture is mixed. Monthly data show deceleration. But year-over-year core PPI accelerated and earnings growth picked up. Staff expects inflation to hold near recent trends (not decline significantly). In 1995, CPI was running around 2.5-3%. The staff's overall message is: recent moderation but underlying pressures have not fully dissipated.

Self-check: "More slowly" in recent months but still elevated year-over-year. The staff describes a transition from "elevated rates" to "more slowly" — this is deceleration from an elevated level. The net position is modestly above neutral: inflation was elevated, is now moderating, but YoY measures remain somewhat firm.

Score: 52. The staff sees recent moderation from elevated rates, but year-over-year core measures haven't improved and earnings are picking up. Net: slightly above neutral pressure.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Staff sees the economy as below potential. Evidence: "economic activity was expanding more rapidly after increasing at a sluggish pace in the second quarter." "Total utilization of industrial capacity dropped somewhat further." Staff forecast: "the prospects for some further easing of pressure on labor and other resources." The economy is recovering from a soft Q2 but resource pressures are easing, not building.

Bright-line check: "Easing of pressure on labor and other resources" = slack emerging/persisting. Score MUST be < 50. "Capacity utilization dropped somewhat further" = below potential.

Score: 42. Below potential — recovering from soft patch, capacity utilization declining, staff expects further easing of resource pressures.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Staff sees the labor market as moderately slack. UE at 5.7% (near but slightly above period NAIRU estimates of ~5.5%). "Employment gains had been modest." "Continuing employment losses in manufacturing that were widespread by industry." "Payrolls continued to increase at a relatively slow pace." "Advances in labor compensation costs remained subdued." Staff forecast: "some further easing of pressure on labor and other resources."

With UE at 5.7%, modest job gains, manufacturing shedding jobs, and subdued compensation, the labor market is moderately soft. The rubric places UE 5.5-7% in the 31-45 range. At 5.7% with subdued wage pressures and the staff noting further easing expected, this is in the upper portion of that range.

Score: 40. Below full employment. UE at 5.7% with modest gains, manufacturing losses, subdued compensation, and staff expecting further easing of labor pressures.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Staff sees spending and investment as roughly at or modestly above trend. Consumer spending "growing at a moderate rate." Housing "rebounding sharply." Business investment "on a solid uptrend." Retail sales "risen appreciably on balance since early spring." Capital goods shipments "continued to grow rapidly." Housing starts "up strongly in July."

However, motor vehicle spending weak. Business spending for transportation equipment "lackluster." Orders for capital goods "edged lower." Staff forecast: investment "projected to slow from the very rapid pace."

The rebound in housing and solid investment offset weak areas. Staff forecast expects consumer spending to "keep pace with incomes" and investment to slow. Net: moderately positive but not booming.

Score: 50. The strong housing rebound and solid business investment are offset by motor vehicle weakness and expected slowing. Consumer spending is "moderate." Nets to roughly trend.
