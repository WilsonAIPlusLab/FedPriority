### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation as slightly BELOW neutral but with emerging upward pressure.
- Evidence: "Consumer prices advanced at a slightly faster rate in May" with energy/food upturn. Core CPI "rose less over the twelve months ended in May than they had over the previous twelve months." Core PPI "continued to rise at a subdued rate." However, "average hourly earnings accelerated further" on year-over-year basis. Staff forecast: "underlying inflation was expected to pick up gradually as gains in compensation increasingly outpaced improvements in productivity."
- The picture is mixed: 12-month core CPI actually decelerated, core PPI subdued, but recent 3-month core CPI accelerated, wages accelerating, and staff expected gradual pickup. Net: modestly below target-equivalent levels with upward tilt.
- Magnitude: "subdued" core PPI, "slightly faster" CPI, "pick up gradually" — these are mild modifiers. Score around 42-46. The acceleration language and wage pressures push it up slightly. Score: 45.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Mixed but slightly below neutral with upward signals = 45. Checks out.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees economy as ABOVE potential.
- Evidence: "expansion in economic activity had slowed considerably after a very rapid advance in the first quarter." However, the slowdown was largely from inventory moderation. Staff forecast: "pressures on labor resources were likely to diminish somewhat as the expansion of economic activity slowed." The language about "growth in aggregate demand might remain so strong relative to the expansion of the economy's potential that inflationary pressures would tend to be generated" (from prior meeting directive) implies above-potential operation.
- The unemployment rate at 4.3% is very low for this era. Manufacturing capacity utilization "edged down to its lowest level in more than two years" — but this is manufacturing only, not total economy.
- Staff forecast indicates slowing toward potential but currently still above. "Very rapid advance in the first quarter" followed by "slowed considerably" — but slowing from very fast growth, not contraction. Economy still expanding, labor tight.
- Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" language from staff, but staff references demand exceeding potential growth capacity (from previous directive context) and tight labor resources. Score should be modestly above 50.
- Score: 55. Late-90s boom, above potential but slowing.
- Anchor check: Late-90s boom anchor is 57-63. Staff sees slowing, so 55 fits.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT, well above neutral.
- Evidence: "Labor markets remained tight." Unemployment at 4.3%. "Nonfarm payroll employment registered substantial increases in April and May." "There were indications of some further acceleration in employment costs." "Average hourly earnings accelerated further" on year-over-year basis. Staff forecast: "Pressures on labor resources were likely to diminish somewhat."
- Unemployment at 4.3% in 1998 is very tight. The rubric says UE below 4% → above 55, but 4.3% with explicit "tight" language also warrants well above 50.
- Magnitude: "tight" (not "very tight" from staff — though members use "very tight"). Staff says "tight" + "acceleration in employment costs" + "substantial" job gains. Score: 65.
- Anchor check: 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75. Mid-1998 at 4.3% UE with "tight" assessment: 65 fits.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. 65 matches tight labor market. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
- Direction: Staff sees spending as ABOVE trend but decelerating.
- Evidence: "Consumer spending, business investment, and residential homebuilding, though remaining robust, apparently also were decelerating somewhat after very strong gains in the first quarter." "Total nominal retail sales posted large gains in April and May." "Sales of homes were very strong." Business fixed investment "slowed somewhat in the second quarter from a very strong pace."
- Staff forecast: "brisk, though gradually diminishing, increases in consumer spending." "Growth in business fixed investment would gradually moderate from the vigorous pace."
- Net: Still robust/strong but decelerating from a very high level. "Robust" and "strong" = above trend. Score: 60.
- Anchor check: Fits between "solid pace" (50-55) and well above trend (66-80). "Robust" but decelerating from "very strong" = 60.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**

- Score: 55, moderate confidence. Staff implies meaningful productivity gains (holding down unit labor costs) but expects them to be outpaced by compensation growth.
