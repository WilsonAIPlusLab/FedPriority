# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 1999-05-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff review content is mixed with participant/Committee discussion. I must score ONLY staff-attributable content, identified by phrases like "the information reviewed at this meeting suggested," "the staff forecast," and factual data summaries in the opening paragraphs.

### Era Context
May 1999 -- late-1990s expansion/boom period. The economy is growing above potential, labor markets are very tight (UE at 4.3%), productivity is accelerating, and inflation remains surprisingly low despite tight labor markets. The Asian financial crisis is fading. The fed funds rate is at 4.75%, and the Committee is beginning to lean toward tightening.

### Identifying Staff vs. Participant Content

- Paragraphs on economic activity data (lines 19-43): "The information reviewed at this meeting suggested..."
- Financial markets and monetary aggregates (lines 46-55)
- Staff forecast (line 58): "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting suggested..."

**Participant content** (to be IGNORED for scoring):

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation as LOW but with recent upticks. Staff reports: "Inflation had remained low, although consumer prices registered a sizable rise in April; labor costs were still quiescent despite very tight labor markets." Consumer prices "rose substantially in April" boosted by energy. Core CPI for 12 months ended April was "slightly higher than for the year-earlier period." Core PPI was "up noticeably" partly due to tobacco. But "labor costs appeared to have remained quiescent." The staff forecast projected "inflation was projected to increase somewhat on balance over the projection period."
- Current inflation: low but ticking up. Not at or above target in a worrisome way yet. The staff language says "remained low" as the summary characterization, with the April CPI as an outlier month. This is below-midpoint to near-midpoint territory.
- Score direction: Slightly below neutral to near neutral. "Low" but with "sizable rise" in one month and core slightly higher. I'll place this at 45.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation "remained low" but with hints of firming. 45 fits -- slightly below neutral.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff says "economic activity had continued to expand vigorously" and the expansion was at a "rapid pace" that was "greater than the growth in the economy's potential" (staff forecast context). The staff forecast says expansion would "gradually moderate to a rate commensurate with the rise in the economy's estimated potential." This implies the economy is currently ABOVE potential.
- Bright-line check: "above potential" language present --> MUST be > 50.
- The late 90s anchor is 57-63. The economy is running above potential but not severely overheating. Industrial capacity utilization "remained below its long-term average" provides a slight offset. But overall, vigorous expansion above potential.
- Score: 58

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff reports unemployment at 4.3%, and explicitly uses the phrase "very tight labor markets" multiple times. Employment growth "moderated somewhat" but "hiring was still relatively rapid." Labor costs "still quiescent" despite tightness.
- UE at 4.3% in 1999. The rubric says UE below 4% should be 55+ almost certainly. At 4.3% with explicit "very tight" language, this maps to the 53-65 range. The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75.
- "Very tight" is strong language. UE at 4.3% is well below estimates of NAIRU for that era (~5%). Hiring still "relatively rapid."
- Score: 67. The staff explicitly says "very tight labor markets" which is strong language.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: "Consumer spending had maintained its strong forward momentum" -- "very strong this year." Business capital spending "decelerated... though to a still relatively rapid pace." Housing "generally had remained at a high level." Overall: "economic activity had continued to expand vigorously."
- Consumer spending: very strong, "robust," "exceptionally high" auto sales. Well above trend.
- Business investment: "decelerated" but "still relatively rapid" and "quite rapid."
- Housing: high level but some loss of momentum.
- Net: strong across the board. This is clearly above trend.
- Score: 65. "Vigorous" expansion, "very strong" consumer spending, "relatively rapid" business investment.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: The staff forecast mentions "the growth of potential was rising as a result of accelerating productivity" -- but this is embedded in participant discussion context. In the staff forecast paragraph: growth would moderate to potential. In participant discussion (which I should largely ignore): "continued acceleration [of productivity] over the past several quarters clearly had helped to contain cost pressures."
- The staff forecast implicitly references rising potential growth from productivity. The data review itself doesn't have a standalone productivity discussion, but the staff forecast paragraph says the economy's "estimated potential" is rising.
- This is thin evidence from staff specifically. Moderate confidence.
- Direction: Above trend productivity growth. Score: 62.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff reports: "increasing optimism about economic conditions... eased concerns about the creditworthiness of business borrowers" and "rates on private obligations registered mixed changes." Equity prices had "sizable gains." The participant section notes "marked improvement in overall financial market conditions since the fall of last year, including the ample availability of financing on relatively favorable terms" -- but this is participant language. The staff data review describes: Treasury yields rose, equity prices gained, private rates were mixed.
- The staff forecast mentions "anticipated waning of positive wealth effects" -- implying current wealth effects are boosting the economy, consistent with loose/accommodative conditions.
- Bright-line check: "ample availability of financing on relatively favorable terms" is participant language but the staff data is consistent with loose conditions (equity gains, mixed private rates despite Treasury rate increases). The overall picture is accommodative/supportive conditions.
- "Accommodative" / "supportive" --> MUST be < 50.
- Score: 35. Conditions are clearly accommodative -- equity markets up, credit available, recovery from fall 1998 stress.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: Staff reports "yields on Treasury securities rose appreciably on balance, with the largest increases occurring in intermediate- and longer-term maturities. The climb in rates reflected not only the strength of incoming data on the U.S. economy but also improved economic prospects in many foreign countries and higher world commodity prices."
- This is yield movement from fundamentals (strong economy, improved foreign prospects). NO mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, auction failures, or market stress.
- Per bright-line rule: rising yields from policy/fundamental expectations = normal (score 45-55). No actual market dysfunction described.
- Score: 45. Normal market functioning, yields moving on fundamentals.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Staff says "increasing optimism about economic conditions... eased concerns about the creditworthiness of business borrowers, especially firms of relatively low credit standing, and rates on private obligations registered mixed changes." This implies spreads were narrowing or stable-to-favorable. Credit conditions are clearly favorable.
- Spreads narrowing/easing = lower stress. Score should be below 50.
- Score: 35. Credit conditions favorable, spreads easing, optimism about creditworthiness.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Direction: Staff reports "most key measures of share prices in equity markets recorded sizable gains over the intermeeting period." The staff forecast references "positive wealth effects stemming from large increases in equity prices." No explicit staff valuation concern language.
- The staff reports equity gains factually without expressing valuation concern. Default to near neutral. However, this is 1999 -- the dot-com era -- and "sizable gains" suggest elevated valuations. But I must score what staff says, not what I know.
- Staff doesn't flag overvaluation concerns explicitly. They note the wealth effect factually.
- Score: 40. Equity prices rising with sizable gains; no explicit concern but the large increases and wealth effects imply some elevation without alarm.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**
Direction: No specific discussion of banking system health or stress in staff content. Credit is available, lending conditions seem favorable based on overall context, but there is no explicit banking system discussion.
- Score: N/A

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**
Direction: Staff reports "the trade-weighted value of the dollar in foreign exchange markets depreciated somewhat over the intermeeting period." The dollar declined against a broad group of trading partners, though it rose against euro and yen.
- Bright-line rule: Dollar depreciation --> MUST be < 50.
- "Depreciated somewhat" = modest weakness. Score: 40.
