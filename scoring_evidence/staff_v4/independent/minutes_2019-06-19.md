# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-06-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Mid-2019. Late-cycle expansion, unemployment at 50-year lows (3.6%), but inflation running persistently below the 2% target. Trade tensions with China and Mexico escalating. Markets pricing in rate cuts. GDP growth moderate but business investment softening.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.5% over 12 months ending April, core PCE at 1.6% — both clearly below the 2% target. Dallas trimmed mean at 2.0% provides one anchor near target, but the headline and core measures are well below. Staff notes low readings early in the year were "expected to be transitory" but projects both total and core to "continue to run below 2 percent." Inflation expectations "little changed" but Michigan reading "dropped significantly." This is clearly below-target inflation with some downside risk. Score range: 35-40. Anchors: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target — 1.5-1.6% readings push toward the lower end.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy operating ABOVE potential. Key evidence: "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." Unemployment "below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." "Labor market conditions judged to be tight." However, the projection was "revised down somewhat" and risks "tilted to the downside." The economy is above potential but modestly so and risks are to the downside. Bright-line check: "above potential" language present → score MUST be >50. Score: 53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as TIGHT, clearly above midpoint. Unemployment at 3.6% — "its lowest level in 50 years." Job openings at high level, quits "unchanged at a high level," initial claims "near historically low levels." Average hourly earnings up 3.1%. "Labor market conditions remained strong" and "judged to be tight." With unemployment at 3.6%, the rubric says score should be 65+ (UE below 3.8%). Wage growth moderate at 3.1% and total labor compensation actually slowed. Score: 65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees this as MIXED, near midpoint but slightly below. Consumer spending "appeared to pick up to a solid rate" in Q2 from weak Q1, with supportive fundamentals (low unemployment, income gains, elevated net worth). But business fixed investment "was soft" — equipment and IP spending "roughly flat," forward indicators "pointed to possible decreases," orders "declined notably," business sentiment "deteriorated further." Residential investment "continuing the decline." Net: solid consumption offset by weak business investment = slightly below trend. Score: 45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as somewhat loose / accommodative. Key phrase: "financing conditions for businesses and households generally remained supportive of economic growth." Nonfinancial business financing "continued to be accommodative overall." CRE financing "generally accommodative." Residential mortgage conditions "supportive." Consumer credit "generally supportive." However, spreads widened and equity prices declined. The overall characterization is "supportive" and "accommodative" — bright-line rule: "accommodative"/"supportive" → score MUST be <50. Score: 38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "fell considerably" but this was orderly market adjustment to trade tensions and policy expectations — NOT dysfunction. Short-term funding markets "remained stable." No mentions of liquidity problems, auction failures, or market dysfunction. The yield curve flattening (10y-3m spread fell to bottom decile since 1971) is a price signal, not dysfunction. Score: 40 (normal functioning with some yield volatility).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff notes corporate credit spreads "widened" and CMBS spreads "widened slightly" but CMBS spreads "remained near the low end of their post-crisis range." Consumer ABS spreads "little changed at low levels." Financing conditions "accommodative overall." Corporate bond credit quality "remained solid" with upgrades outpacing downgrades. The widening is from a low base. Level is still below normal / favorable. Score: 38.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Major indexes "declined, on net" over the intermeeting period. VIX increased. Firms with high China exposure posted "particularly large losses." But prices "regained a significant portion of their losses" later. No staff valuation commentary — just price movement description. Default to 50-55 per rubric. Score: 52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not specifically discussed as a standalone topic. Banks mentioned only in passing (bank stocks declined; bank lending slowed somewhat; CRE lending by banks expanded at slower rate). No discussion of capital adequacy, stress, or health of the banking system. N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index rose a bit over the intermeeting period." Staff outlook references "a higher projected path for the broad real dollar." Dollar appreciation → score MUST be >50. But "rose a bit" is modest. Score: 55.

### Inflation Sub-components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Not broken out specifically. Energy prices slowed considerably, food prices "well below core." These components had low inflation. Score: 30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not broken out separately. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Residential investment declining, home sales mixed/weak. But no specific shelter/rent inflation data. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey-based measures "little changed" but Michigan reading "dropped significantly to below its range in recent years." Dealer/market participant surveys little changed. Inflation compensation from TIPS "decreased notably." Mixed signals — anchored per professional surveys but softening per consumer surveys and market measures. Score: 42.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target. Score 37 = below midpoint. CORRECT.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "above potential." Score 53 > 50. CORRECT.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. UE at 3.6%, 50-year low. Score 65. CORRECT.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "accommodative"/"supportive." Score 38 < 50. CORRECT.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider. Evidence says widened but still near lows. Score 38 < 50. CORRECT.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar "rose a bit." Score 55 > 50. CORRECT.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction described. Score 40. CORRECT.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65)**: Gap of 12. Labor market tighter than output gap suggests, but this is consistent with the late-cycle pattern where unemployment is very low but GDP growth is moderate and risks are tilting down. Acceptable.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (53) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45)**: Gap of 8. Modest divergence — output slightly above potential driven by government spending and net exports earlier, while private domestic spending is mixed. Acceptable.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Aligned. Both indicate accommodative conditions. COHERENT.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (38) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Both in the benign range. COHERENT.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (37) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods ~30, expectations ~42. Parent at 37 is consistent. COHERENT.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), was below 2 percent in April" at 1.5%; "core PCE price inflation...moved down to 1.6 percent"; "Both total and core inflation were projected to...continue to run below 2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff reports inflation persistently below the 2% target with the forecast calling for it to remain below target through the projection horizon.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because PCE is 1.5% and core is 1.6%, both well under the 2% target.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 53 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020"; "The unemployment rate was projected to...remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees the economy operating modestly above potential with growth slightly above trend, though the projection was revised down and risks tilted to the downside.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says GDP growth is "above potential" and unemployment is "below natural rate." Bright-line satisfied (>50).

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate declined to 3.6 percent in April and remained there in May, its lowest level in 50 years"; "the rate of quits was unchanged at a high level"; "With labor market conditions judged to be tight."
**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is very tight by historical standards — 3.6% unemployment (50-year low), high quits, low initial claims — though wage growth is moderate and total compensation growth actually slowed.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is well above neutral because 3.6% unemployment is deeply below the natural rate with multiple indicators confirming tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "household spending growth picked up from the weak first quarter while business fixed investment was soft"; "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property looked to be roughly flat"; "Forward-looking indicators of business equipment spending pointed to possible decreases in the near term."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending rebounded to solid growth, but business fixed investment was soft and forward-looking indicators deteriorating. Residential investment still declining. Net picture is slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral due to weakness in business investment and residential investment partially offsetting solid consumer spending.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "financing conditions for businesses and households generally remained supportive of economic growth"; "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative overall"; mortgage rates decreased 40 bps; consumer credit "generally supportive."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff characterizes financial conditions across all sectors as accommodative and supportive despite intermeeting spread widening and equity declines.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly uses "accommodative" and "supportive." Bright-line satisfied (<50).

### TREASURY_STRESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Nominal Treasury yields posted notable declines"; "Conditions in short-term funding markets remained stable over the intermeeting period."
**Center-of-gravity**: Yields moved significantly but in an orderly fashion driven by policy expectations and trade concerns. No dysfunction described. Short-term funding markets explicitly stable.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral because there is no evidence of market dysfunction — only orderly price adjustment.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "corporate bond spreads widened" but "CMBS spreads widened slightly over the intermeeting period but remained near the low end of their post-crisis range"; consumer ABS "spreads that were little changed at low levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads widened modestly over the period but the LEVEL remains near post-crisis lows and well below historical averages.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because the level of spreads remains historically low/favorable despite recent widening.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Major U.S. equity price indexes declined, on net"; "VIX—increased over the period"; "stock prices regained a significant portion of their losses."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities declined modestly on net with some volatility, but no staff valuation commentary. Prices partially recovered. Near-neutral with mild stress.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is roughly at neutral — prices declined but partially recovered; no valuation assessment provided by staff.

### BANKING_STRESS: N/A
Banking system health/stress not specifically discussed.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 55 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The broad dollar index rose a bit over the intermeeting period"; staff outlook included "a higher projected path for the broad real dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar appreciated modestly and staff projects a higher path going forward, but the movement was small.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar strengthened. Bright-line satisfied (>50).

### INFLATION_GOODS: 30 ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "consumer energy price inflation slowed considerably"; "consumer food price inflation remained well below core inflation."
**Center-of-gravity**: Both food and energy prices showed notably low inflation, pulling overall goods inflation well below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral due to very low food and energy price inflation.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Shelter/rent inflation not specifically discussed.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Recent survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance" but "the preliminary June reading from the University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers dropped significantly to below its range in recent years"; "Measures of inflation compensation derived from Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities also decreased notably."
**Center-of-gravity**: Professional surveys anchored but both consumer survey and market-based measures showed notable declines, suggesting some downside drift in expectations.
**Direction confirmation**: Below neutral because market-based compensation and consumer expectations fell notably, even though professional surveys held steady.
