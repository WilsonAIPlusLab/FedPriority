# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — March 25, 1997

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff review content is intermixed with participant/member discussion and policy action language. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable content (phrases like "the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," data descriptions without member attribution) from participant content ("members referred to," "members observed," "they noted," "a few members argued").

### Staff-Attributable Content Identification
The following sections are staff-attributable:
- Paragraphs beginning with "The information reviewed at this meeting..." through the trade/international data (lines 44-152) — these describe the staff's review of incoming data.
- "The staff forecast prepared for this meeting..." (lines 215-231) — explicit staff forecast.
- The domestic policy directive summary at the end (lines 468-530) — reiterates staff data review.
- Market/financial developments paragraphs (lines 172-212) — factual descriptions of market conditions.

Participant content to IGNORE for scoring: Lines 234-458 ("In the Committee's discussion...", "members referred to...", "they noted...", "a few members argued...").

### Era Context
March 1997: Mid-to-late 1990s expansion. The economy is growing robustly, unemployment is moderate (5.3%), inflation is subdued. This is the period leading into the late-90s boom. The Fed is about to tighten slightly (25bp hike at this meeting). Dollar is appreciating. Stock market is strong.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
- Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as BELOW the midpoint but with upward risks.
- Staff evidence: "The underlying trend in consumer price inflation had remained subdued" (line 54-55); "consumer prices excluding food and energy rose somewhat less than they had over the preceding twelve months" (lines 136-137); "Underlying price inflation has remained subdued" (line 485-486). However: "the increase in average hourly earnings had continued to edge higher" (lines 55-56); "Average hourly earnings...were up appreciably more over the twelve months ending in February than over the preceding twelve months" (lines 148-152); staff forecast: "core consumer price inflation was forecast to increase slightly over the year ahead" (lines 230-231).
- The staff presents current inflation as subdued/contained but with rising wages and a forecast of slightly higher core inflation. This is below-target but with early upward signals.
- Score direction: Below 50 but not far below. "Subdued" = contained/modest = 26-40 range. But wage acceleration and forecast of slight increase pushes toward upper end. Score: ~40.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff says inflation is subdued but wages accelerating and forecast to rise slightly. 40 = below target, consistent with "contained/modest" with some upward pressure. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
- Direction: Staff sees the economy as operating ABOVE potential.
- Staff evidence: "the economy had continued to expand at a relatively robust pace" (line 45); "strengthening markedly in the fourth quarter of 1996" (line 46); staff forecast: "the expansion in economic activity would slow in coming quarters to a pace somewhat above that of the economy's estimated potential" (lines 216-217); "With resource utilization high" (line 229); manufacturing capacity utilization "slightly above its long-term average" (line 79).
- The staff explicitly says growth is "somewhat above that of the economy's estimated potential" and "resource utilization high."
- BRIGHT-LINE: "above potential" language requires score > 50. Score: 57.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Staff says above potential. 57 > 50. Matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
- Direction: Staff sees labor markets as TIGHT.
- Staff evidence: Unemployment at 5.3% (line 69), "unchanged from its average level in the second half of 1996"; "Private nonfarm payroll employment rose substantially further" (line 59); "aggregate hours...were up appreciably" (line 66); "the average workweek increased considerably, reaching a new recent high in February" (line 67-68).
- UE at 5.3% in 1997. NAIRU was estimated around 5.5-6% in this era. So 5.3% is modestly below NAIRU. Staff notes employment gains are large and sustained, workweek at new highs.
- Per the rubric: UE 4.5-5% = 46-52. But 5.3% is slightly above that band. However, the staff describes very strong job gains, substantial payroll increases, and high resource utilization. The labor market is clearly tight for the era. Score: 55.
- Anchor check: 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75. 1997 is not quite as tight. 55 is appropriate.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Staff shows strong employment, below-NAIRU-estimate unemployment. 55 > 50. Matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**

- Staff evidence: "Consumer spending strengthened considerably further in early 1997 after registering a sizable increase over the fourth quarter" (lines 82-83); "Nominal retail sales rose sharply in January and February" (line 84); business investment: "sizable further rise in outlays for producers' durable equipment" (lines 99-100); "continued rapid growth in purchases of computers" (line 101-102). Housing mixed. Staff forecast: consumer spending expected to remain "fairly brisk" though slowing (line 220).
- Consumer spending is very strong ("considerably further," "sharply"), business investment robust. Net picture is clearly above trend.
- Score: 63.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Staff says very strong. 63 > 50. Matches.
