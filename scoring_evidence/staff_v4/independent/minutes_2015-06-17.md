# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring: 2015-06-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: Mid-2015. The U.S. economy was in its sixth year of recovery from the GFC. The Fed was approaching its first rate hike (which would come in December 2015). Unemployment had fallen substantially but inflation remained persistently below 2%. The dollar had appreciated notably in 2014-15H1. Oil prices had fallen sharply. Greece was a concern internationally.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional signals:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW midpoint. "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent." PCE edged up only slightly over 12 months; core PCE was 1.25%. Restrained by energy and import prices. Forecast: "inflation would continue to be below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over 2016 and 2017." This is clearly below target. Score should be in the 26-40 range ("below target; disinflation; contained, modest, running below 2 percent"). Core at 1.25% is well below 2%. Score ~32.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy BELOW potential. GDP "increasing moderately" in Q2 after "edging down" in Q1. Staff projects "real GDP would expand at a faster pace than potential output in 2016 and 2017" -- meaning the economy is currently below potential and closing the gap. "The expansion in economic output over the medium term was anticipated to trim resource slack." Resource slack exists. Bright-line: "slack remains" language present -> score MUST be < 50. Anchors: 2010-14 recovery is 35-42; this is mid-2015 with moderate growth, UE at 5.5%. Score ~43 (approaching potential but still below).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: UE at 5.5% in May. Payrolls expanding at a faster pace in April/May. Participation and employment-to-population ratio rose a bit. Job openings up. But staff expects UE to "decline gradually to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate" -- meaning not yet at NAIRU. UE 5.5% falls in the 31-45 range per the rubric (UE 5.5-7%). However, conditions are improving and the labor market is getting closer to full employment. Score ~43.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "appeared to pick up early in the second quarter from its modest pace in the previous quarter." Vehicle sales "much higher in May." Consumer sentiment near highest level since pre-recession. Housing "improved somewhat" but "continued to be slow." Business equipment spending "appeared to remain relatively slow." Industrial production "decreased during April and May." Drilling investment declining. Net: consumer spending improving modestly, but business investment weak and industrial production declining. Score ~43 (below trend but improving on the consumer side).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff says "Financing conditions for large nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative." "Gross corporate bond issuance remained quite strong." Leveraged loan issuance "picked up significantly." C&I loans increasing "at a solid pace." Small business financing "continued to improve." CRE financing "remained broadly available." Consumer credit "stayed accommodative." Mortgage credit "continued to improve gradually" though "credit remained tight for borrowers with lower credit scores." This is clearly accommodative/loose. Bright-line: "accommodative" -> score MUST be < 50. Score ~30 (loose; "accommodative conditions," "credit freely available").

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields rose 15-35 bps over the period, driven by euro-area yield spillover and employment data. "Various measures typically used to assess liquidity in Treasury and MBS markets were little changed... they have generally pointed to relatively stable market functioning over the past several years." However, respondents to the June survey indicated that "over the past five years, liquidity and functioning in these markets, especially in Treasury markets, have deteriorated." This is a mixed signal -- current liquidity measures stable but a longer-term structural concern. The yield movements are normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. Score ~42 (normal functioning with a note of structural concern but no acute stress).

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened somewhat, on net, while spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds narrowed slightly." Mixed but small movements. Corporate issuance strong. Financing accommodative. Bright-line: conditions are clearly on the favorable side. Score ~38 (somewhat below normal; generally favorable).

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Most broad U.S. equity price indexes moved down a bit, on net." Option-implied volatility "remained near the lower end of its historical range." No valuation commentary from staff. Default to 48-52 per rubric. Score ~50.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not explicitly discussed as a separate topic. Lending conditions described positively (C&I loans growing, CRE broadly available). No mention of bank health, capital, NPLs, or stress. The overall financing picture suggests low stress, but the dimension isn't directly addressed. Score ~32 (implied healthy from lending data, but limited direct evidence). Actually, given that banking is not *specifically* discussed as a banking system health topic, this could be N/A. However, there is indirect evidence from lending conditions. I'll score it with Low confidence.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar increased a bit, on balance, during the intermeeting period." Also, earlier: "continuing effects of earlier increases in the foreign exchange value of the dollar" weighing on manufacturing and imports. The dollar had appreciated notably in prior quarters and continued to rise modestly. Bright-line: dollar appreciation -> score > 50. Also the earlier large appreciation is referenced as still affecting the economy. Score ~58 (modest current strength plus ongoing effects of prior appreciation).

### Inflation sub-components:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices declined sharply earlier; non-energy import prices declining. Industrial production fell. "Held down primarily by earlier large declines in energy prices" and "declines in the prices of non-energy imports." This is low goods inflation. Score ~22.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing activity discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Survey measures of longer-run inflation expectations remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation were still low, although somewhat higher than early in the year." Surveys anchored; market-based low. Staff forecast assumes expectations consistent with 2% in the longer run. Score ~43 (anchored but market measures still low, suggesting slightly below-target anchoring).

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (43) ~ LABOR_TIGHTNESS (43)**: Aligned. Both below 50, consistent with an economy still below potential with remaining labor slack.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (43) ~ CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (43)**: Aligned. Both below 50, consistent with moderate but below-trend activity.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ~ CREDIT_SPREADS (38)**: Directionally consistent -- both below 50, indicating loose/favorable conditions. FC is lower because of the strongly accommodative language.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (30) ~ TREASURY_STRESS (42)**: Consistent -- loose financial conditions with normal Treasury functioning.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) ~ sub-dimensions**: INFLATION_GOODS (22) and INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS (43) average roughly ~32, consistent with the parent score.

### Bright-line verification:
- OUTPUT_GAP: "resource slack" -> < 50. Score 43. PASS.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "accommodative" -> < 50. Score 30. PASS.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Favorable conditions -> < 50. Score 38. PASS.
- BANKING_STRESS: Implied healthy from lending -> < 50. Score 32. PASS.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "increased a bit" -> > 50. Score 58. PASS.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Yield moves from policy expectations = normal. Score 42. No dysfunction signals. PASS.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent and was restrained significantly by earlier declines in energy prices and decreases in prices of non-energy imports." Core PCE at "1-1/4 percent over the same 12-month period." Staff projected "inflation would continue to be below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent over 2016 and 2017."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation well below target at 1.25% core, with transitory drags from energy and imports but expected to remain below 2% through 2017.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running well below the 2% target with no near-term return expected.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP was increasing moderately in the second quarter after edging down in the first quarter." "The expansion in economic output over the medium term was anticipated to trim resource slack; the unemployment rate was expected to decline gradually to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy growing moderately but still below potential, with resource slack remaining. Staff forecasts closing the gap over the medium term.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because staff explicitly references remaining resource slack and projects GDP expanding faster than potential to close the gap. Bright-line satisfied.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate was 5.5 percent in May, about the same as its first-quarter average." "Total nonfarm payroll employment expanded at a faster pace in April and May than in the first quarter." Staff expects UE to "decline gradually to the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving with solid job gains but unemployment still above the natural rate, indicating remaining slack.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because UE at 5.5% is above NAIRU and staff describes the economy as not yet at full employment.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth in real PCE appeared to pick up early in the second quarter from its modest pace in the previous quarter." But "Growth in real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property products appeared to remain relatively slow" and "Industrial production decreased during April and May after declining in the first quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending rebounding modestly from weak Q1 but business investment sluggish and industrial production declining, netting to a below-trend overall assessment.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is below neutral because while consumer spending is picking up, it comes from a weak base and is offset by weak business investment and declining industrial production.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
**Evidence**: No explicit discussion of productivity trends.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for large nonfinancial businesses continued to be accommodative. Gross corporate bond issuance remained quite strong, and institutional leveraged loan issuance picked up significantly." "Financing conditions in consumer credit markets stayed accommodative." "Auto and student loans expanded at a robust pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broadly accommodative financial conditions across corporate, CRE, and consumer credit markets, with strong issuance and solid loan growth.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff repeatedly uses "accommodative" to describe financing conditions across multiple sectors. Bright-line satisfied.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Various measures typically used to assess liquidity in Treasury and MBS markets were little changed over the intermeeting period; they have generally pointed to relatively stable market functioning over the past several years." However, survey respondents indicated "liquidity and functioning in these markets, especially in Treasury markets, have deteriorated" over five years due to dealer balance sheet constraints.
**Center-of-gravity**: Current Treasury market functioning is stable and orderly despite notable yield increases. A structural concern about longer-term liquidity deterioration is noted but not acute.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is slightly below neutral because current measures show stable functioning, though the structural concern prevents a very low score. Yield increases were driven by normal market factors (euro-area spillover, data), not dysfunction.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads on 10-year triple-B-rated corporate bonds over comparable-maturity Treasury securities widened somewhat, on net, while spreads on speculative-grade corporate bonds narrowed slightly." Issuance strong and financing accommodative.
**Center-of-gravity**: Credit spreads mixed with slight IG widening and slight HY narrowing, but overall conditions remain favorable given strong issuance and accommodative financing.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because overall credit conditions are accommodative with strong issuance, and spread movements are small and mixed.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 50 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Most broad U.S. equity price indexes moved down a bit, on net, amid mixed macroeconomic news." "Option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index... remained near the lower end of its historical range."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities declined modestly with no staff valuation commentary. Low volatility suggests no alarm. Default to neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is at neutral because staff describes only minor price movements with no valuation assessment.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: 32 +/-10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' balance sheets continued to increase at a solid pace." "Financing for commercial real estate remained broadly available." No direct discussion of bank health, capital, or stress.
**Center-of-gravity**: Banking system implied to be healthy from strong lending activity, but no direct assessment of bank conditions.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because lending activity is solid and financing is broadly available, implying a healthy banking system.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 58 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The foreign exchange value of the dollar increased a bit, on balance, during the intermeeting period against the currencies of major U.S. trading partners." Earlier: manufacturing output decline "likely reflecting the continuing effects of earlier increases in the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar modestly appreciated over the intermeeting period and the lingering effects of prior large appreciation continue to weigh on manufacturing and trade.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated, and prior appreciation is still affecting the economy. Bright-line satisfied.
