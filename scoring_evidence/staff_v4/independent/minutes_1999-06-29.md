# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — June 29, 1999 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is an **unsegmented pre-2005 minutes** document. Staff and participant content are intermingled. I must carefully distinguish staff-attributable language ("the information reviewed," "the staff forecast," staff-sourced data descriptions) from participant/member language ("members commented," "in the Committee's discussion," "members noted").

### Era Context
Mid-1999: The U.S. is in the late-1990s expansion/boom. Unemployment is near 30-year lows (~4.2%), GDP growth is vigorous, and the dot-com equity boom is underway. The Fed is beginning to tighten after easing in fall 1998. Productivity growth is accelerating. Inflation remains relatively contained despite tight labor markets, thanks to productivity gains and falling import prices.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Staff sees inflation pressure as slightly below to near midpoint but with emerging upside risks.
Evidence: "recent wage and price increases had been a little larger on balance; nonetheless, longer-term inflation trends continued generally favorable in an environment of robust improvements in productivity" (staff review). Core CPI rose "slightly less" in the twelve months ended May than in the prior twelve-month period. Core PPI was "up noticeably" but due to tobacco. Staff forecast: "Price inflation was projected to rise somewhat over the projection horizon." The language is mixed — currently contained but projected to rise "somewhat."
Score reasoning: Current inflation is contained/favorable but there are upside pressures (energy, wages tilting up, staff projecting a "somewhat" rise). This is modestly above the neutral/at-target zone. Late 1990s inflation was running around 2% CPI. Score: 52 +/-5.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Staff sees modest pressure building. 52 > 50. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Staff sees economy operating above potential.
Evidence: "economic activity continued to expand vigorously" (staff review). Staff forecast says the economy needs to "moderate to a rate commensurate with the growth of the economy's estimated potential," implying it is currently above that rate. The staff expects "a better balance between aggregate demand and aggregate supply" to be achieved via higher rates — implying current imbalance (demand > supply). Capacity utilization "edged higher" but "continued to be below its long-run average level" — this is a mixed signal.
Bright-line check: The staff language implies growth is above potential/sustainable rate, which means output gap > 50. Capacity utilization below average pulls slightly toward neutral, but the overall thrust is clearly above-potential growth.
Score: 57 +/-5. Era anchor: late 90s boom = 57-63.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Staff sees above-potential growth. 57 > 50. Matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Staff sees labor market as very tight.
Evidence: "Labor markets remained very tight" (staff review). Unemployment at 4.2%, "matching its low for the year and for the period since 1970." Builders "faced with shortages of workers." Payroll growth remained "robust" though slightly below Q1 pace. Average hourly earnings growing.
Score reasoning: Unemployment at 4.2% is well below NAIRU estimates of the era. Explicit "very tight" language. "Shortages of workers" noted. This is clearly in the 65-75 range per the anchor for 1999-2000. Score: 70 +/-5.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Very tight" + 4.2% UE + shortages = high score. 70 > 50. Matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Staff sees spending as well above trend, though moderating from extraordinary levels.
Evidence: Consumer spending "slowed somewhat from its extraordinary pace" but "underlying trend in consumption remained strongly upward." "Total retail sales rose substantially in May." Business investment in durable equipment "picked up substantially in the second quarter from the already brisk pace of the first quarter." Housing demand "remained robust." Nonresidential construction "weakened."
Score reasoning: The net picture is very strong — consumer spending robust (if slightly moderating from extraordinary), business equipment investment accelerating, housing robust. Only nonresidential construction is weak. This is clearly above trend. Score: 65 +/-5.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says strong. 65 > 50. Matches.

**5. PRODUCTIVITY**
Direction: Staff sees productivity growth as notably strong and above trend.
Evidence: "robust improvements in productivity" (staff review). Staff forecast mentions "rising productivity" offsetting labor cost pressures. "Improving productivity has held down increases in unit labor costs" (directive). Staff projects productivity gains to continue but not fully offset compensation increases.
Score: 65 +/-5. This is clearly above trend — "robust improvements" / "accelerated."
Polarity check: Higher = more productivity growth. "Robust improvements" = high score. Matches.

**6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**
Direction: Staff sees conditions as somewhat accommodative/loose, though tightening slightly.
Evidence: "Interest rates have risen somewhat since the meeting on May 18, 1999." Market rates had risen in expectation of tightening. M2/M3 growth had been rapid. The staff forecast notes "the rise that had already occurred in market interest rates, especially for intermediate and longer maturities" as a restraining factor. The policy action reversed part of 1998 easing, and one dissenter called policy "already sufficiently restrictive." But overall, with the funds rate at 4.75% (about to go to 5%) and vigorous growth continuing, conditions appear broadly accommodative to neutral.
Bright-line check: The overall tone is that conditions are still supportive of growth (credit freely available, strong equity markets, rapid money growth). This suggests < 50.
Score: 40 +/-8. Conditions are somewhat accommodative but rates have risen.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions still accommodative overall → below 50. 40 < 50. Matches.

**7. TREASURY_STRESS**
Direction: No mention of Treasury market dysfunction.
Evidence: Interest rates rose "somewhat" — this is normal price adjustment, not dysfunction. No mention of liquidity problems, auction issues, or market stress in Treasuries.
Score: N/A.

**8. CREDIT_SPREADS**
Direction: Not explicitly discussed in staff-attributable content.

Score: N/A. There is insufficient staff-specific evidence on credit spreads to score.
Actually, reconsidering: The staff review mentions "total domestic nonfinancial debt has continued to expand at a pace somewhat above the middle of its range" — implying ample credit availability. Money growth is rapid. This is indirect evidence of favorable credit conditions. But it's very thin.
Score: N/A — the evidence is too indirect for a confident score.

**9. EQUITY_VALUATION**
Direction: Staff mentions equity prices but with minimal valuation commentary.
Evidence: "broad indexes of share prices in equity markets changed little on balance over the intermeeting period" (staff). Staff forecast references "waning of positive wealth effects associated with earlier large increases in equity prices." No explicit valuation concern language from staff (the "irrational exuberance" concern was Greenspan's personal view, not staff assessment here).
Score: The staff treats equity prices as a factor in the forecast (wealth effects) but does not express valuation concern. Default to 45-55 range per the gotcha rule. Score: 45 +/-10. The mention of "large increases" and wealth effects without alarm suggests modest awareness but no explicit concern.
Polarity check: Higher = more stress/concern. Staff is not expressing particular concern. 45 < 50. Reasonable.

**10. BANKING_STRESS**

**11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH**

"The lagged effects of the earlier rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar were expected to place continuing, though diminishing, restraint on the demand for U.S. exports" (staff forecast). The dollar had been strong (weighing on exports) but was now "edging up" rather than surging.
Bright-line check: Dollar appreciation → score > 50.

Polarity check: Higher = more dollar strength. Dollar appreciated. 57 > 50. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "recent wage and price increases had been a little larger on balance; nonetheless, longer-term inflation trends continued generally favorable in an environment of robust improvements in productivity." Staff forecast: "Price inflation was projected to rise somewhat over the projection horizon."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Current inflation is contained and favorable, but staff projects modest upward pressure from import prices and labor compensation, placing this just barely above neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: Inflation pressure is at/slightly above neutral because current data is benign but staff projects a "somewhat" rise.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "economic activity continued to expand vigorously, though at a somewhat slower pace than earlier in the year." Staff forecast: "the expansion would gradually moderate to a rate commensurate with the growth of the economy's estimated potential" — implying current growth is above potential.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding above its estimated potential, though capacity utilization remains below its long-run average, tempering the score.
**Direction confirmation**: Output gap is above neutral because staff explicitly states growth needs to moderate to reach potential, implying it is currently above. Bright-line satisfied (>50).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor markets remained very tight." "The civilian unemployment rate edged down in May to 4.2 percent, matching its low for the year and for the period since 1970." "Builders were faced with shortages of workers."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are very tight by any measure — unemployment at a 29-year low, explicit worker shortages reported, and robust payroll growth continuing.
**Direction confirmation**: Labor tightness is well above neutral because of "very tight" language, 4.2% unemployment (lowest since 1970), and worker shortages. Consistent with 1999-2000 anchor of 65-75.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "underlying trend in consumption remained strongly upward, with household income and wealth continuing to expand rapidly." "Business investment in durable equipment picked up substantially in the second quarter from the already brisk pace of the first quarter."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business equipment investment are running well above trend, with only nonresidential construction showing weakness.
**Direction confirmation**: Consumption/investment is above neutral because spending is described as "vigorous," "robust," and "substantial" across most categories.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 65 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "robust improvements in productivity." "Improving productivity has held down increases in unit labor costs despite very tight labor markets." Staff forecast references "rising productivity."
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Staff sees productivity growth as notably strong and above historical trend, playing a key role in containing inflation despite tight labor markets.
**Direction confirmation**: Productivity is above neutral because staff describes "robust improvements" and acceleration.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Interest rates have risen somewhat since the meeting on May 18, 1999." M2 and M3 growth had been rapid. Equity markets roughly flat. Staff forecast references rate rises as a restraining factor, but conditions overall remain supportive of vigorous economic activity.
**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions remain somewhat accommodative — money growth is rapid, equity wealth effects persist, and interest rates, while rising, have not significantly restrained the expansion.
**Direction confirmation**: Financial conditions are below neutral (looser) because the economy continues to expand vigorously despite rate increases, and money growth remains rapid. Bright-line: conditions appear accommodative → score < 50. Satisfied.
