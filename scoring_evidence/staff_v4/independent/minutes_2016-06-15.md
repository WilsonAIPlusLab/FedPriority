# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2016-06-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views need to be filtered out.

### Era Context
June 2016: The U.S. economy is in a mature expansion phase, roughly 7 years after the GFC trough. Unemployment has fallen substantially but growth remains moderate. Inflation has persistently undershot the 2% target. The Brexit referendum is imminent. The Fed had just begun its tightening cycle with one hike in December 2015. Market expectations for further hikes are very low.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. Evidence: PCE inflation "about 1 percent" over 12 months, core PCE "a little above 1-1/2 percent," restrained by energy prices and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation "declined from levels that were already low." Michigan survey measure of longer-run expectations "fell to its lowest level on record." Staff projects inflation "slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018." This is clearly below target — consistent with 30-38 range. The language is "contained" / "running below" / "restrained" without being deflationary. Score: 35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees the economy as slightly below to near potential but closing. Evidence: "real GDP appeared to be rising faster than in the first quarter" and staff "continued to forecast that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." The unemployment rate is "projected to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." This means the economy is approaching and expected to slightly exceed potential. But currently, risks are "tilted to the downside." The language suggests the economy is near potential but not yet above it — approaching from below. The GDP growth pickup is from a weak Q1. Business investment is "relatively flat" after "declining sharply." Given UE at 4.7% expected to run "somewhat below" natural rate, the economy is near potential. Score: 48. Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for current conditions. The forecast says GDP will expand "modestly faster" than potential, implying the gap is closing. Score of 48 (near potential, slightly below) is consistent.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as near balanced but with mixed signals. UE at 4.7% suggests near NAIRU or slightly below. However, "pace of improvement in labor market conditions slowed," "payroll employment gains slowed," participation rate and E/P ratio "moved down," part-time for economic reasons "rose noticeably." The rate of hires "declined" and quits "unchanged." Wage growth is moderate (ECI 1-3/4%, AHE 2-1/2%). UE at 4.7% with projected to go "somewhat below" natural rate suggests at/near full employment. But the slowing in job gains and other mixed indicators temper this. Score: 50. The UE rate anchors us near NAIRU but the deceleration in hiring and mixed indicators keep it at neutral. Per the rubric anchor for 2016-17: 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees a mixed picture. Consumer spending is picking up: PCE "appeared to be picking up," retail sales "rose at a solid pace," income gains "solid," consumer sentiment "remained upbeat." Housing showing "continued gradual recovery." BUT business investment is weak: equipment spending "relatively flat" after "declining sharply," nonresidential structures "little changed," drilling still contracting. Industrial production is mixed — rose in April but "likely declined in May." Net: solid consumption offset by weak investment = slightly below trend. Score: 45.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Explicitly discussed: "Labor productivity growth remained slow over the four quarters ending in the first quarter of 2016." This is clearly below trend. Score: 30.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff explicitly states "Domestic financial market conditions remained accommodative." Credit "remained broadly available to households." Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms "improved a bit, remaining accommodative." Consumer credit "generally remained accommodative." CRE conditions "fairly accommodative." Bright-line: "accommodative" must be < 50. Score: 32. This is clearly loose/accommodative with broad evidence across multiple credit categories.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields declined, yield curve flattened. But this is described as normal market functioning driven by policy expectations, employment data, and Brexit uncertainty. Heavy foreign demand is noted. No mention of liquidity problems, settlement failures, or market dysfunction. The yield movements are attributed to fundamentals. Score: 40 (normal functioning). Bright-line: no dysfunction language, so below 55.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Mixed signals. Investment-grade spreads "little changed." High-yield spreads "widened, mainly for firms outside of the energy sector." CMBS spreads "stayed elevated." Some credit quality deterioration: C&I delinquencies increasing, default rate moved up, downgrades outpaced upgrades, expected default rates "moderately elevated." But overall bond issuance remained strong, C&I loan growth "remained strong." Score: 48. Spreads are roughly normal with pockets of stress but overall favorable issuance conditions.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Stock prices "modestly lower, on net." VIX "rose notably from fairly low levels" to "close to its historical median level." No valuation commentary from staff — just price movements. Per rubric: price moves without valuation commentary default to 45-55. Modestly lower suggests slight concern. Score: 52.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not explicitly discussed. C&I loan growth "remained strong, particularly at large banks." CRE loans "increased briskly." Consumer loans "continued to increase at a robust pace." This implies healthy banking activity, but no direct commentary on bank health/capital/stress. The credit quality deterioration (delinquencies, charge-offs) is noted but framed at the borrower level, not bank solvency. Score: N/A — banking system health not directly discussed as a topic.

Wait — the lending activity and credit quality discussion does imply something about banking conditions. The strong loan growth plus rising delinquencies suggest a mixed picture. But the rubric says N/A if banking system not specifically discussed. The text discusses credit markets broadly, not banking system health per se. I'll score N/A.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The exchange value of the U.S. dollar rose in the middle of the intermeeting period," finishing "a bit stronger against the currencies of the AFEs and about 3 percent higher against EME currencies." But it depreciated against the yen. The staff outlook mentions "higher projected paths for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as a restraining factor on GDP. Net: modestly stronger. Bright-line: appreciation = > 50. Score: 55.

### Inflation Sub-Components

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Consumer energy prices declined earlier; non-energy import prices decreased. Goods prices appear restrained. Score: 30.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed from goods. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing mentioned in activity context (starts, sales) but not in inflation/price pressure context specifically. Home values had "further strong increases" but this is in the wealth/consumption context. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Explicitly discussed and concerning: Michigan survey "fell to its lowest level on record." Market-based measures "declined from levels that were already low." Other survey measures "generally little changed." Staff sees risks to inflation "weighted to the downside, reflecting the possibility that longer-term inflation expectations may have edged down." This is below anchor — expectations drifting down from target. Score: 38.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

1. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS (50)**: Within 2 points. Coherent — both near neutral.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (48) vs CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (45)**: Within 3 points. Coherent — both slightly below neutral reflecting mixed conditions.
3. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs CREDIT_SPREADS (48)**: Gap of 16. Financial conditions are clearly accommodative (32) while credit spreads are near normal (48). This makes sense — financial conditions can be accommodative through easy monetary policy while credit spreads reflect some sector-specific concerns (energy, high-yield). The overall FC score captures the broad "accommodative" assessment while spreads capture the pockets of widening.
4. **FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS (32) vs TREASURY_STRESS (40)**: Coherent — not a crisis, accommodative FC with normal Treasury functioning.
5. **INFLATION_PRESSURE (35) vs sub-dimensions**: Goods (30) and expectations (38) average ~34, consistent with overall 35.

### Polarity Verification
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. "Accommodative" = loose = low score. 32 is correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = more stress/wider. Mixed signals, near normal. 48 is correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Dollar appreciated modestly. 55 is correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. No dysfunction. 40 is correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent, restrained in part by earlier decreases in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." "Core PCE price inflation...was a little above 1-1/2 percent." "Inflation was still projected to be slightly below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent in 2018."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation clearly running below target with PCE at ~1% and core at ~1.5%, restrained by multiple factors, with slow projected return to target.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is running well below the 2% target with downside risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real GDP appeared to be rising faster than in the first quarter." "The staff continued to forecast that real GDP would expand at a modestly faster pace than potential output in 2016 through 2018." "The unemployment rate was projected to run somewhat below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is near potential and approaching it from below, with GDP projected to grow modestly above potential going forward. Currently still closing the gap.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is at/slightly below neutral because the economy is near potential but risks are tilted to the downside and the gap is still closing.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The unemployment rate dropped to 4.7 percent in May." "The pace of improvement in labor market conditions slowed in April and May." "The share of workers employed part time for economic reasons rose noticeably in May." Wage measures moderate: ECI "rose 1-3/4 percent," AHE "increased 2-1/2 percent."
**Center-of-gravity**: UE at 4.7% signals near full employment, but slowing payroll gains, declining participation, rising involuntary part-time work, and moderate wage growth suggest balanced conditions rather than tightness.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is at neutral — near NAIRU but without clear signs of tightness (moderate wages, slowing hiring).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Growth in real personal consumption expenditures appeared to be picking up in the second quarter" with "solid pace" retail sales. But "Real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to be relatively flat early in the second quarter after declining sharply in the previous quarter." Mining output "continued to contract."
**Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending is partially offset by flat-to-weak business investment and contracting mining, netting to slightly below trend.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the strong consumption/weak investment mix nets to modestly below trend.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Labor productivity growth remained slow over the four quarters ending in the first quarter of 2016."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff explicitly characterizes productivity growth as slow, which is clearly below historical trend.
**Direction confirmation**: PRODUCTIVITY is below neutral because "slow" productivity growth is below the historical trend.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Domestic financial market conditions remained accommodative over the intermeeting period." "Overall financing conditions for nonfinancial firms improved a bit over the intermeeting period, remaining accommodative." "Credit also remained broadly available to households." "Conditions in consumer credit markets were little changed and generally remained accommodative."
**Center-of-gravity**: Staff repeatedly and explicitly describes financial conditions as accommodative across corporate, consumer, and CRE markets.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral (loose) because staff uses "accommodative" repeatedly. Bright-line satisfied: accommodative < 50.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The nominal Treasury yield curve flattened, on net, over the intermeeting period, mainly reflecting declines in longer-term rates." Yields declined due to employment data, global risk sentiment, and Brexit concerns — all fundamental drivers. No mention of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or auction issues.
**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury market functioning appears normal with yield movements driven by fundamental factors (data, policy expectations, foreign demand).
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is at/below neutral — yield movements reflect normal market adjustment, not dysfunction. Bright-line satisfied: no dysfunction language, score < 55.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Spreads of 10-year triple-B-rated corporate bond yields...were little changed on balance. High-yield spreads widened, mainly for firms outside of the energy sector." "The percentage of C&I loans entering delinquency or being charged off increased further." "Expected year-ahead default rates for nonfinancial firms remained moderately elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Investment-grade spreads stable, high-yield modestly wider, some credit quality deterioration — roughly normal overall with mild pockets of stress.
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is near neutral — mixed signals with stable IG spreads but some HY widening and credit quality concerns.

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION: 52 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**: "Broad stock price indexes...were modestly lower, on net." "One-month-ahead option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index—the VIX—rose notably from fairly low levels and ended the period close to its historical median level."
**Center-of-gravity**: Modest equity declines and rising (but normal-level) volatility without any staff valuation commentary — essentially neutral.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is near neutral — price moves without valuation assessment default to 45-55 range.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS: N/A
**Evidence**: No direct discussion of banking system health, capital adequacy, or bank-level stress. Lending activity is discussed in credit market context.
**Direction confirmation**: N/A — banking system not specifically discussed.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The exchange value of the U.S. dollar...finishing the period a bit stronger against the currencies of the AFEs and about 3 percent higher against EME currencies." Staff outlook notes "higher projected paths for the foreign exchange value of the dollar" as restraint on GDP.
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar modestly appreciated over the intermeeting period, with the staff outlook factoring in a stronger dollar path.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line satisfied: appreciation > 50.

### Inflation Sub-Components
