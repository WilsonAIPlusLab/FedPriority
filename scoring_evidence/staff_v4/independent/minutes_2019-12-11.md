# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2019-12-11

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **post-2005 segmented minutes** document. It contains three clearly labeled staff sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable. No participant/member views are included.

### Era Context
December 2019 — late-expansion period. The Fed had cut rates three times in 2019 (the "insurance cuts"). Unemployment was at a 50-year low of 3.5%. Inflation was running below the 2% target. Trade tensions with China were the dominant risk. Business investment was weak but the consumer was strong. This is the last meeting before COVID.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.3%, core PCE at 1.6% — both below the 2% target. The trimmed mean was at 2%, but the headline and core measures are clearly below target. Staff forecast says inflation "was forecast to continue to run a bit below 2 percent through 2022." This is a persistent below-target situation. Score should be in the 35-42 range per anchors for 2013-19.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output ABOVE potential. Key language: "Output was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth in 2019 through 2021." GDP increasing at a "moderate rate." The staff revised GDP growth up a little. Unemployment below the natural rate. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language mandates score > 50. But the language is "a little above" — so modestly above. Score ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees the labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 3.5% (50-year low), labor force participation steady, job openings and quits at "fairly elevated levels," initial claims at "historically low levels." Unemployment projected to "remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." With UE at 3.5%, the score should be 60+ per the rubric anchor of 55-62 for 2018-19. Wage growth moderate at 2.7-3.7% range — not screaming tightness but the level of unemployment is extreme. Score ~62-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending is strong: "Real PCE continued to expand," "strong gain in the third quarter," supportive factors listed, consumer sentiment "upbeat." Residential investment increasing. BUT business investment is "weak overall after declining in the second and third quarters," industrial production decreased, manufacturing "soft." The net is mixed — strong consumer, weak business. Score ~46-48 (roughly at trend, slightly below due to business investment drag).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not explicitly discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as ACCOMMODATIVE/SUPPORTIVE. Key language: "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained supportive of spending and economic activity," "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained accommodative," CRE "generally accommodative," residential mortgage "remained accommodative," consumer credit "generally supportive." Bright-line rule: "accommodative"/"supportive" must be < 50. Multiple sectors described as accommodative. Score ~30-35.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury yields "little changed," short-term funding markets "stable," trading "orderly, with volumes in normal ranges," "Pressures on rates... were muted," open market operations "proceeded smoothly." No dysfunction at all. This is smooth functioning. Score ~25-30.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: "Corporate credit spreads narrowed slightly," CMBS spreads "remained near the low end of their post-crisis range," consumer ABS spreads "stabilized at levels somewhat above their post-crisis averages." Spreads are compressed/narrow. Bright-line rule: "narrow" must be < 50. Score ~30-35.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad stock price indexes increased moderately," "option-implied volatility... remained near the low end of its historical distribution." No valuation concern expressed. Equities rising with low vol. Staff doesn't express overvaluation concern. Score ~42-48 (roughly fair valued, no concern).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not explicitly discussed as a standalone topic. The credit quality of nonfinancial corporations "deteriorated slightly... but remained solid overall." Lending conditions described as accommodative. No bank stress discussed. Could argue N/A, but the overall picture of accommodative lending and solid credit quality suggests low stress. Score N/A — banking system health is not directly discussed.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The broad dollar index ended the period little changed." Bright-line: little changed = stable = ~46-54 range. Score ~48-50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer price inflation, as measured by the 12-month percentage change in the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), remained below 2 percent in October."
- "Core PCE price inflation... was 1.6 percent over that same 12-month period."
- "both inflation measures were forecast to continue to run a bit below 2 percent through 2022."

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees inflation running persistently below the 2% target at 1.3-1.6%, with the forecast showing it remaining below target through the medium term. The trimmed mean at 2% provides some offset.

**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because PCE is 1.3% and core PCE is 1.6%, both below the 2% target, and the staff projects continued below-target readings.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Output was forecast to expand at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth in 2019 through 2021."
- "real gross domestic product (GDP) was increasing at a moderate rate in the second half of 2019."
- "The unemployment rate was projected to... remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Staff sees the economy operating just slightly above potential, with GDP growth "a little above" the potential rate. The language is measured — "a little above" — not booming.

**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is above neutral because staff explicitly says output growth is "a little above" potential. Bright-line rule satisfied (score 53 > 50).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate... moved back down to its 50-year low of 3.5 percent in November."
- "The unemployment rate was projected to be roughly flat at around its current level through 2022 and to remain below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate."
- Job openings and quits at "fairly elevated levels"; initial claims at "historically low levels."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with a 50-year low unemployment rate at 3.5%, well below the natural rate. Wage growth is moderate (2.7-3.7%), which tempers slightly, but the level of tightness as measured by unemployment is unmistakable.

**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is above neutral because UE at 3.5% is a 50-year low and below the natural rate. Score 63 reflects very tight conditions consistent with the 2018-19 anchor range.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Consumer: "Real PCE continued to expand in October following a strong gain in the third quarter." Key supportive factors listed.
- Business: "Real nonresidential private fixed investment remained weak overall after declining in the second and third quarters." "Industrial production decreased in October."
- Residential: "Real residential investment appeared to be increasing further after rising solidly in the third quarter."

**Center-of-gravity**: Solid consumer spending and recovering housing are offset by notably weak business investment and soft manufacturing. The net is roughly at trend, tilted slightly below because the business investment weakness is persistent and forward-looking indicators "pointed to continued softness."

**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly below neutral because the strong consumer is offset by declining business investment and soft manufacturing.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in short-term funding markets were stable over the intermeeting period."
- "Trading in money markets was orderly, with volumes in normal ranges and spreads narrower relative to the IOER rate."
- "Pressures on rates at October month-end and November mid-month... were muted."
- "The Desk's open market operations... proceeded smoothly."

**Center-of-gravity**: Treasury and money markets functioning well. No dysfunction, orderly trading, muted pressures at settlement dates. This is a low-stress environment.

**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is below neutral because all indicators point to smooth, orderly market functioning. No dysfunction described.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 33 +/-4 [High confidence]**

- "CMBS spreads widened slightly over the intermeeting period but remained near the low end of their post-crisis range."
- "Gross issuance of corporate bonds was robust."

**Center-of-gravity**: Corporate spreads narrowing, CMBS near post-crisis lows, robust issuance — all pointing to compressed/favorable credit conditions. Slightly above the extreme compression anchor but well below normal.

**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because spreads are narrowing and near post-crisis tight levels. Bright-line rule satisfied (score 33 < 50).
