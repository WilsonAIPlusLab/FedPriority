# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2020-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary), post-2005 segmented format with separate "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook" sections. Scoring only staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: April 2020 — the immediate onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The U.S. economy was in freefall following widespread shutdowns in mid-March. This is one of the sharpest economic contractions in modern history. Financial markets experienced extreme stress in mid-March but had partially recovered by the time of this meeting due to massive Federal Reserve intervention.

### Directional Signals

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as below midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.8% (below 2% target), core PCE at 1.8%, CPI at 1.5%. Staff projects inflation to "weaken this year, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices." Energy prices are falling substantially. This points to below-target inflation with downside risks — in the 25-35 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output as far below midpoint. "Real GDP declined sharply in the first quarter" and "an extraordinary contraction in GDP in the second quarter" is expected. "Real GDP was forecast to plummet." This is a massive negative output gap — catastrophic contraction language. Bright-line check: below potential language clearly present, score MUST be < 50. The anchor for COVID Q2 2020 is 15-22.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as extremely loose/slack. "Labor market conditions deteriorated substantially," "job losses surged in March," unemployment jumped to 4.4% in March (and this was before the worst of it), "initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits skyrocketed." Staff forecasts unemployment "to soar in the second quarter." Anchor: 15-22 for 2020 Q2.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Staff sees spending as collapsing. "Real PCE declined steeply," retail sales "fell substantially in March," "real business fixed investment slumped," "industrial production fell precipitously." Consumer sentiment "deteriorated substantially." Residential investment was strong for Q1 as a whole but "started to slow dramatically late in the quarter." This is collapse-level language. Anchor: 15-20 for 2020 Q2.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff sees conditions as very tight/strained. "Financing conditions for businesses, households, and state and local governments were strained." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses were strained in March, particularly for lower-rated firms and small businesses." C&I lending "somewhat tight," CRE conditions "strained," consumer credit "tightened somewhat." However, conditions improved from the extreme mid-March stress due to Fed actions. Bright-line check: "strained" = score MUST be > 50. The mid-March crisis was ~85-95 but by end of period conditions had improved somewhat. The net assessment is still very tight — around 72-78.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Staff describes actual market dysfunction. "Treasury markets experienced extreme volatility in mid-March, and market liquidity became substantially impaired." "Market depth remained exceptionally low and bid-ask spreads for off-the-run securities and long-term on-the-run securities remained elevated." This is genuine dysfunction, not just yield moves. However, conditions "gradually improved" from the extreme. Score should reflect the still-impaired state at meeting time — around 65-72.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Staff sees spreads as elevated. "Corporate bond spreads widened somewhat" on net. Spreads "at the end of the intermeeting period still stood significantly above their levels in January." Speculative-grade bond issuance was "extremely low in March." Spreads retraced most of their mid-March spike but remained elevated. Bright-line check: "widened" / "elevated" = score MUST be > 50. Around 65-72.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: Equities were "exceptionally volatile," "record declines" early in the period. Equity prices "more than retraced their earlier declines" to end "notably higher" but "remained markedly below peaks registered earlier this year." Option-implied volatility "reached a record high" though it declined. Staff describes significant stress with partial recovery. Score around 62-68.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Mixed signals. "The banking sector, including the large banks, was resilient coming into this period. Banks were able to meet surging demand for draws on credit lines while also building loan loss reserves." However, "some notable vulnerabilities" in other parts of the financial system, institutional prime MMFs "faced significant stress," and the staff highlighted "possible vulnerabilities in mortgage servicers, insurance companies, and large, highly leveraged financial intermediaries." Banks themselves are described as resilient, but the broader system has stress. Bright-line check: "resilient" for banks = below 50, but broader financial system stress is notable. Score around 50-55, reflecting the mixed picture — banks sound but system-wide vulnerabilities.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: Staff reports "the staff's broad dollar index increased, with the dollar appreciating modestly against AFE currencies and notably against EME currencies." "The broad dollar index remained significantly higher than at the beginning of the year." Bright-line check: appreciation = score MUST be > 50. Score around 62-68.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target, weakening. Low score = correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says catastrophic contraction. Very low score = correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says collapsing employment. Very low score = correct.
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "strained." High score = correct.
- TREASURY_STRESS: Higher = more dysfunction. Evidence says "substantially impaired" liquidity, elevated bid-ask. High score = correct.
- CREDIT_SPREADS: Higher = wider/more stress. Evidence says "significantly above January levels." High score = correct.
- BANKING_STRESS: Higher = more stress. Banks "resilient" but broader system vulnerabilities. Near-neutral to modestly elevated = correct.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: Higher = stronger dollar. Evidence says "appreciated." Above-50 score = correct.

### Bright-Line Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP: "below potential" / "plummet" -> score < 50. CHECK (scoring ~15).
- FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: "strained" -> score > 50. CHECK (scoring ~75).
- CREDIT_SPREADS: "widened" / "elevated" -> score > 50. CHECK (scoring ~68).
- BANKING_STRESS: Banks "resilient" -> below 50 component; broader system stress -> above 50 component. Net ~52 is justified by mixed signals.
- DOLLAR_STRENGTH: "appreciating" -> score > 50. CHECK (scoring ~65).
- TREASURY_STRESS: Actual dysfunction described (impaired liquidity, elevated bid-ask) -> score > 60 justified. CHECK.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
1. **OUTPUT_GAP (15) <-> LABOR_TIGHTNESS (13)**: Both catastrophically low. Coherent.
2. **OUTPUT_GAP (15) <-> CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (12)**: Both show collapse. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Total PCE price inflation and core PCE price inflation...both increased 1.8 percent over the 12 months ending in February." "Inflation was projected to weaken this year, reflecting both the deterioration in resource utilization and sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation already below target at 1.8% and projected to weaken further amid massive economic contraction and energy price declines.
**Direction confirmation**: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because PCE is below 2% target and staff projects further weakening from resource slack and energy price declines.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real gross domestic product (GDP) declined sharply in the first quarter" and "a variety of economic indicators were already pointing toward an extraordinary contraction in GDP in the second quarter." "Real GDP was forecast to plummet."
**Center-of-gravity**: Catastrophic contraction underway — sharp Q1 decline with "extraordinary" Q2 contraction expected. This is among the worst output gaps in modern history.
**Direction confirmation**: OUTPUT_GAP is far below neutral because staff describes a historic collapse in output. Bright-line: "below potential" / "plummet" = MUST be < 50. CHECK.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 13 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "U.S. labor market conditions deteriorated substantially in March and April." "Initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits skyrocketed in the second half of March through the first half of April." "The unemployment rate to soar in the second quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market in historic collapse — mass layoffs, skyrocketing unemployment claims, with far worse expected. Unemployment already 4.4% in March and expected to soar.
**Direction confirmation**: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is far below neutral because the labor market is experiencing catastrophic loosening with massive job losses.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Real PCE declined steeply in the first quarter." "Real business fixed investment slumped in the first quarter." "Total industrial production fell precipitously in March." "Consumer sentiment measures...deteriorated substantially."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending, business investment, and industrial production all in freefall. Virtually every spending category is collapsing.
**Direction confirmation**: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is far below neutral because spending and investment are collapsing across all categories.

### PRODUCTIVITY: N/A
Not discussed in the staff assessment.

### FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS: 75 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Financing conditions for businesses, households, and state and local governments were strained over the intermeeting period." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses were strained in March, particularly for lower-rated firms and small businesses." "C&I lending conditions were somewhat tight." Banks "reported having tightened their C&I lending standards and terms for firms of all sizes."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions were extremely tight at mid-March and have improved somewhat due to Fed intervention, but remain strained across most markets. Not at crisis peak (85-95) but well above normal.
**Direction confirmation**: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is well above neutral because staff repeatedly uses "strained" and "tight" for financing conditions. Bright-line: "strained" = MUST be > 50. CHECK.

### TREASURY_STRESS: 68 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Treasury markets experienced extreme volatility in mid-March, and market liquidity became substantially impaired." "Market depth remained exceptionally low and bid-ask spreads for off-the-run securities and long-term on-the-run securities remained elevated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Genuine market dysfunction occurred — impaired liquidity, elevated bid-ask spreads, exceptionally low market depth. Conditions improved from the worst but remained abnormal at meeting time.
**Direction confirmation**: TREASURY_STRESS is above neutral because staff describes actual market dysfunction (liquidity impairment, bid-ask widening), not just yield movements. Score > 60 is justified by ongoing dysfunction.

### CREDIT_SPREADS: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "Corporate bond spreads over comparable-maturity Treasury yields widened sharply in the beginning of the intermeeting period, and they subsequently retraced most of their increases to end up only somewhat higher on net." "Corporate bond spreads at the end of the intermeeting period still stood significantly above their levels in January."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spreads spiked dramatically in mid-March, partially retraced, but remain "significantly above" pre-crisis levels. Speculative-grade issuance was "extremely low."
**Direction confirmation**: CREDIT_SPREADS is above neutral because spreads remain significantly elevated despite partial retracement. Bright-line: "widened" / "elevated" = MUST be > 50. CHECK.

### EQUITY_VALUATION: 63 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Stock price indexes were exceptionally volatile early in the intermeeting period, and one-month option-implied volatility on the S&P 500 index reached a record high." "Broad equity price indexes remained, however, markedly below peaks registered earlier this year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Equities recovered from worst levels but remain "markedly below" peaks. Record volatility occurred. Staff describes significant stress with partial recovery, not valuation commentary per se.
**Direction confirmation**: EQUITY_VALUATION is above neutral (more stress) because equities remain markedly depressed from earlier peaks with elevated volatility.

### BANKING_STRESS: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "The banking sector, including the large banks, was resilient coming into this period. Banks were able to meet surging demand for draws on credit lines while also building loan loss reserves." However: "some notable vulnerabilities that had been identified in previous financial stability assessments exacerbated financial strains" and "possible vulnerabilities in mortgage servicers, insurance companies, and large, highly leveraged financial intermediaries."
**Center-of-gravity**: Banks themselves are described as resilient and well-capitalized, but the broader financial system exhibits notable vulnerabilities and stress in non-bank intermediaries. The net picture is near-neutral with a slight tilt toward stress.
**Direction confirmation**: BANKING_STRESS is near neutral. Banks are "resilient" (pulling toward < 50) but broader financial system vulnerabilities and non-bank stress (pulling toward > 50) net out to roughly neutral with a slight stress tilt.

### DOLLAR_STRENGTH: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**: "The staff's broad dollar index increased, with the dollar appreciating modestly against AFE currencies and notably against EME currencies." "The broad dollar index remained significantly higher than at the beginning of the year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Dollar strengthened meaningfully, particularly against EME currencies, reflecting safe-haven flows. The broad index is significantly higher than year-start.
**Direction confirmation**: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar appreciated. Bright-line: appreciation = MUST be > 50. CHECK.

### INFLATION_GOODS: 22 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Substantial declines in consumer energy prices, which were reflecting significantly lower crude oil prices." Staff projects "sizable expected declines in consumer energy prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Goods prices pulled down significantly by energy price collapse. Core goods not separately detailed but the energy drag is dominant.

### INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A
Not separately discussed by staff.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A
Not discussed as an inflation sub-component. Housing activity is discussed but not shelter/rent inflation.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 42 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**: "Recent readings on survey-based measures of longer-run inflation expectations were little changed on balance." "Measures of inflation compensation based on TIPS ended the period higher, on net, but were still low by historical standards."
**Center-of-gravity**: Survey expectations anchored ("little changed"), market-based measures recovered from sharp drop but remain "low by historical standards." Slightly below the anchored-at-target midpoint.
