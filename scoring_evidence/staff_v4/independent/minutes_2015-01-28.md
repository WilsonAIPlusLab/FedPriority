# FOMC Staff Assessment Scoring — 2015-01-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2005 segmented format with clearly labeled sections: "Staff Review of the Economic Situation," "Staff Review of the Financial Situation," and "Staff Economic Outlook." All content is staff-attributable.

**Era context**: January 2015. The US economy is in the mid-expansion phase of the post-GFC recovery. Unemployment has fallen substantially but remains above pre-crisis lows. Inflation is running well below 2% due to falling energy prices. The dollar has been appreciating notably. The Fed has ended QE3 (October 2014) and is approaching but has not yet begun its rate normalization cycle.

### Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as clearly BELOW the midpoint. PCE inflation at 1.25% over 12 months ending November, core at 1.5%. Total CPI at 0.75% over 12 months ending December. 3-month annualized total CPI decreased at 2.5% rate. Core CPI at only 1% pace over 3 months. Staff revised near-term inflation forecast down further. Inflation projected to "remain below the Committee's 2 percent objective" through 2016-2017. Downside risks to inflation "increased somewhat." This is clearly below target — anchoring around 26-35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees economy as BELOW potential but closing. Key language: "slow reduction in resource slack," "slack in labor and product markets anticipated to fade," GDP growing "more quickly than potential output." The economy is expanding solidly but resource slack persists. Bright-line check: "slack remains" language means score MUST be < 50. Anchoring around 41-45 given solid growth pace and closing gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Staff sees labor market as improving but still with slack. Unemployment at 5.6% in December — declining. Payroll gains strong (Q4 above Q3). Job openings up. Part-time for economic reasons declining. But the staff projects unemployment to "decline gradually" toward the natural rate, implying it is currently above NAIRU. UE at 5.6% maps to roughly 38-44 range per the rubric (UE 5.5-7% = 31-45). The market is clearly tightening but not yet at full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending is clearly strong: "robust pace," "briskly," "supportive of further solid gains." But business investment is decelerating: "appeared to decelerate," orders and shipments of capital goods "declined." Housing "improved somewhat but remained slow." Net picture: strong consumption offset partially by weaker investment. Leaning above trend for consumption, below for investment. Net around 50-55.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not specifically discussed. Score N/A.

**FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS**: Staff describes conditions as generally accommodative. CRE financing "stayed accommodative," consumer credit markets "largely accommodative," residential mortgage conditions "remaining tight" but "showing some further signs of gradual easing," mortgage rates near all-time lows. C&I loans expanding at "robust rate." Credit freely available in most sectors. However, leveraged loan issuance slowed and speculative-grade bond issuance "declined" and "remained subdued." On balance, conditions are accommodative. Bright-line check: "accommodative" language means score MUST be < 50. Score around 30-38.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Yields moved lower over the intermeeting period. Trading was "volatile at times" and "interest rate volatility increased somewhat." But there is no language about market dysfunction, liquidity problems, settlement failures, or auction difficulties. This is normal market functioning with some volatility. Score around 40-45.

**CREDIT_SPREADS**: Corporate bond spreads "little changed, on net, for investment-grade issuers" and "ended the period lower for speculative-grade issuers." Credit flows "generally remained strong." However, leveraged loan spreads increased and speculative-grade issuance was subdued. Staff notes "valuation pressures" in corporate debt markets (suggesting compressed spreads). On balance, spreads are below normal / accommodative. Bright-line check: favorable credit conditions = score < 50. Score around 35-40.

**EQUITY_VALUATION**: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes moved higher." Markets were "volatile." Option-implied volatility "in the upper half of the range seen over the past year." Banking stocks moved lower. Staff noted "valuation pressures in some asset markets." No language about distressed or depressed equities. Equities are up, staff notes some valuation pressure (overvaluation concern direction). Score around 40-45 — roughly fair valued with mild overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Staff explicitly states: "Relatively high levels of capital and liquidity in the banking sector" as a factor "limiting the vulnerability of the financial system." C&I loans expanding robustly. Some concern about oil price effects on "other institutions with more concentrated exposures." Banks easing CRE lending standards. Bright-line check: "high levels of capital and liquidity" = healthy = score MUST be < 50. Score around 30-35.

**DOLLAR_STRENGTH**: "The U.S. dollar strengthened against the currencies of most other advanced economies." The euro "depreciated significantly against the dollar." Staff outlook notes "restraint implied by the recent appreciation of the dollar." Bright-line check: dollar appreciation = score MUST be > 50. This is notable strength, not just modest. Score around 65-70.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy prices are declining sharply. "Continuing large decreases in energy prices." Petroleum imports down in both price and volume. Consumer energy prices declined substantially (3-month CPI at -2.5% annualized rate largely from energy). This captures severe goods disinflation from energy. Score around 20-25.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately broken out from core. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing starts at highest since 2008, prices advancing, but no specific discussion of shelter/rent inflation. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Survey measures "remained stable." Market-based measures (TIPS 5-10yr) "declined further to its lowest level in a decade." Mixed signals — surveys anchored but market measures notably declining. Staff downside risks to inflation "increased somewhat." Score around 40-45 — slightly below anchored-at-target given the market measure decline.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer price inflation moved further below the FOMC's longer-run objective of 2 percent, held down by continuing large decreases in energy prices." PCE at 1.25% over 12 months; core CPI at 1.5%; 3-month total CPI "decreased at an annual rate of 2.5 percent."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees inflation clearly running below the 2% target, with the near-term forecast revised further down and downside risks increasing.
- Direction confirmation: INFLATION_PRESSURE is below neutral because inflation is well below target at ~1.25% headline, with energy prices driving further disinflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The expansion in economic activity over the medium term was anticipated to lead to a slow reduction in resource slack." GDP forecast to "rise more quickly than potential output" — implying current output is still below potential.
- Center-of-gravity: Staff sees the economy below potential but actively closing the gap with solid growth above the trend rate.
- Direction confirmation: OUTPUT_GAP is below neutral because "resource slack" persists, satisfying the bright-line rule that slack language requires < 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The unemployment rate declined to 5.6 percent in December." Staff projects UE to "decline gradually and to move slightly below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate for a time" — implying it is currently above the natural rate.
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market is improving with strong payroll gains and declining UE, but remains below full employment with the unemployment rate above the natural rate.
- Direction confirmation: LABOR_TIGHTNESS is below neutral because the labor market has remaining slack (UE above natural rate).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Real personal consumption expenditures appeared to have risen at a robust pace over the second half of 2014." But "real private expenditures for business equipment and intellectual property appeared to decelerate in the fourth quarter" with orders and shipments declining.
- Center-of-gravity: Strong consumer spending more than offsets decelerating business investment. Net position is modestly above trend.
- Direction confirmation: CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT is slightly above neutral — robust consumption is the dominant force, but investment drag keeps the score only modestly above 50.

### 5. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: N/A**
- Evidence: No discussion of productivity growth or trends in the staff sections.

### 6. FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financing conditions in the commercial real estate sector stayed accommodative." "Conditions in consumer credit markets stayed largely accommodative." "Commercial and industrial loans on banks' books continued to expand at a robust rate." Mortgage rates "reaching levels close to their all-time lows."
- Center-of-gravity: Staff describes broadly accommodative financial conditions across CRE, consumer credit, and bank lending, with some pockets of tightening in leveraged/speculative segments.
- Direction confirmation: FINANCIAL_CONDITIONS is below neutral because conditions are "accommodative" across multiple sectors, satisfying the bright-line rule.

### 7. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: 42 +/-4 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Amid trading that was volatile at times, longer-term sovereign yields in the United States... declined." "Interest rate volatility increased somewhat."
- Center-of-gravity: Treasury market functioning normally with orderly yield declines. Some increased volatility but no signs of dysfunction, liquidity problems, or market stress.
- Direction confirmation: TREASURY_STRESS is at/slightly below neutral — normal market functioning with some volatility, but no dysfunction indicators.

### 8. CREDIT_SPREADS
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Corporate bond spreads were... little changed, on net, for investment-grade issuers and ended the period lower for speculative-grade issuers." Staff notes "valuation pressures... most notable in corporate debt markets" — implying compressed spreads. "Credit flows to nonfinancial firms generally remained strong."
- Center-of-gravity: Credit spreads are below normal with strong issuance and flows, though some widening in leveraged segments. The staff's own valuation pressure concern suggests spreads are on the tight side.
- Direction confirmation: CREDIT_SPREADS is below neutral because credit conditions are favorable and the staff identifies valuation pressures (i.e., spreads too compressed).

### 9. EQUITY_VALUATION
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broad U.S. equity price indexes moved higher." Staff noted "valuation pressures in some asset markets." Option-implied volatility "remained in the upper half of the range seen over the past year."
- Center-of-gravity: Equities are rising with some staff concern about valuation pressures, suggesting mild overvaluation concern rather than distress. Score leans toward mild overvaluation concern direction (below 50).
- Direction confirmation: EQUITY_VALUATION is slightly below neutral (toward overvaluation concern side) because staff flags "valuation pressures" while equities are advancing.

### 10. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Relatively high levels of capital and liquidity in the banking sector... continued to be seen as important factors limiting the vulnerability of the financial system to adverse shocks." Minor concern: "other institutions with more concentrated exposures could face strains if oil prices remain at current levels."
- Center-of-gravity: Banking system is healthy and well-capitalized with low stress, though there are minor concerns about concentrated oil-related exposures at some institutions.
- Direction confirmation: BANKING_STRESS is below neutral because the banking sector has "high levels of capital and liquidity," satisfying the bright-line rule.

### 11. DOLLAR_STRENGTH
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The U.S. dollar strengthened against the currencies of most other advanced economies." "The euro depreciated significantly against the dollar." Staff outlook notes "the restraint implied by the recent appreciation of the dollar" offsetting growth support.
- Center-of-gravity: Notable, broad-based dollar appreciation driven by divergent monetary policy and growth concerns abroad. Staff sees it as a meaningful economic headwind.
- Direction confirmation: DOLLAR_STRENGTH is above neutral because the dollar "strengthened" notably, satisfying the bright-line rule. Era anchor for 2015 H1 sharp appreciation is 60-70; score of 67 fits.

### S1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer energy prices declined" sharply. "Continuing large decreases in energy prices." 3-month total CPI "decreased at an annual rate of 2.5 percent, reflecting recent declines in consumer energy prices."
- Center-of-gravity: Goods prices are falling substantially, driven by energy price collapse.

### S2. INFLATION_SERVICES

- Evidence: No specific disaggregation of services inflation in staff discussion.

### S3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: N/A**
- Evidence: Housing market activity discussed but no specific discussion of shelter/rent inflation.

### S4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Measures of expected long-run inflation from a variety of surveys... remained stable." But "market-based measures of inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead declined further to its lowest level in a decade."
- Center-of-gravity: Expectations are anchored per surveys but market-based measures are notably declining, creating modest downside concern.
