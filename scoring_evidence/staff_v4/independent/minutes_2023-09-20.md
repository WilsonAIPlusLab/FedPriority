### Era Context
September 2023. The Fed has been in an aggressive tightening cycle since March 2022. Inflation has been coming down from the 2022 peak but remains above the 2% target. The labor market has been remarkably resilient despite restrictive monetary policy. The economy has surprised to the upside relative to widespread recession expectations.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Staff sees inflation as ABOVE midpoint. PCE at 3.3%, core PCE at 4.2%, CPI at 3.7%, core CPI at 4.3% — all well above the 2% target. However, staff notes inflation is "continued to show signs of slowing" and "both total and core PCE price inflation were lower than a year earlier." The level is clearly elevated but the trajectory is declining. The staff forecast projects ~3.5% at year-end, returning to ~2% by 2026. Staff also flags upside risks to inflation. This is clearly above target with some improvement — fits the 61-75 range. Direction: "somewhat elevated" to "still elevated" — I'll place this around 65.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Staff sees output ABOVE midpoint. GDP "rising at a solid pace," private domestic final purchases "also looked to be rising solidly." The staff forecast says growth will be "slower" in 2024-2026 and will "run below the staff's estimate of potential output growth." This implies that current GDP is above or near potential (since it needs to slow below potential to cool). The economy is clearly performing solidly. However, the staff doesn't explicitly say "above potential" — they say growth is solid now but projected to slow below potential. The current level is likely near or modestly above potential. I'll score 53.

Bright-line check: No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for the CURRENT state. Staff projects future growth below potential. Current solid pace suggests at or modestly above. Score 53 is consistent.

Per the rubric, UE below 4% should be above 55. The easing language moderates somewhat. Score ~62.
