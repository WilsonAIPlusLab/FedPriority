## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the March 15, 2011 FOMC meeting. It is fully segmented: individual speakers are identified by name (e.g., MR. EVANS., MS. YELLEN., MR. PLOSSER., etc.). The document covers both the economic go-around (first part) and the policy discussion (second part). Approximately 16–17 distinct participants are identifiable. The document is very long and rich, enabling High confidence across most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
March 2011 is mid-recovery from the GFC. The economy is expanding but far below potential. Unemployment was ~8.9% and falling. QE2 ($600 billion Treasury purchase program) is ongoing and nearing its June 2011 completion. A key backdrop: oil and commodity prices are surging (Middle East/North Africa turmoil, Japan earthquake/tsunami just occurred). Inflation is a growing concern on the upside, but core inflation remains low (~0.8–1% year-over-year). The zero lower bound constrains policy. The broad macro backdrop anchors expectations but the text must drive the actual scores.

### 3. Directional Analysis — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral but differentiated: headline inflation is rising due to commodity/energy price surges, while core/underlying inflation remains low. There is a clear split: Evans, Pianalto, Williams, Yellen see limited pass-through, low core, and transient headline effects. Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Bullard see upside inflation risks as meaningful, with Fisher citing apparel, food pass-through, and Lacker drawing 2003–04 analogies. Bullard notes TIPS breakevens are above 2% at all horizons. Bernanke acknowledges headline will reach ~2.3% PCE peak in June. The CENTER OF GRAVITY is: commodity prices have elevated headline inflation meaningfully and participants are divided on pass-through, but the majority still view core/underlying as low and transient. Score: above 50 but not dramatically so — headline pressure is real and acknowledged broadly, core remains low. Range: 55–60 is appropriate. The "transitory" framing is actively discussed but the rubric says to score current perceived pressure. Multiple speakers note headline will hit 2.3%+ and inflation expectations are ticking up. Score: 58.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as below potential but recovering. Evans: "substantial slack in labor and most product markets." Williams: "significant amount of slack in the economy today." Yellen: "awfully long road to travel to attain our maximum employment objective." Bernanke: "unemployment fell significantly, though payroll growth has been somewhat less strong overall." Plosser is the outlier calling for above-trend growth and policy tightening, but even he says growth will be "above trend over the next two years" (forward-looking) not that the gap is closed. The center of gravity is clearly: the output gap remains negative/below potential, though improving. Score: 35–38. Anchors say 35–42 for 2010–14 recovery period — consistent. Score: 37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Unemployment at 8.9% with meaningful uncertainty about the drop (Evans, Yellen, Tarullo, Kocherlakota all debate whether the decline reflects real improvement or labor force exits). Evans: "don't think we're looking yet at a vibrant labor market with robust hiring." Yellen: "skeptical that conditions have improved and slack has diminished." Williams: "slack in the economy today." Plosser: employment rising but from depressed levels. Even the more hawkish participants (Plosser, Fisher) don't claim a tight labor market — Fisher boasts Texas employment gains but doesn't claim national tightness. Center of gravity: labor market clearly remains loose, well below full employment, though it is improving. Score: 33–37. Anchors: 35–40 for 2011–14. Score: 35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Mixed signals. Positive: manufacturing strong, auto sales strong, capital spending picking up, business contacts more upbeat. Negative: consumer spending softened, housing deeply depressed, higher gas prices draining purchasing power, Michigan sentiment plunged. Lacker: "household spending continues to show decent momentum." Yellen: "signals pertaining to consumer spending have been harder to read, but on balance point to at least moderate growth." Williams: "consumers appear very cautious about buying certain big-ticket items." Raskin: "rising cost of food and fuel could hinder or even derail growth in consumer demand." Center of gravity: modestly below-to-at trend, with manufacturing and capex strong but consumer more cautious. Score: 42–46. Score: 43.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Duke: "continued gradual improvement in credit quality and weak loan demand." Auto and student loan markets normalized. C&I lending recovering for large firms, small business still weak. Residential real estate credit "mired deep in the mud." Some loosening in financial conditions overall (Bernanke: "credit conditions have improved in some areas"). Fisher raises concerns about covenant-lite loans, leverage, risk-taking — signs of potentially loose/frothy conditions. No participant claims tight financial conditions overall. The picture is: conditions generally supportive/loose, with some pockets of tightness (residential RE, small business) and some concerns about excessive risk-taking. Center of gravity: somewhat loose to neutral. Score: 35–40. Score: 38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This is the most complex dimension. Clear camps:
- **Dovish (complete QE2, extended period, no action now):** Evans, Williams, Yellen, Raskin, Duke, Rosengren, Tarullo, Dudley (~8 speakers) — favor alternative B, complete $600B purchase program, maintain extended period language.
- **Moderate hawk (complete QE2 but signal exit, concerned about inflation):** Pianalto, Bullard, Lockhart, Kocherlakota (~4 speakers) — support B but with hawkish forward lean, want exit strategy discussion.
- **More hawkish (want earlier exit, consider C):** Plosser, Lacker, Fisher (~3 speakers) — Plosser explicitly states "we will need to tighten policy considerably sooner," Lacker "attracted to alternative C" but accepts B, Fisher wary of continued QE.
- Hoenig is referenced briefly (Bernanke mentions "Presidents Fisher and Hoenig" on financial instability) suggesting Hoenig holds hawkish views.

The actual decision: Alternative B passes with broad support — no rate change, complete $600B, maintain extended period. The majority sentiment is strongly dovish on current action. The forward lean is mixed — most want to plan exit but not signal imminent tightening. This is firmly in the 20–35 range (QE era, dovish majority). Score: 28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
The meeting shows notable disagreement. There are distinct camps on: (1) inflation risk assessment, (2) QE tapering (Bullard vs. majority), (3) exit timing (Plosser/Lacker hawkish vs. dovish majority), (4) statement language wording. However, the formal vote appears to pass Alternative B with broad support — no formal dissent is explicitly recorded in the portion read, though Hoenig is referenced and Plosser/Lacker express strong reservations. Lacker says "irresistible attraction to alternative C" but supports B. Fisher says he "can live with" B. This is a meeting with at least 3-4 participants expressing significant reservations and 1 clear dissent tendency (Hoenig is referenced). The rubric notes: 46–60 = "Notable disagreement: distinct camps; 1-2 formal dissents." Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Fisher devotes a significant portion of his go-round to financial stability concerns: covenant-lite loans surging ($30B in 2.5 months vs $24B all of 2006), PIK toggle notes, leverage by insurance/pension funds, retail investors plowing into equities at record pace, oil futures speculation, subprime auto approvals rising to 38%. Bernanke's summary mentions "risks to financial stability have been noted." Rosengren mentions farmland prices. Duke discusses credit conditions at length. Multiple speakers discuss European sovereign/banking stress (Dudley, Tarullo). This is moderately prominent — several participants address it, but it is not the dominant topic; the economic and inflation discussion dominates. Score: 42–50. Score: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The meeting maintains "extended period" language for rates (0–0.25%). The Committee completes QE2 with no tapering. Several participants (Plosser, Kocherlakota, Lacker) signal hawkish forward lean: exit strategy to be discussed at April meeting, possible rate hike by end of 2011. Bernanke: "April will be a very important meeting" for guidance on future purchases. But the dominant message is: no change now, extended period maintained, QE2 completes on schedule. The forward signal is mildly dovish-to-neutral: "extended period" language is preserved, but there are explicit discussions of planning for exit. This sits around 25–35 — easing lean with data dependence, but with exit planning underway. Score: 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Japan earthquake/tsunami (just occurred), Middle East/North Africa political disruptions, European sovereign debt crisis, uncertainty about labor force participation and slack, commodity price pass-through uncertainty, uncertainty about oil price path. Tarullo explicitly: "we are more in the realm of uncertainty than of calculable risk." Dudley: "I find it very hard to be confident." Bernanke acknowledges "implications of which are not yet known" (Japan). Williams: economy "mixed." Yellen: "risks to the recovery have grown in recent weeks, and they diminish my confidence." This is notably elevated uncertainty — multiple, independent, non-economic shocks. Score: 58–65. Score: 62.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bullard: "Since we embarked on QE2, many measures of inflation have bottomed out and turned higher...according to the recent TIPS market, this Committee is set to miss its implicit inflation target of 2 percent or so on the high side at any horizon."
- Plosser: "prices-paid and prices-received indexes in our manufacturing survey hit their highest levels in three years...I see inflation risks as clearly to the upside."
- Pianalto: "The recent surge in oil prices has heightened concerns about inflation...I have shifted the balance of risks on inflation to the upside."
- Evans/Williams/Yellen (counterweight): core PCE ~0.8–1%, limited pass-through expected, "transient" effects, underlying inflation "subdued."

**Center-of-gravity:** Headline inflation is meaningfully elevated by commodity shocks and broadly acknowledged, but the majority of participants still regard core/underlying inflation as low with limited pass-through expected — placing the center modestly above 50 but not dramatically so.

**Minority views:** Fisher, Plosser, and Lacker argue pass-through risks are underappreciated, pushing the upper bound to ~66.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "I continue to believe that there still is substantial slack in labor and most product markets."
- Williams: "given the significant amount of slack in the economy today, I expect core inflation to remain low."
- Yellen: "there is an awfully long road to travel to attain our maximum employment objective."
- Bernanke summary: "Indicators continue to point to a strengthening though moderate recovery in 2011. Private-sector demand may be leading to self-sustaining growth."

**Center-of-gravity:** The economy is below potential but recovering — a gap clearly persists, with broad agreement that slack remains substantial despite improvement since the 2009 trough. Consistent with the 35–42 range for 2010–14 recovery period.

**Minority views:** Plosser argues growth is "above trend" going forward but acknowledges current gap; this informs the upper bound (~43).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- Kocherlakota: "The question for us that's important in terms of slack is: Should we expect those people to return to the labor force?" (U-5 uncertainty).

**Center-of-gravity:** Unemployment at 8.9%, well above NAIRU; broad agreement that labor market is still loose with meaningful slack. Multiple participants debate whether unemployment decline reflects real improvement or labor force exits. Consistent with anchor 35–40 for 2011–14.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota argues slack may be smaller than thought; Fisher/Texas boasts regional tightness — both inform the upper bound (~41).

**Score: 43 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Center-of-gravity:** Manufacturing and capital equipment strong, auto sales strong, but consumer spending cautious and housing deeply depressed. Net position is modestly below trend — below the 46–54 "at trend" range.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Evans are more optimistic about overall trajectory (~50), while Raskin/Yellen flag housing and consumer concerns (~36).
