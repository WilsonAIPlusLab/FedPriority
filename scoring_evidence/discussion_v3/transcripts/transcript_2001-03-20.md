# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2001-03-20 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers the economic go-around and the policy discussion portion. Speakers include: Parry, Minehan, Jordan, Santomero, Moskow, McTeer, Stern, Guynn, Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole, Gramlich, Ferguson, McDonough, Kelley, Meyer, and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 17 participants spoke substantively.

### Era Context
March 2001: The economy is in the early stages of the 2001 recession (though NBER later dated the recession start to March 2001). The dot-com bust is well underway, NASDAQ has fallen sharply, and the Fed had already cut rates 100bp in January 2001 (two intermeeting 50bp cuts). Growth has decelerated sharply from the late-1990s boom. Manufacturing is contracting. The committee is debating whether this is a temporary inventory correction or something deeper.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Inflation is barely discussed by participants. The few mentions suggest it is quiescent and not a concern. Parry: "Core PCE inflation is projected to come in at 2 percent this year and just a bit lower next year." Moskow: "inflation measures also have come in on the high side of expectations" but this is a minor aside. Kelley: "inflation quiescent and quite likely to stay so for some time." Hoenig: "recent inflation developments have been relatively modest." Stern: "tight labor markets... accompanied... by reports of accelerated upward pressures on compensation." The center of gravity sees inflation as contained and not a pressing concern. Some note energy pass-through (Santomero mentions energy adding to CPI, health care costs noted by some). Direction: slightly below or at target, not a worry. Score should be in the 41-50 range — roughly at target, with energy providing mild upward pressure but core contained.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly slowing sharply. Multiple participants describe growth around 0-1% in Q1, well below potential (which the staff estimates at ~4%). Parry: "output growth in our forecast remains below potential throughout the forecast horizon." Gramlich: "even in the more optimistic forecasts, the unemployment rate rises more than I think is necessary to control inflation... we should still worry about the growing output gap." Many describe manufacturing as contracting sharply. However, consumer spending, housing, and employment are holding up better than expected. The consensus is weak growth but not outright collapse — stabilization at a low rate. Direction: clearly below potential. Multiple speakers use phrases like "below potential," "growing output gap." Score must be below 50 per the bright-line rule. Given growth around 1% vs. 3.5-4% potential, there is significant slack opening up. Score around 33-37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is a mixed picture. Unemployment remains low (around 4%) and labor markets are described as still tight in many regions, but loosening at the margin. Minehan: "contacts continue to note tight labor markets as a particular concern." Santomero: "Labor markets in our District are still characterized as tight... unemployment rate... stood at 4 percent." Broaddus: "not hearing quite as many anecdotal comments... about extremely tight labor markets but they are still reasonably firm. Unemployment rates have not moved much." Moskow: "labor markets eased considerably early this year." Hoenig: "supply of labor may not be as tight as it was earlier, but labor also does not appear to be in excess supply." Guynn: "Labor markets have loosened somewhat... Unskilled workers have become more available... but critical shortages remain." The center of gravity: still tight but clearly easing. Unemployment around 4% is near NAIRU, loosening from very tight levels. Score around 47-52 — near NAIRU but softening.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is surprisingly resilient but expected to moderate. Investment is weak and falling. Minehan: "surprised by the degree to which current consumer spending on big ticket items such as cars and houses is at odds with measures of confidence." Guynn: "consumer spending seems likely to grow only moderately... investment spending on equipment will rebound more moderately and less quickly." Greenspan: "a lot of capital investment plans being placed on the shelf." Ferguson: "the capital goods overhang is greater than the Greenbook assumes." Multiple speakers note weak manufacturing, delayed capex, inventory correction ongoing. Auto sales propped up by incentives. Housing still strong. Net picture: consumer spending holding but at risk, investment clearly weakening. Below trend but not collapsing. Score around 33-38.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Credit conditions have tightened — Moskow: "become more difficult to obtain credit... tightening of credit standards and a widening of spreads." Minehan: "credit market tightness is affecting spending plans... tight credit markets." But Jordan: "access to credit is not a problem confronting this economy." Broaddus: "while credit market conditions are tighter, I don't hear people telling me that credit is not available." Poole: "credit is somewhat tighter than it was but is still readily available." Equity markets are declining sharply — NASDAQ crash, wealth effects negative. Meyer: "broader financial conditions have not eased—at least not appreciably—despite the decline in the funds rate... decline in equity prices and strengthening of the dollar have significantly offset the stimulus from lower short-term interest rates." Direction: somewhat tight. Credit tightening modestly, equity decline significant, but no credit crisis. Score around 58-63.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for 50bp cut. The debate is between 50bp and 75bp, with most favoring 50bp. Nobody suggests 25bp as appropriate (Kelley mentions it only to dismiss it). McTeer pushed hardest for more: "We need to do more rather than less, sooner rather than later." Poole preferred 75bp with balanced risks. Gramlich would have gone 75bp. But the vast majority support 50bp with risks toward weakness. Greenspan explicitly argues against 75bp. The committee is clearly dovish — they are cutting actively and see more easing likely needed. Forward lean: most want to keep the door open for more cuts. Direction: dovish. They are easing 50bp with asymmetric bias toward more weakness. This is a solid easing posture. Score around 25-30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is broad agreement on 50bp cut. The main debate is about (1) whether to hint at intermeeting move in the press release and (2) whether 50bp or 75bp is better. Poole preferred 75bp with balanced risks but said he "can support" the Chairman's recommendation. Nobody dissented from the vote. The intermeeting language debate generated the most disagreement — many opposed including the "especially" language, and ultimately it was removed. But the core action (50bp + asymmetric) had near-unanimous support. This is moderate disagreement on tactics, not fundamentals. Score around 28-33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Enormous attention to financial conditions — the stock market decline dominates discussion. Nearly every participant mentions equity market declines, wealth effects, and the risk of further stock market drops. Greenspan devotes a large portion of his remarks to the stock market and the Fed's relationship to it. Ferguson discusses equity-driven wealth effects at length. McDonough: "The gloom on Wall Street has reached the point of advanced silliness." Meyer discusses the equity premium and equilibrium real rate. McTeer: "the stock market is leading the economy down." The venture capital pullback, credit tightening, banking conditions are all discussed. International financial fragility (Japan, Asia, Latin America) also gets substantial attention. This is clearly a prominent topic, rivaling the economic assessment. Score around 68-75.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly dovish — risks weighted toward weakness, door open for intermeeting move, most expect more easing will be needed. Greenspan: "construct language for the press statement that leaves the door wide open on an intermeeting move." Ferguson: "we are prepared to act if and when necessary." McDonough: "we might well move before the next meeting." However, some (Hoenig, Guynn, Jordan) urge caution about signaling too much more easing. Jordan: "at some point... we will reach a position where we feel that... the risks are balanced." Poole: "like to see us come out of this meeting in a posture that does not foster widespread expectations that our next policy adjustment will be another reduction." The center leans clearly toward further easing but with some voices urging pause is coming. Score around 26-32.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Very high uncertainty expressed by many participants. Greenspan: "false dawn" concern, "the uncertainties surrounding the negative wealth effect." Ferguson: "dealing with both an uncertain real economy and unpredictable psychology among consumers and businesses." Kelley: "we cannot know or be overly confident that an upturn is in train... our policy choice must be made in the face of this very fundamental uncertainty." Hoenig: "a great deal of uncertainty in the near term." Meyer: "there is considerable uncertainty about the strength of spending going forward." The mixed signals between real economy (holding up) and financial/confidence indicators (collapsing) create genuine uncertainty. Many note the difficulty of knowing if this is just inventory correction or something deeper. Score around 62-68.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says contained/quiescent. Score ~45 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly below potential. Score ~35 matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says still tight but loosening. Score ~48 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says somewhat tighter. Score ~60 matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says clearly dovish/easing. Score ~27 matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward. Evidence says easing lean. Score ~28 matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): These diverge somewhat. Explanation: The labor market is a lagging indicator. Output has dropped sharply below potential but unemployment hasn't risen much yet — multiple participants explicitly note this disconnect. Gramlich: "the strongest readings come from the labor markets where at this point the changes in unemployment and other measures of labor demand have been moderate." This divergence is well-supported by the text.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (27) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (28): These align — both clearly dovish.

### Speaker Count Summary
- Economy slowing but not collapsing, risks to downside: ~15 speakers (near consensus)
- Manufacturing weak, consumer holding: ~12 speakers
- Favor 50bp cut: ~14 speakers
- Favor 75bp or more aggressive: 2-3 (McTeer, Poole alternative, Gramlich would have)
- Worried about stock market/financial conditions: ~15 speakers
- Concerned about intermeeting language: ~7-8 speakers

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: "With inflation quiescent and quite likely to stay so for some time, I just don't see any necessity to keep such a tight hold."
- MR. HOENIG: "recent inflation developments have been relatively modest, a fact we certainly recognize."
- MR. MOSKOW: "while not yet a major concern, it's important for us to keep in mind that inflation measures also have come in on the high side of expectations."
- MR. PARRY: "Core PCE inflation is projected to come in at 2 percent this year and just a bit lower next year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Inflation is perceived as roughly at target (~2% PCE) and not a concern; energy costs are elevated but core inflation is contained. The focus is entirely on the growth slowdown, not inflation.

**Minority views:** Moskow notes inflation "on the high side of expectations" and Stern mentions "accelerated upward pressures on compensation" in some areas, but these are minor asides.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "output growth in our forecast remains below potential throughout the forecast horizon"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "even in the more optimistic forecasts, the unemployment rate rises more than I think is necessary to control inflation... we should still worry about the growing output gap"
- MR. STERN: "growth has stabilized at a low rate... around 1 percent plus or minus"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discussion of sharp investment decline, inventory liquidation, potential for "false dawn"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Growth has decelerated sharply to ~0-1% versus potential of ~3.5-4%, opening a significant and growing output gap. The economy is clearly operating below potential with most participants expecting weak growth through H1 2001.

**Minority views:** Some note stabilization signs and resilience (Jordan, Broaddus point to some positive indicators), preventing a score below 30.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "contacts continue to note tight labor markets as a particular concern, though high-tech employees may now be easier to find"
- MR. SANTOMERO: "Labor markets in our District are still characterized as tight. The unemployment rate in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Delaware stood at 4 percent"
- MR. MOSKOW: "our labor markets eased considerably early this year. The unemployment rate for our five states moved above 4 percent for the first time since mid-1997"
- MR. HOENIG: "The supply of labor may not be as tight as it was earlier, but labor also does not appear to be in excess supply"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets remain near full employment with low unemployment (~4%), but are clearly easing from the extremely tight conditions of 1999-2000. The market is at or just below NAIRU, with loosening underway but no significant slack yet.

**Minority views:** Moskow and Guynn see more notable loosening; Minehan and Santomero see continued tightness. The range of views is moderate.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. GUYNN: "consumer spending seems likely to grow only moderately... investment spending on equipment will rebound more moderately and less quickly than I had thought earlier"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are also getting some slowing in expenditures from the wealth effect... a lot of capital investment plans being placed on the shelf"
- MR. FERGUSON: "the capital goods overhang is greater than the Greenbook assumes and that investment, therefore, will slow more significantly and for a longer period"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "Auto spending... contracted sharply but has turned back up... Housing construction and overall consumption spending have held up fairly well"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Investment is clearly weakening — high-tech capex is being shelved, manufacturing is contracting, and inventory correction is underway. Consumer spending is surprisingly resilient but expected to moderate under weight of negative wealth effects, higher debt, and reduced overtime/income. Net assessment is below trend.

**Minority views:** Jordan and Broaddus point to some pockets of strength (boat shows, certain industries rebounding), and consumer spending data are stronger than expected, preventing a lower score.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MEYER: "broader financial conditions have not eased—at least not appreciably—despite the decline in the funds rate... the decline in equity prices and strengthening of the dollar have significantly offset the stimulus from lower short-term interest rates"
- MR. MOSKOW: "it has become more difficult to obtain credit... further tightening of credit standards and a widening of spreads"
- MR. BROADDUS: "while credit market conditions are tighter, I don't hear people telling me that credit is not available"
- MR. JORDAN: "access to credit is not a problem confronting this economy... the explosive growth of the various measures of money and credit"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are somewhat tight. Credit standards have tightened, equity markets have declined sharply (creating negative wealth effects), and the dollar has strengthened. However, credit remains available, money growth is rapid, and long-term rates have fallen. The offset between easing policy and tightening financial conditions is the key dynamic.

**Minority views:** Jordan and Poole emphasize rapid money growth and credit availability, suggesting less tightness; Minehan and Moskow see more meaningful credit constraint.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 27 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would propose, therefore, that we reduce the rate by 50 basis points and construct language for the press statement that leaves the door wide open on an intermeeting move"
- MR. MCTEER: "I believe the arguments for front-loading policy easing are just as valid today as they were at the last meeting... We need to do more rather than less, sooner rather than later"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I favor easing monetary policy today... even in the more optimistic forecasts, the unemployment rate rises more than I think is necessary to control inflation"
- MR. GUYNN: "our policy focus should be on the last half of 2001 and into 2002... I would be very cautious about taking more than a modest step because I believe there is a lot of stimulus in the economy"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous support for 50bp cut with risks weighted toward weakness. The committee is actively easing and signaling willingness to do more. The debate is only about pace and signaling — some want 75bp, none want less than 50bp. This is a strong dovish lean tempered by caution about moral hazard and stock market accommodation.

**Minority views:** Guynn, Hoenig, and Jordan urge caution about overdoing it and note lags in policy. Poole wants balanced risks language. But even these voices support 50bp.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- No formal dissents on the 50bp cut with asymmetric risk assessment
- MR. POOLE: "I can support your recommendation, although it is not my first choice" — preferred 75bp with balanced risks
- Significant debate on intermeeting language — Minehan, Broaddus, Guynn, Meyer "very strongly" opposed; Ferguson, McDonough supported
- Informal vote on the word "especially" shows split

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on the core action (50bp + downside risks) with no formal dissents. Meaningful tactical disagreement on press release language and the wisdom of hinting at intermeeting moves. One participant (Poole) preferred a different combination. Disagreement is about communication, not about the economic assessment or the core policy action.

**Minority views:** The intermeeting language debate created genuine tension, elevating this above a rubber-stamp meeting.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of stock market dynamics, "addicts convinced a heightened dose would relieve their pain," false pharmacology analogy, moral hazard concerns
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "The gloom on Wall Street has reached the point of advanced silliness" + extensive international financial stability discussion (Japan banking system, Asian tigers, Argentina/Brazil, Turkey)
- MR. FERGUSON: "the weakness in equity markets might be more severely damaging to consumer confidence—and ultimately spending" + capital goods overhang and productivity linkages
- MR. MEYER: equity premium, equilibrium real rate, broader financial conditions not easing

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability/conditions is a dominant topic at this meeting. Nearly every participant discusses equity market declines, wealth effects, and their implications. Greenspan devotes a substantial portion of his remarks to the stock market and the Fed's proper relationship to it. International financial fragility (Japan, Asia, Latin America) gets extensive attention from McDonough. This rivals the real economy assessment in emphasis.

**Minority views:** None — this is universally discussed.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 28 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "construct language for the press statement that leaves the door wide open on an intermeeting move... we will be prepared to act when and if necessary"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "in addition to today's move, we are prepared to act if and when necessary"
- MR. FERGUSON: "we also will need a great deal of flexibility in the period ahead"
- MR. POOLE: "I would like to see us come out of this meeting in a posture that does not foster widespread expectations that our next policy adjustment will be another reduction in the funds rate"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly toward further easing. The committee adopted downside risk assessment and debated how strongly to signal potential intermeeting easing. Most participants expect more rate cuts will be needed. However, a few voices (Poole, Hoenig, Jordan, Guynn) argue the economy may stabilize and caution against signaling too much more easing.

**Minority views:** Poole's preference for balanced risks and Jordan's concern about communicating a pause represent a modest hawkish minority on forward guidance.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 65 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: "We seem to be in that stage now... we cannot know or be overly confident that an upturn is in train. It is still possible that the various negative forces existing today will drag us into further weakness. Our policy choice must be made in the face of this very fundamental uncertainty."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "false dawn" concern — "it surprises us, and we get a renewed wave of weakness"; "the uncertainties surrounding the negative wealth effect... make me nervous about the outlook"
- MR. FERGUSON: "we are dealing with both an uncertain real economy and unpredictable psychology among consumers and businesses"
- MR. HOENIG: "I also recognize that there's a great deal of uncertainty in the near term and some downside risks as well"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express high uncertainty driven by the disconnect between relatively resilient real economy data and collapsing confidence/equity indicators. The "false dawn" concern, unknown wealth effect magnitude, uncertain investment outlook, and international risks all contribute. Many explicitly frame the policy decision as one made under unusual uncertainty.

**Minority views:** Broaddus and Jordan express somewhat more confidence in the stabilization story, but even they acknowledge significant downside risks.
