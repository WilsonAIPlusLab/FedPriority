It is fully segmented and well-attributed. This is NOT an emergency-meeting stub — it is a full and lengthy discussion covering multiple dimensions in depth.

### 2. Era Context
September 16, 2008 — the day after Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy (September 15, 2008). This is the acute phase of the Global Financial Crisis. The macro backdrop at the time of the meeting: unemployment rising sharply, housing in severe contraction, financial markets in acute stress (Lehman bankruptcy, AIG in crisis, money-market fund "breaking the buck" feared, CDS spreads surging on Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley), and inflation above target due to earlier commodity/energy price surges that were beginning to reverse. The Fed had cut the funds rate aggressively from 5.25% to 2% earlier in the year and was now debating whether to hold, cut, or raise. This is one of the most historically significant meetings on record. I will let the text drive all scores; the macro backdrop serves only as a sanity check.

Bernanke (summary): "recent core and headline numbers have been high" but "inflation pressures are less worrisome now." Kroszner: "at uncomfortably elevated levels now."

Center of gravity: Inflation is clearly above target NOW (elevated headline and core), but the outlook is for moderation given declining commodity prices. Per the "transitory gotcha" rule — score what participants perceive NOW. Most see current inflation as elevated. Score range: 61–72. Given that the majority express that while it is elevated, there are signs of moderation and lower risk going forward, but the CURRENT level remains a concern, I place this at **65**. This aligns with rubric anchor 65-72 for "elevated" inflation period and fits the GFC context where oil had been near $145 and CPI was running at ~5-6% headline.
