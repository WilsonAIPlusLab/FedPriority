# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 1994-04-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. This is an intermeeting telephone conference call. Individual speakers are identified. The discussion is relatively short and focused on a specific policy action (intermeeting rate move). Speakers include Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, Broaddus, Boehne, LaWare, Parry, Stern, Forrestal, Melzer, and Hoenig.

**Era context**: April 1994 -- the Fed is in the midst of its 1994 tightening cycle. The funds rate started at 3% in early 1994 and was being moved up. This was a period of above-trend growth, rising inventory pressures, and bond market turmoil (the "1994 bond massacre"). Inflation was moderate but participants worried about staying ahead of it.

**Speaker count and center of gravity**:
- Greenspan proposes 25bp hike today, discount rate move deferred to next meeting. Notes economy stronger, inventories accelerating, but financial markets still "precarious."
- McDonough: Supports 25bp. Economy "clearly a good deal stronger than we thought." Opposes discount rate move now.
- Broaddus: Supports 25bp but would prefer 50bp (discount rate hike of 50bp with full pass-through). Cites rising prices in basic materials. Wants to get "ahead of the curve."
- Boehne: Supports 25bp. Notes growth is "solid," 25bp "almost surely leaves us behind the curve" economically, but financial market caution warrants restraint. Favors concurrent discount rate hike.
- Parry: Agrees with Greenspan's position. Expects "strong argument" for a larger move at next meeting.
- Stern: Supports 25bp. Notes the backup in long rates and wealth destruction may restrain longer-term growth -- a moderating voice.
- Forrestal: Supports 25bp but "would have been prepared to move even 50 basis points." Economy "moving much faster than most of us would have expected."
- Melzer: Leans toward Broaddus's stronger move. Argues greater decisiveness would help long-term markets by demonstrating Fed commitment to price stability.
- Hoenig: Agrees with Greenspan's 25bp proposal.

**Approximate tally**: All support at least 25bp. About 3-4 (Broaddus, Melzer, arguably Forrestal, Boehne on economic grounds) would prefer a larger move. The center of gravity is firmly for 25bp with recognition that more is coming soon.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation pressure as modestly above the midpoint. There is no alarm about current realized inflation, but there is clear concern about rising prices for basic materials (Broaddus), "inflationary expectation that is in the market" (Forrestal), and inventory-driven cyclical pressures (Greenspan). The concern is more forward-looking/preventive than about current high inflation. Direction: modestly above neutral. Score range: 53-60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy as above potential or at least above trend. Greenspan: "growing economy that's well along in the expansion; growth is widespread" (via Boehne's question). McDonough: economy "clearly a good deal stronger." Boehne: "growth is solid." Forrestal: "economy is moving much faster than most of us would have expected." Greenspan notes inventory acceleration and cyclical pressures building. Multiple participants say 25bp "leaves us behind the curve" -- meaning growth is outpacing the neutral policy stance. Direction: above potential. Score must be >50 per bright-line rule. Score range: 57-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very little direct discussion of labor markets. No mentions of unemployment rate, wage pressures, or labor shortages specifically. Some indirect inference from the strong economy discussion, but no direct evidence. Direction: likely above neutral given the economic strength, but limited evidence. Score: ~52-55, low confidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: McDonough notes "tremendous strength in motor vehicle sales." Boehne: "growth is solid." Greenspan discusses inventory acceleration (implying strong production/demand). Forrestal sees "quite good growth." Direction: above trend. Score range: 58-64.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a major topic. There has been a "sharp decline in both stock and bond prices" (Greenspan). A "significant backup in intermediate- and long-term interest rates" (Stern). "Wealth has been destroyed" (Stern). The bond market turmoil is extensively discussed. Markets are described as "still precarious" (Greenspan). Portfolios are being restructured from "weak to stronger hands" -- but only "half complete." This describes meaningfully tightened financial conditions. Direction: above neutral (tighter). Score range: 60-68.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants support at least 25bp, and this is an intermeeting move (inherently more hawkish than waiting). Several would prefer 50bp. Greenspan signals more moves ahead ("we eventually would like to move in excess of 4 percent"). Parry expects "a strong argument at that meeting for another move, perhaps an even larger move." The unanimous direction is tightening, with debate only about pace. Direction: clearly hawkish. Score range: 68-74.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is no formal vote/dissent structure in this call, but there is notable disagreement about the SIZE of the move. Broaddus and Melzer clearly prefer 50bp. Forrestal was "prepared to move even 50 basis points." Boehne thinks 25bp is insufficient on economic grounds. But all converge on 25bp. The disagreement is about pace/tactics, not direction. Score range: 35-42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial market conditions are a MAJOR topic. Greenspan devotes the bulk of his remarks to market fragility, portfolio restructuring, the 1987 analogy, "surface tension," selling begetting selling. McDonough discusses market adjustment. Stern discusses wealth destruction. Boehne, Forrestal, and Melzer all weigh financial market considerations. This is prominently discussed by most participants. Direction: high attention. Score range: 60-70.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very hawkish forward signal. Greenspan: "we eventually would like to move in excess of 4 percent" (from 3.75 after today's move). He notes at the next meeting they may "move the discount rate 50 basis points and let the whole increase show through." Parry: "strong argument at that meeting for another move, perhaps an even larger move." The signal is clearly that more tightening is coming. Score range: 72-80.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. The main uncertainty is about financial markets -- how much further the adjustment has to go, whether a market implosion could occur. Greenspan: "I don't know how much above 4 percent we're going to have to be." But participants are fairly confident about the economic direction. Score range: 35-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~53): Labor is scored lower due to lack of direct discussion, not contradiction. Acceptable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~76): Both hawkish, with forward tone slightly more so given the explicit signals of further moves. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says rising materials prices, inflationary expectations building. Score above 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says economy stronger than expected, above trend. Score above 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says sharp bond/stock declines, wealth destruction, precarious markets. Score above 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. All favor tightening. Score well above 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 56 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "we are hearing more frequent comments of rising prices and expected future increases in prices, especially for basic materials like paper, building materials, and textiles"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I think it's time that we tried to put to rest this inflationary expectation that is in the market"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (via Boehne's framing): concern about cyclical inventory pressures that could build inflationary momentum

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral -- not yet elevated in realized terms, but with building input price pressures and rising inflation expectations that warrant preemptive action.

**Minority views**: Stern's emphasis on the restraining effect of higher long rates and wealth destruction suggests he sees less inflation pressure ahead.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the economy is clearly a good deal stronger than we thought it was at the last meeting"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "This economy is moving much faster than most of us would have expected"
- MR. BOEHNE: "growth is solid... a 25 basis point increase almost surely leaves us behind the curve"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: inventory acceleration, cyclical pressures building

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly see the economy as running above potential, with growth widespread and accelerating. Multiple members note that even with today's move, policy is "behind the curve," implying output is above its sustainable level.

**Minority views**: Stern notes that the backup in long-term rates may restrain growth going forward, providing a mildly offsetting view.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-8 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- No direct labor market discussion. Inferred from the broadly above-trend growth picture and the characterization of the economy as "well along in the expansion" (Boehne relaying consensus view).
- The absence of specific labor shortage or wage pressure language suggests tightness is not yet a prominent concern.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market tightness is not directly discussed but is implicitly modestly above neutral given the strong growth environment. Scored just above the midpoint with wide uncertainty and low confidence.

**Minority views**: None expressed directly.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "this tremendous strength in motor vehicle sales" and market participants think growth will "move along at about the present pace"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: inventory acceleration suggesting strong demand; "cyclical pressures coming from the inventory area"
- MR. BOEHNE: "growth is solid... these inventory problems are there or will be there"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "directors and other business people are forecasting quite good growth"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending and investment are clearly above trend, with particular strength in motor vehicles and inventory accumulation suggesting robust demand. Growth is widespread across regions.

**Minority views**: Stern's comment about wealth destruction and higher long rates providing future restraint.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the sharp declines in both stock and bond prices since our last meeting... we have an awful lot left in there"; markets are "still precarious"; portfolio restructuring "probably no more than half complete"
- MR. STERN: "the significant backup in intermediate- and long-term interest rates... wealth has been destroyed, at least a bit"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "there's a much bigger financial market out there than I think any of us realized was developing" and the "worldwide efficiency and an aggregate level of transactions that I think is somewhat disturbing"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions have tightened significantly since early 1994, with sharp bond and equity declines, ongoing portfolio restructuring, and markets described as precarious. Conditions are meaningfully tighter than neutral.

**Minority views**: Some (Melzer, Broaddus) implicitly view the tightening as not yet sufficient to discipline inflation expectations, suggesting they see conditions as less restrictive than others do.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 71 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the consensus of this Committee is that we eventually would like to move in excess of 4 percent on the federal funds rate"; proposes 25bp intermeeting hike
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would like to see a 1/2 point increase in the discount rate... put the funds rate at 4 percent"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I would have been prepared this morning to move even 50 basis points"
- MR. BOEHNE: "a 25 basis point increase almost surely leaves us behind the curve"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimously hawkish -- all participants support at least 25bp and this is an intermeeting move, signaling urgency. Several prefer a larger move. The debate is entirely about how fast to tighten, not whether to tighten.

**Minority views**: Stern provides a mild counterweight, noting that market-driven tightening may do some of the Fed's work. But he still supports the 25bp move.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: explicitly prefers 50bp ("If I had my 'druthers'") but goes along with 25bp
- MR. MELZER: argues for "a stronger move along the lines of what Al Broaddus described"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "would have been prepared this morning to move even 50 basis points" but accepts 25bp
- MR. BOEHNE: 25bp "not enough on the economic side" but agrees on financial market grounds

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Moderate tactical disagreement about the size of the move (25bp vs 50bp) and whether to move the discount rate simultaneously. All agree on direction. No fundamental disagreement about the economic assessment or policy framework. Disagreement is about pace, not direction.

**Minority views**: The 50bp camp (Broaddus, Melzer, arguably Forrestal) represents a meaningful minority but all defer to the Chairman's recommendation.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discussion of market fragility, 1987 analogy, "selling begetting selling," "surface tension," portfolio restructuring from "weak to stronger hands," and the risk that "a major contraction or implosion of the financial markets" would have real economic effects
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "financial markets are healthier than they were" but adjustment "in no way over"
- MR. STERN: "wealth has been destroyed, at least a bit... that, too, would have a restraining effect"
- Financial market considerations are explicitly cited as the reason for restraining the size of the move

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability is a prominent topic that directly shapes the policy decision. Greenspan's entire argument for 25bp rather than 50bp rests on financial market fragility. Most participants address it substantively.

**Minority views**: Melzer and Broaddus implicitly downweight the financial stability concern, arguing that a stronger move would actually help markets by demonstrating Fed resolve.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 76 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we eventually would like to move in excess of 4 percent on the federal funds rate"; "There is a good possibility that we may choose in the next meeting to move the discount rate 50 basis points and let the whole increase show through"
- MR. PARRY: "there's going to be a strong argument at that meeting for another move, perhaps an even larger move"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we've got to move the funds rate up somewhat above 4 percent; and frankly, I don't know how much above 4 percent we're going to have to be"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- further tightening is explicitly anticipated at the next meeting and beyond. The Chairman signals a possible 50bp discount rate move at the May meeting. The only question is how far rates ultimately need to go.

**Minority views**: Stern's comments about the restraining effects of the bond selloff suggest he may see less need for aggressive further moves.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I don't know how much above 4 percent we're going to have to be from the economy's point of view"; the portfolio restructuring process is "probably no more than half complete as best I can judge"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: market implosion "has a low probability at all times, but the consequences if it happens are very large" -- classic tail-risk uncertainty
- MR. STERN: uncertainty about whether the bond backup will restrain growth

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about financial market dynamics and the appropriate terminal rate. Participants are fairly confident about the economic direction (strong growth) but uncertain about how fragile markets are and how far rates need to go.

**Minority views**: None diverge sharply on this dimension.
