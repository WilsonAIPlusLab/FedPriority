## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the January 31–February 1, 2017 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (MR. ROSENGREN, MS. BRAINARD, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, MR. FISCHER, MR. HARKER, MS. MESTER, MR. BULLARD, MR. POWELL, MR. KAPLAN, MR. WILLIAMS, MR. EVANS, MS. GEORGE, MR. KASHKARI, CHAIR YELLEN). The document is segmented, covering: (1) a financial stability discussion (Fischer, Brainard, Dudley, Kaplan), (2) the economic go-round (Rosengren, Fischer, Brainard, Harker, Mester, Bullard, Powell, Kaplan, Williams, Evans, George, Kashkari, Dudley, Yellen), and (3) the policy go-round. Lacker's and Tarullo's remarks appear to have been redacted/removed from the released document (the transcript jumps pages 87–96 and pages 93–97 with no content). Gooding's remark is also absent. This is noted but does not materially affect scoring since all other major participants' views are captured.

### 2. Era Context
Early 2017 — first FOMC meeting under the Trump administration. The prior December 2015 liftoff and December 2016 hike have occurred; the funds rate target range is 0.50–0.75%. The economy is growing at roughly 2% (near or slightly above trend); labor market near full employment (~4.7% unemployment); PCE inflation running around 1.6% (below 2% target). Major policy uncertainty from new administration (fiscal, trade, immigration). I will score based on what participants actually say in this document, not era assumptions alone.

The center of gravity is a lean-hawkish hold — comfortable holding now but biased toward continued tightening, with some wanting to move faster (Mester, Rosengren, Harker, George) and others wanting patience (Evans, Kashkari, Brainard, Bullard on rate path). Rubric anchor: "70–75 (2017–18 hiking)". This meeting is early in that cycle — hold decision, but significant portion wanting March. Score is less hawkish than a meeting where they actually hike.
