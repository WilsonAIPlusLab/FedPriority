### 1. Document Type

LOCKHART, VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER, MR. KOHN, MR. WARSH, MR. KROSZNER, MR. MISHKIN, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE summary, plus the full policy go-round). The policy go-round is substantially complete and includes all major participants. The document is fully segmented with participant views clearly attributable.

### 2. Era Context
May 2007: Pre-financial-crisis period. The U.S. economy was experiencing a housing correction concentrated in subprime mortgage markets. GDP growth had slowed significantly in Q1 2007. Core PCE inflation was running around 2.1–2.3% — above the FOMC's implicit 2% comfort zone but not dramatically so. Labor markets remained tight (unemployment ~4.4%). The Fed funds rate was at 5.25%, held since June 2006. This is a mid-cycle soft-patch meeting — NOT a crisis meeting.

### 3. Core Dimension Directional Analysis (Pre-Score)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants widely see inflation as above their comfort zone/target. Multiple participants explicitly state "inflation is too high," "inflation remains somewhat elevated," "core PCE is running north of 2 percent." The most recent print (March) was favorable, but the Greenbook had been revising its core inflation forecast slightly upward. Bernanke's summary: "there are upside risks to inflation, including compensation, the dollar, energy prices, and a slowing in productivity." Lacker: "inflation is unacceptably high." Hoenig: need to be "resolute." Plosser: "I believe inflation is still too high." However, no one is calling this extreme — the level is modestly above a 2% implicit target, not surging. Direction: ABOVE midpoint. Score in the 58–65 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The broad consensus is below-potential growth. Bernanke's summary: "the economy is in a soft patch and will likely grow below trend for a while." Kohn: "income growing at less than the growth rate of potential for several quarters." Geithner: "growth moving back up to potential." Hoenig: "the economy will continue to grow at something below trend." Stern: "I have marked down my forecast for this year... after that, I expect growth to accelerate to near trend." The language is firmly "below potential" currently, with return to potential expected later in 2007 or 2008. Bright-line rule: "below potential" language → score MUST be < 50. Score in the 41–45 range (slightly below potential, not deep recession).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Paradoxically, despite below-trend growth, the labor market is described as still tight. Bernanke's summary: "the labor market remains strong, particularly in the market for highly skilled workers." Lockhart: "Labor markets appear to have remained very tight in the District. The measured unemployment rate is around 4 percent for the District versus 4.4 percent nationally." Plosser: "Job growth has picked up considerably... more than two-thirds of our firms said that they had trouble [filling jobs]." However, there is the "growthless job growth" puzzle Bernanke notes, with some expectation of upcoming softening. The NAIRU debate (some see it at 4.75% vs 5%) puts unemployment slightly below NAIRU. Score in the 58–63 range — tight but not historically extreme.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending has slowed — Bernanke: "consumption has definitely slowed down." Warsh's credit card contact: "consistent with moderate deceleration in real consumption," nominal growth ~4% YoY. Investment showed a promising uptick in durable goods but from a weak base. Housing investment is contractionary. The center of gravity is "moderate/slightly below trend" given the housing drag and consumption slowdown, partially offset by business investment glimmers. Score in the 42–46 range — modestly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a notable feature of the discussion. Multiple participants describe financial conditions as LOOSE/accommodative: Kohn: "supportive financial conditions, especially for businesses; credit availability... narrow credit spreads; low long-term rates." Warsh: "financial risk-taking remains high and may well have even increased... MOVE options index is the lowest it has been in about nine years... credit markets are as strong as ever, and liquidity is plentiful." Geithner: "Credit spreads, other risk premiums, low levels of implied volatility, and the strength of asset prices... imply a level of confidence in ongoing, stable growth." Bernanke's summary: "financial markets are priced for perfection." The concern is that conditions are TOO loose, creating future risk. Direction: LOOSE. Score: 22–28 range (loose, accommodative conditions; concerns about excessive ease).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for Alternative B — hold the funds rate at 5.25%. No one advocates cutting or hiking. The lean is clearly "hold with inflation vigilance as the predominant concern." Bernanke's summary: "the group still appears to view a failure of inflation to moderate as expected to be the predominant risk." Lacker is the most hawkish (wants rate hikes when downside risks abate, wants explicit 1.5% inflation target). Kohn/Mishkin are slightly more dovish (assumed slight easing in their projections). But the center is clearly "hold with hawkish bias." Score: 58–63 range — lean hawkish, hold steady with tightening bias on the forward risk assessment.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal vote dissents are indicated. The policy decision is unanimous (Alternative B). However, there is meaningful divergence on: (1) the appropriate inflation target (1.5% vs. 2% camp — Lacker/Plosser vs. Mishkin/Kohn); (2) how quickly inflation will moderate (Lockhart more pessimistic than Greenbook, Mishkin more optimistic); (3) statement language details ("on balance" debate). These are genuine substantive differences about inflation objectives — an important dimension — but the actual policy action has no dissents. Score: 30–38 range — broad agreement on action, notable differences on inflation target philosophy.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: This receives substantial attention. Warsh devotes most of his remarks to capital markets, private equity, covenant-lite loans, LBO leverage, MOVE volatility index, correlation across asset classes, and mortgage market consolidation. Geithner raises "significant risk of a sharp deterioration in financial markets." Kohn discusses household leverage, financial obligations ratio, and subprime spillover risks. Kroszner discusses covenant-free bank loans to private equity, financial market volatility. Multiple participants discuss the subprime/housing contagion question. Score: 55–65 range — prominent topic, multiple participants discuss financial stability/conditions at length.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The statement retains the language that "predominant policy concern is the risk that inflation will fail to moderate as expected." The Committee holds rates and leans against market expectations of near-term cuts. Geithner: "I favor staying where we are for a while... lean against the expectation that we will move to reduce rates soon." Bernanke: "I'm going to demonstrate a very conservative perspective today." The forward signal is modestly hawkish — hold for now, prepared to tighten if inflation doesn't moderate, but no explicit hiking commitment. Score: 60–65 range.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants explicitly discuss elevated uncertainty. Kroszner reviews "five key uncertainties": investment, productivity/potential, housing/subprime, financial markets, consumption. Kohn "committed the sin of thinking things were more uncertain than usual." Mishkin: "a fair amount of uncertainty." Geithner: "substantial uncertainty about what is happening to underlying trends." Bernanke notes the "unusual situation" of below-potential growth without labor market loosening. However, this is not crisis-level uncertainty — most participants have a central forecast they are reasonably comfortable with. Score: 48–55 range — elevated but not extreme.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PLOSSER: "I believe inflation is still too high. Inflation expectations are stable, but they are too high as well, and we need to bring that rate down."
- MR. LACKER: "inflation is unacceptably high, at least to most of us. When the downside risks have sufficiently diminished, I'm presuming we're going to want to take action to bring inflation down."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "there are upside risks to inflation, including compensation, the dollar, energy prices, and a slowing in productivity. Moreover, a rise in inflation from current levels would be costly, particularly if it involved unhinging inflation expectations."

**Minority views**: Mishkin is relatively more optimistic ("I see inflation coming down to 2.1 percent by the end of 2007 and then to 2 percent"); Stern sees gradual moderation. These pull the score only slightly below 65.
