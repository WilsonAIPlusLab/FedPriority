# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-11-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually identified speakers from the go-around (economic assessment) and the policy discussion. Speakers include: Poole, Broaddus, Moskow, Parry, McTeer, Minehan, Guynn, Jordan, Stern, Rivlin, Boehne, Hoenig, McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Meyer, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 18 participants.

### Era Context
November 1998: The U.S. economy is in the late-1990s expansion. The Asian financial crisis has been ongoing; LTCM collapsed in September 1998; Russia defaulted in August 1998. The Fed had already cut rates twice in intermeeting moves (September and October 1998) by 25bp each, bringing the funds rate to 5%. Financial markets had been in turmoil but were beginning to recover. The domestic economy remained surprisingly resilient despite global stress. This meeting resulted in a third 25bp cut to 4.75% with a move to a symmetric directive.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint, well contained. Greenspan: "underlying inflationary forces are continuing to recede despite the tightness in labor markets," core PCE "barely more than 1 percent," unit labor costs "less than 1-1/2 percent." Moskow: "Price pressures remain benign despite tight labor markets." Parry: "inflation in the core CPI to average 2-1/4 percent." Gramlich: "signs of acceleration of inflation still are not much more visible now than they were" despite unemployment below 5% for seven quarters. Meyer: "inflation will be well contained." Multiple participants note subdued inflation. Direction: clearly below target / benign. Score should be in the 30-40 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as having been running above potential but now transitioning toward or slightly above potential. Poole: "output has overshot full employment and the labor market has been pushed temporarily beyond its long-run sustainable capacity." Minehan: "our forecast puts us about at potential for the fourth quarter and for 1999." Meyer: "growth will be clearly below trend next year." Greenspan notes the economy "is in fact doing reasonably well" but moving to "a slower path." The center of gravity is that the economy is at or modestly above potential, with growth expected to slow to or below trend. The bright-line rule: Poole says "overshot full employment" (above potential), but many expect deceleration to near potential. Current level is modestly above. Score: 53-57.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very tight labor markets are a near-unanimous observation. Poole: "unfilled jobs and the difficulty in finding people to fill certain job positions." Broaddus: "the number who still emphasize the tightness of labor markets." Moskow: "tight labor markets" with "no indication that the labor market is facing significant layoffs." Jordan: "severe shortage of people." Hoenig: "District labor markets are still very tight. More than half of our Beigebook and business contacts are talking about raising wages more than what they consider normal." Greenspan: "very little evidence that those markets are tightening further at this time, but I also would not want to argue that we are seeing any significant labor market easing." Direction: clearly tight, unemployment below 5% for 7 quarters. Score: 65-70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending remains strong/resilient but investment is showing signs of weakening. Broaddus: "Consumer spending... considerably stronger than anticipated. Automobile sales and outlays for other durable goods were especially robust." Rivlin: "Consumers are spending cheerfully, buying houses and cars." Ferguson: "Business fixed investment fell in the last quarter at about a 1 percent annual rate." Greenspan: "Consumer markets continue to be strong" but "capital appropriations... scaling back." Net position: consumption above trend, investment weakening. Overall modestly above trend but decelerating. Score: 55-60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions had tightened dramatically during the LTCM/Russia crisis but have eased somewhat since the intermeeting cuts, though not back to normal. Moskow: "although credit spreads have narrowed since our intermeeting rate cut, financial markets remain jittery and spreads could easily climb again." Greenspan: "yield spreads and risk aversion, which had been quite substantial through October... have eased quite measurably in the last several weeks, have stopped easing." Gramlich: "risk spreads are still high." Broaddus: "Risk premia, of course, are still higher than they were before August." McDonough: "weakness in fixed income markets... still a potentially serious risk." Credit is flowing but via banks rather than capital markets. Conditions are somewhat tight, improving but not normal. Score: 58-63.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Chairman recommends a 25bp cut with symmetric directive (signaling a pause). A majority supports this. Several participants (Broaddus, Stern, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig, Parry) prefer no action (alternative B). Minehan and Poole support the cut reluctantly. Kelley supports "unequivocally" but sympathizes with the hold camp. The action is dovish (another cut) but the symmetric directive signals the easing cycle is ending. This is a moderate dovish lean -- majority for one more ease but signaling done. Score: 35-40.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents, but significant division. Approximately 6-7 participants (Broaddus, Stern, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig, Parry) preferred holding, while others supported the cut. Minehan, Poole, and Kelley supported with notable reluctance. This is a closely divided committee. Score: 40-48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability/conditions are a prominent topic throughout. Nearly every participant discusses financial market conditions, credit spreads, risk aversion, capital market dysfunction, stock market valuations, Brazil risk, year-end pressures. Greenspan devotes substantial attention to it. Hoenig: "a bubble economy syndrome may be building." Kelley worries about stock market boom/crash. This is a moderate-to-prominent topic. Score: 60-68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The move to symmetric directive explicitly signals a pause in easing. Greenspan: "move down and go symmetric... putting policy on indefinite hold." Meyer: "The move to a symmetric directive signals the pause that I believe is in order." Ferguson: "symmetry is an important internal signal that we are prepared to go either way." Poole wants to signal "our easing is finished for the time being." The forward signal is roughly neutral -- done easing for now, data-dependent. Score: 48-52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple participants stress international risks (Brazil, Japan), financial market fragility, difficulty of forecasting. Moskow: "contacts remain more nervous and uncertain about the future." Minehan: "uncertainty seems to occupy a larger proportion of anecdotal conversations." Greenspan puts Brazil risk at "close to 50/50." Kelley: "downside risks... could burgeon much more quickly, perhaps severely and without much warning." But this is not extreme/unprecedented uncertainty -- it's elevated with identifiable risk scenarios. Score: 50-58.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (35): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says benign/receding. 35 < 50. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP (55): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says modestly above potential. 55 > 50. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (67): Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. 67 > 50. Matches.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (57): Higher = stronger. Evidence says consumer strong, investment weakening, net above trend. 57 > 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (60): Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions still tight though improving from crisis levels. 60 > 50. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (37): Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says majority supports 25bp cut. 37 < 50. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50): Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says neutral/symmetric/pause. 50 = neutral. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (67): Both above 50. Labor tightness is notably higher because participants universally describe extremely tight labor markets while the output gap is moderating. This divergence is consistent with the late-90s productivity boom where tight labor did not generate proportional overheating. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (37) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50): The current action is dovish (cut), but forward signal is neutral (symmetric/pause). This divergence is explicitly stated by multiple participants: "complete the adjustment" then "put policy on indefinite hold." Coherent and well-explained.

### Speaker Count by Policy Preference
- **Support 25bp cut + symmetric**: Rivlin, McTeer, Moskow, McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Meyer, Kelley, Boehne, Greenspan (~10)
- **Reluctant support for cut**: Poole, Minehan (~2)
- **Prefer no action (hold)**: Broaddus, Parry, Stern, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig (~6)
- No formal dissents recorded.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the inflation rate is still falling... the real funds rate is rising" and "unit labor costs have been rising at a rate of less than 1-1/2 percent... total unit costs... increasing at a shade above 1 percent"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "We have had steady unemployment at below 5 percent for seven quarters now, and the signs of acceleration of inflation still are not much more visible now than they were then"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Price pressures remain benign despite tight labor markets"
- MR. PARRY: "inflation in the core CPI to average 2-1/4 percent both this year and next"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly view inflation as well contained and possibly still declining, despite very tight labor markets. Productivity gains and international disinflationary forces are keeping prices in check.

**Minority views**: Meyer notes he is "a bit more concerned about the size and inflationary effect of the depreciation of the dollar" and "a little less optimistic about the course of nominal wage change." Hoenig's forecast shows "a less favorable inflation outlook." These push the uncertainty band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. POOLE: "My sense is that output has overshot full employment and that the labor market has been pushed temporarily beyond its long-run sustainable capacity"
- MS. MINEHAN: "our forecast puts us about at potential for the fourth quarter and for 1999"
- MR. MEYER: "growth will be clearly below trend next year" (but currently above)
- MR. GUYNN: "the U.S. economy has considerable resiliency and remains relatively strong"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential currently, with widespread expectation of deceleration toward or below trend in 1999. The current position is above potential but the gap is narrowing.

**Minority views**: Some (Broaddus, Hoenig) see growth remaining closer to trend rather than falling below it. Minehan's forecast has the economy near potential already.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "We are suffering from a severe shortage of people... there just are not enough people" and construction workers "complaining that they're not getting their usual time off for deer hunting"
- MR. HOENIG: "District labor markets are still very tight. More than half of our Beigebook and business contacts are talking about raising wages more than what they consider normal"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the number who still emphasize the tightness of labor markets and the difficulty they are having finding qualified people at all levels"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "very little evidence that those markets are tightening further at this time, but I also would not want to argue that we are seeing any significant labor market easing"

**Center-of-gravity**: Nearly every participant reports very tight labor markets with difficulty finding workers, unfilled positions, and wage pressures. Unemployment has been below 5% for seven quarters. However, Greenspan notes tightening has stabilized.

**Minority views**: Narrow band -- virtually all agree labor markets are very tight. Some note manufacturing layoffs (Minehan mentions Gillette, Raytheon, Pratt & Whitney), but these are sector-specific, not economy-wide.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "Consumer spending in October in particular was considerably stronger than anticipated. Automobile sales and outlays for other durable goods were especially robust"
- MS. RIVLIN: "Consumers are spending cheerfully, buying houses and cars and maintaining a fairly robust outlook"
- MR. FERGUSON: "Business fixed investment fell in the last quarter at about a 1 percent annual rate... even investment in producers' durable equipment barely budged"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Consumer markets continue to be strong" but "there is a scaling back of capital appropriations"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is notably above trend and resilient, but business fixed investment has turned negative. The net position is modestly above trend, weighted toward consumer strength but with a drag from weakening investment.

**Minority views**: The wider uncertainty band reflects the divergence between strong consumption and weakening investment. If investment deteriorates further, the score could fall; if consumer resilience persists, it stays elevated.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "yield spreads and risk aversion, which had been quite substantial through October and have eased quite measurably in the last several weeks, have stopped easing. In fact, there actually has been some minor upturn"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "risk spreads are still high, and the real federal funds rate is still high also. I think the period of the credit crunch is not entirely behind us"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "weakness in fixed income markets, although somewhat reduced, is still a potentially serious risk to the real economy"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Financial markets have improved from their earlier unstable condition, but they are not yet back to normal"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat tight -- improved substantially from the crisis peak in September/October but still notably tighter than pre-August 1998 norms. Risk spreads remain elevated, capital markets are not fully functional, and bank lending has surged to fill the gap.

**Minority views**: Jordan reports his district contacts found no credit crunch; small business lending remained competitive. This contrasts with money center bank stress described by McDonough and Greenspan.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I see very little risk in moving down another 25 basis points today, provided we indicate that we are on hold... move down and go symmetric"
- MR. MEYER: "another quarter point move would complete what I think would be an appropriate adjustment to the discrete change in the outlook"
- MR. BROADDUS (opposing): "I just respectfully do not believe that either of those two arguments is very compelling today... I would have a strong preference for alternative B"
- MS. MINEHAN (reluctant): "I am not going to dissent because I think it's a close call... I will support your proposal to ease, but I am nervous about it"

**Center-of-gravity**: Majority supports a 25bp cut as insurance against downside risks, framed as completing the easing cycle. The symmetric directive signals the end of easing. About 6 participants prefer holding. The lean is moderately dovish for one more cut, but the forward posture is neutral.

**Minority views**: The hold camp (Broaddus, Parry, Stern, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig) is substantial -- roughly a third of participants. Several supporters express reluctance. The uncertainty band is relatively wide.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would have a strong preference for alternative B" (hold)
- MR. HOENIG: "my preference would be to wait... However, like others, I am not going to dissent"
- MR. POOLE: "I will support your recommendation, but with considerable reluctance. I am concerned that we are pouring gasoline rather than water onto this economy"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting clearly in favor of waiting... I will support your proposal to ease, but I am nervous about it"

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissents, but approximately 6 participants preferred holding and at least 3 more were reluctant supporters. The committee was closely divided, with the Chairman's persuasion carrying the day. The disagreement extends to economic assessment (whether risks are truly skewed to the downside) not just tactics.

**Minority views**: N/A -- the score captures the dispersion itself.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Nearly every participant addresses financial market conditions, credit spreads, capital market dysfunction, stock market valuations
- MR. HOENIG: "I have concerns that a bubble economy syndrome may be building"
- MR. KELLEY: "I worry more today about the potential for very serious mischief that could arise in the stock market if it should boom to new highs. That could put us in severe jeopardy of a crash"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The concerns about an asset bubble are not without validity, and that is where I have my greatest concerns about easing"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions are a prominent topic -- discussed at length by most participants. Topics include credit market dysfunction, risk spreads, stock market valuation/bubble concerns, Brazil contagion risk, year-end funding pressures. This rivals the economic assessment in emphasis for many speakers.

**Minority views**: Narrow band -- nearly all devote significant attention to financial conditions.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "move down and go symmetric... putting policy on indefinite hold... stay on hold, and watch events as they materialize over the weeks and possibly even months ahead"
- MR. MEYER: "The move to a symmetric directive signals the pause that I believe is in order after the move"
- MR. POOLE: "I would hope that... you could indicate to the market that it looks as if our easing is finished for the time being"
- MR. FERGUSON: "symmetry is an important internal signal that we are prepared to go either way if the facts so indicate"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is explicitly neutral. The symmetric directive after cutting signals the easing cycle is complete. Multiple participants frame this as the final cut. The committee is data-dependent going forward, prepared to move in either direction. This is a textbook neutral forward guidance.

**Minority views**: McDonough hints at possible year-end emergency action if needed. Some hold-camp members imply rates might need to come back up. But the dominant signal is neutral pause.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "contacts remain more nervous and uncertain about the future than they were in mid-summer"
- MS. MINEHAN: "uncertainty seems to occupy a larger proportion of anecdotal conversations"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I judge the risks here [Brazil] at close to 50/50... maintaining the Brazilian exchange rate is going to be very difficult"
- MR. KELLEY: "downside risks... could burgeon much more quickly, perhaps severely and without much warning"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated, driven by international risks (Brazil, Japan), financial market fragility, and difficulty predicting the path of the domestic economy. Multiple participants explicitly note heightened uncertainty among their contacts. However, the domestic economy is viewed with reasonable clarity -- the uncertainty is concentrated on international and financial channels.

**Minority views**: Some participants (Stern, Broaddus) are relatively more confident in the domestic outlook's resilience.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: 35
Multiple participants note weak/declining goods prices. Moskow: "auto industry is negotiating a 3 percent reduction in steel prices." Greenspan: "manufacturing... prices have been weakening quite measurably." Steel imports doubling market share, commodity prices falling. Goods deflation evident.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A
Not prominently discussed as a separate category by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A
Greenspan discusses housing in the CPI/PCE weighting context (technical measurement), but participants do not discuss housing inflation pressure as a substantive concern.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 45
Parry: "price inflation continues to benefit from low inflation expectations." Greenspan notes the real funds rate is rising because inflation is falling. Expectations appear anchored slightly below target. Not a major topic but mentioned by 2+ participants.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: 62
Greenspan: "the presumption that we are going to see a gradual return to full and fluid capital markets is only a hope at this stage." McDonough: "weakness in fixed income markets... still a potentially serious risk." Spreads still elevated, capital markets not fully functional, banks absorbing spillover. Improving but still stressed.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: 30
Concern is about overvaluation/bubble, not crash/undervaluation. Greenspan: "concerns about an asset bubble are not without validity." Hoenig: "a bubble economy syndrome may be building." Kelley: "the stock market... could boom to new highs... severe jeopardy of a crash." The concern is about excessive valuation and what it could lead to. Lower score = more bubble/overvaluation concern.

**BANKING_STRESS**: 40
Banks are absorbing credit demand from capital markets but with some strain. Greenspan: banks "quite unhappy about having to accommodate this additional loan demand." McDonough notes year-end pressure on money center banks. But banks are functioning, lending actively. Modest stress, not severe.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A
Not specifically discussed as Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A
Dollar mentioned in passing (depreciation as a positive for growth, Meyer notes inflationary effect), but no substantive concern about dollar levels by 2+ participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 68
Multiple participants note strong productivity growth. Moskow: "productivity growth has been strong enough to largely offset" wage gains. Parry: "higher trend growth in productivity." Greenspan: "output per hour for nonfinancial corporations" has accelerated alongside compensation. Productivity is viewed as above historical trend.
