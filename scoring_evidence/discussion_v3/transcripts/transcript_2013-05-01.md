### 2. Era Context
May 2013 is mid-QE3 era (the open-ended $85B/month program launched September 2012). The economy is in a moderate recovery from the Great Recession — still well below potential, unemployment elevated (~7.6%), inflation running significantly below the 2% PCE target (~1.0% on core PCE). The "taper tantrum" has not yet occurred; this meeting is largely about whether and when to begin tapering QE3. The era anchor for INFLATION_PRESSURE is 38–42, and for OUTPUT_GAP is 35–42. These are consistent with what the text actually says.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "Inflation falling further below our target and fiscal austerity that is worse than we expected provide ample reasons to strongly prefer alternative B to alternative C...the miss on inflation is getting quite large."
- Bullard: "the headline PCE inflation rate measured from one year earlier is approaching 1 percent, as is the associated core measure. These are particularly low readings."
- Evans: "year-over-year PCE inflation is 1.0 percent. Judged against our 2 percent long-run objective—how would we feel if inflation were 3 percent?"
- Bernanke summary: "inflation remains soft, with headline and core PCE inflation most recently near 1 percent."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimously, participants observe PCE inflation at ~1.0%, well below the 2% target, with several expressing concern about the downward trend since early 2012.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Pianalto see current low readings as partly temporary (one-time commodity price shifts) and note stability in inflation expectations; Pianalto argues CPI measures are "relatively stable" and she is "not more concerned about disinflation." These views widen the upper bound slightly but do not change the central score.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Dudley: "my expectation...is that the contractionary fiscal impulse will dominate over the near term...Economic growth will disappoint."
- Yellen: "the employment gap currently stands at about 4.2 million, or 1.7 percent of the population. By this dual mandate metric, we are falling very short of meeting our maximum employment mandate."
- Bernanke summary: "the outlook remains for moderate economic growth with slowly improving conditions. Somewhat more resilient private-sector demand, including housing, is being offset, to some degree, by fiscal restraint."
- Lacker: "we've seen several short-lived spurts in economic growth over the last four years, and at this point, it looks to me as if we're fluctuating around a slow growth trend."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants uniformly describe an economy below potential, with fiscal drag (sequestration), weak business investment, and moderate growth around 2%, though private-sector resilience (housing, durables) provides some offset.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Bullard are somewhat more optimistic (expecting ~3% growth for 2013), and Powell strips out "one-timers" to argue underlying growth is stronger. This lifts the uncertainty band slightly upward.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "the overall shortfall of employment from its maximum sustainable level, the employment gap, currently stands at about 4.2 million, or 1.7 percent of the population."
- Rosengren: "unemployment well above full employment...only modest improvement in the unemployment rate that is, to a large degree, reflecting not the strength of labor markets but the weakness in labor force participation."
- Kocherlakota: "unemployment will remain elevated for even longer than [two to three years]."
- Bernanke summary: "labor gap may be higher than the unemployment gap...job growth has been slow and somewhat volatile. Unemployment has come down more quickly than expected...lower unemployment, in part, reflects lower participation rates."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** With unemployment at 7.6% and broad measures of labor underutilization elevated, participants universally describe a labor market that is improving but still substantially below full employment, with "substantial improvement" threshold not yet met.

**Minority views:** Pianalto argues that given declining trend labor force participation, a 168,000/month pace "should perhaps be considered as substantial." Powell and Stein note significant improvement since September. These push the uncertainty band upward modestly but do not change the broad consensus of continued slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Lockhart: "businesses remain very cautious about expansion-oriented investment and adding to payrolls."
- Tarullo: "capital expenditures now seem to be leveling off as they return to something approaching their historical averages...a lot of large businesses are saying that they're essentially done with investment until they see enough additional demand."
- Fisher: "enterprise demand, as I like to call it—that is, non-consumer-driven demand—remains weak."
- Bernanke summary: "investment is leveling off, and much of it is directed at increasing productivity and reducing costs...Housing is a source of growth."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and housing show resilience (housing notably strong), but business investment is leveling off and enterprise demand is weak; the net position is moderately below trend, with the housing bright spot partially offsetting cautious business spending.

**Minority views:** Plosser notes Q1 consumer spending grew at 3.2% (surprisingly healthy). Bernanke sees "private domestic final demand, that is, C plus I, has been growing pretty rapidly over the past six months." This pushes the uncertainty band upward.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Stein: "credit conditions are very accommodative. I think the 15-year mortgage just hit an all-time low. We continue to see record lending volumes in leverage lending...For C&I lending, the percentages reporting easing their terms or the spreads are really near record levels."
- Bernanke: "financial conditions are easier. That's shown by many indicators, including financial conditions indexes from the Chicago Fed and other Federal Reserve Banks."
- Evans (reporting contacts): "no one indicated that financial conditions were worse; by all accounts, our accommodative policies are continuing to facilitate business activity."
- Fisher: "quantitative easing is unambiguously inflating asset prices and, according to some, is distorting financial market functionality."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, with record-low mortgage rates, near-record C&I lending easing, cov-lite issuance approaching 2007 peaks, and triple-C market access at highs — participants describe an environment of easy credit and compressed spreads, with concern focused on the risk-taking implications rather than stress or tightness.

**Minority views:** Bernanke notes "mortgage supply conditions are still tight" via FHA/GSE tightening of standards; Lacker mentions consumers unable to access credit as freely as during the boom. These push the uncertainty band slightly upward (toward neutral).

**Minority views:** Fisher, Plosser, George each have significant reservations about the program's costs, and Plosser/George explicitly prefer beginning to taper. This dissenting minority pushes the uncertainty band upward.
