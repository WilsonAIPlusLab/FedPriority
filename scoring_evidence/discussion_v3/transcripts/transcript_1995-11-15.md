# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-11-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker statements from a go-around and subsequent policy discussion. Speakers identified by name include: Parry, Jordan, Boehne, Moskow, Stern, Minehan, Broaddus, Lindsey, Melzer, McTeer, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, Yellen, Hoenig, Greenspan. Approximately 17 participants speak.

### Era Context
November 1995: Mid-1990s expansion, roughly six years into a recovery. The fed funds rate is at 5.75% after a July 1995 cut of 25bp. Inflation running around 2.75-3% CPI. Unemployment at 5.5%. The government shutdown ("train wreck") is underway. Strong Q3 GDP surprised to the upside. This is a mature expansion with debate about whether policy is too restrictive.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as slightly below the midpoint of concern. The recent CPI news has been "favorable" (Parry), with core PPI "unchanged" and ECI growing at "a mere 2.3 percent" (Yellen). Most participants forecast CPI around 3%, which is roughly where it has been. Several note "no acceleration of inflation" despite tight labor markets (Stern). Parry and Broaddus warn that no further progress toward price stability is concerning and 3% inflation is not acceptable. The center of gravity sees current inflation as contained but not improving -- slightly below the neutral alarm zone. Direction: participants perceive inflation as present but restrained, not alarming. Score should be in the 41-49 range -- inflation not falling meaningfully but not accelerating either.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is described as operating "at a high level" (Melzer, Moskow), with Q3 GDP growth of 4-5% surprising to the upside. Greenspan describes the economy as "as close to middle ground as one ever sees it." Phillips notes "5.5 percent unemployment" and evidence that "NAIRU may be lower than 6 percent." Broaddus says the projected path "may be very close to potential and conceivably even a little above it." Several note recent "sogginess" (Greenspan) and mixed signals. The center of gravity is approximately at potential -- maybe slightly above given the strong Q3 and high utilization. Score should be around 50-53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Moskow reports unemployment rates of 3.3-4.8% in Seventh District with "more reports of rising wage pressures." Stern reports labor markets are "very, very tight across the District and across skill and experience levels." Phillips notes 5.5% unemployment. Minehan reports relatively low unemployment in New England despite weak job growth. But notably, wage inflation has NOT accelerated despite tightness -- Stern: "no real sign of any acceleration in wage increases." The ECI is restrained. The labor market is tight by level but not generating wage pressure. Score should be around 53-58 -- tight but not extremely so, and wage pressures absent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Very mixed signals. Q3 GDP was strong. Business investment especially in computing equipment is "quite robust" (Broaddus). High-tech is booming. Housing is strong in many districts. However, retail sales are weak, auto sales have eased, consumer confidence is low, and consumer debt concerns are prominent (Lindsey detailed analysis, Kelley concurs). Greenspan describes recent economy as "soggy." The net position is roughly at trend -- strong investment offset by cautious consumer spending. Score around 48-52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Stern reports "credit is readily available" with "striking lack of reports of difficulty in finding credit." Phillips notes "stock prices are up; bond prices are up; the yield curve has flattened." Minehan mentions "low interest rates and booming financial markets." Broaddus notes bankers report "loan margins generally had narrowed and competitive pressures had intensified." Kelley says the real funds rate hasn't "put much of a damper on credit extensions or the stock market or the housing market." This points to loose-to-neutral financial conditions. Score around 35-40.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The decision is unanimous for "no change" (alternative B). The debate is whether the directive should be symmetric or asymmetric toward ease. The majority favors symmetric. But multiple participants (Blinder, Lindsey, McDonough, Yellen, Forrestal, Boehne) lean toward easing soon or at the December meeting. Lindsey explicitly argues for a cut today. McDonough says "monetary policy is too tight and therefore has to be adjusted." Jordan says the funds rate has "become increasingly restrictive." Greenspan himself suggests "at some point we should be looking toward a somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996." The hawkish voices (Melzer, Broaddus, Parry, Stern) argue for maintaining current stance to protect inflation gains and credibility. Center of gravity: hold with a lean toward easing. Score around 44-47 -- lean dovish, hold steady with easing bias building.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent on the vote. But there is meaningful disagreement: Lindsey explicitly wants to cut today; McDonough wants asymmetric toward ease; Blinder, Yellen, Forrestal lean dovish. Broaddus, Melzer, Parry, Stern lean hawkish on maintaining stance. The directive debate (symmetric vs. asymmetric) shows genuine disagreement. Score around 30-38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion. Broaddus mentions bankers noting loan quality deterioration and narrowing margins. Lindsey has extensive discussion of consumer debt/balance sheets, but this is more of a macro risk than financial stability per se. Phillips notes "there do not appear to be major imbalances in the real economy or the financial markets." This is a 1990s meeting with minimal financial stability focus. Score around 15-20.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal leans dovish. Greenspan says "at some point we should be looking toward a somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996." Multiple participants expect a rate cut at the December meeting or early 1996. Boehne: "the federal funds rate is on the high side of where we will want it going forward." Jordan argues the funds rate has become "inadvertently more restrictive." The hawkish camp (Melzer, Stern) argues for caution and maintaining restrictive stance. But the overall forward signal is mildly toward easing. Score around 38-42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated uncertainty is a recurring theme. Greenspan: "it's not clear which way it is going." Stern: "I must admit to a fair amount of uncertainty." Yellen: "the level of uncertainty has increased." Minehan notes her staff is evenly split on the next move direction. Broaddus describes conditions as "conflictive." Phillips: "we said that we would know more at this meeting, and now we are all scratching our heads." The government shutdown adds uncertainty. Score around 45-55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (~51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~55): Both slightly above 50, coherent. Labor market is a bit tighter than overall output gap because wage pressure is absent despite low unemployment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~40): Both lean mildly dovish, coherent. The forward signal is slightly more dovish than today's action because multiple participants expect easing in December.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says restrained, around 3%, not accelerating. Score ~44 (slightly below midpoint). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy near potential, maybe slightly above. Score ~52. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Markets are tight but wages restrained. Score ~55. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are loose to neutral. Score ~37. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Center holds with easing bias. Score ~45. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Signal leans toward easing. Score ~40. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "Recent inflation news, as we all know, has been favorable. Although I hope it continues in this vein, it seems to me there are some reasons to worry about whether that will happen."
- MS. YELLEN: "total hourly compensation grew at a mere 2.3 percent in the third quarter" and "core PPI which has remained unchanged"
- MR. STERN: "despite tight labor markets, rapid growth in demand, and tight product markets we have not seen any acceleration of inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants perceive current inflation as restrained at roughly 3% CPI, with favorable recent readings, but most expect no further disinflation and some worry about future risks -- placing perceived pressure slightly below neutral.

**Minority views:** Parry, Broaddus, and Melzer express concern that 3% inflation with no further progress is unacceptable (upside risk). Stern and McTeer are slightly more optimistic on inflation. This informs the +/-7 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "It moves the projected path of the economy away from one that might have seemed to be a bit below the economy's potential to a path that may be very close to potential and conceivably even a little above it."
- MR. MELZER: "the economy continues to operate at a high level, with growth slowing to its 10-year average rate."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the economy seems to be as close to middle ground as one ever sees it"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants view the economy as operating very near potential, perhaps marginally above it given strong Q3 GDP and high utilization rates, but with recent sogginess suggesting no clear overheating.

**Minority views:** Jordan and Lindsey see meaningful softening and downside risks. Phillips and Kelley see upside potential. The range is narrow.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "labor markets are very, very tight across the District and across skill and experience levels" but "no real sign of any acceleration in wage increases or price increases"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Unemployment rates in our District range between 3.3 and 4.8 percent. We are now receiving more reports of rising wage pressures, especially for low skill entry-level jobs."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "At 5.5 percent unemployment, the labor market appears to be a bit better than expected."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets are tight with low unemployment and reports of difficulty finding workers, but the notable absence of wage acceleration keeps the score moderately above neutral rather than in the clearly tight range.

**Minority views:** Jordan reports his District is softening with "much easier to hire than before." This is a regional outlier.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "Business investment, especially in computing equipment but in a lot of other things as well, is still quite robust."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Retailers are disappointed with sales and concerned about the outlook for holiday sales."
- MR. LINDSEY: Extensive analysis of consumer debt buildup -- "40 percent of growth in PCE in the last 2-1/2 years has been financed by higher non-housing consumer debt."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Strong business investment and housing are roughly offset by weak retail sales, softening auto demand, and consumer debt concerns, yielding a net assessment of approximately trend-level spending.

**Minority views:** Lindsey is substantially more pessimistic on consumer spending sustainability. Kelley and Greenspan note the economy still has potential to accelerate. Wide uncertainty band reflects the "conflictive" data.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "credit is readily available. There has been a striking lack of reports of difficulty in finding credit."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Stock prices are up; bond prices are up; the yield curve has flattened during the intermeeting period; the dollar has been steadier."
- MR. KELLEY: "so far it's hard to see where it has put much of a damper on credit extensions or the stock market or the housing market."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Despite a real funds rate that many consider somewhat high, actual financial conditions are loose -- credit is freely available, equity markets are booming, bond yields have fallen, and lending is robust.

**Minority views:** Broaddus reports some bankers see loan quality deteriorating and margins narrowing, suggesting competitive excess rather than tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would recommend that we do nothing" but "at some point we should be looking toward a somewhat lower real funds rate in 1996."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I am sufficiently convinced of the likelihood that monetary policy is too tight and therefore has to be adjusted that I would prefer an asymmetric directive toward easing."
- MR. MELZER: "I favor alternative B... I believe it maintains a stance of policy that is somewhat restrictive... I strongly prefer a symmetric directive."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous support for holding rates, but the Committee is split between those who see an easing ahead (Blinder, Lindsey, McDonough, Yellen, Forrestal, Boehne, Jordan) and those who want to maintain a restrictive posture (Melzer, Broaddus, Parry, Stern). The modal outcome is B symmetric, but with a mild lean toward eventual ease.

**Minority views:** Lindsey explicitly advocates a cut today. Melzer and Broaddus argue strongly against easing signals, citing need to protect anti-inflation credibility.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Lindsey argues for cutting today; most others favor holding.
- The symmetric vs. asymmetric directive debate reflects genuine differences: Blinder, Yellen, McDonough favor asymmetric toward ease; Broaddus, Melzer, Hoenig, Kelley, Phillips, McTeer favor symmetric.
- MR. JORDAN: "while I would like to be arguing in favor of lowering the funds rate I don't have a criterion for doing so and I am stuck"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissent and ultimate agreement on "no change," but notable differences on the direction and timing of the next move, with the committee roughly split on the directive's symmetry, indicating moderate disagreement.

**Minority views:** Lindsey is a clear outlier wanting immediate action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "several bankers expressed the view that loan quality was deteriorating... and loan margins generally had narrowed and competitive pressures had intensified."
- MR. LINDSEY: Extended discussion of consumer debt and household balance sheet risks (macro-focused rather than systemic stability).
- MS. PHILLIPS: "there do not appear to be major imbalances in the real economy or the financial markets."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives only passing attention. Lindsey's consumer debt discussion is an economic risk assessment rather than a systemic stability concern. Only Broaddus's banker reports touch directly on financial conditions quality.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 40 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if this economy fails to pick up and continues to be soggy into the next meeting, the evidence in favor of a move at that time would probably become more compelling than it is today."
- MR. BOEHNE: "the federal funds rate is on the high side of where we will want it going forward and... we will need to adjust it downward within the next several months."
- MR. STERN: "we need to have a discussion... on whether we want to try to move toward lower inflation deliberately as opposed to opportunistically." (hawkish-leaning forward concern)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal leans mildly toward easing. Greenspan himself hints at a December move if the economy remains soft. Multiple participants expect a lower funds rate in coming months. But a meaningful group argues against signaling any easing direction.

**Minority views:** Melzer, Broaddus, and Stern argue that forward signals should not suggest comfort with 3% inflation, pushing back against easing assumptions.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "I must admit to a fair amount of uncertainty."
- MS. YELLEN: "from my perspective the level of uncertainty has increased."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it's not clear which way it is going" and the economy is "soggy" but "I would not rule out a surprising acceleration."
- MS. MINEHAN: Her staff has money wagered on whether the next move is up or down -- illustrating genuine uncertainty about direction.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express elevated uncertainty, driven by conflicting data (strong Q3 GDP vs. recent softness), the government shutdown, unclear consumer outlook, and debate over whether the NAIRU has shifted. Multiple participants explicitly use uncertainty language.
