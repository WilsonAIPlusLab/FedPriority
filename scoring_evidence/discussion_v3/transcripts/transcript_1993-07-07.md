# FOMC Meeting Scoring - 1993-07-07 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the July 6-7, 1993 FOMC meeting. It includes a go-around with individual participant statements on economic conditions (Day 1) and a policy discussion on monetary aggregate ranges plus the short-run policy directive (Day 2). Speakers are identified by name. The final portion discusses leak policy and is not relevant for scoring.

### Era Context
Mid-1993: The U.S. is roughly two years into a slow, jobless recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate is at 3%, extremely low by historical standards. Inflation has recently ticked up after a period of apparent progress. Unemployment remains elevated. Fiscal policy uncertainty looms (Clinton budget/tax plan, health care reform). Europe is in recession. M2 aggregates are running well below target ranges.

### Speaker-by-Speaker Assessment (Center of Gravity)

**Economy / Output:**
- McTeer: Southwest slowing relative to nation, still moving forward. Credit crunch still alive. Uncertainty from NAFTA, taxes.
- Boehne: District mixed, growth slower than nation. Manufacturing deteriorating. Retailers doing somewhat better. Residential construction positive. Risks on the down side.
- Hoenig: District continuing modest growth. Manufacturing sluggish due to uncertainty. Credit improving. National forecast slightly above Greenbook.
- Stern: Very modest recovery likely to continue. No disagreement with central tendency.
- Melzer: District strong. National projection at high end. Stimulative policy showing through.
- Kelley: No GDP growth engine visible, but underlying dynamism of American economy may provide lift once uncertainty clears.
- Mullins: Still sees moderate growth (2.5-3%). Auto sales strong. Housing recovery possible. Employment showing steady growth (1 million jobs in first half). Downgraded from 3-3.5% to 2.5-3%.
- Syron: New England softening. Manufacturing mixed. Risks centered but could go down.
- LaWare: Primary risks on the down side. Consumers cautious. Worried about stagflation.
- Angell: Pushes back on pessimism. Nominal GDP may be stronger than staff suggests.
- Phillips: Return to 2.5% moderate growth pattern. Considerable job creation but barely keeping up with demographics. Uncertainties remain.
- Lindsey: Risks on downside for real economy due to fiscal drag ($37B drag in FY94). Risks on upside for inflation.
- Forrestal (referenced): Growth concerns, risks on downside.

**Center of gravity on output:** Slow-to-moderate growth (~2.5%), below potential, with significant uncertainty and downside risks. Multiple participants note manufacturing weakness, fiscal drag, and uncertainty as headwinds. No one describes the economy as booming or even at potential. This is a below-potential, slack economy.

**Inflation:**
- Boehne: No upward price pressures currently. Wage increases around 4% higher than expected. Further inflation gains will be tough.
- Stern: No more progress on inflation likely. Expectations not working in Fed's favor. Greatest concern.
- Melzer: Stimulative policy showing through to prices. Inflation could be higher than projected.
- Mullins: Detailed inflation data review - core CPI averaged ~4% for 8 months, ECI accelerated to 4.2%, fixed-weight index bottomed at 2.2% and rose to 4.3%. Bond market had two inflation-fear episodes. Recent data (May) somewhat better. "Long blip" in broadly-based inflation measures.
- Hoenig: Without policy change, inflation could be somewhat higher than Greenbook.
- Syron: Business contacts expect 3-3.5% inflation as norm. Deeply held conviction that inflation has a floor around 2.5-3%.
- Angell: Conditions favorable for inflation reduction but we're getting 3.3%. Monetary policy has missed its restraining force. Gold price up 20% since February.
- Phillips: Additional inflation gains extremely hard to come by. Deceleration in labor costs leveling. Inflation expectations ratcheting up.
- Lindsey: Central tendency is 3.1% but risks are to upside. Staff raised forecast 0.7 points. Michigan survey up 0.6.
- LaWare: Worried about revisions pointing toward stagflation.

**Center of gravity on inflation:** Inflation is perceived as modestly above where they want it, in the 3-3.5% range, with upside risks. Multiple participants note inflation expectations are sticky and firmly held. Several measures have accelerated. However, participants do not describe this as an alarm-level situation -- it's "not a problem" to many contacts, but it IS a problem to the Committee. This is modestly elevated, with the committee consensus around CPI of 3-3.5%.

**Labor Market:**
- Phillips: Job creation enough to lower unemployment rate in theory but barely keeping up with demographics so unemployment hasn't gone down.
- Mullins: Employment showing steady growth, ~161K jobs/month in Q2, 1 million in first half of 1993. Gradual improvement.
- Boehne: Employment increased but not greatly. Wage increases around 4%.
- LaWare: People anxious about jobs, no new job opportunities visible.
- Multiple participants: Restructuring, layoffs, defense cutbacks, health care cutbacks.

**Center of gravity on labor:** Employment is growing but slowly, not enough to meaningfully reduce unemployment. The labor market has slack -- people are anxious about jobs, layoffs continue across sectors. This is below full employment but improving.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Scoring

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Multiple measures (CPI, ECI, fixed-weight index) have accelerated. The staff raised its forecast by 0.7pp. Expectations are sticky at 3-3.5%. However, this is not extreme -- participants describe it as frustrating stickiness rather than runaway inflation.
Magnitude: "Somewhat" above where they want it, with concern about staying power. The 3-3.5% CPI range is clearly above the implicit ~2% goal many have. This is in the 55-62 range.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation has accelerated and is sticky above desired levels. Score above 50. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Below potential. The economy is growing at 2.5% -- slow-to-moderate. Slack remains. Manufacturing is weak. Unemployment has not come down meaningfully. Multiple participants see downside risks.
Magnitude: This is not a deep recession but well below potential. "Slow," "modest," "sluggish" language. Several participants note continued slack. 35-42 range.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score < 50. Matches.
Anchor sanity: 1993 slow recovery fits 35-42 anchor for 2010-14 recovery, similar dynamics.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Below full employment. Unemployment elevated, not coming down. Layoffs continuing. Anxiety about jobs. But employment IS growing steadily.
Magnitude: Below full employment but not deeply so. ~35-40.
Cross-check with OUTPUT_GAP: Both below 50. Coherent.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Below trend to moderate. Auto sales strong (but described as replacement necessity). Housing recovering. Capital spending has been strong but may decelerate due to tax uncertainty. Consumers cautious. Retailers somewhat mixed.
Magnitude: "Moderate" pace, "sluggish" in some areas, some pockets of strength. Net assessment is slightly below trend. ~42-45.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Conditions are somewhat loose to mixed. Fed funds rate at 3% is very accommodative. Long rates at record lows at times. Credit crunch still mentioned by some (McTeer, Boehne) but improving. Banks actively marketing loans. Mutual fund inflows massive. Low mortgage rates (lowest in 21 years).
Magnitude: Very low short rates, record low long rates at times, but credit crunch still lingering. Overall quite accommodative. ~28-35.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose. Score below 50. Matches.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The overwhelming consensus is to hold steady (no change in fed funds rate) with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Most participants support this. However, there is notable hawkish sentiment:
- Angell: Wants immediate 25bp hike. Fed funds rate "way too low."
- Jordan: Policies not moving toward long-term objective, should correct.
- Melzer: Need to be prepared to move promptly. Very stimulative policy.
- Mullins: Inflation data concerning but recent data weakens case for immediate action.
- Several others: Prefer symmetry but accept asymmetry.
- Forrestal, LaWare, McTeer: Downside risks, cautious.
The center of gravity is hold with asymmetric bias toward tightening. This is a lean-hawkish hold. ~52-55.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with tightening bias, 2 participants wanting immediate hikes. Score slightly above 50. Matches.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Moderate disagreement. The policy decision itself is broadly supported (hold steady, asymmetric). But there are notable divisions:
- Angell and Jordan clearly want to tighten now.
- Several participants (Forrestal, Hoenig, Oltman, Keehn) prefer symmetry but accept asymmetry.
- On M2 ranges, substantial debate but ultimately a technical issue.
- Views on inflation risks vs. growth risks diverge (Lindsey's "stagflation" framing vs. Mullins' moderate optimism).
Magnitude: No formal dissents recorded in the policy vote shown, but Angell and Jordan clearly dissented in spirit. The go-around showed a range from pessimism (LaWare, Lindsey) to relative optimism (Angell on growth, Kelley). ~35-42.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Limited. Some mentions: Syron notes concern about money flowing into mutual funds and what happens when there's a correction. Angell warns about driving savers into stocks and junk bonds. Phillips mentions anomalous inflation expectations not showing up in asset shifts. But this is not a major theme.
Magnitude: A few scattered references, not prominent. ~15-20.
Anchor sanity: 1993 typical = 1-10. These scattered mentions warrant slightly above that.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: Mildly hawkish forward. The asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten. Multiple participants emphasize the need to be vigilant on inflation. Melzer: "we need to be prepared to move promptly." Greenspan frames the asymmetry as not presupposing action but keeping the option open. Lindsey: "the tough call is only going to become more difficult as time goes on."
Magnitude: Lean toward tightening but no commitment. Data-dependent. ~55-60.
Cross-check with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Both slightly above 50. Coherent.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Elevated. Uncertainty is a MAJOR theme. Nearly every participant mentions uncertainty from: fiscal policy (budget, taxes), health care reform, NAFTA, floods, European recession, M2 breakdown, inflation dynamics. Boehne: "the uncertainties that are out there do put the risks on the down side." Kelley: "uncertainty and perceptions are very, very dominant." Phillips: "The uncertainties that remain are really quite considerable." The M2 discussion is essentially about fundamental uncertainty regarding monetary aggregates.
Magnitude: "Very, very dominant" (Kelley), "quite considerable" (Phillips), multiple participants emphasize it. This is elevated but not extreme/Knightian. ~50-58.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "for eight months now, since the end of the third quarter, core CPI has averaged roughly 4 percent versus about 2-1/2 percent for the previous six months... Both the implicit price deflator and the fixed weight index have accelerated over the last couple of quarters"
- MR. LINDSEY: "The staff has raised its forecast for 1993 inflation by seven-tenths. On average at this table we've raised ours five-tenths. And the Michigan survey is up six-tenths."
- MR. STERN: "I also agree with those who said that it doesn't look as if there's going to be a lot of further progress in bringing down the rate of inflation... That is my greatest concern."
- MR. SYRON: Business contacts have "a deeply held conviction that the American economy has some inflationary bias built in of around 3 percent."

Center-of-gravity summary: Participants perceive inflation as modestly above desired levels (~3-3.5% CPI vs. implicit goal of ~2%), with multiple measures having accelerated over recent quarters, sticky expectations, and limited prospect for further improvement. However, this is not alarm-level -- it is frustrating persistence rather than acceleration into dangerous territory.

Minority views: Angell sees this as evidence monetary policy has been too loose (more hawkish read). Some contacts of participants see inflation as "not a problem" (less concerned). The range is relatively narrow on assessment, broader on prescription.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "I still see moderate growth... at a rate of perhaps 2-1/2 to 3 percent... where perhaps before I saw 3 to 3-1/2 percent."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "this first-quarter slowdown we've had seems to be a return to a pattern of slower growth... 2-1/2 percent rate that we've averaged over the last 18 months."
- MR. ANGELL: "We've existed in a world in 1993 in which use of resources is way down."
- MR. BOEHNE: "The District economy continues to be mixed... its growth has been slower than the nation's all along."

Center-of-gravity summary: The economy is growing at a slow-to-moderate pace (~2.5%), clearly below potential with slack remaining. The recovery is now nine consecutive quarters old but has not closed the output gap. Manufacturing is weak and multiple headwinds persist (fiscal drag, uncertainty).

Minority views: Melzer's district is strong and his national forecast is at the high end. Angell pushes back on pessimism. But the majority sees slow growth with downside risks.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "There has been considerable job creation; we have had enough job creation to lower the unemployment rate over the last year, but we're just barely keeping up with the demographics, so the unemployment rate hasn't gone down."
- MR. MULLINS: "employment has shown steady growth despite the weak June numbers. We've seen more jobs created in the first half of 1993 than in all of 1992, roughly a million jobs."
- MR. LAWARE: "People are anxious about jobs, and they don't see any developments... that will generate new job opportunities."
- MR. BOEHNE: "people are as much if not more concerned about their own jobs, their own companies, their own futures"

Center-of-gravity summary: The labor market is below full employment with meaningful slack. Job creation is positive but slow, barely keeping up with demographics. Unemployment is not declining. Workers are anxious and restructuring/layoffs continue. Wage increases around 4% are somewhat puzzling given slack.

Minority views: Mullins is more upbeat about the improving employment trend. Most participants see labor slack as persistent.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "On consumer spending, I as yet see no compelling evidence that we're falling off the path of sustained moderate growth. Auto sales continue to be quite strong... business spending has been quite strong."
- MR. LAWARE: "I think auto sales are an aberration caused by the advanced age of the auto inventory... It's a necessity kind of move."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "There are some real areas of strength or potential strength: autos, housing, durables, capital equipment. But... the focus is on productivity and efficiency; it's not as much on expansion."
- MR. BOEHNE: Manufacturing sector showing "noticeable deterioration... cut significantly into capital spending plans." Retailers doing somewhat better.

Center-of-gravity summary: Spending is mixed -- autos strong (possibly replacement-driven), housing recovering with lowest mortgage rates in 21 years, capital equipment spending solid but narrowly focused on efficiency. Consumers are cautious due to uncertainty. Manufacturing investment plans being cut. Net position is slightly below trend.

Minority views: Mullins is more optimistic about consumption durability. LaWare sees consumer caution as dominant.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "We have the lowest mortgage rates in 21 years." Bond market long rate had fallen to "new record lows."
- MR. MCTEER: "the credit crunch was still alive and well... banks going out and aggressively marketing loans now, I don't have a lot of indications that many more loans are being made."
- MR. ANGELL: "We have a low interest rate... we now have a fed funds rate that is just clearly out of sight in regard to world reasonableness."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "at some point we're going to have to address negative real rates. But it doesn't appear to me that at this time they are causing particular harm."

Center-of-gravity summary: Financial conditions are broadly loose -- record-low long rates, 3% fed funds rate (negative in real terms), massive mutual fund inflows. However, a lingering credit crunch is noted by several participants, with banks trying but struggling to make loans. The net picture is accommodative overall with some residual bank lending tightness.

Minority views: McTeer and Boehne emphasize the still-present credit crunch. Most others focus on the extremely low rate environment.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 53 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Proposes hold with asymmetric directive toward tightening. "The general consensus appears to be 'B,' asymmetric."
- MR. ANGELL: "I don't think this is a very good time to increase interest rates. But, unfortunately, our interest rates are way too low... I would be satisfied to do a measly 1/4 percentage point."
- MR. JORDAN: "our policies are not at the moment moving us in the direction of our long-term objective. We ought to correct that."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I would certainly support your prescription with respect to no change in policy... I have a preference for symmetry."
- MR. MELZER: "I support what you say... we really need to be watching things pretty carefully here and that we need to be prepared to move promptly."

Center-of-gravity summary: The strong consensus is to hold the fed funds rate at 3% with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Two participants (Angell, Jordan) explicitly favor tightening now. Several others (Melzer, Mullins, Lindsey, Phillips) signal that tightening will be needed at some point. A few (Forrestal, Hoenig, Oltman, Keehn) would prefer symmetry but accept asymmetry. No one advocates easing. This is a lean-hawkish hold.

Minority views: Angell and Jordan are clearly more hawkish (wanting immediate action). Forrestal and a few others lean more neutral/balanced but not dovish.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. ANGELL: "I'm clearly not in tune with the other members of the FOMC. I don't see any need to wait for any information." (Dissents from hold)
- MR. JORDAN: "our policies are not at the moment moving us in the direction of our long-term objective." (Also dissents from hold)
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I have a preference for symmetry" (mild disagreement on tilt direction)
- Multiple participants (Hoenig, Oltman, Keehn): "slight preference for symmetry" but accept asymmetry.

Center-of-gravity summary: There is broad agreement on the hold decision but meaningful disagreement at the margins. Angell and Jordan clearly dissent from the majority by wanting immediate tightening. Several others disagree on symmetry vs. asymmetry. The M2 range debate also revealed divisions (though more tactical). The overall pattern is moderate differences with 2 likely formal dissents.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. SYRON: Contacts "expressed some concern about all this money flowing into mutual funds and about what will happen when we get a little market correction... they're really quite concerned about this and think we might be in a fragile situation."
- MR. ANGELL: "If we drive every household that saves conservatively to buy stocks they don't feel they are knowledgeable enough to buy or to buy junk bonds... if we then ever have to catch up on interest rates, those investments in common stocks and junk bonds could really fall off the table."

Center-of-gravity summary: Financial stability receives scattered but not prominent attention. A couple of participants flag concerns about money flowing into mutual funds and the risk of a correction, and about low rates driving risk-taking. But this is not a major discussion focus -- it arises incidentally rather than as a dedicated topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Asymmetric directive: "slightly greater reserve restraint would or slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable" -- the asymmetry signals a lean toward tightening.
- MR. MELZER: "we really need to be watching things pretty carefully here and that we need to be prepared to move promptly... eventually the time will come when it's no longer a warning shot."
- MR. LINDSEY: "we should also have to face the fact that for us to make the tough call it is only going to become more difficult as time goes on."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "at some point we're going to have to address negative real rates."

Center-of-gravity summary: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. The asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten. Multiple participants emphasize that tightening will eventually be necessary and it will only get harder to act. However, there is no commitment to a timeline and the next action is explicitly data-dependent. This is a mild tightening bias with data dependence.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. KELLEY: "expectations and perceptions in the form of uncertainty are very, very dominant."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The uncertainties that remain are really quite considerable: the continued layoffs; the deficits of federal, state, and local governments; the whole restructuring process; and the international weaknesses."
- MR. BOEHNE: "the uncertainties that are out there do put the risks on the down side... the uncertainty instead of abating, if anything, is increasing."
- MR. HOENIG: "the uncertainty is having an effect... on some of these activities in the manufacturing sector."

Center-of-gravity summary: Uncertainty is elevated and is a pervasive theme of this meeting. Nearly every participant mentions it -- fiscal policy uncertainty (budget, taxes, health care), NAFTA, floods, European recession, M2 breakdown, and the inflation outlook. However, this is elevated policy/economic uncertainty, not Knightian "uncharted territory" uncertainty. Participants can still articulate a central forecast.
