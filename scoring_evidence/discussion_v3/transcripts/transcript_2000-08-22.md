# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2000-08-22 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript of individual participant statements from the go-around and policy discussion. Speakers identified by name. This is a full-length meeting with extensive commentary from approximately 16 participants.

**Era context**: August 2000 — late-stage expansion with the Fed having raised rates 175bp since mid-1999. The dot-com bubble had partially corrected, productivity growth was surging, and the economy was showing initial signs of slowing from very strong growth. The fed funds rate was at 6.50%.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above midpoint. The center of gravity is that core inflation remains "modest" or "constrained" but risks tilt upward. Moskow: "risks are tilted toward higher inflation" but "core PCE inflation has remained modest." Parry: "core inflation this year and next is still expected to be higher than it was last year." Greenspan: "the acceleration of price inflation is essentially still a forecast" and notes core PCE for last three months is only "slightly higher than 1/2 percent at an annual rate." McTeer: "outside of the inflation numbers in February and March, there has not been a major inflation breakout this year." Gramlich's historical comparison explicitly argues the situation looks far less inflationary than 1966. Poole and Minehan are more concerned about energy passthrough and upside risks. Meyer notes core CPI is "exactly the same as it was at the end of 1995." The consensus is that inflation is mildly elevated but contained, with upside risks.
- Direction: Slightly above midpoint. "Somewhat elevated" but not alarming. Score ~55.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says modest but with upward tilt. 55 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as above potential but the gap is narrowing. Greenspan: "the gap between aggregate supply and demand is beginning to close" — this implies it was positive (demand > supply). McDonough: "growth substantially below potential over the forecast horizon" implies they expect the gap to close. Multiple participants note the economy is "still strong" but "moderating." Parry forecasts 3.75% growth vs. potential of ~4-4.75%. The economy was running hot but slowing toward potential.
- Direction: Above potential but closing. Score ~55.
- Bright-line rule: Greenspan explicitly says gap between demand and supply exists and is "beginning to close" — must be >50. 55 fits.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slightly above but narrowing. 55 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Near-unanimous that labor markets are very tight. Broaddus: "Fairfax County...0.9 percent" unemployment. Minehan: "exceedingly tight, with several New England states recording unemployment rates below 3 percent." Parry: "labor markets remain tight." Hoenig: "Unemployment rates continue to hover around the 3 percent range." McTeer: "Texas unemployment rate at 4.1 percent is now at its lowest level since 1974." Ferguson: labor markets "super hot" going to "warm." Some modest easing signs (Moskow, Ferguson), but levels remain very tight. Greenspan: "tightening of the labor markets."
- Direction: Very tight. Score ~70.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence clearly shows very tight labor markets. 70 matches.
- Anchors: 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75. 70 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture — consumer spending clearly slowing, but capital investment booming. Moskow: "demand growth moderated from the first quarter to the second." Retail sales weakening across many districts. Housing slowing. But Greenspan: "capital investment continues to boom." Minehan: semiconductor business "90 percent increase in revenues." The net position: consumer spending below trend but investment well above. Weighting by discussion emphasis, consumer slowing gets more attention but investment boom is noted as a powerful offset.
- Direction: Slightly above trend on net — consumer weakness offset by investment strength. Score ~52.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Mixed signals net to roughly at trend. 52 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Ferguson notes "financial conditions have in fact eased a little since our last meeting. Real interest rates fell." But also notes banks "reporting a tightening of standards and a firming of terms on business loans." Jordan and McTeer report bankers tightening lending. Guynn: "pronounced element of caution and restraint" from bankers, declining loan demand. The combination suggests somewhat tight conditions from the banking/lending side, but market-based conditions have eased slightly.
- Direction: Roughly neutral to slightly tight. Score ~53.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Bank tightening is present but market conditions eased. 53 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for holding rates unchanged with asymmetric bias toward inflation. No one advocates for a rate cut. No one strongly pushes for an immediate hike, though several (Minehan, Guynn) note they might want to move later. The consensus is firmly hold + watch + maintain tightening bias. Greenspan: "no change directive and retention of the statement that the risks are toward higher inflation." Every single speaker supports this.
- Direction: Hold with tightening bias — lean hawkish. Score ~58.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with inflation bias is lean hawkish. 58 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Remarkably low disagreement. Every participant supports the Chairman's recommendation for no change and maintaining the inflation risk bias. Minor differences in emphasis (Hoenig suggests broadening risk language beyond just labor markets; Jordan raises philosophical points about fiat money). No formal dissents. No opposing camps.
- Direction: Near-consensus. Score ~12.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Virtually none here. 12 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited direct attention. Some mentions: Kelley notes "stock market and consumer debt are dangerously high, and household savings are dangerously low." Greenspan briefly touches on equity wealth effects. Ferguson mentions financial conditions easing. But financial stability is not a focal topic for most participants.
- Direction: Brief mentions, not a focus. Score ~20.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Limited attention. 20 matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is hold-with-tightening-bias. All agree risks tilt toward inflation, implying the next move would be up if needed. But there's no urgency. Greenspan: "a lot of things could be fairly significantly worse, but they are not." Stern: "we have some additional time to assess." Moskow: "if the inflation data deteriorate...we have to be prepared to act very quickly." The signal is patient but leaning hawkish.
- Direction: Mild tightening bias. Score ~62.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. 62 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate to somewhat elevated uncertainty. Many participants emphasize uncertainty around productivity — Broaddus: "a lot of room for doubt and uncertainty about the persistence of its recent behavior." Poole: "we know so little about productivity." Jordan: lengthy remarks on international surprises. Meyer: "we are only beginning to learn about the complex dynamics." Minehan: "what if it's wrong?" But there's also broad agreement on the general picture (slowing demand, tight labor, strong investment). Uncertainty is above normal but not extreme.
- Direction: Somewhat elevated. Score ~42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Genuine uncertainty about productivity and outlook. 42 matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor tightness is higher than output gap, which makes sense — the economy was slowing toward potential but labor markets were still very tight (lagging indicator). The text supports this divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Aligned — both lean hawkish. Forward guidance slightly more so because the bias language explicitly signals potential future tightening even while holding now. Makes sense.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "The acceleration of price inflation is essentially still a forecast...the rate of increase of core PCE inflation for the last three months is...only slightly higher than 1/2 percent at an annual rate."
- Parry: "core inflation this year and next is still expected to be higher than it was last year...the balance of risks remains tilted toward higher inflation."
- Meyer: "Core CPI inflation today is exactly the same as it was at the end of 1995 on a methodologically consistent basis."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see core inflation as modestly contained but with clear upside risks from tight labor markets and energy; the actual data are benign but the forecast tilts up.
**Minority views**: Poole/Minehan push harder on energy risks (would score ~60-65); Gramlich/McTeer see less pressure (~48-52).

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "The gap between aggregate supply and demand is beginning to close" and "the Greenbook forecast implies that that gap will narrow considerably more."
- McDonough: "growth substantially below potential over the forecast horizon, we expect the unemployment rate to begin rising."
- Guynn: "I still judge the economy to be strong, as evidenced by the strong GDP report and the continued strength in business investment."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential but clearly decelerating toward it. The gap is positive but narrowing.
**Minority views**: Those emphasizing investment boom (Greenspan, Minehan) see more above-potential pressure; those focusing on consumer slowdown see closer to potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Broaddus: "Fairfax County, Virginia...the reported unemployment rate is 0.9 percent."
- Minehan: "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight, with several New England states recording unemployment rates below 3 percent."
- Ferguson: "the job market has gone from super hot to what I would describe as sort of warm."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are very tight by any historical standard, with unemployment well below NAIRU estimates. Some modest easing at the margin.
**Minority views**: Ferguson and Moskow see initial easing signs; most others see continued extreme tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Moskow: "demand growth moderated from the first quarter to the second...retailing, motor vehicles--especially heavy trucks--machine tools, steel, aluminum, and paper" all slowing.
- Greenspan: "In complete contrast to developments in the consumer and housing sectors is what is going on in the capital goods markets...overall capital investment continues to boom."
- Minehan: semiconductor business reporting "90 percent increase in revenues and a 40 percent increase in orders."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending clearly moderating, housing weakening, but business investment booming. Net position is roughly at trend.
**Minority views**: Some see consumer weakness as dominant (score lower); the investment boom partially offsets.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 53 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Ferguson: "financial conditions have in fact eased a little since our last meeting. Real interest rates fell...real interest rates for investment-grade borrowers appear to be up only about 1 percentage point since May 1999."
- Jordan/McTeer: Bankers tightening standards, declining loan demand, "bankers have really tightened conditions by demanding more equity."
- Guynn: "amazingly consistent report from our larger banks of a falloff in loan demand across the region."

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed — market-based financial conditions eased slightly, but bank lending standards have tightened and loan demand is falling. Net: roughly neutral to slightly tight.
**Minority views**: Ferguson emphasizes easing (lower score); several presidents emphasize bank tightening (higher score).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "we ought to stay where we are...At the same time, it would be highly risky in my judgment to depart from the bias...asymmetric toward tightening."
- All participants: "I support your recommendation" — unanimous hold with inflation risk bias.
- Meyer: "we have to be particularly vigilant about an upward creep in inflation going forward."

**Center-of-gravity**: Firm consensus for holding steady with explicit tightening bias. No one advocates easing; no one pushes for immediate tightening. Classic "watchful hold with hawkish lean."
**Minority views**: Very narrow range — some slightly more dovish (Gramlich, McTeer) and some slightly more hawkish (Minehan, Guynn), but all within a tight band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Every participant explicitly supports both parts of the Chairman's recommendation.
- Minor emphasis differences: Hoenig suggests broadening risk language; Jordan makes philosophical points.
- No formal dissents; no expressed reservations about the decision.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous agreement on both the action and the risk assessment language.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 20 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Kelley: "the stock market and consumer debt are dangerously high, and household savings are dangerously low."
- Greenspan: passing references to wealth effects and equity values.
- Ferguson: brief mention of financial conditions easing.

**Center-of-gravity**: A few passing references but financial stability is not a primary topic of discussion. Focus is overwhelmingly on productivity, inflation, and real economy.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "retention of the statement that the risks are toward higher inflation."
- Moskow: "If the inflation data deteriorate...we have to be prepared to act very quickly."
- Minehan: "it is also not the time to think we can throw in the towel for the rest of the year."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear signal that the next move would be up if needed, but patience for now. Forward lean is mildly hawkish.
**Minority views**: None dissent from this forward characterization.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Broaddus: "there is still a lot of room for doubt and uncertainty about the persistence of its recent behavior and where the trend will ultimately settle going forward."
- Poole: "we know so little about productivity and the underlying economics of productivity determinants."
- Jordan: "it is sobering and humbling to think back...we have to be very open to the idea that there will be a lot of unexpected events."

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, centered on productivity assumptions and their implications for inflation and growth. Multiple participants note genuine difficulty in assessing the outlook. But there's also broad agreement on the current state.
