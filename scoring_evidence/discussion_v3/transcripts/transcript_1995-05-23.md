# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-05-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers both the economic go-around and the policy discussion. Speakers include: Broaddus, Moskow, McDonough, Minehan, Parry, Boehne, Hoenig, McTeer, Stern, Melzer, Jordan, Kelley, Phillips, Lindsey, Yellen, Blinder, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-populated go-around with approximately 17 speakers.

### Era Context
May 1995: The Fed had completed a tightening cycle in early 1995 (fed funds at 6%). The economy is slowing from above-trend growth, with an inventory correction underway. Bond yields have declined significantly. The "soft landing" narrative is dominant. Core CPI is showing some cyclical uptick but wage pressures remain restrained. Mexico peso crisis is a background concern.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as modestly above target/comfortable levels with some cyclical uptick, but mostly viewed as temporary.
- Melzer: "The annualized growth rate of the CPI was 4.9 percent in April... 3.6 percent annual rate since December... 'low inflation' of 3 percent or more is endemic." Most hawkish on inflation.
- McDonough: forecasts 3.6% CPI in 1996, "appreciably less optimistic than the Greenbook" at 3%.
- Minehan: skeptical that core CPI "can remain so well behaved in 1996 after a relatively small blip in 1995."
- Boehne: "a temporary cyclical upturn that is not sustainable."
- Hoenig: "we do have to be diligent in watching those inflation numbers."
- Phillips: "cyclical pressures clearly are showing through in the core CPI now, but I don't think we have a firm indication that this is a permanent ratcheting up."
- Yellen: "the economy is in effect experiencing a favorable supply shock" on compensation; some "downward risks on the inflation side."
- Greenspan: uptick in inflation is "strictly the expected slowdown in productivity" — "a cyclical phenomenon."
- Stern: "relatively negative about the inflation outlook, at least for this year" but "marginally more confident that this may turn out to be a temporary acceleration."

Center of gravity: Most participants acknowledge a cyclical uptick in core CPI (now around 3%), but the majority view it as temporary/cyclical rather than a permanent shift. Several (Minehan, Hoenig, Melzer, Parry) are more concerned it could be lasting. In the mid-1990s context with no explicit 2% target, 3% CPI was considered modestly elevated. Score: 57. Modestly above comfortable levels but viewed as largely cyclical.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says modest uptick, mostly cyclical. 57 matches — somewhat above midpoint.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as having been above potential but now slowing toward it.
- Broaddus: "most people agree that the level of real GDP currently is above its longer-term potential level."
- Parry: expects "final sales to keep growing at or even somewhat above the growth rate of potential output."
- Yellen: "the economy may have overshot potential output."
- Greenspan: inventory correction causing softening, but "no evidence of a cumulative decline in the economy"; "the fairly solid underlying status of this recovery."
- Blinder: shades forecast "a bit lower on real growth in the near term."
- Kelley: "the expansion is obviously slowing."

The economy was above potential but is slowing. With the inventory correction, the gap is narrowing. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language from Broaddus and Yellen → must be >50. But slowing rapidly toward potential. Score: 52. Still modestly above potential but converging.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor markets are tight in many districts, but not generating broad wage pressure.
- Moskow: "labor markets remain tight, with the District's unemployment rate at its lowest level in over 15 years." "Pockets of labor shortages."
- Melzer: "Parts of the District continue to report tight labor market conditions for both entry-level and skilled workers." Unemployment at 4.5%.
- Stern: "Labor markets remain quite tight, especially for skilled workers, but as usual that does not seem to be translating into broad-based wage pressures."
- Hoenig: "nonfarm jobs in the District were up almost 4 percent in March."
- Jordan: Ohio at 4% unemployment, many areas under 3%.
- Yellen: compensation growth has been surprisingly restrained despite tight labor markets — "a favorable supply shock."
- Phillips: "It doesn't appear that, at least for the moment, wage pressures are consistently building."

Center of gravity: Labor markets are clearly tight (unemployment below NAIRU estimates), but wage pressures are surprisingly restrained. Score: 58. Tight but not generating the expected wage inflation.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Consumer spending is softening, especially durables/autos. Business investment remains strong but slowing.
- Multiple speakers: auto production cutbacks, housing starts down, retail sales disappointing in many districts.
- But: capital goods orders still strong (Moskow: "strong orders for products such as machine tools, agricultural equipment, and heavy-duty trucks"), nonresidential construction strengthening, exports holding up.
- Greenspan: "capital goods and exports, and I might add homebuilding, are showing signs of sustainability." Profits "gangbusters."
- Lindsey: consumer debt concerns, saving rate must decline to sustain consumption.
- Blinder: "downside risks exceed the upside risks" for near-term growth.
- Boehne: "Business investment is likely to be off in 1995 relative to 1994, but still strong enough."

Net position: Consumption is clearly softening (autos, housing, retail). Business investment is still solid but moderating. The inventory correction is dragging GDP. Overall below trend in the near term. Score: 43. Below trend due to inventory correction and consumer softening, but investment provides a floor.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions have eased significantly from the tightening cycle.
- Bond yields down substantially; stock market near record highs.
- McDonough: "stock market is near record highs; bond prices are up; higher household wealth... declines in long-bond rates have a very positive effect on fixed mortgage rates... at about 7-3/4 percent."
- Lindsey: credit terms are easing — "four delegations of bankers... complaining not only that their competitors were easing conditions, but that they were easing conditions too much." 1.2 billion credit card solicitations.
- Phillips: "Banks and other lenders appear to be able to support growth as needed, so I don't think we are looking at a bottleneck with respect to the availability of credit."
- Greenspan: "credit terms are still tighter than they were in the late 1980s; but that is scarcely the standard."
- McTeer: "the markets have already done that [easing], both interest rates and the exchange market."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are accommodative — bond yields down, stock market up, credit freely available, credit terms easing. Score: 30. Conditions are on the loose side, especially given the bond rally and credit easing.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: Unanimous support for holding rates unchanged ("B symmetric"). No one advocated a rate change.
- Greenspan proposes "B symmetric" (hold, no bias).
- McTeer: "the recent performance of the economy would argue for some easing today, but the markets have already done that" — leans slightly dovish but supports hold.
- Blinder adopts McTeer's words — subtle dovish lean.
- Broaddus: hold, but "we need to be sensitive to" downside risk; also concerned about "continuing longer-term inflation risk."
- Minehan, Hoenig, Parry, Stern: support hold but flag upside inflation risk — modest hawkish lean.
- Melzer: "We may have a moderately tight monetary policy, but that is appropriate... we should not be trying to fine-tune."
- Moskow: "it clearly would be inappropriate to respond too hastily to any signs of weakening."

Center of gravity: Firm consensus for hold with symmetric directive. A few lean slightly dovish (McTeer, Blinder), several lean slightly hawkish on inflation vigilance (Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Melzer). Net is balanced to very slightly hawkish. Score: 52. Hold with a very mild hawkish tilt from inflation vigilance voices.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Complete unanimity on "B symmetric." No dissents, minimal disagreement.
- Every single speaker supports the Chairman's recommendation.
- Some mild differences in emphasis: McTeer/Blinder more concerned about downside risk; Minehan/Hoenig/Melzer more concerned about inflation. But all converge on same action.
Score: 15. Near-consensus with only minor emphasis differences.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Meaningful attention to financial stability issues.
- Greenspan: extended discussion of asset price bubbles — "if this economy works as well as the Greenbook suggests, the really serious problem... will be how we should respond to asset price bubbles." References February 1994 bond bubble.
- Lindsey: extensive discussion of credit easing, consumer debt buildup, nonresidential construction possibly being overdone. "1.2 billion credit card solicitations."
- Phillips: markets demonstrate "resilience"; P/E ratios "not dramatically out of line."
- McTeer: yield curve flattening makes him "a little bit nervous" about financial stability.
- Greenspan's bubble discussion is a notable forward-looking financial stability concern.

This is more than passing reference — Greenspan and Lindsey devote significant attention. Score: 30. Limited but notable discussion, concentrated in 2-3 speakers (Greenspan, Lindsey, McTeer).

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: Neutral/symmetric. "B symmetric" = no directional bias.
- Greenspan: "just do 'B' symmetric and wait until the next meeting."
- Parry: "if we in fact are somewhat agreed about the direction that we would like to see the rate of inflation go in the longer term, it will make us rather cautious in moving toward lower rates." — mild tightening bias for the longer term.
- Broaddus: wants to be positioned to ease if cumulative downturn materializes, but also sees longer-term inflation risk.
- The overall signal is truly symmetric — wait and see.
Score: 52. Balanced/neutral with a barely perceptible hawkish undertone from those emphasizing inflation vigilance.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Moderate to somewhat elevated uncertainty.
- Kelley: "a classic wait-and-see period... too early right now to be making firm judgments."
- McDonough: "substantial uncertainty about the forecast."
- Broaddus: "two distinct and somewhat sequential risks" — near-term downside, later upside.
- Hoenig: "the projections are subject to considerable uncertainty."
- Phillips: "wild cards in the outlook" — fiscal policy, Japan trade, dollar.
- Greenspan: acknowledges inventory adjustment may be worse than expected; uncertainty about asset bubbles.
- Blinder: "without a great deal of conviction."

Many participants note balanced but real uncertainty. Not extreme — this is a standard uncertain forecasting period. Score: 42. Somewhat elevated uncertainty, with multiple participants noting genuine difficulty in the outlook.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Reasonable — economy above potential but slowing; labor markets still tight from the boom phase. The slight divergence reflects the lag in labor market adjustment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Aligned — both balanced/neutral.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (30, loose) is consistent with the bond rally and credit easing described.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MELZER: "The annualized growth rate of the CPI was 4.9 percent in April... many private forecasters expect inflation to rise throughout 1995."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Although there is some uptick in the rate of inflation, I doubt that what we are seeing is a cumulative upturn in the rate of inflation, but rather a temporary cyclical upturn."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I am concerned about the possibility that it is not necessarily all cyclical."

Center-of-gravity: Most participants see a cyclical uptick in core CPI toward 3%, which is modestly above comfortable levels. The majority view it as temporary, but a significant minority worries it could be more persistent.

Minority views: Melzer is the most hawkish, seeing endemic 3%+ inflation. Yellen sees downside risks to inflation from restrained compensation. This spreads the band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think most people agree that the level of real GDP currently is above its longer-term potential level."
- MS. YELLEN: "the economy may have overshot potential output and there is continuing pressure from the prices of intermediate materials."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "no evidence of a cumulative decline in the economy" but "the rate of expansion is going to be shading down a little."

Center-of-gravity: Economy modestly above potential but rapidly converging toward it via inventory correction. The gap is small and narrowing.

Minority views: Blinder and McTeer shade more toward downside risk. Hoenig sees upside risk.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor markets remain tight, with the District's unemployment rate at its lowest level in over 15 years."
- MR. STERN: "Labor markets remain quite tight, especially for skilled workers, but as usual that does not seem to be translating into broad-based wage pressures."
- MR. JORDAN: "The state of Ohio has overall unemployment of 4 percent... a lot of metropolitan areas that report their unemployment rates officially as under 3 percent."

Center-of-gravity: Labor markets are clearly tight with low unemployment across multiple districts, but wage pressures are notably restrained, which is a puzzle to participants.

Minority views: Some districts show more slack (New York at 6.8% unemployment). Yellen notes compensation restraint as a "favorable supply shock."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MOSKOW: "economic activity in the Seventh District in the last two months seems to have continued the slowing pace of growth... led, of course, by developments in the automobile sector."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "preliminary reports suggest that April retail sales were up only 3 to 5 percent on a year-over-year basis despite the favorable timing of Easter, and merchants reported widespread disappointment."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "capital goods and exports, and I might add homebuilding, are showing signs of sustainability... the profit picture still looks increasingly supportive."

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is clearly softening, led by autos and durables. Business investment remains supportive but moderating. Net spending is below trend due to inventory correction.

Minority views: Hoenig and Kelley see upside potential from wealth effects, lower long rates, and consumer confidence.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LINDSEY: "Our surveys... are all saying that credit terms are easing... four delegations of bankers through my office complaining not only that their competitors were easing conditions, but that they were easing conditions too much."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "The stock market is near record highs; bond prices are up; higher household wealth... declines in long-bond rates have a very positive effect on fixed mortgage rates."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Banks and other lenders appear to be able to support growth as needed."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose — bond yields down significantly, stock market at highs, credit freely available and terms easing aggressively. The fed funds rate is at 6% but market conditions have eased substantially.

Minority views: McTeer notes the yield curve is flattening, which historically precedes recessions — a mildly tightening signal. But the dominant view is of accommodative conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "We can, as I see it, just do 'B' symmetric and wait until the next meeting."
- MR. MCTEER: "I think the risks are somewhat unbalanced to the down side, and I think the recent performance of the economy would argue for some easing today, but the markets have already done that."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I agree with your proposal. I think I am just a little more nervous about the inflation prospects."

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous support for holding at 6% with symmetric directive. Mild dovish voices (McTeer, Blinder) offset by mild hawkish voices (Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Melzer). Net is essentially balanced.

Minority views: McTeer/Blinder would lean toward easing but accept the hold. Minehan/Melzer emphasize inflation vigilance. Neither camp pushes for action.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Every speaker (17+) explicitly supports "B symmetric."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I support 'B' symmetric." MR. KELLEY: "I support 'B' symmetric." (Typical of nearly all speakers.)

Center-of-gravity: Near-perfect consensus. Differences are purely in emphasis (degree of inflation concern vs. growth concern), not in policy prescription.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the really serious problem that we will have in setting monetary policy... will be how we should respond to asset price bubbles."
- MR. LINDSEY: "1.2 billion credit card solicitations were mailed out... cranes are back on the horizon just 2-1/2 years later."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "price-earnings ratios are not dramatically out of line with historical averages."

Center-of-gravity: Greenspan and Lindsey provide substantive discussion of financial stability concerns (asset bubbles, credit easing, overbuilding). But most other participants address it only in passing or not at all. A concentrated discussion among 2-3 key speakers.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "just do 'B' symmetric and wait until the next meeting... it is very unlikely that the next meeting is going to be one where we are going to be confronted with an economic expansion that is picking up."
- MR. PARRY: "if we in fact are somewhat agreed about the direction that we would like to see the rate of inflation go in the longer term, it will make us rather cautious in moving toward lower rates."
- MR. BROADDUS: wants to be positioned to ease quickly if downturn materializes, but concerned about credibility.

Center-of-gravity: Truly symmetric forward posture. Data-dependent. No directional signal for the next meeting.

Minority views: Parry/Melzer signal reluctance to ease, creating a very mild hawkish undercurrent. Broaddus/McTeer want flexibility to ease if needed.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "there is substantial uncertainty about the forecast so that an open mind is very much indicated."
- MR. HOENIG: "the projections are subject to considerable uncertainty."
- MR. KELLEY: "a classic wait-and-see period... too early right now to be making firm judgments."

Center-of-gravity: Multiple participants note genuine uncertainty about whether the slowdown is temporary or cumulative, whether inflation is cyclical or persistent, and how fiscal policy and trade tensions will play out. This is somewhat elevated but not extreme.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Score 60 ±8. Multiple speakers discuss rising intermediate materials prices, paper, steel, cement. Moskow: "Prices of some materials such as cement, paper, and steel scrap have been increasing." But also rollbacks: "a number of steel suppliers are reported to have rolled back earlier price increases." Parry: "rising prices of intermediate goods." Modestly elevated goods inflation pressures.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A. No specific discussion of services inflation as a distinct category.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A. Housing prices discussed in activity context, not as inflation component.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Score 52 ±8. Melzer: "longer-term inflation forecasts indicate that market participants expect inflation at or above its current level for horizons as long as five or ten years." Phillips: long-term rate declines "partly I hope due to lowered inflation expectations." Mixed signals — Melzer sees them elevated, Phillips sees improvement. Roughly anchored but not fully at desired levels.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A (credit conditions discussed under FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS; no stress per se — conditions are loose).

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A. Stock market near highs; Greenspan mentions bubble risk as forward-looking concern but no current stress.

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A. Banks are healthy and lending aggressively.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A. No dysfunction discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Score 40 ±10. Several mentions of dollar weakness. McTeer: "The weakest component of that index is the Texas value of the dollar because the peso is so important." Minehan: "some of our software companies are experiencing upticks in sales as a result of the decline of the dollar." Phillips: "we have had a shock to the dollar." Parry: "inflationary pressures from the dollar." Moderate concern about dollar weakness (not strength), so score below 50. (Higher = more strength concern; weakness concern = below 50.)

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Score 55 ±10. Yellen: compensation restraint may imply "a decline in the natural rate of unemployment. At a minimum, the economy is in effect experiencing a favorable supply shock." Greenspan: "the expected slowdown in productivity that occurs as a consequence of fixed costs not being spread over the aggregate volumes." Mixed — Yellen sees potential positive productivity story; Greenspan sees cyclical productivity slowdown. Slightly above trend given Yellen's supply shock framing.
