# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2000-02-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of individual participant statements from both a go-around (Day 1) and a policy discussion (Day 2). Speakers are identified by name. The discussion is extensive, with approximately 15+ participants contributing substantive remarks. There is also a debate about monetary aggregate ranges and inflation targeting before the policy go-around.

### Era Context
February 2000 is the peak of the late-1990s technology/productivity boom. The economy is growing well above trend, unemployment is at multi-decade lows, the stock market (especially Nasdaq) is at very elevated levels, and the Fed is in an incremental tightening cycle. The key macro tension: extraordinary productivity growth is keeping unit labor costs contained, but demand growth is outstripping even this elevated supply growth, creating an overheating risk. The Fed had already raised rates three times in 1999 (June, August, November).

### Dimensional Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral and rising. The center of gravity is that inflation is low NOW but pressures are clearly building. Parry: "fourth-quarter surge in GDP-based inflation measures...a serious concern"; his staff forecast shows "core CPI from just under 2% to 2-1/4% this year and 2-1/2% next year." Moskow: "core CPI inflation rising this year to about 2-1/2%." Guynn: "we are beginning to see the whites of inflation's eyes." Kelley: "a number of price series are beginning to move...it is still only a trickle and far from a flood." Meyer: "risks have now become significantly tilted toward higher inflation." Greenspan: "This is not an inflationary price environment or an inflationary cost environment" but acknowledges the wealth-effect-driven demand excess must be addressed. Multiple participants note input price increases (energy, steel, copper, construction materials, health care). However, unit labor costs are "nearly stable" (Gramlich), firms still report inability to pass through costs, and inflation expectations from TIPS are flat. The center of gravity is that inflation is slightly above target and participants perceive building pressure, but it has NOT yet accelerated meaningfully. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score ~57-60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly all participants describe the economy as operating above potential. Meyer: "output has moved beyond potential...inflationary consequences of this imbalance." Hoenig: "the U.S. economy is experiencing the effects of excess demand." Minehan: "substantial policy tightening is necessary to bring the economy back to a reasonable balance between demand and supply." Gramlich: "the unemployment rate is now well below the structural estimate of NAIRU." Greenspan: demand is outstripping supply via wealth effect. Ferguson: "the economy is continuing to expand at a faster pace than its potential." GDP growth was 5-3/4% in second half of 1999, forecasts ~4% for 2000 with potential around 3.5-4%. Bright-line rule: clear "above potential" language. Direction: clearly above midpoint. Score ~60-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Essentially every District president reports extremely tight labor markets. Moskow: "It is difficult, if not impossible, to find any signs of easing in our very tight labor markets. Manpower's survey shows net hiring strength near a 22-year high." Minehan: "supply of available labor...seems lower than ever." Broaddus: "the increasing difficulty of finding qualified workers across a broad range...is really the key preoccupation of all our business contacts." Guynn: restaurant operating at half capacity due to inability to hire. However, several note wage increases remain moderate (3-5% range), though some acceleration is beginning. The era anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Direction: well above midpoint. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Spending is described as very strong by virtually all participants. Minehan: "consumption is booming, confidence is high, the saving rate is even lower." Moskow: "holiday shopping season was exceptionally good...auto producers expect 2000 to be the second best year ever." Jordan: "auto sales were very strong." Guynn: "holiday selling season was a blockbuster." Kelley: "record highs of the stock market and consumer confidence are resulting in continuing strong consumption. Investment spending remains high." Housing has only modestly softened. Multiple participants note the wealth effect is driving extraordinary spending. Direction: well above trend. Score ~67-70.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This requires scoring CONDITIONS, not policy. Conditions are mixed. On one hand, the stock market is at record highs, consumer confidence is high, credit demand is very strong (Jordan: "commercial and consumer loan demand remained strong"). On the other hand, Stern notes "bankers report they are tightening credit standards." Jordan notes "deposit growth evaporated," "interest margins are under pressure," bank stocks declining. Mortgage rates have risen, causing some refinancing to dry up. Greenspan notes the equity premium is "downright scary" low, suggesting very loose conditions in equity markets. Bond yields have risen significantly (10-year nominal at 6.6%). Overall, conditions are somewhat loose due to strong equity markets and abundant credit, though some tightening at the margin from higher rates. Direction: somewhat below neutral (loose). Score ~38-42.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for tightening. The debate is 25bp vs 50bp, not whether to tighten. Greenspan proposes 25bp with asymmetric (inflation-risk) statement. He explicitly says "we are going to have to move the funds rate quite significantly higher" and envisions moves "at every meeting." Parry and Broaddus prefer 50bp. Guynn came in wanting 50bp but accepts 25bp. All support asymmetry toward inflation. Meyer: "we are likely to have to implement a series of tightenings this year." The vote is unanimous for 25bp + inflation risk bias. This is moderate-to-strong hawkish. Direction: clearly above midpoint. Score ~68-72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote is unanimous for 25bp. However, Parry openly advocates 50bp and suggests an intermeeting conference call. Broaddus says "my preference would be to go the full 1/2 point." Guynn came in wanting 50bp. So there is a meaningful minority (3-4 participants) wanting faster tightening, but they accept 25bp. No one dissents formally. There is also significant intellectual debate about monetary aggregate ranges, inflation targeting, and productivity. Direction: moderate differences within a hawkish consensus. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Multiple participants discuss financial stability concerns at length. Greenspan devotes significant time to equity market valuations: "our latest evaluation of the equity premium in the stock market is that it is downright scary!" He explicitly worries about "cracking the market" with aggressive tightening. Minehan discusses household/corporate balance sheet vulnerabilities and "growing liabilities and debt service burdens." Ferguson: "I have a growing concern that a placid surface may disguise some underlying turmoil." Meyer discusses the wealth effect and partial vs general equilibrium. Jordan discusses bank stock fund runoffs. Stern notes banks tightening credit standards. This is a meeting where financial stability is a prominent topic, though not dominant. Direction: moderately elevated attention. Score ~50-55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Greenspan: "we are going to have to move the funds rate quite significantly higher" and envisions "moving at every meeting" by 25bp. The press release includes "balance of risks toward inflation." Meyer: "likely to be appropriate to make a move at each meeting for a period of time." Poole: "likely to mean a sequence of 25 basis point increases at each meeting...for quite some time." Broaddus asks for "asymmetry plus." Direction: clearly hawkish forward signal. Score ~72-76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is meaningful uncertainty expressed, particularly about productivity trends, NAIRU, and the sustainability of the expansion. Stern: "I'm much less confident about the outlook for inflation...we really can't say very much about the outlook for productivity." Greenspan's extended remarks about model humility: "I am a little concerned about the definitiveness of some of these results." Meyer: "Given the uncertainties about trend growth, NAIRU, and inflation dynamics." Minehan: "I'm becoming much more a believer...I don't know where the NAIRU is." But this is not crisis-level uncertainty; participants have a clear directional sense (economy strong, need to tighten). Direction: somewhat elevated. Score ~40-45.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says building but moderate. Score 58 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Clear "above potential" language. Score 62 matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Extreme tightness described. Score 70 matches.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. "Booming" consumption. Score 68 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Loose equity conditions, some tightening at margin. Score 40 matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous tightening, debate is 25 vs 50bp. Score 70 matches.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous vote but meaningful 50bp minority. Score 32 matches.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Higher = more attention. Prominent but not dominant. Score 52 matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward. "Move at every meeting." Score 74 matches.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Higher = more uncertainty. Somewhat elevated, especially on supply side. Score 42 matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-boom overheating. Labor tightness is higher because labor market discussion is more extreme than output gap language, which is tempered by productivity optimism.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (74): Closely aligned. Both reflect strong hawkish consensus with expectation of serial tightening. Forward guidance slightly higher because the explicit "move at every meeting" language is very directional.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "fourth-quarter surge in GDP-based inflation measures...a serious concern...our staff forecast shows an upward trend in core CPI from just under 2 percent last year to 2-1/4 percent this year and 2-1/2 percent next year."
- MR. GUYNN: "I believe and would even argue that we are beginning to see the whites of inflation's eyes."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "This is not an inflationary price environment or an inflationary cost environment...unit labor costs have barely moved."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "unit labor costs are rising less rapidly than before and, in fact, they are nearly stable."
- MR. KELLEY: "a number of price series are beginning to move...it is still only a trickle and far from a flood."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants perceive inflation as slightly above target and building, with core measures expected to drift from ~2% toward 2.5%, but current cost pressures remain contained by strong productivity growth and firms' inability to pass through costs.

**Minority views**: McTeer sees "somewhat less inflation" than staff; Greenspan emphasizes current inflation is not yet problematic. On the other side, Guynn and Parry are more alarmed. The +/-7 band reflects this spread.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MEYER: "I continue to believe that output has moved beyond potential...the persistent imbalance between the growth in supply and demand."
- MR. HOENIG: "I'm convinced that the U.S. economy is experiencing the effects of excess demand."
- MS. MINEHAN: "substantial policy tightening is necessary to bring the economy back to a reasonable balance between demand and supply."
- MR. FERGUSON: "the economy is continuing to expand at a faster pace than its potential, even if that potential has gone up."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Virtually all participants agree the economy is operating above potential, with demand growth persistently exceeding supply growth despite elevated productivity. The above-potential gap is clear but tempered by recognition that potential itself has risen substantially.

**Minority views**: Some participants (McTeer, Stern) are more optimistic about potential, which would imply a smaller gap. The band is narrow because the directional consensus is strong.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "It is difficult, if not impossible, to find any signs of easing in our very tight labor markets...net hiring strength near a 22-year high."
- MS. MINEHAN: "The supply of available labor, and not just skilled labor, seems lower than ever."
- MR. BROADDUS: "the increasing difficulty of finding qualified workers across a broad range of service and manufacturing industries is really the key preoccupation of all our business contacts."
- MR. GUYNN: "a new restaurant owner was telling me he had been operating at half capacity for several weeks because of his inability to hire enough cooks and servers."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are described as extremely tight across virtually all Districts, with severe shortages in multiple occupations (nurses, truckers, construction workers, restaurant staff). Wage growth remains relatively contained at 3-5% but is beginning to accelerate.

**Minority views**: Poole notes FedEx/UPS "see no problems with either staffing or labor costs." McTeer notes "wage gains have been moderating" in Texas. These are minor counterpoints.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MINEHAN: "consumption is booming, confidence is high, the saving rate is even lower, the trade deficit is higher, and consumers are borrowing more and more."
- MR. MOSKOW: "the holiday shopping season was exceptionally good for most District retailers...auto producers expect 2000 to be the second best year ever."
- MR. KELLEY: "record highs of the stock market and consumer confidence are resulting in continuing strong consumption. Investment spending remains high, manifested by rising durable goods orders and backlogs."
- MR. GUYNN: "the holiday selling season was a blockbuster one that clearly exceeded expectations."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is described in superlatives across nearly all Districts -- "booming," "blockbuster," "record" -- driven by wealth effects and high confidence. Business investment also remains robust. Housing has softened only marginally.

**Minority views**: Very little dissent on demand strength. Some note incipient housing softening, but this is minor.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "both commercial and consumer loan demand remained strong...deposit growth evaporated completely...interest margins are under pressure."
- MR. STERN: "bankers report that they are continuing to compete aggressively for loans but they are tightening credit standards."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "our latest evaluation of the equity premium in the stock market is that it is downright scary" (implying very loose equity market conditions).
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the real yield has gone up to about 4.3 percent, and...the nominal yield is all the way up to 6.6 percent on 10-year bonds."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall, with record-high stock markets and strong credit demand, but tightening at the margin as rates rise, bank margins compress, and some lenders tighten standards. The equity market is providing enormous stimulus even as bond markets tighten.

**Minority views**: The mixed picture (loose equities vs tightening credit conditions) widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I happen to think that, this year, we are going to have to move the funds rate quite significantly higher...I would propose that we move the funds rate up by 25 basis points today...with a balance of risks toward inflation."
- MR. PARRY: "I would prefer a 50 basis point increase in the funds rate."
- MR. MEYER: "I also agree with you that we are likely to have to implement a series of tightenings this year; it is likely to be appropriate to make a move at each meeting."
- MR. GUYNN: "I came into the meeting with the intention and inclination to argue for a 50 basis point tightening."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous consensus for tightening with a strong hawkish forward lean. The debate is not whether to tighten but how aggressively (25bp vs 50bp). All support an inflation-risk bias in the announcement. Multiple participants explicitly envision a series of further moves.

**Minority views**: 3-4 participants (Parry, Broaddus, Guynn) preferred 50bp, making the hawkish wing somewhat more aggressive than the consensus.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Vote is unanimous for 25bp + inflation risk bias.
- MR. PARRY: "I would prefer a 50 basis point increase" but can accept 25bp with intermeeting vigilance.
- MR. BROADDUS: "my preference would be to go the full 1/2 point...I am able to accept the 1/4 point increase."
- Vigorous intellectual debate on monetary aggregate ranges, inflation targeting, and productivity, but no fundamental policy disagreement.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on direction with moderate tactical differences about pace. The 50bp minority (Parry, Broaddus, Guynn) all accept 25bp. The debate is within a hawkish consensus, not between camps.

**Minority views**: N/A -- the disagreement IS the minority view, and it is about tactics, not direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "our latest evaluation of the equity premium in the stock market is that it is downright scary...I am concerned that, if we are too aggressive in this process of tightening, we could crack the market."
- MS. MINEHAN: "growing liabilities and debt service burdens that are reasonable only when one factors in growing market values of assets...this is an economy that is increasingly vulnerable to changes in market perceptions."
- MR. FERGUSON: "I have a growing concern that a placid surface may disguise some underlying turmoil...this market is subject to some volatility, including significant downside corrections."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability concerns are a moderately prominent topic, with Greenspan, Minehan, and Ferguson devoting significant attention to equity market fragility, balance sheet vulnerabilities, and the risk of market disruption from policy tightening. Several others touch on it more briefly.

**Minority views**: Some participants (Poole, McTeer, Hoenig) give minimal attention to financial stability.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 74 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would argue that we should think in terms of moving at every meeting and that we move by 25 basis point increments...we will remain in a very unusual period...balance of risks is toward inflation."
- MR. POOLE: "likely to mean a sequence of 25 basis point increases at each meeting...for quite some time."
- MR. MEYER: "likely to be appropriate to make a move at each meeting for a period of time."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would hope...that we will underscore the intensity with which we are watching developments and our readiness to move more aggressively."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is strongly hawkish, with the Chairman explicitly outlining a strategy of 25bp moves at every meeting, accompanied by a formal inflation-risk bias statement. Multiple participants reinforce this expectation of serial tightening.

**Minority views**: McTeer urges caution about pre-committing: "I hope we will keep looking at the data and make our decisions one step at a time." This is a minor voice.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "I'm much less confident about the outlook for inflation...we really can't say very much about the outlook for productivity."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I am a little concerned about the definitiveness of some of these results...I believe we have to be terribly careful about the notion of what we know and what we don't know."
- MR. MEYER: "Given the uncertainties about trend growth, NAIRU, and inflation dynamics."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I'm becoming much more a believer...I don't know where the NAIRU is."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about the supply side (productivity trends, NAIRU) and financial market reactions to policy. However, there is strong directional confidence that the economy is growing above potential and that tightening is needed.

**Minority views**: Greenspan's extended critique of econometric model precision expresses deeper methodological uncertainty.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Multiple participants discuss goods prices. Input prices rising (steel, copper, energy, construction materials). But firms "cannot pass these higher costs on to their customers" (Moskow). Minehan: "contacts continue to believe they cannot raise prices, particularly for commodity-type products." Some steel price increases implemented. Modest pressure building. **Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Health care costs discussed prominently. Minehan: HMO receivership, "increase in benefits costs in the most recent ECI data." Multiple participants note healthcare/pharmaceutical double-digit increases. Office rents surging in several cities. **Score: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Parry: "In San Francisco, lease rates on Class A office space increased by about 25 percent last year." Minehan: "Rents for Class A office space in Boston remain very high." Residential prices "rose strongly in most markets." This is commercial AND residential. **Score: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Gramlich: "the bond market is now building in a pretty hefty inflation premium." But Greenspan: TIPS-derived inflation expectations are "barely different today than in December 1997...absolutely flat." Mixed signals. **Score: 53 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A. Credit conditions are discussed but not in terms of stress per se; credit is readily available.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Greenspan: "equity premium in the stock market is that it is downright scary...we are in a far worse situation now than we were [in 1987]." Ferguson: "some underlying turmoil." Jordan: "bank stock mutual funds are experiencing very substantial runoffs." This is overvaluation/bubble concern, which per the rubric scores LOW (toward 1). **Score: 18 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A. Banks are sound; the discussion is about margins and lending standards, not stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A. No dysfunction discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "a very clear danger from the growing current account deficit as a percentage of GDP." But the dollar is discussed as stable ("held the dollar essentially unchanged" - Greenspan). Not enough directional concern about dollar strength/weakness from multiple participants. **N/A**

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Extensive discussion. Greenspan: "I see no evidence at the moment that the acceleration in productivity has stopped. The second derivative for the latest data is still positive." Multiple participants describe extraordinary productivity gains. Gramlich: "unit labor costs are nearly stable." Kelley: "Productivity gains continue to be very strong." **Score: 80 +/-7 [High confidence]**
