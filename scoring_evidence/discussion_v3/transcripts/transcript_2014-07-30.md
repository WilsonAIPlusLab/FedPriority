### 2. Era Context
July 2014: The economy is ~5 years into recovery from the GFC. The taper of QE3 is underway (ending by October 2014). The FOMC is debating when to lift off from the zero lower bound. Unemployment is at 6.1% and falling faster than expected. Inflation is running below 2% target (PCE ~1.6–1.8%). The standard anchor for this era per the rubric is: INFLATION_PRESSURE ~38–42 (below target), OUTPUT_GAP ~35–42 (recovery but below potential), LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~38–42 (improving but still slack), HAWKISH_DOVISH ~40–50 (pre-liftoff phase).

### 3. Directional Analysis — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW TARGET but rising. Rosengren: "core PCE inflation to remain below 2 percent into 2016." Williams: "the broad set of incoming data is consistent with moderate growth for the rest of the year, as well as gradual progress toward our inflation goal." Plosser: "Inflation appears to be firming, and I believe it will approach the Committee's target by year-end." Kocherlakota: "PCE inflation and core PCE inflation to be around 1.6 percent over the course of 2014…only slow…gets back to target by around 2018." Evans: "we are not yet clearly on a sustained path toward our 2 percent target." Mester: "data continue to be consistent with inflation gradually picking up to our 2 percent goal." Fischer: "inflation is very close to the inflation target." Powell: "headline PCE inflation has come up to about 1.8 percent, and core is about 1.6 percent." Bullard: "Inflation is just a few ticks from target." Chair Yellen summary: "inflation has moved higher but is still running below our 2 percent objective." The emerging picture is inflation in the 1.6–1.8% range, below target but no longer as far below as in prior years. Some upside risk noted (Plosser, Fisher, Bullard) but no over-target alarm. This is clearly below-50 territory, broadly 38–43 range, biased toward the higher end of the sub-target zone given the firming noted. Score: **40**.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy as BELOW POTENTIAL (output gap negative) but narrowing. The key facts: first-quarter GDP fell ~3% (widely seen as anomalous); staff revised down potential GDP to be consistent with Okun's law. Mester: "The current Tealbook now sees the output gap being eliminated by next summer." Plosser: "The economy appears to be bouncing back from a weak first quarter and is poised to grow at a pace somewhat above trend." Fischer: "according to this forecast, we're moving the point at which the natural rate is reached three quarters earlier." Bullard: "unemployment, at 6.1 percent, is only somewhat higher than many of the estimates of the long-run rate." Kocherlakota: "I would assign more of the weak real GDP number in the first quarter to higher slack as opposed to lower potential." Brainard: "stark anomaly suggests that either potential is much weaker than we have hoped or the labor market data are substantially overstating the take-up in slack." Overall consensus: still below potential, gap being eliminated rapidly. Score: **40** (below potential but not deeply — language is "approaching" not "far from").

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as BELOW FULL EMPLOYMENT but improving rapidly. Unemployment at 6.1%. Rosengren: "I still view labor markets as having substantial slack." Yellen summary: "labor market conditions do seem to be improving somewhat more rapidly than I had expected." Most participants acknowledge improvement but substantial debate about residual slack (part-timers, long-term unemployed, participation rate). Lacker research suggests U-3 is a good statistic; Rosengren, Tarullo, Brainard, Evans, Kocherlakota emphasize broader slack measures. No one argues the labor market is already tight or at full employment; dominant view is still "slack remains" but "rapidly closing." Per rubric anchors, 2014 recovery is 35–40 range; this meeting sees it a bit higher given the positive momentum. Score: **40** (still clearly below full employment by most measures, but closing faster than expected).

**OUTPUT_GAP / LABOR_TIGHTNESS cross-check**: Both at 40 — consistent. Both are below 50 (below potential/below full employment), both acknowledging rapid improvement. This is coherent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending/investment as BELOW TREND but improving. Lockhart: "Retail industry contacts recounted a nice bounceback in consumer spending." Not collapsing, but not robustly above trend either. Score: **46** (approaching trend, moderate pace).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Participants express SOMEWHAT ELEVATED uncertainty. Score: **40** (several factors harder to assess than usual, especially Q1 GDP mystery and natural rate uncertainty, but fairly clear modal path).
