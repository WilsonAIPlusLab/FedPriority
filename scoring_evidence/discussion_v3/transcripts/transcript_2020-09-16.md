# FOMC Transcript Scoring — September 15–16, 2020

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from the September 15–16, 2020 FOMC meeting. It is fully segmented: individual speakers are identified by name (MS. DALY, MR. CLARIDA, MR. BOSTIC, MR. ROSENGREN, MR. BULLARD, MS. MESTER, MR. KAPLAN, MR. EVANS, MR. HARKER, MR. BARKIN, MS. GEORGE, MS. BRAINARD, MS. BOWMAN, MR. QUARLES, MR. KASHKARI, VICE CHAIR WILLIAMS, CHAIR POWELL). The meeting unfolds in two sessions: a Day 1 "go-around" on the economic situation, and a Day 2 policy discussion on forward guidance alternatives (A, B, C). Approximately 17 distinct participants speak.

### 2. Era Context

September 2020 is a COVID-19 recovery meeting. The economy suffered a catastrophic contraction in Q2 2020 (GDP collapsed, unemployment spiked to ~15%). By September, the recovery had begun — Q3 2020 GDP was on track for a ~30% annualized rebound, unemployment had fallen from ~15% to ~8.4%, consumer spending had recovered roughly 75% of pandemic losses. BUT: ~11.5 million jobs were still missing vs. pre-pandemic, inflation was below 2%, unemployment was still very elevated, fiscal cliff risks were prominent, and the policy discussion centered on adopting outcome-based forward guidance under the new flexible average inflation targeting (FAIT) framework. The meeting adopted Alternative B, with two formal dissents (Rosengren, Bostic) who preferred Alternative C.

### 3. Directional Signals Per Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as **below target**. Multiple quotes: Rosengren: "slack in the labor market and will be below our 2 percent inflation target throughout the three-year forecast horizon"; Kashkari: "inflation remains below 2 percent; we had core PCE inflation running at around 1.3 percent"; Powell: "overall inflation is running well below our 2 percent objective"; Brainard: "price inflation remains below pre-crisis levels and is projected to take several years to move close to target." Several note a recent bounce-back (core CPI +0.4% in August, some used cars and goods price spikes) but the overwhelming center of gravity is: well below 2%, some recent improvement but not at or above target. Score well below 50.

- Direction: below target, modestly recovering from COVID trough but nowhere near concerning. The recent pickup is noted positively but still well below 2% PCE. Score: ~28.
- Magnitude: "well below" = roughly 20-25 points below midpoint; "running at around 1.3 percent" core PCE = clearly below. Range 26-32 seems right. Anchor check: 2013-19 was 38-42 when below target but not alarmingly so. 2020 is more dire given COVID-driven collapse. But by September 2020, partial recovery means not at the trough (that was 2020 Q2). Score ~28.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as **substantially below potential**. Powell: "GDP will still be about 4 percent below pre-pandemic levels"; Mester: "level of output down 4 percent from where it was at the end of last year"; Daly: "nonfarm payroll employment is still down more than 7 percent, about 12 million jobs, from its pre-recession peak...this is a deeper ditch than at any time during the Great Recession." Strong "below potential/slack remains" language. BUT: a very strong Q3 rebound (~30% annualized) is underway; this is NOT the COVID trough (March/April 2020). The economy has recovered roughly half the COVID losses, but remains significantly below potential. Score ~25-30.
- Anchor: COVID trough (Q2 2020) = 15-22. By September 2020, partial recovery puts us in the 25-32 range. The level is still deeply below potential but not catastrophic nadir. Score ~27.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as **still significantly slack**. Unemployment = 8.4% (vs. ~3.5% pre-COVID). Brainard: "payrolls remain 11½ million lower than pre-pandemic levels, and unemployment is 5 percentage points above its pre-pandemic level"; Rosengren: "slack in the labor market"; Mester: "unemployment rate at 7.5 percent... inflation still well below our 2 percent goal." Multiple participants note 12 million fewer jobs than pre-pandemic. However, there IS a notable improvement underway (from ~15% to 8.4%), and Bullard notes 45% of unemployed are on temporary layoff. The center of gravity: significant slack, elevated unemployment, still far from full employment.
- Score ~25. Anchor: 2009, 2020 Q2 = 15-22. By September 2020, partial recovery moves it higher but still in range 22-30. Score ~25.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending and investment as **recovering but still below pre-pandemic trend**. Consumer goods spending is now ABOVE pre-pandemic levels (Brainard: "Consumer goods spending posted robust gains in June and July, supported by earlier fiscal support, and is now well above pre-crisis levels"). Housing sales back to pre-pandemic. Auto sales strong. Business equipment investment recovering sharply. BUT: services spending sharply depressed (airlines, hotels, restaurants), overall consumer expenditures recovered ~three-fourths of earlier decline. The net picture: strong sectors (goods, housing, autos) coexist with deeply depressed contact-sensitive sectors. Powell: "consumer spending has retraced 75 percent of its pandemic-related decline." Net: significantly below pre-COVID trend for total economy despite very strong goods/housing.
- Polarity check: higher = stronger spending. Evidence says: recovering strongly but below total trend. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants broadly describe **accommodative/supportive financial conditions**. Kaplan: "financial conditions are highly accommodative" (unless in contact-intensive industries); Brainard: "conditions remain broadly supportive...market sentiment has improved"; Williams: "dramatic reversal in financial markets since the dark days of March." Quarles: "loans are available for creditworthy borrowers"; "net issuance of corporate bonds has been robust." However, several note pockets of tightness: Brainard: "bank lending to businesses remains relatively tight, C&I loan growth has been negative since July"; SLOOS "indicated restrictiveness not seen since the acute phases of the Global Financial Crisis"; small businesses tight; CRE hotel/retail stressed. Rosengren warns about financial amplification. Kaplan's contacts say conditions are "highly accommodative" for large firms but tight for small. Center of gravity: broadly accommodative at aggregate level with notable pockets of stress.
- Score: ~32 (somewhat loose/supportive). Anchor: 2017-19 normal = 45-55; post-March 2020 recovery with Fed facilities = significantly easier than normal. The broad market is very accommodative; pockets of stress pull it modestly toward neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Policy go-around shows overwhelming consensus for **maximum accommodation and forward guidance committing to ELB for extended period**. The vote result was adoption of Alternative B (outcome-based forward guidance, ELB maintained until maximum employment and 2%+ inflation achieved) with only two formal dissents (Rosengren and Bostic) who preferred Alternative C — which was itself a more moderate dovish option, not hawkish. No participant advocated tightening. Multiple participants explicitly support "do whatever it takes" style accommodation for years. Kashkari preferred even stronger guidance (Alternative A). The policy action is maintaining the ELB with new strong outcome-based forward guidance committing to rates at zero for years. This is firmly in the 15-25 (strong dovish/max accommodation) range.
- Score: ~15. During QE era/ELB discussions where participants argue about "how accommodative" = 15-25 range per rubric. This meeting adopts new threshold-based forward guidance tying rates to goals — very strong dovish commitment.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (Rosengren and Bostic voted for Alternative C over Alternative B). Additionally, Mester explicitly stated she would have preferred to handle this differently (preferred Alternative C or a two-step approach), Barkin preferred Alternative C, Kaplan preferred Alternative C, and Evans preferred Alternative A (even more dovish). Harker expressed significant reservations but ultimately supported Alternative B. So: 2 formal dissents, plus 3-4 significant reservations, plus 1 who wanted even MORE accommodation (Evans). The disagreement is about strategy/communications/timing of forward guidance, not about the basic dovish direction. This is notable disagreement (multiple formal dissents + multiple reservations) within a broadly accommodative consensus.
- Score: ~48. Rubric: "Notable disagreement: distinct camps; 1-2 formal dissents; disagreement extends to assessment" = 46-60. Two formal dissents plus several expressing strong reservations fits ~48-52. Score: ~50.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Multiple participants raise financial stability prominently. Kaplan devotes a significant portion to discussing excessive risk-taking, "bubble," "distorted," "artificial" language from contacts. Rosengren devotes major discussion to CRE stress, banking fragility, and financial amplification risks. Barkin, Mester, Evans, Brainard, George all discuss financial stability in the context of forward guidance debate. Multiple participants wrestle with whether the ELB commitment creates financial instability risks. Powell also addresses financial stability at the end. This is a clear moderate-to-prominent topic.
- Score: ~50. Anchor: 2013-19 typical = 20-35. This meeting is more elevated due to the CRE/banking stress discussion and the forward guidance debate about financial stability escape clauses. Score ~50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee adopts outcome-based forward guidance committing to maintain rates at 0-0.25% until maximum employment is reached AND inflation has risen to 2% and is on track to moderately exceed 2% for some time. This is a very strong easing commitment — threshold-based guidance promising near-zero rates for years. Multiple SEP participants project liftoff no earlier than 2023-2024. Multiple rule simulations show ELB through 2025-2027.
- Score: ~5-8. Rubric: "Strongest easing commitment: threshold-based or date-based guidance promising near-zero rates (e.g., Evans Rule, 'at least through 2015')" = 1-5; "Strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation ahead" = 6-15. This is literally a threshold-based forward guidance (like the Evans Rule). Score ~6.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. My evidence says strongly dovish/easing forward signal. Score 6 is correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is very prominently expressed by virtually all participants. Bostic: "tremendous amount of uncertainty that still exists...the increased dispersion in experiences...other sources of uncertainty make forecasting difficult"; Mester: "the level of uncertainty surrounding the outlook remaining very high"; Kashkari: "continued high uncertainty...the outlook's even more uncertain—uncertain in the path of the virus as well as high policy and political uncertainty"; Williams: "I still feel the magnitude of uncertainty regarding the outlook to be elevated relative to historical norms"; Bullard: "macroeconomic uncertainty remains exceptionally high." Multiple scenarios (second wave, no fiscal support, vaccine surprise) are discussed. The virus path, fiscal policy, and election uncertainty compound to create "genuinely elevated" uncertainty.
- Score: ~72. Rubric: 61-75 = "High: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range; standard models questioned." Nearly all participants explicitly use "highly uncertain" or equivalent. But this is NOT the emergency meeting level of March 2020 (85-95). By September 2020, there is somewhat more visibility than in the spring, but still deeply uncertain. Score ~72.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (27) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25): Both below 50, consistent with each other. In line: deep recession with high unemployment. The economy is recovering from COVID trough but still far below potential. Consistent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (15) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (6): Both very dovish. HAWKISH is 15 because participants maintain max accommodation / favor ELB + QE. FORWARD is 6 because the adopted guidance is threshold-based ELB commitment. Slight divergence: HAWKISH slightly higher because while current action is max dovish, participants also explicitly discuss financial stability concerns and escape clauses (mildly hawkish contingencies). This divergence is small and justified: current stance and forward guidance are both very dovish, with very minor contingency language in paragraph 5.
- DISSENT (50) vs. overall dovish lean (15): The dissent is about the FORM of the dovish commitment (how strong, what type) not about direction. Two dissenters wanted a weaker commitment (Alt C), one wanted a stronger one (Kashkari preferred Alt A). This explains the higher dissent with a uniformly dovish lean — the disagreement is tactical, not directional.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 28 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Powell: "overall inflation is running well below our 2 percent objective. Despite a few idiosyncratic factors...I see by far the likeliest outcome is that inflation will remain below target for some time in context of high unemployment."
- Kashkari: "inflation remains below 2 percent; we had core PCE inflation running at around 1.3 percent."
- Brainard: "price inflation remains below pre-crisis levels and is projected to take several years to move close to target and several years beyond that to meet the average inflation goals set out in the Committee's new framework."

**Center of gravity:** Participants uniformly see inflation as well below target (~1.3% core PCE), with modest recent pickup from COVID trough but requiring years of accommodation to return to 2%. This maps to the 26-40 range ("below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation") with a score of 28 reflecting that this is somewhat below the 2013-19 baseline (38-42) given COVID-specific disruptions and the deeper hole.

**Minority views:** George and Bullard note upside inflation risks from fiscal stimulus + potential vaccine/demand surge, and a few participants (George, Barkin) note that some goods sectors are seeing meaningful price increases. These views push uncertainty band upward. Rosengren explicitly expects to remain below 2% for three-plus years.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 27 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Powell: "Even if third-quarter real GDP growth is 30 percent, as broadly expected, GDP will still be about 4 percent below pre-pandemic levels."
- Daly: "nonfarm payroll employment is still down more than 7 percent, about 12 million jobs, from its pre-recession peak. And to put that in perspective—this is a deeper ditch than at any time during the Great Recession."
- Mester: "in terms of the level of output and employment, we are still considerably down from where we were in February. In fact, we've lost about six years of output and job growth."

**Center of gravity:** The economy has recovered roughly half of COVID losses (Q3 ~30% GDP rebound) but remains ~4% below pre-pandemic GDP and ~12 million jobs short. Participants universally invoke "deep hole," "substantially below potential," "far from where we were." Maps to 16-30 range (deep recession, far below potential). Score 27 reflects that we are off the absolute Q2 trough (which would be 15-22) but still in a deep recession context with substantial slack remaining.

**Minority views:** Bullard takes a more optimistic view (sees faster-than-expected recovery, Q3 ~30% pace), and Clarida notes "broad-based positive surprises." But even optimists emphasize the level remains far below potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 25 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "Payrolls remain 11½ million lower than pre-pandemic levels, and unemployment is 5 percentage points above its pre-pandemic level. Unemployment is 13 percent for Black workers."
- Rosengren: "I expect that we will have slack in the labor market...throughout the three-year forecast horizon."
- Mester: "District payroll employment remains almost 9 percent below its level in February, and the unemployment rate is about 9.5 percent. These data suggest that there is a large pool of available workers."

**Center of gravity:** With unemployment at 8.4% nationally (down from ~15% at peak), 11.5 million jobs missing, and near-universal language about "far from full employment," this maps to the 16-30 range (high unemployment, significant slack). Score 25 reflects improvement from the COVID trough (which would be 15-18) but still deeply below the pre-pandemic labor market (which was at 65-70 range). Several participants note the unusual features: both high unemployment AND reported difficulty finding workers for some jobs (childcare, benefits issues), but the center of gravity on labor market conditions is clearly "significant slack."

**Minority views:** Bullard is more optimistic (45% of unemployed are temporary layoffs, sees rapid recall); Barkin notes the strange juxtaposition of high unemployment + firms struggling to fill jobs. These are dissenting data points but don't shift the center of gravity.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 40 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "Consumer goods spending posted robust gains in June and July, supported by earlier fiscal support, and is now well above pre-crisis levels...total consumer expenditures have recovered about three-fourths of their earlier decline."
- Powell: "consumer spending has retraced 75 percent of its pandemic-related decline. Goods spending is now actually above pre-pandemic levels. Outlays for services have been slower to recover."
- Mester: "Durable and nondurable goods consumption, housing activity, and business investment have improved notably this quarter."

**Center of gravity:** Goods spending above pre-pandemic; housing/autos very strong; business equipment investment recovering. But total spending is still 25% below the pandemic-related decline (i.e., three-fourths recovered means one-fourth still missing). Services (airlines, hotels, restaurants) deeply depressed. Net: below pre-crisis trend, recovering strongly but not yet restored. Maps to ~40, which is below trend but approaching it.

**Minority views:** Clarida and Quarles are more optimistic about the consumer bounce-back and pent-up savings deployment. Brainard and Kashkari are more skeptical about the savings drawdown driving future spending. Evans worries consumer cannot be the engine of growth going forward.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 32 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kaplan: "financial conditions are highly accommodative. Market contacts are increasingly expressing concern to me about excessive risk-taking."
- Brainard: "conditions remain broadly supportive, as Lorie Logan noted, despite the recent selloff in equities...Overall financing conditions are accommodative."
- Brainard (pockets of tightness): "bank lending to businesses remains relatively tight, and C&I loan growth has been negative since July...the SLOOS bank lending standards index in late July indicated restrictiveness not seen since the acute phases of the Global Financial Crisis."

**Center of gravity:** The aggregate financial conditions picture is "broadly supportive" and "highly accommodative" for large firms and market participants. Multiple participants note easy credit, low rates, strong corporate bond issuance. However, notable pockets of tightness: small businesses, commercial real estate (hotels, retail), and bank lending to businesses. Rosengren specifically flags financial amplification risks. The center of gravity is "broadly supportive / somewhat loose" given Fed facilities and low rates, but notably tighter than the pre-COVID normal for some segments. Score 32 = somewhat loose to neutral range, reflecting the accommodation-vs-pockets-of-stress balance.

**Minority views:** Rosengren views financial conditions as more fragile; Kaplan, Brainard, and Mester warn about financial stability risks from prolonged accommodation. These are hawkish tail concerns but don't shift the overall accommodative characterization.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 15 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Adopted outcome: Alternative B — commitment to maintain 0-0.25% target range until maximum employment reached AND inflation at 2% on track to moderately exceed 2%. This is a maximum accommodation posture.
- Kashkari: "I strongly support the new Statement...I prefer alternative A in this meeting. I think alternative B is an important step forward...My preference is that it would have been stronger."
- Brainard: "We need to remind ourselves just how difficult it will be to return inflation to 2 percent...It is imperative that we commit to take forceful action if we want to have any real chance of changing inflation expectations."

**Center of gravity:** The Committee unanimously holds rates at ELB (0-0.25%) and adopts strong outcome-based forward guidance. Even the two formal dissenters (Rosengren, Bostic) voted for Alternative C, which was ALSO a dovish, accommodative option — their objection was to the form of the forward guidance, not to accommodation per se. Kashkari wanted even more accommodation (Alt A). Score 15 reflects "strong dovish: majority favors easing or maintaining max accommodation" — this is a full-accommodation meeting with no voices calling for tightening.

**Minority views:** No one advocates for tightening. Some (Rosengren, Kaplan, Barkin, Mester) express concern about financial stability risks from prolonged ELB, but they translate this into "softer forward guidance" (Alt C), not hawkish intent. This pulls the uncertainty band slightly upward from the most extreme dovish range.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
