# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-03-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It contains the go-around discussion of regional and national economic conditions, followed by Chairman Greenspan's summary and the policy round. Speakers include: Parry, Broaddus, Boehne, Hoenig, McTeer, Minehan, Moskow, Guynn, Stern, Melzer, McDonough, Kelley, Yellen, Phillips, Jordan, Lindsey, and Greenspan.

### Era Context
March 1996: mid-1990s expansion, over five years into the recovery. CPI running around 3%. The Fed had just cut the funds rate at the January meeting (to 5.25%). Strong February employment report (705,000 jobs) surprised markets. Long-term rates had risen sharply (50+ bps since last meeting). Economy operating near potential. Inventory correction from 1995 was winding down.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as roughly stable at around 3% CPI -- not accelerating but not declining either. Parry: "core CPI inflation remaining around 3 percent." Boehne: "little if any acceleration in inflation...inflation will continue to be subdued." Hoenig: "Wage and price pressures still remain modest." Moskow: "little upward pressure or declines for a broad range of items." Phillips: "inflation risks have increased since the last FOMC meeting." Melzer is more hawkish, noting "worrisome" ECI data and concern about 3% inflation expectations. Minehan sees "some rather modest upward pressure on wages and prices." The center of gravity is that inflation is stable around 3% (above the implicit target) with modest upside risks. This is slightly above where they'd want it but not accelerating. Direction: slightly above comfortable level, but contained. Score should be in the 48-55 range. CPI at 3% in mid-1990s context, with most seeing it as contained but a few noting upside risks. Score: 52.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation stable around 3%, slightly above comfort, risks tilted up. 52 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Multiple participants describe the economy as operating "near potential" or "near full capacity." Parry: "growth perhaps slightly above its 2 percent potential rate" and "unemployment and capacity utilization rates are near common estimates of their natural rates." Broaddus: "the economy has been operating for a while now somewhere in the neighborhood of nonaccelerating inflation capacity." Moskow: economy at "high levels." Hoenig: "the fundamentals remain strong...growth at about the potential rate of 2 percent." Moskow's analysis: "the output gap was about nil prior to 1995 and...some slack has built up." Guynn thinks "2 percent is probably a little less than potential." The center of gravity: economy near potential, perhaps very slightly above. Bright-line rule: "near potential" = 48-52. Score: 51.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Near potential with slight upside lean = 51. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Widespread reports of tight labor markets. Hoenig: "labor markets appear to continue to get tighter. More of our directors are reporting tight labor markets." Stern: "Labor markets remain very tight." Moskow: "labor markets remain tight throughout the District...unemployment rate in our states is still averaging about 1 full percentage point below the national average." Lindsey: "anecdotal comments around the table universally have referred to tight labor markets...'you can't hire people at any wage.'" Minehan: "Unemployment in the region is low, only 5 percent...New Hampshire now down to 3 percent." McTeer: "Labor shortages continue to be mentioned as a constraint." However, wage growth remains subdued despite tightness -- Greenspan's "job insecurity" hypothesis. The labor market is tight by level. Score: 57.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Widespread tight labor market reports with low unemployment = 57. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: The economy is showing resilience. Broaddus: retail contacts "guardedly optimistic," "industrial production, car sales, and other retail sales are stronger than we had anticipated." Boehne: "consumer...still appears to have ample purchasing power for sustainable spending growth." Moskow: "housing and retail sales continue to perform well." Melzer: "Retail sales, factory orders, and housing starts also indicate underlying strength." Phillips: business fixed investment "pretty unlikely...continue at the same pace as in 1994 and 1995" but no major pullback. Housing strong in many districts. Overall: moderate to slightly above trend, with resilience being the key theme. Score: 55.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Reports of resilient, moderately strong spending = 55. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term rates rose sharply (50+ bps since last meeting). Broaddus notes "extraordinary 25 basis point jump in the 30-year bond rate." But credit availability remains good. Hoenig: "loans are readily available at our banks." Phillips: "plenty of credit availability. The banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion." Melzer: "Loan demand, especially for commercial loans, is still strong." The rise in long rates is a tightening signal, but credit conditions remain easy. Mixed but leaning slightly tight from the rate backup. Score: 48.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Long rates up but credit freely available = roughly neutral, 48. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous agreement to hold at 5.25% with symmetric directive. No dissents. The center of gravity is clearly "hold steady." Several participants note that the next move could be up if data strengthen. Parry: "If economic activity continues to pick up, it might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate." Broaddus: "we may need to reverse the move we made last month at some point in the not too distant future." Melzer: concerned policy may not be neutral, thinks stance should be "somewhat restrictive." Minehan: "be vigilant on the side of keeping interest rates and monetary policy restrictive enough." Phillips: "our next move could be either up or down." The lean is hold with a mild tightening bias -- several voices suggesting the next move is more likely up than down. Score: 55.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with mild tightening bias, several noting next move likely up = 55. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. Broad agreement on holding. Melzer expressed reservations about the prior easing and the current inflation strategy, but agreed with holding. The range of views is modest -- mostly agreement on the outlook and policy. Score: 18.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Near-unanimous with minor emphasis differences = 18. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan devoted significant attention to stock market valuations: "The market is probably high by any objective measure." Yellen: "concerned...about the possibility of a significant stock market correction." Lindsey discussed capital market price levels as an "unresolved issue." But most other participants did not focus on financial stability. This is more than passing mention but not a dominant topic for the group. Score: 25.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. A few participants (Greenspan, Yellen, Lindsey) discuss meaningfully, most do not = 25. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive adopted. Greenspan: "doing nothing today" with risks balanced. But forward lean is mildly hawkish -- multiple participants flag that the next move could be a tightening. Broaddus wants a conference call if March data are strong. Parry says it "might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate." The forward signal is neutral to mildly hawkish. Score: 55.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Hold with mild tightening bias going forward = 55. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Phillips: "more uncertainty than would usually be the case" due to weather/shutdowns. Greenspan: "we probably are going to be surprised by more rather than less volatility." Phillips and Guynn note fog in the data. Yellen mentions risks in both directions. Jordan notes difficulty interpreting numbers. But overall, this is standard-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty, not extreme. Score: 38.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Somewhat elevated due to data fog but not extreme = 38. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Labor tightness slightly higher, which makes sense as participants universally report tight labor markets while the output gap is described as roughly at potential. Labor tightness is running ahead of the output gap reading -- consistent with the era.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Aligned. Both reflect a hold with mild tightening bias.

### Speaker Count for Center of Gravity
- On inflation: ~12 speakers address it. Most see it as stable/contained (Boehne, Minehan, McTeer, Guynn, Hoenig, Moskow, Jordan). A few see upside risks (Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Phillips, Stern). Net: stable but with upside risks.
- On policy: All ~17 participants agree on holding. Several lean toward next-move-up.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "What I find most striking about the current situation is the consistency of many inflation indicators. They all seem to be pointing toward core CPI inflation remaining around 3 percent."
- MR. BOEHNE: "We are more than five years into an economic expansion. We have seen little if any acceleration in inflation."
- MR. MELZER: "some recent data are worrisome, including the employment cost index for total compensation...the long-bond yield...also has increased more than 50 basis points since the last meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants see inflation as stable around 3% CPI -- slightly above comfort -- with no acceleration but modest upside risks from tight labor markets and the ECI jump.

**Minority views:** Melzer sees inflation expectations at an unacceptable 3% and wants active disinflation. Guynn is optimistic inflation could moderate to 2.5%. These widen the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "both the unemployment and the capacity utilization rates are near common estimates of their natural rates"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the economy has been operating for a while now somewhere in the neighborhood of nonaccelerating inflation capacity"
- MR. MOSKOW: "the output gap was about nil prior to 1995 and...some slack has built up in the aggregate economy"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants describe the economy as operating at or very near potential, with the February rebound restoring growth after weather/inventory disruptions.

**Minority views:** Moskow thinks some slack may have built up (slightly below). Parry thinks unemployment and capacity are "slightly on the low side of their natural rates" (slightly above). Narrow range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. LINDSEY: "The anecdotal comments around the table universally have referred to tight labor markets...In Indianapolis...'you can't hire people at any wage.'"
- MR. STERN: "Labor markets remain very tight. There are a few more, but still scattered, indications of increasing wage pressures."
- MR. HOENIG: "labor markets appear to continue to get tighter. More of our directors are reporting tight labor markets for entry-level and some skilled jobs."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually every district reports tight labor markets, but wage pressures remain surprisingly subdued due to "job insecurity" effects. Tightness is real but not yet translating into wage acceleration.

**Minority views:** Minehan notes surprisingly low hourly earnings growth in New England despite 3-5% unemployment. The puzzle of tight markets without wage pressure narrows the band somewhat.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "most of the other recent monthly reports on industrial production, car sales, and other retail sales are stronger than we had anticipated"
- MR. BOEHNE: "The consumer, while somewhat stretched in terms of debt, still appears to have ample purchasing power for sustainable spending growth. And the strengthening in residential construction reflects positive consumer sentiment."
- MS. PHILLIPS: business fixed investment "pretty unlikely...continue at the same pace as in 1994 and 1995" but "the cost of capital is still fairly low" and "profits and cash flows should be reasonably strong."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is resilient and housing is strong across most districts. Business fixed investment is moderating from high levels but still solid. The February rebound was broad-based. Overall moderately above trend.

**Minority views:** Yellen concerned about possible stock market correction and lagged impact of higher rates on housing. Minehan sees possible downside risk. Slight downward pull on the uncertainty band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "the really spectacular rise in both intermediate- and long-term interest rates since the beginning of the year including...the extraordinary 25 basis point jump in the 30-year bond rate"
- MR. HOENIG: "loans are readily available at our banks to both commercial and other borrowers"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "plenty of credit availability. The banks and the markets are still reasonably well positioned to support expansion."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Long-term rates have risen sharply (50+ bps), representing some tightening of conditions. However, credit remains freely available and bank lending is strong. Net conditions are roughly neutral.

**Minority views:** Jordan notes the yield curve had been priced for much lower rates and has now readjusted. The rate backup could tighten conditions further if sustained.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "further easing would be inappropriate, given the increasing strength of the economy...If economic activity continues to pick up, it might soon be appropriate to increase the funds rate."
- MR. BROADDUS: "we have to contemplate the possibility that we may need to reverse the move we made last month at some point in the not too distant future"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would subscribe...to doing nothing today...an obvious symmetry."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous support for holding at 5.25% with symmetric directive. The lean is mildly hawkish -- several participants signal the next move is more likely up, and no one advocates further easing. The prior cut is now seen as sufficient insurance.

**Minority views:** Melzer thinks policy may not even be neutral and advocates a more restrictive stance. McTeer is the most optimistic on inflation. Range is narrow on action, somewhat wider on forward lean.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- All participants explicitly agree with the Chairman's recommendation to hold with symmetric directive.
- MR. MELZER expresses concern that "the current policy stance is inappropriate given the 3 percent inflation expectations" but agrees with holding for now.
- No formal dissents.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on holding with symmetric bias. Minor differences in emphasis about inflation risks and the wisdom of the prior easing, but no real disagreement on the current action.

**Minority views:** Melzer's critique of the prior easing and inflation strategy represents the strongest dissenting voice, but he does not dissent on today's action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The market is probably high by any objective measure that we can find...If we get a very significant contraction in stock prices, I think it will have a quite measurable wealth effect."
- MS. YELLEN: "I am particularly concerned at this stage about the possibility of a significant stock market correction. The current level of stock prices is not impossible, but it is increasingly difficult to justify in terms of fundamentals."
- MR. LINDSEY: "the level of prices in capital markets...a booming capital market has made equity capital very cheap"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Greenspan, Yellen, and Lindsey devote meaningful discussion to stock market valuations and financial conditions. Most other participants do not focus on financial stability concerns. Moderate attention from a few key voices.

**Minority views:** N/A -- this is about attention level, not direction.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Symmetric directive, "doing nothing today"
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would hope that we might have a conference call" if March data show continued strength, to consider reversing course
- MS. PHILLIPS: "I can well imagine that our next move could be either up or down"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Forward signal is neutral on paper (symmetric directive) but the discussion lean is mildly hawkish, with multiple participants flagging that the next move is more likely a tightening if data continue strong. No one suggests further easing.

**Minority views:** Phillips notes next move could be either direction. Guynn remains optimistic on inflation, suggesting less urgency to tighten.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "unusual circumstances of late 1995 and early 1996 certainly have muddied the waters considerably and left us with more uncertainty than would usually be the case"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we probably are going to be surprised by more rather than less volatility in the economy. I do not know in which direction the economy will be going."
- MR. GUYNN: "the unusual circumstances...have muddied the waters considerably"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated due to weather disruptions, government shutdowns, and data revisions making the recent past hard to read. But most participants feel the underlying outlook is reasonably clear -- moderate growth near potential. Not extreme uncertainty.

**Minority views:** Greenspan's warning about volatility and the inevitability of recession at some point pushes uncertainty somewhat higher.
