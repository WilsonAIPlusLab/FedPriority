## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from a special videoconference held on March 4, 2014 — Yellen's first meeting as Chair. It is **not** a standard FOMC policy meeting. The sole stated purpose is to discuss options for revising forward guidance language ahead of the March 18–19, 2014 FOMC meeting. There is **no economic go-around**, no staff presentation on current conditions, and no policy vote. Speakers are identified by name (Kocherlakota, Bullard, Rosengren, Lockhart, Fisher, Williams, Evans, George, Tarullo, Stein, Powell, Dudley, Yellen). Several speakers (Pianalto, Weinberg/Lacker substitute) appear to have been redacted from the transcript (pages 32–35 jump with only headers visible).

**Critical implication for scoring**: Because this is a communications/governance session rather than an economic assessment session, the normal scoring dimensions are severely constrained. Participants are NOT describing their current views on the economy as a whole; they are discussing which language formulation to adopt for the FOMC statement. Economic data is cited only incidentally to justify policy communications choices, not as the focus of deliberation.

### 2. Era Context

Early 2014: The U.S. economy is in mid-recovery. Unemployment at 6.6% (mentioned by Yellen, near the 6.5% threshold). Inflation running below 2% PCE target — multiple participants note this as a reason for continued accommodation. QE tapering is underway ("asset purchase program" being wound down). The ZLB is still binding. "False dawns" concern Yellen. The broad macro backdrop is: slack remains, inflation below target, accommodative policy warranted — but liftoff is on the horizon in 1–2 years. I will score based strictly on what participants state in this document, not from era assumptions.

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants consistently reference inflation running **below** the 2% target. Yellen: "possibility of inflation running persistently and significantly below our objective has become more prominent." Kocherlakota argues for an inflation floor because "we are not returning to its historical patterns" — implying below-target is the problem. Fisher discusses "lower-than-expected inflation outcomes." Powell: "projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-term goal." Evans: "I don't think [inflation] is going to increase to 2 percent very soon." Multiple participants discuss how 2% is perceived as a ceiling not a target, confirming persistent below-target inflation. Direction: **below target, but not alarming deflation**. Score should be in the 35–42 range per anchors (2013–19 below target).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Very limited direct assessment. Yellen mentions "numerous false dawns" and the recovery is still underway. Powell explicitly notes "output gap is not directly observable and famously difficult to estimate in real time" and that "uncertainty about the size of the gap is unusually high and is probably skewed in a way that indicators, like the unemployment gap, are likely to understate the real resource gap." Stein references "as we normalize" — implying below potential. Bullard notes "output growth today is relatively robust." The dominant view is that recovery is ongoing but below potential — with significant uncertainty about how much below. Direction: **below potential, recovery continuing**. Score in low-to-mid 30s (2011–14 recovery band).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The unemployment rate is explicitly cited at 6.6% (Yellen). The 6.5% threshold is about to be crossed. Evans mentions "unemployment rate of late suggests that it might not be the best marker." Kocherlakota warns against "employment threshold" because it could let the Fed raise rates prematurely before inflation returns. Powell notes labor market is recovering but "complete recovery" is not there yet. Multiple participants suggest the labor market is still recovering but approaching threshold level. Direction: **still below full employment but significantly improved** — unemployment at 6.6%, slack remains. Per anchors: 2011–14 labor market = 35–40. By early 2014 with 6.6% UR (above NAIRU of ~5.5%), the market is tighter than 2011–12 but still below full employment. Score ~38–42.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: This dimension receives essentially **no direct participant discussion**. Yellen mentions "weaker tone of recent data" in passing, and Stein mentions "as the news comes in a little bit good." Bullard notes "output growth today is relatively robust." There is a brief reference to recovery continuing. Given the near-absence of discussion, this should be scored at low confidence with limited signal; the tone of participants treating the economy as still-recovering but not strong suggests below-trend spending. However, this is inferential. Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Not the focus of this meeting. The meeting is about forward guidance language, not financial market conditions. Several participants discuss whether to include "financial developments" or "financial stability" language in the statement, but this is about what to say in communications, not assessments of current conditions. Yellen mentions keeping "financial conditions accommodative." Stein says "if we just keep the LSAPs going until roughly the end of 2014, it's hard for people to envision a world in which we lift off before mid-2015" — implying current conditions are still accommodative. The overall picture from the few references: financial conditions are broadly accommodative (consistent with ongoing QE, ZLB). Score in the 25–35 range (loose/accommodative).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants support continued accommodation. Zero participants advocate for raising rates or tightening now. The debate is entirely about how to communicate the conditions for future rate increases, not whether to tighten now. The dominant orientation is: keep rates at ZLB for "a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends." Most converge on option 5 (minimalist), which preserves the accommodative stance. Kocherlakota is the most dovish (wants inflation floor, no rate rise until inflation projects at 2%). Bullard and Fisher are most "hawkish" in their skepticism of committing to extended accommodation but still favor option 5 (maintaining status quo). No one advocates for near-term tightening. Direction: **clearly dovish/accommodative lean**. Per anchors: QE era / pre-liftoff 2014 = 40–50. This is a discussion meeting, not a decision meeting; all views reflect maintaining accommodation. Score ~35–42 (lean dovish, substantial accommodation maintained).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There are meaningful disagreements about which option to adopt, but the differences are about communication tactics, not fundamental disagreements about economic conditions or near-term policy direction. The divisions include: option 3 supporters (Kocherlakota, Bullard partially) vs. option 5 supporters (Bullard, Lockhart, Fisher, Evans, George, Stein, Powell, Dudley, Williams leans toward option 2/5 hybrid). On specific language: some dislike "complete recovery" language (Stein, George, Powell), some dislike tying liftoff to asset purchases (Lockhart, Fisher). On financial stability language: Rosengren wants none; George, Fisher, Tarullo, Powell want minimal mention; Dudley prefers "financial market developments." Kocherlakota has the most distinctive position (option 3, no employment threshold). These disagreements are real but tactical, not fundamental. No formal dissents (this is not a policy vote meeting). Score: moderate disagreement, ~35–45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Multiple participants discuss whether and how to reference "financial stability" or "financial developments" in the statement. Bullard, Fisher, George, Tarullo, Williams, Powell, Dudley all weigh in. However, this is a **meta-discussion** — not an assessment that financial stability is currently at risk. Participants are debating the appropriate amount of weight to place in the language, not assessing current risks. The actual attention paid to financial stability as a substantive topic (vs. as a phrase to include in text) is limited. Score: moderate-low, perhaps 25–35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: This is the entire subject of the meeting. All participants discuss what forward guidance to provide. The guidance favored by the majority (option 5 variants) involves: "considerable time after asset purchase program ends," "complete recovery in the labor market more than a year away," and inflation running below 2% as a condition for maintaining accommodation. This is a **dovish forward signal** — the guidance commits to ZLB for an extended period post-tapering. The discussion of "paragraph 6" about post-liftoff rates adds a mild forward signal that rates post-liftoff will be low for structural reasons. The overwhelming direction: **extended accommodative period, dovish forward guidance**. Per anchors: 2014 taper era = 8–15. Score: ~10–18 (strong easing/accommodation bias, explicit commitment to remain at ZLB for considerable period).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty expressed. Powell: "output gap is not directly observable…uncertainty about the size of the gap is unusually high." Yellen: "numerous false dawns," "asymmetry" at ZLB. Stein notes uncertainty creates asymmetric case for waiting. Bullard flags uncertainty about employment shortfall concept. However, participants are not expressing extreme uncertainty about the economic path — the tone is that the recovery is proceeding and they can see liftoff approaching. The uncertainty is primarily about measurement/operationalization of guidance language, not about the economic trajectory. Score: ~35–45 (somewhat elevated, specific concerns about real-time measurement, not deep epistemic uncertainty about macro path).

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, both in recovery-but-below-potential territory. Gap between them is modest and justified — labor market is somewhat more advanced in recovery than overall output. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (38) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (14): Both on dovish side. The gap (14 vs 38) reflects the fact that HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN captures current policy stance (accommodative, hold), while FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE specifically captures the forward signal content (explicit commitment to ZLB for "considerable time"), which is even more dovish than a simple "hold with dovish lean." This divergence is appropriate: the current action preference is "hold" at ZLB (moderately dovish), while the forward signal is explicitly "remain at ZLB for a long time after purchases end" (very dovish). Consistent with rubric guidance that separates current action from forward lean.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 37 ±5 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "the possibility of inflation running persistently and significantly below our objective has become more prominent."
- Powell: "particularly if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-term goal."
- Kocherlakota: "we are fully committed to keeping inflation close to 2 percent" — implying it currently is not; the need to "build the credibility of our 2 percent target by assuring the public and markets that we are in fact doing whatever we can to facilitate a rapid return to 2 percent inflation."
- Evans: "I doubt that inflation is going to increase very quickly, and I don't think it's going to increase to 2 percent very soon."

**Center of gravity**: Participants unanimously treat inflation as running meaningfully below the 2% target, with some concern about persistent undershooting; this places the score in the disinflation range (26–40), leaning toward the higher end of that band given that it is not described as alarming deflation but rather a persistent shortfall.

**Minority views**: No participant argues inflation is at or above target; the disagreement is only about how much to feature the inflation shortfall in forward guidance language.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 35 ±7 | Low confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "I would urge everyone to remain mindful, though, of the numerous 'false dawns' the economy has presented."
- Powell: "the output gap is not directly observable and famously difficult to estimate in real time. And I believe that, today, uncertainty about the size of the gap is unusually high and is probably skewed in a way that indicators, like the unemployment gap, are likely to understate the real resource gap."
- Stein: Discusses asymmetry at ZLB because "if you get a little bit behind, you can always raise, but if you raise prematurely, you can't undo that" — implying slack remains.
- Bullard: "output growth today is relatively robust" — the lone voice suggesting stronger real activity.

**Center of gravity**: The dominant view is that the economy is still below potential with significant remaining slack, though recovering. Powell's explicit statement that the unemployment gap likely understates the true resource gap suggests the center of gravity is below 50 and meaningfully so. The "robust output growth" reference from Bullard is an outlier. Score of 35 is consistent with the 2011–14 recovery anchor range (35–42), toward the lower end given Powell's slack emphasis.

**Minority views**: Bullard's "relatively robust output growth" view is the only signal pointing toward a higher score, but this is offered as an aside, not a central argument.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 40 ±5 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "The unemployment rate, at 6.6 percent, is very close to the threshold in our FOMC statement."
- Kocherlakota: "An employment threshold actually works against the promotion of maximum employment" — citing concern that employment is still not at maximum.
- Evans: "complete recovery in the labor market appears to be more than a year away" — quoted as a candidate guideline, reflecting participant consensus that full employment is not yet achieved.
- Powell: "complete recovery in the labor market as a threshold — you know, you think about those words and we may never have that, if you take it literally."

**Center of gravity**: Unemployment at 6.6% is above NAIRU (typically estimated ~5.5%), so labor slack remains. However, the market has tightened considerably from crisis lows. The 2011–14 anchor range is 35–40; by early 2014 with unemployment approaching the 6.5% threshold, the labor market is tighter than mid-recovery 2011–12. Score of 40 reflects: still below full employment, but meaningfully better than 2011–12.

**Minority views**: No participant argues the labor market is tight or at full employment. The debate is only about how to characterize remaining slack in the statement language.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 28 ±8 | Low confidence**

**Evidence**:

- Rosengren: "Given the weakness in incoming data, I would certainly not want the language to tighten financial conditions."
- Stein: "we've got considerable support—beyond what's in the FOMC statement—for the guidance, both in your credibility on this issue and in the LSAP timing factor, which is that if we just keep the LSAPs going until roughly the end of 2014, it's hard for people to envision a world in which we lift off before mid–2015." — Implies financial conditions remain anchored/accommodative.

**Center of gravity**: The explicit stated goal of the new forward guidance is to keep financial conditions accommodative. Participants express concern about language that might "tighten financial conditions." The current state is described as accommodative. ### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 38 ±6 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "it remains important that our forward guidance continues to convey, in terms that are as clear as possible, the conditions under which policy will remain highly accommodative in order to promote recovery."
- Evans: "The economic and global environments continue to call for strongly accommodative monetary policy in the United States."
- Kocherlakota (most dovish): Advocates option 3 with inflation floor, no rate rise until inflation projects at 2%.
- Bullard/Fisher/Lockhart (least dovish within group): Favor minimal guidance changes (option 5), maintaining current accommodative stance without additional commitments — but explicitly not advocating tightening.

**Center of gravity**: Every participant favors maintaining the ZLB and accommodative stance. Zero voices for near-term tightening. The debate is about how much additional dovish commitment to add to the statement. This places the meeting squarely in the "lean dovish to balanced" zone (41–50), but the universal support for continued accommodation with no tightening advocates pushes it modestly below 41. Score of 38 reflects the pre-liftoff, QE-tapering-era anchor of 40–50, adjusted downward for the explicit "strongly accommodative" framing from multiple participants.

**Minority views**: Fisher and Bullard are relatively the most conservative/hawkish, but they still favor option 5 which maintains full accommodation; they simply do not want additional commitments.

**Center of gravity**: Meaningful disagreement exists on specific formulations. However, on the core question — maintain accommodation, no tightening — there is unanimity. Score of 38 reflects "moderate differences: notable differences about pace/risk; 1 dissent or 2–3 reservations" range (31–45), toward the lower end because the underlying policy stance is unanimous.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 14 ±5 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "it remains important that our forward guidance continues to convey…the conditions under which policy will remain highly accommodative in order to promote recovery and a lasting end to this lengthy zero-interest-rate episode."
- Williams (proposed language): "a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy will remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends."
- Kocherlakota: The FOMC is "highly unlikely to raise the target fed funds rate at least until the one- to two-year-ahead outlook for inflation has risen back to 2 percent."
- Dudley: "I very much prefer providing guidance about the trajectory of interest rates after liftoff, both the gradient and the terminal level of the funds rate" — the preferred trajectory is a slow, gradual rise from low levels.

**Center of gravity**: The entire meeting is focused on crafting guidance that reinforces a low-for-long signal. Even the most conservative participants (Bullard, Fisher) explicitly want language that maintains accommodation. The proposed paragraph 6 language (about post-liftoff rates remaining unusually low) adds an additional easing signal. This firmly places the tone at the "strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation ahead" level (6–15). The 2014 taper anchor (8–15) is exactly right. Score of 14 reflects that the Committee is slightly beyond the strongest "threshold/date-based" commitment (option 3 abandoned) but still explicitly signaling extended accommodation.

**Minority views**: No participant advocates for a hawkish forward signal. The only variation is how explicitly dovish to be (Kocherlakota wanting more vs. Bullard wanting less).

The uncertainty is more about measurement and communications than about the trajectory. Score of 42 is in the "somewhat elevated" range (31–45), consistent with the 2013–14 stable-expansion anchor (25–35) pushed upward for the explicit Powell/Yellen/Stein uncertainty language.

**Minority views**: No participant expresses near-certainty or dismisses uncertainty. It is broadly shared but not extreme.
