# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — March 20-21, 2007

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the March 20-21, 2007 FOMC meeting, covering both the economic go-around (Day 1) and the policy discussion (Day 2). Speakers are identified by name. The transcript is largely segmented, though the file is missing pages 44-48 (covering Minehan's full go-around and possibly brief remarks by others) and pages 89-91 (Moskow's and portions of Yellen's policy go-around). Despite these gaps, the bulk of substantive discussion is preserved: Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Pianalto, Lockhart, Stern, Poole, Geithner, Kohn, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, and Bernanke's summary are all substantially represented. Staff content is ignored for scoring.

### 2. Era Context
March 2007 is late in the 2004-2007 expansion. The Fed has been at 5.25% since June 2006. The housing correction is deepening, the subprime market is in turmoil, and core inflation has recently re-accelerated after a brief lull in late 2006. The economy is growing modestly below trend. This is NOT a recession meeting but a period of elevated uncertainty about whether housing/subprime problems will spill into broader demand weakness — classic "soft landing vs. stagflation lite" tension.

### 3. Core Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants overwhelmingly view inflation as ABOVE target and not yet on a convincing downward path. Plosser: "twelve-month change in core CPI was 2.7 percent... upward trend in core inflation over the past year, from 2 to 2¾ percent, troubling." Fisher: "On a twelve-month basis, the trimmed mean was stuck at 2.5 percent... sixty-eight percent of the components rose 3 percent or more." Lacker: "Core inflation continues to firm... it now seems clear that the fourth quarter's energy-induced lull is over." Pianalto: "primary risk we face is that inflation could remain stuck higher." Kohn: "upside risks to the gradual downdrift in inflation." Bernanke summary: "Some, but not all, think that inflation will continue to moderate... general disappointment with recent inflation readings... some were skeptical that any meaningful progress against inflation is being made." The center of gravity is ABOVE target — clearly elevated, with most participants expressing disappointment. However, this is not a 2022-style extreme. Core PCE is running ~2.3-2.7%, with participants expecting gradual moderation but acknowledging resistance. Score: modestly above target but concerning — **63-67** range.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
The transcript consistently characterizes growth as "below trend" or "soft." Kohn: "output is still running close to potential; in level terms, it might even be beyond potential." But the dominant narrative: Greenbook has below-trend growth, housing correction ongoing, business investment weak. Plosser: "the weakness we are seeing on the real side... should moderate over the year." Lacker: "current episode of below-trend growth is fundamentally a transitory phenomenon." Bernanke: "growth looks as though it's going to be slower." However, LABOR_TIGHTNESS remains strong, and Kohn notes output "close to potential." The bright-line rule: references to "below-trend growth" → score must be < 50. But multiple speakers note it's at or just below potential, not deep in recession. Score: slightly below potential — **44-47** range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Near-universal assessment of TIGHT labor markets. Plosser: "Labor markets in the region remain tight... employers continue to complain of difficulty finding both skilled and unskilled labor." Hoenig: "solid job gains led by further tightening in our labor markets... shortages of skilled labor... shortages of some lower-skilled workers." Lacker: "Labor markets remain tight in most jurisdictions." Kohn: "unemployment rate is low by historical standards" (4.5%). Bernanke: "labor market continues to be tight, with some noting increases in wages." At 4.5% unemployment with widespread reports of skill shortages and wage acceleration — this is firmly tight but not the extreme tightness of 1999-2000 or 2022. Score: **62-67** range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending: not collapsing but slowing. Business investment: notably weak, described as "a puzzle." Fisher: "capital spending, which we see slowing across the nation." Geithner: "spending has continued to come in well below our expectations." Warsh: "business fixed investment... now consider it a very real downside risk." Poole: "I'm just uneasy about the outlook." Consumer side: Stern: "no obvious break in consumer outlays." Lacker: "outlook for consumer spending remains fairly healthy." The net: consumer spending near trend but investment disappointing, housing in correction. Overall slightly below trend but not contracting. Score: **42-46** range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Financial conditions are characterized as broadly supportive/accommodative outside the subprime sector. Kohn: "Outside the subprime market, financial conditions remain supportive of growth. Intermediate and long-term rates are low in real as well as in nominal terms." Geithner: "overall liquidity seems fine. There's been very limited contagion from mortgages into other credit markets." Stern: "generally liquid financial conditions." The subprime market has specifically tightened (risk spreads widened, originator failures), but the broader financial system is described as accommodative. Score slightly loose to neutral — **35-42** range, with the subprime stress not yet transmitting broadly.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The policy vote is unanimously to HOLD at 5.25% (alternative B). The dominant orientation is: hold firm with inflation being the primary risk. However, the statement language debate shows movement away from an explicit tightening bias — the "firming" language is being removed. Hoenig and Lacker represent the most hawkish voices (want to preserve tightening bias, concerned inflation not falling). Yellen, Geithner, Kohn lean toward neutral/balanced. No one advocates cutting rates. Bernanke: "I think we all see the risk that inflation won't moderate as still being substantial and probably greater than the risk of a shortfall in output." The lean is hold-with-inflation-concern, transitioning from explicit tightening bias toward neutral — this is lean hawkish but not strongly so. Score: **54-60** range.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
No formal voting dissent (all agree on alternative B). However, there are notable differences: Hoenig and Lacker want to preserve explicit tightening bias; Yellen, Geithner, Kohn/Mishkin lean toward greater flexibility. The statement language debate is substantive with multiple distinct positions on "predominant" vs "principal," the financial conditions phrase, and section 3 wording. These are disagreements about tactics/communication more than fundamental economics. Score: **28-35** range — broad agreement on rate hold with notable but not sharp tactical disagreements.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability/subprime receives substantial attention. Warsh devotes his entire remarks to market dynamics. Kroszner discusses subprime survey data at length. Fisher, Pianalto, Geithner, Kohn all address subprime spillover risks and credit market conditions. Lockhart discusses regional subprime exposure detail. This is NOT the dominant topic displacing economics, but it is a co-equal concern that multiple participants address substantively. Score: **50-60** range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The meeting explicitly debates removing the "additional firming" language from the statement and replacing it with "future policy adjustments will depend on..." language. The consensus outcome is alternative B — removing the tightening bias signal but maintaining that inflation is the "predominant" concern. Geithner: "the probability that we will tighten further has significantly diminished." Bernanke: "I counsel patience on the rate." The forward signal is moving from mild tightening toward neutral — a meaningful dovish shift in forward guidance despite the hold. Score: **50-58** range — nearly neutral, slight residual hawkish lean from inflation emphasis.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Multiple participants explicitly note increased uncertainty. Plosser: "my uncertainty of that forecast has increased somewhat." Geithner: "We face greater uncertainty about the near-term outlook than we have over the past few meetings." Poole: "I'm just uneasy about the outlook." Bernanke: "uncertainty about growth prospects and downside risk have increased." Kroszner: "uncertainty is a bit up; so the tails are somewhat fatter." Kohn: "downside tails that are fatter than the upside tails." The phrase "fat tails" is used repeatedly by multiple participants. This is elevated but not extreme uncertainty — standard model uncertainty plus specific subprime unknowns. Score: **47-55** range.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows pressure above target. Score > 50. Check.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slightly below trend/potential. Score < 50. Check.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight, low unemployment, shortages. Score > 50. Check.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence says slightly soft, investment weak. Score slightly < 50. Check.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says broadly accommodative (low). Score < 50. Check.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with inflation concern, removing tightening bias but still leaning hawkish. Score slightly > 50. Check.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish signal. Removing "firming" language = dovish shift, moving toward neutral. Score near 50, slight lean hawkish due to inflation emphasis. Check.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (45) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (64): Divergence is real and explicitly discussed — participants note labor markets are tight even as growth is below trend. This is a characteristic of the 2006-2007 period (labor hoarding, productivity uncertainty). The divergence is documented and explained by multiple speakers (Kohn, Bernanke). This is appropriate.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (54): Both just above 50, consistent. The current action is hold (not hike), the forward signal is being softened from explicit tightening bias toward neutral. Slight hawkish lean remains in both due to inflation emphasis. Coherent.

### 6. Speaker Count by Direction (Inflation Concern)

Inflation clearly elevated and not yet convincingly moderating: Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Pianalto, Kohn, Warsh (concerned), Bernanke — roughly 8 speakers expressing clear concern.
Inflation expected to moderate gradually/not alarming: Geithner, Mishkin, Stern, Kroszner, Lockhart — roughly 5 speakers more sanguine.
Center of gravity clearly in the "elevated, concerning, not yet moderating" camp.

### 7. Optional Dimensions

- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Multiple speakers address directly. Plosser: expectations "moved down somewhat," ten-year SPF fell to 2.35%. Mishkin: "inflation expectations seem to be very solidly grounded... around 2 percent." Geithner: "inflation expectations remain stable at reasonable levels." Consistent anchoring narrative — score near neutral/anchored at target (not extreme either direction). Score: **52** (anchored at target, slight above-2% concern but well-contained).
- **CREDIT_STRESS**: Subprime market clearly stressed (spreads widened, originator failures), but broader credit "fine." Score: moderately elevated but not crisis — **55-60**.
- **BANKING_STRESS**: Brief mentions of bank soundness, survey of subprime lenders. Banks themselves not stressed; it's the non-bank originators. Score: **35-40** (sound, with some fringe stress).
- **PRODUCTIVITY**: Multiple participants discuss explicitly. Geithner: "productivity growth per quarter... significantly below 2 percent... lose conviction that trend growth is still in the neighborhood of 2½ percent." Mishkin: "weaker numbers in terms of productivity... think that is just sort of cyclical." Bernanke: "productivity might signal something." This is a live concern. Score: **38** (below historical trend, uncertainty about whether structural or cyclical).
- **DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Fisher notes global capacity tight, import price inflation shifting from deflationary. Pianalto notes "dollar depreciation is finally having an effect" (positive for exports). No major concern about dollar strength. Mild concern about dollar weakness contributing to import price pressures. Score: **35** (mild weakness concern, not extreme).
- EQUITY_STRESS, TREASURY_STRESS: Not prominently discussed by 2+ participants in the scoring sense. N/A.
- INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING: Services inflation elevated (Fisher: medical, OER); housing OER still firm but expected to ease; goods inflation picking up (Fisher: 68% of CPI components rising 3%+). These are discussed by multiple participants but as part of the aggregate inflation narrative rather than distinct sub-component emphasis. Score these.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 65 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Plosser: "The twelve-month change in the core CPI was 2.7 percent, and the three-month change has reaccelerated. I find the upward trend in core inflation over the past year, from 2 to 2¾ percent, troubling."
- Fisher: "On a six-month basis through January, both our trimmed mean at the Dallas Federal Reserve Bank and your PCE excluding food and energy are stuck at 2.1 percent. On a twelve-month basis, the trimmed mean was stuck at 2.5 percent... sixty-eight percent of the components rose 3 percent or more."
- Bernanke summary: "Some were skeptical that any meaningful progress against inflation is being made... resource utilization pressures, particularly tight labor markets, pose a longer-term inflation threat."

**Center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as clearly above the 2% target and not yet on a convincing downward path, with core measures running 2.3-2.7%, generating widespread concern and outweighing the minority view that moderation is underway.

**Minority views:** Geithner, Mishkin, and Lockhart are more sanguine — Mishkin notes "inflation expectations seem to be very solidly grounded" and Geithner: "not much has really changed in our view." These moderate the score from a higher level.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 45 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "Growth modestly below potential, along with the unwinding of some special factors like rent increases, should allow further declines in inflation."
- Lacker: "I still think the current episode of below-trend growth is fundamentally a transitory phenomenon."
- Bernanke summary: "Most participants today agree that growth looks as though it's going to be slower... Many people have marked down growth expectations for the remainder of the year."

**Center-of-gravity:** The economy is running at or slightly below potential; the housing correction is the primary drag with non-housing components still growing. Not deeply below potential — Kohn even suggests output "might even be beyond potential" in level terms, but trend growth has clearly downshifted.

**Minority views:** Hoenig maintains a more optimistic view (>2.5% growth for the year); Lockhart similarly sees only modest slowdown. These pull the score upward toward 47.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 63 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Plosser: "Labor markets in the region remain tight... employers continue to complain of difficulty finding both skilled and unskilled labor... acceleration in wages appears to be more widespread."
- Hoenig: "solid job gains led by further tightening in our labor markets... shortages of skilled labor... shortages of some lower-skilled workers."
- Kohn: "at 4½ percent, the unemployment rate is low by historical standards, and this suggests to me that the risks from resource utilization remain toward higher inflation."

**Center-of-gravity:** Unemployment at 4.5%, below NAIRU, widespread reports of skill shortages, wage acceleration in professional and managerial occupations — clearly tight, though not the extreme of 1999-2000 or 2022-23.

**Minority views:** No meaningful dissent — labor market tightness is near-universally acknowledged.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 44 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Geithner: "the recent numbers suggest both a deeper adjustment in housing and a broader weakness in the economy than we anticipated, notably in capital goods orders... spending has continued to come in well below our expectations."
- Warsh: "business fixed investment... I had thought... a very real upside opportunity and now consider it a very real downside risk."
- Lacker: "The outlook for consumer spending remains fairly healthy... Real disposable income growth has been powered by continuing gains in employment."

**Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending holding near trend (not contracting), but business investment has been persistently weak — a "puzzle" multiple participants cannot explain. Housing investment in active correction. Net position: slightly below trend, weighed down by investment weakness.

**Minority views:** Hoenig and Stern see spending as closer to moderate trend; Stern: "no obvious break in consumer outlays."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 38 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "Outside the subprime market, financial conditions remain supportive of growth. Intermediate and long-term rates are low in real as well as in nominal terms."
- Geithner: "overall liquidity seems fine. There's been very limited contagion from mortgages into other credit markets. Debt issuance seems to be continuing."
- Stern: "generally liquid financial conditions are likely to maintain the expansion."

**Center-of-gravity:** Broad financial conditions remain accommodative — low real rates, liquid markets, limited credit contagion. The subprime sector specifically has tightened (widened spreads, impaired originator access), but this is not yet transmitting to overall financial tightness. Conditions are on the loose side of neutral.

**Minority views:** Warsh and Fisher flag risks of contagion and a possible rapid shift to risk aversion if private equity/structured credit markets stress. This widens the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 57 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Hoenig: "I continue to favor statement language that reflects an explicit bias toward firming... I remain firm until we actually see the inflation numbers consistently improve."
- Yellen: "I remain somewhat more concerned about inflation risk, and it is wise for our message on that to be consistent over time." [Supports hold, asymmetric bias retained]
- Bernanke: "I think we all see the risk that inflation won't moderate as still being substantial and probably greater than the risk of a shortfall in output... I counsel patience on the rate."

**Center-of-gravity:** Unanimous vote to hold at 5.25%. Inflation is explicitly labeled the "predominant" concern. No participant advocates cutting. However, the removal of the "additional firming" language represents a meaningful dovish shift in the forward lean. The current meeting is best described as lean hawkish / inflation-focused hold.

**Minority views:** Hoenig and Lacker lean more hawkish (want to preserve tightening bias language); Geithner and Mishkin lean more toward neutral (more comfortable opening flexibility for cuts).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 30 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Full committee agrees on alternative B (no rate change). No formal dissents.
- Substantive debate on statement language: Hoenig wants "firming" language retained; Lacker agrees "we should not be loosening the sense of our tightening bias"; vs. Kohn, Geithner, Mishkin comfortable with moving to more neutral language.
- On "predominant" vs. "principal": split without strong conviction on either side; ultimately "predominant" prevails by mild consensus.

**Center-of-gravity:** Broad agreement on policy action (hold) with notable but manageable tactical disagreements about statement language. The economic assessment is also broadly shared with differing emphasis on downside risks vs. inflation persistence. This is a one-dissent-intensity level meeting without any formal dissent — well-captured in the 25-35 range.

**Minority views:** Hoenig's consistent preference for more explicit hawkish language represents the main outlier.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 55 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Warsh devotes his entire go-around to financial market dynamics, structured credit, private equity risks, and the subprime contagion question: "could this market volatility of recent weeks spread beyond the subprime market and lead to the kind of credit crunch that would have meaningful downside risks?"
- Kroszner presents detailed survey data on subprime lender practices and refinancing options, spending most of his remarks on this.
- Geithner: extensive discussion of structured mortgage products, valuation complexity, credit spread vulnerability.

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial stability receives substantial multi-participant attention — it is clearly a significant topic at this meeting, discussed substantively by at least 6-7 speakers, but it does not displace the economic assessment as the primary focus. It is a co-equal concern. Score at moderate-to-prominent range.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 54 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- The meeting explicitly removes "additional firming" language and replaces it with "future policy adjustments will depend on..." — a deliberate move away from tightening signal.
- Geithner: "the probability that we will tighten further has significantly diminished... we need to give ourselves the flexibility now to move to neutral sooner than might have seemed likely."
- Bernanke: "our challenge is to recognize that the real economy has shifted somewhat... maintaining our primary emphasis on inflation risks... not inducing a further decline in expected federal funds rates."

**Center-of-gravity:** Forward guidance shifts from explicit tightening bias to data-dependent/balanced posture while retaining an asymmetric inflation emphasis. This is essentially neutral forward guidance with a slight hawkish lean from the inflation "predominant concern" language. Score near 50-55.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 52 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Plosser: "the uncertainty surrounding my own forecast that growth will soon return to trend has increased since our last meeting."
- Geithner: "We face greater uncertainty about the near-term outlook than we have over the past few meetings."
- Bernanke summary: "the views of most participants were that... uncertainty has increased, and that the tails of the distribution have become fatter."

**Center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants explicitly note increased uncertainty versus the prior meeting, using "fat tails" language frequently. However, this is described as a modest uptick from a relatively confident baseline, not a qualitative regime shift to high uncertainty. The standard forecast remains intact with wider error bars. Score at the low end of elevated uncertainty.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 58 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

Fisher: "pickup in core goods inflation." Fisher: "Prices for imported U.S. goods have clearly moved away from deflationary trends to neutral or slightly inflationary." Plosser: "prices for industrial goods continue to move up." Multiple speakers note goods inflation shifting from prior deflationary tailwind. Slightly above 50.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 62 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

Geithner: "sources of the recent negative surprises in the core data, which seem concentrated in medical services." Fisher: trucking/logistics pricing pressure. Plosser: wage acceleration in professional services. Multiple participants note service-sector price pressures, including OER and medical costs, as key upside inflation risks.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 52 ±10 | Confidence: Low**

Bernanke: "rents and owners' equivalent rent are still pretty important here. They have not yet slowed much." Fisher mentions rental prices increasing due to condo conversions draining apartment supply. However, housing itself is in a price correction. OER/rent elevated but expected to ease. Mixed — slightly above 50 with high uncertainty.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 50 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**

Plosser: ten-year SPF inflation expectations moved down to 2.35%. Mishkin: "inflation expectations seem to be very solidly grounded... around 2 percent." Geithner: "inflation expectations remain stable at reasonable levels." Lacker: "inflation expectations remain centered at or above 2 percent." Anchored near target; slight above-target reading but not unanchored. Score near 50-52.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 58 ±10 | Confidence: Moderate**

Subprime spreads clearly widened, originator failures occurring. Lacker: "Mortgage-backed securities have lost value as risk spreads have widened, and there have been insolvencies among firms that specialized in this sector." But broader credit "fine" per Geithner. Concentrated stress in one segment, limited contagion. Moderate stress overall.

**BANKING_STRESS: 35 ±8 | Confidence: Low**

Large banks not stressed per Pianalto: "CEOs of the large banks from my District... don't seem to be particularly concerned." The stress is in non-bank originators, hedge funds, structured vehicles — not the core banking system. Banks sound but monitoring subprime exposure. Below 50 (sound overall).

**PRODUCTIVITY: 38 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

Geithner: "productivity growth per quarter at an annualized rate has, over the past ten quarters, come in significantly below 2 percent... some of the analysts... are starting to lose conviction that trend growth is still in the neighborhood of 2½ percent." Mishkin: "weaker numbers in terms of productivity." Bernanke: concerned productivity weakness may be structural signal. Multiple speakers note below-historical-trend productivity as a real risk. Score below 50 (below trend productivity).

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: 35 ±10 | Confidence: Low**

Fisher emphasizes global capacity tightness and import price inflation shifting from deflationary. Pianalto: "dollar depreciation is finally having an effect" (positive for exports). The dollar is weak, contributing mild import price pressure but not a primary concern. Score at 35 (mild dollar weakness concern, not strength concern).
