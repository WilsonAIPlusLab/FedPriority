# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1994-03-22 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the March 22, 1994 FOMC meeting, featuring individual speaker statements in a go-around format followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: MR. STERN, MR. JORDAN, VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH, MR. MELZER, MR. MCTEER, MR. HOENIG, MR. KELLEY, MR. LAWARE, MS. PHILLIPS, MR. LINDSEY, CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN, MR. BOEHNE, MR. PARRY, MR. BROADDUS, MR. FORRESTAL, and others.

### Era Context
March 1994: The U.S. economy is well into its expansion from the 1990-91 recession. The Fed began tightening on February 4, 1994 with a 25bp hike (first tightening since 1989). The economy is accelerating, slack is narrowing, but measured inflation remains low. Bond markets have experienced significant turbulence since the February move.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation pressure as slightly below to near the midpoint. There is a striking split: almost all participants acknowledge commodity price increases, anecdotal reports of materials/construction shortages, and rising inflationary expectations in bond markets. However, the center of gravity recognizes that measured final goods inflation has NOT yet accelerated — core CPI is at its lowest in a long time (2.8%). Greenspan explicitly notes "very little evidence of accelerating inflation in any of the data on final prices." Stern says "nothing very broadly based or very significant" on wage/price pressures. Kelley emphasizes trends are still declining. On the other side, Melzer notes consumer prices accelerated from 1.8% to 2.7%, Hoenig sees "inflationary pressures increasing more rapidly than the Greenbook," and Jordan cites small business plans to raise prices at highest since 1991. The center of gravity: current inflation is low/contained but participants perceive emerging pipeline pressures and worry about the near future. This is not "at target balanced" (50) — it's more like modest concern about building pressures despite benign current readings. Score ~47-48: slightly below midpoint on actual pressure, but with prospective concern pulling it up.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says current inflation is still low but pipeline pressures building. Score of 47 reflects slightly below target current readings with upward concern. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants overwhelmingly see the economy as expanding strongly, approaching or at potential. Greenspan: "I'm hard pressed to remember when the outlook itself looked as unequivocally expansionary as it does today." Melzer: "capacity utilization has reached the level recorded at the previous cyclical peak." Jordan: "extraordinary upward movement" in small business indicators. Phillips: "economic strength is broad-based" and "running ahead of potential." Multiple district reports are uniformly positive. Kelley and LaWare offer some caution that the expansion may slow per the Greenbook forecast. But the center of gravity is clearly that the economy is at or modestly above potential.

Bright-line rule: Phillips explicitly says "running ahead of potential." Multiple participants describe capacity utilization at cyclical peaks. Score must be >50. Given "approaching" peak utilization and strong but not overheating language, score ~55-57.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says economy at/modestly above potential. Score 56 matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor markets are tightening meaningfully but not yet severely tight. Melzer: "labor market conditions adjusted for recent changes in survey methods are rapidly approaching the degree of tightness that existed at [the 1990 cyclical peak]." Jordan: "job openings are the highest since 1989; plans to hire are the highest since 1990." Hoenig: "strains in terms of getting access to labor, but not necessarily in wages yet." McTeer: "hours worked in manufacturing in the District continue to be at record levels." Stern: "while there may be a minor increase in reports of greater wage or price pressures, there is nothing very broadly based." Kelley: unemployment projected to stop declining. The center of gravity is tightening toward full employment but not yet severely tight. Score ~52-55.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says approaching tightness of previous cyclical peaks but not there yet. Score 53 matches.

Coherence check: OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53) both modestly above 50, consistent.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Spending and investment are clearly above trend. Multiple districts report strong activity across sectors. Jordan: capital outlays highest since 1989-90. Construction is very strong (Hoenig, McTeer). Auto production adding 1.5 percentage points to Q1 growth. Housing brisk (Stern — lack of supply of unsold homes). Loan demand picked up considerably (McTeer). Phillips: "sustainable and it may well be at or above potential." Kelley offers caution on consumer debt loads and wealth effects. The center of gravity is solidly above trend. Score ~58-60.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence shows broad-based strength. Score 59 matches.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are a major discussion topic at this meeting. The picture is mixed/transitional. Greenspan describes major bond market turbulence, capital losses, portfolio adjustments, but notes "risk premiums have remained quite low" and "yield spreads... are still quite low by any historic standard." McTeer: banks are aggressively seeking loans ("hell yes"). Phillips: "overall cost of capital remains relatively low. Banks are in a strong position to finance further expansion." Kelley: "real rates are low and stimulative." However, long rates have risen 70-120bp, which Kelley calls "a powerful restraint." McDonough describes near-crisis conditions in early March (rumors about JP Morgan and Bankers Trust, serious phone work with central banks). The net position: conditions have tightened from very loose but are still in the loose-to-neutral range. Score ~40-43.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tightening from very loose but still broadly loose/accommodative. Score 42 matches.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: Near-unanimous agreement that rates need to rise. The debate is about pace (25bp vs 50bp), not direction. Strong 50bp advocates: Boehne, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan (implied). 25bp advocates: Greenspan, Forrestal, Kelley, Stern, McTeer, Lindsey, McDonough, Hoenig (reluctantly), LaWare (reluctantly), Phillips. Kelley says "I do not believe that the real economy requires a tightening move at this point but I think the markets probably do." Most 25bp supporters explicitly say they expect to continue hiking at subsequent meetings to reach 4%+ by midyear. Greenspan: "we have to restore policy to neutrality as fast as we can" and "eventually we have to be at 4 to 4-1/2 percent." This is a clearly hawkish meeting — debate is between moderate and strong hawkish. Score ~67-70.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Consensus to tighten, debate is 25 vs 50bp, with expectation of continued tightening. Score 68 matches.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Notable disagreement. The 25 vs 50 debate is substantive — Boehne, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer argue strongly for 50bp; several others prefer 50bp but accept 25bp (LaWare, Hoenig). There is also a symmetric vs asymmetric debate. Kelley and LaWare provide distinctly different economic assessments (more cautious). No formal dissents in the end, but multiple participants express strong preferences for 50bp. Additionally, Jordan disagrees on framework (doesn't like Phillips curve approach). Score ~40-45.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: This is a MAJOR topic. Greenspan devotes extensive discussion to bond market turbulence, 1987 crash parallels, mutual fund industry risks, speculative bubble. McDonough describes near-crisis bank rumors and active phone coordination with money center banks and European central banks. Phillips discusses market portfolio adjustments, derivatives complexity, yield curve effects. Lindsey discusses excessive risk-taking, margin debt, overseas speculation. Greenspan frames the key policy constraint as financial system fragility: "I don't think the financial system can take a very large increase without a break in its tensile strength." This is an unusually prominent financial stability discussion for this era. Score ~55-60.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Greenspan explicitly states the funds rate needs to reach 4-4.5%. Multiple participants say they expect continued tightening. Parry: "financial markets appear to be expecting significant increases in interest rates over the next six months." The debate about symmetric language vs. asymmetric was resolved as symmetric, but with the understanding that conference calls could be used. The overall forward signal is further tightening ahead, moving meeting by meeting. Score ~70-73.

Coherence check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72) align — current action is moderately hawkish, forward signal is also hawkish. The slight gap reflects that the current action (25bp) was more moderate than the forward commitment.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Moderate-to-elevated uncertainty. Greenspan explicitly raises puzzlement about the inflation/credit dynamic: "There is something different going on. I don't know yet what it means." Stern: "the confidence interval around any forecast is very wide. And I think that argues for caution." McDonough: "I personally have much greater uncertainty about whether the economy will perform about in line with the Greenbook forecast." Kelley raises the possibility that the economy is "behaving in some new ways." Phillips: "the markets don't need any more uncertainty." However, the economic outlook itself is seen with reasonable confidence — the uncertainty is more about the financial system and the inflation/credit puzzle. Score ~42-46.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both modestly above 50, consistent with economy at/slightly above potential.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Aligned, forward signal slightly more hawkish than current action, consistent with 25bp now but committed to further moves.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (47) relatively low despite OUTPUT_GAP above 50: Explained by the extensive discussion about why inflation hasn't yet materialized despite tightening slack — this is a key theme of the meeting.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "We have very clearly seen some pickup in underlying commodity prices; we have seen very little evidence of accelerating inflation in any of the data on final prices."
- MR. KELLEY: "Three years into an expansion, the 12-month change in the core CPI last month, the latest figure, was the best we have had in a long time... The trends are still in the direction of slowly declining inflation."
- MR. MELZER: "Over the past five months both consumer and producer prices have accelerated... Consumer prices accelerated from a 1.8 percent to a 2.7 percent rate."
- MR. HOENIG: "we see inflationary pressures that are increasing more rapidly than shown in the Greenbook"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Current measured inflation remains benign and near cyclical lows, but pipeline pressures (commodity prices, capacity utilization, anecdotal materials shortages) and rising inflation expectations in bond markets create prospective concern. The score reflects the tension between low current readings and building forward pressures.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Hoenig see inflation already accelerating; Kelley sees current trends as still disinflationary. This dispersal widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I'm hard pressed to remember when the outlook itself looked as unequivocally expansionary as it does today."
- MR. MELZER: "Capacity utilization has reached the level recorded at the previous cyclical peak in July 1990, and labor market conditions... are rapidly approaching the degree of tightness that existed at that time."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The economy has been running ahead of potential, and if it continues at that same pace, eventually at some point we are going to see price pressures."
- MR. KELLEY: "After this quarter, unemployment ceases to go down in the Greenbook projection and capacity utilization stops going up."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is clearly expanding at a strong pace, with capacity utilization at cyclical peak levels and slack being absorbed. Most participants view the economy as at or modestly above potential, though not dramatically overheating.

**Minority views:** Kelley and LaWare see the Greenbook projection of slowing as plausible, suggesting less overheating than others perceive.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "the unemployment rate, adjusted for changes in measurement, seems to have declined in an amount about equal to 1 percentage point over the last year" and "labor market conditions... are rapidly approaching the degree of tightness that existed at [the 1990 peak]."
- MR. JORDAN: "job openings are the highest since 1989; plans to hire are the highest since 1990"
- MR. HOENIG: "strains in terms of getting access to labor, but not necessarily in wages yet"
- MR. STERN: "while there may be a minor increase in reports of greater wage or price pressures, there is nothing very broadly based or very significant there yet"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets are tightening toward full employment, with scattered reports of labor shortages in specific sectors (construction, manufacturing), but broad-based wage pressure has not yet materialized.

**Minority views:** Kelley notes unemployment is projected to stop declining per the Greenbook.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 59 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "Planned capital outlays are the highest since 1989 and 1990" from the small business survey; "everything there also tends to be uniformly positive"
- MR. HOENIG: "construction activity prospectively for the next 90 days is as high as he's ever seen it, higher than the 1985-1986 period"
- MR. STERN: "there has been a further step-up in activity pretty much across the board both by region and by sector... we are getting reports from realtors... the problem they face is a lack of supply of unsold homes."
- MR. MCTEER: "Loan demand has picked up considerably and the larger banks are reportedly seeking loans very aggressively."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and business investment are clearly above trend with broad-based strength across districts and sectors. Construction, auto production, and capital spending are all strong.

**Minority views:** Kelley notes heavy consumer debt loads and potential wealth-effect shocks from bond/stock losses; McDonough flags possible diminution in consumption.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "risk premiums have remained quite low... yield spreads... are still quite low by any historic standard"
- MR. KELLEY: "real rates are low and stimulative... But on the other hand, this 70 to 100, maybe 120 basis point increase in long rates that we have seen... is a powerful restraint."
- MR. MCTEER: quoting a contact who describes bank lending attitude as "it's not 'yes,' it's 'hell yes.'"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "overall cost of capital remains relatively low. Banks are in a strong position to finance further expansion."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Despite significant long-rate increases and bond market turbulence, overall financial conditions remain on the loose side of neutral. Banks are lending aggressively, risk premiums are still low, and real short rates remain stimulative. The long-rate increase provides some restraint but has not pushed conditions to truly tight territory.

**Minority views:** McDonough's account of near-crisis bank rumors suggests pockets of acute stress existed briefly in early March.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think we have to restore policy to neutrality as fast as we can... eventually we have to be at 4 to 4-1/2 percent."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I prefer a 1/2 percentage point increase... We pressed hard on the monetary accelerator to get the economy moving, and now... we have to ease off the accelerator to avoid having to slam on the brakes down the road."
- MR. PARRY: "the real federal funds rate still appears to be, in my view, unsustainably low... I feel that a 50 basis point increase is needed."
- MR. KELLEY: "rates are too stimulative and I think we ought to seek a neutral policy stance"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous agreement that policy is too accommodative and rates must rise. The debate is purely about pace: roughly 5-6 participants argue for 50bp (Boehne, Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, with LaWare and Hoenig expressing preference for 50bp but accepting 25bp), while the majority accept 25bp with the expectation of continued tightening at subsequent meetings.

**Minority views:** Nobody argues for holding rates steady. Kelley offers the most cautious economic assessment but still supports tightening.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "I prefer a 1/2 percentage point increase" vs. CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN's 25bp recommendation
- MR. LAWARE: "Instinctively, I would prefer in the best of all worlds a 50 basis point move... I will reluctantly agree to the 25 basis points"
- Symmetric vs. asymmetric debate: multiple participants (Lindsey, Hoenig, Kelley, Stern, LaWare, McTeer, Phillips) push back against Greenspan's initial asymmetric proposal
- MR. JORDAN's framework disagreement: "I have a lot of problems with Phillips curves and gap or slack type models"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Substantive disagreement on the magnitude of the rate move (25bp vs 50bp) and on the directive language (symmetric vs asymmetric). No formal dissents in the end, but several participants express strong preferences and "reluctant" acceptance. Jordan raises fundamental framework disagreements.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discussion of bond market turbulence, 1987 crash parallels, mutual fund industry risks, speculative bubble unwinding; "I don't think the financial system can take a very large increase without a break in its tensile strength"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "we were working the phones at least as actively with the money center banks in New York and with the central banks of Europe... as we ever have in earlier better known episodes of financial problems"; describes rumors about JP Morgan and Bankers Trust
- MR. LINDSEY: discusses negative real after-tax returns driving risk-taking, margin debt at levels not seen since 1987, overseas speculation
- MS. PHILLIPS: extensive discussion of portfolio adjustments, derivatives complexity, market sorting out

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is a prominent topic at this meeting, well above the norm for early-to-mid 1990s meetings. Greenspan explicitly frames the financial system's fragility as the key constraint on the pace of tightening. McDonough provides a near-crisis narrative about early March.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "My own view is that eventually we have to be at 4 to 4-1/2 percent"; envisions meeting-by-meeting 25bp hikes to reach 4% by midyear
- MR. PARRY: "financial markets appear to be expecting significant increases in interest rates over the next six months"
- MR. STERN: "we'll be at this for some time; it may well be that the funds rate has to go to 4 percent or more by the time we are done"
- MR. MELZER: "the quicker we get to neutrality, the better off everyone is going to be"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly hawkish — further tightening is essentially certain, with most participants expecting the funds rate to reach at least 4% by midyear. The only question is pace. The symmetric directive was adopted, but with the explicit understanding that conference calls could be used if conditions warrant faster action.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "There is something different going on. I don't know yet what it means" (on the inflation/credit puzzle); "we are looking at something not terribly significantly different... prior to our move on February 4th, the market had drifted into a state of somnambulance"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I personally have much greater uncertainty about whether the economy will perform about in line with the Greenbook forecast"
- MR. STERN: "the confidence interval around any forecast is very wide. And I think that argues for caution."
- MR. KELLEY: "it's possible that we have a situation here where the economy, at least for a while, is behaving in some new ways"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, driven by Greenspan's puzzlement about why inflation hasn't appeared despite tightening slack, financial market turbulence creating downside risks, and the novel character of the expansion. However, the economic expansion itself is viewed with high confidence.
