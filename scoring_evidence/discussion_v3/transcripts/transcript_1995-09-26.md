# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1995-09-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript with individual speaker attributions. This is a go-around format followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Parry, Broaddus, Boehne, Hoenig, Minehan, Moskow, McTeer, Melzer, Stern, McDonough, Jordan, Yellen, Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, and Chairman Greenspan.

**Era context**: Mid-1995. The Fed had raised rates aggressively in 1994 (from 3% to 5.75%). By mid-1995, the economy had slowed (inventory correction in Q2), and there was debate about whether the funds rate was now too high. CPI inflation was running around 3%. Unemployment was near 5.6%. Budget deficit reduction discussions were prominent.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants generally view inflation as contained but not declining. Parry: "CPI inflation at 3 percent or slightly less... somewhat above last year's 2.6 percent rate." Hoenig: "I question whether the overall core inflation rate will drop much in the next year." Melzer: "Private forecasters predict that inflation in 1995... will be higher than inflation in 1994." Moskow: "price increases for packaging materials have dissipated... price index components continued to move lower." Minehan: businesses report "an inability to raise prices even in the face of rising demand." McTeer: "the slowing of inflation has been very welcome." Blinder: "core CPI inflation reads 3.1, 2.8, and 2.7 percent." Phillips: "we have a distance to go on getting the inflation rate down." The center of gravity: inflation is around 3% CPI, roughly contained but not declining toward price stability. Modest upward pressure from capacity utilization, but no acceleration. Direction: slightly above a comfortable level but well-behaved. Score should be in the 45-52 range. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says inflation is present but contained, near 3%, not accelerating. Score ~48.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy experienced an inventory correction in Q2 1995 and is now recovering to moderate growth (2-2.5%). Parry: "real GDP growth has picked up to a moderate pace." Broaddus: "operating at close to full capacity in many industries." Hoenig: growth "slightly below trend, but in the 2 to 2-1/2 percent range... appropriate, given... the current level of resource utilization." Parry: "The unemployment and capacity utilization rates indicate that it is unlikely that there currently is any excess capacity pushing down on inflation." Blinder: GDP growth of 2.1, 2.2, 2.0 percent -- "all below the growth rate of potential." So the economy is near potential but growth is slightly below trend, meaning a small gap could open. Most see the economy as roughly at potential with risk of slipping slightly below. Score ~49-50. Bright-line: Parry says no excess capacity (at potential), Blinder says growth below potential (gap opening). Center of gravity is at or just slightly below potential. Score: 49.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Several districts report tight labor markets. Broaddus: "significant upward pressure on wages over the last six months" in the Carolinas "where labor markets are exceptionally tight." Moskow: "District labor markets remain tight and wage pressures continue to intensify at the low end of the wage scale" with unemployment "4.6 percent, a full percentage point below the national average." Jordan: director "could not hire anybody." Stern: "Labor markets are tight. The rate of unemployment in Minnesota is at or near an all-time low." National unemployment is 5.6%. Minehan notes labor insecurity. Phillips: "Employment is holding fairly steady." The picture is mixed -- nationally unemployment is moderate at 5.6%, but many districts report tight conditions and wage pressure in specific segments. Center of gravity: moderately tight, near full employment. Score ~53-55. Higher = tighter. Evidence shows tight conditions in many districts, national unemployment around NAIRU. Score: 54.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Broaddus: "the rebound in automobile sales in August was impressive. Overall consumer spending and consumer confidence... have been more firmly maintained." Moskow: "Consumer spending seems to have strengthened in September." Phillips: "consumer spending and housing have shown improvement... business investment growth is slowing somewhat." McDonough: "consumer demand appears to have weakened" in his district. Minehan: "continued growth... at a pace close to trend." Boehne: "Retail sales are up a little." The picture is moderate -- housing recovering, autos rebounding, business investment slowing from rapid pace but still positive. Center of gravity: roughly at trend, perhaps slightly above as recovery from Q2 weakness. Score ~50-52. Score: 51.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Phillips: "financial markets since our last meeting have generally improved--the dollar, the stock market, the bond markets. This means a continued availability of relatively low cost capital." Yellen discusses long-term yields embodying "an assumption of declining short-term rates." Several note that the real funds rate may be above equilibrium (Greenspan, Blinder, Yellen, Lindsey), suggesting policy is somewhat tight but financial conditions themselves (markets) have eased. Melzer notes "total bank loan growth has been rapid." Boehne: "Banks report that loan demand generally is flat." The discussion is primarily about the policy stance (real funds rate too high) rather than conditions per se. Financial conditions themselves seem neutral to slightly loose -- markets improving, capital available, loan growth solid. Score ~42-45. Score: 43.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for holding rates steady ("B symmetric"). However, the tilt of the discussion is clearly toward eventual easing. Greenspan: "I generally agree" the real funds rate is above long-term equilibrium; "it is probably unwise to move today" due to fiscal uncertainty but "our next move" implies easing. Yellen: "termites in the basement" problem -- real funds rate too high. Blinder: extensive case for eventual easing, current path unsustainable. Lindsey: "we have a real federal funds rate that is too high." Minehan: "more harmful right now to raise rates than to lower them. Lowering them may be the next action." Phillips: "We should have some room to ease sometime this fall." Boehne: "Almost surely we will have to adjust policy at some point in coming months." BUT Melzer: policy is "somewhat restrictive, and I think that is quite appropriate" -- wants to see inflation expectations decline before easing. Parry: "I would be reluctant to cut the funds rate." Hoenig: "I am very comfortable with where we are." Broaddus, Kelley side with the more hawkish hold rationale. So the center holds steady, but a strong majority lean toward eventual easing. This is a hold with a clear easing bias. Score ~43-45. Score: 44.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent -- unanimous for "B symmetric." However, the reasoning is divergent. The dovish camp (Blinder, Yellen, Lindsey, Minehan, Phillips, Boehne) sees easing as needed soon. The hawkish camp (Melzer, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Kelley) sees the current stance as appropriate and is not eager to ease. Despite agreeing on the action, the underlying views on the economy and future policy direction differ notably. Score ~30-35. Score: 32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very little discussion of financial stability per se. Phillips briefly mentions financial markets improving. The "train wreck" fiscal scenario is mentioned as a risk to markets, but this is about fiscal default, not financial stability broadly. No discussion of asset bubbles, banking stress, or systemic risk. Score ~8-12. Score: 10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The dominant signal is toward eventual easing. Greenspan: "our next move" will likely be easing, timing uncertain. Blinder, Yellen make extensive cases for eventual cuts. Multiple members say easing should come "sometime this fall" or "in coming months." But no immediate action, and some members (Melzer, Parry) want to see more evidence first. The forward signal is mildly dovish -- hold now, ease later. Score ~35-40. Score: 37.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. The fiscal situation (budget negotiations, "train wreck" scenario) is the main source. Kelley lists "an exceptional number of rather basic questions" (NAIRU, productivity, wage dynamics). Phillips: fiscal impact is a "major unknown." Greenspan cautions about model reliability. Boehne: "I don't know what to take into account at this point." But overall the economic outlook itself is fairly clear -- moderate growth, contained inflation. The uncertainty is primarily about fiscal policy and its effects. Score ~38-42. Score: 40.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (54): Slight divergence -- labor is modestly tighter than the output gap suggests. This is consistent with the text: many districts report tight labor markets even as overall growth is only moderate. The 1990s structural shift (productivity gains allowing growth without overheating) explains the gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (44) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (37): Both lean dovish but forward guidance is more dovish than current lean. This makes sense -- the committee holds steady today but the forward signal is clearly toward easing. The current action is neutral-ish while the forward lean is more clearly dovish.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 48: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says contained near 3%, not accelerating but not declining. Slightly below midpoint is correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 43: Higher = tighter conditions. Markets improving, capital available, but real rates arguably high. Slightly below neutral fits.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 44: Higher = more hawkish. Committee holds but leans toward easing. Below midpoint = dovish lean. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 48 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "CPI inflation at 3 percent or slightly less in 1995, 1996, and 1997--somewhat above last year's 2.6 percent rate."
- MS. MINEHAN: "many business executives... continue to report an inability to raise prices even in the face of rising demand. Competition remains too keen."
- MR. HOENIG: "I question whether the overall core inflation rate will drop much in the next year."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants view inflation as roughly contained near 3% CPI -- present but not accelerating, with little prospect of meaningful further decline. This places it near the midpoint, just slightly below (not quite "at target" since the implicit target was lower than 3%).

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Moskow report regional wage pressures that could signal upward risk; Blinder and the Greenbook see a gradual declining trend. This explains the ±6 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "The unemployment and capacity utilization rates indicate that it is unlikely that there currently is any excess capacity pushing down on inflation."
- MR. BROADDUS: "we are operating at close to full capacity in many industries and sectors."
- MR. BLINDER: Growth rates of "2.1, 2.2, and 2.0 percent... all below the growth rate of potential."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is roughly at potential following the Q2 inventory correction, but growth is running at or slightly below trend, meaning the output gap is near zero with a risk of opening slightly negative.

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Kelley see upside risks (above-potential growth possible). Blinder and Yellen see a gradually opening negative gap. Band reflects this range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 54 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "District labor markets remain tight and wage pressures continue to intensify at the low end of the wage scale... average unemployment rates in the five District states were 4.6 percent."
- MR. JORDAN: Director reported "he could not hire anybody. His firm runs ads and nobody applies."
- MR. STERN: "Labor markets are tight. The rate of unemployment in Minnesota is at or near an all-time low."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are moderately tight at the national level (5.6% unemployment, near NAIRU) with notably tighter conditions in several districts. Wage pressure is emerging in specific segments (entry-level, skilled trades) but is not yet broad-based.

**Minority views**: Minehan notes worker insecurity and that businesses are trying to operate with smaller workforces; some southern New England states are weak. This partially offsets the tight-market reports.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "the rebound in automobile sales in August was impressive. Overall consumer spending and consumer confidence... have been more firmly maintained."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "consumer spending and housing have shown improvement. And although business investment growth is slowing somewhat, we all recognize that the previous rates of expansion were not sustainable."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "consumer demand appears to have weakened" in the Second District.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending and investment are roughly at trend -- consumer spending firming, housing recovering, autos rebounding, but business investment decelerating from previously rapid rates. Net position is approximately at trend.

**Minority views**: McDonough sees weakness in the Northeast; Kelley and Broaddus lean more bullish. Narrow band reflects broad agreement on moderate picture.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 43 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "financial markets since our last meeting have generally improved--the dollar, the stock market, the bond markets. This means a continued availability of relatively low cost capital."
- MR. MELZER: "Total bank loan growth has been rapid over the last two years."
- MR. BLINDER / MS. YELLEN: The real federal funds rate is "almost certainly above the equilibrium real federal funds rate," implying policy is restrictive.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are somewhat supportive -- markets have improved, credit is available, loan growth solid. However, the elevated real funds rate implies a degree of policy-driven restraint that filters into conditions. Net: slightly loose of neutral.

**Minority views**: Boehne reports flat loan demand; some see the real rate as a binding constraint. This widens the band modestly.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 44 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would suggest that it be done with the Committee's concurrence... I put on the table 'B' symmetrical." Also: "I think it is correct to argue that we are probably at a real funds rate level that is higher than the long-term equilibrium."
- MS. YELLEN: "We have what Charles Schultz dubbed a 'termites in the basement' problem... the real funds rate would need to decline."
- MR. MELZER: "I view policy as being somewhat restrictive, and I think that is quite appropriate... I would be very careful not to ease before we saw some evidence" of declining inflation expectations.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous support for holding rates steady, but the majority lean toward eventual easing. A significant minority (Melzer, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Kelley) favors the current restrictive stance as appropriate. The net lean is mildly dovish -- hold now with an easing bias.

**Minority views**: The hawkish bloc (5 members) favors maintaining restrictiveness to cement inflation gains. The dovish bloc (Blinder, Yellen, Lindsey, Minehan, Phillips, Boehne) sees easing as needed in coming months. This divergence explains the ±8 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote for "B symmetric" -- no formal dissents.
- However, reasoning diverges significantly: MR. PARRY supports the hold because "I would be reluctant to cut the funds rate at this time," while MR. BLINDER supports it while noting "the current real federal funds rate is almost certainly above the equilibrium."
- MS. YELLEN: "I support your proposal... although I do find myself very much in sympathy with President Forrestal's analysis" (who made a case for moving today).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: No formal dissent, but clearly two camps with different rationales for the same action. Agreement on the current decision masks genuine disagreement about the appropriate future direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 10 ±4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. PHILLIPS: Brief mention that "financial markets since our last meeting have generally improved."
- The "train wreck" fiscal scenario is discussed as a risk to market stability, but this is fiscal default risk, not financial stability broadly.
- No discussion of asset bubbles, banking health, systemic risk, or credit market dysfunction.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives minimal attention. Passing references to market conditions exist, but no participant treats financial stability as a meaningful topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 37 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "our next move certainly will be at a time when this Committee is meeting" -- implying the next move is easing.
- MR. BOEHNE: "Almost surely we will have to adjust policy at some point in coming months."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "We should have some room to ease sometime this fall."
- MR. MELZER: "I would be very careful not to ease before we saw some evidence" of declining inflation expectations -- pushing back on imminent easing.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is mildly dovish. Most participants expect the next move to be an easing, possibly within a few months, but the timing is data-dependent and complicated by fiscal uncertainty. A minority resists near-term easing signals.

**Minority views**: The hawkish bloc (Melzer, Parry, Hoenig) would not signal easing and prefers to wait for clearer evidence. This widens the band.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. KELLEY: Lists "an exceptional number of rather basic questions" including "the level of the NAIRU, the need for improved measurement of the inflation rate, the trend of productivity, the longevity of this absence of wage pressures."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "there are a couple of major unknowns. The train wreck scenario is one."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I don't know what to take into account at this point" regarding fiscal effects.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, driven primarily by fiscal policy unknowns (budget negotiations, possible government default) and structural questions (NAIRU, productivity). The economic outlook itself is relatively clear, but the policy environment injects meaningful uncertainty.
