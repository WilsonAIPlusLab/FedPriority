# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-03-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified (go-around format plus policy discussion). Speakers include: Jordan, Parry, Moskow, Boehne, Guynn, Hoenig, Minehan, McTeer, Broaddus, Stern, Poole, Rivlin, Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips (minimal), and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-populated meeting with extensive discussion from approximately 18 participants.

### Era Context
March 1998: Late 1990s boom. The U.S. economy is in the midst of an extended expansion fueled by technology investment and rising equity prices. The Asian financial crisis is underway (Thailand, Korea, Indonesia, Japan in recession), but its effects on the U.S. have been modest. Oil prices are low. The federal funds rate has been held steady at 5.50% for nearly two years. Inflation is very low by historical standards, but labor markets are extremely tight.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as currently low/below historical concern but with building pressures beneath the surface. Greenspan: "We just cannot take oil out... the overall inflation rate has shown no evidence of accelerating at this stage." Parry: "core price inflation probably should come in at a rate of around 2 percent." Multiple participants note temporary factors holding inflation down (oil, imports, dollar, BLS methodology). Compensation growth is running ~4%, but product price inflation is not yet evident. Moskow's academic council "every council member expects inflation to accelerate noticeably over the next 12 months." The center of gravity is: inflation is currently subdued but pressures are building from tight labor markets, and temporary favorable factors may unwind.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Current inflation is low (~2% core), but wages are pressing, and most participants see upside inflation risk. The actual measured inflation is low, but the perception of latent/building pressure is notable. Score should reflect the NET perception: modest current inflation with recognized building pressures. This puts it in the 41-49 range -- slightly below target/neutral but with clear concern about the trajectory. I'll score 45.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as operating above potential. Parry: "real GDP growth remaining near potential." Poole: "Economic activity is bumping along... above and beyond capacity." Meyer: "it is not imprudent to operate for a while beyond the point of long-run sustainable capacity." Greenspan: "we have an economic performance that is essentially unsustainable." Moskow: "GDP growth consistently above its trend rate." Hoenig: "District firms report that they continue to operate at very high levels of capacity." Nearly universal view that the economy is above potential.

Bright-line rule: "above potential" language is pervasive. Score MUST be > 50. The language is strong -- "above and beyond capacity," "unsustainable," "very high levels of capacity," GDP growth "well above" Greenbook. This is clearly above potential but not extreme overheating (no shortages of goods/materials, only labor). Score: 60.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Virtually every participant describes labor markets as "very tight." Jordan: 50,000 job openings on 550,000 employees (10%). Moskow: "labor markets remain very tight." Boehne: "qualified workers are scarce." Minehan: "Even entry-level employees... are difficult to find." Broaddus: employer defines "hot job prospect" as someone who shows up and passes a drug test. Hoenig: "about half the firms... were responding to labor shortages by raising wages more than normal." Multiple anecdotes of firms going to extraordinary lengths. Compensation running ~4%.

This is clearly well above NAIRU. Extremely tight by any standard. The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. This is March 1998 -- same story. Score: 68.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Domestic demand is described as exceptionally strong. Broaddus: consumer spending "going great guns." Kelley: "Consumer confidence is sky high." Meyer: "continued exceptional momentum in private domestic demand." Greenspan: "extraordinary domestic demand." Parry: "considerable momentum." McTeer: "Retail sales have been quite healthy." Housing strong. Business investment strong, especially high-tech capital goods. Some offset from net exports and potential inventory correction, but domestic demand dominates.

Score should be well above trend. "Exceptional momentum," "extraordinary" -- this is 65-70 range. Score: 66.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are described as very loose/accommodative. Minehan: "monetary policy is not tight; it is not even neutral. It is accommodative to an increasingly speculative environment." Meyer: "Real long-term rates are steady to declining, equity prices are soaring, credit availability is ample, underwriting standards may have eased somewhat, loan pricing is aggressive." Ferguson: "Risk spreads for private debt instruments are quite narrow." Greenspan: "yield spreads have fallen to unbelievable numbers." Multiple reports of loose lending standards and speculative activity.

Polarity check: Higher = TIGHTER. The text overwhelmingly says conditions are loose/accommodative. This points to a low score. Score: 18.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The decision was to hold rates but move to an asymmetric tightening bias. The center of gravity strongly favors eventual tightening. Several participants (Jordan, Broaddus, Minehan, Gramlich) explicitly state they would have supported a 25bp hike today. Most others support holding but with strong language about needing to move soon. Greenspan: "we are running out of time... unless this system starts to self-correct fairly shortly, we will have no choice but to move." McDonough: "risks... somewhat on the high side." Nobody advocates easing.

The lean is clearly hawkish. Hold with tightening bias, several wanting to move now. This is in the 58-65 range. The decision is to hold, but the forward lean is clearly toward tightening, and multiple members wanted to act immediately. Score: 62.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: No formal dissent, but there is a meaningful spread. The majority supports holding with asymmetry. But Broaddus, Jordan, and Minehan explicitly say they would have preferred to raise rates today. Gramlich says he would have supported a hike too. On the other side, Rivlin and Ferguson are more cautious about acting. The disagreement is real but contained -- it's about timing (now vs. soon), not about direction. Most agree on the direction.

Score: 38. Notable differences about pace/timing, with 3-4 wanting to move now, but broad agreement on direction and the asymmetric recommendation.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Financial stability/conditions receive substantial attention. Multiple participants discuss: speculative real estate (Jordan, Hoenig, McTeer, Guynn, Rivlin), stock market overvaluation (Greenspan at length, McDonough, Kelley), loose credit/lending standards (Jordan, Meyer, Minehan), asset price inflation (Jordan, Poole). Greenspan devotes a large portion of his remarks to the stock market, equity premium, earnings expectations, and the "virtuous wealth-effect cycle." Minehan warns about "feeding speculative excess."

This is a prominently discussed topic but not the dominant topic -- the economy/labor/inflation assessment still takes more time. Score: 55.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Greenspan: "the clock is starting to tick... unless the evidence of an Asian slowdown is fairly pronounced and/or the stock market goes in the other direction, we will be singing a different tune the next time we meet." Boehne: "we indeed will have to face up to making a policy adjustment and probably more than once." Kelley: "a move to tighten is becoming increasingly advisable." Discussion about laying groundwork for a rate hike via policy speeches. The signal is: tightening is coming unless conditions change materially.

Score: 68. Clear tightening signal ahead, though conditional on data.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Moderate uncertainty. The main uncertainty is the Asian impact -- whether it will be large enough to slow the economy. Rivlin: "it is not inconceivable that we will be back in six weeks when something... has weakened the expansion." Greenspan: the timing/magnitude of Asian effects is uncertain. Parry: "it is uncertain... whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure." The productivity puzzle adds uncertainty. But participants express considerable confidence in the near-term strength of domestic demand. The uncertainty is more about specific channels (Asia, stock market correction) than about the fundamental state.

Score: 40. Somewhat elevated -- specific uncertainties about Asia and the stock market, but overall confidence in the domestic economy's strength.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness is more extreme than output gap because even as the economy runs above potential, productivity and other factors are keeping product markets from showing shortages -- it's the labor market that shows the strain.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Both hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current action. Consistent: they held rates but the forward signal is clearly toward tightening. The slight divergence reflects the "hold today but move soon" consensus.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "the overall inflation rate has shown no evidence of accelerating at this stage... we are beginning to see pressures emerge in the cost structure"
- PARRY: "core price inflation probably should come in at a rate of around 2 percent" but "it is uncertain... whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure"
- MOSKOW: "Every council member expects inflation to accelerate noticeably over the next 12 months"
- MINEHAN: "Wage and benefit increases have crept up by some measures into the 4 percent range"

Center of gravity: Inflation is currently low (~2% core) thanks to temporary favorable factors (oil, imports, dollar), but compensation is running ~4% and most participants see building pressures that will emerge as temporary factors fade. The score reflects modestly below-midpoint current inflation with significant latent pressure.

Minority views: A few (McTeer, Stern) emphasize productivity may keep inflation contained longer; most see upside inflation risk ahead.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- POOLE: "Economic activity is bumping along... above and beyond capacity"
- MEYER: "it is not imprudent to operate for a while beyond the point of long-run sustainable capacity"
- GREENSPAN: "we have an economic performance that is essentially unsustainable"
- MOSKOW: "GDP growth consistently above its trend rate"

Center of gravity: Near-universal agreement the economy is operating above potential, with GDP growth well above trend and capacity utilization high. Not extreme overheating since no product/materials shortages, but clearly above potential.

Minority views: None meaningfully dissent from the above-potential assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- JORDAN: "550,000 employees... over 50,000 [job openings]. That total is 10 percent of current employment"
- BOEHNE: "The most frequently heard comment is that qualified workers are scarce and they are becoming more expensive"
- BROADDUS: employer "now defines a 'hot job prospect' as somebody who shows up for an interview and passes a drug test"
- PARRY: "there is little question that labor markets will remain very tight this year. There is already significant upward pressure on wages"

Center of gravity: Every single participant describes labor markets as "very tight." Compensation growth ~4%. Extraordinary anecdotes of difficulty hiring at all skill levels. Firms deploying creative strategies (apprenticeships, welfare-to-work, robots). This is among the tightest labor markets in decades.

Minority views: None disagree on tightness; some (Boehne, Stern) emphasize that productivity may be absorbing some of the pressure.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 66 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "the extraordinary domestic demand that we are observing is almost surely a spillover from the unbelievable uptrend in the ratio of wealth to disposable income"
- MEYER: "continued exceptional momentum in private domestic demand and payroll employment"
- BROADDUS: consumer spending "going great guns"
- MOSKOW: "Consumer fundamentals are strong and firms seem poised to remain big spenders for high-tech capital goods"

Center of gravity: Domestic demand is exceptionally strong across consumer spending, housing, and business investment. Some drag from net exports and potential inventory correction, but domestic fundamentals overwhelm the negatives. Well above trend.

Minority views: McTeer notes some regional softening in the Dallas district due to oil/Asia, but this is an outlier.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MINEHAN: "monetary policy is not tight; it is not even neutral. It is accommodative to an increasingly speculative environment"
- MEYER: "equity prices are soaring, credit availability is ample, underwriting standards may have eased somewhat, loan pricing is aggressive, and money growth is rapid"
- GREENSPAN: "yield spreads have fallen to unbelievable numbers, the slack attitude of lending officers has become a potential concern"
- FERGUSON: "Risk spreads for private debt instruments are quite narrow... credit to private nonfinancial borrowers... is running well above the pace of the last couple of years"

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are extremely accommodative -- loose credit, narrow spreads, soaring equities, ample liquidity, aggressive lending. Multiple participants describe conditions as fueling speculation.

Minority views: None; universal agreement conditions are very loose.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "I think we ought to be moving back to asymmetry... I do not think it is appropriate to move at this stage" but "we are running out of time"
- BROADDUS: "it will not surprise you or anyone else that I would still prefer to take action today"
- MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting prepared to vote for an increase of 25 basis points"
- JORDAN: "I would strongly prefer taking action today"
- GRAMLICH: "if somebody had proposed that we raise the fed funds rate by 25 basis points, I would have supported that, too"

Center of gravity: The committee voted to hold rates but adopted a tightening bias (asymmetric directive). At least 4 members would have preferred an immediate hike. No one advocated easing. The lean is moderately hawkish -- hold now but prepare to tighten soon.

Minority views: 4 members preferred immediate tightening (Broaddus, Jordan, Minehan, Gramlich). Rivlin was slightly more cautious. The spread is meaningful but directionally unified.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- BROADDUS: "I would still prefer to take action today"
- MINEHAN: "I came to this meeting prepared to vote for an increase of 25 basis points... and I also came prepared to dissent"
- GRAMLICH: "if somebody had proposed that we raise the fed funds rate by 25 basis points, I would have supported that, too"
- RIVLIN: "It is not inconceivable that we will be back in six weeks when something... has weakened the expansion"

Center of gravity: No formal dissents, but 3-4 members would have preferred immediate action. Disagreement is about timing (now vs. soon), not direction. All agree on the asymmetric tightening bias. The spread is moderate -- meaningful differences about pace but agreement on diagnosis.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of stock market valuation, equity premium, earnings expectations, wealth effect cycle, and vulnerability of the financial system
- JORDAN: "I also continue to be concerned that we may never see the effects of monetary excesses in output prices, but rather that we will see them in asset prices"
- HOENIG: Anecdotes of speculative condo buying and investment fund excess demand
- MINEHAN: "I think we face a real risk of feeding the speculative excess that will ultimately come to haunt us"
- RIVLIN: "our worries about speculation, loose credit"

Center of gravity: Financial stability and speculative excess receive substantial attention from many participants. The stock market, real estate speculation, and loose credit are discussed at length. This is a significant but not dominant topic -- the macroeconomic assessment still receives more total airtime.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "the clock is starting to tick... unless the evidence of an Asian slowdown is fairly pronounced and/or the stock market goes in the other direction, we will be singing a different tune the next time we meet"
- BOEHNE: "we indeed will have to face up to making a policy adjustment and probably more than once"
- KELLEY: "a move to tighten is becoming increasingly advisable"
- Discussion about laying groundwork for tightening via policy speeches

Center of gravity: The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. The committee discussed how to prepare markets for a rate hike. The asymmetric directive itself is a tightening signal. The conditionality (depends on Asia, stock market) keeps this from being higher.

Minority views: Rivlin notes conditions could change; Ferguson warns against premature action.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- PARRY: "it is uncertain in my view whether the special factors moderating inflation will endure"
- RIVLIN: "it is not inconceivable that we will be back in six weeks when something... has weakened the expansion and we may not be quite as sure"
- GREENSPAN: Discussion of unknown timing of Asian effects and stock market correction
- STERN: "the kicker here is productivity. I don't want to get caught up in how we measure productivity"

Center of gravity: Moderate uncertainty. Key unknowns are: (1) magnitude/timing of Asian spillover, (2) whether the stock market will correct, (3) sustainability of productivity gains, (4) persistence of favorable supply shocks. But participants express strong confidence in the near-term domestic picture. Uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not deeply unsettling.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 38**
Multiple participants discuss falling goods prices from Asian imports, lower oil, weak commodity prices. McTeer: "Price pressures remain subdued." Greenspan: "pressures on product prices were 'eerily calm.'" Goods inflation is below normal due to import competition and commodity weakness.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 58**
Minehan: "Wage and benefit increases have crept up... into the 4 percent range, particularly in services. There are selected price increases in service industries." Jordan notes rising teacher salaries and rental rates. Services inflation is modestly above neutral.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
Housing prices mentioned in passing (Jordan: "Home prices are rising very rapidly"), but shelter inflation as a CPI component is not discussed by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48**
Hoenig: "bond markets that are signaling benign inflation expectations." Ferguson: "low inflation expectations." Expectations appear well-anchored near target despite tight labor markets. Slightly below midpoint since they are described as "benign" and "low."

**CREDIT_STRESS: 25**
The opposite of stress -- credit is too loose. Jordan: "credit quality standards definitely have been lowered." Meyer: "underwriting standards may have eased somewhat, loan pricing is aggressive." Spreads are extremely narrow. Stress is very low; risk-taking is elevated.

**EQUITY_STRESS: 20**
Not stress in the sense of crash/undervaluation. Rather, overvaluation concern. Greenspan discusses equity premium at low end of historical range, earnings expectations unrealistic. McDonough: "equity markets are overpriced in my view." This is a bubble/overvaluation concern direction (low score per rubric: 1 = extreme overvaluation concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
Banking system health not discussed prominently. Some passing mentions of lending standards loosening, but no discussion of bank stress/capital/failures.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
Not discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: N/A**
Dollar strength mentioned as a factor holding down import prices, but not discussed as a concern per se by 2+ participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 70**
Extensively discussed. Boehne: "pervasive use of technology throughout the economy to increase productivity... one gets the sense that the end is nowhere in sight." Guynn: multiple reports of productivity gains across industries. Greenspan: "output per hour... still shows a 2.3 percent increase." Stern: "business people have asserted for a long time that it is [on a favorable trend] and they continue to do so." Productivity is widely seen as above historical trend, though measurement issues exist.
