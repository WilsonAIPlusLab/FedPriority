## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the March 19–20, 2013 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (MR. KOCHERLAKOTA, MR. LOCKHART, MR. WILLIAMS, MR. ROSENGREN, MR. FISHER, MR. EVANS, MR. PLOSSER, MR. STEIN, MS. PIANALTO, MS. GEORGE, MR. BULLARD, MR. LACKER, MS. YELLEN, MS. DUKE, MR. TARULLO, MR. POWELL, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, and summary remarks from CHAIRMAN BERNANKE). Governor Raskin is referenced by the Chairman but her statement appears to have been omitted from this decomposed file. The discussion covers: (a) the go-around on the policy decision (Alternative B), (b) discussion of tapering strategy, exit principles, and statement language, and (c) brief policy discussion remarks from the Chairman. The document is rich and long — high confidence is warranted for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
March 2013 is in the QE3 era. The FOMC has been purchasing $85 billion/month in Treasuries and MBS since September 2012 under an open-ended, outcome-based framework. The unemployment rate stands at 7.7 percent, PCE inflation is 1.2 percent (12-month), both below mandate-consistent levels. Fiscal headwinds (sequester, payroll tax increases) are prominent. The meeting marks early "taper talk" — many participants are beginning to frame conditions under which purchases could be slowed. This is broadly in the 2013 "below-target on both sides of the mandate" period. I note this for anchor comparison but will score based purely on what the text says.

### 3. Directional Analysis — Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below target** — meaningfully so. Rosengren: "The 12-month change in the overall PCE chain index stands at 1.2 percent through January, 0.8 percentage point below our 2 percent target." Williams: "Inflation is well below our target. We're missing on both sides of our mandate." Yellen: "my forecast for inflation one to two years out is still that it will continue running below our 2 percent objective." Bullard: "Inflation is currently low according to our preferred measures...We definitely need to defend our inflation target from the low side." Kocherlakota: "when the outlook for inflation is as weak as it is, and where we have a target, we've said, of 2 percent inflation — it's very hard for us to be backing away from that." George mentions risk of "higher long-term inflation expectations" in the future — a tail risk, not a current reading. Center of gravity: inflation is clearly below target but not in deflation territory. Score direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: 0.8pp below target, described as "well below" — this places it in the 35–42 range per anchors for 2013.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential**. Yellen: "growth barely exceeding potential and little further improvement in the labor market over this coming year as increased fiscal drag offsets the improved momentum." Williams: "The economy is only slowly improving." Rosengren: "the progress toward normalcy is frustratingly slow." Kocherlakota: "there is a strong argument for actually adding to accommodation." Lockhart notes Q4 GDP growth was only ~0.6%, and SEP forecasts anticipate "a faster pace of growth in 2014 and 2015" — implying current pace is below desired trend. Bullard: "Continuing good news will put us in a fairly good position to adjust the pace of purchases at future meetings" — forward-looking, not current strength. George: "private-sector demand and housing markets appear poised to support growth going forward" — cautiously positive but not above potential. Bright-line: "below potential" language throughout → score MUST be < 50. Magnitude: "barely exceeding potential" with fiscal drag → economy is modestly but clearly below potential. 2013 recovery period anchor is 35–42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see the labor market as **well below full employment but improving**. Rosengren: "The unemployment rate is 7.7 percent, 2½ percent above my estimate of full employment." Yellen: "Labor market slack did diminish during the late summer, and payroll employment growth has picked up in recent months." Powell: "The unemployment rate has come down about ½ percentage point more than expectation. Payroll employment is stronger, averaging 200,000 for the past five months." Duke: "job creation held up remarkably well." Pianalto: "I would judge the outlook for labor market conditions to have improved substantially once I see six to nine months of average job gains of about 200,000 per month. Since we have already had five months at that pace, I am almost there." Bright-line: unemployment at 7.7%, explicitly cited as "2½ percent above full employment" → score MUST be < 50. Anchor for 2011–14: 35–40. The labor market is improving (5 months of ~200K payrolls) but still in the "slack remains" zone. Score toward upper end of 35–40 band given recent improvement narrative.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see spending/investment as **below trend but with positive momentum**. Lockhart: Q4 GDP growth ~0.6%, characterizes as "below the trend of the recovery." Yellen: "growth barely exceeding potential." Williams: "The economy is only slowly improving." But there are positive notes: Duke: "job creation held up remarkably well as we approached the fiscal cliff and, more recently, in the face of increases in payroll and income taxes." Rosengren: "The economy is continuing to grow, despite much more fiscal austerity than many people anticipated." George: "private-sector demand and housing markets appear poised to support growth going forward." The overall picture is "below trend but recovering" — consistent with 38–45 range. The tone is "sluggish/cautious" but not "contracting." Anchor 2011–13 moderate: 35–42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants characterize financial conditions as **accommodative/loose** — the LSAP program is described as producing "significant positive benefit in terms of financial conditions." Dudley: "We are getting significant positive benefit in terms of financial conditions, and the improvement in financial conditions does seem to be lending support to the economy." Fisher discusses the banking sector: "2012 banking data show it was a good year for banks...The proportion of banks that are 1- and 2-rated is now 70 percent." Rosengren: "peripheral Europe to date has actually weathered the storm surprisingly well, so that the yields on Spanish and Italian debt have been pretty well behaved." George notes risks of "reach for yield" and "financial imbalances" from the easy stance. Plosser expresses concern about "buoyancy in asset prices" from communications. These concern-about-looseness observations confirm financial conditions are loose, not tight. Score direction: below midpoint (loose). Magnitude: "accommodative," "supportive" → low-to-moderate 20s–30s range. Higher = tighter; current conditions are loose → low score.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The center of gravity is **moderately dovish** — all participants support Alternative B (continuation of $85B/month purchases) and the overwhelming majority explicitly oppose moving to Alt C (taper now). However, a significant minority (Plosser, George, Stein, Pianalto, Duke, Powell, Fisher) are explicitly framing tapering in June as desirable/likely. Kocherlakota, Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen favor continuing current pace. Lacker: "I'd favor moving toward tapering as soon as possible... feasible at this meeting." Plosser: "Were I not afraid of surprising the public, I would advocate beginning to taper purchases at this meeting." But both ultimately support Alt B (no change today). The unanimous vote for Alt B means NO taper, NO acceleration, current $85B/month maintained. This is a QE-in-progress meeting → by rubric, "participants arguing 'how much more to buy' are in the 15–25 range." However, the dominant discussion is tapering optionality and exit — this pushes the lean somewhat less dovish than a pure QE expansion discussion. The center of gravity: strong majority supports current QE (dovish), but a substantial and growing minority is actively framing upcoming taper as appropriate. Score: moderate dovish lean, roughly 25–35. Rubric anchor: QE era 2010–12 is 15–25; this meeting has more hawkish lean than pure QE2 debates given the widespread taper discussion.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
There is **moderate disagreement** — no formal dissent is recorded in this transcript (all support Alt B), but there are very clear and vocal distinct camps. Plosser and Lacker openly favor immediate taper. George favors starting taper now. Stein, Pianalto, Duke, Powell, Fisher all explicitly advocate June taper. On the other side, Kocherlakota, Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen strongly defend current pace and credibility. There are also genuine sub-debates about: (a) balance sheet cap vs. credibility tension; (b) stock vs. flow effects; (c) forward guidance quality; (d) MBS sales in exit strategy; (e) statement language. The meeting is characterized by "a basic tension" and "no way to cut this Gordian knot." However, no formal voting dissents were cast — the disagreement remained at the rhetorical/preference level. Score: 40–50 range. A zero-dissent meeting but with multiple distinct camps and sharp substantive differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Multiple participants discuss financial stability topics explicitly and at length. Plosser: extended discussion of asset price buoyancy, wealth effects, and the risk of a sharp market correction. George: extended discussion of "reach for yield" risks, insurance companies/pension funds taking on risk. Lacker: discussion of MBS REITs, moral hazard, non-Treasury holdings. Stein: discussion of "market fragility issues," costs/efficacy. Bullard: balance sheet size relative to GDP. Fisher: banking sector health. Dudley: agency MBS REIT moral hazard. Powell: financial stability risk from exit strategy. This is a prominent topic across many speakers, but it is not the dominant concern — the main focus is on policy framework/tapering sequencing. Score: moderate-to-prominent, 35–45 range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The forward signal is **mildly dovish with emerging optionality**. The meeting keeps the Evans Rule (6.5% unemployment threshold) and the open-ended QE framework intact. The Chairman explicitly says the message is "we might taper, but we think we might taper because we think the economy is getting better." The changes to the statement are mostly cosmetic. However, many participants are signaling via their statements that tapering in June is likely if data cooperate — this is a forward lean toward tapering, but NOT a formal signal change. The forward guidance remains essentially: threshold-based at 6.5% unemployment + open-ended QE continuing. The rubric anchor for 2012–13 Evans Rule/QE3 is 3–8. This meeting is slightly more hawkish than pure Evans Rule maintenance because of the taper optionality discussion, but the guidance itself has not changed. Score: roughly 8–15 range — strong easing commitment plus slight nudge toward signaling optionality.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Participants express **moderate uncertainty** — mostly the standard fiscal headwinds/European risks sort. Rosengren notes Cyprus/Italy but characterizes it as "surprisingly well." Yellen: forecasts "barely exceeding potential" but admits this may change. Tarullo explicitly invokes "a little bit of uncertainty" as a desirable feature of the communications. Kocherlakota discusses uncertainty about what the public understands their commitment to mean. Bullard calls open-ended purchases "absolutely brilliant" for its automatic-stabilizer property — which implies he sees the macro path as uncertain. Fisher: "our credibility will be underscored best if we're not driven by any fixed date but instead by being practicable." The uncertainty here is not extreme — it's the normal uncertainty of a recovery. No participant uses language like "unprecedented" or "cannot bound the range." Score: 30–40, standard caveats with some elevated concerns about fiscal drag and European risks but no deep uncertainty.
