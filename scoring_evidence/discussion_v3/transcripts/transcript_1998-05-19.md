# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-05-19 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a go-around of economic assessments from regional presidents and governors, followed by a policy discussion led by Chairman Greenspan. Speakers include: Boehne, Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, McTeer, Guynn, Stern, Jordan, Gramlich, McDonough, Ferguson, Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, Poole, and Greenspan.

### Era Context
May 1998: The U.S. economy is in the late-1990s boom. GDP growth was a "stunner" in Q1 1998. Asian financial crisis is unfolding (Indonesia, Japan, Korea). Unemployment at 4.3%. Inflation remains very low by historical standards (GDP deflator under 1%, core CPI around 2.1% on 12-month basis). The fed funds rate was at 5.5%, unchanged since March 1997.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently low but with upside risks. Multiple speakers note tight labor markets creating wage pressures, but almost all acknowledge that actual price inflation is remarkably subdued. McTeer: "GDP deflator in 1997 was the lowest since 1964"; "PPI has been zero or negative" in 13 of 16 months. Meyer notes CPI on 3-month basis is 2.8% (rising) but 12-month is 2.1%. Moskow raised his CPI forecast to 2.5%. The center of gravity is: inflation is currently low but risks are tilting upward. Several participants (Broaddus, Moskow, Minehan, Meyer) are more concerned; McTeer, Gramlich, and Rivlin are less so. Direction: slightly below the midpoint for CURRENT perception, but the upside risk language pushes it modestly above neutral. The actual inflation readings are very benign, but the wage pressures and risk language suggest participants see emerging pressure. Score should be around 42-48 -- inflation is low but participants increasingly worry about upside risks. I'll score 45.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants unanimously describe the economy as operating above potential. Broaddus: "growth of demand has already outstripped the growth of productivity for a long time." Moskow: GDP growth "above estimates of a sustainable trend." Meyer: "labor markets are very tight" and growth consistently outperforms. Greenspan: "very dramatic increase in underlying demand." The bright-line rule says "above potential" language requires >50. Multiple speakers describe growth as "unsustainably strong" (Boehne), GDP growth at 3%+ vs. trend of 2.75%. Score: 58-62. The economy is clearly running above potential but not dramatically overheating given supply-side productivity gains that are partially accommodating the demand. Score: 59.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is perhaps the most uniformly discussed topic. Nearly every speaker describes labor markets as "extremely tight" or "very tight." Moskow: unemployment at 3.8% in his five states, hiring plans highest since 1978. Minehan: "cannot hire check clerks," "cannot find people to deliver papers." Guynn: Wal-Mart postponing expansion for lack of workers, 8,000 welder shortage. Poole: a firm "cannot even find recruiters." Jordan: teller getting job offers from customers. The unemployment rate is 4.3%, well below estimated NAIRU. This is clearly very tight. Anchors say 65-75 for 1999-2000. We're approaching that. Score: 68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Domestic demand is described as very strong by virtually all speakers. Guynn: "aggregate demand remains strong" and sectors "have not fallen back as I expected." McDonough: retailers reporting sales ahead of plans. Moskow: auto sales at 15.4 million, highest of year. Broaddus: hard to see deceleration. Greenspan: "very dramatic increase in underlying demand." Business investment is strong, driven by tech/productivity. Consumer spending robust driven by wealth effects and confidence. Score: 65 -- well above trend, robust across consumer and business investment.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants describe financial conditions as very accommodative/loose. Boehne lists: narrowing spreads, easing credit terms, high consumer debt, subprime expansion, 125% LTV ratios, rapid REIT activity, narrow risk spreads, junk bond growth. Stern: "many implicit signs... suggest there is not much monetary restraint." Jordan: "people have more money than sense"; lending driven by asset assessments not cash flows. Minehan: "monetary policy is accommodative right now." Ferguson: "monetary and credit aggregates are quite accommodative." This is clearly loose conditions. Higher = tighter, so this should be well below 50. Score: 20 -- loose, accommodative conditions, easy credit, narrow spreads.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The decision was to hold rates with an asymmetric tightening bias. The center of gravity is "hold but lean toward tightening soon." Several speakers would have preferred to tighten today: Poole, Minehan, Broaddus, Jordan, Guynn, Meyer ("I would support a tightening today if the consensus were there"). Others favor holding: Gramlich, McDonough, Rivlin, McTeer, Kelley, Boehne. Greenspan advocates holding due to Asia and productivity uncertainty but maintains asymmetric tightening bias and says "I don't think we can wait indefinitely." Ferguson: "appropriate to move before the summer is out." Moskow: "only a matter of time before we will have to tighten." The lean is clearly hawkish -- not a question of whether to tighten but when. Score: 58. Hold steady with clear tightening bias; many participants would have preferred action now.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent on the vote, but there is substantial disagreement about timing. At least 5-6 participants (Poole, Minehan, Broaddus, Jordan, Guynn, Meyer) expressed a preference for tightening today. Others (Rivlin, McTeer, Gramlich) were more comfortable waiting. The disagreement is real but focused on timing rather than direction -- nearly all agree tightening is likely needed. Score: 42 -- moderate differences about pace, no formal dissents but several expressed clear preference for immediate action.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Extensive discussion of financial conditions, asset prices, and the stock market. Boehne devotes substantial remarks to financial excess indicators. Jordan discusses lending driven by asset assessments, questionable acquisition prices. Greenspan gives an extended analysis of whether there's a stock market bubble. Meyer proposes two rules for stock market policy. Gramlich discusses equity premium. McDonough mentions banking sector. Multiple speakers discuss accommodative financial conditions as a risk. This is a prominent topic -- most participants address it meaningfully. Score: 58.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The directive is asymmetric toward tightening. Greenspan: "maintaining asymmetry toward tightening is essential." Multiple speakers signal likely tightening ahead. Ferguson: "appropriate to move before the summer is out." Moskow: "only a matter of time." Meyer: "monetary policy needs to edge toward a tighter stance and soon." The forward signal is clearly tilted toward tightening. Score: 63 -- mild tightening bias with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Substantial uncertainty is expressed. Kelley: "We just cannot know yet how all this is going to develop and net out." Boehne: "data that remain unclear." Greenspan: extensive discussion of uncertainty about productivity trends, stock market, Asia. Gramlich: "uncertainties are great enough." Poole: "I do not have a conviction that however I vote my decision will turn out to be right." Stern: "so much uncertainty that I conclude we should not do very much." Multiple participants emphasize inability to forecast. Score: 55 -- elevated uncertainty, multiple participants emphasize unusual difficulty in reading the outlook.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (59) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Labor tightness is higher, which makes sense -- the labor market is tighter than the overall output gap because productivity gains are partially absorbing the demand, preventing full overheating in output prices even as labor markets are extremely tight.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Aligned. Current action is hold but forward lean is toward tightening. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish than the current lean, which is consistent with "hold now but tighten soon."

### Speaker Count by Policy View Direction
- Prefer tightening now: Poole, Minehan, Broaddus, Jordan, Guynn, Meyer (~6)
- Prefer holding with tightening bias: Greenspan, McDonough, Ferguson, Moskow, Parry, Stern, Gramlich, Boehne, Rivlin, Kelley, McTeer (~11)
- No one advocates easing

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MCTEER: "The GDP deflator in 1997 was the lowest since 1964... In 13 of the last 16 months, the PPI has been zero or negative."
- MR. MEYER: "The three-month rate is now 2.8 percent and the six-month rate is 2.5 percent... the risk of higher inflation has escalated a bit further since the last meeting."
- MR. MOSKOW: "we have raised our forecast for core CPI inflation this year to 2-1/2 percent... the risks... seem to be more decidedly [to the upside]."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Actual inflation readings remain very low (GDP deflator under 1%, core CPI 12-month at 2.1%), but a growing number of participants see upside risks from tight labor markets and wage acceleration. The score reflects inflation that is currently benign but with rising risk perceptions.

**Minority views**: McTeer and Gramlich see less inflation concern (anchoring the low end). Moskow and Meyer see more imminent risk (anchoring the high end). This produces the ±8 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 59 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BOEHNE: "Employment and output growth appear unsustainably strong."
- MR. MOSKOW: "we see GDP growth of about 3 percent for this year... above estimates of a sustainable trend."
- MR. MEYER: "growth turned out to be 2 percentage points faster in the first quarter than expected... the tighter labor market we worried about last time has already arrived."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is clearly running above potential with GDP growth well above trend estimates (~2.75%), but the degree of overheating is moderated by supply-side productivity gains that are partially accommodating the strong demand.

**Minority views**: McTeer emphasizes supply-side accommodation (lower end). Jordan and Meyer emphasize stronger overheating (upper end).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "Labor markets are extremely tight by all measures... hiring plans are the highest since 1978."
- MS. MINEHAN: "Our labor markets remain very tight even at entry levels... We cannot hire check clerks... cannot find people to deliver the papers."
- MR. POOLE: "a woman who runs a computer services firm said that she cannot even find recruiters anymore!"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Virtually every speaker describes labor markets as extremely or very tight. Unemployment at 4.3% is well below estimated NAIRU. Anecdotal evidence of shortages is pervasive across regions and skill levels. This is among the tightest labor market conditions described in the late-1990s cycle.

**Minority views**: Broad consensus on this dimension; narrow uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "aggregate demand remains very strong... consumer spending, housing, and business fixed investment have not fallen back as I expected."
- MR. MOSKOW: "April's light vehicle sales were at an annual rate of 15.4 million units; that is the highest so far this year."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a very dramatic increase in underlying demand in the economy."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is robust, driven by wealth effects and high confidence. Business investment is very strong, especially in high-tech capital goods. Housing and construction remain vigorous. Virtually all speakers describe domestic demand as surprisingly strong. Well above trend.

**Minority views**: Some note potential for slowing from inventory correction and Asia drag, but the current reading is unambiguously strong.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 20 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BOEHNE: "narrowing of spreads, strong competition for lending that has led to an easing in credit terms... narrow risk spreads between Treasuries and corporate bonds including junk bonds."
- MR. STERN: "Credit seems to be readily available... many implicit signs such as the number of mergers and so forth that suggest there is not much monetary restraint."
- MS. MINEHAN: "In my view monetary policy is accommodative right now."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are widely described as very accommodative. Credit is freely available, spreads are narrow, lending standards are easing, money growth is rapid, and asset prices across equities and real estate are elevated. This is clearly loose territory.

**Minority views**: Broaddus notes real interest rates are "relatively high," but even he concludes policy may be "still inflationary." Consensus is clearly loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I conclude that I would like to stay where we are... but I believe that maintaining asymmetry toward tightening is essential."
- MR. MEYER: "I would support a tightening today if the consensus were there."
- MR. JORDAN: "we need to do something now or we will find that the toothpaste is already out of the tube."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The committee holds rates but with a strong tightening bias. About 6 participants preferred immediate tightening; the rest agreed to wait primarily due to Asia and uncertainty about productivity, while acknowledging tightening would likely be needed soon. No one advocated easing.

**Minority views**: Hawks (Jordan, Poole, Minehan, Broaddus) push the upper band. Doves (Rivlin, McTeer, Gramlich) anchor the lower band, though even they accept tightening bias.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "if it were my choice, I would still prefer to make a small move today."
- MR. GUYNN: "I feel that we should tighten today based on domestic considerations."
- MS. RIVLIN: "I do not share the view of Presidents Poole and Minehan that there is much of a risk of waiting too long."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: No formal dissents, but roughly a third of participants expressed a clear preference for immediate tightening. The disagreement centers on timing (now vs. soon) rather than direction. Several speakers explicitly stated they were deferring to the Chairman's judgment rather than fully agreeing.

**Minority views**: The split is roughly 6 for immediate action vs. 11 for waiting, but several of those 11 expressed significant reluctance.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BOEHNE: Devoted extensive remarks to "excesses in the financial sector" -- spreads, subprime lending, P/E ratios, REITs, merger premiums.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of stock market bubble question: "I have been giving a lot of thought to the question of whether we are experiencing a stock market bubble."
- MR. JORDAN: "lending is being driven by asset assessments and not at all by cash flows."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability and asset price concerns receive significant attention from most participants. Stock market valuation, credit conditions, real estate speculation, and the broader question of financial excess are discussed at length by Boehne, Broaddus, Jordan, Greenspan, Meyer, Gramlich, Minehan, and others. It is a prominent topic but shares attention with labor markets and Asia.

**Minority views**: Consensus on the importance of the topic; some disagreement on severity.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 63 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. FERGUSON: "I believe it probably will be appropriate to move before the summer is out."
- MR. MOSKOW: "I think the balance of risks strongly suggest that we will have to tighten policy this year, maybe not today but relatively soon."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "it might get harder to make changes later in the year. We should be mindful of that and perhaps think hard about it at our next meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward tightening. The asymmetric directive points toward rate increases. Multiple participants explicitly suggest tightening at the next meeting or within months. The only question is when, not whether.

**Minority views**: Rivlin expects Asia to make tightening unnecessary. McTeer is more uncertain about the need. Most others signal tightening is coming.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. KELLEY: "We just cannot know yet how all this is going to develop and net out."
- MR. POOLE: "I do not have a conviction that however I vote my decision will turn out to be right after the fact."
- MR. STERN: "there is, at least in my mind, so much uncertainty that I conclude we should not do very much."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Elevated uncertainty is a major theme. Participants are uncertain about productivity trends (structural vs. cyclical), the severity and spillover of the Asian crisis, the sustainability of the expansion, the NAIRU, and stock market valuation. Multiple speakers explicitly cite uncertainty as a reason to wait. This is genuinely elevated uncertainty, not mere rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: Some (Jordan, Meyer) express more conviction about the need to act despite uncertainty.

---

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. MOSKOW: "all the business people I talk to in the goods producing sector continue to emphasize that they cannot raise prices because of fierce competition."
- MR. MCTEER: PPI "zero or negative" in 13 of 16 months.
Goods prices are clearly subdued/declining due to import competition, tech deflation, and low commodity prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: 55 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. MOSKOW: "increases in consumer prices are being driven by the prices of services."
- Multiple speakers note rising rents, health care cost increases.
Services inflation is modestly above neutral with some acceleration.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. BROADDUS: "rents are rising and... he actually has seen some acceleration in rent increases."
- MS. MINEHAN: "Rents are up everywhere."
- MR. HOENIG: "median increases in housing prices ranging from 8 percent in Denver... to as high as 15 percent."
Housing/rent prices are clearly rising above normal.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 45 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. POOLE: "solid, perhaps even rock solid, expectations in the marketplace of continuing low inflation."
- MR. PARRY: "lower oil prices, and diminished inflation expectations."
Expectations are anchored at or slightly below target. But Poole warns they could "come unstuck."

**CREDIT_STRESS**: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
- MR. BOEHNE: "strong competition for lending that has led to an easing in credit terms... 125 percent loan-to-value ratios for home equity lending."
- MR. JORDAN: "lending is being driven by asset assessments and not at all by cash flows."
Credit conditions are very easy, spreads compressed, standards easing. Low stress = underpriced risk.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: 20 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I conclude that the stock market is significantly overvalued."
- MS. RIVLIN: "the stock market, which clearly is too high."
- MR. GRAMLICH: equity premium still positive but reduced.
Concern is about overvaluation/bubble, not crash. Score toward the low end (bubble/irrational exuberance direction).

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A
No substantive discussion of banking system stress or impairment by 2+ participants.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A
No discussion of Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A
Dollar mentioned as a factor keeping inflation down, but not discussed as a concern by 2+ participants in its own right.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 72 ±8 [High confidence]
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive analysis showing productivity acceleration to 3.2% in nonfinancial corporate sector, "very significant acceleration in productivity."
- MR. PARRY: "favorable aggregate supply conditions."
- MS. RIVLIN: "I believe that there has been a productivity upshift."
Most participants acknowledge significant productivity gains, though some (Meyer, Minehan) are more skeptical about the magnitude or permanence.
