# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-11-15 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the November 15, 2000 FOMC meeting. It includes a full go-around with individual participant statements (Hoenig, Guynn, Parry, Moskow, Stern, McTeer, Broaddus, Minehan, Santomero, McDonough, Poole, Gramlich, Ferguson, Kelley, Meyer, Greenspan) and a policy discussion. Approximately 16+ participants speak.

### Era Context
November 2000: The economy has been growing above potential for several years, driven by the tech/productivity boom of the late 1990s. The Fed raised rates six times from June 1999 to May 2000, bringing the fed funds rate to 6.5%. Oil prices are elevated. The dot-com bubble has deflated significantly. The economy is showing clear signs of deceleration but from very high levels. Core inflation is modestly rising. This is a classic late-cycle inflection point.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfortable levels and rising. Nearly every speaker references a pickup in core CPI and core PCE of about 0.5 percentage point. Greenspan: "the core rate of inflation is rising is in my view unambiguous." Broaddus: "core measures of inflation have risen 1/2 percentage point." Santomero: "core inflation is accelerating." Moskow: "higher inflation is already more than a risk." However, the level is not alarming -- Hoenig calls the underlying rate "relatively modest," and Greenspan notes unit labor costs in the corporate sector are "slightly negative." Long-term inflation expectations have not moved. The center of gravity: inflation is perceived as modestly above comfort zone and trending up, but not at crisis levels. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat" to "moderately" elevated = ~58-62. Score: 60.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says rising core inflation, tight labor markets, energy pass-through risk. Score of 60 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy has been running well above potential for years but is now decelerating toward (or slightly below) potential. Meyer: "below-trend growth when output is above potential, as I believe is the case, is desirable." Greenspan's statement says the economy "could expand for a time at a pace below the productivity-enhanced rate of growth of its potential." Broaddus: "aggregate hours worked changed little... aggregate demand may be growing somewhat more slowly than potential output." Santomero: GDP growth at 2.7% "is below estimates of potential." Multiple speakers note the economy is still at "high levels" even as growth slows. The consensus is that the output level is still above potential (due to 4+ years of above-trend growth), but growth is now at or slightly below potential, beginning to close the gap. Direction: still modestly above potential. Score: 53-55.

Bright-line rule check: Meyer explicitly says "output is above potential." Most speakers describe the economy as at "high levels" with slowing growth. Score above 50 is correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Virtually every speaker describes labor markets as "very tight" or "still very tight." Unemployment at 3.9-4.0%, well below NAIRU estimates. Moskow's Iowa anecdote (260 laid-off workers all rehired same day), Minehan's Vermont ski areas importing workers from Jamaica/Australia, Broaddus's hospitals delaying surgeries due to nurse shortages. Meyer: "unemployment rate today is at its cyclical low and significantly below the staff's estimate of the short-run NAIRU." Near-universal assessment of exceptional tightness. Direction: well above midpoint. This is one of the tightest labor markets in a generation. Anchor: 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score: 70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is moderating from very strong levels (Q3 PCE grew 4%+ but slowing in Q4). Retailers nervous about holiday season. Auto sales softening. Business investment decelerating significantly -- Greenspan notes high-tech capex growth falling from 50% to 20%, fiber optics oversupply, profit margins eroding. Residential construction clearly cooling. Multiple speakers note the slowdown is from very high levels. Hoenig: "economy to grow near its sustainable rate." The net position is still near trend but decelerating. Direction: near trend, perhaps slightly below as growth decelerates. Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is prominently discussed. Hoenig: "financial conditions have tightened... higher interest rate spreads... tighter lending terms and standards, lower equity prices." Guynn: bank examiners see "measurable slowdown in loan demand... further tightening in credit standards... loan pipeline is almost dry." Parry: "District banks have tightened credit standards." Greenspan: "quite remarkable tightening not only of the junk bond markets but of all the investment-grade markets, and very particularly the banking markets." Ferguson: "rising borrowing costs for low-rated borrowers... widening of credit spreads... declining stock market... extra tightening." Gramlich: "sharp rise in borrowing costs for low-rated borrowers." This is clearly above neutral -- conditions have tightened meaningfully. But Greenspan notes "this is not 1998, when for a few days it was impossible to get a bid." Direction: meaningfully tight. Score: 63.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee unanimously votes to hold rates unchanged with an inflation-tilted risk statement. Most participants (Greenspan, McDonough, Parry, Kelley, Stern, Moskow, Broaddus, Meyer, Santomero, Minehan, Guynn, Gramlich reluctantly) favor maintaining the inflation risk tilt. A minority (Poole, Hoenig, McTeer, and partially Ferguson/Gramlich sympathize) prefer a balanced risk statement. NO ONE advocates an actual rate increase. Several speakers (McTeer, Guynn, Poole) explicitly anticipate that the next move will be an easing. Greenspan's proposed language acknowledges economic weakness but maintains inflation tilt. This is a "hold with hawkish tilt" but clearly transitioning -- the forward lean is more data-dependent than aggressive. Score: 55. This is lean-hawkish: hold steady with a tightening bias in the statement, but the Committee is clearly moving toward recognizing balance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents on the vote, but significant disagreement about the risk statement. At least 3-4 participants (Poole, Hoenig, McTeer) explicitly prefer balanced risks; Gramlich says he has "four friends" who share that view and calls it a "close call." Ferguson is sympathetic but supports the Chairman. The policy discussion is extended with genuine back-and-forth. This exceeds a typical one-dissent meeting but does not reach the level of sharp division. Score: 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are a major topic. Nearly every participant discusses credit tightening, equity market declines, bond spreads, and banking conditions at length. McDonough devotes most of his remarks to international financial risks (Japan, Argentina, Southeast Asia, Europe). Ferguson extensively discusses financing gaps and credit conditions. Greenspan discusses fiber optics oversupply and profit squeeze at length. Financial stability/conditions is a prominent topic rivaling the core economic assessment. Score: 60.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is complicated. The statement maintains inflation tilt (hawkish), but the new language acknowledging economic softening is a clear signal of transition. Multiple speakers anticipate the next move is an easing. McTeer: "my guess is that our next move will be an easing move." Guynn: "our next key policy decision may be about when to ease or when to signal a more balanced view." The forward signal is mildly hawkish in its explicit language but the subtext is moving toward neutral/dovish. Score: 55 -- a mild tightening bias that is clearly fading.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants emphasize elevated uncertainty. Gramlich: "considerable uncertainty about the underlying risks... How do we stay ahead of the curve when we can't even see its basic direction?" Ferguson: "the future is as hazy as it has been in the period I've been here." Guynn: "considerable uncertainty about that." Minehan: "founded more than ever on a great deal of uncertainty." Greenspan: NAIRU debate is unresolved. Meyer: balance of risks itself is the key question. Many speakers note risks on both sides are large. This is clearly elevated uncertainty, not extreme but above normal. Score: 55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Divergence is explained by the lag -- growth has slowed to near/below potential but the labor market hasn't yet adjusted (unemployment is a lagging indicator). Multiple speakers explicitly note this.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Aligned. Both reflect a mildly hawkish posture that is transitioning toward balance.

### Speaker Count by Policy View Direction
- Favor maintaining inflation tilt (hawkish lean): Greenspan, McDonough, Parry, Kelley, Stern, Moskow, Broaddus, Meyer, Santomero, Minehan, Guynn, Gramlich (reluctant) = ~12
- Favor balanced risks: Poole, Hoenig, McTeer = 3
- Sympathetic to balanced but vote with Chairman: Ferguson, Gramlich = 2

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "That the core rate of inflation is rising is in my view unambiguous, no matter what price series we deal with."
- BROADDUS: "core measures of inflation have risen 1/2 percentage point... the ECI was up 4.6 percent over the last four quarters. I believe that's the biggest four-quarter increase in ten years."
- MOSKOW: "higher inflation is already more than a risk; as the Greenbook notes, both core CPI and core PCE inflation are running 1/2 point above their lows for the cycle."
- HOENIG (counterpoint): "the underlying inflation rate remains relatively modest in my opinion."

Center of gravity: Participants see core inflation as modestly rising above comfort levels, with energy costs adding further pressure. The pickup is clear but not alarming, and long-term expectations remain anchored.

Minority views: Hoenig and Greenspan note the underlying rate is still relatively modest; McTeer notes pipeline inflation has cooled. But the dominant view is that inflation is trending in the wrong direction.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MEYER: "below-trend growth when output is above potential, as I believe is the case, is desirable. That's exactly what we need to mitigate the risks of rising core inflation."
- GREENSPAN's statement: "the economy could expand for a time at a pace below the productivity-enhanced rate of growth of its potential to produce."
- SANTOMERO: "Growth definitely has slowed in the third quarter--to a reported 2.7 percent pace, which is below estimates of potential... this slowdown in economic growth is from a high level."

Center of gravity: The economy has been running above potential for years and is now decelerating. Output level is still modestly above potential but the gap is narrowing as growth has dropped below potential growth rates.

Minority views: Some speakers (McTeer, Broaddus) emphasize growing downside risks to the real economy more strongly, while Meyer/Kelley emphasize the economy is still robust.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MOSKOW: "Circuit City... announced the layoff of 260 workers. By the end of the day... their human resources people had found new jobs at other nearby firms for all 260 workers." Unemployment in Iowa at 2.3%.
- MINEHAN: "Ski areas in Vermont are importing seasonal workers from unlikely places like Jamaica, Australia, and New Zealand."
- BROADDUS: "Some retailers have actually had to cut back on the hours they are open because they can't find people to staff their stores."
- MEYER: "unemployment rate today is at its cyclical low and significantly below the staff's estimate of the short-run NAIRU."

Center of gravity: Near-universal assessment that labor markets are exceptionally tight, at or near the tightest in decades. Unemployment at 3.9%, well below estimated NAIRU. Severe worker shortages across all Districts and sectors.

Minority views: A few speakers (Hoenig, Guynn) note the "slightest" beginning of relief in some labor markets, but this is barely perceptible.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "high-tech area... growth is decelerating from 50 percent to 20 percent... we are indeed seeing some softening in consumer expenditures."
- KELLEY: "consumer spending in the weaker third quarter was considerably stronger than in the more robust second quarter... Consumer confidence remains very high."
- FERGUSON: "investment spending is on a bit more of a knife-edge now than it has been for some time... consumption is also on a trajectory for slower growth."
- SANTOMERO: "Business fixed investment... grew at half the second-quarter pace."

Center of gravity: Consumption still relatively solid but moderating from very strong levels. Business investment decelerating notably, especially in tech. Residential construction clearly slowing. Net position is near trend with downward momentum.

Minority views: Kelley emphasizes continued underlying strength; McTeer and Minehan emphasize growing weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "quite remarkable tightening not only of the junk bond markets but of all the investment-grade markets, and very particularly the banking markets. We are getting some serious restraint."
- GUYNN: "bank examiners... see a measurable slowdown in loan demand across most types of business loans and a further tightening in credit standards... the loan pipeline is almost dry beyond 60 days."
- HOENIG: "financial conditions have tightened... higher interest rate spreads... tighter lending terms and standards, lower equity prices."
- GREENSPAN (counterpoint): "this is not 1998, when for a few days it was impossible to get a bid on an investment-grade bond."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions have tightened meaningfully since the last meeting -- wider credit spreads, tighter bank lending standards, equity market decline, reduced credit availability. Clearly restrictive but not crisis-level.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: Proposes holding rates unchanged with inflation-tilted risk statement but adds new language acknowledging economic weakness.
- MEYER: "I would prefer that we be very cautious about removing an assessment that the risks are weighted mainly toward higher inflation."
- POOLE (minority): "I would prefer a balanced risks statement... I would not like to leave the market with the message that we don't see any reasonable prospect of a policy easing."
- MCTEER (minority): "I would strongly prefer a statement of balanced risks."

Center of gravity: The majority favors holding rates and maintaining the inflation tilt, but the Committee is clearly transitioning. No one advocates a rate increase. The added language acknowledging weakness is a significant dovish shift within the hawkish framework. Net: lean hawkish but fading.

Minority views: 3-5 participants prefer a balanced risk statement, suggesting the next move will be an easing.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GRAMLICH: "I have four friends in this argument--Bill (Poole), Tom (Hoenig), Roger (Ferguson), and Bob (McTeer)--and I would join that list, though supporting your statement."
- POOLE: "I would prefer a balanced risks statement."
- HOENIG: "I too would prefer a balanced risks statement."
- MCTEER: "I would strongly prefer a statement of balanced risks."

Center of gravity: No formal dissents in the vote, but a significant minority (3 explicit, 2 sympathetic) openly prefer a different risk assessment. The disagreement is substantive but expressed collegially. The Chairman's compromise language helps unify the Committee.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Nearly every participant discusses credit tightening, equity declines, and banking conditions at meaningful length.
- MCDONOUGH: Devotes most of his extensive remarks to international financial risks -- Japan, Argentina, Southeast Asia, Europe.
- FERGUSON: Extended discussion of financing gaps, credit conditions, corporate balance sheets.
- MINEHAN: "financial market volatility... larger [banks] are seeing some deterioration in syndicated and other credits."
- GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of fiber optics oversupply, profit margins, credit tightening.

Center of gravity: Financial stability/conditions is a prominent topic discussed by most participants. It rivals the standard economic assessment in emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN's proposed statement: maintains "risks continue to be weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate heightened inflation pressures" but adds "softening in business and household demand... suggest that the economy could expand for a time at a pace below... potential."
- GUYNN: "Several of us have suggested at recent meetings that our next key policy decision may be about when to ease."
- MCTEER: "my guess is that our next move will be an easing move."
- KELLEY: "The Greenbook and at least some of us here see a further tightening as the most likely next move."

Center of gravity: The official forward signal retains a mild tightening bias, but the new language is clearly softening. The Committee is positioning to shift toward neutral/easing in coming meetings. Net forward tone is mildly hawkish but transitioning.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GRAMLICH: "considerable uncertainty about the underlying risks... How do we stay ahead of the curve when we can't even see its basic direction?"
- FERGUSON: "the future is as hazy as it has been in the period I've been here."
- GUYNN: "considerable uncertainty about that."
- MINEHAN: "founded more than ever on a great deal of uncertainty."
- GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of NAIRU uncertainty and data interpretation challenges.

Center of gravity: Multiple participants explicitly emphasize elevated uncertainty about both the growth and inflation outlook. The economy is at an inflection point and participants are unsure of direction.
