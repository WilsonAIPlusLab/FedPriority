# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-07-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Individual speakers identified by name in a go-around format followed by a long-term inflation goals discussion and a policy go-around. This is a two-day Humphrey-Hawkins meeting (July 2-3, 1996).

**Era context**: Mid-1996, mid-expansion. Unemployment around 5.5%, GDP growth accelerating to ~4% in Q2, CPI inflation around 3% but core PCE around 2%. The economy had been eased in January 1996 (two 25bp cuts in late 1995/early 1996). Bond yields had risen. The "new paradigm" debate about whether the NAIRU had shifted lower was underway. This is a period of strong growth with surprisingly contained inflation.

**Speakers in economic go-around**: Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Boehne, Hoenig, McTeer, Guynn, Melzer, Stern, McDonough, Jordan, Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, Yellen, Meyer, Rivlin (18 speakers). Then a long-term inflation goals discussion. Then a policy go-around with many of the same speakers.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: The discussion is nuanced. CPI is around 3%, but core PCE is about 2%. Multiple participants note that inflation is surprisingly well-contained despite tight labor markets. Yellen: "core inflation in the CPI and PPI remains well contained." Phillips: "the general inflation trend has been down." Kelley: "Most of our inflation measures--the core CPI, the core PPI, and the GDP deflator--are flat on a rate-of-change basis or else the rate of change is beginning to drift down." Meyer: "if there is any surprise about inflation, it is how well contained it is rather than how high it is." Greenspan: "The rate of inflation still appears to be falling." However, several note upside RISKS: Broaddus, Parry, Melzer see risks of inflation rising. ECI wage data was "very disturbing" (Greenspan). Direction: Participants see inflation as near or modestly below the 3% CPI level, well-contained, with risks tilted to the upside but actual readings benign. Using CPI at ~3% as the reference, and PCE at ~2%, and considering the pre-2% target era, this is "at or slightly above comfortable levels but surprisingly contained." Score should be around 48-53. The center of gravity leans toward "contained but with upside risk." I'll score 50 -- balanced, near target for the era (no formal 2% target yet; 3% CPI was considered acceptable).

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is contained but near 3% CPI. In mid-1990s context, 3% CPI was not alarming but slightly above comfort. Score ~50 fits.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Strong consensus that GDP growth in Q2 was 4%+, well above trend. But most expect moderation to trend in H2. Parry: "it would not be enough to prevent the economy from stretching beyond the full utilization of its resources." Hoenig: "growth to remain slightly above potential." Broaddus: "upside risks in the projection are considerably more pronounced." Greenbook expects return to trend. Meyer: "growth for the year of about 2.7 percent" on Q4/Q4 basis. Kelley: four-quarter GDP growth "a little over 2.5 percent." The economy is viewed as near potential, perhaps slightly above, with strong momentum. Unemployment at 5.5% is near or slightly below estimated NAIRU of 5.75%. Multiple participants see the economy as roughly at capacity. Direction: Slightly above potential. Score ~53.

Bright-line check: Several say "above trend" or "above potential" for current quarter, but many expect return to trend. Net: slightly above. Score >50 required. 53 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very strong consensus that labor markets are tight. Nearly every district president reports tight labor markets. Broaddus: "labor markets in the District have tightened further recently... increasing reports of recruitment difficulties." Parry: "labor markets are tight enough to place substantial pressures on wages" (in some states). Moskow: "Labor markets remain tight throughout the District" but "very few reports of mounting wage pressures." Melzer: "District labor market appears tight." Stern: "labor is scarce in the District." Minehan: high demand for IT workers. Hoenig: "strong indications of tight labor markets." However, wage acceleration is NOT broad-based. Yellen: "labor markets do seem tight, although they are no tighter now than they have been for most of the last two years." The puzzle is tight markets without broad wage acceleration. Direction: Tight labor markets, around or slightly above NAIRU. Score ~58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Strong reports. Auto sales robust (15.2M units), housing strong, durable goods strong in first half. Moskow: "other durable goods producers in our District generally have seen somewhat stronger-than-expected demand." Business fixed investment has been growing at double-digit rates (Phillips). But many expect moderation in H2. Boehne: "Growth in business fixed investment is already moderating." Phillips: "consumer spending has been a consistent source of strength." Lindsey gives an extensive analysis of consumer debt supporting continued but fragile consumption. Direction: Above trend in first half, expected to moderate. Current readings strong. Score ~58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are discussed as generally accommodative/loose. Minehan: insurance company contact says "almost never seen such an availability of capital... almost any project can get financed... IPO market in particular has gotten frothy." Phillips: "The cost of equity capital is really quite favorable. The term structure of interest rates is fairly healthy." Stern (policy go-around): "financial conditions seem to be what I would describe as generally permissive. Credit is readily available on attractive terms." Lindsey discusses loose credit conditions extensively -- 72-month auto loans, aggressive mortgage lending, rising delinquencies. Long-term rates have risen somewhat, which is having some effect on housing. Direction: Conditions are loose/accommodative. Score ~28.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says loose. Score below 50. 28 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the key policy dimension. The vote was unanimous for Alternative B (no change, asymmetric toward tightening). However, several participants WANTED to tighten:
- Broaddus: wanted 50bp increase, or at minimum 25bp
- Parry: wanted 50bp, would accept 25bp with asymmetry
- Stern: preferred 25bp increase now
- Melzer: wanted 50bp increase plus 50bp discount rate increase
- Minehan: could support a move now but accepts waiting until August

Those supporting hold with asymmetry (the majority): Greenspan, McDonough, Meyer, Phillips, Boehne, Rivlin, Kelley, Jordan, Guynn, Moskow, McTeer, Yellen, Lindsey.

Almost everyone expects a tightening will be needed in coming months. Greenspan: "in all likelihood... we probably will do so at the August meeting or later." The lean is clearly hawkish -- no one contemplates easing. The question is whether to tighten now or wait. Center of gravity: hold but lean to tighten. This is solidly in the 55-62 range. Several wanted immediate action. Score ~58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote was unanimous for hold + asymmetry. But the discussion reveals a significant minority (Broaddus, Parry, Melzer, Stern, Minehan) who wanted to tighten immediately. That's roughly 5 of ~18 participants preferring a different action. On the long-term inflation discussion, there was a lively debate about goals but ultimately broad convergence around 2% as an interim target. The policy disagreement is about timing, not fundamental direction. Score ~35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Lindsey gives an extensive speech on consumer debt, delinquencies, credit quality, and financial fragility -- probably the most detailed financial stability discussion of this meeting. Jordan mentions financial stresses from declining credit quality. Minehan's insurance contact mentions frothy IPO market. Phillips discusses equity capital costs. Hoenig asks about artificiality in financial markets. But for most participants, financial stability is not a central focus. It gets meaningful attention from 3-4 participants. Score ~25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric directive clearly signals a tightening bias. Greenspan expects a move at the August meeting. Multiple participants say they expect to tighten in coming months. McDonough: "reasonable likelihood that we will decide at the next meeting that we have enough information to warrant a tightening move." The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- no commitment but clear lean. Score ~60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: This is a meeting defined by uncertainty. The entire Greenspan presentation is about not understanding why inflation hasn't risen despite tight labor markets. The "new paradigm" debate is fundamentally about uncertainty in the models. Greenspan: "The current period reminds me of our struggle several years ago to cope with the breakdown of M2." Kelley: "watershed period... too early to tell just which way the water is going to fall." Rivlin: "extremely hard for us to explain the current set of facts to ourselves with the models that all of us have been using." Boehne: "that higher degree of uncertainty ought to make us more cautious." Yellen: "I consider this a period of great uncertainty." McDonough: "We are in a period of very considerable uncertainty." This is genuinely elevated uncertainty. Score ~55.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Both above 50, labor slightly higher. This makes sense -- labor markets tighter than overall output gap because unemployment is at/near NAIRU even though GDP growth is near trend on average.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Aligned, both lean hawkish. Makes sense -- hold now but expect to tighten.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (28, loose) with CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (58, strong): Coherent -- loose conditions supporting strong spending.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The rate of inflation still appears to be falling... The more analytically accurate core PCE chain-weighted price index is increasing now at a rate of about 2 percent."
- MR. KELLEY: "Most of our inflation measures--the core CPI, the core PPI, and the GDP deflator--are flat on a rate-of-change basis or else the rate of change is beginning to drift down."
- MR. PARRY: "we face the substantial risk that underlying inflation, abstracting from oil prices and the like, is set on a gradual upward trend."
- MS. YELLEN: "core inflation in the CPI and PPI remains well contained."

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is contained at roughly 3% CPI / 2% PCE, which in the mid-1990s context was at or near acceptable levels. The majority sees inflation as well-behaved currently, but a significant minority sees upside risks materializing soon.

Minority views: Broaddus, Parry, Melzer see inflation as a growing threat, pushing the uncertainty band upward.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "I would expect growth to remain slightly above potential."
- MR. PARRY: "it would not be enough to prevent the economy from stretching beyond the full utilization of its resources."
- MR. MEYER: "growth for the year of about 2.7 percent on a fourth-quarter to fourth-quarter basis."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "We are an economy that at the moment does not resemble most of our textbook models. The unemployment rate is low and has remained low for quite a while."

Center-of-gravity: Economy is operating near or slightly above potential, with Q2 GDP growth of ~4% well above trend but expected to moderate. Unemployment at 5.5% is near/slightly below estimated NAIRU.

Minority views: Some (Guynn, Jordan) are less convinced economy is above potential; Parry and Melzer see it as clearly above.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "labor markets in the District have tightened further recently. We hear increasing reports of recruitment difficulties not only for skilled workers but for less skilled workers as well."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Labor markets remain tight throughout the District" with unemployment "about a percentage point below the national average."
- MR. MELZER: "The District labor market appears tight. Some businesses are coping with a shortage of entry-level workers."
- MS. YELLEN: "labor markets do seem tight, although they are no tighter now than they have been for most of the last two years."

Center-of-gravity: Universally described as tight, with worker shortages reported across districts. Unemployment at 5.5% is near or slightly below NAIRU. But broad-based wage acceleration is strikingly absent, which is the central puzzle of this meeting.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MOSKOW: "other durable goods producers in our District generally have seen somewhat stronger-than-expected demand... auto industry... light vehicle sales in June were coming in near the average pace of 15.2 million units."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "business fixed investment would slow in 1996, but so far we have clocked in a double-digit rate of increase."
- MR. BROADDUS: "the surprising strength of job growth, housing activity, and consumer spending... highest ratio of household net worth to disposable income in a couple of decades."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Growth in business fixed investment is already moderating."

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and business investment have been above trend, supported by strong auto sales, housing, and capital spending. Most expect moderation in H2 but current readings are robust.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. MINEHAN: insurance company contact "has almost never seen such an availability of capital. From venture capital, mezzanine financing, to commercial loans and mortgage lending, according to him almost any project can get financed... IPO market in particular has gotten frothy."
- MR. STERN: "financial conditions seem to be what I would describe as generally permissive. Credit is readily available on attractive terms."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The cost of equity capital is really quite favorable. The term structure of interest rates is fairly healthy."
- MR. LINDSEY: extensive discussion of ever-loosening credit standards -- 72-month auto loans, high LTV mortgages, rising delinquencies.

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Credit is freely available, equity markets are strong, capital abundant. Long-term rates have risen but not enough to significantly tighten conditions.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would hope that this Committee... would nonetheless accept an asymmetric bias toward tightening... in all likelihood, if the Committee does not move at this meeting or during the intermeeting period, we probably will do so at the August meeting or later."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think the case for a tightening of policy today is a strong one. I personally believe that a solid case can be made for an increase of 50 basis points."
- MR. PARRY: "these considerations support a 50 basis point increase in the federal funds rate."
- MR. MEYER: "I am very comfortable with your recommendation for no change in policy at this meeting and an asymmetric policy directive."

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous hold with asymmetric tightening bias. A significant minority (Broaddus, Parry, Melzer, Stern, possibly Minehan) preferred immediate tightening. The majority is willing to wait but acknowledges likely tightening ahead.

Minority views: Broaddus and Melzer wanted 50bp; Parry wanted 50bp but would accept 25bp; Stern wanted 25bp. On the other side, Jordan, Rivlin, and Guynn were least inclined toward near-term tightening.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Vote was unanimous for Alternative B with asymmetric tilt.
- But ~5 participants (Broaddus, Parry, Melzer, Stern, Minehan) expressed clear preference for immediate tightening.
- Extended debate on long-term inflation goals showed varying views but eventual convergence.
- MR. JORDAN expressed discomfort with asymmetry and was skeptical a move would be warranted in August.

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the hold decision and direction of risk, but meaningful differences on timing and urgency. No formal dissents. The disagreement is about pace, not direction.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LINDSEY: extensive remarks on consumer debt, delinquencies, credit quality, auto loan and mortgage lending standards -- probably 2+ pages of text devoted to financial fragility.
- MR. JORDAN: "The frequency with which companies talk about financial stresses is interesting... bankers and nonbankers alike who are worried about declining credit quality."
- MS. MINEHAN: "IPO market in particular has gotten frothy."
- MR. HOENIG: question about "artificiality" in financial markets.

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability gets meaningful attention from 3-4 participants (primarily Lindsey), but it is not a central concern for the majority. The discussion is more about credit quality as a macroeconomic variable than systemic risk.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "in all likelihood... we probably will do so at the August meeting or later."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "there is a reasonable likelihood that we will decide at the next meeting that we have enough information to warrant a tightening move."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I assume will be a necessary move at the August meeting."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "a tightening of policy is going to be needed in the next several months."

Center-of-gravity: Clear forward lean toward tightening, with most participants expecting action in coming months. The signal is data-dependent but directionally hawkish.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. KELLEY: "we are in that period, although I think it is too early to tell just which way the water is going to fall."
- MS. RIVLIN: "extremely hard for us to explain the current set of facts to ourselves with the models that all of us have been using."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "We are in a period of very considerable uncertainty."
- MS. YELLEN: "I consider this a period of great uncertainty."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: entire analysis questions whether the standard Phillips curve model is operative.

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty, driven primarily by the puzzle of tight labor markets without inflation acceleration. Multiple participants explicitly use the word "uncertainty" and describe the difficulty of interpreting current conditions through standard frameworks.

### OPTIONAL DIMENSIONS

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Several participants discuss commodity prices falling, industrial commodity prices declining. Greenspan: "industrial commodity prices falling." Kelley: "Commodity prices are now falling almost across the board." Phillips: "crude materials excluding food and energy are down 12-1/2 percent." Score: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]. Goods prices are declining or contained.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not discussed as a separate category by 2+ participants. N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing prices are rising in several districts (Stern: "home prices are rising significantly"; Minehan: Boston housing market booming). But this is discussed more as an economic activity indicator than as an inflation sub-component. N/A.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Yellen: "our most recent readings on long-term inflationary expectations also are quite positive, suggesting the absence of any deterioration there." Stern: "bond market participants clearly are not convinced at this point that inflation is dead... still a significant inflation premium in long-term interest rates." Melzer: "The slowing in inflation that we forecast will not occur if the current inflation gets embedded in expectations, which we see as a growing risk." Score: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]. Expectations are roughly anchored but with some concern about potential drift.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Lindsey provides extensive data on rising delinquencies. Jordan reports companies mentioning financial stresses, declining credit quality. FHA delinquencies at record highs. But banks are well-reserved. Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]. Below-normal stress in aggregate but some emerging pockets of concern.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Minehan mentions "frothy" IPO market. Stern notes wealth effects from stock price run-up. Hoenig asks about "artificiality" in markets. Discussion leans toward bubble/overvaluation concern rather than crash concern. Score: 30 +/-10 [Low confidence]. More concern about excessive valuations than about crash.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Lindsey: "I do not believe that these issues are of the type or magnitude that will affect the integrity of the banking system. The banks are well reserved." Jordan: bankers worried about declining credit quality. McTeer: "condition of the ag banks is pretty good." Score: N/A (not prominent enough as a standalone topic).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Yellen mentions "a somewhat stronger dollar" affecting exports. Not discussed prominently. N/A.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Extensively discussed. Jordan gives a long speech about mismeasured productivity gains in services vs. manufacturing. Greenspan discusses productivity in policy context. Yellen's inflation goals discussion centers on productivity growth rates. Jordan: "The anecdotal reports at the micro level tell us that productivity is booming, especially in manufacturing where productivity gains of 4 or 5 percent or sometimes even 6 or 7 percent are mentioned." But aggregate productivity stuck at ~1%. Score: 52 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]. Aggregate productivity is at historical trend, but participants believe actual productivity is higher than measured, especially in manufacturing.
