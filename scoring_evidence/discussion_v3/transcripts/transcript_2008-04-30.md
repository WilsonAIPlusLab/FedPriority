## Part 1: Thinking / Reasoning

### 1. Document Type and Structure

This is a two-day transcript (April 29–30, 2008) with individual participant statements clearly identified by name (e.g., "MR. PLOSSER.", "MR. KOHN.", "MS. YELLEN.", "CHAIRMAN BERNANKE.", "VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER.", etc.). The document contains: (a) a liquidity-facility/TSLF discussion on Day 1, (b) a staff economic briefing Q&A session, (c) a full go-around (economic assessment by each participant), and (d) a policy discussion (rate decision) on Day 2. All participant-attributable statements are clearly segmented. This is a full, long transcript with rich evidence.

### 2. Era Context

April 29–30, 2008: Early-to-middle phase of the Global Financial Crisis. The FOMC had already cut rates 300 bp since September 2007 (200 bp in less than 60 days in early 2008). Bear Stearns had been rescued in March. Housing was in freefall. Core PCE had been above 2% every year since 2004. Headline CPI was running near 4%, oil at historically elevated levels. The economy was likely in or entering recession, while inflation was uncomfortably high — a true stagflationary challenge. Financial markets had improved slightly since the Bear Stearns episode but remained fragile. The LIBOR-OIS spread was widening again.

### 3. Directional Assessment (Pre-Score, Per Dimension)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants across the board describe headline inflation as substantially above target (4% CPI, 3.4% PCE headline, core PCE above 2% since 2004). Multiple participants (Plosser, Fisher, Hoenig, Lacker, Bullard) use language like "unacceptably high," "dangerous period," "inflation dynamic... feedback loop," "highest input cost pressures in recent memory." Even the more dovish participants (Yellen, Kohn, Mishkin, Geithner) acknowledge high headline and note upside risks. The center of gravity is clearly above target, with several participants expressing alarm. Score well above 50. Direction: above target / elevated.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly all participants describe the economy as weak, declining, or in recession. Bernanke says "I am reasonably confident that we are in a recession." Plosser: "payroll employment... declining," GDP forecast around 1.5% for 2008. Yellen: "the economy has stalled and may have fallen into a recession." Greenbook projects a nonlinear step-down. All participants see significant slack opening, unemployment rising above NAIRU into 2010. This is firmly below potential. Direction: below potential / negative output gap.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Job losses average -77K to -100K/month. Unemployment rising (above 5% and heading toward 5.5-6%). Lacker: "payroll employment... declining an average of 77,000 per month in the first quarter." Rosengren: "private payroll employment declined by approximately 100,000 jobs on average over the past three months, and the unemployment rate increased 0.3 percentage point." Lockhart: "employment growth is quite weak." Some mention isolated District strength, but the national picture is deteriorating. Direction: below full employment, loosening labor market.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is uniformly described as weak, sluggish, or declining. Fisher cites MasterCard data showing "year-over-year retail sales… 2.2 percent, the lowest he has ever seen." Warsh: "consumer spending in March was as bad as anyone… could have envisioned." Plosser: "Retail sales were also sluggish." Business investment also softening. Housing investment in freefall (starts down 60%+). Some offset from exports. Direction: below trend / weak.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is extensively discussed. LIBOR-OIS spread widening again. Mortgage securitization markets "essentially shut." Banks tightening credit standards (Senior Loan Officer survey). Jumbo spreads "still extremely wide." Credit cards tightening. HELOC market tightening. Many measures of spreads "back only to January levels" which were themselves impaired. Geithner: financial system "still very fragile... short of the capital necessary to support growth in lending." Kohn: "Baa corporate bonds, long-term muni bonds, and prime jumbo mortgages are all higher than before we did any easing." However, some improvement from Bear Stearns lows — equity markets up, some CDS spreads narrower. This is tight but not at the panic extreme of September 2008. Direction: significantly tight.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The committee votes 8-2 for a 25bp cut (Alt B) with a strong pause signal. Advocates for cutting: Kohn, Stern, Rosengren, Yellen, Lockhart, Kroszner, Mishkin, Warsh, Bernanke (and implicitly others). Advocates for holding (Alt C): Plosser, Evans, Fisher, Lacker, Hoenig, Bullard (6 participants favor no cut). The majority vote to cut but accompany it with strong pause language, removing "downside risks" language and "act in a timely manner." This represents a moderately dovish current action (cut) but with a clear pivot signal. This is not a strong cut meeting — the forward lean is toward pausing. Direction: lean dovish (majority for cut) but moving toward neutral; moderate dovish overall.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: At least 5-6 participants explicitly favor Alt C (no cut) vs. the majority favoring Alt B. Plosser, Evans, Fisher, Lacker, Hoenig, and Bullard all openly advocate pausing. This is an unusually large minority advocating the alternative, even though the vote ultimately produces 2 formal dissents (Fisher and Plosser voted against). Multiple "reluctant" B supporters (Warsh, Mishkin). The discussion shows genuine, substantive disagreement on the appropriate policy framework (how to measure real rates, whether 1970s analogy applies, whether credibility is eroding). Direction: notable to significant disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial market conditions, credit spreads, banking stress, the TSLF, TAF, PDCF, and the Bear Stearns episode dominate much of the discussion across Day 1 and throughout the go-around. Most participants devote significant time to financial conditions. This is one of the dominant themes of the meeting alongside inflation. Direction: high emphasis.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The statement (Alt B) removes "downside risks to growth," removes "act in a timely manner," adds "continue to monitor," signals a "soft pause." Bernanke explicitly says current data within expected range should not cause further easing. This is clearly signaling an end to the cutting cycle and a data-dependent pause. Multiple speakers frame this as a pivot from rapid easing to watchful waiting. This is a mild tightening/neutral signal for the forward path — easing bias removed. Direction: mildly hawkish forward signal (pause signal, removing easing bias).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Yellen notes "forecast dispersion is indicative of the unusually high degree of uncertainty." Kohn: "We are sailing in a fog in uncharted waters." Geithner: "Uncertainty around both paths... is unusually high." Multiple participants note the difficulty of measuring the stance of monetary policy given spread widening. Questions about commodity price pass-through, dollar dynamics, inflation expectations dynamics all flagged as "particularly uncertain." This is clearly elevated above normal uncertainty. Direction: high uncertainty.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 68 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Plosser: "Inflation is high, unacceptably high in my view, and has been that way for a sustained period... year-over-year CPI inflation was 4 percent in March, and year-over-year PCE inflation was 3.4 percent—well above their 2007 levels."
- Fisher: "Something persistent and pernicious, Mr. Chairman, has been occurring on the inflation front... prices paid and prices received are near 20-year highs for our firms."
- Even Kohn (dovish): "Uncomfortably high total inflation that is feeding through to some limited extent into core inflation and, by some measures, into inflation expectations, especially near-term expectations."
- Hoenig: "Inflation outlook has worsened considerably... we are entering a dangerous period in which inflation expectations are beginning to move higher."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Every single participant acknowledges inflation running clearly above target (headline ~4%, core PCE above 2%) with meaningful upside risks from commodities, dollar depreciation, and possible expectations drift; the center-of-gravity is clearly elevated but not yet characterized as extreme or accelerating into a wage-price spiral.

**Minority views**: Mishkin, Rosengren, and Geithner offer more sanguine readings — noting that core has not accelerated, labor compensation is contained, and long-run expectations remain "reasonably well anchored around 2 percent," which prevents a score above 75.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 30 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Bernanke: "I am reasonably confident that we are in a recession. We don't see these dynamic patterns of employment, sentiment, and so on without a recession being eventually called by the NBER."
- Plosser: "Real GDP growth coming in around 1.5 percent for 2008... output gap opening this year."
- Evans: "A noticeable output gap opening this year... possible that some portion of 2008 might eventually be labeled a recession."
- Rosengren: "Unemployment rate remains well above the NAIRU, even at the end of 2010."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The consensus view is that the economy is in or entering a mild-to-moderate recession with a clearly negative output gap opening and rising unemployment expected to remain elevated through 2010, placing this well below potential but not at catastrophic GFC-trough levels.

**Minority views**: Lacker is less convinced of severe spillovers from financial markets to the real economy; Evans was "pleasantly surprised" no major downside surprises occurred since March, nudging toward the higher end of the band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Rosengren: "Private payroll employment declined by approximately 100,000 jobs on average over the past three months, and the unemployment rate increased 0.3 percentage point. Labor market weakness was widespread across industries."
- Lacker: "Payroll employment has continued to decline, falling an average of 77,000 per month in the first quarter. Unemployment is rising, although it is still relatively low."
- Bernanke (summary): "Payrolls are falling, although there are some pockets of strength. Unemployment is likely to keep rising. It may remain somewhat high into 2010."
- Yellen: "The unemployment rate increases to just over 5½ percent by the end of this year."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Employment is declining at a measurable pace, unemployment is rising from "still relatively low" levels toward a peak above the NAIRU; this places labor tightness below full employment but not yet at deep recession slack levels.

**Minority views**: Hoenig notes his District continues to show "better employment growth and firm labor markets" — but this is a local observation, not a national center-of-gravity signal.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 32 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Fisher: "Year-over-year retail sales to date in April—ex-autos and ex-gas—were 2.2 percent, the lowest he [MasterCard CEO] has ever seen."
- Warsh: "Consumer spending in March was as bad as anyone who was in the business of retail or credit card spending could have envisioned."
- Plosser: "More firms have decreased their capital spending plans, with 10 percent indicating that they either delayed capital spending or postponed it indefinitely."
- Bernanke (summary): "Retail sales, sentiment, and consumer spending have generally been soft... Investment has softened somewhat, including declines in commercial real estate investment."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending and business investment are both clearly below trend — sluggish to contracting — with housing investment in free fall; export strength provides a partial offset but is not sufficient to bring the aggregate into "moderate" territory.

**Minority views**: Some noted pockets of strength in export-oriented manufacturing and, per Evans, a shopping mall developer saw a small improvement in April retail sales vs. March — but these were exceptions noted against a broadly weak picture.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 74 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Kohn: "Baa corporate bonds, which is about the median borrower rating for a corporation, long-term muni bonds, and prime jumbo mortgages are all higher than before we did any easing... nonprice terms and standards are being tightened considerably."
- Geithner: "The financial system as a whole still looks as though it is short of the capital necessary to support growth in lending to creditworthy households and borrowers... securitization markets are still essentially shut."
- Kroszner: "LIBOR–OIS spread... still high and is continuing to increase. That is the most troubling for me—that we are moving back to the extreme highs we saw when we had the end-of-the-year problem."
- Kohn (go-around): "Spreads have retraced only a small portion of the run-up since last summer... in January, we thought the markets were pretty impaired. So they are still very, very high by historical standards."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly and significantly tight across multiple markets — interbank, mortgage securitization, credit cards, HELOCs, commercial credit — with some improvement from March Bear Stearns lows but spreads and credit availability still severely constrained; not at September 2008 panic but well into "tight/restrictive" territory.

**Minority views**: Lockhart, Bullard, and Evans note that the "discovery process concerning asset-backed securities... has effectively come to a close" and markets have "improved substantially" — which adds uncertainty about the exact level but does not change the directional signal.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- The majority favors cutting 25bp (Alt B): Kohn: "I think we should be lowering interest rates 25 basis points." Yellen: "I definitely support a 25 basis point cut." Bernanke: "I'm going to recommend alternative B—25 basis points but with a stronger indication of a pause."
- Significant minority favors holding (Alt C): Plosser: "What I would like to do... is to make the case for why we should stand pat today." Fisher: "I am in favor of a pause." Lacker: "I believe that we should leave the fed funds rate alone today." Hoenig: "I do continue to hold the view that easing policy today is a mistake." Bullard: "I suggest that we put that on hold." Evans: "I, too, favor maintaining the federal funds rate at 2¼ percent today."
- Final vote includes at least 2 formal dissents (Fisher and Plosser).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The majority votes to ease 25bp but pairs this with a strong pause signal removing easing-bias language; this is a moderately dovish lean — leaning toward providing some accommodation — but substantially restrained by the inflation concerns driving 6 participants toward holding.

**Minority views**: The unusually large minority (6 out of ~16-17 participants) explicitly favoring no cut pushes the score toward the higher (less dovish) end of the moderate-dovish band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Explicit Alt C advocates: Plosser, Evans, Fisher, Lacker, Hoenig, Bullard — 6 participants openly advocate a different policy action.
- Geithner notes "the extent of the gap in this Committee in how we think about the way to measure the stance of monetary policy" — identifying a fundamental framework disagreement.
- Mishkin: "I'm actually sitting exactly on the fence between alternative B and alternative C."
- Warsh: "I can support alternative B, but I must admit I can't do it with the conviction that I would prefer."
- Formal dissents: at least 2 (Fisher and Plosser dissented).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: This meeting features genuine, substantive disagreement — 6 participants vs. majority, two formal dissents, a "fence-sitter" governor, and explicit debate about fundamental issues of measurement of policy stance, inflation dynamics, and 1970s parallels; this is clearly above normal one-dissent disagreement.

**Minority views**: Even the majority B supporters express significant reservations and reluctance, amplifying the effective disagreement beyond just the formal dissents.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 78 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- The entire Day 1 discussion is devoted to TSLF/TAF/swap-line liquidity facilities — directly about financial stability.
- Every participant in the go-around devotes substantial time to financial market conditions, credit spreads, banking stress, LIBOR-OIS, securitization markets, and Bear Stearns aftermath.
- Bernanke (summary): "Financial conditions and the housing market probably remained the most important downside risks to growth."
- Rosengren: "Several ominous trends remain in financial markets. The LIBOR–OIS spread has widened... the asset-backed commercial paper market is once again experiencing difficulties."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability is the co-dominant topic alongside inflation — virtually all participants address it at length, it drives most of the policy rationale, and an entire Day 1 session is dedicated to liquidity facility design; this approaches "dominant" territory but economic assessment also receives extensive discussion.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 60 ±8 [Moderate-High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Bernanke: "We have reached the point where further aggressive rate-cutting is not going to be productive and that we should now be signaling a willingness to sit, watch, and listen for a time."
- Bernanke on statement changes: "We removed any reference to downside risk to growth... we've added the phrase 'continue to monitor'... we have taken out the 'timely manner.'"
- Lockhart: "I am inclined to pause after this move, provided that the incoming data are not too adverse."
- Geithner: "A modest recalibration of policy with a hawkish soft pause."
- Bernanke: "Data that come in within the general, broad ranges of what we're expecting, even though they will be weak, should not cause us to ease further, given this statement."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal clearly removes the easing bias and signals a data-dependent pause; this is a mild-to-moderate hawkish forward signal relative to the recent aggressive cutting cycle, though participants preserve flexibility for downside scenarios and do not signal imminent tightening.

**Minority views**: Rosengren explicitly says "it is not at all clear to me that we should pause after this meeting" — a dovish forward lean — but he is in the minority of the minority.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 70 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen: "In looking at the latest Blue Chip forecasts... the range between the highest and lowest is among the largest on record... Such forecast dispersion is indicative of the unusually high degree of uncertainty that we are facing."
- Kohn: "We are sailing in a fog in uncharted waters, and the depth finder is on the fritz."
- Geithner: "Uncertainty around both paths [growth and inflation], though, is unusually high."
- Mishkin: "There is very high uncertainty right now."
- Plosser: "The instability of these measures of inflation expectations itself is a cause of concern... standard caveats: uncertainty about pass-through from commodity prices and oil... imprecise and which we don't really know much about."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Multiple participants explicitly flag unusually high uncertainty — about the recession outlook, commodity price pass-through, inflation expectations dynamics, dollar trajectory, and the appropriate stance of policy — well above typical hedging language.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Score 72 ±8 [Moderate confidence]

Multiple participants discuss goods price pressures: Hoenig: "For the first time in many years, we are seeing significant inflation pressure from goods prices, especially imported goods prices." Fisher cites specific goods price increases (food up, jet fuel up 70%, apparel costs up). Plosser: "Indexes of prices paid and prices received are near 20-year highs." Score reflects strong goods price pressures driven by commodity prices and dollar depreciation.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Score 48 ±10 [Low-Moderate confidence]

Hoenig notes: "recent moderation in monthly inflation numbers is coming mainly from some softness in service prices, which in my opinion, is unlikely to continue." Some pass-through mentioned but less prominent than goods. Score near neutral as services not a primary driver discussed.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Score 28 ±10 [Moderate confidence]

Multiple speakers discuss falling housing prices and weak rent dynamics. Yellen: "vacant housing units will continue to put downward pressure on rent increases." Housing is deflationary in this period. Score well below 50.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Score 62 ±10 [Moderate confidence]

Michigan 1-year expectations at 4.8% (up from 3.4% in December). Plosser: "Near-term measures of inflation expectations have risen sharply." 5yr/5yr-ahead TIPS at 2.8% (high). Some improvement since early 2008 but unstable. Long-run expectations described as "reasonably anchored" by Mishkin/Kohn but with risk of drift. Score above midpoint, reflecting genuine concern about near-term expectations drift even if long-run remains partly anchored.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Score 78 ±8 [High confidence]

Extensive discussion of credit market stress: LIBOR-OIS widening to near year-end extremes, securitization markets "essentially shut," jumbo spreads "extremely wide," credit card tightening, HELOC curtailment, ABCP market stressed again. Banks tightening standards across all categories. Score in high-stress range.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Score 38 ±12 [Low confidence]

Markets improved from Bear Stearns lows — Warsh mentions equities up. Some discussion of CDS spreads on financials still high. Participants discuss improvement without expressing extreme stress concern. Score somewhat below midpoint (some improvement, moderate residual concern rather than panic).

**BANKING_STRESS**: Score 72 ±8 [Moderate-High confidence]

National City Bank capital injection ($7B). IMF projects $945B in credit losses, staff $700-800B; only ~$60B acknowledged so far — "about one-third of the way through total losses" (Bernanke). Banks raising capital but balance sheets still impaired. LIBOR-OIS widening suggests continued bank stress. Warsh: "income statements for these financial institutions remain highly challenged."

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A

Little discussion of Treasury market dysfunction specifically. The TSLF discussion mentions GC repo rate only briefly.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Score 30 ±10 [Moderate confidence]

Multiple speakers discuss dollar weakness concern but framed as "moderate concern about further depreciation" rather than extreme strength concern. Fisher and Geithner discuss dollar dynamics extensively. Geithner: "I am very worried about this dynamic now." The dollar had actually rallied in the recent intermeeting period. Score below midpoint (concern about weakness, not strength — i.e., low score per polarity: higher = more strength concern, lower = more weakness concern).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: N/A

Not prominently discussed by 2+ participants.
