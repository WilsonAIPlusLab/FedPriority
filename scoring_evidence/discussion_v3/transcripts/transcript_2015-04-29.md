# FOMC Transcript Scoring — April 28–29, 2015

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the April 28–29, 2015 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (e.g., "MR. WILLIAMS.", "MS. MESTER.", "VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY.", "CHAIR YELLEN."). The document is fully segmented with distinct speaker turns.

The transcript covers three distinct sections:
- A Board of Governors discussion on liftoff governance/implementation (IOER, ON RRP delegation)
- Economic go-round (April 28 session) and Chair Yellen's summary
- Policy discussion / statement language debate (April 29 session)

Per rubric: score PARTICIPANT/MEMBER views only. Staff-attributable content (Bill Wascher briefing, Thomas's policy options presentation) is excluded from scoring. Chair Yellen's substantive economic summary at the end of day 1 is treated as participant input.

---

### 2. Era Context
April 2015: The U.S. economy was approximately 5.5–6 years into post-GFC recovery. Unemployment had fallen to ~5.5%, well off the crisis peak but still near (or arguably slightly above) conventional NAIRU estimates. Inflation had been persistently below the 2% target; headline PCE was near or below 0% due to oil price decline; core PCE around 1.3–1.4%. The FOMC was in pre-liftoff mode, debating when conditions would be met for the first rate increase since 2006. Q1 2015 GDP was about to be reported (nearly zero). The meeting included a financial stability segment and a special discussion of European negative rates. This is NOT an emergency meeting.

### 3. Core Dimension Directional Signals (Pre-Scoring Analysis)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Participants see inflation as well below target. Multiple participants cite core PCE at 1.3–1.4%, headline near zero/negative Q1. Williams: "continued low inflation," "economy still operating below potential." Dudley: "fears I had of inflation getting unanchored to the downside seem to have diminished" but "inflation... seems to be stabilizing." Fischer: "core PCE inflation was 1.3 percent over the 12 months through March." Kocherlakota: "inflation is far below target," "inflation does not return to FOMC's target of 2 percent until 2019." Brainard: "core PCE remains persistently soft." Yellen: "Both core and headline PCE inflation rates are running well below 2 percent." The few hawkish voices (Lacker, Bullard) acknowledge inflation is below target but argue it is firming/transitory. Center of gravity: inflation clearly well below target, but not in deflationary spiral territory. Score range: 26–40.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
Participants see the economy as below potential, though recovering. Widespread discussion of "slack remains," "resource gaps," "below potential." Williams: "economy still operating below potential." Kocherlakota: "insufficient aggregate demand to use available resources," "There is insufficient aggregate demand to use available resources." Evans: "resource gaps closing." Yellen: described "question marks on the outlook" for growth, weak Q1. However, Bullard argues labor markets "relatively strong" and unemployment heading below 5%; George references Laubach-Williams suggesting output "above potential" under one framework. Fischer: unemployment "not that much above many estimates of the natural rate today." Majority view is still below potential / recovering with slack; minority view from Bullard is more optimistic. Bright-line rule: majority use "slack remains," "below potential" language → score MUST be < 50. Score range: 38–44.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Mixed picture but center of gravity is near (slightly below) full employment. Unemployment at 5.5%, widely viewed as approaching NAIRU. Fischer: "not that much above many estimates of the natural rate today." George: Tenth District unemployment 4.2%. Mester: District at 5.2%. Lacker: "shortages of skilled workers," broader wage pressures emerging. Williams: "still some considerable slack in the labor market." Kocherlakota: "wage pressures remain muted." Rosengren: conditions for liftoff "not sufficiently met." Brainard: "slack remains." Center of gravity: near NAIRU/full employment, some slack remaining. Score range: 44–50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Broadly weaker than trend in Q1, though fundamentals considered positive. "Disappointing data" on retail sales, housing, business fixed investment, exports. Yellen: "broad range of spending and production indicators, including retail sales, housing starts, orders and shipments of capital goods, nonresidential construction, drilling, exports, and industrial output" weak. Brainard: "subpar data on retail sales have now persisted for four months." Powell: "weak spending data." Oil sector investment plunging. Most expect rebound but uncertain. Lockhart: GDP tracking "just marginally above zero." Consumer fundamentals seen as decent (sentiment, income). Auto sales rebounded in March. Net assessment: below trend but not contracting. Score range: 38–45.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Financial conditions discussed as generally accommodative / supportive but with concerns about tightening at liftoff. Williams: "commercial real estate... abundance of financing that can be obtained at very generous terms." Market discussion of tightening from dollar appreciation. Rosengren and Kocherlakota warn that liftoff could cause bond market disruption, corporate bond liquidity issues. Tarullo extensively discusses European negative rates and dollar strengthening risk. Center of gravity: conditions still loose to somewhat loose, with emerging concerns about potential tightening at liftoff. Score range: 30–38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
The vote was to hold rates at 0–0.25% (alternative B). The policy discussion center of gravity: nearly all favor B (hold), majority skeptical June is feasible. Kocherlakota openly advocates alternative A (more accommodation/easing signal). Evans and Rosengren lean very dovish (2016 liftoff). Brainard, Tarullo, Vice Chairman Dudley skeptical of June. Mester, Lacker, George keep June on table and lean slightly hawkish. Fischer: "early and gradual" preferred, supports keeping June option. Bullard: supports June if data cooperate, worried about asset bubbles from low rates. The clear majority voted B (hold) with dovish forward lean. No one called for immediate tightening. The liftoff debate is about June vs. September vs. 2016. Overall lean: hold with mild dovish-to-neutral bias, some keeping hawkish option open. Score range: 40–48.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**
No formal dissents recorded in this meeting (all supported alternative B or implicitly acquiesced). However, there are notable differences in views: Kocherlakota explicitly favors alternative A (more easing signal); Lacker and Bullard clearly hawkish-leaning, wanting to keep June live; Evans and Rosengren dovish (2016 liftoff); Mester and George more centrist-hawkish. The inflation target discussion (Rosengren/Kocherlakota vs. Fischer) shows substantive disagreement. Score: moderate differences with distinct camps but no formal dissents. Score range: 30–42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Notably elevated. A separate pre-go-round segment was devoted to financial stability comments (Rosengren on corporate bond liquidity, Kocherlakota on liftoff-induced volatility, Williams on market liquidity/regulation tradeoff). Brainard opened her go-round remarks with extended financial stability discussion (Oct 15 Treasury volatility, bond mutual funds). Tarullo devoted substantial time to European negative rates and potential portfolio shifts. Bullard raised asset price bubble risk from low rates. Multiple participants addressed it at length. Score range: 55–68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
The adopted alternative B maintains hold with data-dependent framing, no date-based guidance, keeping June "on the table" but implicitly leaning later. Majority favors removing "at least" and changing "largely" to "partly" on inflation — slight dovish lean. Kocherlakota's alternative A advocacy for more accommodation signal is minority. The forward signal is: hold, data-dependent, June possible but unlikely for most participants. This is a mild dovish-to-neutral lean, slightly pre-liftoff patient stance. Anchors: 2015 pre-liftoff = 25–40. Score range: 32–42.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Elevated. Chair Yellen explicitly states "more uncertain about the outlook." Dudley: "of two minds." Fischer: "fair probability that will happen, but it's not a guarantee." Brainard: "key question... is how much of this unanticipated weakness represents a slowing in the underlying momentum of activity and how much is due to transitory disturbances?" Multiple participants note the Q1 signal is hard to read. Tarullo: "one has to be a little agnostic right now." International risks: Greece, China, European negative rates. Yellen: "new data will confuse rather than clarify." Score range: 48–60.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

---

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 32 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen (summary): "Both core and headline PCE inflation rates are running well below 2 percent and are likely to do so for the rest of the year... headline inflation will be under 1 percent this year on a Q4-over-Q4 basis, and that core inflation this year is only going to run at 1¼ percent."
- Fischer: "Core PCE inflation was 1.3 percent over the 12 months through March... nontransitory core inflation [is] something like 1.6 percent... nontransitory core inflation rate of 1.7 percent."
- Kocherlakota: "inflation is far below target... inflation does not return to the FOMC's target of 2 percent until 2019."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually all participants acknowledge inflation running significantly below the 2% target, with core PCE around 1.3–1.4% and headline near zero; the only disagreement is whether the firming is emerging or still insufficient — but the level is unambiguously low.

**Minority views:** Lacker and Bullard argue inflation is firming and heading toward 2% sooner; Fischer emphasizes 1.7% "nontransitory core" — these push the score toward 35–38 rather than lower.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 42 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "low inflation readings as being consistent with an economy still operating below potential and facing temporary disinflationary factors."
- Kocherlakota: "There is insufficient aggregate demand to use available resources in an effective fashion... this year now looks like it may be even worse than this weak average performance."
- Yellen: "At a minimum, I am more worried about downside risk and am more uncertain about the outlook... I find it particularly disturbing that the slowdown in business investment appears to have become more widespread."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants see the economy as below potential or approaching it from below, with slack remaining in both labor and product markets, confirmed by weak Q1 GDP; a minority (Bullard, George using Laubach-Williams) argue output may be at or above potential under some r* frameworks.

**Minority views:** Bullard argues unemployment will fall into the 4% range and economy is approaching boom; George notes Laubach-Williams implies positive output gap — these push the score upward within the 38–46 range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 48 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Fischer: "The unemployment rate remains at 5.5 percent, a level that is not that much above many estimates of the natural rate today... some, though not all, of the other labor market indicators... appear to be returning to pre-recession levels."
- Lacker: "Any discussion of labor markets these days turns quickly to the subject of shortages of skilled workers... wage pressures [having] noticeable effects... broader wage pressures may be about to emerge."
- Brainard: "all of the indicators of slack have moved sideways in the last month... slack remains."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants view the labor market as near (but not quite at) full employment — unemployment at 5.5% approaching NAIRU, skilled-worker shortages reported anecdotally, but aggregate wage data still muted and aggregate slack indicators showing no further improvement in March.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota and Rosengren emphasize insufficient slack absorption; Bullard and George expect unemployment to fall well below 5% without labor force participation bounce.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 40 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "unexpected weakness we've seen in a broad range of spending and production indicators, including retail sales, housing starts, orders and shipments of capital goods, nonresidential construction, drilling, exports, and industrial output."
- Brainard: "the negative effects of dollar appreciation and weakness in activity abroad on net exports and business investment... appear to be greater than expected, and the boost to consumption has not materialized... subpar data on retail sales have now persisted for four months."
- Powell: "the U.S. economy has grown at about 2.7 percent over the last two years, with net exports at around zero. With net exports now contributing negative 0.7 to negative 0.8 percentage point... it really has to come from consumption... the consumer, in effect, is now ebulliently saving."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Q1 showed broad-based weakness across consumption and investment — drilling investment plunging ~50% annualized, retail sales soft for 4 months, housing starts weak, business fixed investment broad decline — with most participants expecting a rebound but acknowledging significant uncertainty; conditions are below-trend but not contracting.

**Minority views:** Several participants (Lockhart, Williams, Mester) see transitory factors dominating and expect rebound; auto sales rebounded in March. Some upside potential from consumer spending fundamentals.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 33 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "some of my contacts in different sectors, including commercial real estate, have emphasized the abundance of financing that can be obtained at very generous terms."
- Kocherlakota: "current policy has not produced any material signs of financial instability" (reading from QS report).
- Tarullo: "term premiums in benchmark U.S. and other advanced economy bond yields are again near historical lows reached before the taper tantrum, raising the risk of similarly outsized market reactions at liftoff."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are described as accommodative and loose — generous credit terms, low term premiums, near-historical-lows on bond yields — with the main concern being that conditions could tighten abruptly at or after liftoff rather than being tight currently.

**Minority views:** Rosengren raises corporate bond liquidity concerns; Brainard notes potential fire-sale dynamics — these concerns are about potential future tightening rather than current tightness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 44 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kocherlakota: "I favor alternative A... we should make clear... that we are willing to use all of our tools to return inflation to target within one to two years."
- Evans: "economic conditions will most likely be consistent with an appropriate time for liftoff being sometime in 2016... I'm a little nervous over the latest data on activity."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "I think it's unlikely, speaking for myself, that I'm going to see sufficient news in the next seven weeks that'll cause me to want to lift off at the June FOMC meeting."
- (Counterweight) Lacker: "I believe the case for raising rates would be strong [if data cooperate by June]." Fischer: "early and gradual is a much better approach to raising the interest rate than late and steep."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimity for alternative B (hold); clear majority skeptical of June liftoff with several (Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota) pointing to 2016 or no near-term liftoff; a significant minority (Lacker, Bullard, Mester, George, Fischer) keep June viable but not committed — producing a lean-dovish-to-neutral center of gravity.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota explicitly favors easing signal (alt A); Lacker and Bullard lean hawkish. Range of views is notable but does not produce formal dissents.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kocherlakota clearly advocates alternative A while all others support B.
- Rosengren raises inflation target debate (2015 reexamination), supported by Kocherlakota; Fischer explicitly pushes back: "we should not go public with any doubts that we might have about the goals of monetary policy."
- Lacker and Bullard lean toward earlier liftoff; Evans and Kocherlakota toward later; Brainard and Tarullo very reluctant on June.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissents, but the meeting features clear camps: a dovish bloc (Kocherlakota, Evans, Rosengren), a hawkish-leaning bloc (Lacker, Bullard, and partly Mester/George), and a centrist majority (Williams, Fischer, Powell, Yellen, Dudley, Brainard, Tarullo, Lockhart); additionally a substantive debate about the inflation target adds to disagreement depth.

**Minority views:** The governance/implementation discussion at the top of the meeting also showed notable divergence (Kocherlakota opposed delegation; others supported; Lacker on discount rate spread).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 62 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren (dedicated financial stability segment): "concerns with corporate bond market liquidity as we approach liftoff bear watching... a crowded exit with few buyers could lead to a rapid increase in the yield on corporate bonds."
- Brainard: "one area that bears heightened scrutiny is the potential for spikes in volatility due to changes in market liquidity... episodes of severe volatility, like the wild swings we saw in Treasury bond yields on the morning of October 15, are likely to become the new normal."
- Tarullo: devoted ~3 pages to European negative rates and implications for dollar, term premiums, and financial stability risks.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability received substantial dedicated attention — a separate pre-go-round segment with three speakers, plus additional discussion woven throughout the economic round (Brainard, Williams, Bullard on asset bubbles, Kocherlakota on liftoff-stability linkage) — making it a prominent but not dominant topic alongside the economic assessment.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota frames financial stability concerns as arguing *against* liftoff rather than as a standalone concern; Williams raises regulatory-liquidity tradeoff. Attention is high but not overwhelming.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen (closing summary): "personally think it's unlikely that the conditions we've set out for a firming of policy will be met by June, but I have an open mind."
- Majority language outcome: removal of "at least" and change from "largely" to "partly" in inflation sentence — both dovish-leaning adjustments to the statement.
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "I hope the economy is going to cooperate and is going to make a September liftoff feasible, but I don't even really have that much confidence about that, either."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is patient and data-dependent, explicitly not telegraphing near-term action; majority view implies liftoff timing later than June, keeping all meetings nominally "live" but signaling willingness to wait for clearer evidence on both labor markets and inflation — consistent with a mild easing lean / low-for-longer posture.

**Minority views:** Lacker, Bullard, Mester, George keep June genuinely on table and resist any language that signals ruling it out, pushing the score slightly above 35.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 56 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "I am more worried about downside risk and am more uncertain about the outlook... new data will confuse rather than clarify our assessments of the state of the economy."
- Dudley: "I'm of two minds... In terms of the growth side, I'm more uncertain about the growth outlook and more concerned about the potential downside risks to growth."
- Fischer: "There's a fair probability that will happen, but it's not a guarantee that we're going to bounce right back... The situation is never clearer in the future. It's just unclear in a different way."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple senior participants — including Chair Yellen, Vice Chairman Dudley, Fischer, Brainard, and Tarullo — explicitly cite elevated uncertainty about whether Q1 softness is transitory or persistent; uncertainty about international conditions (Greece, China, European negative rates); uncertainty about the timing and impact of liftoff; raised uncertainty band but not at crisis-level "uncharted territory."

**Minority views:** Williams, Mester, and Lacker express more confidence that Q1 is transitory and the recovery will reassert — preventing the score from moving higher.
