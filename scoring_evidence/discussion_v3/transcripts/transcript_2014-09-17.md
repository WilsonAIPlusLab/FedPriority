## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** — individual participant statements from the economic go-around (Day 1, September 16) plus the policy go-round (Day 2, September 17). Speakers are clearly identified by name/title. The document spans from President Plosser's opening go-around through Chair Yellen's closing policy summary and the vote. There is a brief staff interlude at the start of Day 2 (Wilcox on the CPI reading), which is staff content and excluded from scoring.

### 2. Era Context
September 2014: the U.S. economy is in the middle of a post-GFC recovery. The taper of QE3 is nearly complete (final taper expected at the October meeting). The unemployment rate stands at 6.1%. PCE inflation is running roughly 1½% year-over-year, below the 2% target. Growth appears to be re-accelerating after a Q1 2014 weather-driven contraction. This is a pre-liftoff environment — the Committee is debating "considerable time" language and the timing of eventual rate increases, with most SEP submissions showing liftoff somewhere in 2015.

### 3. Directional Signals Per Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below target**. Evans: "Core PCE inflation is running about 1½ percent, with the past couple of monthly readings back on the soft side." Dudley: "we flattened out in terms of core inflation, about ½ percentage point short of our 2 percent inflation objective." Brainard: "subdued import prices and a drop in energy prices...no imminent threat of a surge in inflation...still some distance from achieving our inflation goals." Chair Yellen: "rates of increase in both total and core PCE now stand around 1½ percent...edged down very slightly." Several speakers (Fisher, George, Lacker) note some upward wage pressures in specific sectors and the possibility of a convex Phillips curve, and Fisher notes supply-driven food/energy price declines. But the center of gravity is clearly below target, not alarmingly so, and risks are described as diminishing on the downside rather than building on the upside. Score should be in the 35–42 range (below target, not deflationary, not alarming). I'll place at **38**.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential but recovering, with some approaching potential**. Most SEP submissions show growth of approximately 3% — "above trend" — which would be consistent with closing the gap over the horizon. Mester: "I continue to project GDP growth to be about 3 percent in the second half of this year through 2016 before it returns to trend growth of 2½ percent." Williams: "resource gaps closing over time." Lacker: more skeptical of achieving 3%, sees slower productivity. Dudley: "economy on track… 3 percent for the second half and in 2015." Fischer: "we are now...relatively close to the attainment of the goals of the dual mandate." But Tarullo: "a lot of slack remaining" and Brainard: "slack in the labor market—more than suggested by the fall in unemployment alone." The balance: economy is meaningfully below potential (still recovering, QE ongoing, unemployment at 6.1%) but gap is narrowing. Range 35–42 consistent with anchors for 2010–14 recovery. Score: **40**.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see the labor market as **improving but still below full employment**. Unemployment at 6.1%, natural rate estimates range from 5.0 to 5.5%. Yellen (summary): "significant underutilization of labor resources" is the operative language. Brainard: "more slack than suggested by the fall in unemployment alone." Tarullo: "considerable amount of slack remaining." Powell: "share of people working part time for economic reasons is about 2 percentage points higher than it should be." But Fisher: "Labor market slack continues to decline...reasonable probability of reaching full employment before the end of 2016." Bullard: clear improvement, discount LFPR decline as secular. Plosser: "labor market has been on a very healthy run over the past year." Most participants project unemployment reaching the natural rate by end-2015 to end-2016. This is a labor market clearly below full employment but making steady progress — consistent with the 37–42 range (below full employment, slack remains, but declining). Score: **40**.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see spending/investment as **moderate to mildly above trend**. Most forecast growth around 3% (above the ~2% trend), supported by improving consumer sentiment. Plosser: "more bullish than the Tealbook concerning the consumer…latest retail sales report adds support." Evans: "consumer outlook seems on a better footing after the recent retail sales and quarterly services reports…orders for capital goods in ISM surveys point to a good pickup in business investment." George: "jump in the saving rate since last year as leaving some additional upside potential" and "growth of core capital goods shipments picked up considerably." Lockhart: "industrial activity has been strong…mixed reports on consumer spending…overall impression was of fairly lackluster consumer activity." Dudley: "don't see many sources that would fuel a meaningful pickup in the growth rate from here." Housing is a consistent soft spot. The center of gravity is moderate-to-solid, not booming, not sluggish. Score: **52** (at-to-slightly-above trend).

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Financial conditions are described as **loose/accommodative** — QE still in progress, near-zero rates. Fisher raises concerns about stretched equity valuations, M&A froth, junk bond covenants disappearing: "we are pushing the extremes of financial instability." Powell: "high degree of froth in financial markets." But these are concerns about conditions being too loose, not tight. Brainard and Mester: "financial-stability risks seem low" in the near term. Dudley: conditions are accommodative. The policy rate is at the zero lower bound; QE ongoing. This is clearly in the loose range (16–30). Score: **28** (conditions are loose/accommodative, some froth concern but no tightness).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This is a complex meeting. The policy vote is on Alternative B (hold/taper), which carries. Most participants support B. Fisher dissents (hawkish — wants to change language). Plosser dissents (hawkish — wants to drop "considerable time"). The majority (Lockhart, Rosengren, Williams, Evans, Kocherlakota, Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, Dudley, Fischer, George) support B as written or with minor tweaks. Evans explicitly prefers liftoff in early 2016 (most dovish). Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Brainard: "patient" and want to keep "considerable time." George: more hawkish, concerned about keeping near-zero rates too long. Bullard: wants to be more opportunistic and nimble, open to earlier liftoff. Lacker: would have supported removing "considerable time." The center of gravity is: hold policy unchanged, taper QE, maintain "considerable time" language. This is pre-liftoff — there are 2 formal dissents to the dovish side (Fisher, Plosser) wanting less accommodation. The stance leans **modestly hawkish-to-neutral** within a broadly dovish QE-era context. The QE taper is nearly complete — this is not a pure QE meeting. The actual decision is hold with tapering (consistent with 40–50 range). Given the dissents pushing hawkish and the majority arguing for patience (dovish lean), but the overall trajectory being toward normalization, score: **47** (lean dovish to balanced; hold steady with taper; majority favors patience on liftoff).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Two formal dissents: Fisher (too dovish) and Plosser (too dovish). There are also several participants with reservations — Bullard wants changes to language but votes for B; Mester was hoping to change forward guidance today; George concerned about keeping near-zero too long; Lacker would have preferred dropping "considerable time." On the other side, Evans wants even more patience/delay. This is a meeting with 2 formal dissents plus 4–5 notable reservations but not "different prescriptions" for the fundamental framework — everyone agrees on ending QE and the general direction. Score: **42** (moderate disagreement: 2 dissents, multiple reservations, notable differences in pace/timing views, but not sharp irreconcilable camps).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability is discussed by multiple participants but is not the dominant topic. Fisher: spends significant time on equity markets, M&A activity, junk bond market froth, leverage. Powell: "high degree of froth in financial markets." Mester: references the 2014 Shared National Credits exam results on leveraged lending. Brainard: "low for long" environment creating perverse incentives; vigilance needed. Yellen (summary): "rising valuations...heavy M&A activity, stock buybacks, spread compression...deterioration in leveraged lending standards." This is meaningful discussion — several participants address it, it comes up in both the go-around and policy round. But it is subordinate to the labor market/inflation debate. Score: **40** (moderate discussion; several participants address it meaningfully but it does not rival the main economic assessment).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The forward signal is **mildly dovish** — the Committee retains "considerable time" language, which most read as ~6 months before liftoff from end of QE (i.e., roughly mid-2015). The majority support keeping this language unchanged. Evans explicitly supports liftoff in early 2016. Kocherlakota emphasizes "extraordinary patience." But the normalization principles are being released, the SEP median shows liftoff in mid-2015, and the taper is ending in October. The forward signal is: accommodation will be maintained for a "considerable time" but normalization is approaching. This is in the 25–40 range (easing lean with data dependence, transition toward normalization). Score: **33** (mild easing bias, "considerable time" language retained, liftoff horizon roughly 6–9 months out, but normalization principles released).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Uncertainty is present but at a relatively normal level for this era. Fischer: "uncertainty about what that rate will be is massive"; "enormous uncertainty that besets our forecasts." Williams: risks "roughly balanced" and magnitude "similar to the past 20 years." Tarullo: "cannot really know" if quit rate will return to pre-crisis levels. Lacker: "forecasting medium-term swings in labor productivity growth is notoriously difficult." Brainard: downside risks "elevated." The dominant uncertainties are: how much labor market slack remains (structural vs. cyclical), the path of foreign growth (Europe, China), and exchange rate effects. These are real but not extraordinary — standard recovery-era uncertainties. Score: **38** (somewhat elevated: certain factors "harder to assess than usual," multiple participants note uncertainty, but not "particularly uncertain" or "uncharted territory").

### 4. Polarity Verification
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose (QE, ZIRP, frothy markets) → score below 50. Score 28. Check.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Majority supports patience/hold → lean dovish. Score 47. Check.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Considerable time" retained, majority wants patience → lean dovish forward signal. Score 33. Check.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy still recovering, below potential → score below 50. Score 40. Check.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, consistent — economy still in recovery, slack remains. Good coherence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (33): HAWKISH_DOVISH slightly closer to neutral (QE taper, two hawkish dissents, normalization discussion) while FORWARD_GUIDANCE is more dovish (retaining "considerable time," majority wants patience). This divergence is expected and explainable: the *current action* is neutral/slightly hawkish (continuing taper, releasing normalization principles), but the *forward signal* is still deliberately dovish ("considerable time"). This is a known pattern and consistent with the rubric's note to "separate CURRENT ACTION from FORWARD LEAN."

Participants in go-around: Plosser, Fisher, Lacker, Lockhart, Evans, Bullard, Mester, George, Williams, Kocherlakota (2-hander), Dudley, Fischer, Tarullo, Powell, Brainard (+ Yellen summary).

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "Core PCE inflation is running about 1½ percent, with the past couple of monthly readings back on the soft side. Wages continue to rise modestly at about a 2¼ percent rate."
