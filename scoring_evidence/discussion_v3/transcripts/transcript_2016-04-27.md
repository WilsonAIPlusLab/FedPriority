# FOMC Transcript Scoring — April 26–27, 2016

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of a two-day FOMC meeting (April 26–27, 2016). Individual speakers are identified by name. The document as extracted begins at page 27 of 261, covering:

- **Day 1 (April 26):** An academic symposium/discussion on monetary policy and financial stability (Fischer, Lacker, Kashkari, Powell, Tarullo, and others presenting and debating research papers). Also includes a Quantitative Surveillance (QS) financial stability assessment presentation with participant comments (Rosengren, Brainard, Fischer, Dudley). Followed by a global economic discussion (Dudley presenting risks, Fischer questioning). Then the **economic go-round** begins (Williams, Lacker, Fischer, Rosengren, Brainard, Bullard, Powell, Kaplan, Evans, Lockhart, Harker, Mester, George, Tarullo, Kashkari, Dudley, Yellen).

- **Day 2 (April 27):** Policy discussion go-round (Rosengren, Tarullo, Brainard, Lacker, Bullard, Lockhart, Kashkari, Evans, Kaplan, Harker, Mester, George, Williams, Powell, Fischer, Dudley, Yellen closing).

**Scoring focus**: All participant views expressed across both sessions, including economic go-round, policy go-round, and qualitative comments in the financial stability discussion where participants express substantive views about conditions.

---

### 2. Era Context

April 2016: The FOMC had lifted off in December 2015 after seven years of near-zero rates. The economy was experiencing a weak Q1 2016 (GDP tracking ~0.4%), strong labor markets (~200K jobs/month, unemployment ~5%), below-target inflation (core PCE ~1.5–1.7%), a prior bout of financial market turbulence in January–February 2016 that had largely reversed, concerns about China/global growth, the pending Brexit vote (June 23), and persistent productivity weakness. Policy: Alternative B (hold steady) was adopted unanimously. The general question was whether June 2016 would be the next hike opportunity.

### 3. Dimension-by-Dimension Pre-Scoring

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as **below target** but gradually improving. Core PCE is running ~1.5–1.7%, with several participants citing the "gradual" path toward 2%. Williams: "core PCE inflation rose 1.6 percent from the first quarter of last year." Lacker: "measures of underlying inflation rise toward 2 percent." Rosengren: "core PCE inflation currently at 1.7 percent, there is more evidence of a positive trend." Brainard: "core PCE prices should move down from the 1.7 percent readings... to about 1.5 percent this quarter." Mester: "core PCE inflation at 1½ percent... up 20 basis points compared with a year ago." Bullard: concerned that "market-based inflation compensation has recovered partially... 10-year TIPS breakeven at around 1.65 percent." Kashkari: "inflation and the inflation outlook also remain weak." Yellen: "core PCE inflation will come in at about 1½ percent on a four-quarter basis this year." Dominant view: below target, trending up slowly. Score direction: below midpoint, in the 36–42 range (below target; positive trending, not alarming).

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Participants see the economy as **slightly below to near potential**. Q1 GDP at 0.4% is viewed mostly as transitory ("residual seasonality"). Most see 2% full-year GDP, roughly at or just below trend. Multiple participants invoke "potential growth" language in both directions. Tarullo raises possibility that potential itself has been marked down. Lacker: "we should transition to a new trend growth rate for real GDP of less than 1½ percent." Brainard: "domestic spending indicators have disappointed for the second consecutive quarter." Bullard: "all of the surrounding quarters are pretty low... average of 1.5 percent." Several note that Q1 weakness is offset by labor market strength. The center of gravity is around slight-below-potential, though close to it. Score direction: below 50 but marginally so — range 44–48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Participants see labor market as **tight / near or at full employment**. Very strong consensus. Lacker: "no slack left in labor markets… essentially full utilization." Williams: "labor market at full employment." Mester: "economy is at maximum employment from the perspective of what monetary policy can do." Rosengren: "tighter labor market." Fischer: "very close to full employment." However, Brainard and Kashkari express meaningful reservations: Brainard cites "prime-age EPOP still 1½ percentage points below the average level before the crisis" and "subdued pace of wage growth," and Kashkari: "I don't have confidence that there is no more slack." Yellen: "still see some room for further improvement." The center of gravity is strongly near-full-employment, with a minority (Brainard, Kashkari, Tarullo) noting remaining slack margins. Score direction: ~50–55, i.e., near full employment with slight tilt toward tight but not dramatically tight.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Mixed — spending data weak (Q1), expected to rebound; investment (especially business) weak. Multiple participants describe Q1 consumption as "soft," "disappointing," "surprisingly weak." Powell: "real GDP increased at just a ½ percent rate in the first quarter after growing at only a 1.4 percent rate in Q4." Yellen: "consumer spending...has slowed markedly from the robust pace seen last year." However, all expect a rebound: Rosengren: "I am expecting the first quarter to be a lull rather than a trend." Business investment was also weak — Brainard: "significant decline in business fixed investment last quarter." But the tone is "temporarily soft, fundamentals support rebound." Score direction: below-trend currently but expected to normalize — around 42–46.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Financial conditions described as having **eased significantly** from January–February highs. Multiple participants emphasize the improvement: Rosengren: "stock prices are higher, stock volatility is down, many interest rate spreads have narrowed." Brainard: "substantial easing of financial conditions." Powell: "stabilization of financial markets." Yellen: "financial conditions have significantly improved with the dollar down... risk spreads on corporate bonds narrower, equity prices up almost 5 percent." Evans: "improvement in credit conditions that started in mid-February." However, some residual caution: Brainard: "corporate bond spreads... remain elevated." Dudley: "financial conditions are now much easier compared with just a few months ago." Direction: conditions are somewhat loose/accommodative but not dramatically so — score range 35–42 (somewhat loose, generally supportive).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
Direction: The meeting's decision is to **hold rates steady** (Alternative B), adopted with broad support including from a significant majority. However, the discussion is considerably nuanced. Several participants explicitly favor June hike: Lacker (wanted to hike NOW), George (wanted to hike NOW), Mester (operating assumption of June hike), Harker (prefers more hawkish statement language), Kaplan (looking to June). Several are data-dependent with June possible: Rosengren, Williams, Fischer, Powell, Dudley (all "two hikes this year" camp). Several are more cautious: Brainard, Tarullo, Kashkari, Evans (less urgency). No one advocates easing. The dominant lean is hold-with-meaningful-hawkish-tilt-forward. The center of gravity: ~55–58. This is not a strongly hawkish meeting (no hike taken, significant Brexit uncertainty), but the clear direction is toward further normalization.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Direction: No formal voting dissents recorded in this meeting (unanimous support for Alternative B). However, there are notable differences:
- **Lacker** explicitly states "this would have been a fine meeting to raise the target federal funds rate" — a near-dissent.
- **George** similarly says "economic fundamentals consistent with a 25 basis point rate increase at this meeting."
- **Mester** expresses strong concern that statement language may not adequately signal June.
- **Bullard** advocates for a fundamental communications strategy overhaul (dot plot elimination).
- **Brainard/Kashkari** are more dovish and cautious about June.
The range of views is notable — from wanting to hike now (Lacker, George) to cautious about any near-term move (Brainard, Kashkari). Score direction: moderate disagreement — no formal dissents but multiple strong reservations spanning both directions. Range: 35–45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Direction: This is a two-day meeting where **Day 1 was entirely devoted** to a special symposium on monetary policy and financial stability (academic papers, QS presentation). Multiple participants — Fischer, Lacker, Kashkari, Powell, Tarullo, Brainard, Rosengren, Dudley — all devoted substantial remarks to financial stability topics. This is far above typical meeting discussion. Score direction: Very high — dominant topic on Day 1. Range: 75–85.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Direction: The statement (Alternative B) is held with some hawkish forward signal. Participants broadly signal June as a "live" meeting: Yellen says "we definitely need to keep our options open for June." Rosengren, Williams, Powell, Fischer, Kaplan all discuss two hikes this year. But Alternative B language is deliberately non-committal. The signal is a **mild-to-moderate tightening bias** but conditioned on data. Score direction: 55–63. The forward guidance leans toward further tightening (hiking cycle ongoing), but is deliberately cautious about timing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Direction: Multiple participants express elevated uncertainty. Specific sources cited: residual seasonality vs. genuine slowdown, productivity growth trajectory, Brexit outcome (Tarullo: "more like Knightian uncertainty than risk assessment"), global growth risks, China currency path, market expectations divergence from Committee projections. Yellen: "we will have little choice but to monitor developments carefully and avoid being overly confident in any projection." Tarullo: "I genuinely don't know how to make an ex ante assessment" on Brexit. However, uncertainty is not catastrophic — it is described as "somewhat elevated," focused on a few specific known unknowns. Score direction: elevated but moderate — range 45–55.

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 38 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "core PCE inflation rose 1.6 percent from the first quarter of last year to the first quarter of this year... a big part of this step-up reflects the dissipation of transitory factors."
- Brainard: "core PCE prices should move down from the 1.7 percent readings reached in January and February to about 1.5 percent this quarter... progress toward our inflation target is likely to remain quite gradual."
- Yellen: "the current Tealbook forecast that core PCE inflation will come in at about 1½ percent on a four-quarter basis this year... I continue to expect that inflation will move up to 2 percent by late 2018."
- Kashkari: "inflation and the inflation outlook also remain weak... measures of inflation expectations remain at extremely low levels."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly see inflation as below target (core PCE ~1.5–1.7%), gradually improving but not yet on a clear trajectory to 2%. The dominant framing is "gradual progress" with no urgency.

**Minority views:** Lacker (more hawkish on inflation trajectory) and Mester (more confident it will reach target sooner) lean higher; Brainard and Kashkari lean lower, citing risks to the downside.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 46 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "the Tealbook expects the first-quarter real GDP growth rate to be only ½ percent, which is slow even with diminished assumptions about potential real GDP growth... I am expecting the first quarter to be a lull rather than a trend."
- Tarullo: "my baseline outlook was for economic growth of roughly 2 percent, or maybe a little bit more, this year... risks weighted to the downside almost exclusively because of global risks."
- Bullard: "annualized growth rates of real GDP... average of these four numbers is 1.5 percent. That's below my estimate of longer-run trend growth."
- Yellen: "incoming data create sufficient concern about the outlook... a persistent slowdown in aggregate spending may be under way, which could hinder further improvement in labor market conditions."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly see the economy at or slightly below potential — Q1 weakness viewed as mostly transitory but still real. The four-quarter average of ~2% GDP is considered approximately trend-level; the first-quarter miss keeps the assessment just below the midpoint.

**Minority views:** Lacker argues the economy will soon reach "overutilization"; Tarullo and Brainard lean slightly more toward output below potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 53 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Lacker: "I agree with the Tealbook that there's no slack left in labor markets, and measures of unemployment and labor force participation indicate essentially full utilization of labor resources."
- Mester: "the economy is at maximum employment from the perspective of what monetary policy can do. I note that the Tealbook now assesses there is essentially no slack left in the labor market."
- Rosengren: "as the labor market has tightened, more people are moving from out of the labor force to being employed" and notes "probing how low the natural rate of unemployment is currently will likely encourage more people to reenter the labor force."
- Brainard (minority): "prime-age EPOP ratio still remains 1½ percentage points below the average level before the crisis... still-subdued pace of wage growth... just a little more slack remaining."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Strong consensus that labor market is at or near full employment, with robust job growth (~200K/month) and rising participation. A meaningful minority (Brainard, Kashkari, Yellen with caveats) notes remaining margins of slack, keeping the score from moving further above 50.

**Minority views:** Brainard, Kashkari, and Tarullo express skepticism about the "no slack" characterization.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 44 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "consumer spending...has slowed markedly from the robust pace seen last year despite solid income gains, a relatively high level of household wealth, and healthy readings on consumer sentiment."
- Brainard: "weakness in business investment, lower corporate profits... suggest a significant decline in business fixed investment last quarter."
- Powell: "real GDP increased at just a ½ percent rate in the first quarter after growing at only a 1.4 percent rate in Q4... weakness is in consumption and investment, the two main engines for growth."
- Rosengren: "I am fairly confident that spending will rebound... Like the Tealbook, I expect consumption to rebound in the remainder of the year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Both consumer spending and business investment were soft in Q1 2016 (and Q4 2015), but participants broadly expect a rebound given labor and income fundamentals. The current level is clearly below trend, but transitory framing moderates the score.

**Minority views:** Bullard and Evans flag potential persistence of weakness; Lockhart and Kaplan note some business caution from contacts.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- Rosengren: "stock prices are higher. Stock volatility is down. Many interest rate spreads have narrowed, and many global financial markets have improved."
- Brainard: "substantial easing of financial conditions... equity prices have rebounded... corporate bond spreads have come down, although they remain elevated."
- Evans: "improvement in credit conditions that started in mid-February... continuation of the improvement in credit conditions."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions have eased substantially since the January–February turbulence, driven in part by the perceived downshift in U.S. rate expectations. Conditions are now described as "accommodative" and "much easier," placing them in the somewhat-loose range.

**Minority views:** Dudley and Brainard note some residual fragility and that the improvement is "closely tied to recent monetary policy communication."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 56 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Minority views:** Brainard and Kashkari are the most dovish; Lacker and George are the most hawkish.
