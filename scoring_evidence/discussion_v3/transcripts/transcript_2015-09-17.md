## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the September 16–17, 2015 FOMC meeting. It includes two distinct sessions: (1) a September 16 discussion on balance sheet / reinvestment policy and the economic go-round, and (2) a September 17 policy discussion / go-round. Speakers are individually identified by name (MR. LACKER, MS. MESTER, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, CHAIR YELLEN, etc.). The document is well-segmented and rich in participant-level content. Approximately 18–19 distinct voices appear.

### 2. Era Context
September 2015 is a pre-liftoff meeting in the post-GFC recovery. The economy has been growing at moderate above-trend pace; unemployment has fallen to 5.1 percent, near the median NAIRU estimate; but inflation is running substantially below target (core PCE ~1.2 percent year-over-year) with oil prices and dollar appreciation as the main suppressors. China devalued its currency in August 2015, triggering global equity volatility in late August. The Committee is debating liftoff from the zero lower bound—the first rate hike in nearly 7 years. The final decision is to hold (alternative B), with roughly 4 dissenters preferring alternative C (immediate liftoff) and 1 preferring alternative A (ease further / signal no hike). This is a period of meaningful dissent and genuine uncertainty about both inflation dynamics and global spillovers.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants perceive inflation as BELOW TARGET. Core PCE is running at ~1.2 percent. The majority acknowledge this is largely due to oil prices and dollar appreciation (transitory in their modal view), but several participants (Evans, Kocherlakota, Brainard, Tarullo, Dudley, Powell, Yellen) express genuine concern that underlying inflation may not return to 2 percent in the medium term and worry about de-anchoring. Fischer, Lacker, Mester, Williams, Harker argue these factors are transitory and express reasonable confidence in return to 2 percent. The center-of-gravity is clearly below 50 — this is meaningfully below-target inflation. The range is roughly 26-40 on the rubric ("below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation"). Given the persistent miss, near-zero headline PCE, core at 1.2 percent, and widespread concern — but most still expect eventual convergence — I place this at approximately 32.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy is described as growing at "above trend" or "slightly above trend" by multiple participants (Bullard: "above-trend growth"; Mester: "GDP growth picking up to above-trend pace"; Lacker: "consumption growth has returned to 3 percent annual rate"; Harker: "modest above trend"). However, the staff and some participants (Dudley, Brainard, Yellen) emphasize international headwinds dragging at growth, and the Tealbook projects only 2 percent for H2 2015 and 2016. The center of gravity is near potential but slightly above — the economy is growing at or a bit above trend. Several participants say "at or near full employment." Bright-line: "above trend" language is used by several → must be above 50 but not far above given headwinds. Score: approximately 52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Strong consensus that labor market is at or very near full employment. Unemployment at 5.1 percent (at or within sight of NAIRU estimates of 4.8–5.2 percent). Lacker: "virtually all measures of labor market utilization have fallen enough to be statistically indistinguishable from any plausible normative benchmark." Mester: "economy is at or nearly at full employment." Williams: "at or very near full employment." Yellen: "unemployment rate stands just a touch above the median of our estimates of its longer-run normal level." Brainard, Dudley, Tarullo, Powell see some residual slack (participation, involuntary part-time). The center-of-gravity places this squarely in the "near NAIRU / full employment" zone (46-52 on rubric), not yet tight but no longer loosely below. Score: approximately 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending described broadly as solid. Lacker: "real PCE growth to remain 2½ to 3 percent range"; Mester: "strong auto sales"; Evans: "domestic demand relatively strong"; Brainard: "domestic final sales rose 2½ percent in first half of 2015." Business fixed investment is mixed — energy capex declining sharply (Kaplan), but nonresidential construction improving (Mester, Brainard). Net: moderate pace, around or slightly above trend — squarely in the 46–54 range of the rubric. Score: approximately 51.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants note tightening of financial conditions since the July meeting: dollar appreciation, equity market decline (~7 percent), widening credit spreads, elevated VIX. Yellen: "financial conditions have tightened materially in recent weeks." Brainard: "international developments alone have produced a notable tightening roughly equivalent to an increase of 50 basis points." Dudley: "financial conditions have already tightened significantly." Powell: "U.S. financial conditions have tightened since the July meeting by well more than 25 basis points." However, Bullard and others note that financial stress indexes are still well below 2011 European debt crisis and far below 2008 levels. Conditions have tightened from their prior loose state but are not "crisis tight." Roughly in the 55–62 range (somewhat tight; "conditions have tightened"; "spreads widened"). Score: approximately 58.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The final decision is to hold (alternative B) — no rate hike. However, this is not a comfortable hold. Counting the policy preference positions:
- Alternative C (immediate hike): Lacker (voting dissent), Mester (prefers C but goes with B), Williams (prefers C but accepts B), Bullard (supports C), George (supports C) — approximately 5 strongly hawkish
- Alternative B (hold, lean toward year-end liftoff): Lockhart, Rosengren, Harker, Kaplan, Fischer, Tarullo, Powell, Dudley, Yellen — all support hold but signal liftoff "later this year" or "very soon." All of this group are hawkishly leaning in that they see liftoff within months.
- Alternative A (no near-term liftoff / more accommodation): Kocherlakota (leans toward A), Evans (supports B but wants 2016 liftoff)

The center of gravity: the majority voted to hold but are strongly signaling upcoming liftoff in 2015. This is the pre-liftoff period — closer to 51-60 range on the rubric ("lean hawkish: hold steady with tightening bias; approaching time to begin"). With multiple members preferring actual immediate tightening and the hold group strongly signaling near-term hike, I place this at approximately 57.

This is a meeting with clear camps: the "hike now" camp (~4–5 members), the "hike soon this year" camp (majority, ~8–9), and the "wait longer / no near-term hike" camp (~2). ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 32 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Gov. Fischer: "Many discussions of inflation start with the 12-month core PCE inflation rate, which is currently 1.2 percent and projected to be 1.3 percent by the end of the year, although total PCE inflation is projected to be only 0.3 percent at that time."
- Pres. Evans: "the year-over-year increase in core PCE inflation is still just 1.2 percent... none of my inflation models we regularly run at the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago get us to 2 percent within the forecast horizon."
- Pres. Rosengren: "the recent data on wages and prices are consistent with the continued persistent undershooting of our 2 percent inflation goal... my own forecast does not expect us to reach our inflation target within two years."
- Gov. Brainard: "the 12-month change in core PCE inflation appears stubbornly mired at around 1¼ percent, where it has been for some years now."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants overwhelmingly characterize inflation as persistently below target at ~1.2 percent core PCE, largely attributed to transitory factors (oil, dollar), but several express genuine uncertainty about the return path, placing the score firmly in the below-target disinflation range.

**Minority views:** Lacker, Mester, Fischer, Williams, and Harker argue the transitory factors will dissipate and underlying inflation is closer to target (~1.6–1.7 percent on trimmed measures), and express reasonable confidence in return to 2 percent. These views narrow the center but don't shift it above 40.

**Score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The majority of participants view unemployment at/near NAIRU (5.1 percent vs. estimates of 4.8–5.2 percent), but wage data and other utilization metrics suggest residual slack, placing the center directly at the NAIRU boundary (score ~50).

**Minority views:** Lacker and Bullard see labor as clearly tight with JOLTS/openings data suggesting minimal slack; Evans and Brainard see meaningful residual slack. The tension directly supports a 50 score — evenly contested.
