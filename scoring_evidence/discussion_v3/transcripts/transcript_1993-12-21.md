# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1993-12-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually identified speakers in a go-around format followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include Presidents (Parry, Hoenig, Forrestal, Jordan, Syron, Boehne, McTeer, Melzer, Stern, McDonough, Broaddus, Keehn) and Governors (Angell, Kelley, LaWare, Mullins, Lindsey, Phillips), plus Chairman Greenspan. This is a late-1993 early transcript -- the go-around structure is clear.

### Era Context
December 1993: The economy is in the middle of a recovery from the 1990-91 recession, now roughly 2.5-3 years into expansion. The fed funds rate has been at 3% (historically low) for about 17 months. GDP growth has averaged about 3% for the past two years, and the fourth quarter of 1993 is expected to come in at ~5%. Inflation (core CPI) is running around 3%, and the key debate is when to begin raising rates from this very accommodative level. Unemployment is 6.4-6.5%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as slightly below the midpoint -- present inflation is low and contained, but there are emerging concerns about future pressures. Multiple participants report it is "very difficult to raise prices" (Forrestal, Boehne, Syron, Stern). Core CPI is around 3%, with Melzer noting core CPI at 3% (3.6% last two months). Phillips notes "worrisome signs on the horizon" including gold prices, capacity constraints, and construction materials prices. The center of gravity: current inflation is contained but not at target; concern is forward-looking. CPI at 2.5-3% is modestly above what would be "price stability" but participants mostly emphasize the difficulty of raising prices and subdued current pressures. I score this around 40-43. There's no acute inflation alarm but it's not deeply subdued either -- it's in a "manageable but not at goal" zone. Melzer and Angell are more hawkish on inflation outlook, but the majority see current pressures as muted.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says current pressures muted but not negligible, with some forward concerns. Score of 42 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy growing strongly (~3% average for 2 years, 5% in Q4), but still with slack remaining. Parry: "labor market slack" still present, gap between potential and actual GDP "may be diminishing at a faster rate." Syron: "key question is how much slack there is in the economy." Mullins: capacity utilization at 83 vs. peak of 84.8, unemployment at 6.5% down from 7.7%. Greenspan: expansion is sustainable, not yet at capacity. Melzer: "output growth would be at or above potential next year." The economy is approaching but not yet at potential -- still "below potential but closing fast." Multiple Districts report strong growth, some "boom conditions" (Hoenig: Denver, Albuquerque; Jordan: central Kentucky).

Score: 45. The economy is slightly below potential but closing the gap rapidly. Not yet above potential -- slack still present per multiple speakers.

Bright-line check: Multiple speakers reference remaining slack and the gap "diminishing" -- score must be < 50. 45 is consistent.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Unemployment at 6.4-6.5%, down from 7.7% in summer 1992. Factory workweek and overtime at post-WWII highs (Mullins). But multiple participants note "marginal employment growth" (Jordan) and productivity gains reducing need for hiring. Boehne: "not seeing the kinds of increases in employment" expected. Manufacturing hours at 43 (McTeer). The labor market is tightening but still has slack -- unemployment still well above what would be considered "tight."

Score: 38. Improving but still below full employment. Unemployment at 6.4-6.5% in 1993 is above NAIRU estimates of that era (~5.5-6%).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. 38 indicates below full employment with some slack remaining. Consistent with 6.4-6.5% unemployment.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Very strong signals. Q4 GDP at ~5%, driven by motor vehicles, residential construction, consumer spending. Melzer: "retail and auto sales are very strong." McDonough: forecasting 3% GDP growth for 1994, above Greenbook. Forrestal: "holiday activity has been quite good." Capital goods outlook "brightened." Mullins: "sustainable moderate growth led by business investment, consumer spending on durables, and especially housing." Phillips: "people are voting with their feet by spending, and they are borrowing to do it." Multiple Districts report strong construction, manufacturing pickup. The spending/investment picture is clearly above trend.

Score: 60. Above trend -- "robust" spending led by autos, housing, and beginning capital goods strength. Not "unsustainably rapid" in a broad sense but clearly strong.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. Multiple participants note very low real interest rates (near zero or negative), ample liquidity. Angell: "ample money on the table." Mullins: "real short-term interest rates of approximately zero, if not marginally negative." Lindsey: "real after-tax return on short-term vehicle is now about minus 1-1/2 percent." Phillips: "financial sector is certainly well positioned to support growth." McDonough: "financial services firms... doing very well." Banks report strengthening loan demand. Long rates have backed up ~50bp recently (Broaddus, Phillips note this).

Score: 22. Loose conditions -- very accommodative with near-zero real rates, strong financial sector, expanding credit. The recent backup in long rates is modest and doesn't change the overall loose picture.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Very loose conditions = low score. 22 is appropriate.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: This is the critical dimension. The meeting voted for "B symmetric" -- no change in the 3% funds rate. However, the overwhelming sentiment is that tightening is coming soon. Speakers advocating immediate action: Broaddus (explicit 25bp hike now), Angell (50bp now), Melzer (increase pressure now), Lindsey (25bp now), Phillips (preference to tighten now). Speakers favoring hold but expecting imminent tightening: Mullins (strong case for moving now, accepts B symmetric), McDonough (convinced we must firm "early part of next year"), Jordan (agrees symmetric but wants to move ahead of strong data), McTeer (close call, prepared to follow Chairman). Speakers favoring patience: Greenspan (hold now, move "fairly early in the year"), Boehne ("wait-and-see" but days numbered), Forrestal (wait a little longer), Kelley (wait and see), LaWare (premature to move now), Stern (close call, not persuasive to move now). Even the "hold" camp explicitly says accommodation must end soon.

This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish meeting. No one argues for easing. The debate is between "tighten now" and "tighten very soon." I score this 58. The current action is hold, but the forward lean is clearly toward tightening, and a significant minority wanted to move immediately.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: The vote was unanimous for "B symmetric" -- no formal dissents. However, at least 5 participants (Broaddus, Angell, Melzer, Lindsey, Phillips) explicitly stated they would prefer to tighten now, with Angell wanting 50bp. They acquiesced to the Chairman's proposal but made their preferences clear. This is a case of zero formal dissents but multiple "reluctant" supporters.

Score: 40. No formal dissents but distinct camps -- a "move now" group of ~5 vs. a "wait" group. The rubric says "zero dissents but 4-5 reluctant supporters can score 40-50."

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Very little direct discussion of financial stability risks. Lindsey mentions asset re-diversification into speculative OTC stocks and real estate. Hoenig mentions some "boom-like conditions" and "speculation in residential real estate." Mullins briefly notes stock market sideways for 6 months and that "economic outlook has caught up with the market." Phillips mentions long-rate backup showing "deterioration in inflation expectations." These are passing references, not sustained financial stability discussion.

Score: 15. Brief mentions, mostly in passing. Consistent with 1993 era anchor of 1-10, slightly above given Lindsey's more detailed discussion of asset price behavior.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- tightening is coming. Greenspan: "the days of accommodation have got to be over... I find it very difficult to believe that we can go very long into 1994 without starting the process." He envisions "not a single upward adjustment but a whole succession of them." McDonough: "absolutely convinced that we have to bring monetary policy into a neutral stance in the early part of next year." Boehne: "days of this degree of accommodation are numbered. It's likely to be sooner rather than later." Virtually everyone signals tightening ahead. This is a strong forward tightening signal.

Score: 68. Clear tightening signal for the near future. The entire committee signals a cycle of rate increases is about to begin.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Moderate uncertainty. Boehne: "we just don't know" whether this is a true takeoff. Syron: "we simply don't know what's going to happen." Kelley: "terribly close call." Stern: "it gives me a little pause." McDonough: "not yet sufficiently certain of that forecast." But many others express considerable confidence in the expansion's durability (Mullins: "two years of 3 percent growth is enough"). The uncertainty is mainly about timing of policy action, not the fundamental economic trajectory.

Score: 35. Somewhat elevated but not extreme. Standard uncertainty about first-quarter dynamics and whether Q4 strength is sustainable, but broad confidence in the expansion's durability.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (45) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38): Both below 50, consistent with an economy still below potential with labor slack remaining, though closing. The slight divergence (labor more slack than output) reflects the "jobless recovery" quality -- output recovering faster than employment. This is coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action, which makes sense -- the current decision was hold, but the forward signal was strongly toward tightening. The divergence is explained and appropriate.

### Speaker Count Summary
- Favor immediate tightening: Broaddus, Angell, Melzer, Lindsey, Phillips (~5)
- Favor hold now but tighten very soon: Greenspan, McDonough, Jordan, Mullins, McTeer, Hoenig, Forrestal, Boehne, Syron, Parry (~10)
- Favor patience/caution: Kelley, LaWare, Stern (~3)
- No one favors easing.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FORRESTAL: "We see very little evidence of price pressures. The anecdotal information supports that idea that price increases are very difficult to obtain even in this more positive atmosphere."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I keep hearing over and over how difficult it is to raise prices and that the only way to protect margins is still on the cost side."
- MR. MELZER: "When these are dropped the CPI increased at a 3 percent rate during the first nine months of the year and at a more troubling 3.6 percent rate during the past two months."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "There are some very worrisome signs on the horizon, however, with respect to inflation: the general acceptance of 3 percent as the right number... the price of gold, which has been elevated now for quite a while."

Center-of-gravity: Current inflation is contained with difficulty raising prices, but running around 3% core CPI -- above price stability. Forward-looking concerns exist from a hawkish minority but the majority emphasizes muted current pressures.

Minority views: Melzer and Angell see more inflation risk from monetary aggregates and commodity prices; Stern and Syron are more optimistic on the price outlook.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "It wouldn't be surprising to see labor market slack... the gap between potential and actual GDP may be diminishing at a faster rate than is anticipated."
- MR. MULLINS: "Capacity utilization: at the end of the second quarter of '92 it was 79-1/2; now it's 83. The peak in the last cycle in the late '80s was 84.8."
- MR. MELZER: "All indications are that output growth would be at or above potential next year."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: notes the expansion is sustainable but industrial production gains suggest some inventory effect.

Center-of-gravity: Economy is slightly below potential but closing the gap rapidly. Q4 GDP at ~5% is above trend, but the level of output has not yet reached potential.

Minority views: Melzer sees the economy at or approaching potential; LaWare and Kelley emphasize remaining drags that will moderate growth.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "Unemployment in the summer of '92 was 7.7 percent; it's down to 6.4 percent now... The factory workweek and factory overtime both stand at post-World War II highs."
- MR. JORDAN: "Through all of this, though, we've only had what we would call marginal employment growth. Anecdotally, most manufacturing-type businesses report very substantial increases in productivity."
- MR. BOEHNE: "We are not seeing the kinds of increases in employment that appear to be the case in other Districts."

Center-of-gravity: Labor market improving but still below full employment. Unemployment at 6.4-6.5% with slack remaining, though factory hours at highs suggest tightening in manufacturing.

Minority views: Mullins notes factory hours at post-WWII highs suggesting more tightness; Jordan emphasizes marginal employment growth suggesting more slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "A traditional pattern of interest-sensitive spending seems to be well entrenched and is fueling sustainable moderate growth led by business investment, consumer spending on durables, and especially housing."
- MR. MELZER: "Retail and auto sales are very strong and most of the other firms we surveyed report increases in sales, orders, and employment."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "People are voting with their feet by spending, and they are borrowing to do it."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Motor vehicles and residential construction driving the acceleration; Q4 GDP ~5%.

Center-of-gravity: Spending and investment are clearly above trend, driven by autos, housing, and improving business investment. The expansion is broad-based.

Minority views: Kelley worries consumer spending is unsustainable given low savings and high debt; LaWare sees dampening forces ahead.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "We've had real short-term interest rates of approximately zero, if not marginally negative, during much of this time."
- MR. ANGELL: "Monetary policy is more accommodative at the current level of rates than it was 17 months ago."
- MR. LINDSEY: "Your real after-tax return on that short-term vehicle is now about minus 1-1/2 percent."
- MR. JORDAN: "Banks say they are getting business loan demand."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose -- near-zero real rates, expanding credit, strong financial sector. Multiple participants describe policy as "very accommodative" or "quite stimulative."

Minority views: Phillips notes backup in long-term rates showing some tightening at the long end, but this is modest.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The days of accommodation have got to be over... I find it very difficult to believe that we can go very long into 1994 without starting the process."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think it is time to... let real short-term interest rates rise... I would favor a 1/4 point increase in the funds rate now."
- MR. ANGELL: "Do 50 and do it now."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I think the days of this degree of accommodation are numbered. It's likely to be sooner rather than later."

Center-of-gravity: Hold steady now but with a strong tightening bias. About 5 participants wanted immediate action; the rest agreed tightening was imminent. No one favored easing.

Minority views: Broaddus, Angell, Melzer, Lindsey, Phillips all favored immediate tightening (would push score to 63-65). LaWare and Stern were more patient (would push toward 50-52).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "I really think we need to move now... I would favor a 1/4 point increase in the funds rate now."
- MR. ANGELL: "I really think we ought to do 50 basis points now."
- MR. LAWARE: "I'm reluctant to take any action prematurely."
- Vote was unanimous for "B symmetric" despite these strong preferences.

Center-of-gravity: No formal dissents, but roughly 5 participants clearly preferred immediate action. The disagreement was about timing, not direction -- all agreed tightening was needed. Unanimous vote masks genuine internal tension.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LINDSEY: Extended discussion of asset re-diversification into OTC stocks, real estate, commodities driven by negative real after-tax returns.
- MR. HOENIG: "We're seeing more speculation in residential real estate."
- MR. MULLINS: Brief note on stock market having gone "essentially sideways for half a year" with economic outlook "filling in underneath the market."

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability was not a major focus. Lindsey's discussion of asset re-diversification was the most substantive mention, but it was framed as a tax/monetary policy issue rather than a systemic stability concern.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Unless this outlook suddenly deteriorates... we would consider moving to that path fairly early in the year... not of a single upward adjustment but a whole succession of them."
- MR. MCDONOUGH: "I'm absolutely convinced that we have to bring monetary policy into a neutral stance in the early part of next year."
- MR. MULLINS: "The time is fast approaching to begin the realignment of policy from the accommodative stance... to a more neutral stance."
- MR. ANGELL: Neutral is "closer to 4 percent than to 3 percent."

Center-of-gravity: Very clear forward tightening signal. The Chairman himself laid out a vision of a series of rate increases beginning early in 1994. Near-unanimous agreement on direction.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BOEHNE: "I don't think we know the answer to the bottom line question: How similar is this to a year ago and how much of a takeoff are we really seeing in the economy?"
- MR. SYRON: "We simply don't know what's going to happen with respect to an issue such as the tax increase and how much people have adjusted their consumption patterns."
- MR. KELLEY: "This choice gets tougher and tougher... this [policy decision] a terribly close call."
- MR. MULLINS: "I think the data have confirmed the durability and sustainability of the economic growth process and I'm getting a bit tired of holding my breath after two years."

Center-of-gravity: Moderate uncertainty, mainly about the timing and pace of growth in early 1994 and when to begin tightening. Broad confidence in the expansion's sustainability but genuine uncertainty about first-quarter dynamics. Not unusually elevated.

But Forrestal, Boehne, Stern all emphasize difficulty raising prices. Mixed picture with commodity pressures emerging but pass-through limited.

Not specifically discussed as a category.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48**
Phillips: "recent backup that we've seen in long-term rates is showing the deterioration in inflation expectations." Angell: gold at $389 is "too high," signals potential inflation. But Stern suggests we may be "closer to [price stability] than we realize" with business people adjusting to few price increases. Mixed -- some deterioration signals but still broadly contained.

**CREDIT_STRESS: N/A**
No discussion of credit stress/spreads per se.

**EQUITY_STRESS: N/A**
Mullins notes stock market sideways but no valuation concern expressed broadly.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
Banks doing well; no stress discussed.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
Not discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: N/A**
Lindsey briefly mentions dollar appreciation might be helpful, but this is not a concern discussed by 2+ participants prominently.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62**

McDonough: "industrial and service firms... are still concentrating on reducing staff and improving productivity." Multiple participants note strong output with limited employment gains, suggesting above-trend productivity.
