### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the December 17–18, 2013 FOMC meeting. It is a segmented, two-day document. Day 1 (Dec 17) contains the economic go-around with individual named speaker statements. Day 2 (Dec 18) contains the policy go-around. Speakers are clearly identified by name (MR. KOCHERLAKOTA, MR. ROSENGREN, MR. EVANS, MR. LOCKHART, MR. FISHER, MR. WILLIAMS, MS. PIANALTO, MR. BULLARD, MR. PLOSSER, MS. GEORGE, MR. LACKER, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, MS. YELLEN, MR. TARULLO, MR. STEIN, MR. POWELL, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE). Approximately 16–17 individual participant voices are present. This is a rich, long transcript — confidence levels can be High for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
December 2013. The recovery from the GFC is underway but uneven. The FOMC is actively debating the "taper" of QE3 (currently $85B/month in asset purchases). Unemployment is at 7.0%; the 6.5% threshold has not yet been crossed. Inflation is well below target (PCE ~0.7% headline, ~1.1% core). The key policy debate is whether to begin tapering asset purchases ("taper") at this meeting vs. waiting for March 2014, and what enhanced forward guidance to attach to any taper. Era anchors: INFLATION_PRESSURE expected ~38-42; OUTPUT_GAP expected ~35-42; LABOR_TIGHTNESS expected ~35-40.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Rosengren: "With PCE inflation at 0.7 percent, we are now further from our publicly stated inflation target of 2 percent than the Bank of Japan is from its publicly stated target."
- Evans: "Total PCE inflation moved down to 0.7 percent in October, year over year, and core PCE inflation remains at 1.1 percent... we are well below this level."
- Kocherlakota: "core and headline inflation have fallen to around 1 percent, and the outlook is that inflation will remain muted. The DSGE models all project that inflation will be below 1.8 percent through 2016."

**Center-of-gravity**: The overwhelming participant consensus is that inflation is well below the 2% target (0.7% headline, 1.1% core), with a muted outlook and multiple speakers raising deflation risk concerns — placing this firmly in the 26-40 disinflation band, at the lower end given the severity of undershoot.

**Minority views**: Fisher is comparatively unconcerned, citing stable trimmed-mean PCE at 1.3% for seven months and arguing deflation risk is mitigated. Dudley also less worried than others. These views inform the upward uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 39 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Yellen: "output growth has not yet moved noticeably above the 2 percent range" and projects "real GDP projected to expand just 2¼ percent."
- Dudley: "we have not, in my view at least, definitively broken out of the 2 percent annualized growth trajectory we've been on for several years."
- Bernanke summary: "somewhat more upbeat picture" but most forecasts "did not make substantial changes."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing at or slightly below potential (~2-2.5%), with most participants forecasting a pickup to ~3% in 2014 but not yet there. Slack remains in most assessments.

**Minority views**: Williams and Bullard are somewhat more optimistic, arguing the drag factors are waning. Lacker is more pessimistic, projecting only ~2% growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 39 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Rosengren: "the unemployment rate, which, at 7 percent, remains above the peak unemployment rate of the previous recession."
- Yellen: "there is still a long way to go" on maximum employment.
- Williams: "I would characterize the marked decline in the unemployment rate since we started our latest asset purchase program as representing substantial progress toward our maximum employment goal" — but still at 7%, well above NAIRU.

**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 7.0% with NAIRU estimated around 5.2-5.5% represents meaningful slack in the labor market. Substantial improvement since September 2012 is acknowledged, but full employment remains distant.

**Minority views**: Kocherlakota argues the employment-to-population ratio for prime-age workers is essentially flat for two years, suggesting the labor market is even weaker than the unemployment rate implies. Tarullo is skeptical of conclusions about structural vs. cyclical participation decline.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 44 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Evans: "The recent increases in retail sales, motor vehicle demand, and sentiment point to a healthy pickup in consumer spending in the current quarter."
- Dudley: "Business fixed investment still seems inexplicably weak relative to the fundamentals. We have a rising stock market, high cash flow, relatively clean balance sheets, and the level of investment is not high enough to really grow capacity at any meaningful pace."
- Lacker: "it's worth noting that business investment and new capital goods orders have been relatively weak of late, and housing has been relatively weak as well."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is picking up modestly from depressed levels, but business capital spending is conspicuously weak and housing has slowed due to higher mortgage rates. The net position is below-to-at trend — approaching the lower end of "at trend."

**Minority views**: Williams and Stein are more upbeat on consumer spending momentum; Lacker remains skeptical about whether the spending pickup is durable.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Fisher: "Sustained low interest rates, abundant credit, improved household and strong corporate finances, and pent-up demand for durable goods are providing the basis for a slight acceleration."
- Fisher: "we are seeing loan tenor, structure, and pricing pressures in our high-growth area...we very clearly see banks rushing in to provide credit."
- Stein: concern about "reaching-for-yield pressures may continue to build" — implying conditions are currently very loose.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose, with low interest rates, abundant credit, competitive bank lending, and rising asset prices. The financial stability concern is precisely that conditions are too easy.

**Minority views**: Tarullo warns that wealth-effect-dependent consumption is vulnerable to any tightening — acknowledging the fragility of current loose conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Majority decision: Alternative B (taper $10B in asset purchases beginning January), supported by ~14 of 17 participants.
- Dudley: "I think the time has come to begin to reduce the pace of asset purchases at this meeting."
- Kocherlakota (dissent): "My recommendation will be that the Committee not taper at this meeting." Rosengren: "My strong preference is to do nothing at this meeting."
- Evans (reluctant): "I can support the current alternative B" as a "third-best policy."

**Center-of-gravity**: The majority favors beginning normalization via a modest $10B taper, marking the first concrete step toward unwinding accommodation. However, the forward guidance is explicitly dovish (rates low "well past" the 6.5% threshold), and several key participants are reluctant or opposed. The action is a lean-hawkish step with strong dovish guardrails.

**Minority views**: 2 clear dissenters (Kocherlakota, Rosengren), 1 reluctant third-choice supporter (Evans), and multiple participants wanting either a larger taper (Plosser, Lockhart, George, Fisher, Lacker) or more certainty in the path.

**Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Kocherlakota: explicitly recommends against tapering; will likely dissent formally.
- Rosengren: "I cannot support the current alternative B."
- Evans: supports B as "third-best policy" only, with significant reservations about financial conditions tightening.
- Tarullo: "my instincts would lean more with Narayana and Eric" but supports B.

**Center-of-gravity**: Meaningful disagreement across multiple dimensions — timing of taper, size of taper, forward guidance design, interpretation of labor slack, and importance of low inflation. Two formal dissents likely; several reluctant supporters. The disagreement extends to fundamental economic questions (Phillips curve, optimal control, how to read the unemployment rate), not just tactics.

**Minority views**: The disagreement is multi-directional — some want more aggressive tapering (Plosser, Lockhart), others oppose any tapering (Kocherlakota, Rosengren).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Stein: extensive presentation on financial stability thresholds, reaching-for-yield, and the "worry index" from 4 to potentially 7-8 if rates stay at zero through 2017-18.
- Bernanke: "the most convincing cost [of QE] was financial stability."
- Kocherlakota: "I am open...to the inclusion of a financial stability knockout clause."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a meaningful but not dominant topic. Stein gives the most substantive presentation; Bernanke explicitly names it as the most compelling cost of continued QE; several others make passing references. It informs but does not dominate the policy discussion.

**Minority views**: Most participants are not alarmed about current conditions; Stein and George are the most emphatic. The concern is prospective (building risks) rather than current stress.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 18 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Yellen: "I think it's appropriate to wait to raise the funds rate target well past the time that unemployment declines below 6½ percent. Under my modal outlook...I consider it appropriate to defer liftoff until late 2015."
- Evans: calls for strengthening the statement to emphasize low inflation monitoring; prefers lowering the unemployment threshold to 6%.
- Alternative B paragraph 5: funds rate to remain low "well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6½ percent" — "especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly dovish — an explicit commitment to holding rates near zero well past the already-low 6.5% unemployment threshold, with inflation referenced as an additional reason to delay liftoff. Multiple participants (Evans, Rosengren, Kocherlakota) want even more dovish guidance. The signal is in the "mild easing bias / low for long" range (8-20 per anchors for 2014 taper era).

**Minority views**: Plosser, George, and Lacker prefer less forward guidance extension or a different formulation. Plosser prefers articulating the reaction function rather than extending qualitative time-based guidance.

**Minority views**: Williams and Fisher are more confident in their respective assessments; Kocherlakota and Rosengren express deeper uncertainty about whether the baseline scenario will materialize.
