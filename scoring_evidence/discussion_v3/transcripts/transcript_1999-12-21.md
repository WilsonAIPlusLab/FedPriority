# FOMC Scoring: 1999-12-21 Transcript

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It is a lengthy go-around with approximately 15+ participants contributing substantive economic remarks, followed by a policy discussion on the directive (symmetric vs. asymmetric) and a lengthy procedural debate about changing disclosure practices (balance-of-risks language). The meeting covers the economic go-around, Chairman Greenspan's extended remarks, and the policy vote.

### Era Context
December 1999: Late-1990s boom. The economy is experiencing exceptionally strong growth, very tight labor markets, a surging stock market (dot-com bubble), strong productivity gains, and remarkably subdued inflation. The Fed had raised rates three times earlier in 1999 (June, August, November) to reverse the 1998 easing. Y2K concerns are the dominant reason for holding steady at this meeting. The consensus expectation is for a rate hike at the February 2000 meeting.

### Core Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently benign but with upside risks. The center of gravity is that core inflation is low (around 2%) but expected to rise modestly. Parry: "moderate inflation," core CPI forecast 2.1% rising to 2.3-2.4%. Hoenig: "core inflation is likely to rise to about 2.25-2.5%." Minehan: "core is rising to fill the gap." McTeer: "supply expanding in tandem with increased demand." Ferguson: "rate of increase in core inflation has moderated." Greenspan: "I see no overheating other than in the stock market. The price indexes are really quite benign." McDonough: "core CPI will start creeping up...hit about 2.8% in 2001." Meyer: "few signs of rising core inflation" but "core inflationary pressures are likely to increase markedly." Multiple participants note the puzzle of tight labor markets not generating wage acceleration. The center of gravity: inflation is currently low but risks are tilted upward. Direction: slightly below midpoint for current conditions but with forward upside risks. Score should be around 48-53, reflecting "modestly below but rising toward target" with acknowledged upside risks.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Participants see low current inflation but rising risks. A score around 50-52 captures "near balanced with slight upside risk."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants overwhelmingly describe the economy as growing very rapidly, above trend/potential. Parry: "rapid growth in economic activity." Guynn: "amazed at the raw strength...economy looks even more resource constrained...on an unsustainable growth path." Minehan: "things seem to have gotten that much hotter...Growth this quarter will be somewhere close to 5 percent." McTeer: "national economy, if anything, seems stronger--and uniformly stronger." Poole: "no significant restraint from any quarter." Greenspan: "There are very evident imbalances in demand over supply." Meyer: "unsustainably rapid pace of growth on top of an already unsustainably high labor utilization rate." Multiple participants reference growth at 4-5% and above-trend demand. However, Greenspan pushes back on calling this "overheating," emphasizing the supply side (productivity) is also expanding rapidly. The center of gravity is clearly above potential but moderated by the productivity narrative. Direction: clearly above midpoint. The late 90s boom anchor is 57-63.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy running well above potential. Score ~60-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Nearly every participant describes labor markets as very tight. Parry: "tight labor markets." Hoenig: "District's unemployment rate is about 3.25%." Jordan: "fast food restaurants...gone to weekends-only policy because they simply do not have the staff." Broaddus: "Labor markets are still very tight." Guynn: "economy looks even more resource constrained now on the labor side." Minehan: "Labor markets are, if anything, tighter." Stern: "Labor markets remain very tight." McTeer: "continued widespread talk about tight labor markets." Moskow: "very tight labor markets and increasing wage pressures." Greenspan discusses the pool of willing workers declining toward zero. The 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75. This is extremely tight by any measure -- unemployment nationally near 4%, districts reporting 3.25% or below, widespread anecdotes of inability to hire.

Direction: well above midpoint. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Overwhelmingly strong. Parry: "rapid growth in economic activity." Broaddus: "Consumer spending remains strong...nominal increases of 5-6%." Guynn: "Christmas buying season...quite strong...High-end items, including expensive jewelry, are reported to be hot." Moskow: "light vehicles market continues to boom." Jordan: "explosion of demand for communications equipment...40% increase in fiber mileage." Minehan: "Stores are packed. The highways are jammed." Greenspan: "if we look across the spectrum of the capital goods markets, there is very little evidence of any weakening." Meyer: "strong momentum in private domestic demand." Direction: well above trend. Score ~67-70.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. All evidence points to very strong spending. Score ~68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants describe financial conditions as quite accommodative despite the three rate hikes. Minehan: "financial market conditions in general seem quite accommodative, notwithstanding our recent tightening." Guynn: "few signs that our previous rate hikes have yet begun to bite except for the moderation in housing." Kelley notes "substantial tightening of recent months that has not yet had its full impact." Greenspan discusses rising BBB corporate bond yields and widening spreads as discount factors rising, but also notes the stock market remains very buoyant. The wealth effect is described as very powerful. Some modest signs of housing slowing from higher rates, but overwhelmingly conditions are described as loose/accommodative. Direction: below midpoint (loose). Score ~28-32.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Participants describe conditions as still quite accommodative/loose. Low score is correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee unanimously holds rates steady, but this is ENTIRELY due to Y2K concerns, not economic assessment. Virtually every participant says further tightening will be needed, likely starting at the February meeting. Guynn: "Were it not for the special year-end considerations this year, I would be trying to develop the case more fully this morning for another snugging policy move." Minehan: "we are in agreement with the staff that policy does need to be tighter...75 basis points or more." Meyer: "unsustainably rapid pace of growth...we have our work cut out for us." Broaddus: "we may be at least a little behind the curve." Greenspan: "the notion that at the end of the day we are going to need to tighten more comes out of any type of evaluation." McDonough: "I don't think that is something that the Committee should wish to see happen." The debate is only about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive -- both options clearly signal tightening ahead. The actual hold is tactical (Y2K), not a reflection of policy preference. Direction: clearly hawkish. The hold-for-Y2K with strong forward tightening signal maps to about 63-68. Not as high as meetings where they actually hike, but the expressed intent is unmistakable.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Clear consensus for further tightening, only delayed by Y2K. Score ~65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote on rates was unanimous for holding steady. The debate was only about the directive language (symmetric vs. asymmetric), which Greenspan himself described as not mattering much. On the economic assessment, there is broad agreement: growth is strong, labor markets are very tight, inflation is benign but risks are upward, more tightening is needed. The main areas of nuance: some (Stern, McTeer) are slightly less worried about inflation; Greenspan emphasizes the supply-side story more. On the disclosure debate, there are 4 dissenters on balance-of-risks language, but that's a procedural matter, not a policy disagreement. Direction: low to moderate disagreement. Score ~20-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants discuss financial stability topics. Parry discusses the wealth effect from IPOs and stock market extensively. Greenspan devotes much of his extended remarks to the stock market, wealth effect, equity valuations, discount factors, and the unsustainability of the wealth effect. Moskow: "concerned about high valuation levels for the stock market, particularly for the Internet stocks." McDonough discusses the external deficit. Minehan mentions the stock market continuing "to surprise on the upside" and conditions being "quite accommodative." The wealth effect and stock market valuation are significant recurring themes. Credit quality issues mentioned briefly by Guynn. Score ~40-48. Multiple participants address it meaningfully but it doesn't dominate the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. Both versions of the press release "are saying, in effect, that we are very likely to move rates at the next meeting" (Greenspan). Minehan: "policy does need to be tighter...75 basis points or more...and when." Meyer: "next year will be an especially key one for monetary policy." The symmetric directive was described as "the most hawkish symmetric language any human being has ever seen" (McDonough). Direction: clearly hawkish forward signal. Score ~70-73.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence clearly points to further tightening expected. Score ~72.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Several participants note uncertainty about specific elements. Moskow: "several key aspects of the outlook are quite uncertain...can't predict with any confidence the future path of stock prices...don't really know whether productivity growth will slow." Stern: "I have a lot less conviction about the inflation outlook." Gramlich discusses fundamental uncertainties about labor markets, stock market, dollar. Parry: "significant risks on both sides." But overall, the tone is more confident than uncertain -- the expansion is strong, labor markets are clearly tight, and the path forward (more tightening) seems relatively clear. Y2K is a specific near-term uncertainty. Direction: somewhat elevated but not extremely uncertain. Score ~35-40.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Both hawkish -- coherent. Forward guidance is slightly more hawkish because the hold at this meeting was purely tactical for Y2K.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "recent data continue to show the rapid growth in economic activity and moderate inflation...core CPI from 2.1 percent this year to 2.3 and 2.4 percent in the next two years"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I see no overheating other than in the stock market. The price indexes are really quite benign."
- MR. MOSKOW: "the deceleration in our price measures has ended. Indeed, it seems likely that price and wage movements will worsen in the year ahead"
- MR. FERGUSON: "rate of increase in core inflation has moderated even a bit more"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is currently near target and benign, but the overwhelming consensus is that risks are tilted upward -- nearly all participants expect some acceleration in core inflation in 2000-2001 given the tight labor markets and strong demand, placing this just above the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: McTeer and Stern are somewhat less worried about inflation risks; Minehan and McDonough see inflation rising more meaningfully toward 2.8-3%. This widens the band somewhat.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MINEHAN: "things seem to have gotten that much hotter in the overall economy. Growth this quarter will be somewhere close to 5 percent."
- MR. GUYNN: "the economy looks even more resource constrained now on the labor side and is likely on an unsustainable growth path"
- MR. MEYER: "unsustainably rapid pace of growth on top of an already unsustainably high labor utilization rate"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "There are very evident imbalances in demand over supply" (while also emphasizing supply-side expansion)

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is clearly operating above potential with GDP growth near 5%, widespread resource constraints, and demand outpacing supply. Greenspan's supply-side emphasis provides some moderation but does not override the consensus that the economy is running hot.

**Minority views**: Greenspan argues the demand strength is matched by supply-side gains and cautions against calling it "overheating," which slightly lowers the center of gravity from where it might otherwise be.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "some fast food restaurants in central Ohio have now gone to a weekends-only policy because they simply do not have the staff to operate in the middle of the week"
- MR. HOENIG: "The District's unemployment rate is about 3.25 percent. Except for New Mexico and Wyoming, all District states have unemployment rates below 3.25 percent"
- MR. GUYNN: "The major threat to continuation of strong growth in our region is the availability of labor...the pool of additional workers has dried up"
- MR. STERN: "Employment gains in the District have slowed; that appears to reflect mostly just a lack of unemployed workers"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are very tight by virtually every measure -- unemployment well below 4% nationally, districts reporting 3.25% or lower, widespread anecdotes of inability to hire across sectors from fast food to construction to high-tech. This is one of the tightest labor markets in the postwar period.

**Minority views**: McTeer notes retailers surprisingly found adequate holiday workers; wage pressures remain moderate despite tightness. This prevents a score above 75.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "Consumer spending during the important Christmas buying season is now reported to be quite strong...High-end items, including expensive jewelry, are reported to be hot"
- MR. JORDAN: "'explosion' of demand for communications equipment...budgeting for a 40 percent increase in fiber mileage"
- MR. BROADDUS: "Consumer spending remains strong. Our retail contacts are looking for nominal increases in sales over last year in the range of 5 to 6 percent"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if we look across the spectrum of the capital goods markets, there is very little evidence of any weakening"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment are running well above trend. Holiday retail sales are very strong, auto sales are booming, high-tech investment is surging with massive increases in telecom/Internet equipment, and capital goods orders remain strong across the board.

**Minority views**: Some modest slowing in housing noted by several participants, and Guynn's contacts show some caution in manufacturing investment plans. But these are overwhelmed by the broad strength.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MINEHAN: "financial market conditions in general seem quite accommodative, notwithstanding our recent tightening"
- MR. GUYNN: "few signs that our previous rate hikes have yet begun to bite except for the moderation in housing...higher real rates may be necessary to get a comparable degree of monetary restraint"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: discusses rising BBB yields and widening spreads, but also notes the stock market remains extremely buoyant and the wealth effect is powerful
- MR. KELLEY: "substantial tightening of recent months that has not yet had its full impact"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite three rate hikes in 1999, financial conditions remain quite accommodative. The stock market is surging, the wealth effect is powerful, credit is available, and most participants see little restraint from higher rates except modest housing slowing. Some tightening in bond markets (rising yields, wider spreads) provides partial offset.

**Minority views**: Greenspan's discussion of rising discount factors and BBB spreads suggests some tightening is beginning in credit markets, which widens the band upward.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "Were it not for the special year-end considerations this year, I would be trying to develop the case more fully this morning for another snugging policy move"
- MS. MINEHAN: "we are in agreement with the staff that policy does need to be tighter to keep inflation from rising above 3 percent...75 basis points or more"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the notion that at the end of the day we are going to need to tighten more comes out of any type of evaluation"
- MR. BROADDUS: "we think the risks are on the upside and that we may be at least a little behind the curve"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The Committee is clearly hawkish, with near-universal consensus that further tightening is needed and likely beginning at the February meeting. The hold at this meeting is entirely due to Y2K concerns, not dovish sentiment. The symmetric directive was chosen only to avoid disturbing markets during the Y2K period.

**Minority views**: Hoenig argues the effects of prior tightening need time to play out; McTeer emphasizes virtuous supply-side dynamics. But even these participants acknowledge tightening is likely needed.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- The vote to hold rates was unanimous.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I assume that nobody here wants to raise rates at this time. At least I didn't hear that view expressed."
- The symmetric vs. asymmetric debate was described by Greenspan as: "I don't think our decision on this issue today matters one way or the other."
- Broad agreement on economic assessment and forward policy direction.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: This is a meeting with very broad consensus on both the economic assessment and the forward policy direction. The only debate -- symmetric vs. asymmetric directive -- was about communication tactics during Y2K, not substance. The lengthy disclosure-procedure debate (Option 1 vs. Option 2) is procedural, not a policy disagreement.

**Minority views**: Stern and McTeer are slightly less worried about inflation than others, creating minor differences in emphasis but not in policy prescription.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: Extensive discussion of wealth effect from IPOs and stock market; "a collapse in market values would impose obvious downside risks"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of wealth effect, equity valuations, discount factors, bubble risk; "the wealth effect cannot continue to stimulate excess demand indefinitely"
- MR. MOSKOW: "I continue to be concerned about high valuation levels for the stock market, particularly for the Internet stocks"
- MR. GUYNN: mentions credit quality problems in health care lending

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability concerns -- particularly the stock market bubble, wealth effect, and equity valuations -- receive meaningful discussion from several participants and dominate Greenspan's extended remarks. However, these are discussed primarily as a macroeconomic risk channel (wealth effect driving demand) rather than as a systemic stability concern per se. Credit conditions receive limited attention.

**Minority views**: Most participants outside of Greenspan, Parry, and Moskow mention financial conditions only in passing.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "both of these drafts are saying, in effect, that we are very likely to move rates at the next meeting"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: [describing the symmetric version] "the most hawkish symmetric language any human being has ever seen"
- MR. POOLE: "the Committee is clearly anticipating...that we will make a policy move in February"
- MS. MINEHAN: "policy does need to be tighter...The questions are by how much--75 basis points or more--and when"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is unmistakably hawkish. Both the symmetric and asymmetric draft statements signal a rate hike at the February meeting. The debate is entirely about communication tactics during Y2K, not about the direction of future policy. Several participants discuss the need for 75+ basis points of additional tightening over the coming year.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully dissent from the forward tightening signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 37 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "several key aspects of the outlook are quite uncertain...we can't predict with any confidence the future path of stock prices...we don't really know whether productivity growth will slow, remain high, or continue to accelerate"
- MR. STERN: "I have a lot less conviction about the inflation outlook"
- MR. GRAMLICH: discusses fundamental uncertainties in labor markets, stock market, and dollar
- MR. PARRY: "significant risks on both sides of the forecast"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty about specific factors -- productivity trends, stock market trajectory, inflation dynamics -- but the overall tone is relatively confident. The economic expansion is strong and broadly recognized, the policy direction is clear, and the main uncertainties are about magnitudes rather than directions. Y2K adds a specific near-term uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Stern expresses the most uncertainty about inflation; Greenspan raises deep uncertainty about measurement and the nature of supply-side gains.
