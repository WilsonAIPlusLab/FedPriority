# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2001-06-27 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers both the economic go-around and the policy discussion segment. Speakers include: Jordan, Guynn, Moskow, Parry, McTeer, Santomero, Minehan, Stern, Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole, McDonough, Gramlich, Ferguson, Kelley, Meyer, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-attended meeting with extensive discussion -- roughly 17 participants.

### Era Context
June 2001: The U.S. is in the midst of the 2001 recession (later dated March-November 2001 by NBER). The Fed had already cut rates 250bp since January 2001. The tech/telecom bust is ongoing, manufacturing is in recession, but housing and autos remain strong. The tax rebate checks are about to go out. International outlook (Argentina, Brazil, Europe, Japan) is deteriorating.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint -- contained, subdued, not a primary concern currently. Most participants focus on economic weakness, not inflation. However, a notable minority (Jordan, Guynn, Moskow, Hoenig, Poole, Broaddus, Meyer, Kelley) express concern about FUTURE inflation risks from aggressive easing. Current inflation readings are soft: Greenspan notes "core PCE for May is unchanged," "very little pricing power." Parry expects core PCE "just over 2 percent this year." Ferguson says "inflation is unlikely to be an important factor in the near to intermediate term." The center of gravity is that current inflation is contained/slightly below target, but forward-looking inflation risks are noted by a significant minority. Score should reflect CURRENT perceived pressure, which is moderate-low. "Somewhat" below midpoint = 38-42.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says contained/muted currently. Score below 50 is correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as clearly below potential. Growth is "at or below 1 percent for the third consecutive quarter" (Meyer). Economy is "stumbling along" (Guynn). Manufacturing "in recession" (Ferguson). Capacity utilization "nearly matching the lows of 1990-91" (Ferguson). However, Kelley and Greenspan emphasize the LEVEL of activity is still high -- housing strong, autos strong, consumer spending holding up. Meyer explicitly notes "the slowdown in growth began with output above potential" and projects the economy will be "close to full employment" when growth returns. The center of gravity is that the economy is below potential but not deeply so -- a mild/moderate below-potential reading. Several note it's "bumping along the bottom" rather than in freefall.
Bright-line rule: "below potential" language is present from multiple speakers. Score MUST be < 50. Given that it's clearly below potential but not catastrophically so (housing/autos/consumer spending holding), score around 35-38.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential. Score below 50 is correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor markets are loosening but not yet severely slack. Multiple speakers note rising unemployment, layoffs (especially tech/manufacturing), rising initial claims ("mid-400,000 range, levels not too dissimilar from 1991" - Ferguson). However, some sectors still show tightness: healthcare shortages (40% staff openings - Jordan), difficulty hiring urban bank workers, truck drivers. McTeer notes Texas unemployment rose to 4.5%. Moskow reports "lower skilled workers being let go at faster-than-expected pace" while firms try to hold skilled workers. Kelley notes unemployment "rising from its recent historically low level" but may not exceed NAIRU. The center of gravity: labor market clearly loosening but not deeply slack. Still somewhat tight in certain sectors. Score around 38-42 -- below full employment, slack appearing.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Markets are loosening. Score below 50 is correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: A clear split -- consumer spending holding up "reasonably well" but business investment is in "sharp retrenchment." Housing is strong. Autos strong. But business fixed investment, especially high-tech and equipment, is very weak. The NET position is below trend. Guynn: "Consumer spending other than for houses and autos is now more subdued." Retail sales are "sluggish." Investment is the main drag. The combined picture is below trend but not collapsing -- consumer spending is the "mainstay." Score around 33-37.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Net is below trend. Score below 50 is correct.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Mixed signals. Credit is available -- Jordan notes "neither the price nor the availability of credit is a problem" for smaller companies. Poole says "firms that are reasonably creditworthy have no difficulty borrowing." Broaddus says credit markets are "accommodative." Money growth is very high (MZM up 15-20%). However, some tightening: Guynn notes "tighter lending standards," rising bankruptcies, credit quality concerns. Long-term rates have NOT come down despite 250bp of cuts (Poole, Gramlich). Equity markets weak. Minehan says "financial markets seem less than accommodative to excess." The center of gravity: conditions are somewhat loose on the credit side (ample liquidity, easy borrowing) but equity market stress and lack of long-rate response create a mixed picture. Score around 40-45 -- somewhat loose to neutral.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions are generally on the loose side despite some tightening elements. Score below 50 seems right, around 42.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The Committee voted for a 25bp cut (down from 50bp pace). The center of gravity is DOVISH but decelerating. Greenspan proposes 25bp cut with downside risk language. Most support this. McTeer wanted to "continue to reduce" -- more dovish. Poole dissents, preferring no cut or balanced risk statement -- more hawkish. Jordan, Hoenig, Kelley, Stern express reluctance/caution about further easing. Meyer says "a case could be made" for holding. But the majority supports the 25bp ease plus downside risk language. The lean is moderately dovish -- still easing but slowing pace. Score around 33-37.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Majority still favors easing (dovish). Score below 50 is correct.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Notable disagreement. Poole explicitly "does not support the recommendation." Hoenig says he "would have stopped the easing process by now" but can "accept" the recommendation. Jordan hoped for no move. Kelley supports "reluctantly." Stern "does not disagree strongly." On the other side, McTeer and Ferguson favor continued easing with conviction. So there's a range from "we should stop easing" (Poole, Hoenig, Jordan) to "more ease needed" (McTeer, Ferguson) with the majority at "25bp reluctantly." One formal-equivalent dissent (Poole), several reservations. Score around 40-48.
Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Significant range of views. Score around 45.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Moderate attention. Stock market valuations discussed (Poole notes P/E ratios still high, Greenspan discusses earnings expectations). Credit quality mentioned (Guynn on bankruptcies). Long-term rates behavior extensively discussed (Poole, Gramlich). Argentina/Latin America financial instability mentioned by several (Guynn, McTeer). Wall Street firms' problems noted (McDonough on Merrill Lynch). But financial stability is not THE dominant topic -- it's discussed as context, not the central concern. Score around 35-45.
Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate discussion. Score around 38.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is mildly easing but approaching neutral. Greenspan hopes "that will be the end of it." The Committee retains downside risk language but moves to 25bp (from 50bp). Greenspan wants to "signal to the market that we are now in the process of completing our easing actions." Multiple speakers hope "this is our last move." The forward tone is easing-leaning but transitioning toward neutral. Score around 35-40.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. The signal is still mildly dovish (downside risk statement retained) but approaching neutral. Score around 37.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple speakers emphasize unusual nature of this cycle. Poole: "many unique characteristics... decidedly atypical." Greenspan: "we don't in fact know what the distribution of probabilities is." Moskow: "a great deal of uncertainty remains about the outlook." Minehan: "struck by a sense of increasing uncertainty on the downside." Meyer: "high degree of uncertainty about both the sustainable unemployment rate and the rate of trend growth." Gramlich discusses the conflict between output forecasters and bond market forecasters. This is clearly elevated uncertainty -- many participants addressing it, unusual cycle. Score around 58-65.
Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Extensive uncertainty language. Score above 50 is correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, coherent -- economy weakening, labor loosening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (35) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (37): Both dovish-leaning, coherent -- still easing but decelerating.

### Speaker Count by Direction (Policy)
- Clear dovish (more easing): McTeer, Ferguson (~2)
- Support 25bp cut (moderate dovish): Greenspan, McDonough, Broaddus, Minehan, Parry, Gramlich, Santomero, Moskow (~8)
- Reluctant support/near-pause: Kelley, Stern, Jordan, Hoenig, Meyer (~5)
- Against cut (hawkish): Poole (~1)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "Core PCE as estimated by the staff for May is unchanged. The most recent price trends are very soft. There is very little in the way of pricing power in the economy."
- PARRY: "We anticipate increases in the core PCE index of just over 2 percent this year and of just under 2 percent next year."
- FERGUSON: "Inflation is unlikely to be an important factor in the near to intermediate term."
- HOENIG: "Price pressures should be contained for the time being. I recognize that the current stance of policy has the potential to push up the underlying inflation rate as we go forward."
- POOLE: "I think the inflation risks are rising" (minority hawkish view on inflation).

Center-of-gravity: Current inflation readings are soft and contained, running near or slightly below 2% PCE. Most participants see inflation as not a pressing near-term concern, though a significant minority flags forward-looking inflation risks from aggressive easing.

Minority views: Poole, Jordan, Hoenig, Broaddus, and Moskow express concern about future inflation from excessive easing, widening uncertainty band upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MEYER: "Growth appears to be at or below 1 percent for the third consecutive quarter."
- FERGUSON: "Capacity utilization at a level nearly matching the lows of the 1990-91 period."
- KELLEY: "The economy continues to operate at a very high level... Momentum is our problem, not the level of activity."
- GUYNN: "The economy continues to stumble along, with little evidence that the 250 basis points of policy easing... is yet having much noticeable effect."

Center-of-gravity: Economy is clearly below potential with stalled growth, manufacturing in recession, and capacity utilization very low. However, housing, autos, and consumer spending remain at high levels, preventing a deeper reading.

Minority views: Kelley emphasizes the level of activity remains high; several note the unusual nature of the downturn being concentrated in manufacturing/tech.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 39 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- FERGUSON: "Weekly initial claims data point toward ongoing labor market weakness and, at the mid-400,000 range, initial claims are at levels not too dissimilar from those seen in 1991."
- MOSKOW: "Lower skilled workers were being let go at a faster-than-expected pace" while firms try to retain skilled workers.
- JORDAN: "Some hospital health-care organizations report openings in as many as 40 percent of their staff positions."
- MCTEER: "Mass layoffs in Texas rose sharply in May... Nokia will close a Fort Worth plant and lay off 1,500 workers. Texas Instruments will idle two Dallas plants."

Center-of-gravity: Labor markets are loosening materially -- rising layoffs, rising unemployment claims, and easing hiring difficulty. But some sectors (healthcare, certain services) still face shortages, and unemployment has not risen dramatically from low levels.

Minority views: Jordan and others note pockets of continued tightness; Kelley argues unemployment may not exceed NAIRU.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 34 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MEYER: "A sharp retrenchment in high-tech investment" and "continued drag from an incomplete process of inventory correction."
- GUYNN: "The chief financial officer of UPS... told us earlier this week that they are holding off on the purchase of new trucks and airplanes until business resumes."
- BROADDUS: "Investment in both high-tech and in more traditional capital goods is quite weak. But household spending still seems to be holding up."
- MOSKOW: "An increasing number of firms were delaying investment expenditures... Some firms are simply refusing to buy any more computer equipment."

Center-of-gravity: Business investment is in sharp retrenchment -- the primary drag on the economy. Consumer spending is holding up (autos, housing strong; other spending sluggish). The net is clearly below trend, dragged down heavily by the investment collapse.

Minority views: Some note tentative signs of stabilization (corrugated boxes rebound, some manufacturing orders firming).

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- JORDAN: "Neither the price nor the availability of credit is a problem for the smaller companies."
- POOLE: "Credit markets are accommodative. The amount of funds being raised is ample, and firms that are reasonably creditworthy have no difficulty borrowing."
- POOLE: "MZM has grown 15 percent in the last year; it has grown in excess of 20 percent at an annual rate in the last six months."
- GUYNN: "Loan activity has slackened, and that is only partly due to tighter lending standards... credit quality concerns remain."
- GRAMLICH: "Long-term bond markets... as short rates go down, implied forward rates often go up."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall -- ample liquidity, easy credit access, rapid money growth. But long-term rates have not declined, equity markets are weak, and some credit quality deterioration (rising bankruptcies) tempers the looseness.

Minority views: Wide range -- some emphasize extremely loose monetary conditions (Poole on money growth); others note tightening in certain areas (commercial real estate, equity markets).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 35 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "I think we can afford to reduce the rate another 25 basis points. I would hope that will be the end of it."
- MCTEER: "It seems clear that we need to continue to reduce our target funds rate."
- POOLE (dissent): "I do not support the recommendation because I believe that the balance of risks statement will be read by the market as a forecast of more ease to come."
- HOENIG: "If I had my preference, I would have stopped the easing process by now."
- MEYER: "We may be at an important inflection point for monetary policy... at least decelerate the pace of easing."

Center-of-gravity: Majority supports a 25bp cut (down from prior 50bp pace) with downside risk language. This is moderately dovish -- still easing but clearly decelerating. Multiple participants hope this is the last cut.

Minority views: Poole opposes the cut; Hoenig and Jordan would prefer to stop; McTeer and Ferguson lean toward continued easing readiness.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- POOLE: "I do not support the recommendation" (explicit dissent).
- HOENIG: "If I had my preference, I would have stopped the easing process by now."
- KELLEY: "I agree with your suggestion. I must say I do so reluctantly."
- STERN: "I do not disagree strongly with your recommendation" (lukewarm support).
- MCDONNOUGH vs POOLE: Direct disagreement on balance of risks interpretation -- "I think that assessment... is completely wrong."

Center-of-gravity: Notable but not extreme disagreement. One explicit non-supporter (Poole), several reluctant supporters, a sharp exchange between McDonough and Poole on balance-of-risks interpretation. The range spans from "stop easing now" to "continue reducing rates."

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- POOLE: Extended discussion of P/E ratios, earnings expectations, stock market valuation -- "the stock market is still generously priced."
- GREENSPAN: Discussion of earnings expectations being too high, SEC disclosure rules affecting analyst communications.
- GUYNN: Rising bankruptcies (12-26% increase), credit quality concerns, Argentina financial instability risk.
- MCDONOUGH: "The disarray in the major securities firms is continuing... profits warning from Merrill Lynch."

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives moderate attention -- equity market valuation, earnings expectations, Latin American instability, and credit quality are discussed by several participants, but economic weakness and policy are the dominant topics.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "If we move 25 basis points we will signal to the market that we are now in the process of completing our easing actions."
- GREENSPAN: "We ought to indicate in our press statement that we have reduced the rate by 275 basis points since the beginning of the year."
- Balance of risks statement retained toward economic weakness (forward easing bias).
- MEYER: "It makes it easier for us to hold the funds rate constant at the next meeting, while also retaining the flexibility to ease further."

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is mildly dovish -- downside risk language retained, but strong signals of approaching the end of the easing cycle. The 25bp step-down from 50bp is itself a forward signal of deceleration.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- POOLE: "The economic situation we face has many unique characteristics. It is decidedly atypical of U.S. business cycle experience."
- GREENSPAN: "We don't in fact know what the distribution of probabilities is, and I think that we face a degree of risk that we are not able to assess fully."
- MINEHAN: "I've been struck by a sense of increasing uncertainty on the downside."
- MEYER: "A high degree of uncertainty about both the sustainable unemployment rate and the rate of trend growth."
- GRAMLICH: Extended discussion of conflicting signals between output forecasters and bond market forecasters.

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty -- many participants explicitly flag the atypical nature of this cycle, difficulty in forecasting, and wide range of possible outcomes. This goes beyond standard hedging to genuine expressed difficulty in assessing the outlook.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- GREENSPAN: "With inflation expectations if anything falling at this point" and TIPS spread "has come down a good deal recently."
- POOLE: "Inflation expectations are holding steady. That's a vote of confidence in the Committee."
- STERN: "Our responsibility to promote sustained economic progress by keeping inflation and inflationary expectations low."
Anchored near or slightly below target -- participants see expectations as well-behaved.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- GUYNN: Bankruptcies estimated to rise 20-26%; "credit quality concerns remain."
- MINEHAN: "Deterioration in loan portfolios... has begun to taper off and remains confined to large syndicated loans."
- POOLE/JORDAN: Credit readily available for creditworthy borrowers.
Mild stress emerging in specific areas (syndicated loans, telecom) but broadly available credit.

**EQUITY_STRESS: 58 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- POOLE: "The stock market is still generously priced" by conventional measures but earnings expectations are too high.
- GREENSPAN: "Security analysts have earnings per share estimates for the S&P 500 that... reflect a double-digit rise for the third quarter. That just doesn't square with what I have heard."
- MCDONOUGH: "Disarray in major securities firms... profits warning from Merrill Lynch."
Moderate stress/concern -- market still elevated but earnings disappointments loom.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 60 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**
- MEYER: "Both the Board staff and the Boston Fed believe that structural productivity growth will remain elevated relative to the experience from the early '70s to the mid-'90s."
- MEYER: "The Board staff believes that structural productivity growth may now have decelerated" (partial reversal).
- MOSKOW: Academic advisors say "we should be cautious in basing our forecast of productivity trends too heavily on the last several years."
Above historical trend but possibly decelerating from recent highs.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: N/A**
Dollar mentioned briefly (strong dollar hurting exports/textiles) but not prominently discussed by 2+ participants as a concern in itself.

**INFLATION_GOODS: N/A** -- not separately discussed.
**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A** -- not separately discussed (healthcare costs mentioned but not scored as a service inflation dimension by 2+ participants systematically).
**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A** -- not discussed as inflation sub-component.
**BANKING_STRESS: N/A** -- bank examiners "still expressing confidence" (Guynn); not prominently discussed.
**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A** -- not discussed.
