## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the January 30–31, 2018 FOMC meeting. It is a two-day meeting (January 30 and 31) with a structured format: Day 1 includes a special inflation-dynamics discussion (staff presentations followed by participant comments), a financial situation review/Q&A, a financial-stability discussion, and an economic go-round. Day 2 includes a monetary policy discussion and vote. Speakers are individually identified (e.g., MR. WILLIAMS., MS. MESTER., VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY., CHAIR YELLEN.). This is a very long, richly substantive transcript—well over 200 pages.

### 2. Era Context
January 2018 is the late phase of the post-GFC expansion. The unemployment rate is at 4.1%, near the lowest since December 2000. GDP grew 2½% in 2017. Core PCE inflation ran 1.5% in 2017—persistently below the 2% target. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act was just enacted, and the FOMC is calibrating its implications. This is Chair Yellen's final meeting; Jerome Powell is the incoming Chair. The Fed has been in a gradual hiking cycle since December 2015. This is broadly an era of: tight labor market, below-target inflation, above-trend growth, and a debate about how much more to tighten.

### 3. Directional Assessment per Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants broadly agree inflation is *below* the 2% target. Core PCE was 1.5% in 2017. Multiple speakers cite this: Brainard ("Core PCE prices rose only 1½ percent"), Powell ("inflation has disappointed, continuing to fall short of our 2 percent objective"), Kashkari ("inflation does remain low"), Evans ("incoming data generally have been positive and hint that we might be at the beginning of a turnaround. But this is far from definitive"). However, there is optimism that it will move up: Mester projects "inflation will reach our 2 percent goal on a sustained basis in the first half of 2019," Williams expects it to reach 2% "by the middle of next year," and Rosengren and Dudley anticipate firming. Bullard is skeptical, arguing inflation has been below target since 2012. The center of gravity is: **below target, with modest recent improvement and expectations of gradual rise**. This is clearly below 50 on the inflation-pressure scale. Given the anchor that 38–42 corresponds to 2013–19 below-target period, and given some more recent modest uptick and expectations of rise, a score around 40–42 is appropriate.

Direction: below target, slightly below midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants consistently describe the economy as growing **above trend** and **at or beyond full employment**, with the unemployment rate at 4.1% and expected to fall below 4%. Multiple speakers use "above-trend" language explicitly: Williams says "economy is growing at an above-potential rate," Mester says "output growth firmly above trend," Brainard says the economy is "at full employment" absorbing fiscal stimulus, Rosengren cites Tealbook expecting unemployment to reach 3.4%, and Dudley says "economy will continue to grow at an above-trend pace." Yellen says "After a few additional hikes, short-term rates will only have to move up..." Kashkari is the most skeptical, arguing potential LFP may still be below full and there may be remaining slack, but even he acknowledges above-trend growth.

Bright-line rule: "above potential/above trend" language → score MUST be > 50. Multiple participants use exactly this language. The economy is projected to overshoot full employment meaningfully (unemployment toward 3.2–3.6%), which is above the 50–55 "near potential" range. Score: around 58–62.

Direction: above potential, but not dramatically overheating yet.

With the labor market already this tight AND expected to tighten further to sub-4%, and widespread anecdotal reports of worker shortages, score is around 63–67.

Direction: tight and tightening further, clearly above NAIRU.

Rubric: 16–30 = normal, standard caveats; 31–45 = somewhat elevated. ### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 41 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 59 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

- Mester: "I expect above-trend growth will continue this year...With positive momentum, healthy underlying fundamentals, accommodative financial conditions, fiscal stimulus, and improving economies abroad."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 65 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants universally describe a tight and tightening labor market, with the 4.1% unemployment rate at a 17-year low and expected to fall further to 3.2–3.6%; widespread reports of worker shortages and wage pressures, although aggregate wage growth remains modest.

**Minority views:** Kashkari and Brainard note the prime-age employment-to-population ratio remains more than 1 percentage point below pre-crisis peaks, suggesting some residual slack; Kashkari explicitly argues labor slack "may still remain."
