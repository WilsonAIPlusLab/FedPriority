## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the April 26–27, 2011 FOMC meeting. Speakers are individually identified (MR. EVANS, MR. PLOSSER, MS. YELLEN, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, etc.). The transcript is fully segmented. The document is exceptionally long (~244 pages) and covers three major sessions: (a) an exit strategy discussion/go-around, (b) an economic conditions go-around, and (c) a policy discussion/go-around. All three are scored collectively as participant views.

**Note on Hoenig**: President Hoenig's actual remarks are largely absent from the decomposed file — his speaking turns appear as "Thank you. President Hoenig." followed by nothing. Historical context and the transcript's internal references (e.g., Plosser citing "our colleague Tom Hoenig has been saying that even after exit begins, policy will remain very accommodative for some time"; Fisher noting he "was tempted to embrace alternative C and to dissent at this meeting; I will not do so") strongly imply Hoenig dissented at this meeting, consistent with his dissent at every 2011 meeting. His views are not scored directly (no textual evidence) but the dissent is noted for DISSENT_INTENSITY and flagged.

### 2. Era Context
April 2011: QE2 nearing completion ($600B program, ending June 2011). Unemployment around 8.8%. Core PCE inflation approximately 0.9–1.2% year-over-year, but headline PCE surging above 3% annualized due to energy/oil (+27 since January) and commodity prices. Fed funds rate at 0–0.25%, "extended period" language in effect. ECB has begun hiking; Fed on hold. First-quarter GDP tracking weak (~1.7% annualized). Recovery ongoing but facing headwinds: oil prices, housing distress, fiscal drag ahead, European sovereign debt stress. The era anchor for OUTPUT_GAP is 35–42 (2010–14 recovery zone). INFLATION_PRESSURE should reflect: headline inflation well above target but core/underlying near 0.9–1.2%; majority sees it as transitory but significant minority sees persistent risks.

### 3. Directional Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants perceive ABOVE target current conditions. Headline PCE running at ~3–5% annualized, oil prices the main driver. Core PCE approximately 0.9–1.2%, but accelerating. Most participants (Evans, Williams, Rosengren, Yellen, Bernanke) argue inflation is elevated but transitory, with underlying measures still low. A vocal minority (Fisher, Lacker, Plosser, Bullard, Kocherlakota) emphasizes upside risks, pass-through concern, and rising inflation expectations. The CENTER OF GRAVITY: headline clearly elevated; core/underlying still subdued but rising; risks predominantly to upside. This is genuinely elevated current inflation with moderate persistence concern. Score: above 50, reflecting the actual near-term inflation situation (headline elevated, upside risks) while accounting for most members' view that underlying/medium-term stays below 2%. Score approximately 62–65.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Recovery under way but well below potential. Chairman Bernanke: "total hours of work is still about 6 percent below pre-recession levels." Yellen: "I expect the output gap and unemployment to decline, but slowly, so both will be substantially higher than normal levels at the end of the forecast horizon." Rosengren: "GDP growth over the past four quarters has averaged only 2.8 percent, just slightly above potential." Bernanke's Taylor rule calculation: output gap approximately –5 percent using NAIRU ~6%. Nearly every speaker confirms significant negative output gap. This is firmly BELOW potential. Score: 33–37 (below potential but recovering, consistent with 2010–14 recovery anchor of 35–42).

However, the discussion includes significant hawkish voices (Plosser wants to signal exit sooner, Lacker supports B but would be comfortable with C, Bullard wants to stop $100B short, Kocherlakota notes policy rules call for rate increases by fall 2011). This is the QE era / near-peak accommodation — 15-25 range is appropriate for participants actively debating more QE vs. hold. Additionally, there is substantial disagreement between those viewing current inflation as transitory and manageable (Evans, Rosengren, Williams, Yellen, Raskin, Tarullo, Lockhart, Rosengren) vs. those seeing genuine upside risk requiring action (Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, Bullard, Kocherlakota). ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 63 ±8 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- MR. FISHER: "What was previously a faint theme is now a very loud, uniform drumbeat on two fronts... the first is that cost inflation is pushing forward price increases in all sectors beyond energy and basic commodities... Every CEO I talk to, large or small, public or private, is now budgeting for and managing to inflation."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "The staff estimate of the 12-month change in core PCE for this month is still about 0.9; market-based core is 0.9. The Dallas trimmed mean PCE is 1.0, and the core CPI is 1.2." — Then: "headline PCE inflation drops from 4.56 to 2.39" excluding gasoline/fuel oil.
- MS. YELLEN: "measures of underlying inflation remain well below the 2 percent level I consider consistent with our dual mandate... the market-based core PCE price index has risen less than 1 percent over the past year."
- MR. LACKER: "Price growth picked up in both our manufacturing and services surveys this time, and is at or approaching the all-time highs for those series that they reached in 2008."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "inflation risks seem to the upside."

**Center of gravity**: Headline inflation clearly elevated (4%+ annualized recently) but underlying/core near 1%. Majority sees this as transitory; hawkish minority sees real pass-through risk. The committee perceives CURRENT inflation above target (headline) with substantial upside risk, offset by still-low underlying measures. Score of 63 reflects: above target on current headline, upside risks, but not a "clearly above target / broadening pressures / concerning" scenario at the core level (that would be 65–75). The transitory view holds center, but just barely.

**Minority view**: Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, Bullard see more persistent and broader inflation threat — would push toward 70+.
