## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the August 5, 2008 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (MR. LOCKHART, MS. YELLEN, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE, etc.). The document covers two rounds: the economic go-around and the policy discussion. It is well-segmented and very long (~145 pages).

Staff content (Greenbook references, David Stockton, Brian) is present but participants are citing staff material within their own remarks; participant views are clearly attributable. I score only participant-attributable content.

### 2. Era Context
August 2008 — mid-GFC. The financial crisis began in August 2007; Bear Stearns was rescued in March 2008; IndyMac failed in July 2008; the GSEs (Fannie/Freddie) were under severe stress. The federal funds rate had been cut to 2%. Oil prices had recently peaked and were pulling back from ~$147/bbl. Headline CPI was running near 5%; core PCE had risen modestly. The economy was growing weakly (~1.9% Q2 2008 GDP) but not officially in recession. Unemployment was rising (5.7% by July). This is a meeting of extreme dual-mandate tension: participants simultaneously worried about recession/financial collapse AND inflation/expectations unanchoring. This context informs the anchor sanity checks but does NOT override what the text actually says.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 67 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FISHER: "we appear to be transitioning from vigilance on the price front to acquiescence. Inflation expectations are, I think, becoming unmoored." Also: "the percentage of small business owners citing inflation as 'the number one problem they face,' rose to 20 percent in June, the highest rating since 1982."
- MR. BULLARD: "The headline CPI inflation rate is running close to 5 percent measured from one year earlier... The June CPI annualized inflation rate was a 1970s-sounding 13.4 percent."
- MR. PLOSSER: "year-over-year core PCE inflation has exceeded 2 percent every month but one since April 2004. That is four years."
- MS. YELLEN (moderating view): "inflation risks obviously remain. Even with the recent decline, energy prices are well above year-ago levels and are not only pushing up headline inflation but also spilling, to some extent, into core."
- MR. KOHN (somewhat reassuring): "there's no evidence or very little evidence that it's spilling over into other prices in the economy" — but acknowledges "upside risks still prevail."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually all participants agree inflation is substantially above target (CPI near 5%, core PCE above 2% for years), with the debate focused on whether it is persistent/broadening (hawkish camp) or transitory pass-through with contained expectations (dovish camp); the center clearly sits in the "clearly above target / broadening pressures" zone.

**Minority views:** Yellen, Mishkin, Rosengren argue the inflationary process has not yet spilled into wages/core in a sustained way, pulling the band down; Fisher/Lacker/Plosser argue it is more entrenched, pulling up.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GOVERNOR KOHN: "the incoming information strongly suggests that we are on a trajectory that at least for some time will have the economy growing appreciably below the growth rate of its potential."
- MS. YELLEN: "our forecast for real GDP growth for the second half of the year [is] just ¾ percent" and projects "a correspondingly higher unemployment rate."
- MR. MISHKIN: "we are going to have subpotential growth for quite a period of time."
- MR. BERNANKE: "the best guess is for a slow second half, a slow beginning of 2009, and an unemployment rate that continues to rise from here."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous agreement that the economy is operating below potential and will remain so for multiple quarters; no participant argues above-potential growth; the magnitude is described as "weak" or "near-recessionary" but not catastrophic, placing this in the 35-40 zone.

**Minority views:** Bullard is "not as pessimistic as most" on the labor market dimension and notes Q2 growth was 1.9%, suggesting some resilience, but still acknowledges below-potential trajectory.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ROSENGREN: "the unemployment rate has already reached 5.7 percent... payroll employment declining for the past six months... significant risk that the second half of this year would look a lot like the 'severe financial stress' or 'typical recession' scenarios."
- MS. YELLEN: "there is already significant slack in the labor market... high unemployment is suppressing wage gains."
- GOVERNOR KOHN: "continuing declines in employment and no sign of near-term strengthening in the initial claims data... you need another 150,000 or 200,000 jobs rather than minus 60,000."
- MR. BERNANKE: "the unemployment rate has risen as quickly as in previous episodes, and any look at the unemployment rate would suggest that this is something close to a normal recession dynamic."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Rising unemployment (5.7% and climbing), six months of job losses, and widespread reference to "slack" in the labor market place this firmly in below-full-employment territory; wage growth remains subdued, consistent with labor slack.

**Minority views:** Bullard notes job losses are milder than in past recessions; Hoenig notes Tenth District still adding jobs; Fisher notes Dallas area employment still growing, but these are regional/relative observations, not a challenge to the national assessment of slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is weakening (rebate-check boost fading, auto sales plunging, housing wealth declining), business investment is being marked down, and the overall picture is one of below-trend spending on both dimensions; the rubric's 31-45 "sluggish/cautious consumers" range is appropriate.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 73 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GOVERNOR KOHN: "despite the downward movement in Treasury interest rates and in the expected federal funds rate path, financial conditions for households and businesses have tightened since the last FOMC meeting... Mortgage interest rates have actually risen on balance, as have corporate bond yields across many risk categories."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous agreement that financial conditions have tightened materially since the last meeting; credit spreads are elevated across all categories; lending standards are tightened; the GSE disruption is worsening mortgage conditions; this is clearly in the "tight/restrictive" range, though not yet a full credit freeze (Sept 2008 level).

**Minority views:** Plosser argues spreads reflect appropriate credit-risk pricing rather than market dysfunction; Bullard argues systemic risk has "dropped dramatically"; but neither argues conditions are loose or neutral.
