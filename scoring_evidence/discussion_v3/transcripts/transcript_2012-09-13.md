# FOMC Transcript Scoring — September 12–13, 2012

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of a two-day FOMC meeting (September 12–13, 2012). Individual speakers are identified by name (MR. LACKER, MR. PLOSSER, MR. FISHER, MR. BULLARD, MR. WILLIAMS, MR. ROSENGREN, MR. KOCHERLAKOTA, MR. LOCKHART, MS. PIANALTO, MS. GEORGE, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, MS. YELLEN, MR. TARULLO, MR. STEIN, MR. POWELL, MS. DUKE, MS. RASKIN, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE). The meeting covers: (a) a staff briefing on LSAPs and financial developments; (b) discussion on the consensus forecast project; (c) the economic go-round (September 12 afternoon); and (d) the policy go-round (September 13 morning). Scoring draws on the participant sections — the go-round and policy discussions — not the staff presentations.

### 2. Era Context
September 2012 is mid-recovery from the GFC. The economy has been growing at roughly 2% per year since the trough. Unemployment is 8.1–8.3%. Inflation is below target. The Fed is at the zero lower bound. This meeting launches QE3 (open-ended MBS purchases at $40 billion/month under Alternative B). The era places this squarely in the QE era, which the rubric anchors at 15–25 for HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN.

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants across the spectrum describe inflation as below target and not an immediate threat. Rosengren: "inflation is quite low." Fisher: "everybody agrees at the table that inflation is not an immediate or presently foreseeable threat." Pianalto: "deflation is not a serious risk" (but also not elevated inflation). Williams: "inflation will return toward 2 percent faster." Most participants see inflation well below target. Kocherlakota notes "modestly more inflationary pressures" than the consensus. Plosser sees "more inflationary pressures" but is an outlier. Lacker projects inflation "closer to 2 percent over the next year or two." George raises concern about inflation expectations rising from LSAP. Fisher notes breakeven inflation rates are rising. Center of gravity: inflation is below target but not deeply deflationary — this is the 2012 QE era where PCE was running near or slightly below 2%. Score direction: BELOW midpoint. The mainstream view is below target but not in deflation territory. Roughly 35–42 range per anchors for 2013–19 below target.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Multiple participants describe the economy as operating well below potential. Lacker: "We're in a period of sluggish economic growth." The staff model (EDO) estimates a gap near 3 percent (discussed by Lacker in the go-round). Williams: "progress toward our policy goals would remain stalled" without stimulus. Rosengren: unemployment is "quite high," "progress toward our mandate has stalled." Plosser: "The recovery is frustratingly slow. The unemployment rate is uncomfortably and tragically high." Bernanke: "very serious problems with unemployment are not improving." GDP growth has averaged ~2.2%, below trend. Fiscal cliff and Europe are significant downside risks. The bright-line rule says "below potential / slack remains" → score MUST be < 50. This is 2012, consistent with the 35–42 anchor range for 2010–14 recovery. Score: ~35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Unemployment is 8.1–8.3% at the time of the meeting. Participants repeatedly call this "uncomfortably high," "quite high," "far from full employment." Rosengren: "unemployment is quite high." Plosser: "unemployment rate is uncomfortably and tragically high." Pianalto: "unemployment rate at 8.1 percent and the August employment report as disappointing as it was." August payrolls are weak. Williams says progress is "stalled." This is squarely in the 2011–14 recovery anchor range of 35–40, perhaps slightly lower given the 8%+ unemployment rate. Score: ~33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants characterize consumer spending and investment as sluggish/below trend. Lacker: "middling" data, "sluggish economic growth." Bullard: "middling" economic data. The recovery is proceeding "at a 2 percent pace." Fisher cites that businesses are not investing in capex beyond maintenance — they are buying back stock instead. Small/medium businesses are not borrowing. Housing is recovering but "still operating below long-run potential" (Fisher). Activity is "weak" (Lacker). The consensus forecast is weak for 2013 given fiscal cliff concerns. Multiple participants note downside risks. Direction: below trend. Score: ~37.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants describe financial conditions as generally accommodative/supportive, though not extremely loose. Bullard: "financial stress, which is relatively low, at least for now." Pianalto: "Financial markets are not in disarray." Markets are functioning. High-yield spreads and equity prices have improved (Fisher: "things obviously are going our way" on panel 5 and 6). The ECB OMT program is viewed as having reduced European tail risks (Dudley at length). Long-term rates are "exceptionally low" (Bullard). Some concern about future exit, but current conditions are accommodative. This maps to the 31–45 range — "somewhat loose to neutral, generally supportive." Score: ~35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This meeting adopts QE3. The vast majority supports Alternative B (open-ended MBS purchases of $40 billion/month). The key supporters of further easing include: Williams (strong, wants $40B + 5′ + lower IOER), Rosengren (strong, $40B + 5′), Kocherlakota (strong, B+5′), Dudley (supportive, B+5′), Yellen (supportive, B+5′ preference), Evans (supportive), Tarullo (supportive), Raskin (mentioned supporting), Stein (broadly supportive though reservations about efficacy), Powell (broadly endorses), Lockhart (cautious supporter of B+5), Duke (cautious supporter, concerned about fiscal cliff and efficacy), Pianalto (reluctant supporter of B+5). The opponents/skeptics: Lacker (against asset purchases, will dissent), Plosser (against, prefers B+5 but only under duress), Fisher (reluctant B+5 supporter — against efficacy but not voting against in this session), George (against but can tolerate some version of B). Formal dissents: at minimum Lacker dissents; Plosser is strongly opposed. The majority (12+) support B. The rubric anchor says QE era debates are 15–25 for the dovish side. This is a meeting launching a new open-ended QE program with broad majority support, which maps to the 15–20 range. Score: ~18.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
There is notable disagreement. Lacker formally dissents. Plosser strongly opposes the policy and expresses that at length. Fisher is a reluctant opponent who will support but has deep reservations. George is against further action. Pianalto is a reluctant supporter who preferred to wait. Bullard is skeptical and opposes 5′. That is at least 3–4 distinct dissenting/reluctant voices against the majority who firmly support B. The debate is substantive: structural vs. cyclical unemployment, efficacy of LSAPs, communication challenges with 5′, inflation risks. This maps to 46–60 ("notable disagreement: distinct camps; 1–2 formal dissents; disagreement extends to assessment"). Lacker dissents formally; Plosser and George are in a distinct camp. Score: ~52.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability receives meaningful but not dominant discussion. Vice Chairman Dudley gives a substantial report on the ECB and European situation (pages 47–49). President George comments on the financial stability monitoring report and raises concerns about future imbalances in a low-rate environment. Rosengren asks about money market funds. Bullard and Dudley discuss nonfinancial credit-to-GDP. George: "I continue to find a lot of value in this" (financial stability report). The ECB OMT action is discussed at length by Dudley as a financial stability issue. There is also discussion of money market fund reform risk. However, financial stability is not the dominant topic of the meeting — that remains the LSAP decision and forward guidance. The discussion maps to the "moderate discussion; several participants address it meaningfully" range of 40–60. Score: ~45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The Committee adopts Alternative B with paragraph 5 (calendar-based guidance, not 5′). Liftoff is still pinned to "at least through mid-2015." The statement language emphasizes that the Fed "will continue its purchases... and undertake additional asset purchases... until such improvement is achieved" — open-ended easing commitment. The forward signal is strongly dovish: promising to continue buying until the labor market substantially improves, maintaining near-zero rates through mid-2015. This maps to the 3–8 range per rubric anchor ("2012–13 Evans Rule/QE3 thresholds"). Score: ~8. Note: the Committee debated 5′ (threshold-based with 6.5% unemployment and 2.5% inflation cap) but settled on paragraph 5 (calendar guidance). The tone is still one of extended accommodation with open-ended asset purchases — highly dovish forward signal. Score: ~8.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Multiple participants express elevated uncertainty. Williams notes "all the uncertainties of the economy, the fiscal cliff, and Europe." Plosser expresses deep uncertainty about LSAP efficacy and stopping rules. Pianalto: "it is impossible to be fully prepared for the environment that we might actually face three years from now." Bullard notes the underlying forecast has "zillions" of subtleties. Dudley: "we don't really know how the market is going to react." Lockhart earlier noted uncertainty about the transmission mechanism ("may or may not prevail in this current situation"). Fisher: "going beyond the first part of 2013 is largely pure guesswork." Plosser calls model estimates "very speculative." The fiscal cliff and European sovereign crisis add substantial uncertainty to the outlook. This maps to the 46–60 range — "elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty; particularly uncertain." Score: ~58.

### 4. Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more inflation. Evidence says below target. Score 36 (below 50). Check: correct, low score = low inflation pressure.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential, large slack. Score 35 (well below 50). Check: correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very slack (8%+ unemployment). Score 33. Check: correct, low score.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence says weak/sluggish. Score 37. Check: correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Score 35. Check: correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says QE3 launch, strong dovish majority. Score 18. Check: correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says strong easing commitment, open-ended purchases, rates through mid-2015+. Score 8. Check: correct.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, consistent. Labor market slightly lower than output gap — consistent with labor market underperforming relative to activity in post-crisis recovery.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (18) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both strongly dovish and aligned. Forward guidance tone is even more dovish than current action because of the open-ended QE commitment and extended accommodation signal. This coherent — forward guidance extends further than the immediate action.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (35) and OUTPUT_GAP (35): Consistent — loose financial conditions are in place but not driving growth, reflecting the weak transmission environment noted by many participants.

### 6. Speaker Count by Direction (Policy Lean)
Strong/firm supporters of B with easing bias: Williams, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Dudley, Yellen, Evans, Tarullo, Raskin (~8)
Cautious/reluctant supporters: Stein, Powell, Duke, Pianalto, Lockhart (~5)
Against/dissenting: Lacker (dissent), Plosser (strong against), George (against), Fisher (reluctant, noted reservations) (~4)
Bullard: opposed to aggressive action/5′ but accepts B (~1 skeptic)

Center of gravity: Clear majority supporting further easing → HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN firmly in dovish territory.

### 7. Optional Dimensions Assessment
- INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: Multiple participants discuss this. Fisher notes breakeven inflation rates rising. George concerned LSAP could push longer-term expectations above target. Kocherlakota: "modestly more inflationary pressures." Lacker: "inflation a couple of tenths stronger, closer to 2 percent." But mainstream view is expectations "well anchored." Score: ~48 (near target, anchored but with some upside concern).
- CREDIT_STRESS: Bullard notes "financial stress relatively low." Dudley notes markets functioning with improved spreads. Score: ~35 (somewhat below normal stress, accommodative).
- EQUITY_STRESS: Fisher notes "equity price response" going "our way." Bullard: "U.S. equity markets suggest continuing faith in U.S. expansion." No significant equity stress concern raised. Score: ~35 (no crash concern, equities seen as supportive signal).
- INFLATION_GOODS, INFLATION_SERVICES, INFLATION_HOUSING, BANKING_STRESS, TREASURY_STRESS, DOLLAR_CONCERN, PRODUCTIVITY: Not prominently discussed by 2+ participants in ways that qualify for scoring. N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 36 ±7 | Moderate confidence**

- Evidence: Rosengren: "Inflation is quite low, and unemployment is quite high." Fisher: "everybody agrees at the table that inflation is not an immediate or presently foreseeable threat." Pianalto: "deflation is not a serious risk" (implying neither is inflation).
- Minority views: Kocherlakota sees "modestly more inflationary pressures than what are in this forecast, maybe one-tenth or two-tenths or three-tenths more." Plosser forecasts "more inflationary pressures." George raises concern that another LSAP "could push longer-term expectations above a level consistent with our objectives."
- Center-of-gravity: The vast majority perceive inflation as running below the 2% target but not in deflation territory, with inflation expectations anchored; a small minority sees modest upside pressure.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 ±7 | High confidence**

- Evidence: Lacker: "We're in a period of sluggish economic growth, clearly... GDP growth has averaged around 2 percent since the recovery began." Plosser: "The recovery is frustratingly slow. The unemployment rate is uncomfortably and tragically high." Bernanke: "very serious problems with unemployment are not improving."
- Minority views: Lacker (go-round) challenges whether the "relevant gap is quite large," noting that models based on price stickiness may overstate the gap; however, this is a methodological point rather than a claim that the economy is near potential.
- Center-of-gravity: The economy is operating clearly below potential with persistent slack — consistent with 2010–14 recovery anchor of 35–42. Placing at lower end given the sluggish 2% growth path and August payroll disappointment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 ±6 | High confidence**

- Evidence: Pianalto: "With the unemployment rate at 8.1 percent and the August employment report as disappointing as it was." Rosengren: "most of our forecasts for the past nine months have overestimated how quickly we would see improvements in either element of our mandate." Williams: "progress toward our policy goals would remain stalled."
- Minority views: No participant argues the labor market is tight or approaching full employment.
- Center-of-gravity: The labor market is uniformly characterized as weak, with 8%+ unemployment, disappointing payrolls, and stalled progress — mapping to the 35–40 anchor range for 2011–14; placed at the lower end given the specific August disappointment.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 ±8 | Moderate confidence**

- Evidence: Lacker: "the tenor of our anecdotal reports has been middling of late." Bullard: "We are looking at economic data that are middling." Fisher (on business fixed investment): "9 out of 10 [CEOs] was, you could cut the cost of borrowing by 1 full percentage point and we'll use it to buy back stock" rather than job-creating capex.
- Minority views: Housing is described as "just beginning to recover" (Williams) and is a positive durable goods sector. But this is against a weak broader backdrop.
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and business investment are broadly sluggish/below trend; housing is a modest positive but does not offset overall weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 ±8 | Moderate confidence**

- Evidence: Bullard: "financial stress, which is relatively low, at least for now." Fisher (on financial markets dashboard): "things obviously are going our way" on high-yield spreads and equity prices. Pianalto: "Financial markets are not in disarray."
- Minority views: Dudley describes the European situation as "tenuous" and "still a long way to go," suggesting some lingering external financial risks. George raises concern about imbalances building in a low-rate environment.
- Center-of-gravity: Domestic financial conditions are generally accommodative and supportive; spreads are compressed, equities are supportive, and credit is available — consistent with the loose-to-neutral range.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 18 ±8 | High confidence**

- Evidence: Williams: "The case for further action is clear. In the absence of significant additional monetary stimulus, progress toward our policy goals would remain stalled. Such an outcome is simply inconsistent with the notion of appropriate monetary policy." Kocherlakota: "additional accommodation is appropriate... act forcefully and to enhance the benefits of our large-scale asset purchases as much as possible." Rosengren: "Alternative B embraces the sense of doing what it takes to move the economy at an acceptable pace toward full employment."
- Minority views: Lacker: "I cannot support a new asset purchase program... Further stimulus at this time is unlikely to improve economic growth much without causing an unwanted increase in inflation." Plosser: "not in favor of undertaking a new round of asset purchases today... I am not in favor of B." George: "the risks associated with further action carry less precise measurement, but pose significant cost if realized." Fisher is deeply skeptical of efficacy but accepts B as the least-bad option.
- Center-of-gravity: A strong majority (roughly 12–13 out of 17–19 participants) supports launching QE3 (open-ended MBS purchases at $40B/month). The presence of an open-ended QE debate and launch firmly places this in the 15–20 range per rubric anchors.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 52 ±10 | High confidence**

- Evidence: Lacker formally dissents from the action. Plosser: "Obviously, I am not in favor of B" — extensive policy-level objection. George: "the data flow since the last meeting was a bit softer than I had expected, but not sufficiently so to warrant a further expansion of our balance sheet." Fisher: "I disagree with this course. I think it is a mistake."
- Minority views supporting the majority: Williams, Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Dudley, Yellen are firm supporters; Stein, Powell, Pianalto, Duke, and Lockhart are reluctant/cautious supporters.
- Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent (Lacker), at least 2–3 others strongly opposed (Plosser, George, Fisher), with several reluctant supporters (Pianalto, Duke, Lockhart). Disagreement extends to the fundamental assessment of efficacy, appropriate response to weak data, and communication design (5 vs. 5′). Maps to 46–60 range — notable disagreement with distinct camps.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 ±10 | Moderate confidence**

- Evidence: Vice Chairman Dudley: extensive remarks on the ECB OMT and European sovereign situation (pages 47–49). George: "I continue to find a lot of value in this" (financial stability monitoring) and raises concern about imbalances in low-rate environment — "the point at which to know when to take signal from some of those things is very difficult." Rosengren asks about money market fund vulnerabilities in Europe.
- Minority views: Other participants do not dismiss financial stability but treat it as secondary to the immediate LSAP/forward guidance decision.
- Center-of-gravity: Several participants address financial stability meaningfully, particularly around Europe and the ECB; monitoring of potential domestic imbalances is also discussed. The topic receives moderate attention — not a dominant focus but more than passing mentions.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 8 ±5 | High confidence**

- Evidence: The Committee adopts open-ended MBS purchases until the labor market "substantially improves," plus extended funds rate guidance through mid-2015. Williams: open-ended purchases "serve as a useful automatic stabilizer." Kocherlakota (advocating for 5′): the guidance "provides protection against inflation" and is explicitly designed as maximum accommodation. Chairman Bernanke (summarizing): "we want to see some progress — not sharp progress, but some progress — in the unemployment rate."
- Minority views: Bullard supports B but not the aggressive 5′ language. Lacker, Plosser, and George prefer less commitment.
- Center-of-gravity: The adopted policy combines open-ended QE with extended low-rate guidance — a very strong easing commitment. The rubric anchor for 2012–13 QE3/Evans Rule era is 3–8. The final statement retains paragraph 5 (calendar-based mid-2015) rather than 5′ (threshold-based), which is slightly less aggressive than the Evans Rule but still firmly in the strong easing commitment range. Score: 8.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±10 | High confidence**

- Evidence: Williams: "all the uncertainties of the economy, the fiscal cliff, and Europe." Fisher: "going beyond the first part of 2013 is largely pure guesswork." Dudley (on yield curve confidence intervals): "we are probably even more uncertain about the shape of the yield curve because you have never gone from purchasing all of these assets to selling all of these assets. So we don't really know how the market is going to react." Plosser: "The consequences may prove very disruptive to the U.S. economy."
- Minority views: Bernanke and staff present point estimates but explicitly acknowledge wide uncertainty bands.
- Center-of-gravity: Multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty — the fiscal cliff, Europe, LSAP exit mechanics, and model uncertainty all feature prominently. Maps to elevated uncertainty range (46–60). The meeting is not as severely uncertain as GFC or COVID (no deep financial crisis underway), but the multiple simultaneous risks and the "uncharted territory" of exit from QE3 push the score toward the higher end of this range.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±8 | Moderate confidence**
- Evidence: Fisher: "breakeven inflation rates... bottomed in the spring of this year, and the slope is upward." Bernanke: "The 5-by-5 is pretty flat and is lower than it was last year." Mainstream view is expectations "well anchored" (multiple speakers). Kocherlakota: "modestly more inflationary pressures." George: concerned LSAP could push long-term expectations above objective. Center: expectations are roughly at target — anchored but with a slight upward drift in near-term break-evens that some participants flag. Score near neutral.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 35 ±10 | Low confidence**
- Evidence: Bullard: "financial stress, which is relatively low, at least for now." Fisher on high-yield spreads: "things obviously are going our way." Dudley discusses nonfinancial credit-to-GDP as still elevated (from crisis). Near-normal to slightly loose credit conditions. Score below 50 (lower stress than normal, accommodative conditions).

**EQUITY_STRESS: 35 ±10 | Low confidence**
- Evidence: Fisher: "equity price response" — equities are rising and supportive. Bullard: "U.S. equity markets suggest continuing faith in U.S. expansion." No participants raise concerns about equity market collapse or overvaluation. Score below 50 — markets are supportive with no stress signal.
