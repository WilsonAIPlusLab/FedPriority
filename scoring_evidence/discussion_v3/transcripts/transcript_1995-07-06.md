# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1995-07-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a full go-around (Day 1) with regional and national economic assessments, followed by a Day 2 policy discussion. Speakers identified include: Vice Chairman McDonough, Presidents Parry, Minehan, Boehne, Broaddus, Hoenig, Moskow, Melzer, McTeer, Jordan, Stern; Governors Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, Yellen, Blinder; and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 17-18 participants speak substantively.

### Era Context
Mid-1995. The economy had been growing rapidly through 1994 after the Fed raised rates 300bp from early 1994 to February 1995. By mid-1995, growth has stalled dramatically — possibly a negative GDP quarter in Q2. The Fed is now contemplating its first rate cut. CPI inflation is running around 3-3.5%. Unemployment is near 5.7%. This is the famous "soft landing" episode.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly elevated but peaking cyclically. McDonough: CPI "3-1/4 percent in 1995 and inching up to about 3-1/2 percent in 1996." Broaddus: "3.5 percent for 1995." Boehne: "uptick in cyclical inflationary pressures" but "wage pressures subdued and price competition intense." Jordan: "prices are going to be less of a problem" — steel price increases not happening, rollbacks occurring. Kelley: "inflation may be peaking cyclically somewhere right in here." Blinder: "risk of rising inflation now looks to be minimal." Greenspan: "encouraging evidence that the cyclical peak in inflation may be close at hand." The center of gravity is that CPI is around 3-3.5% (above the implicit target of ~2%), so there IS inflation pressure, but it's seen as peaking and likely to come down. This is "somewhat elevated" but fading — roughly 51-58. Direction: above midpoint because inflation is above target, but only modestly so given confident language about peaking. Score ~55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is perceived as going through a pause or possible mild contraction. Parry: "real GDP declined at a rate between 1 and 2 percent in the second quarter" and "flirting with recession." Boehne: "Philadelphia District economy is flat to down." Greenspan: "we clearly have managed to contain a highly unstable... business cycle expansion" and describes the economy as going through an "adjustment process." Hoenig: "economy is going through a pause and an adjustment phase right now... couple of quarters of growth below trend." Most participants see the economy at or slightly below potential and getting weaker. The strong growth of 1994 is gone. Blinder puts recession probability at ~33%. The center of gravity is an economy that was recently above potential but has now dipped to roughly at or slightly below potential. Score ~44-47. Direction: slightly below midpoint because economy is in a pause/possible mild contraction but not a deep recession.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Jordan: many counties with under 3% unemployment. Hoenig: "unemployment levels across the states are consistently below the national average." Melzer: "qualified workers are in short supply. Most contacts... report rising costs and some moderate wage pressures." But nationally, unemployment is 5.7% and rising. McDonough: "unemployment rate reaching 6 percent this year." Phillips: "relatively close to full employment." Boehne: "Wage and price pressures remain subdued." Minehan: "Inflation on both the price and the wage side remains well in check." The center of gravity: labor market is still reasonably tight in many districts but loosening. Near NAIRU or slightly above. Score ~48-50. Some districts very tight, national picture loosening.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is weak. Blinder: "retail sales were up just 0.4 percent from November to May... in nominal terms and not at an annual rate." Moskow: auto sales disappointing. Boehne: "Retailers say sales are up but below expectations." But business fixed investment is holding up. Greenspan: "equipment markets... remain quite solid. Unfilled orders still seem to be rising." Phillips: "Business investment... is still relatively strong." Housing is starting to turn up due to lower mortgage rates. The net picture: consumption is below trend, investment still positive, housing turning. Score ~40-43. Direction: below midpoint — spending is sluggish.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are generally seen as accommodative/supportive. Melzer: "equities market is riding high, and long-term interest rates are down significantly." Kelley: "Credit is still flowing nicely, maybe in the view of some a little too strongly." McTeer: "directors continue to report exceptionally easy credit standards throughout the District." Phillips: "The financial markets are not pointing to an economic downturn. The banking system is strong. There is no credit crunch in sight." Stern: bond and stock market rallies functioning as "automatic stabilizers." The consensus is that financial conditions are loose/supportive. Score ~25-30. Direction: below midpoint — conditions are loose.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the meeting where they cut 25bp. Greenspan proposes -25bp. Blinder, Yellen, McTeer, Parry, McDonough, Lindsey all wanted or would have preferred -50bp. Melzer and Hoenig preferred no change but would not dissent on -25bp. Minehan also came in wanting no change but accepted -25bp. Broaddus nervous but supportive. The center of gravity: moderate dovish lean — majority favors easing, with the debate being between -25bp and -50bp. Score ~35-38. Direction: below midpoint — dovish lean, easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad agreement on the direction (ease) but disagreement on magnitude. Three camps: (1) -50bp preferred (Blinder, Yellen, McTeer, Parry, Lindsey, McDonough — though most accepted -25bp); (2) -25bp (Greenspan, Boehne, Kelley, Phillips, Jordan, Moskow, Stern, Broaddus); (3) no change preferred (Melzer, Hoenig, Minehan — but all accepted -25bp). No formal dissents. The spread is notable — from no change to -50bp — but everyone ultimately coalesced around -25bp asymmetric. Score ~35-40. Notable differences but convergence on the outcome.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion of financial stability per se. McDonough discusses Japan's financial sector weakness at length (banks, securities firms, life insurance companies). Several mention the stock market rally and bond market rally as positive factors. McTeer mentions easy credit standards. But financial stability is not a major topic — it's mentioned as context, not as a concern about the US system. Score ~15-20. Passing references, mostly about international financial risks and positive US financial conditions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The directive is asymmetric toward further ease. Greenspan: "we could readily, if we so chose, add another 25 basis points in August or later." Blinder: "the ultimate need is going to be more than 50 basis points." Lindsey: "a year from now we will be 100 basis points under where we are now." McDonough: "likely total easing requirement is 50 basis points." The forward signal is clearly toward further easing. Score ~30-35. Mild easing bias with data dependence — they signal more easing likely but it depends on data.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated uncertainty about whether the economy will dip into recession or recover. Phillips: "most difficult of my tenure... so many conflicting economic signals." Blinder: recession probability models "flashing a number in the 30 to 60 percent range." Greenspan: "we will know within 6 weeks or 2 months at the latest whether the adjustment process... will cumulate into significant recession pressures." Stern: "rare... to find a contraction in GDP that isn't ultimately associated with a recession." McDonough: "very concerned about the possible dangers from... economic weakness abroad." Many participants explicitly express uncertainty about the path. Score ~48-55. Elevated — multiple participants emphasize the unusual fork in the road.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. CPI at 3-3.5% is above target. Score 55 = modestly above target. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy is in a pause, slightly below potential. Score 45 = slightly below. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Near NAIRU, loosening. Score 49. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose. Score 27. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Committee is easing. Score 36. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Signal is toward further easing. Score 32. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (45) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49): Close together, both near but slightly below midpoint. Coherent — economy slowing from above potential, labor market still reasonably tight but loosening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (36) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Both in the moderate dovish range. Coherent — cutting today with expectation of further cuts.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "3.5 percent for 1995... we are on the high side there"
- MR. KELLEY: "inflation may be peaking cyclically somewhere right in here... we are not at price level stability"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "encouraging evidence that the cyclical peak in inflation may be close at hand"
- MR. BOEHNE: "uptick in cyclical inflationary pressures. However, with wage pressures subdued and price competition intense... we still appear to be on track for achieving price stability over time"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see CPI inflation at 3-3.5%, modestly above implicit target, but broadly believe the cyclical peak is at hand and the secular downward trend continues.

**Minority views:** Melzer is more hawkish on inflation ("trend CPI inflation of 3 percent just isn't good enough"); McDonough forecasts inflation "inching up to about 3-1/2 percent in 1996." Jordan sees very little inflation pressure in his district. The range is moderate, reflected in +/-7.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "real GDP declined at a rate between 1 and 2 percent in the second quarter... flirting with recession"
- MR. HOENIG: "economy is going through a pause and an adjustment phase... couple of quarters of growth below trend"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we clearly have managed to contain a highly unstable and inflationary business cycle expansion... I don't think we have solved the problem of the business cycle"
- MR. BLINDER: "we are in a cloud... retail sales were up just 0.4 percent from November to May"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy has shifted from above potential (late 1994) to at or slightly below potential, with a possible negative GDP quarter. Most see a pause rather than recession, with growth expected to return to potential by late 1995 or early 1996.

**Minority views:** Blinder and Yellen see more downside risk and a larger negative output gap developing. Melzer and Jordan see the economy's fundamental strength persisting. The wide range of views is reflected in +/-8.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 49 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "ten counties in Kentucky and more than ten counties in Ohio that report unemployment rates of under 3 percent"
- MR. MELZER: "qualified workers are in short supply. Most contacts in our District report rising costs and some moderate wage pressures"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Wage and price pressures remain subdued... still a lot of help-wanted signs in the region... but they are mostly for lower-paying jobs"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "even if unemployment does notch up a bit, we are still relatively close to full employment"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The national labor market is near NAIRU (5.7% unemployment, expected to rise to 6%) with many districts still reporting tightness, but the direction is toward loosening. Wage pressures are generally subdued despite low regional unemployment rates.

**Minority views:** Jordan's district is extremely tight (sub-3% in many counties); some districts (New York, New England) are weaker. The +/-8 reflects this geographic dispersion.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 41 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BLINDER: "retail sales were up just 0.4 percent from November to May... in nominal terms and not at an annual rate"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Retailers say sales are up but below expectations... customers are again more susceptible to postponing purchases"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "equipment markets... remain quite solid. Unfilled orders still seem to be rising at a modest pace"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Business investment, while not growing at a high double-digit rate, is still relatively strong"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is clearly below trend and sluggish, while business fixed investment remains a relative bright spot. Housing is starting to recover. The net position is below trend due to weak consumption, partially offset by still-solid investment.

**Minority views:** Some see consumption rebounding with lower rates (Kelley); others worry about accelerator effects pulling investment down (Blinder). The +/-7 reflects this uncertainty.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "equities market is riding high, and long-term interest rates are down significantly from their peaks of last November... Bank lending is strong"
- MR. KELLEY: "Credit is still flowing nicely, maybe in the view of some a little too strongly"
- MR. MCTEER: "directors continue to report exceptionally easy credit standards throughout the District"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The financial markets are not pointing to an economic downturn. The banking system is strong. There is no credit crunch in sight"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are decidedly loose — strong equity and bond markets, easy credit standards, healthy banking system. Multiple participants describe conditions as supportive or accommodative.

**Minority views:** Very little disagreement on this dimension. All participants describe supportive financial conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 36 +/-10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I do think we should move... probably the best thing to do is to move the funds rate downward by 25 basis points"
- MR. BLINDER: "I strongly feel we should be easing today... I feel that a 50 basis point, symmetric move would be better"
- MR. MELZER: "my preference would be to maintain the current restrictive policy... if it is the strong consensus... a 25 basis point cut... I would not dissent"
- MR. HOENIG: "I am inclined to leave policy unchanged... it would be prudent for us to adopt an asymmetric directive and wait"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The Committee voted to ease 25bp. The majority clearly favored easing, with a significant minority (Blinder, Yellen, McTeer, Parry, Lindsey, McDonough) preferring 50bp. A small minority (Melzer, Hoenig, Minehan) preferred no change but acquiesced. This is a moderate dovish lean.

**Minority views:** The wide range from no-change (Melzer, Hoenig) to -50bp (Blinder, Yellen, and others) creates a large uncertainty band of +/-10.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BLINDER: "50 basis point, symmetric move would be better than 25 basis points, asymmetric... But, for the sake of unity, I could go along"
- MR. MELZER: "my preference would be to maintain the current restrictive policy... I would not dissent"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I came into the meeting wanting to vote for a no-change, although asymmetric, directive. I am not going to dissent over 25 basis points"
- MR. HOENIG: "I am inclined to leave policy unchanged"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissents, but notable differences in preferred action spanning from no change to -50bp. Three clear camps existed, with the Chairman's proposal serving as a compromise. Multiple members explicitly stated they would not dissent despite disagreeing.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: Extended discussion of Japan's financial sector — "banks, securities firms, and especially life insurance companies--have been severely weakened by operating losses and bad debts"
- MR. MELZER: "The equities market is riding high... Bank lending is strong... few signs of structural imbalance"
- MR. MCTEER: "exceptionally easy credit standards"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is mentioned in passing — mostly as a positive (strong US banking, healthy markets) or as an international risk (Japan). It is not a focal topic of domestic concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 32 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "We could readily, if we so chose, add another 25 basis points in August or later... I think what we would do is probably create an expectation in the marketplace that indeed we will move again"
- MR. BLINDER: "the ultimate need is going to be more than 50 basis points... at the end of this episode the nominal funds rate will be below 5-1/2 percent"
- MR. LINDSEY: "I would say that a year from now we will be 100 basis points under where we are now"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the likely total easing requirement is 50 basis points"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly toward further easing, with the asymmetric directive and widespread expectation of at least another 25bp cut. The debate is about how much total easing, not whether more is needed.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Hoenig would prefer to hold or even reverse course if conditions warrant, creating some pull toward the hawkish side in the forward signal. Broaddus and Parry emphasize being "prepared to reverse course."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the most difficult of my tenure... so many conflicting economic signals. We seem to be at the classic fork in the road"
- MR. BLINDER: "statistical indicators of recession... flashing a number in the 30 to 60 percent range"
- MR. STERN: "rare, though not wholly impossible, to find a contraction in GDP that isn't ultimately associated with a recession"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we will know within 6 weeks or 2 months at the latest whether the adjustment process... will cumulate into significant recession pressures or whether it will end up as a pause"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Elevated uncertainty — participants are genuinely unsure whether the economy will dip into recession or recover. The "fork in the road" framing by Phillips captures the sentiment. Mixed data, conflicting signals, and a wide range of plausible outcomes characterize the discussion.
