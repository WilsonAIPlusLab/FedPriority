### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an FOMC telephone conference/interim meeting held April 16, 2003. Individual speakers are identified by name (e.g., "MR. BERNANKE.", "MS. MINEHAN.", "CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN."). The meeting is a shortened "truncated roundtable" format — Greenspan explicitly describes it as such. It covers a go-around of regional conditions and a brief policy discussion. The document is segmented by speaker. The content represents participant views, including district roundtable reports and a policy discussion toward the end. Staff-attributable content (references to Greenbook, David Wilcox's report) is present but minimal in the participant speech; those portions will be ignored.

### 2. Era Context
April 2003: The U.S. economy is in a sluggish, slow-growth phase post-2001 recession, still not having fully recovered. The Iraq War has just begun/ended. Core PCE inflation is running below 1.5% and falling. Unemployment elevated relative to prior peaks. The Fed funds rate is already quite low (1.25% at this time). Key backdrop: deflation concerns are emerging. This is a period of sub-potential growth, weak labor demand, and below-target inflation. The Iraqi war had just concluded major operations, and uncertainty was lifting somewhat.

### 3. Directional Signal Assessment Per Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Bernanke explicitly states core PCE at 1.4%, core CPI at 1.2%, market-based core PCE at 0.9%, and says "all of these inflation measures have been falling in the last year." He raises concern about disinflation and says he is "uncomfortable with measured inflation in the range of 1 percent or below." Kohn references "low inflation." No participant cites inflationary pressure. Direction: well below target, with active deflation/disinflation concern. This falls in the 15-25 range (meaningful deflation risk; well below target).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Universally, participants describe the economy as "sluggish," "flat," "muddling along," "disappointing," "still not growing much." Minehan: "they don't see it growing much." Kohn: "2 percent growth rut." Greenspan: "really very little of a positive nature has happened." Santomero: "the order books were meandering and falling into somewhat of a malaise." Manufacturing surveys showing negative indexes. Pianalto: "growth remains subdued." Broaddus: "economy is still fairly sluggish overall." Bernanke notes "considerable economic slack going into 2005" even under optimistic growth assumptions. Direction: clearly below potential with meaningful slack. Score: 30-36 range (below potential; recovering but gap persists).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants note soft labor markets. Guynn: "subdued growth in employment," companies requesting fewer temporary workers, burden of new hire high due to healthcare/pension costs. Moskow: "Labor demand is still soft but does not appear to be eroding further." Broaddus: "labor markets are soft." Parry: "still getting reports of layoffs, and firms appear reluctant to add to their payrolls." McTeer: workers being rehired at much lower wages. No participant describes tight labor conditions. Direction: clearly below full employment, elevated slack, but not catastrophic (not 2009-level). Score: 30-38 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending described as "sluggish," "fragile," "moving forward at a relatively sluggish pace." Business investment: Moskow says contacts "do not see any underlying catalysts that will rekindle robust growth" for capex; Parry: "Capital investment that can be postponed still is being postponed." Retail sales running below year-ago levels (Santomero). Some bright spots in housing and residential, small biotech firms growing. But the net picture is below-trend spending with weak investment. Direction: below trend, sluggish. Score: 35-42 range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Pianalto: bankers "flush with deposits" and "net interest margins are being squeezed" — suggests easy credit/accommodative conditions. Hoenig: "steady loan growth but loan demand is not strong at all. So they have good liquidity." McDonough notes global settlements with financial firms pending, which would remove uncertainty. Greenspan notes that "financial markets have been behaving so well recently." No participants describe tight financial conditions. Direction: conditions are accommodative/loose, banks flush with deposits, low rates driving refinancings. Score: 25-35 range (loose/accommodative).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: No policy action is taken at this meeting; the discussion resolves to hold steady until May 6. The consensus is to maintain current stance. McTeer explicitly notes "zero sentiment for easing monetary policy" from his district contacts, though he says he "tends to agree" that easing might "send out the wrong signal." Bernanke is the most dovish voice, explicitly calling for keeping "potential further easing on the table." Gramlich and Greenspan agree to wait and see. Moskow: "I certainly think the current stance of monetary policy is fine until May 6." The dominant lean is hold with a mild dovish tilt — Bernanke's call to keep easing on the table, and the implicit possibility of action in May, tips the needle slightly dovish. But there is no active call for immediate easing (except implicitly from Bernanke). Direction: lean dovish to balanced — hold with potential easing bias. Score: 38-44 range (lean dovish: hold steady with mild easing bias; "prepared to act if needed").

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is near-unanimity at this meeting. The policy decision is to hold and cancel weekly calls, and participants one after another express agreement. No formal dissent. Bernanke is mildly more dovish (wanting to keep easing on table) and McTeer slightly more hawkish on perception (zero sentiment for easing). But these are not sharp disagreements — they are emphasis differences. Greenspan's proposal receives explicit affirmative endorsements from McDonough, Moskow, Santomero, Stern/Kohn, Minehan, Broaddus, Poole, Hoenig. Direction: near-consensus, minor emphasis differences. Score: 15-22 range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are mentioned passing — Pianalto on bankers flush with deposits, Hoenig on loan growth, McDonough on global financial settlements and investment house sentiment. Greenspan discusses markets briefly. No sustained focus on financial stability risks or financial system stress per se; these are more passing references as context. Direction: limited/passing discussion, not a focal topic. Score: 20-28 range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The signal is to hold steady and revisit in May, with Bernanke explicitly wanting to keep further easing "on the table." No signal of tightening. The bias is mildly dovish — "wait and see" with possible easing. This is not a strong commitment either way; it is a data-dependent hold with a slight easing lean. Direction: mild easing lean / easing bias, waiting for May. Score: 28-38 range (easing lean with data dependence).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is pervasive uncertainty language throughout. Minehan: "almost all of the contacts we talked to continue to see a great deal of uncertainty... extreme caution." ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BERNANKE: "The twelve-month inflation rate as measured by the core PCE deflator is currently 1.4 percent... All of these inflation measures have been falling in the last year... there will still be considerable economic slack going into 2005... there seems to be a reasonable likelihood of further disinflation."
- MR. BERNANKE: "I personally am uncomfortable with measured inflation in the range of 1 percent or below... I hope that we will keep potential further easing on the table."
- MR. KOHN: "Given the low level of resource utilization and low inflation, I think it will be important to look for some early signs that the economy is going to break out of this 2 percent growth rut."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is meaningfully below target and falling, with active deflation/disinflation concern raised by Bernanke; no participant sees upside inflation risk. Score of 20 reflects the 11-25 range ("meaningful deflation risk; well below target; subdued, muted").

**Minority views**: No participant takes a contrarian view on inflation being elevated or concerning on the upside. The minority note is that McTeer's contacts showed "zero sentiment for easing" suggesting some reluctance to characterize deflation risk as requiring immediate action.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 34 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. KOHN: "The general sense of a very sluggish economic expansion hasn't left us yet... I think it will be important to look for some early signs that the economy is going to break out of this 2 percent growth rut."
- MR. BERNANKE: "If the U.S. economy grows 3 percent in 2003 and 4 percent in 2004 as projected, there will still be considerable economic slack going into 2005."
- MS. MINEHAN: "They don't see the economy moving into negative territory, but they don't see it growing much either; and they talked about the risks almost to the exclusion of the opportunities."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Really very little of a positive nature has happened in the economy as a consequence of the end of the war."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: All participants describe the economy as well below potential — sluggish, flat, or at best slowly recovering — with considerable slack explicitly identified by Bernanke. Score of 34 places this in the 31-40 range (below potential; recovering but gap persists).

**Minority views**: Pianalto notes "the economy is still expanding" and some optimism from contacts for improvement in H2 — suggests the floor is not collapsing but does not alter the below-potential assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "I think we might expect subdued growth in employment... the burden associated with a new hire is even higher now because of escalating health care costs."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Labor demand is still soft but does not appear to be eroding further."
- MR. PARRY: "We're still getting reports of layoffs, and firms appear reluctant to add to their payrolls."
- MR. MCTEER: "To the extent that workers are being reemployed in high-tech industries, they are being hired at much lower wages than they earned before they were laid off."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are soft across all districts — layoffs continuing, reluctance to hire, wages under pressure for re-hires — but not deteriorating sharply further. Score of 35 reflects the 31-45 range (below full employment, slack remains, unemployment elevated).

**Minority views**: Broaddus notes a "drop in unemployment rates in a couple of our District states recently," and Pianalto notes steady/increasing employment in nondurables — but these are isolated bright spots against a broad backdrop of soft labor.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- MR. SANTOMERO: "Retail sales in the District were running below year-ago levels in all lines of merchandise."
- MR. PARRY: "Capital investment that can be postponed still is being postponed... Consumer spending can be described as fragile."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Consumer spending still seems to be moving forward, though at a relatively sluggish pace... our contacts do not see any underlying catalysts that will rekindle robust growth."
- MS. MINEHAN: "Companies want a lot more evidence of a rebound in economic activity before they are going to hire or spend on much of anything that doesn't help them cut costs."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment are below trend — consumer spending fragile/sluggish, capex deferred. Score of 38 places this in the 31-45 range (below trend, sluggish, cautious consumers).

**Minority views**: Pianalto notes strong mortgage refinancings and some nondurable manufacturer optimism. Housing activity described as "still rather strong" (Broaddus). These bright spots are insufficient to shift the center of gravity above 40.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence**:

PIANALTO: "The bankers in our District continue to see strong mortgage refinancings... commercial borrowers were taking advantage of the lower interest rates by refinancing their outstanding loans. **Center-of-gravity summary**: Banks are flush with deposits, refinancings are strong, net interest margins squeezed by low rates — all hallmarks of loose/accommodative financial conditions. Score of 30 reflects the 16-30 range (loose/accommodative).

**Minority views**: No participant describes tight financial conditions. The only complexity is elevated geopolitical/policy uncertainty, but financial markets per se are described positively (Greenspan: "financial markets have been behaving so well recently").

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence**:

Score of 42 reflects "lean dovish to balanced: hold steady with mild easing bias."

**Minority views**: McTeer and his contacts lean toward not easing, providing a slight hawkish counterweight. Gramlich and Greenspan lean toward a wait-and-see approach without committing to action.
