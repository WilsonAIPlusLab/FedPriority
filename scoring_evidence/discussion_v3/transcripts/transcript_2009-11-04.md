### 2. Era Context
November 2009: The GFC trough is approximately six months behind (June 2009 trough). Q3 2009 GDP posted +3.5% — the first positive print after four consecutive quarterly contractions. Unemployment is rising toward 10%. The Fed funds rate has been at the zero lower bound since December 2008. Agency MBS QE program ($1.25 trillion) is ongoing and approaching completion in Q1 2010. Era anchors: OUTPUT_GAP expected 15–22 range (GFC era); INFLATION_PRESSURE expected ~15–20 (deflation fears); LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~15–22 (2009 anchor).

### 3. Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as below target and tilted toward deflation risk, though near-term deflation risk is described as "fading." The dominant view (Rosengren, Evans, Yellen, Kohn, Dudley, Tarullo, Hoenig, Pianalto) is that disinflation/subdued inflation persists due to large resource slack, wage deceleration, and falling unit labor costs. Core PCE is ~1.3% and trending down. A minority view (Plosser, Bullard, Fisher) emphasizes medium-term upside risks from policy accommodation, dollar weakness, and anchoring concerns. Bernanke's summary: "core inflation and median inflation are slowing," "subdued inflation." Yellen: "expect substantial slack in labor and goods markets to reduce core PCE price inflation from about 1½ percent this year to 1 percent in 2010 and 2011." Dudley: "core PCE deflator has risen only 1.3 percent over the past year, down more than a percentage point from a year ago." Bullard: "near-term deflation risk is fading" but medium-term risk of higher inflation. This is firmly in the "below target, meaningful deflation risk fading" range — anchor 15–20 for 2009. Score: ~20–22. The deflation alarm has eased somewhat from the spring 2009 trough but remains a live concern for several.

Direction: BELOW target, disinflation underway, deflation risk present but fading.
Magnitude: "Meaningful deflation risk; well below target; subdued, muted" = 11–25 range. Core at 1.3% and falling → score 20.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
All participants agree the economy is still far below potential. "Very deep and very severe recession" (Bullard), "substantial excess capacity" (Rosengren), "economy operating well below potential" (implicit across all). Positive Q3 GDP growth of ~3.5% is welcomed as a turning point but participants uniformly expect a slow recovery well below potential for years. Bernanke: unemployment expected to remain above 8% through 2011, "employment-to-population ratio at a long-term low." Yellen: "unemployment rising to 10 percent this year and remaining above 8 percent through 2011." Dudley: "unemployment rate is very high; core inflation is low." BRIGHT-LINE: All participants use "slack remains," "below potential," "substantial resource slack" — score MUST be < 50.

Direction: FAR below potential. Still in deep recession trough recovery.
Magnitude: GFC anchor 15–22. Q3 2009 is just after the trough — first positive GDP print. Recovery is beginning but gap is enormous. Score: ~22–25 (slightly above deepest trough as some recovery is underway, but still in "deep recession" territory).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Extremely weak labor market is the dominant theme. Unemployment approaching 10%. Payrolls still declining. Bernanke: "labor market remains very weak," "insured unemployment...ten million and has not peaked," "employment-to-population ratio is at a long-term low." Fisher: "pricing power is nil," workers being shed. Bullard: "labor market outcomes will continue to lag behind the rest of the economy." Multiple participants cite "jobless recovery" risk. BRIGHT-LINE: "High unemployment; significant slack; far from full employment." Anchor: 15–22 for 2009.

Direction: Very loose labor market, high unemployment, far from full employment.
Magnitude: Still rising toward 10%; "far from full employment" — score 18–22.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Mixed but net below trend. Q3 consumption and housing "grew" but supported by government programs (cash-for-clunkers, homebuyer tax credit) now winding down. Business investment: "domestic cap-ex is dead" (Fisher/Fluor CEO), "no enthusiasm for expanding payrolls and cap-ex" (Fisher), "still cautious consumers" (multiple). Warsh: "real incomes are still declining even as PCE was bolstered by car and housing subsidies." Rosengren: "growth in consumption and housing tend to be drivers in recoveries, and although both grew in the third quarter, they were supported by a variety of government programs, which are now in the process of winding down." Positive: consumer spending "modestly stronger even outside autos" per Bernanke summary. Housing: some stabilization signs. Net: below trend, "sluggish"/"cautious consumers." Anchor: 15–20 (2008 Q4-2009 Q1); transition recovery in late 2009. Score: ~32–38 — below trend but no longer in freefall.

Direction: BELOW trend; consumer spending stabilizing at low level; business investment very weak.
Magnitude: "Sluggish," "cautious consumers," mostly cost-cutting not revenue growth → 31–45 range. Score: 35.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Two-track description: large firms with capital market access are finding conditions "closer to normal" (Yellen, Fisher, Evans), but bank credit for small/medium businesses remains tight. Kohn: "bank credit remains quite tight...banks continue to cut back on all kinds of credit lines." Dudley: "constraints on credit availability, do not appear to have subsided." Evans: "small and medium-sized companies...continue to complain about onerous terms and an inadequate supply of borrowing options." Rosengren: "capital constraints will not ease over the next year. In fact, they might tighten." But: "credit spreads much narrower" (Bernanke summary), "risk spreads have come down" (Yellen), large firms accessing credit fine. Net: conditions are tight overall, especially through banking channel, though securities markets have improved substantially. Score: ~58–63 (somewhat tight, bifurcated).

Direction: TIGHTER than neutral, primarily through bank lending channel; securities markets have eased.
Magnitude: "Conditions have tightened," "credit hard to obtain" for small firms, but not crisis-level — score ~60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The overwhelming consensus is to maintain the "extended period" language, hold rates at zero, and continue the QE program to completion. Almost all participants support alternative B (hold). Only Plosser explicitly advocates for alternative C signals and earlier exit; Bullard argues for more active use of QE adjustment; Hoenig "would have some preference for alternative C." Most others (Rosengren, Evans, Yellen, Kohn, Duke, Tarullo, Pianalto, Dudley, Lockhart) strongly support staying the course. Dudley: "many quarters away from any need to tighten monetary policy." Evans: "low likelihood of a policy switch for some time." Yellen: strongly against tightening before inflation and employment objectives are achieved. This is firmly dovish — "strong dovish: majority favors maintaining max accommodation." Anchor: 2–5 (March 2009 emergency), but Nov 2009 is a few months past trough; the debate is about exit timing, not about additional easing.

Direction: STRONGLY DOVISH. Consensus holds; only 1-2 prefer earlier exit signals.
Magnitude: "Majority favors maintaining max accommodation." QE ongoing. Zero rates maintained. Score: 18–22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
The meeting is notable for broad consensus to support alternative B, with vigorous debates within that consensus about language nuance (the "including" word, "very low" vs. "low" resource utilization). Formal dissent: Plosser preferred alt C elements but voted for B. No formal dissents recorded in the vote (per the roll call at the end). Substantive disagreements: Plosser/Bullard/Fisher on the other side from Yellen/Evans/Rosengren/Kohn/Dudley on inflation dynamics and exit timing. Multiple extended speeches about inflation models (Phillips curve vs. expectations-based). Bernanke summary: "Participants continue to disagree on the appropriate weights to place on resource slack, inflation expectations, and other factors in forecasting inflation." The debate is intellectually substantive (QE design, inflation theory, output gap measurement) but ends in near-consensus on the vote.

Direction: Moderate disagreement — clear camps on inflation theory and exit timing, but no formal dissents; broad agreement on today's action.
Magnitude: "Moderate differences: notable differences about pace/risk... 1 dissent or 2-3 reservations." Score: 38–42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability/conditions are discussed at considerable length, but more as context than as a primary alarm. Topics covered: banking system stress (Duke's extensive CRE/bank discussion), commercial real estate deterioration (Yellen's "zombie banks," multiple participants), equity market moves (Warsh), MBS program effects (Dudley, Lacker, Yellen, Bullard), dollar stability concerns (Fisher, Bernanke), carry trade risk (Fisher), asset bubble monitoring (Bernanke). Multiple participants address financial conditions meaningfully, but this is not an emergency-dominated meeting.

Direction: MODERATE financial stability discussion — several participants address it meaningfully.
Magnitude: "Moderate discussion; several participants address it meaningfully." Score: 50–55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The explicit forward guidance is "extended period" language retained for near-zero rates. Alternative B keeps "extended period" with new conditionality language. Dudley clarifies "extended" = no tightening for more than 6 months. Kohn's tightening path: mid-2011. Evans: "any tightening...before June 2010 seems unlikely." The guidance is firmly in the "strong dovish: signal further/extended accommodation ahead" range — it's a QE era with explicit "extended period" language.

Direction: DOVISH forward signal. "Extended period" of near-zero rates plus QE completion — no tightening signal.
Magnitude: "Strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation ahead" = 6–15 range. Score: 12–15. The "extended period" is being maintained with new conditionality language — it is not quite as dovish as the March 2009 emergency signal but is still strong easing commitment. Score: 13.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Substantial uncertainty expressed by virtually all participants. Warsh: "giant amount of uncertainty around any forecast." Tarullo: "considerable uncertainty" about 2H 2010 and beyond. Fisher: uncertainty about future policy environment creating cap-ex hesitancy. Yellen: "economic outlook remains very uncertain." Pianalto: "commercial real estate problems add another dimension of uncertainty." Bernanke: "leaving considerable uncertainty about the strength of the recovery going forward." Multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty about inflation dynamics (output gap debate), about banking sector trajectory, about consumer spending sustainability. However, near-term direction (moderate recovery) has some clarity; it's the 2010-2011 outlook that's most uncertain.

Direction: ELEVATED uncertainty — "multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty."
Magnitude: "Elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty; particularly uncertain" = 46–60 range. Near-term recovery path has some consensus, but 2H 2010 onward is highly uncertain. Score: 58.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Dudley: "The core PCE deflator has risen only 1.3 percent over the past year, down more than a percentage point from a year ago...unit labor costs are falling quite rapidly."
- Yellen: "I expect substantial slack in labor and goods markets to reduce core PCE price inflation from about 1½ percent this year to 1 percent in 2010 and 2011."
- Bullard: "I continue to believe that there is a near-term deflation risk and a medium-term risk of higher-than-desired inflation...the near-term deflation risk is fading."

**Center of gravity:** The overwhelming majority view near-term inflation as subdued and below target, with core PCE at ~1.3% and falling; deflation risk acknowledged but described as fading. A minority (Plosser, Bullard) warn of medium-term upside risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 23 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

**Center of gravity:** All participants agree the economy is far below potential. Q3 GDP was +3.5% but is the first positive print after four consecutive declines. The output gap is large; recovery is beginning but slow. Consistent with GFC trough anchor of 15–22, scoring slightly above deepest trough given first positive growth print.

**Minority views:** Plosser projects ~3% average growth through 2010–12 and is more confident about recovery sustainability, but even he acknowledges the gap is large.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 35 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Fisher: "Little—actually no—enthusiasm for expanding payrolls and cap-ex...domestic cap-ex is dead." AT&T cutting cap-ex 15%, head count by 12,000.
- Rosengren: "Growth in consumption and housing...both grew in the third quarter, they were supported by a variety of government programs, which are now in the process of winding down."

**Center of gravity:** Spending and investment are below trend. Government programs (cash-for-clunkers, homebuyer credit) provided temporary lift; underlying private demand is weak. Business investment is effectively frozen. Housing stabilizing from low base. Not in freefall (recovery underway) but clearly "below trend; sluggish."

**Minority views:** Lacker and Bernanke note some upside risks — consumer spending "modestly stronger even outside autos." Evans notes 2–3% growth more consistent with contacts than the 3.5% GDP print.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "Bank credit remains quite tight. On the quantity side, bank lending continued to decline very rapidly...banks continue to cut back on all kinds of credit lines."
- Evans: "Small and medium-sized companies that rely on bank credit continue to complain about onerous terms and an inadequate supply of borrowing options."
- Bernanke: "Nominal C&I bank loans outstanding are down about 12 percent, or $200 billion, over the past three quarters...terms and conditions...far greater than would be expected historically."

**Center of gravity:** Conditions are bifurcated — large firms with capital market access find conditions "closer to normal" (spreads narrowed), but the banking channel is severely restricted for small/medium businesses. Net effect: somewhat tight overall, tighter than neutral especially through bank credit channel.

**Minority views:** Fisher, Yellen: large firms have "robust and better priced credit markets," "risk spreads have come down." This bifurcation informs the wider ±8 band.
