### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** from the September 21, 2010 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (MR. PLOSSER., MR. BULLARD., MS. YELLEN., etc.). The document includes both the economic go-around and the subsequent policy discussion round. The transcript is well-segmented with clear speaker attributions throughout. The policy discussion is particularly rich, running from roughly page 83 to page 127 of the original.

### 2. Era Context

September 2010 is mid-post-GFC recovery. The economy has been in recovery since mid-2009 but the recovery is widely described as "slow," "tepid," or "sluggish." Unemployment is near 9.6 percent. The FOMC is at the zero lower bound and debating whether to launch a second round of large-scale asset purchases (QE2, ultimately announced in November 2010). Core inflation is running around 1 percent, well below the Committee's implicit ~2 percent objective. This context establishes baseline expectations: OUTPUT_GAP should be well below 50 (significant slack), LABOR_TIGHTNESS should be well below 50 (very high unemployment), INFLATION_PRESSURE should be well below 50 (disinflation), HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN should be firmly dovish (debating more easing).

I note this context but score ONLY from what the text actually says.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 22 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Participants overwhelmingly describe inflation as well below target and trending lower, with disinflation the baseline condition and deflation a credible tail risk.

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "We continue to see evidence of disinflation. The core CPI is currently just below 1 percent and is expected to remain far below 2 percent through the forecast period of 2012."
- Bullard: "Inflation measured from one year ago continues to be about 1 percent by a variety of measures. Inflation expectations measured by market-based TIPS break-even inflation rates seem to be moving somewhat lower."
- Yellen: "Inflation is running below desired levels" and "the probability distribution of future inflation outcomes is too low."
- Bernanke (summary): "Inflation remains low, with measures of underlying inflation having fallen since the start of the recession."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Essentially all participants see inflation significantly below the ~2% target, with most characterizing the trajectory as disinflation and several explicitly flagging deflation as a credible risk; this places the score firmly in the 11-25 range (meaningful deflation risk, "subdued," "muted").

**Minority views:** Lacker views current 1.5% inflation as acceptable and not alarming; Plosser sees "little evidence to suggest that sustained deflation is a serious risk." These hawks are outliers — even they do not claim inflation is at or above target.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 37 ±5 | Confidence: High**

Participants see the economy as clearly below potential, with significant slack persisting, though positive output growth is occurring.

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "there is about 3 percentage points of excess unemployment, considerably more than at this stage of the recoveries in 1975 and 1983... there will be considerable slack remaining for years."
- Bernanke: "Economic growth has slowed moderately further... At this pace of growth, progress in reducing the unemployment rate is likely to be quite slow."
- Evans: "the economy continues to grow, but the pace of expansion is inadequate to meaningfully reduce the rolls of the unemployed at anything but a glacial pace."
- Tarullo: "nothing that has happened in the intermeeting period has changed my view that the only open question is how much the economy will underperform for the foreseeable future."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants agree the output gap is substantial and persistent (anchor zone 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery); no one claims economy is near potential, but positive growth distinguishes this from the 2009 trough (15-22).

**Minority views:** Hoenig takes a relatively sanguine view ("we are really on a fairly good path"), and Lacker sees "modest recovery in place," but neither claims above-potential growth. Warsh raises supply-side (lower potential GDP) as a mitigant of measured gap.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 30 ±5 | Confidence: High**

With unemployment at ~9.6%, widespread job loss across sectors, and only modest private payroll growth, the labor market is deeply slack.

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "Only 10 percent [of small businesses] said yes [to hard-to-fill jobs], which remains near the 25-year low for this survey question."
- Yellen: "with an abundance of qualified applicants and a lack of sales, firms may well see some option value in holding vacancies open longer as they search for the really exceptional candidate."
- Bernanke (summary): "The labor market remains weak, having started from a deep hole. Employment in many sectors has fallen significantly... Wage gains remain very limited."
- Evans: businesses "have no intention of doing any serious hiring. At best, they are recalling some laid-off workers."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unemployment near 9.6%, widespread sectoral job losses, worker abundance rather than scarcity — firmly in the 16-30 range (high unemployment, significant slack, "far from full employment"). Scored at 30 rather than lower because private payrolls have been positive since January and recovery is underway.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota raises structural factors that might imply a higher effective NAIRU (perhaps 8%), which would mean cyclical slack is somewhat smaller. Plosser also emphasizes structural/supply factors. These views are noted but are minority positions on this dimension.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 36 ±6 | Confidence: High**

Consumer spending growing modestly but below trend; housing deeply depressed; business investment mixed (equipment/software brisk, hiring frozen).

**Evidence:**
- Lacker: "Consumer spending has been rising pretty steadily at an annual rate of about a 2 percent. It is likely to continue to expand at that pace."
- Pianalto: "lack of demand as the single most important problem facing the respondents [small businesses]... not expecting more than a gradual pickup in demand."
- Duke: "residential construction falling at an annual rate of 60 percent in the second quarter... construction and land development loans continue to deteriorate."
- Bernanke (summary): "moderate increases in consumer spending and modest job creation... Housing has continued to be very weak."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The net picture across consumer spending (modest), business investment (mixed, with housing severely depressed), and overall demand is clearly below trend — "sluggish" is the dominant characterization, placing this in the 31-45 range.

**Minority views:** Fisher reports a surprising pickup in retail activity in September, and Lacker notes business investment in equipment/software is "expanding briskly." These pockets of strength are real but do not shift the net assessment above trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

Financial conditions are accommodative-to-neutral for large firms; restrictive for housing/households; aggregate picture is broadly supportive but with pockets of tightness.

**Evidence:**
- Fisher: "my interlocutors report their companies have even better access to credit than before... enjoy abundant liquidity." Hoenig: "We have more deposits than we can deploy. Liquidity is not an issue for us."
- Warsh: "Credit spreads continue to be narrow, and corporate issuance continues to be impressive."
- Duke: "loans for expansion or to cover cash flow shortfalls hard to get"; housing and CRE credit severely restricted; "weak loan demand."
- Dudley: "significant constraints on credit availability remain in place" for households.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** For the corporate/financial sector credit is quite loose (low rates, narrow spreads, abundant liquidity); for households and housing it is tight. The overall picture leans slightly loose to neutral (below 50), consistent with post-QE1 conditions at the zero lower bound. Scored 38 reflecting the accommodative overall stance offset by household/housing credit restriction.

**Minority views:** Hoenig raises concern that easy monetary conditions are fueling risk-taking in farmland and duration-extension, suggesting conditions may be too loose. This is a concern about future financial stability, not current tightness.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The Committee majority — including the Chair — is actively preparing QE2 and firmly in the 15-25 QE-era dovish range. The 22 score reflects that actual QE action was not taken today but is clearly imminent.

**Minority views:** Hoenig (wants to remove "extended period," opposes balance sheet expansion), Plosser (not in favor of LSAP or signaling it), Lacker (does not favor moving, thinks current stance appropriate), Fisher (skeptical of transmission mechanism). These 4 hawks hold the score above the 1-10 emergency dovish range.

The anchor for 2010-11 QE2 debates is 55-65; scored at 57 reflecting significant but not extreme division.
