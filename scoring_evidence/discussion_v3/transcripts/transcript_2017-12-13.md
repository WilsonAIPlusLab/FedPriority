# FOMC Transcript Scoring — December 12–13, 2017

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an FOMC meeting held over two days (December 12–13, 2017). Individual participant statements are clearly attributed by name. Day 1 (December 12) contains the economic go-around; Day 2 (December 13) contains the policy go-around. Both are included in the source file. Speakers include: Rosengren, Evans, Williams, Bullard, Mester, Bostic, Harker, Kaplan, Quarles, George, Kashkari, Brainard, Powell, Dudley (Vice Chairman), and Chair Yellen. Acting President Mullinix appears briefly but makes no substantive remarks on the record.

### 2. Era Context
December 2017. The U.S. economy is in late-cycle expansion: unemployment at 4.1 percent, GDP growing above potential (~2.5 percent). Core PCE inflation is running around 1.4–1.5 percent year-over-year — persistently below the 2 percent target, though recent monthly readings (September, October) had ticked up. A large corporate/individual tax cut package (Tax Cuts and Jobs Act) is imminent. The Fed is in a hiking cycle begun in December 2015; this meeting is expected to deliver the third 25bp hike of 2017. Financial conditions remain accommodative despite three years of gradual tightening. The yield curve is flattening (10yr–2yr spread ~58bp, lowest since 2007). This context frames the scoring — I will use what participants actually say, not just the macro backdrop.

### 3. Directional Analysis per Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants broadly see inflation as **below target** — core PCE ~1.4–1.45 percent on a 12-month basis, which multiple participants note is "disquietingly low" (Yellen), "substantially below" our 2 percent target (Evans), or "large shortfall" (Kashkari). However, the most recent monthly data (September, October) show some firming (annualized 3-month core PCE at ~1.9 percent per Rosengren; George cites 1.9 percent annualized over past 3 months). The center of gravity: inflation is clearly below target, with tentative signs of firming. Most participants expect it to gradually return to 2 percent over 2–3 years; a minority (Evans, Bullard, Kashkari) are more pessimistic. This maps to the 36–42 range (below target; disinflation/subdued but with cautious hope for firming). The "below target" language is strong and consistent across virtually all speakers, but the alarm level is moderate, not extreme. Score: ~38.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Virtually every participant describes GDP growth as **above potential** — Williams: "above the economy's potential growth rate"; Powell: "above-trend pace"; Yellen: "real GDP appears to have continued to expand somewhat faster than potential output"; Williams: "growing well above the potential rate"; Mester: "above-trend real GDP growth." Estimates coalesce around 2.5 percent growth vs. ~1.75 percent potential. The bright-line rule requires score > 50 for any "above potential" language, and this language is near-unanimous. The degree of above-trend is moderate (not extreme), suggesting 53–58. Score: ~55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants nearly universally describe the labor market as **tight or at/beyond full employment** — Powell: "by most indicators, suggests we are now at full employment"; Williams: "unemployment rate...close to 4 percent and moved an array of other labor market indicators well past pre-recession levels"; Yellen: "4.1 percent, now stands almost ½ percentage point below my assessment of its longer-run sustainable level"; Mester: "unemployment rate falling further below most estimates of its longer-run rate." Rosengren forecasts it will fall 1 percentage point below sustainable NAIRU. Kashkari and Evans are the outliers, arguing slack remains (Kashkari: "we've likely underestimated labor market slack"). The majority view, however, is clearly tight or beyond full employment, with the unemployment rate below most NAIRU estimates (4.1 percent vs. ~4.5–4.75 percent estimates). Score: ~63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants describe consumer spending as solid and business investment as picking up — Williams: "solid gains in consumer and business spending"; Evans: "business demand for capital equipment appears to be strong...blowout"; consumer spending "remains on solid footing"; Bostic: "holiday season activity has been strong"; Brainard: "business spending on equipment and intangibles is projected to increase at an 8.5 percent annual rate." Several note uncertainty about tax cut effects on investment but characterize current underlying spending as solid/robust. Score: ~57.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Nearly all participants describe financial conditions as **accommodative/loose** — Brainard: "financial conditions remain very accommodative by historical standards"; Dudley: "financial market conditions today are even easier than when we began to remove monetary policy accommodation in 2015"; Mester: "accommodative monetary policy and financial conditions"; Williams: "supportive financial conditions." Equity prices up ~18 percent YTD, credit spreads near historical lows, dollar depreciated. This maps to low/moderate financial tightness (conditions are loose, not tight). Higher = tighter. Score: ~28.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The committee is in a hiking cycle. The clear majority supports alternative B (25bp hike). Support for hike: Williams, Rosengren, Mester, Bostic, Quarles, Kaplan, George, Powell, Dudley, Yellen, Harker — roughly 11 of 17 speaking participants explicitly support B. Dissent/no-hike: Evans (explicit "I do not support raising the federal funds rate today"), Kashkari (supports alternative A, no hike), Bullard (accepting today's hike but strongly against further 2018 hikes). The forward lean from the majority is toward 2–3 more hikes in 2018, though several urge patience (Harker: 2 hikes; Brainard: gradual; Bostic: data-dependent). This is a meeting where most participants advocate continued tightening — consistent with the 70–75 range (strong hawkish: consensus for continued tightening). Score: ~72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Two participants explicitly do not support the hike (Evans, Kashkari). Bullard accepts the current hike but argues the Committee is too hawkish for 2018. Several participants (Harker, Brainard, Bostic) express qualified support with significant inflation-related reservations. The vote will record 2 formal dissents (Evans and Kashkari on the no-hike side). The rubric notes formal voting dissents > reservations; 2 dissents + 3-4 meaningful reservations puts this at moderate-to-notable disagreement. Score: ~45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Multiple participants discuss financial stability or financial conditions meaningfully — Rosengren (detailed leveraged loan market analysis), Kaplan (elevated valuations, need for "heightened vigilance"), Mester ("lofty" CRE, high stock valuations), Brainard (very accommodative conditions, dollar, equity prices), Dudley (financial conditions accommodative), George (balance sheet normalization implications). This is more than passing mentions but not the dominant focus — the primary focus is inflation/employment. Score: ~42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The majority supports a gradual continuation of hikes — Williams envisions 3 hikes in 2018 and 3 in 2019; Rosengren wants 5 hikes in 2018; George keeps 4 hikes for next year; Dudley and Powell pencil in 3 hikes for 2018. The median SEP path implies ~3 hikes. The signal is clearly hawkish tightening direction, but "gradual" is the operative word. Brainard and Harker emphasize data dependence and downside risks. Forward guidance (in terms of the paragraph 4 debate) signals continued normalization with some data conditionality. Score: ~75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Several participants highlight multiple sources of uncertainty: the tax cut effects (acknowledged as highly uncertain by nearly all — Williams, Quarles, Yellen, Powell, Brainard), the Phillips curve (flat or broken?), inflation trajectory (the dominant uncertainty), yield curve implications. Harker notes "my concern with my inflation projection remains elevated"; Bostic: inflation forecast is "an article of faith"; Brainard flags "considerable uncertainty" about fiscal effects. However, the overall uncertainty level is "elevated but not extreme" — standard hedging about an ongoing recovery with known upside from fiscal stimulus and the usual downside risks. The tax reform uncertainty is novel but bounded. Score: ~45.

### 4. Polarity Verification
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative. Score of 28 = loose. Matches (low score = loose conditions). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score 55. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says majority hawkish (hiking, 2–3 more in 2018). Score 72. Correct.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more inflation. Evidence says below target, ~1.4% core PCE. Score 38. Correct.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63): GDP is above potential but labor market is notably tighter. This is coherent — the labor market is often described as the leading signal, and most participants explicitly say unemployment is already below NAIRU while GDP is modestly above potential. The slight divergence (labor tighter than GDP gap would suggest) is mentioned in the text (Dudley notes asymmetry between labor market tightness and manufacturing capacity).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (75): Very well aligned. A hiking meeting with a signal of continued gradual hikes.

### 6. Speaker Count by View Direction
- Inflation below target / worrisome: Evans, Bullard, Kashkari, Brainard, Harker, Bostic (6 speakers explicitly concerned; majority)
- Inflation temporary/transitory and will firm: Williams, Rosengren, Powell, Mester, Quarles, Yellen, George, Dudley (8 speakers; majority, but with caveats)
- No hike today: Evans, Kashkari (2 explicit)
- Support hike: 11+ participants

### 7. Optional Dimensions
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Multiple participants discuss inflation expectations explicitly — Evans (survey-based too low, DSGE model below 2%), Bullard (market-based expectations signal miss on inflation target), Brainard (Cleveland 10-yr down >0.5pp from pre-crisis; Michigan subdued; SPF lower than pre-crisis), Kashkari (market- and survey-based measures low), Dudley (NY Fed 3-yr stable at 2.8%). Center of gravity: expectations are somewhat below target but not dramatically de-anchored; modest slippage. Score: ~38 (below target anchor, not unanchored). Meets the 2+ speaker threshold.
- **EQUITY_STRESS**: Several discuss equity markets but primarily as context for financial conditions (accommodative), not stress. Equity prices are high, seen as elevated but not crash/stress. The rubric says EQUITY_STRESS is about concern; valuation concern (bubble direction) = low score. Kaplan and Mester note "high stock valuations" and potential imbalances — modest valuation concern but not extreme. Score: ~20 (slight overvaluation concern, low stress from collapse perspective). Meets threshold.
- **DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Brainard discusses dollar explicitly: dollar has depreciated ~6% YTD, partially unwinding prior appreciation; risk of future appreciation given divergent monetary policy. Concern is more about potential future strength, not current extreme. Score: ~52 (slight strength concern noted but not acute). Borderline — only 1 participant discusses it substantively. Setting to N/A.
- **PRODUCTIVITY**: Bullard discusses productivity at length (low productivity growth state, 90–95% probability); Kaplan discusses disruption and technology replacing workers; Williams discusses g* revision upward to 1.7%. Broadly: productivity seen as low, but some hints of possible improvement. Score: ~35 (below historical trend; low state). Meets threshold.
- **CREDIT_STRESS**: Rosengren discusses leveraged loan market and covenant exceptions; Mester mentions "doesn't seem to be excessive leverage." Brief/limited. N/A.
- **BANKING_STRESS**: Mester: "banks have relatively high levels of capital and liquid assets." No stress discussion. N/A.
- **TREASURY_STRESS**: Multiple yield curve discussions but about shape/inversion risk, not market dysfunction. N/A.
- **INFLATION_GOODS**: Dudley and Brainard discuss goods vs. services inflation briefly. Goods prices low globally; manufactured goods prices not responsive to wages. Score: ~30 (below trend, dampened by global factors). Borderline — 2 speakers. Score it.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES**: Discussed by Mester (service sector price increases), Dudley (services wages feed through to services prices). Services inflation somewhat firmer. Score: ~47. But evidence is limited. N/A (insufficient distinct 2-speaker emphasis on services specifically).
- **INFLATION_HOUSING**: George explicitly mentions housing services inflation at "annualized rate of about 3½ percent over the past six months." One primary source. N/A (only 1 strong mention).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±6 | Moderate Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "the 12-month measure of core inflation remains disquietingly low—well below our 2 percent objective"
- Kashkari: "The core 12-month PCE inflation rate remains low at 1.45 percent on a 12-month basis. This is a large shortfall relative to our target of 2 percent."
- Rosengren: "the annualized core PCE inflation rate over the past three months has been 1.9 percent, a rate substantially above the average over the past year"
- Brainard: "over the 12 months through October, core PCE price inflation was only 1.4 percent...As we look over the past five years, core inflation has systematically fallen short of our 2 percent objective"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as below target (~1.4% core PCE year-over-year), with transitory factors given credit for much of the 2017 shortfall and tentative recent signs of firming, but no participant describes current inflation as at or above target.

**Minority views:** Rosengren and Williams see the most recent monthly data as meaningfully encouraging (3-month annualized near 1.9%), pulling marginally toward the 40s; Evans, Kashkari, and Bullard are more pessimistic about the trajectory, pushing toward the low 30s.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 55 ±5 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "real GDP growth will likely come in close to 2.5 percent this year...this pace is above the economy's potential growth rate"
- Yellen: "real GDP appears to have continued to expand somewhat faster than potential output in the second half of the year"
- Rosengren: "real GDP growth for the past three quarters of this year likely to exceed my estimate of potential growth of 1¾ percent by more than 1 percentage point"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous agreement that GDP is growing above potential, but the excess is moderate (~0.5–1 percentage point above trend), placing the economy modestly above potential rather than severely overheated.

**Minority views:** Bullard notes slowing to ~2 percent is "most likely scenario" and emphasizes low long-run potential growth, suggesting the overshoot may be modest or temporary.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 ±7 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Powell: "the labor market, by most indicators, suggests we are now at full employment. Indeed, the unemployment rate is now below all of our individual assessments of the natural rate."
- Yellen: "at 4.1 percent, now stands almost ½ percentage point below my assessment of its longer-run sustainable level"
- Mester: "unemployment rate will move below 4 percent next year, well below most estimates of its longer-run level"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The overwhelming majority of participants view the labor market as at or beyond full employment, with unemployment (4.1%) below individual NAIRU estimates, though wage growth has not fully responded (moderate surprise), consistent with tight but not unprecedented tightness.

**Minority views:** Kashkari and Evans argue there is more residual slack than the headline suggests — Kashkari: "we've likely underestimated labor market slack...continued strong job growth coupled with a lack of wage growth is hard to reconcile with the idea that we're at maximum employment."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 ±6 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "business spending on equipment and intangibles is projected to increase at an 8.5 percent annual rate in the second half of the year"
- Evans: "business demand for capital equipment appears to be strong. Caterpillar, for example, characterized sales last quarter as a 'blowout.'"
- Bostic: "holiday season activity has been strong"
- Williams: "solid gains in consumer and business spending"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is on a solid footing and business investment has reaccelerated notably in H2 2017, placing aggregate demand clearly above trend; the tax cut debate centers on whether investment will accelerate further, from an already elevated base.

**Minority views:** Bostic's survey found two-thirds of firms say tax reform wouldn't affect capex — suggesting investment's forward trajectory is uncertain, holding the score below the 60s.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±6 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "financial conditions remain very accommodative by historical standards—a prominent theme in my discussions with market participants. Equity prices are up nearly 3 percent since our meeting and 18 percent so far this year. Corporate bond spreads remain below longer-run averages, especially in the case of high-yield bonds, for which spreads are near historical lows."
- Dudley: "financial market conditions today are even easier than when we began to remove monetary policy accommodation in 2015"
- Rosengren: "the modest reductions in the balance sheet and the increases to date in the federal funds rate have not led to a tightening of overall financial conditions"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple participants converge on the assessment that financial conditions remain loose/accommodative despite three years of rate hikes, with equity prices elevated, credit spreads compressed, and the dollar weaker — this is loose, not tight, financial conditions.

**Minority views:** None — this assessment is near-unanimous.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 72 ±7 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I support alternative B...I envision three rate increases in the coming year and an additional three in 2019"
- Rosengren: "I support alternative B...five ¼ point change increases over the course of 2018"
- Mester: "I support alternative B...the gradual removal of accommodation as being the best strategy"
- Evans: "I do not support raising the federal funds rate today"
- Kashkari: "I support alternative A...I continue to believe that we should not raise rates further absent evidence of a sustained increase in inflation"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Approximately 11 of 13 substantive policy voters support the 25bp hike (alternative B), with the median path implying 2–3 further hikes in 2018; 2 participants formally oppose (Evans, Kashkari), and a few others (Harker, Bullard) express reservations about the forward path but accept today's action.

**Minority views:** Evans and Kashkari favor no action; Bullard accepts the hike but argues the projected 2018 path is too aggressive; Harker and Brainard urge caution/patience on future moves.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 ±8 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "I do not support raising the federal funds rate today" (explicit dissent)
- Kashkari: "I support alternative A" (explicit dissent)
- Bullard: accepts today's hike but: "I think we're too 'hawkish' for 2018" and prefers language from alternative A for paragraph 4
- Harker: "I support alternative B at this meeting, but I'm somewhat disappointed with the removal of the paragraph 4 language"
- Bostic: "I support the policy action in alternative B. However, I continue to worry that my forecast of inflation is overly optimistic"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Two formal dissents (Evans, Kashkari), one participant effectively dissenting on the forward path (Bullard), and three participants expressing significant reservations — this is notable disagreement, in the 40–50 range, above a routine one-dissent meeting but below a deep committee split.

**Minority views:** The dissents are all from the dovish side (no hawkish dissents), limiting the "cross-camp" character of disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 42 ±8 | Moderate Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: extended analysis of leveraged loan market and regulatory arbitrage; "financial stability risks posed by maintaining low interest rates"
- Mester: "commercial real estate valuations are lofty, and the combination of low market volatility and equity prices that appear high in relation to earnings...suggests that financial stability risks may be building"
- Kaplan: "elevated levels of today's real asset and financial markets versus historical measures...need for heightened vigilance"
- Brainard: extensive discussion of financial conditions (equity prices, credit spreads, dollar)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is a meaningful but secondary topic — raised substantively by 4–5 participants with specific examples (CRE, leveraged loans, equity valuations), but it is clearly subordinate to the inflation and employment discussion.

**Minority views:** Dudley is relatively unconcerned about financial stability risks at this juncture ("these moves have not yet tightened overall financial market conditions").

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 75 ±8 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I envision three rate increases in the coming year and an additional three in 2019, with the funds rate eventually reaching 3 percent in 2020"
- Rosengren: "five ¼ point change increases over the course of 2018"
- Yellen: "a somewhat steeper path of the federal funds rate over the next few years may be called for...perhaps three hikes in 2018 might be appropriate"
- George: "four rate increases for next year"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The collective forward signal is for continued gradual tightening — the median of participant projections implies approximately 2–3 more hikes in 2018 and further hikes in 2019, consistent with a clear tightening signal; the "gradual" qualifier keeps this from scoring higher.

**Minority views:** Evans (no hike today, wants to pause), Kashkari (no further hikes absent inflation pickup), Bullard (projects current policy as already at or above neutral); Harker and Bostic favor only 2 hikes in 2018.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 ±8 | High Confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Williams (on tax reform): "major tax overhauls are not frequent, and estimates of their effects come with considerable uncertainty"
- Quarles: "I do think there's a very high level of uncertainty about what the consequences of the change in the bill in the pass-through tax rate are going to be"
- Harker: "my concern with my inflation projection remains elevated"
- Bostic: "my modal expectation is still that inflation will converge to target by the end of next year. But, as I said last time, that is an article of faith"
- Powell: "I also realize that this is far from certain" (on transitory inflation factors)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is elevated above normal, primarily driven by (1) the novel tax legislation and its uncertain macroeconomic effects, and (2) the persistently unexplained inflation shortfall. However, most participants still have a clear modal forecast and balanced risk assessment, preventing this from reaching the "high/extreme" uncertainty range.

**Minority views:** Williams notes he "views the risks to this outlook as balanced and not unusually large relative to the past 20 years" — pulling toward the lower end.
