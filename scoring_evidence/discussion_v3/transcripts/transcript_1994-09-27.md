# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-09-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers the go-around (economic conditions by district) and a policy discussion. Speakers include: Forrestal, Parry, Boehne, McDonough, Broaddus, Lindsey, Jordan, Hoenig, Moskow, Stern, McTeer, Melzer, LaWare, Kelley, Blinder, Phillips, and Chairman Greenspan. Yellen and Minehan appear to have been skipped or had no recorded remarks.

### Era Context
September 1994: The Fed had already raised rates from 3% to 4.75% over the course of 1994 in a preemptive tightening cycle. The economy was in its fourth year of expansion following the 1990-91 recession. GDP growth had been running around 4% over the prior four quarters. Capacity utilization was elevated. Inflation was around 3% CPI but there were signs of upward pressure in input prices and PPI. The bond market was nervous, with long rates at their highest since the 1990-91 recession.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target and with upside risks. Multiple speakers note rising input/raw materials prices (Forrestal, Boehne, Hoenig, Moskow, Melzer, Phillips). However, the center of gravity notes that pass-through to final goods is limited by competitive pressures. Greenspan himself notes "surprisingly little talk of more price pressures around the room" despite strong activity. CPI around 3%, with projections of 3-3.25% going forward. Melzer is the most alarmed (CPI at 4.1% annualized in Jul-Aug, PPI over 6%). The majority sees inflation as somewhat elevated but not dramatically so. Direction: modestly above target, with upward risk. Score range: 55-62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly all participants describe the economy as operating at or above potential. Parry: "Labor and product markets currently appear to be tight." Moskow: "little slack remains in the economy." Phillips: "most of the slack in labor and product markets is used up." Greenspan: economy approaching capacity limits. Multiple districts report strong activity, manufacturing at capacity, auto production "maxed out." Blinder argues the strength is concentrated in durables (10-12% of economy) and the rest grew only 1.6%. But the center of gravity clearly sees the economy above potential. Direction: above potential. Score range: 55-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants describe tight labor markets. Jordan: "Labor markets generally are characterized as tight throughout the District" with "shortage of teenagers." Melzer: "Reports of labor shortages in certain trades and geographic areas are increasing," unemployment at lowest since August 1974. Moskow: temporary workers paid 5-10% more, bonuses for engineers and truck drivers. Stern: "Labor markets have tightened further and jobs are certainly readily available." Phillips: "over 3 million jobs this past year." However, wage pressures remain largely subdued -- Boehne: "Wage pressures remain subdued." Multiple speakers note limited pass-through to wages for permanent workers. Direction: tight but without broad wage pressure yet. Score range: 57-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: The economy shows above-trend spending and investment. Business fixed investment and consumer durables are booming (Blinder: 11.3% growth in these sectors). Auto production at capacity, capital expenditure plans improving, retailers optimistic about holiday sales. However, some moderation in consumer spending noted, housing slowing. Greenspan sees inventory building as voluntary. The center of gravity: above trend overall, driven by investment and durables. Direction: above trend. Score range: 58-65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Bond rates at highest since 1990-91 recession (Broaddus, Melzer). Housing has slowed in response to rate hikes. But Stern notes credit is "a good deal more readily available today to small and medium-size businesses." Boehne reports "slippage in underwriting standards." Phillips: "capital markets are producing a cost of capital that is clearly supportive of investment and expansion." McTeer notes aggressive bank lending competition. The picture is mixed: rates have risen significantly but credit conditions remain supportive. Direction: somewhat tighter than neutral due to rate increases, but credit still flowing freely. Score range: 48-55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The vote was 11-1 for no change (Broaddus dissented wanting 50bp hike). Greenspan proposes "B" asymmetric (hold with tightening bias). Most support this. Broaddus and Melzer wanted 50bp immediately (alternative D). Stern also leaned toward immediate action. The majority supports holding but expects further tightening soon -- Greenspan says next move likely 50bp, probably at November meeting. Parry: "we should raise the funds rate again fairly soon." This is a hold-with-strong-tightening-bias meeting. Direction: moderately hawkish. Score range: 62-68.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Broaddus). Melzer and Stern also wanted to move immediately. Jordan expressed discomfort with asymmetry concept. But the broad majority agreed on holding with asymmetric bias. The disagreement is mainly about timing (now vs. November), not direction. Score range: 30-40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Relatively limited discussion. Phillips briefly mentions stock market resilience and bond market nervousness. Lindsey discusses potential market turmoil from Japan trade negotiations and German elections. Boehne notes slippage in bank underwriting standards. But financial stability per se is not a major focus -- the discussion is about macro conditions and inflation. Direction: limited. Score range: 15-25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very clearly hawkish forward signal. Greenspan: "I suspect we are going to need to move further somewhere along the line" and if they move at November meeting, "that would probably require us to move another 50 basis points and probably move the discount rate as well." McDonough: next move would be 50bp. Parry: should raise fairly soon. Multiple participants expect further tightening. Direction: clearly hawkish forward lean. Score range: 70-78.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. Boehne: "The major uncertainty, however, is what lies beyond the next quarter or two." Greenspan discusses competing models (Phillips curve vs. monetary). Forrestal: "simply too early to determine whether we should be worried or not." Multiple speakers note difficulty distinguishing cyclical blip from sustained inflation. Blinder notes concentrated nature of growth creates analytical challenges. But this is standard mid-expansion uncertainty, not extreme. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score range: 38-48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, aligned. Labor slightly tighter than output gap reflects the widespread reports of shortages even while some sectors still have slack.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (74): Forward guidance more hawkish than current action, which makes sense -- they held today but signal 50bp coming. This divergence is explained by the "for the time being" language from August constraining current action.

### Speaker Count Summary
- Strong economy / above potential: Forrestal, Parry, Broaddus, Jordan, Hoenig, Moskow, Stern, Melzer, Phillips, Greenspan (~10 speakers)
- Some moderation possible: McDonough, Boehne, Blinder, LaWare (~4 speakers)
- Hold rates: Forrestal, McDonough, Boehne, Jordan, Hoenig, Moskow, McTeer, Blinder, Phillips, Kelley, LaWare, Lindsey, Parry, Greenspan (~14)
- Move now (50bp): Broaddus, Melzer, Stern (~3)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. FORRESTAL: "We have inflation edging higher until at least the middle of 1996... the current pressure on prices is due to aggregate demand growing more quickly than aggregate supply."
- MR. MELZER: "consumer prices accelerated to an annual rate of 4.1 percent and producer prices to an annual rate of more than 6 percent. Inflation is certainly headed in the wrong direction."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I did read your comments on inflation as implying slightly less upward pressure on prices. There was surprisingly little talk of more price pressures around the room."
- Multiple speakers note rising input prices but limited pass-through to final goods due to competitive pressures (Forrestal, Boehne, Moskow, Jordan).

**Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the ~3% level, with input price pressures building but final goods prices still somewhat contained by competition and productivity. Upside risk acknowledged broadly but most do not see inflation as alarming yet.

**Minority views**: Melzer sees inflation as clearly headed wrong direction (would push toward 65+). Jordan's contacts say inflation concern is "same or down" (would push toward 52-55). The range reflects this spread.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "Labor and product markets currently appear to be tight. This impression was reinforced by the sizable increase in capacity utilization rates."
- MR. MOSKOW: "We concur with the Greenbook assessment that little slack remains in the economy."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "most of the slack in labor and product markets is used up"
- MR. BLINDER: Growth concentrated in 25% of GDP (11.3% growth) while rest grew 1.6%, tempering the overheating narrative.

**Center of gravity**: The economy is operating modestly above potential, with capacity utilization elevated and labor markets tight. Growth above potential rate but not dramatically overheating.

**Minority views**: Blinder argues the overheating is narrow (concentrated in durables manufacturing), which would push score lower. But even he does not claim the economy is below potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "Labor markets generally are characterized as tight throughout the District... In some communities, people associated with retail or fast food operations actually complain about a shortage of teenagers."
- MR. MELZER: "unemployment rate hovers near its lowest level since August 1974. Reports of labor shortages in certain trades and geographic areas are increasing."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Demand for temporary workers in our region is very strong, with these firms having to pay wages that are 5 to 10 percent higher than last year to attract workers."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Wage pressures remain subdued."

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are tight with widespread reports of shortages in specific trades and regions, but broad-based wage pressure has not yet materialized. Tight level, not extreme.

**Minority views**: Some districts (e.g., California at 9% unemployment per Parry) still have slack, tempering the national picture slightly.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 61 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BLINDER: "Those three sectors [BFI, consumer durables, housing] over the last four quarters registered an 11.3 percent rate of growth."
- MR. JORDAN: "Motor vehicle production essentially is at capacity... Retail sales were quite good."
- MR. BROADDUS: "The strong recent growth in investment activity, both fixed and inventory investment, is certainly not suggestive of a business expansion that is about to run out of steam."
- Housing slowing noted by multiple speakers; some consumer moderation reported.

**Center of gravity**: Business investment and consumer durables are well above trend, driving overall spending growth. Housing is softening. Net spending position is above trend but with emerging moderation on the consumer/housing side.

**Minority views**: McDonough sees GDP slowing to 2.25-2.5% range; Blinder expects weaker second half. But even these views acknowledge strong investment.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 50 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "credit is a good deal more readily available today to small and medium-size businesses than it was a year ago or even less. It does not appear that our moves toward higher interest rates have had any restraining effect in that sector."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Banks are reporting increasing loan volumes... There also are reports of a fairly general slippage in underwriting standards."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the capital markets are producing a cost of capital that is clearly supportive of investment and expansion."
- MR. BROADDUS: "The bond rate is now at its highest level since back in the 1990-1991 recession."

**Center of gravity**: Despite 175bp of rate hikes in 1994, financial conditions remain roughly neutral to slightly loose. Credit is freely available, banks are lending aggressively, and capital markets support investment. Bond rates are high but not creating meaningful tightening yet.

**Minority views**: The high bond rates (over 7.75%) suggest some tightening in rate-sensitive sectors (housing), but credit availability offsets this.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I suspect we are going to need to move further somewhere along the line... If we do move then [November], I think that would probably require us to move another 50 basis points and probably move the discount rate as well."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would prefer to move. My choice would be alternative D" (50bp hike now).
- MR. MELZER: "I'd favor 'D' combined with a discount rate increase; I'd do the 50 basis points now."
- MR. BLINDER: "'B' asymmetric is the right policy."

**Center of gravity**: Strong majority supports holding today but with a clear tightening bias. Nearly all participants expect further tightening; debate is about timing (now vs. November), not direction. Three participants wanted immediate 50bp action.

**Minority views**: Broaddus, Melzer, Stern wanted immediate action (~75-80 range). Blinder and Jordan more cautious on timing but still accept asymmetry toward tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Vote was 11-1 (Broaddus dissented for alternative D, 50bp hike).
- MR. MELZER: Also wanted alternative D with discount rate increase but voted yes.
- MR. STERN: Also leaned toward immediate action but did not formally dissent.
- MR. JORDAN: Uncomfortable with asymmetric directives but "would not dissent from it."

**Center of gravity**: One formal dissent with 2-3 additional participants expressing preference for immediate action. Disagreement is about timing, not fundamental direction. Moderate differences but broad agreement on overall trajectory.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 20 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. PHILLIPS: Brief discussion of stock market resilience and bond market nervousness: "stock markets have shown quite amazing resilience... Bond markets are certainly nervous."
- MR. LINDSEY: Extended discussion of potential market turmoil from Japan trade negotiations and German elections.
- MR. BOEHNE: "reports of a fairly general slippage in underwriting standards."

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is not a major focus. A few passing references to bond and stock markets, bank lending standards, and potential international disruptions, but the meeting is centered on real economic conditions and inflation.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 74 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I suspect we are going to need to move further somewhere along the line" and next move "probably require us to move another 50 basis points and probably move the discount rate as well."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "a tightening of monetary policy could be called for by incoming data over the intermeeting period... it would likely be a move of 50 basis points."
- MR. PARRY: "we should raise the funds rate again fairly soon."
- Asymmetric directive adopted tilted toward tightening.

**Center of gravity**: Very clear forward signal toward further tightening. The only question is timing (intermeeting vs. November), not whether. The signal is for a significant move (50bp) rather than incremental (25bp).

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. FORRESTAL: "it strikes me pretty forcefully that it's simply too early to determine whether we should be worried or not."
- MR. BOEHNE: "The major uncertainty, however, is what lies beyond the next quarter or two when some of the slackening of pent-up demand and lagged effects of previous interest rate hikes will be felt more fully."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of competing analytical frameworks (Phillips curve vs. monetary); "we are approaching a point where we will get interesting tests" of these models.
- MR. STERN: warns about "our ability to analyze the price data and come up with all these fine reasons why something unusual is going on and the rise in prices won't last."

**Center of gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Participants are confident about current conditions (strong economy, tight markets) but uncertain about the near-term trajectory -- whether growth will slow enough, whether inflation is a blip or persistent, and how much restraint is already in the pipeline. This is above normal hedging but not deeply uncertain.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: 58
Multiple speakers discuss rising input/raw materials prices -- steel (Melzer: two price increases totaling 10-12%), paper, chemicals, aluminum (Moskow). But competitive pressures limiting pass-through to final goods. Forrestal: "not able to pass these higher costs through to finished goods." Goods inflation pressure is real at the input level but dampened at the final goods level.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A
No specific discussion of services inflation by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A
Housing activity discussed (slowing) but not housing/shelter inflation specifically.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 58
Broaddus: "inflation expectations are rising at least in the financial markets. The bond rate is now at its highest level since back in the 1990-1991 recession... longer-term inflation expectations of market participants is something closer to 4 percent." Jordan notes "Wall Street versus Main Street" divergence -- financial markets worried about inflation, Main Street not. Greenspan references this divergence. Expectations somewhat above anchored level in financial markets.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A
Credit is described as freely available, not stressed. No meaningful discussion of credit stress per se.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A
Phillips notes stock market resilience but no valuation/stress concern discussed by 2+ participants.

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A
Banks described as in very good shape (Jordan: "strongest year in decades"). No stress discussion.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A
No discussion of Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A
Lindsey discusses dollar in context of Japan negotiations, but this is geopolitical, not a broad participant concern about dollar strength/weakness for the economy. Fewer than 2 participants address it substantively.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 62
Greenspan: productivity gains are "the major factor in suppressing the pass-through to final prices." Phillips discusses whether 1990s productivity trajectory will continue. Moskow: employers "believe that they can offset higher wages through productivity increases." Unit labor costs well contained. Productivity seen as above historical trend, helping contain inflation.
