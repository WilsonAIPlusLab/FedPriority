# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1993-02-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of a special FOMC session. It is NOT a regular policy meeting with a go-around on economic conditions. The entire session is devoted to discussing Chairman Greenspan's upcoming congressional testimony regarding President Clinton's deficit reduction/fiscal package announced the night before. Participants are reviewing and commenting on the draft testimony language, not assessing economic conditions or deliberating monetary policy.

### Era Context
February 1993: The U.S. economy is in the early stages of recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate is at 3%, a very low level. President Clinton has just announced a major deficit reduction package involving tax increases and some spending changes. Long-term bond rates have been declining. The discussion is dominated by fiscal policy communication strategy, not economic assessment.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: There is virtually no systematic discussion of current inflation conditions by participants. The only inflation-related content is (a) Greenspan's prepared testimony arguing one cannot "inflate our way out" of the structural deficit, which is a rhetorical point, not an assessment; (b) MR. ANGELL's advocacy for maintaining the price stability commitment; (c) a brief exchange about whether bond rate declines reflect deflation concerns (Greenspan: "there's a good chance that what that bond rate is doing is not an anti-inflation issue but a deflation--"; Mullins: "Yes."). This single exchange suggests some concern about deflationary risk, but it is a passing remark not a thorough assessment. The overall evidence is extremely thin. Given the era (early 1993, inflation was moderate/low around 3% CPI), and the limited signal of low-inflation/deflation concern, I score this modestly below the midpoint. Direction: slightly below target. Score: ~38, but with very low confidence.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Very limited discussion. Greenspan mentions Q4 GDP will likely be revised above 5% but Q1 is weaker ("if it's 3 percent they're doing well--and it looks less than that maybe"). He also notes one side of questioning will argue "faltering recovery with no employment growth." This suggests the economy is below potential but recovering unevenly. Direction: below potential but improving. Score: ~40. Low confidence due to minimal discussion.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Greenspan mentions "faltering recovery with no employment growth" as one line of questioning he expects. No other participant discusses labor markets. This suggests labor markets are weak. Direction: below full employment. Score: ~33. Low confidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: No substantive discussion of consumer spending or business investment by any participant. The GDP references are the only tangential evidence. N/A would be appropriate, but the rubric says to always score core dimensions. The GDP discussion suggests moderate/uneven growth. Score: ~40. Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants discuss long-term bond rates declining (the long bond "may well have a 6 before it instead of a 7" per Mullins). The broad tone is that financial conditions are accommodative -- the fed funds rate is at 3%, bond rates are falling. Greenspan and Corrigan discuss the risk that bond rates could reverse "with a vengeance" if fiscal credibility collapses, but current conditions are loose. Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative. Score: ~25. Moderate confidence (multiple participants discuss bond market conditions).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is not a policy deliberation meeting. There is no vote or policy discussion. However, the tenor of the discussion reveals key signals: (a) The fed funds rate is at 3% and no one suggests changing it; (b) Greenspan and others are very careful not to promise easing in response to fiscal consolidation; (c) Melzer explicitly warns against implying the Fed would ease if long rates fall; (d) The overall posture is "hold steady, stay noncommittal." This suggests a balanced-to-mildly-dovish hold posture. Direction: roughly balanced, leaning slightly dovish given 3% funds rate and no tightening talk. Score: ~45. Low-to-moderate confidence.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is no policy disagreement because there is no policy decision. The discussion shows broad agreement on the approach to testimony, with minor wording suggestions (Corrigan, Angell, Jordan, Melzer all offer refinements that Greenspan largely accepts). The only real tension is Angell's insistence on mentioning price stability, which others (Corrigan) gently push back on. This is mild disagreement about communication, not policy. Direction: low disagreement. Score: ~18. Moderate confidence.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The discussion touches on bond market reactions and the risk of reversal, but this is in the context of fiscal credibility and testimony preparation, not financial stability concerns per se. No participant raises systemic risk, credit conditions, or financial fragility. The bond market discussion is about market reactions to fiscal policy, not stability concerns. Direction: minimal. Score: ~12. Moderate confidence.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The explicit discussion about forward guidance is that the Fed should NOT give forward guidance. Melzer warns against implying easing, Corrigan warns against "dangling something out there," Jordan distinguishes between policy objectives and actions. The consensus is to remain deliberately vague about future policy direction. Direction: neutral/deliberately opaque. Score: ~45. Moderate confidence (several participants explicitly discuss this).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants express uncertainty about how the fiscal package will play out, how Congress will react, what bond markets are really signaling, and how monetary policy should respond. Greenspan: "There is absolutely no way we can know" (re: decomposition of long-rate moves). Corrigan: "this could turn around in a flash." The uncertainty is elevated but focused on fiscal/political developments rather than the economic outlook per se. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score: ~42. Moderate confidence.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, consistent with an economy still recovering from recession. Labor market slightly weaker, which matches "no employment growth" language.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (45): Both slightly dovish/neutral, consistent. The Fed is holding at 3% and deliberately not signaling.

### Important Caveats
This is an **anomalous meeting**. It is not a standard FOMC policy meeting. The entire discussion concerns the Chairman's congressional testimony about fiscal policy. There is no economic go-around, no staff presentation review, no policy vote. Scores are necessarily low-confidence and should be interpreted with extreme caution.

**Evidence**:

- MR. ANGELL pushes for more explicit price stability language; VICE CHAIRMAN CORRIGAN pushes back gently: "I really don't think it's in your interest--as a matter of fact I think it might be almost suicidal--to try to entangle the price stability argument into that context."

**Center-of-gravity**: Low disagreement. Participants are largely aligned on the communication strategy with only minor emphasis differences.

- VICE CHAIRMAN CORRIGAN: "this could turn around in a flash and with a vengeance" -- this is a market risk observation, not a systemic stability concern.

**Center-of-gravity**: Minimal financial stability emphasis. Bond market discussed only in the context of fiscal credibility communication.

**Center-of-gravity**: Neutral to slightly dovish. The Fed is at 3% and the discussion explicitly avoids committing to any direction, though the context (potential fiscal drag, deflation hint) leans slightly dovish.
