## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** — individual participant statements from a two-day FOMC meeting (July 28–29, 2015). Speakers are identified by name. The document covers: (a) a balance-sheet normalization discussion (Day 1, first session), (b) a brief financial-stability/Puerto Rico discussion, (c) the full economic go-around (Day 1, second session), and (d) the policy discussion/go-around (Day 2). Content is long and substantive. Participant count is high (approximately 17 identified speakers across both sessions). Confidence is therefore High for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
July 2015: The economy is in the post-GFC expansion, roughly six years into recovery. The Fed has been at the zero lower bound since December 2008. Liftoff from zero is imminent — most participants expect it in September 2015. Inflation is persistently below the 2 percent PCE target. The unemployment rate is near 5.3 percent, close to but arguably still above most natural-rate estimates. Internationally, China's stock market and growth slowdown and the Greek debt situation are prominent downside risks. The dollar has appreciated significantly, holding down inflation. I will score based solely on what participants say in this document, not any external priors.

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants overwhelmingly describe inflation as running below the 2 percent target. Core PCE is estimated at approximately 1.25–1.7 percent depending on measure and timeframe. Most participants describe the shortfall as due to transitory factors (oil price decline, dollar appreciation) and express "reasonable confidence" that inflation will return to target, but several (Evans, Kocherlakota, Brainard, Tarullo) express significant skepticism or worry. The word used repeatedly is "below target." No participant describes current inflation as at or above target. Some note signs of "firming" (Mester, Harker, Lacker) but acknowledge current readings are clearly below. Direction: clearly BELOW target → score must be well below 50. Based on anchor range 38-42 for 2013-19 below-target period, and noting the specific concern level here is moderate (most expect return), score around 34-38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as growing at "moderate pace" around 2 percent real GDP, approximately at trend or slightly below. Several say "above trend" for near-term forecast (Williams: "growth above trend"), but characterize current conditions as roughly at or approaching potential. Yellen says "we still have a ways to go beyond what is likely to be accomplished in the next couple of months" in labor/output gap terms. Brainard describes "still-present economic slack." Kocherlakota notes the level of GDP is below earlier expectations. Fischer says the economy is "near full employment." The preponderance of views places the economy slightly below or approaching potential — not clearly above. Several participants note resource gaps "closing by late 2016" (Evans). Direction: slightly BELOW potential, approaching it → score just below 50. Using the bright-line rule: "slack remains" language pushes below 50. Score around 44-47.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment is at 5.3 percent, near most NAIRU estimates (most participants put NAIRU at around 5 percent or slightly below). Most participants say labor market is "near full employment" or "approaching" it. Mester: "at or nearly at the Fed's mandated monetary policy goal of maximum employment." Williams: "very near to achieving our employment objective." Fischer: "unemployment rate is near most estimates of the longer-run rate." Tarullo and Brainard note some remaining slack (participation rate, involuntary part-time). Kocherlakota notes Ninth District unemployment at 4 percent. Multiple participants note continued job gains of ~200-220K/month, rising quits, tightening conditions. Majority view is approaching or at full employment. Direction: NEAR full employment, approaching it from slightly below → score around 48-53. Anchor for 2016-17 "near potential" is 50. Given nearly everyone is clustered around "near NAIRU," score around 51-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending described as "moderate" (repeated across many participants). Harker: "consumer will underpin economy at or a bit above trend." Lockhart: "strengthening sales." Mester: "pickup in spending since Q1." Dudley: "household spending should be well supported." Business investment: mixed — capital goods new orders tepid, oil-related investment still a drag, net exports negative. Fischer: consumer spending expected to grow 3+ percent, residential investment 5+ percent. Brainard notes "consumption remains moderate" but not strong enough to overcome investment drag. Net picture: at or slightly above trend for consumer spending, but business investment lagging. Overall close to trend. Direction: AT trend, slightly above for consumers, below for business investment → balanced around 50-52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions receive limited direct discussion. Participants note financial conditions broadly accommodative (policy still at zero, QE balance sheet in place). Kocherlakota mentions market-based measures of inflation somewhat contained. No significant references to spreads widening, credit availability tightening, or stress in credit markets. Puerto Rico discussed but characterized as limited contagion. Greece risk seen as diminished. China stock market cited as risk. Financial conditions QS report described as "moderate vulnerabilities." Policy itself is ultra-accommodative. Direction: conditions are LOOSE to accommodative → score below 50 (lower = more accommodative). Score around 25-30.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the most important dimension to score carefully given the meeting's focus. The policy vote was for alternative B — holding rates unchanged. However, the overwhelming majority of participants anticipate and support liftoff in September (Lacker, Mester, Williams, Bullard, George, Harker, Lockhart, Fischer, Powell, Dudley — all express support for September liftoff conditioned on data). Two participants lean significantly dovish: Kocherlakota recommends Alternative A (no liftoff), Evans is skeptical liftoff is appropriate by year-end, Brainard prefers December optionality, Tarullo says September is "not my preferred policy option." So approximately 10-11 lean hawkish (September or sooner) vs. 3-4 lean dovish (later/more patience). The center of gravity is clearly: hold now, liftoff very soon (September likely). The anchor says 40-50 is "2014-15 pre-liftoff." By July 2015, the language is significantly more hawkish than early 2015 — most participants are leaning toward September. Score: around 57-62. This is a "lean hawkish" meeting with the majority favoring tightening very soon.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote is unanimous for alternative B, but there is notable disagreement in the go-round. Kocherlakota explicitly recommends alternative A (dovish). Brainard prefers December. Evans is skeptical of year-end liftoff. Tarullo says September is "not my preferred." There are also substantive disagreements about the inflation outlook (how confident to be), the degree of labor market slack, and the appropriate liftoff language. This is a meeting with no formal dissents but several "reluctant" supporters and one explicit alternative A supporter. Anchor says "0-1 dissents but reservations can score 40-50." Kocherlakota's explicit alternative A recommendation plus 3 others with reservations → score around 40-45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability appears in a dedicated short discussion (Rosengren reports on a tabletop exercise, Kocherlakota proposes adding FS language to FOMC statement, Dudley discusses Puerto Rico contagion risk). China stock market mentioned as financial stability risk. However, this is not the dominant topic — the meeting is dominated by labor market, inflation, and liftoff timing. A few participants (Tarullo, Kocherlakota, Bullard mention financial-stability considerations (valuation pressures, leverage). Overall this is "limited-to-moderate" discussion. Score around 28-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The statement adopted (alternative B) says conditions are "approaching" but not yet sufficient for liftoff; the "some" language was added to signal near-term progress. Most participants are explicitly signaling September liftoff as their expectation. Yellen says "liftoff at our next meeting, in September, is a distinct possibility." The overall message is: hold now, tighten very soon. This is a mildly hawkish forward signal — moving from neutral/hold to indicating imminent tightening. Anchor: "25-40 (2015 pre-liftoff)" and "60-75 (2015-18 hiking cycle)." This is still pre-liftoff, but clearly at the hawkish end of pre-liftoff range. Score around 55-62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Several participants emphasize uncertainty, especially about inflation returning to target, about China, about the dollar, about whether Phillips curve will reassert. Yellen emphasizes data dependence. Kocherlakota expresses uncertainty about inflation not returning until "next decade." Brainard emphasizes downside risks and uncertainty about China. Multiple participants acknowledge they don't know if September conditions will be met. However, this is not "uncharted territory" uncertainty — participants have strong views about the labor market and the economy's direction. Uncertainty is elevated relative to normal (due to inflation outlook and China) but not extreme. Score around 42-50.

Many participants explicitly acknowledge inflation is below target while still supporting liftoff.

Overwhelming center of gravity: hawkish (imminent tightening), but with meaningful dovish minority.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 36 ±7, Moderate-High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Brainard: "Inflation remains noticeably and persistently below our 2 percent target...the recent data suggest an underlying trend of price inflation of around 1¼ to 1½ percent."
- Evans: "on the pricing front, there were no reports of any pickup in inflation. To the contrary, anyone with exposure to commodity markets was talking about downward price pressures."
- Kocherlakota: "core PCE inflation has been below our adopted target of 2 percent for nearly seven years."
- Mester: "Inflation continues to run below our 2 percent target, but recent readings show signs of firming."
- Powell: "staff estimates that total PCE inflation was only 0.2 percent for the 12 months ending in June, and that core inflation was only 1¼ percent."

**Center-of-gravity**: Every participant acknowledges inflation is clearly below the 2 percent PCE target (core approximately 1.25–1.7 percent), with temporary factors (oil, dollar) holding it lower; most expect eventual return to target, but the current state is clearly deflationary relative to the mandate.

**Minority views**: Lacker and Mester see recent firming signs (median CPI near 2.25 percent, core CPI at 2.4 percent annualized recently) as somewhat more encouraging; Bullard notes private-sector forecasts project headline PCE near target by year-end 2016.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 46 ±6, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Brainard: "we have not seen a bounceback sufficient to compensate for the slowdown in the first quarter...still-present economic slack."
- Yellen: "I still believe that we still have a ways to go beyond what is likely to be accomplished in the next couple of months" (before reaching full employment); "starting the normalization process before all slack in the economy has been eliminated."
- Evans: "resource gaps essentially closing by late 2016" (not yet closed).
- Fischer: "labor market...near full employment and is projected to continue strengthening" — notes divergence between labor market strength and GDP output.
- Williams: "With growth above trend, I expect continued progress in closing output and unemployment gaps." (Forward-looking; current gaps not yet closed.)

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is approaching but not yet at potential; resource gaps are closing but not closed. Most participants see the output gap as slightly negative and narrowing.

**Minority views**: Bullard argues the unemployment path is more optimistic (implying output closer to or above potential by some measures); Mester says "at or nearly at...maximum employment," implying the economy may be near or at potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 52 ±6, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Mester: "the evidence suggests that the economy is at or nearly at the Fed's mandated monetary policy goal of maximum employment."
- Williams: "we are finally closing in on full employment...we're very near to achieving our employment objective."
- Fischer: "the unemployment rate is near most estimates of the longer-run rate of unemployment projected by participants in the SEP."
- Brainard: "Looking at a wide range of labor market indicators suggests to me that some slack still remains, even with overall unemployment at 5.3 percent."
- Tarullo: "I still believe there is a nontrivial amount of additional slack that could be taken up."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unemployment at 5.3 percent is near but arguably slightly above NAIRU (~5.0–5.1 percent per most estimates); most participants see the labor market as near full employment, with some noting residual slack from participation and involuntary part-time; tightening continues at strong pace (200K+ jobs/month).

**Minority views**: Kocherlakota and Tarullo argue the natural rate may be lower than 5 percent, implying more slack remains; Lacker and George believe labor conditions fully or more than justify liftoff now.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 50 ±7, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Fischer: "Personal consumption expenditures are expected to continue to grow at an annual rate of a little more than 3 percent...residential investment...more than 5 percent."
- Brainard: "Consumption remains moderate, buoyed by strong fundamentals, but apparently not strong enough to overcome the continued drag on investment and production from earlier oil price declines."
- Dudley: "Household spending should be well supported over the near term by solid job gains."
- Evans: "businesses that manufacture heavy equipment continue to experience sluggish domestic demand."
- Harker: "consumer will underpin an economy that should perform at or a bit above trend."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is solid and growing at moderate pace (~3 percent); residential investment picking up; business/capital goods investment subdued, especially energy-related; net exports a drag. Net picture is at-trend or very slightly above for consumption, below-trend for business investment — overall balanced near 50.

**Minority views**: Lockhart and Mester are more optimistic (Lockhart: "economy remains on a track characterized as moderate economic growth"); Brainard more cautious (investment drag, tepid).

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 28 ±8, Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Policy remains at zero lower bound with $4.5 trillion balance sheet — context is ultra-accommodative.
- Kocherlakota: "financial system vulnerabilities were moderate" (QS report assessment).
- Bullard: "policy remains at very aggressive settings, with the policy rate at zero and the balance sheet at $4.5 trillion."
- George: "the Tealbook r* measure remains more than 1 percentage point above the actual real federal funds rate...this is among the most accommodative settings of policy over this period."
- No significant discussion of credit spreads widening, lending standards tightening, or financial market stress.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as extremely accommodative/loose — zero rates, large balance sheet, credit available. Easing of lending standards for residential mortgages noted (Dudley). No credit stress signals.

**Minority views**: Kocherlakota and Tarullo note some valuation pressures in certain asset classes; Tarullo references leverage concerns outside prudentially regulated institutions. These are noted but not alarm-level.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 59 ±8, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Majority (Lacker, Mester, Williams, Bullard, George, Harker, Lockhart, Fischer, Powell, Dudley): Support alternative B with September liftoff anticipated. Fischer: "The criteria for beginning normalization have very nearly been met, and we need to be willing to move." Lacker: "the case for raising rates has been getting even stronger." Williams: "I anticipate that it will be appropriate to raise the target funds rate at our September meeting."
- Minority (Kocherlakota, Evans, Brainard, Tarullo): Kocherlakota recommends alternative A explicitly. Brainard: "I would favor liftoff in September only if both inflation and labor market conditions make it compelling, and I'd like to maintain optionality on December." Evans: "I'm still skeptical that the data will support reasonable confidence in this inflation outlook by the end of the year."
- Yellen: "liftoff at our next meeting, in September, is a distinct possibility."

**Center-of-gravity**: The majority favor imminent tightening (September liftoff) with current hold and pre-commitment signals; this is a "lean hawkish" meeting where the policy rate is 0 but tightening is clearly anticipated and favored by most. Score of 59 reflects "hold steady with tightening bias; approaching time to begin."

**Minority views**: Kocherlakota (strongly dovish, prefers alternative A and defer liftoff), Evans and Brainard (dovish lean, uncertain about inflation confidence), Tarullo (moderate dove on inflation criteria).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 42 ±8, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Vote: unanimous for alternative B — zero formal dissents.
- Kocherlakota explicitly recommends alternative A in the go-round: "I recommend alternative A."
- Evans: "I'm still skeptical that the data will support reasonable confidence in this inflation outlook by the end of the year, if then."
- Brainard: prefers December optionality, not convinced September conditions will be met on inflation.
- Tarullo: "as things currently stand and as I expect them to develop in the next seven weeks, that would not be my preferred policy option."

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissents but one explicit alternative A recommendation plus three participants with meaningful reservations about September. Disagreement about inflation outlook and labor slack is substantive. Consistent with "moderate differences, 1 dissent or 2-3 reservations" range (31-45).

**Minority views**: Some disagreement is over language (the "some" in paragraph 3, "balanced approach" in alt C) rather than fundamental policy direction.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 58 ±8, High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Yellen in go-round: "Economic conditions appear to be evolving in a manner that will likely make it appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate sometime this year."
- Statement alternative B adds "some" progress language to signal September readiness.
- Williams: "barring any major surprises, I anticipate that it will be appropriate to raise the target funds rate at our September meeting."
- Bullard: "The Committee is in a good position for a September liftoff, depending on the incoming data."

**Center-of-gravity**: The meeting holds rates unchanged but sends a clear signal that tightening is imminent (September "a distinct possibility"), with the path expected to be gradual. This is a mild-to-moderate hawkish forward signal — clearly moving from the "low for long" phase toward the "normalization beginning" phase. Fits "mild tightening bias or 'begin normalization' language" (60-75) but held back by data-dependence emphasis and some dovish input.

**Score: 48 ±8, High confidence**

**Evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is notably elevated relative to a calm expansion (China, inflation outlook, dollar, natural rate debates), but participants are not in "uncharted territory" territory.
