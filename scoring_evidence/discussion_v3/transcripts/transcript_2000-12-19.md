# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-12-19 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the December 19, 2000, FOMC meeting. It contains individual participant statements from the economic go-around and the policy discussion. Speakers are identified by name. The transcript is substantial in length with contributions from approximately 15+ participants.

### Era Context
Late 2000: The Fed had raised rates to 6.5% by May 2000 to cool an overheating economy that had been growing at 5-6%. By December, the economy was clearly decelerating -- the dot-com bust was underway, equity markets (especially NASDAQ) were declining sharply, manufacturing was weakening, and energy prices were elevated. The question was whether the slowdown would stabilize at a sustainable pace or tip into recession.

### Core Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly contained but not fully benign. The center of gravity is that core inflation remains modest and is not accelerating significantly, despite some lingering concerns.
- Jordan: "Prices of construction materials are reported to be below levels prevailing at the beginning of the year"; labor markets "eased considerably."
- Hoenig: "core inflation remains modest... I expect it to remain modest for the foreseeable future."
- Guynn: "price pressures remain in check for the most part"
- Broaddus: "prices have drifted upward in recent weeks" -- minority concern
- Santomero: "core PCE and core CPI ticked downward in October, but core CPI ticked back up in November"; "inflation remains a concern"
- Gramlich: extensive argument that inflation risk is "relatively quiescent"; core PCE "still below its levels of 1996 and 1997"
- Greenspan: "the outlook is for a very significantly subdued rate of inflation"; "pricing power in the corporate sector has been falling quite appreciably"
- Meyer: "the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity" -- inflation concern lingers
- Kelley: "By most measures inflation is off its lows and is slowly rising"

Direction: Inflation pressure is slightly above the midpoint -- participants acknowledge some upward drift in core measures and tight labor markets, but the dominant view is that inflation is modest and likely to level off or decline given the slowdown. Score should be in the 48-55 range. The center of gravity leans toward "inflation modestly contained but not fully gone as a concern." Score: 52.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is modest but slightly above recent lows, with some upward drift noted. 52 matches -- slightly above neutral.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as having been above potential but now rapidly decelerating toward or below trend.
- Broaddus: "6 percent growth rate was not sustainable... we are getting the slowing we needed"; Greenbook projects below-trend growth with unemployment rising to 4.7-5%.
- Greenspan: "rate of economic expansion has very clearly and unambiguously moved down dramatically"; growth "if it stabilized at its current level it would be sustainable"
- Meyer: "the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity before this loss of momentum"; period of below-trend growth is "constructive"
- Moskow: "The national economy has clearly entered a period of below-trend growth"
- Parry: "our forecast of real GDP... around 2-1/2 percent"; "this may be the slowdown we need to contain inflationary pressures"
- Multiple participants describe below-trend growth rates of ~2-2.5%.

Direction: The economy was above potential earlier in the year but is now at or slightly below trend. Most participants describe "below-trend growth" but the level of output is still close to potential (unemployment still at 4%). The bright-line rule: "below trend" growth language but the output LEVEL is still near potential. Score should reflect transition from above to near potential: ~48-50. Score: 49.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy transitioning from above to slightly below potential. 49 fits -- just below the midpoint.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor markets are still tight but beginning to ease.
- Jordan: "Labor markets are reported to have eased considerably" in his district
- Guynn: "Somewhat slower growth appears to be moderating pressures on labor markets just a bit, but scarcities in nursing and other technical skills remain"
- Hoenig: labor markets "softening"; "initial claims have risen"
- Broaddus: "labor markets are loosening a bit; they are a little less tight"
- Moskow: "Labor markets are still tight, but an increasing number of layoffs and plant shutdowns"
- Santomero: "labor markets in the District as still tight"; unemployment "4 percent or less all year"
- Stern: "Labor markets remain tight"
- Poole: "considerably easier conditions" from contacts
- Minehan: "continue to see resource constraints in labor markets"
- Greenspan acknowledges tight markets but easing trend

Direction: Still tight by historical standards (unemployment ~4%), but clearly easing at the margin. Center of gravity: tight but loosening. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000 tightness. By December 2000, clearly moving down from that peak. Score: 60.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Markets still tight but easing. 60 = tight but moderating. Fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Spending and investment are weakening notably.
- Jordan: Retailers report "sharply lower year-over-year sales"; truck production "off sharply"; steel "dire"
- Guynn: "slowed"; housing "flat to down slightly"; autos weakening
- Hoenig: consumer spending slowing; "automobiles and light trucks" weakest; "businesses appear to be taking a very clear wait-and-see attitude toward investment decisions"
- Moskow: "holiday sales have been soft"; auto sales "declined considerably"; manufacturing slowing; contacts use "sharp," "dramatic," "tremendous"
- Parry: "slower pace of consumer spending"; high-tech outlook "softened recently"
- Stern: "consumer spending has to be described as no better than mediocre"
- Broaddus: "Consumer spending in particular has throttled down"
- Gramlich: "the stock market has been level to declining for 18 months"; "Auto sales are weak"; "Manufacturing output beyond auto sales is turning down"; "Even high-tech investment is beginning to weaken"
- Ferguson: earnings revisions "fast and furious"
- Kelley: "seriously weakening news about autos, consumer confidence, corporate earnings, retail sales, high-tech expenditures"

Direction: Consumption and investment are clearly below trend and weakening. Holiday sales soft, autos declining, manufacturing contracting, investment plans being cut. Score: 33-38. Score: 35.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says weak and weakening. 35 = below trend, sluggish. Fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions have tightened significantly.
- Hoenig: "tighter financial conditions pose an additional and important downside risk"; "higher interest rate spreads, lower equity prices, tighter lending terms and standards, increased risk aversion"
- Guynn: "bankers are telling us that loan growth has slowed considerably"; borrowers "complaining about the shrinking syndicated loan markets and having to pay higher prices"
- Jordan: "venture capital funds have dried up"; banks reporting higher charge-offs
- Parry: "tightening financial conditions on a broad range of fronts"
- Moskow: "Tighter credit standards for businesses"
- McDonough: "flight to quality in the debt markets and the decline in equity values"
- Ferguson: "ongoing tightening of financial conditions"; Moody's expects 385-400 defaults, triple previous year; "tenderness in balance sheets"
- Poole: "much wider credit spreads that are reducing credit availability to more marginal borrowers"
- Minehan: "widening credit spreads" but "bankable credits are getting banked"; banks "generally healthy"

Direction: Financial conditions are clearly tighter than neutral. Spreads widened, credit standards tightened, equity prices down, venture capital dried up. Score: 63-68. Score: 65.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence clearly says conditions have tightened. 65 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The committee is shifting toward easing but divided on timing.
- Favoring immediate ease (25bp cut today): Gramlich (strong case), Poole (explicit), Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer -- ~5 participants
- Favoring hold + downside asymmetry + possible intermeeting move: Greenspan, McDonough, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, Moskow, Santomero, Minehan, Stern, Parry -- ~10 participants
- Broaddus preferred balanced risk statement (most cautious) -- 1 participant
- Guynn initially thought balanced risks but accepted downside asymmetry

The vote was unanimous for no change + downside risk language. The clear majority favored hold now but expected to ease soon (January). This is a lean dovish posture: hold with strong easing bias.

Direction: The committee held rates but adopted an asymmetric statement toward weakness, with clear expectation of easing very soon. This is lean dovish. Score: 40-44. Score: 42.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Committee leaning dovish but not yet cutting. 42 = lean dovish to balanced with easing bias. Fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is notable disagreement about timing and approach.
- Clear camps: ~5 wanted to cut now, ~10 preferred to wait, 1 wanted balanced risk statement
- No formal dissent (unanimous vote on hold + asymmetry)
- But Gramlich, Poole, Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer all explicitly stated preference for immediate cut
- Broaddus preferred balanced risk statement (hawkish outlier)
- Exchange between Jordan and Greenspan about asymmetric language shows tension

Direction: Notable disagreement about timing but general agreement on direction (easing soon). Multiple "reluctant" supporters. Score: 42-48. Score: 45.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Clear camps but no formal dissents; substantial range of views. 45 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions/stability received significant attention.
- Hoenig devoted substantial discussion to financial risks: spreads, equity prices, lending terms, risk aversion, junk bond defaults, C&I delinquencies, debt-to-net-worth ratios
- Ferguson: extensive discussion of defaults (Moody's data), earnings revisions, balance sheet tenderness
- Guynn: bankers' loan growth, credit quality discussion
- Poole: credit spreads
- Moskow: tighter credit standards

Direction: Financial conditions were a prominent topic discussed by many participants at length. Not the dominant topic (economic slowdown was), but clearly a major secondary theme. Score: 50-58. Score: 55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly toward easing.
- Greenspan: explicitly stated committee might need to move before next meeting; "quite conceivable that we may have to have a telephone conference and move the rate before the next meeting"
- McDonough: "we have to be very flexible during the month of January"
- Gramlich: "I share the view... that we've seen enough to ease fairly soon"
- Meyer: "an intermeeting move could certainly be a possibility"; markets price "cumulative easing of 100 basis points over the course of next year"

Direction: Forward signal is clearly toward easing. Score: 30-35. Score: 32.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence clearly says easing ahead. 32 = easing lean with data dependence. Fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is elevated and explicitly discussed.

- Meyer: "at turning points, especially difficult to forecast"

Direction: Uncertainty is clearly elevated. Multiple participants explicitly note unusual difficulty in forecasting at this juncture. Score: 55-62. Score: 58.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): These diverge somewhat. Explanation: The economy is transitioning. Labor tightness is a lagging indicator -- still reflecting the boom -- while output gap reflects the current deceleration. This divergence is appropriate for a turning point.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Forward guidance is more dovish than current action, which makes sense: the committee held rates but strongly signaled imminent easing. This pattern (dovish current lean with even more dovish forward signal) fits the text.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Hoenig: "Despite higher energy prices and rising import prices, core inflation remains modest."
- Gramlich: "The core PCE bounces around and we make a lot of the fact that it has risen recently, but it's still below its levels of 1996 and 1997."
- Greenspan: "the outlook is for a very significantly subdued rate of inflation... pricing power in the corporate sector has been falling quite appreciably in the last six to eight weeks"
- Kelley: "By most measures inflation is off its lows and is slowly rising."
- Santomero: "inflation remains a concern to me"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants view core inflation as modest and likely to remain so given the slowdown, though a minority notes it has drifted up from recent lows, keeping it slightly above a fully neutral read.

**Minority views:** Broaddus notes prices "drifted upward"; Santomero sees inflation as a continuing concern; Meyer worries economy was beyond sustainable capacity. These inform the +8 uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "rate of economic expansion has very clearly and unambiguously moved down dramatically from its pace of earlier this year... if it stabilized at its current level it would be sustainable"
- Moskow: "The national economy has clearly entered a period of below-trend growth"
- Meyer: "the economy was operating beyond sustainable capacity before this loss of momentum"
- Broaddus: "6 percent growth rate was not sustainable... we are getting the slowing we needed"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy was above potential earlier in 2000 but has decelerated to roughly potential, with growth now below trend. The output level is near potential with unemployment still at 4%, though growth momentum points below.

**Minority views:** Meyer and Broaddus emphasize the economy was recently above potential (suggesting >50); Gramlich argues 4% unemployment may be sustainable (suggesting potential is not being exceeded). Band reflects this range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Moskow: "Labor markets are still tight, but an increasing number of layoffs and plant shutdowns have been reported."
- Broaddus: "labor markets are loosening a bit; they are a little less tight. Trucking companies in particular have reported fewer difficulties finding and retaining drivers."
- Jordan: "Labor markets are reported to have eased considerably with the major exception of the health-care sector"
- Santomero: "labor markets in the District as still tight... unemployment rate in our region has remained at 4 percent or less all year"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets remain tight by historical standards (unemployment ~4%) but are clearly easing at the margin, with reports of layoffs, easier hiring, and moderating pressures across multiple districts.

**Minority views:** Jordan and Poole report more substantial easing; Minehan and Stern see continued tightness. Band reflects this spread.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Jordan: "Retailers... report sharply lower year-over-year sales"; steel industry "described as dire"; trucking industry "in a depression not a recession"
- Moskow: "contacts have used words like 'sharp,' 'dramatic,' and 'tremendous' to describe the slowdown"
- Stern: "consumer spending has to be described as no better than mediocre"; "profit margins are being squeezed"
- Kelley: "seriously weakening news about autos, consumer confidence, corporate earnings, retail sales, high-tech expenditures"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and business investment are clearly below trend and weakening rapidly, with particularly sharp declines in autos, manufacturing, and technology sectors, though some areas (construction, energy) retain strength.

**Minority views:** Guynn and Santomero see a more moderate picture; Jordan describes near-depression conditions in some sectors. Band captures this range.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Hoenig: "tighter financial conditions pose an additional and important downside risk. These include higher interest rate spreads, lower equity prices, tighter lending terms and standards, increased risk aversion toward more speculative ventures, and a rise in junk bond default rates."
- Ferguson: "Moody's... expectation is that between 385 and 400 companies will default... more than triple the number of defaults we've had since December of 1999"
- Guynn: "bankers are telling us that loan growth has slowed considerably across all business lines... borrowers are reportedly complaining about the shrinking syndicated loan markets"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions have tightened significantly through wider spreads, lower equity prices, tighter credit standards, reduced credit availability for marginal borrowers, and rising defaults, though banks remain healthy and bankable credits continue to be funded.

**Minority views:** Minehan argues conditions are healthy overall and "bankable credits are getting banked." This slightly tempers the otherwise strong consensus on tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 ±10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "no change in the funds rate, but with a bias toward the down side and the recognition that, if the erosion continues, we very likely will have to move before the next meeting"
- Gramlich: "I think monetary policy has become too tight... I see very few costs in cutting rates soon and very many benefits"
- Poole: "this is an appropriate time to ease... we need to reposition ourselves to get back in the middle of the playing field"
- Broaddus: "I think we still have time to proceed in a measured manner... there is a risk in easing policy more than expected at a time when labor markets... are still quite tight and inflation is drifting upward"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee voted unanimously to hold rates but shifted the balance of risks toward economic weakness, with roughly a third of participants preferring an immediate 25bp cut and the rest favoring waiting for more data while signaling imminent easing.

**Minority views:** ~5 participants (Gramlich, Poole, Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer) wanted immediate easing; Broaddus preferred a balanced risk statement. Wide band reflects this range.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Poole: "as you know from my earlier comments, I would have preferred to reduce the federal funds rate at this meeting by 25 basis points"
- Gramlich: "the policy you suggested is not my first choice... I share the view of a few others around the table who think that we've seen enough to ease fairly soon"
- Broaddus: "My preference would have been to have a balanced risk statement"
- Jordan extensive back-and-forth with Greenspan about the logic of asymmetric language without action

**Center-of-gravity summary:** While the vote was unanimous, there were distinct camps -- roughly 5 wanting immediate cuts, 10 preferring to wait, and 1 wanting a more hawkish stance -- with substantive exchanges about the logic and risks of each approach.

**Minority views:** No formal dissents but multiple explicit statements of preference for alternatives.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Hoenig: extensive discussion of spreads, equity prices, lending terms, junk bond defaults, C&I delinquencies, debt-to-net-worth ratios
- Ferguson: Moody's default data, earnings revisions, balance sheet "tenderness"
- Greenspan: stock market declines, NASDAQ impact on high-tech sector

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions and stability were a prominent secondary topic, with several participants addressing credit spreads, equity declines, lending standards, and default risks at meaningful length, though the primary focus remained the real economic slowdown.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 32 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "it is quite conceivable that we may have to have a telephone conference and move the rate before the next meeting... triggered... by a telephone conference sometime in the early days of January"
- Meyer: "markets already anticipate a move early next year and a cumulative easing of 100 basis points over the course of next year. I don't think we should do or say anything at this meeting that would disconfirm or unwind those expectations"
- McDonough: "we have to be very flexible during the month of January"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly toward easing, with the Chairman explicitly raising the prospect of an intermeeting move and multiple participants endorsing flexibility to cut rates imminently, while maintaining data dependence.

**Minority views:** Broaddus hopes "it's not a foregone conclusion that we're necessarily going to ease further." Minehan: "Hopefully we won't have to stand by our telephones."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "we do not have the capability of reliably forecasting a recession... we really do not know the answer for the intermediate period"; extensive discussion of nonlinear dynamics and model limitations
- Meyer: "once the economy has slowed to below trend, the downside risks... quickly become a source of serious concern... at turning points, especially difficult to forecast"
- McDonough: "the most important thing in public life is to know what you don't know. And we don't in fact know enough at this point"
- Moskow: "In a period of uncertainty like this"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple participants explicitly emphasize elevated uncertainty about whether the slowdown will stabilize or deepen, with Greenspan providing an extended discussion of the inherent limits of forecasting at turning points.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Guynn: "nonperforming assets have grown... still at modest and manageable levels." Kelley: "banks remain very healthy." Minehan: "generally healthy banking conditions." Score: 42.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Not prominently discussed. Score: N/A.
