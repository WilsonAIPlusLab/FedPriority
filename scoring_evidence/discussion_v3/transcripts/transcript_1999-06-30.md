# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 1999-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually identified speakers from the go-around (economic discussion on June 29) and the policy discussion (morning of June 30). Speakers include: Parry, Hoenig, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Guynn, Stern, McTeer, McDonough, Poole, Jordan, Kelley, Ferguson, Meyer, Gramlich, Greenspan, and several others. This is a late-1990s boom-era meeting.

### Era Context
June 1999: Late in the longest U.S. peacetime expansion. Unemployment near 4.2-4.3%. Productivity acceleration driven by IT revolution. Federal funds rate at 4.75% following three 25bp easing moves in fall 1998 during LTCM/global financial crisis. World economy recovering from Asian/Russian crises. Oil prices rebounding. The Committee ultimately voted for a 25bp hike at this meeting, going to 5.00%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently low/benign but with upside risks building. Multiple speakers note core CPI is modest/stable -- "the inflation numbers are still coming in at a modest level" (Hoenig), "Amazingly, wage and price increases continue to be moderate" (Minehan), "continuing to grow rapidly and without increased inflation" (McDonough). However, many see upside risks: Moskow notes "price increases that stick," Jordan notes rising consumer price survey measures, Meyer notes "core inflation has now stabilized" and "best inflation news is behind us," Gramlich sees "early if intermittent signs of acceleration in prices." Greenspan notes unit costs are effectively flat. The center of gravity is: inflation is currently at or modestly below target, but risks are tilted upward. This places it around 45-50 -- not yet elevated but no longer improving.

Direction: Participants see inflation pressure as roughly at the midpoint -- current inflation is low/benign but the tide may be turning. Score should be near 48-52.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly running above potential in participants' view. Multiple speakers describe "excessive growth in domestic demand" (Broaddus), "above trend" growth (Ferguson: "vigorous growth, which may well be above trend"), "running hot." Greenspan describes aggregate demand running in excess of potential growth by about 0.5%. Meyer notes "forecast growth in aggregate demand is coming in above that of aggregate supply." Gramlich: "there is some imbalance." However, strong productivity is cited as potentially raising potential output. Greenspan is ambivalent -- noting we don't see overheating in prices or product markets, only in labor markets. The bright-line rule says "above potential" or "above trend" language requires > 50.

Direction: Participants see the economy as modestly to clearly above potential. Score ~57-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is the single most emphasized topic. Nearly every speaker describes extremely tight labor markets. Hoenig: "labor market in our District remains extremely tight" with 3.5% unemployment. Moskow: "District labor markets are still extremely tight" with temp firms unable to find workers. Minehan: teenagers making $9-14/hour, "former welfare recipients are getting lucrative offers." Broaddus: "continued exceptionally tight labor markets," McDonald's stuffing applications in bags. Stern: Twin Cities unemployment at 1.6%. Jordan: theme parks can't open concession stands, fast food drive-through only. Greenspan: "Where we do have a problem is in the labor markets." National unemployment at 4.2-4.3% for several quarters. This is very tight by any standard.

Direction: Participants see labor markets as very tight. Score ~70-75, consistent with the 1999-2000 anchor of 65-75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is described as robust/strong by virtually all speakers. Broaddus: "robust consumer spending," "consumer spending may actually have accelerated." Moskow: auto sales at 12-year highs, 16.4 million units. Jordan: "banner year for retailer earnings," "phenomenal." Greenspan: "motor vehicle sales are running well in excess of past trends. Housing sales are absurdly high. Capital investment is strong." Kelley: "Consumption continues to increase at a rapid pace, led by the big-ticket cyclicals." This is clearly well above trend.

Direction: Participants see consumption/investment as well above trend. Score ~65-70.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described as loose/accommodative. Hoenig: "the stance of monetary policy is accommodative," "risk spreads are in fact lower." Moskow: "risk spreads, while still somewhat elevated, are back to more reasonable levels, and creditworthy borrowers are finding financing." Multiple speakers note the stock market is up strongly (Dow up 14% YTD). Broaddus: "excessively accommodative monetary policy." Greenspan worries about "financial imbalances now evidenced in high stock market PE ratios and in the sizable growth in corporate and personal debt." Credit is available. Some anecdotal reports of bank examiners seeing sloppy loan administration (Poole). Overall, conditions are accommodative/loose.

Direction: Financial conditions are loose. Higher = tighter. Score ~25-30.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: There is a clear consensus to raise rates 25bp. Nobody argues for no action (McTeer comes closest but acknowledges the "box" they're in). Several participants (Meyer, Gramlich initially, Broaddus, Minehan) considered or favored 50bp. The debate is primarily about 25bp symmetric vs. 25bp asymmetric. Greenspan proposes 25bp + symmetry. The center of gravity is clearly hawkish -- everyone agrees to tighten, several want more, and most see further tightening ahead.

Direction: Moderate hawkish -- consensus for tightening, debate is about pace. Score ~63-67.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: While there is broad agreement on the 25bp hike, there is notable disagreement on (a) the degree of tightening needed (25 vs 50bp), and (b) symmetric vs asymmetric directive. The symmetry/asymmetry question splits the Committee roughly evenly. McTeer is clearly more dovish. Broaddus, Meyer, Parry, Guynn, Minehan, Moskow, Ferguson favor asymmetry; Greenspan, McDonough, Kelley, Hoenig, Jordan, Stern, Gramlich, Poole lean symmetric. No formal dissent appears to occur. This is moderate disagreement -- broad agreement on direction but differences on magnitude and communication.

Direction: Moderate differences. Score ~35-40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability issues are discussed by several participants but are not the dominant focus. Minehan mentions "financial imbalances now evidenced in high stock market PE ratios and sizable growth in corporate and personal debt." Kelley: "stock market that is in a very precarious position. It's very fragile." Poole: bank examiners seeing early concerns about loan administration. Jordan: bankers concerned about personal bankruptcies and consumer credit quality. Broaddus: "some hints of imbalances in the market for consumer credit." Greenspan discusses stock market valuations. This is a secondary topic -- mentioned by several participants but not the dominant theme.

Direction: Moderate discussion. Score ~35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. While the Committee adopts symmetry, the accompanying language and the tenor of the discussion make clear that further tightening is likely. Meyer: "50 basis point tightening this year... would in my view be prudent and appropriate." Minehan: "both the Greenbook and our own forecast suggest that at least 50 to 75 basis points of tightening are needed this year." Greenspan: "Unless the economy comes apart unexpectedly, I think the chances of our having to move the funds rate lower are close to zero. The next move we are likely to make is almost surely up." The symmetric directive is meant to calm markets, but the underlying message is that more tightening is coming.

Direction: Mild-to-moderate tightening bias. Score ~63-68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is a prominent theme. Parry: "no way to determine the size or persistence of the current supply shock," "hard to tell how fast the economy can grow without inflationary pressures." Minehan: "forecast is surrounded by a cloud of uncertainty." McDonough: "How long will this improved productivity last?... We do not know." Greenspan: extensive discussion of whether productivity acceleration is captured by models, "something seems to be happening that none of us has ever witnessed before." Meyer: "unusual degree of uncertainty on all counts." Gramlich: "great deal of forecasting uncertainty." The uncertainty is specifically about the productivity revolution and whether traditional Phillips curve/NAIRU frameworks still apply. This is elevated but not extreme.

Direction: Elevated uncertainty, driven by structural change. Score ~50-58.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says current inflation low but risks building. Score ~50 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly above. Score ~58 matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says extremely tight. Score ~72 matches.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence says well above trend. Score ~67 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score ~27 matches (low).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says consensus to tighten. Score ~65 matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish signal. Evidence says further tightening likely. Score ~65 matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): Labor tightness is higher than output gap because participants repeatedly note that the overheating shows up in labor markets but NOT in product markets or prices. This divergence is explicitly discussed by Greenspan.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Aligned -- moderate hawkish action with moderate hawkish forward signal.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- Clearly hawkish (favor 25bp+ and further tightening): Parry, Hoenig, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Guynn, Kelley, Ferguson, Meyer, Gramlich, Greenspan (~11)
- Moderately hawkish (accept 25bp reluctantly): McDonough, Poole, Stern (~3)
- Dovish leaning (prefer no action or minimal): McTeer (~1)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "the inflation numbers are still coming in at a modest level... I still judge that the risks on the inflation outlook are on the upside"
- MR. MEYER: "core inflation has now stabilized and... the best inflation news is behind us"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the change in unit costs is effectively zero over the past four quarters"
- MR. MOSKOW: "we increasingly hear reports of price increases that stick, both for final and intermediate goods"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see current inflation as roughly at target (core CPI around 2%), but broadly agree the risks are tilted to the upside due to tight labor markets and waning favorable supply shocks. The balance between benign current readings and perceived upside risk places this right at the midpoint.

**Minority views:** McTeer argues we are "still in a deflationary world environment" with many deflationary forces still in play (lower end). Moskow and Jordan cite evidence of price increases beginning to stick (upper end).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FERGUSON: "incoming data continue to point to vigorous growth, which may well be above trend"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the forecast growth in aggregate demand is coming in above that of aggregate supply... This is not a huge supply/demand imbalance, but I think there is some imbalance"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "aggregate demand is currently running in excess of potential growth by about [0.5 percentage points]... We have a situation that is out of equilibrium"
- MR. BROADDUS: "we seem to have a fairly significant overall macroeconomic imbalance currently. More specifically, I think we have a classic case of excessive growth in domestic demand"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly agree the economy is running above potential, with demand growth outpacing supply growth. However, the degree of overheating is moderated by strong productivity growth that is raising potential output, making the gap smaller than traditional models suggest.

**Minority views:** McTeer argues capacity utilization under 80% suggests slack remains in goods markets, and that global slack (Asia, Europe, Latin America) acts as a buffer. McDonough questions whether traditional measures capture the new economy's potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "the labor market in our District remains extremely tight. The District's unemployment rate edged down to about 3-1/2 percent"
- MR. STERN: "the unemployment rate in the Twin Cities metropolitan area is now 1.6 percent"
- MS. MINEHAN: "temporary help firms reaching out to teenagers... offering them $12 to $14 per hour"; "former welfare recipients are getting lucrative offers"
- MR. JORDAN: "fast food restaurants in both central and northwest Ohio are often now open for drive-through business only... because of a lack of staff"
- MR. BROADDUS: "Some McDonald's stores are now stuffing employment applications in the bag with the burger and fries"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually every participant reports extremely tight labor markets in their district. National unemployment at 4.2-4.3% with vivid anecdotes of severe worker shortages across skill levels -- from IT professionals to teenage summer workers to fast food staff. This is among the tightest labor markets in FOMC history.

**Minority views:** McTeer notes capacity utilization under 80% suggests not all factors are tight. McDonough notes that pools of previously discouraged workers are being drawn in, suggesting labor supply may be more elastic than assumed.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "Light motor vehicle sales in May matched their 12-year high... June sales will exceed the strong year-to-date sales pace of 16.4 million units"
- MR. BROADDUS: "robust consumer spending and housing activity... consumer spending may actually have accelerated in our region in May and June"
- MR. JORDAN: "this year is going to be phenomenal and will go down as a banner year for retailer earnings"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "motor vehicle sales are running well in excess of past trends. Housing sales are absurdly high. Capital investment is strong"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is running well above trend with auto sales, housing, and retail all at or near record levels. Business investment remains strong, particularly in technology. Some expect moderation ahead but see no hard evidence of it yet.

**Minority views:** McTeer's Dallas Fed forecasts 2.9% Q2 GDP growth, suggesting some moderation. Minehan's forecast projects slowing from buyer satiation and waning wealth effects.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "the financial turmoil that occurred last fall has largely subsided, risk spreads are in fact lower... the stance of monetary policy is accommodative"
- MR. MOSKOW: "risk spreads, while still somewhat elevated, are back to more reasonable levels, and creditworthy borrowers are finding financing"
- MR. BROADDUS: "we may well be fueling [excessive demand] with an excessively accommodative monetary policy"
- MR. KELLEY: "the Dow Jones industrial average is up over 14 percent so far this year, and that's not too shabby"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are clearly accommodative. Risk spreads have normalized from the fall 1998 crisis, equity markets are strong, credit is widely available, and the funds rate is 75bp below where it was a year ago despite a stronger economy. Multiple participants explicitly describe policy as accommodative.

**Minority views:** Some note risk spreads are "still somewhat elevated" (Moskow), and Poole reports bank examiners seeing early signs of credit quality concerns, suggesting conditions may not be as uniformly easy as they appear.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think we have to move at this meeting... I would suggest... that we raise the funds rate 25 basis points today"
- MR. MEYER: "the 50 basis point tightening this year that is incorporated into the Greenbook forecast would in my view be prudent and appropriate"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I concluded that a 50 basis point increase was appropriate [but] you've all talked me out of a 50 basis point change"
- MR. MCTEER: "if I had the deciding vote, I probably would vote for a small tightening just because of the box we're in. But since I won't have the deciding vote... I remain to be convinced"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous support for a 25bp rate hike with several participants having considered or preferred 50bp. The debate centers on pace and communication rather than direction. This is a moderate hawkish consensus -- not aggressive enough for 70+ given the measured 25bp step and genuine uncertainty about the productivity story.

**Minority views:** McTeer provides the dovish pole, arguing the economy should be allowed to test its limits. On the hawkish end, Broaddus, Meyer, and initially Gramlich favored 50bp.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- On action: Near-consensus for 25bp. Only McTeer would prefer no change but accepts the move.
- On directive: Sharp split. MR. BROADDUS: "I respectfully but strongly would oppose the move back to symmetric directive language." MR. GUYNN: "I still think the probability of our needing to tighten further is fairly high. To me it is more intellectually honest and more credible to go ahead and tell people that."
- Greenspan reads the Committee as "strongly in support of a 25 basis point increase and modestly in support of symmetry."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on direction and the 25bp action, but notable disagreement on the symmetry/asymmetry question and the total amount of tightening needed. The split on communication strategy is substantive but does not result in formal dissent.

**Minority views:** The Committee is roughly evenly divided on symmetric vs asymmetric directive, creating moderate internal tension.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "financial imbalances now evidenced in high stock market PE ratios and in the sizable growth in corporate and personal debt"
- MR. KELLEY: "a stock market that is in a very precarious position. It's very fragile and I think very shock prone"
- MR. JORDAN: "bankers increasingly express their worries about personal bankruptcies"; "customers don't have any money in their accounts"
- MR. POOLE: "our bank examiners are expressing a little concern... about loan administration in the banks"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives moderate attention. Several participants flag equity market overvaluation, rising consumer debt/bankruptcies, and early signs of lending standards slipping. However, these are secondary to the main discussion about inflation risks and the productivity puzzle.

**Minority views:** Most participants mention financial conditions only in passing; Kelley and Minehan give it more sustained attention.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the chances of our having to move the funds rate lower are close to zero. The next move we are likely to make is almost surely up"
- MR. MEYER: "Given the risk of higher inflation, the 50 basis point tightening this year... would in my view be prudent and appropriate. That would mean doing the 25 basis points today and another 25 basis points later in the year"
- MS. MINEHAN: "both the Greenbook and our own forecast suggest that at least 50 to 75 basis points of tightening are needed this year just to keep a lid on inflation in 2000"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is moderately hawkish. While the Committee adopts a symmetric directive, the substance of the discussion overwhelmingly signals further tightening ahead. Most participants expect at least one more 25bp move; some expect much more. The symmetric directive is a communication choice, not a reflection of genuine policy neutrality.

**Minority views:** McTeer hopes the 25bp move can be accompanied by symmetry to "take some of the nervousness out." McDonough argues for "tactical symmetry" against a backdrop of "strategic concern about inflation."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "no way to determine the size or persistence of the current supply shock at this point in time"
- MS. MINEHAN: "this forecast is surrounded by a cloud of uncertainty"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "something seems to be happening that none of us has ever witnessed before -- perhaps a once-in-a-century structural shift"
- MR. MEYER: "There is no question that there is an unusual degree of uncertainty on all counts"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "there is still a great deal of forecasting uncertainty in all of this"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express elevated uncertainty, primarily about the nature and persistence of the productivity acceleration and whether traditional Phillips curve/NAIRU frameworks remain valid. This is genuine, substantive uncertainty about the economic structure -- not merely rhetorical hedging. However, participants are fairly confident about the direction of policy needed, which limits the overall uncertainty score.

**Minority views:** Broaddus and Hoenig express more confidence that traditional frameworks apply and inflation risks are real. McTeer and McDonough express more confidence in the new economy narrative.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Several participants discuss goods prices. Moskow: "price increases that stick, both for final and intermediate goods," Chicago PMI prices paid component rising. Greenspan: unit costs flat. Jordan: food processing costs rising. Score: **48 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**. Goods inflation is contained but showing early signs of turning.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Limited specific discussion. Minehan notes service and retail wage pressures. Guynn: "Health care is a good example... where we are seeing some relatively large upward price adjustments." Score: **55 +/-10 [Low confidence]**. Services prices showing somewhat more pressure than goods.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Broaddus: construction cost estimates "up about 30 percent" in one year in Boston. Minehan: "Rents for commercial space continue to rise and now exceed those in both New York and San Francisco." Strong residential and commercial real estate. Score: **62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**. Housing/shelter costs are clearly elevated.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Hoenig: "inflation premium embedded in the Treasury inflation-protected securities has been rising since last fall." Jordan: consumer price outlook surveys show "both perceptions of current inflation and expectations about future inflation have moved up sharply." Score: **55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**. Expectations are drifting up from low levels but not unanchored.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A (credit conditions discussed but framed as loose/accommodative, not stressed).

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Kelley: "stock market... very precarious position. It's very fragile." Greenspan: "we simultaneously argue that stock market prices are overvalued." Minehan: "high stock market PE ratios." These are overvaluation/bubble concerns. Score: **20 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**. Direction: 1 = extreme overvaluation concern (bubble). Participants have bubble-type concerns but it is not the dominant theme.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Poole: "bank examiners are expressing a little concern... about loan administration." Jordan: "examiners also are starting to say that they are seeing very early signs of things they are not comfortable with." Score: **38 +/-8 [Low confidence]**. Early signs of concern about lending standards rather than actual stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Kelley: "The dollar's foreign exchange value could begin to weaken." McTeer: "bulk of our price improvement in recent years came from lower import prices due to the strong dollar, but the dollar is still pretty strong." Limited discussion. Score: N/A (mentioned only in passing, not prominently by 2+ participants with concern about dollar strength per se).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Extensively discussed. McDonough: "productivity may have grown at an annual rate of 4 percent or even more." Greenspan: "something profoundly important" -- a possible "once-in-a-century structural shift." Moskow: directors report "technology has enhanced productivity pretty much across the board and that its potential to yield further productivity improvements seems limitless." Score: **78 +/-8 [High confidence]**. Participants see productivity growth as exceptional and possibly accelerating further.
