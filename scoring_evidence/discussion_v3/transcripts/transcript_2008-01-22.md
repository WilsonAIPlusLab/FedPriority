### 2. Era Context

January 2008 is the early phase of the Global Financial Crisis. The housing market had been contracting for over a year, subprime mortgage losses had crystallized in mid-2007, and by January 2008 monoline insurer downgrades and broader credit deterioration were the focal concerns. U.S. equity markets had fallen ~16.5% from the prior FOMC meeting. The Fed had cut rates 100bp since August 2007 (to 4.25%) and was now contemplating an emergency 75bp cut (to 3.50%). This is emphatically a crisis-management/pre-recession call, not a normal policy meeting.

**Broad macro backdrop**: Economy widely feared to be entering recession; credit markets tightening; financial stability risks acute and intensifying. Inflation was elevated (~4% headline CPI/PCE) but participants expected it to moderate as the economy slowed.

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 65 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Hoenig: "total inflation is above...year over year—4 percent. I understand the concerns about the markets...but we are taking these very dramatic actions right now, more perhaps than what we need when you say 'where interest rates need to be.'"
- Stern: "the inflation numbers haven't been all that I might have hoped for, and even core inflation has been running a little higher than I thought it would."
- Fisher: "if we were to cut rates to this level today...we would have a negative real rate of interest" (implying current headline inflation exceeds the funds rate).

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is perceived as clearly above target (~4% headline, core also elevated above expectations), acknowledged by most participants as a genuine concern, but the committee collectively subordinates it to financial stability and growth risks, and expects disinflation as the economy slows.

**Minority views**: Fisher and Hoenig argue more forcefully that cutting with negative real rates is problematic. Bernanke counters that real rates should be assessed on forward expected inflation, not past headline.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 28 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- Yellen: "the risk of a severe recession and credit crisis is unacceptably high, and it is being clearly priced now into not only domestic but also global markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Virtually all participants believe the economy is at or very near recession entry, with substantial downside risk to a "deep and protracted" downturn; the economy is clearly below potential and deteriorating rapidly.

**Minority views**: Fisher is less convinced of severe recession: "I don't hear a widespread expectation of recession...the words 'severe recession' I have yet to hear from the lips of anybody but those in the housing business." This is the sole significant dissenting voice on economic severity.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 82 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Bernanke: "there is building in the market a real dynamic of withdrawal from risk, withdrawal from normal credit extension, which I think is very worrisome."
- Warsh: "losses appear to be self-reinforcing. Panic appears to be begetting further pullbacks by investors, retail and institutional alike...this pullback strikes me as quite nondiscriminate, geographically and in terms of sectors, companies, and even entire asset classes."
- Kohn: "We have a vicious cycle in housing between the financial markets and the housing markets, where the decline in the housing markets is feeding into the credit markets, which is feeding back on the housing market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are very tight and worsening rapidly, with a self-reinforcing panic dynamic, widespread credit tightening across sectors, monoline downgrades adding further pressure; not yet a full credit freeze (short-term funding markets had improved via TAF) but clearly in severe stress territory.

**Minority views**: Lockhart notes "the TAF actually appears to have done its work pretty well, and the need for the TAF may be diminishing" — a small silver lining in short-term funding, but no participant argues conditions are anything other than severely tight/deteriorating.
