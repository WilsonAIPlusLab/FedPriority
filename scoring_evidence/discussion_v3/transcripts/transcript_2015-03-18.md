## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from the March 17–18, 2015 FOMC meeting. Speakers are individually identified (MR. ROSENGREN., MR. LOCKHART., CHAIR YELLEN., etc.). The document contains two distinct sections: (a) a technical discussion on Day 1 about ON RRP facility mechanics for liftoff (operational normalization implementation), and (b) the economic go-round and policy discussion on both days. The file is well-segmented with a "=== Policy Discussion ===" divider. Speaker attribution is clear throughout.

### 2. Era Context

March 2015: The FOMC is in the pre-liftoff period of 2015. The unemployment rate is approximately 5.5% (near NAIRU estimates of 5.0–5.5%). Core PCE inflation is running around 1.3%, well below the 2% target. Oil prices have plunged since mid-2014. The dollar has appreciated sharply (~15% on a broad trade-weighted basis since June 2014). ECB QE has recently commenced. The Fed still has its policy rate at the ZLB. The key decision at this meeting is whether to remove "patient" from the forward guidance statement (they do, supporting Alt-B). The ZLB is not binding as an acute crisis constraint — this is pre-liftoff normalization planning. I will score based solely on what participants say in this document.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**

Direction: Participants see inflation as clearly BELOW target. Core PCE is at 1.3% (Brainard), the Dallas trimmed mean three-month rate is 0.7% (Brainard). Market-based inflation compensation at historical lows. Rosengren lowered his 2015 Q4-Q4 core PCE forecast to 1.2%. Evans says inflation is "only slowly crawling back up to 2 percent sometime in 2018 or later." Kocherlakota projects inflation won't return to 2% until late 2018. The majority view is that inflation is meaningfully below target with uncertainty about the return path.

However, most participants are NOT expressing deflation alarm. They view the miss as significant but expect eventual return to 2%. Lacker and Mester are relatively more confident. Fischer is "reasonably confident inflation will move back to 2%." The dominant view is "below target, disinflationary transitory forces (oil, dollar), but not deflation." This places inflation well below the midpoint. Given the anchor table, 2015 pre-liftoff sits in the 38–42 range generally. Here, participants are even more subdued on current inflation than the typical 2013–19 mean — Rosengren, Evans, Kocherlakota are markedly pessimistic. But there is no deflationary-spiral language. Score: approximately **30–33**.

**OUTPUT_GAP**

Direction: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential but approaching it. Williams projects unemployment dipping to 5% (below his 5.2% NAIRU estimate) by end of year. Mester projects unemployment at 5.2% by end of 2015 (below her 5.5% NAIRU). Most participants project above-trend GDP growth for 2015: Lacker 2.2%, Mester 3%, Bullard above 3%, Williams 2.5%. However, several note Q1 is very soft (Atlanta Fed nowcast: real GDP growth "significantly below 1%"), and concerns about dollar headwinds and lower potential growth estimates. Yellen's summary: "continuing to improve…outlook still sufficiently favorable to support continued improvement in the labor market." Most participants see slack remaining but economy approaching potential. Chair Yellen explicitly: "In spite of the decline in the unemployment rate, all but two of you continue to think that some slack remains in the labor market."

The economy is still below potential (slack remains by broad consensus), but approaching it rapidly. This is consistent with ~38–43 range. The strong employment momentum pulls toward the higher end; the Q1 softness, dollar drag, and inflation miss pull lower. Score: **42**.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**

Direction: Participants see the labor market as TIGHTER than prior years, near but not quite at full employment. Unemployment at 5.5%, heading toward 5.0%. Payrolls averaging 280,000+ last three months. Yellen: "all but two of you continue to think that some slack remains." Several presidents note improving labor conditions — vacancies up, job openings at cyclical high, U-6 declining. Multiple participants note wage growth still sluggish (~2%), which signals remaining slack. Kocherlakota: "other than in a few specific occupations and locales, compensation pressures remain, at best, moderate." Brainard: "just how much slack remains is difficult to say, but there appears to be some slack along several margins."

The labor market is distinctly better than 2010–14, but still has residual slack in broader measures. The anchor for 50 is 2016–17; this is slightly earlier and weaker. Score: **46** — near the NAIRU boundary but not yet through it, with broad agreement that some slack remains.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**

Direction: Mixed — consumer spending described as moderate/solid trend but recent data has been soft. Williams: "business contacts remain very optimistic, with reports of underlying consumer strength," but "recent national spending data look lackluster relative to expectations." Lockhart: Q1 GDP nowcast "significantly below 1 percent." Lacker: "consumer spending trend appears to be attributable to the strong labor market, improving income prospects, and rising real wealth." Business investment: mixed — energy sector cutting sharply (drilling/capex), non-energy business investment modest. Housing: Brainard "residential investment remains very weak." Net exports: large drag from dollar appreciation (1 percentage point off GDP per Tealbook). Chair Yellen's summary: "general agreement that current readings on overall activity have, on balance, moderated since the January meeting." Consumer is expected to be a bright spot; other components are weak.

This is in the "at-to-slightly-below trend" range with transitory softness. Not contraction but clearly below the 50–55 normal range. Score: **43**.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**

Direction: Financial conditions are discussed primarily in the context of dollar appreciation and its tightening effect on the external sector. Evans explicitly notes: "the dollar's appreciation is already imparting some restrictiveness that is somewhat like a monetary policy tightening." Williams and others discuss ECB QE driving down long-term rates globally. Bond term premiums are very low (Dudley discusses extensively). No participant describes tight credit conditions, wide spreads, or restricted lending. The domestic financial environment from the Fed's stance is still highly accommodative (ZLB + large balance sheet). However, the dollar and external financial conditions represent a tightening impulse.

The overall financial conditions picture is of loose domestic conditions but meaningful tightening from external sources (dollar, imported disinflation). Credit is not described as restricted. This is in the neutral-to-slightly-loose range, perhaps 38–42, with the dollar-driven external tightening acknowledged but not dominating domestic conditions. Score: **40**.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**

Direction: The CENTER OF GRAVITY is toward removing "patient" and opening the door to June liftoff, while maintaining a very gradual expected path. This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish meeting. The final vote on Alt-B (remove "patient") is near-unanimous — only Kocherlakota formally dissents and supports Alt-A. All others support Alt-B. However, within Alt-B supporters, there is a wide range:
- Hawkish end (June or soon liftoff): Lacker, Mester, George, Fischer, Bullard
- Moderate (September more likely but June possible): Powell, Williams, Dudley
- Lean dovish within Alt-B (December liftoff, support Alt-B for strategic reasons only): Tarullo, Brainard, Rosengren, Evans

This meeting removes "patient" (a definite hawkish step) but in a carefully hedged way. The forward guidance is data-dependent but tilted toward eventual tightening. The modal liftoff view in SEPs is June–September 2015. The rubric anchor for "40–50 (2014–15 pre-liftoff)" is appropriate here, but this meeting IS the actual step of removing patient, which is a hawkish action. This puts it more toward 52–58 range — lean hawkish current action with the caveat that liftoff timing is still uncertain. Score: **55**.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**

Direction: There is one formal dissent (Kocherlakota supports Alt-A rather than Alt-B). Evans supports Alt-B but makes clear his preferred liftoff is Q1 2016 — he is a reluctant supporter. Rosengren supports Alt-B but says he won't be "reasonably confident by June." Tarullo supports Alt-B but moved his liftoff to December. Brainard supports Alt-B but expresses hesitancy about June conditions. On the other side, Lacker wants to signal June more strongly. The meeting thus has 1 formal dissent (Kocherlakota) plus a cluster of 3–4 participants who express significant reservations about the pace.

On the normalization technical discussion (Day 1), there is broad agreement on the no-cap option at liftoff, though Tarullo has ongoing financial stability concerns about the ON RRP facility and Powell prefers an $800B cap (minority). The "fairly soon" language debate was roughly evenly split (Yellen noted "one more in favor of keeping it than removing it").

Overall: 1 formal dissent, 3–4 reluctant supporters, but also 1 language dissenter (Lacker on statement wording). This is a meeting with notable but not extreme disagreement. Score: **38**.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**

Direction: Financial stability is discussed meaningfully but is not the dominant topic. Tarullo raises ON RRP financial stability risks at length on Day 1. Dudley discusses bond term premiums as a financial stability wild card. Kocherlakota mentions financial stability risks are "not material for the macroeconomy." Tarullo raises Brazil and dollar-denominated debt risks. Brainard discusses implications of dollar strength and Greek exit for financial stability. These are meaningful but scattered mentions — no single participant makes it the primary focus of their economic go-round remarks. Score: **35**.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**

Direction: The meeting removes "patient" and transitions to meeting-by-meeting data dependence with liftoff possible "at any meeting after April." The forward guidance is a mild tightening signal — it opens the door to June but does NOT commit to it. Yellen explicitly says: "I don't view the removal of 'patient' as a signal that liftoff in June is particularly likely." The new forward guidance uses "reasonably confident that inflation will move back to 2%" as the threshold. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening signal in the forward guidance — not a strong commitment to imminent rate hikes, but clearly moving in that direction. Per rubric anchor: "25–40 (2015 pre-liftoff)" for easing lean; this meeting is at the transition point — removing patient tips it toward 50–60. Score: **55**.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**

Direction: Multiple participants explicitly call out elevated uncertainty. Fischer: "We face two significant puzzles." Evans: "it is still very hard to be confident that we're headed back to our target." Powell: "there's actually, to me, very notable how much uncertainty there is around the inflation creation process." Rosengren: "there is great uncertainty around key variables." Brainard: "the fading of the so-called temporary factors may, in fact, take more time than we currently expect." Tarullo: extensive discussion of downside risks. Kocherlakota discusses possibility of permanently un-anchored expectations.

The uncertainty expressed is real and elevated — multiple participants emphasize it, especially around inflation. The unusual disconnect between labor market strength and inflation/growth is repeatedly flagged as puzzling. However, this is not "uncharted territory" or "cannot bound the range" language. It is more in the "harder to assess than usual" zone, particularly around inflation. Score: **52**.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "the incoming price data indicate that even the lowest forecast in the range for December will likely be too high. The Tealbook has lowered its forecast for 2015 Q4-to-Q4 core PCE inflation from 1½ percent to 1.3 percent, and I have lowered mine to 1.2 percent."
- Brainard: "The 12-month change in core PCE prices in January was 1.3 percent…the three-month change in the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas trimmed mean rate was only 0.7 percent."
- Evans: "I see inflation only slowly crawling back up to 2 percent sometime in 2018 or later."
- Kocherlakota: "I don't see inflation returning to 2 percent until late 2018" and "low inflation and the low-inflation outlook has reduced the credibility of the inflation target."
- Lacker (hawkish outlier): "prices have been held down lately by two clearly temporary factors: low oil prices and the strengthening dollar…I am confident that both headline and core inflation will move to 2 percent once these two temporary influences are behind us."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal agreement that current inflation is well below the 2% target (core PCE ~1.3%), with the majority either uncertain about or pessimistic on the return path; only a minority (Lacker, Fischer, Mester) express high confidence in timely return to target.

**Minority views:** Lacker and Mester are notably more confident that the below-target readings are truly transitory and inflation will return to 2% by late 2016 or early 2017. Fischer expresses "reasonable confidence" as well. These views widen the uncertainty band slightly upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen (summary): "all but two of you, based on your SEPs, continue to think that some slack remains in the labor market" and "the outlook is still sufficiently favorable to support continued improvement."
- Rosengren: "I had been expecting real GDP growth to be well above potential this year. With the dollar continuing to appreciate…I am now anticipating growth only modestly above its potential rate."
- Lockhart: "the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta's GDP-tracking estimate suggests a very soft first-quarter growth rate…nowcast model is showing real GDP growth significantly below 1 percent."
- Mester: "I continue to project GDP growth to be about 3 percent this year and next. Factors supporting above-trend growth include highly accommodative monetary policy, improving household and business balance sheets."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is still slightly below potential (slack remains, Q1 very soft) but the consensus expects above-trend growth for the remainder of 2015 that will close the remaining gap; the output gap is approaching zero from below rather than from above.

**Minority views:** Mester and Bullard project above-trend growth that may push the economy to or through potential by year-end. Several others (Dudley, Brainard, Tarullo) are more cautious and see downside risks from dollar strength and global slowdown.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 46 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "The unemployment rate has fallen more than 1 percentage point over the past year, and I expect it to decline to under 5 percent by the end of this year, falling below my estimate of the natural rate of 5.2 percent."
- Mester: "By a broad set of indicators, labor market conditions are strong and continue to improve. Monthly job gains have averaged more than 280,000 over the past three months…We are nearing our goal of maximum employment."
- Kocherlakota: "In the Ninth District…other than in a few specific occupations and locales, compensation pressures remain, at best, moderate…businesses continue to be able to find the workers that they want without paying them a lot."
- Yellen: "in spite of the decline in the unemployment rate, all but two of you continue to think that some slack remains in the labor market."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is nearing but has not yet reached full employment; unemployment at 5.5% is approaching NAIRU estimates of ~5.0–5.2%, but wages remain subdued (~2% growth), suggesting meaningful residual slack — particularly in broader measures like U-6 and involuntary part-time employment.

**Minority views:** Lacker sees the labor market as more clearly at or near full employment. Kocherlakota sees the 9th District as nearly full employment but notes that wage growth is still muted even there.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 43 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Lockhart: "Atlanta Fed's GDP-tracking estimate suggests a very soft first-quarter growth rate…nowcast model is showing real GDP growth significantly below 1 percent." Contacts remain "upbeat" despite mixed data.
- Brainard: "consumer spending appears to have increased robustly at the end of last year. Although auto and retail sales softened in February…Residential investment remains very weak…Business fixed investment looks to increase only modestly this year, particularly with lower oil prices pushing down drilling investment."
- Williams: "recent national spending data look lackluster relative to expectations…much of this disappointment appears to reflect transitory factors."
- Lacker: "fairly robust trend in consumer spending…should be sufficient to keep GDP growth above trend going forward."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is the relative bright spot but recent data has been soft; business investment is being dragged down by energy sector cuts; residential investment is "very weak"; the overall spending picture is slightly below the moderate trend pace, with multiple participants expecting a rebound in Q2–Q3 once transitory weather/port factors pass.

**Minority views:** Mester and Lacker are more sanguine, projecting near-3% growth. Dudley and Brainard are more cautious about sustained momentum.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "the dollar's appreciation is already imparting some restrictiveness that is somewhat like a monetary policy tightening."
- Dudley: "bond term premiums stay very low as QE continues in Europe and Japan" — domestic financial conditions remain loose; concern about potential snapback.
- Kocherlakota (policy discussion): "signs of excessive risk-taking are not widespread, and use of short-term financing instruments and indicators of leverage remain moderate."
- No participant describes restricted credit, widened spreads, or difficulty obtaining financing; focus is on policy still being "extremely accommodative."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Domestic financial conditions remain loose (ZLB policy + large balance sheet), but the sharp dollar appreciation is creating a meaningful external tightening impulse; the overall financial conditions picture is modestly loose rather than neutral, though the external headwinds are beginning to bite.

**Minority views:** Some participants (Evans, Tarullo, Brainard) view the dollar appreciation as more materially tightening; others (Bullard) are skeptical that dollar strength translates meaningfully into U.S. GDP drag.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Near-unanimous vote for Alt-B (remove "patient," open door to June liftoff): Only Kocherlakota dissents in favor of Alt-A.

- Kocherlakota (dissent): "just removing the word 'patient,' in and of itself, is a tightening of monetary policy. I'm opposed to such a move at this time."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee takes a definitive lean-hawkish step by removing "patient" and signaling liftoff is possible at any post-April meeting, though the forward guidance is carefully hedged and the Chair explicitly says June is not particularly likely — the stance is thus "approaching liftoff" rather than "committed to liftoff."

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota (1 formal dissent) prefers maintaining accommodation; Evans, Rosengren, Brainard, and Tarullo support Alt-B but strongly favor September or later, moderating the hawkish lean.
