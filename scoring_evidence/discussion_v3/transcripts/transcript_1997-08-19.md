# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-08-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the August 19, 1997 FOMC meeting. It includes a go-around with individual participant statements from regional presidents and governors, followed by a policy discussion, and a vote on the directive. Speakers include Minehan, Hoenig, McTeer, Guynn, Broaddus, Stern, Boehne, McDonough, Kelley, Rivlin, Meyer, Jordan, Phillips, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
August 1997 is in the middle of the late-1990s expansion. Unemployment is low (around 4.8% nationally, with some districts reporting 2-3%), GDP growth has been solid, and inflation has been surprisingly benign. The Asian financial crisis is just beginning (Thailand crisis in July 1997) but is not yet a major concern in this meeting. The stock market is strong. The FOMC has been holding rates steady since March 1997 with an asymmetric tightening bias.

### Directional Analysis for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint -- still declining or very subdued. Greenspan emphasizes "we still may be experiencing disinflation," with price indexes "coming in lower than in 1996." Multiple participants note muted inflationary pressures: Boehne says "actual signs of an inflationary buildup are shadowy at best," Guynn finds "no new evidence of unusual developments in prices," Stern notes "wage and price pressures are generally absent." Kelley notes "PPI has fallen for seven straight months." McDonough sees core goods prices "subdued." However, several participants note upside risks from tight labor markets. Meyer: "concerned about the risks of higher inflation as a result of an economy operating beyond its sustainable capacity." The CENTER OF GRAVITY is that current inflation is low/benign, but with acknowledged upside risks. Score should be in the 35-42 range -- below target, with favorable surprises.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as operating ABOVE potential. McDonough explicitly states "the economy is at present operating about 2 percentage points above what would appear to be potential GDP." Hoenig: "the economy expanding at a pace a bit over potential." Meyer: "an economy that is operating beyond its sustainable capacity." Multiple districts report strong activity, tight labor markets. The bright-line rule requires > 50. The center of gravity suggests moderately above potential. Score: 57-60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Nearly every participant reports tight labor markets. Minehan: unemployment 4.2% in New England, "targeted compensation schemes" for tight markets. Hoenig: 2% unemployment in Nebraska. McTeer: "tight labor markets continue." Guynn: "Labor markets in our region also remain tight." Stern: "employers are going to new extremes to find workers." Jordan: "construction being constrained only by labor shortages." Greenspan: "the number of people who want to work is diminishing at a very rapid pace," labor supply squeeze. This is clearly tight -- the 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75, and mid-1997 is approaching that range. Score: 63-67.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Q2 was driven by inventory/capex with consumer weakness, but Q3 shows consumer rebound. Broaddus: "activity did pick up fairly markedly in our region in June and July, especially in retail and services." Jordan's retail contact reports "double-digit rates of increase." McDonough: "sales were above plan in July." Guynn: tourism "at or near capacity." Strong capex. But some moderation expected from inventory correction. Net: above trend but not booming. Score: 58-62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions as tight or loose. Kelley notes "long-term interest rates have eased on balance; the yield curve is flatter and lower." The dollar is strong. Stock market is booming. Jordan reports some credit standard loosening by regional/super-regional banks. McDonough discusses strong dollar helping keep inflation down. Greenspan/Minehan discuss asset prices booming with benign product price inflation. Overall, conditions seem accommodative/loose. Score: 25-30 (loose conditions).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous agreement to hold rates steady with asymmetric tightening bias ("B" alternative). Greenspan: "there is very little reason to move today... the risks are on the upside and there also is little reason to discard the asymmetric directive toward tightening." Every single participant agrees. This is a hold-with-tightening-bias stance. Score: 53-57 (lean hawkish -- holding but biased to tighten).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric tightening bias is the forward signal. Guynn: "an anticipatory anti-inflationary policy move in the future is still quite likely." Greenspan frames future tightening as conditional: "if we find instead that final demand is significantly higher... we will have no choice but to tighten." Meyer: "we may have more time to refine our forecast." The forward signal is mildly hawkish but patient. Score: 58-63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Near-unanimous. Every single participant agrees with Greenspan's recommendation of hold + asymmetric tightening bias. No formal dissents. No strongly divergent views -- the differences are about paradigm interpretation (old vs new paradigm) not policy. Score: 10-15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Some notable discussion. Greenspan raises asset prices vs product prices dilemma: "What do we do when there is no inflation but asset prices are booming?" Minehan follows up on this. Jordan discusses farmland price bubbles, commercial real estate concerns, and banks lowering credit standards. Broaddus discusses inflation expectations volatility. But this is not a dominant topic -- it's raised but not deeply pursued by most. Score: 25-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Already scored above.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. Greenspan discusses productivity measurement mystery at length. Meyer notes "the Greenbook forecast is very finely balanced." Kelley: "straws in the wind that are swirling in all directions." Rivlin: "stuck between the old and the new paradigms." Jordan: "it really is going to be November before we get a reading." The paradigm debate itself reflects genuine uncertainty about the structure of the economy. Score: 40-48.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/declining. Score ~38 (below 50). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score ~58. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score ~65. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score ~27. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold + tightening bias. Score ~55. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65) both above 50 -- consistent with late-cycle boom. Labor is tighter than output gap suggests, which aligns with participants noting the puzzle of tight labor without inflation.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60) both mildly hawkish -- consistent. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish than current action (hold), which makes sense with the asymmetric bias.
- Low INFLATION_PRESSURE (38) with high OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65) -- this IS the puzzle the entire meeting discusses. Participants explicitly wrestle with why tight markets aren't producing inflation. Coherent with the text.

### Speaker Count
Approximately 13-14 speakers in the go-around. On policy: all agree on hold + asymmetric tightening. On inflation: consensus that it's currently low but risks tilt up. On growth: consensus that economy is strong, above potential.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "we still may be experiencing disinflation... the inflation rate over these last six months has been coming in lower than in 1996"
- BOEHNE: "actual signs of an inflationary buildup are shadowy at best"
- KELLEY: "the PPI has fallen for seven straight months"
- MEYER: "concerned about the risks of higher inflation as a result of an economy that is operating beyond its sustainable capacity" (minority upside risk view)

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation readings are favorable and declining, but nearly all participants acknowledge upside risks from tight labor markets. The score reflects the present-tense assessment of subdued inflation with recognized future risk.

**Minority views**: Meyer and Broaddus lean more concerned about inflation risk, widening the band upward. Kelley raises the possibility of deflation (downside outlier).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MCDONOUGH: "the economy is at present operating about 2 percentage points above what would appear to be potential GDP"
- HOENIG: "We currently see the economy expanding at a pace a bit over potential"
- MEYER: "an economy that is operating beyond its sustainable capacity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement that the economy is operating above potential, though there is an expectation of moderation in H2 1997 due to inventory correction. The "2 percentage points above potential" from McDonough is among the stronger statements; most others characterize it as modestly above.

**Minority views**: None significantly below 50; the question is how far above, not whether above.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- HOENIG: "The rate is 2 percent in Nebraska"
- GREENSPAN: "the number of people who say they want a job but are not currently seeking one has gone down 1-1/2 million in the last three years... a fairly dramatic squeeze on the potential labor supply"
- STERN: "employers are going to new extremes to find workers"
- JORDAN: "construction in the region is being constrained only by labor shortages"

**Center-of-gravity**: Nearly universal reports of tight labor markets across all districts. Unemployment is low, labor supply is shrinking, and anecdotal evidence of shortages is widespread. Yet wage pressures are contained -- the central puzzle of this meeting.

**Minority views**: None -- all agree labor markets are tight. The uncertainty is about whether this tightness will eventually produce wage/price pressures.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- BROADDUS: "activity did pick up fairly markedly in our region in June and July, especially in the retail and services sectors"
- JORDAN: retail contact reports "double-digit rates of increase" in early Q3
- GUYNN: "hospitality and tourist industry... is operating at or near capacity"
- JORDAN: "the whole high-tech sector has ramped up sharply"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is rebounding strongly in Q3 after a softer Q2, while business investment remains robust. The mix is shifting from inventory/capex toward consumer spending. Above-trend but expected to moderate as inventory investment slows.

**Minority views**: Some uncertainty about sustainability; Jordan's retail contact "not yet confident that the strength can be sustained." McDonough notes possible downside risk if inventory correction is sharper.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- KELLEY: "long-term interest rates have eased on balance; the yield curve is flatter and lower"
- JORDAN: "the regionals and super regionals continue to lower their credit standards"
- MINEHAN/GREENSPAN exchange on "asset prices booming" with no inflation -- implying accommodative financial conditions

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are loose. Long rates have declined, the stock market is soaring, credit is freely available (with some banks loosening standards), and the strong dollar is dampening import prices. No stress indicators mentioned.

**Minority views**: Limited discussion -- scoring reflects what is in the text. Confidence is moderate because financial conditions are not a primary focus.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 ±4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "the risks are on the upside and there also is little reason to discard the asymmetric directive toward tightening"
- GREENSPAN: "there is very little reason to move today"
- All participants agree: "B" asymmetric (hold + tightening bias)
- GUYNN: "an anticipatory anti-inflationary policy move in the future is still quite likely"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with tightening bias. No one advocates for immediate action in either direction. The lean is mildly hawkish because of the asymmetric bias, but the actual stance is patient/watchful.

**Minority views**: Essentially none on policy -- remarkable unanimity. Narrow band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Every participant explicitly agrees with the Chairman's recommendation
- Vote on directive language change carries "unanimously" by show of hands
- BOEHNE: "I agree with your 'B' asymmetric proposal." RIVLIN: "So do I." PARRY: "I agree as well." (and so on for every member)

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-total consensus on both the policy action and the economic assessment. Differences are analytical (paradigm debate) not prescriptive. This is one of the most consensual meetings imaginable.

**Minority views**: Broaddus raises a point about directive language prioritizing price stability, but does not press it to a dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN/MINEHAN exchange: "What do we do with monetary policy when there is no inflation but asset prices are booming?" and reference to "irrational exuberance"
- JORDAN: "farmland... sudden and sharp increases in prices that make no sense"; "developers... getting later and later in their payments"; banks "continue to lower their credit standards"
- BROADDUS: discusses volatility in inflation expectations from indexed bonds

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives meaningful but not dominant attention. Greenspan and Minehan engage in a notable exchange about asset prices and monetary policy, and Jordan raises real estate and banking concerns. But most participants focus on real economy conditions, not financial stability.

**Minority views**: Jordan is the most concerned about real estate/credit excess; most others do not engage on these themes.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Asymmetric tightening bias retained in the directive
- GREENSPAN: "if we do not [tighten], the risks will be too high" (conditional)
- GUYNN: "an anticipatory anti-inflationary policy move in the future is still quite likely"
- MEYER: "we may have more time to refine our forecast before we have to pen our conclusion"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- the next move, if any, will be a tightening. But there is no urgency, and the timing is data-dependent. The asymmetric bias is the primary forward signal.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge from this characterization.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "The important question that we have to ask ourselves is whether the fairly significant pickup in effective demand... will moderate before the pressures on wages begin to work their way through to prices"
- KELLEY: "straws in the wind that are swirling, I would guess, in all directions"
- RIVLIN: "stuck between the old and the new paradigms at the moment and not helped very much by the flow of data"
- JORDAN: "it really is going to be November before we get a reading on how good a job the staff has done"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, centered on the productivity puzzle and whether the traditional Phillips curve framework still applies. The "old paradigm vs new paradigm" debate permeates the meeting. Multiple participants express genuine uncertainty about the economic structure, not just rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: Some (Boehne, Hoenig) are more confident in the standard outlook; others (Kelley, Rivlin, Jordan) express more uncertainty.

## Optional Dimensions

### INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- KELLEY: "PPI has fallen for seven straight months"
- MCDONOUGH: "core goods... subdued"
- BROADDUS: "Prices for finished goods and manufacturing inputs are up about a percentage point from the almost negligible increases" -- a very modest uptick from a low base

### INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MCDONOUGH: "more concerned about increases in the prices of services where competition is less severe and more local... such prices will creep up"
- GREENSPAN: "the services CPI may look a little stronger in the near future than we have seen of late" (housing-related)
- Multiple mentions of healthcare cost increases: HOENIG, STERN, GUYNN

### INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "the little pop that we saw in the last CPI" in owners' equivalent rent; "Rent as a ratio to the market value of single-family homes has been falling for quite a long period of time" and may be stabilizing/reversing
- MINEHAN: "We have noticed an uptick in housing-related components of the CPI"

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- BROADDUS: Inflation-indexed bond spread implies "true expected trend rate of inflation is somewhere below 2 percent" after Boskin adjustment -- "very nice"
- But BROADDUS also notes "this indicator has been very volatile recently" with recent increase -- "we still do not have a level of credibility I would regard as fully consistent with price stability"

### CREDIT_STRESS
**Score: N/A**

### EQUITY_STRESS
**Score: 15 ±10 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MINEHAN: "there are increases in other asset prices that people are worried about, obviously the stock market"
- GREENSPAN: references "irrational exuberance" speech; "What do we do... when asset prices are booming?"
- This reflects overvaluation/bubble concern direction (low score = more concern about overvaluation)

### BANKING_STRESS
**Score: N/A**

Not enough prominent discussion by 2+ participants to score separately from financial stability.

### TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: N/A**

### DOLLAR_CONCERN
**Score: N/A**

The dollar is discussed as helpful for inflation restraint (strong dollar keeping import prices down), but this is not framed as a concern about dollar strength per se. Insufficient concern-focused discussion.

### PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 62 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "the productivity increase for the nonfinancial corporate sector looks something like 2-1/4 percent" -- above historical norms; discusses possible acceleration
- JORDAN: multiple anecdotes about technology enabling higher output -- "smarter equipment coupled with less skilled workers to get the same output"; software enabling flexible workforce use; training cost reduction
- RIVLIN: "There still seems to be a major mystery about what is going on, especially about productivity"
