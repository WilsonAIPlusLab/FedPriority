## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (May 2–3, 2017). Day 1 contains: (a) a financial stability discussion (Fischer, George, Brainard) and (b) the economic go-round (Bullard, Rosengren, Evans, Brainard, Mester, Kaplan, Powell, Harker, Fischer, George, Kashkari, Williams, Dudley, Yellen). Day 2 contains the policy go-round (Rosengren, Kashkari, Fischer, Harker, Bullard, Mester, Powell, Evans, Kaplan, Williams, George, Brainard, Dudley, Yellen) plus a lengthy statement-language/balance-sheet normalization discussion. Speakers are individually identified. The transcript is well-segmented, very long (~2,300+ lines), and has a large number of participants speaking. Confidence is HIGH for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
May 2017: Post-GFC recovery, well into the hiking cycle (December 2015 liftoff; hikes in December 2016 and March 2017 already in place). Federal funds rate target at 0.75–1.00%. Unemployment at 4.5%. Core PCE running at ~1.6%. First-quarter 2017 real GDP came in at 0.7% (weak, widely attributed to residual seasonality). "Soft data" (sentiment) considerably stronger than "hard data." Balance sheet normalization is being actively discussed. Era anchor per rubric: LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~50 (2016–17), INFLATION_PRESSURE 38–42 (2013–19 below target).

### 3. Directional Assessment — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as **below target**. The dominant signal across nearly all speakers is that core PCE is running at 1.6%, unchanged from a year ago, with no progress toward 2%. Multiple participants (Brainard: "substantially below our objective"; Kashkari: "we still have not reached our 2 percent target… we may not even be making progress"; Evans: "policy has some work to do"; Williams: "inflation only modestly below our 2% goal"). A minority (Mester, George, Fischer) cite CPI-based measures at or above 2% and argue March was idiosyncratic, expecting convergence to 2% by end-2017/early 2018. Majority view: slightly to moderately below target. The center of gravity is in the 36–42 range, consistent with the 2013–19 "below target" anchor of 38–42. March's soft print actually pushed slightly more dovish than usual for this era.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see output as **near to slightly above potential**, but with Q1 weakness. Most view the 0.7% Q1 GDP print as transitory/seasonal (virtually unanimous). The medium-run outlook is for GDP slightly above the 1¾–2% trend. Several participants explicitly state the economy is "at or above potential" or "above trend": Williams ("we have now reached or exceeded everyone's longer-run estimate of full employment"; "economy appears on track to outpace potential"); George ("current economic output is above its potential"); Rosengren ("projects above-potential GDP growth"). Bullard is the main dissenter, arguing the economy is in a balanced-growth steady state. The BRIGHT-LINE RULE: language of "above potential" from multiple speakers → score MUST be > 50. Score 53–56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as **tight, near or slightly beyond full employment**. Unemployment at 4.5%, below most natural rate estimates. Williams: "We have now reached or exceeded everyone's longer-run estimate of full employment"; Fischer: "unemployment rate… almost ½ percentage point below the staff's estimate of the natural rate"; Rosengren: "economy now appears to have exceeded full employment." The rubric anchor for 2016–17 is 50; current discussion describes a slight overshoot. Multiple references to "shortages of workers," "hard to find qualified workers." Score: 55–58, reflecting tight but not drastically so (wage growth still muted).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending as **slightly below trend in Q1 but expected to rebound; business investment picking up**. Q1 consumer spending was "only 0.3%" annualized (near halt), but nearly all attribute this to transitory factors. Business investment was strong in Q1. The expected bounceback means the medium-run assessment is around trend. Net: slightly below trend right now, but with high confidence of recovery. Score: 44–48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see financial conditions as **somewhat loose / accommodative**. George: "financial conditions appear to have eased over the past six months despite our actions to remove monetary accommodation on two occasions"; Dudley: "our recent moves to remove accommodation have not tightened financial conditions"; Brainard: "financial conditions have eased significantly, on net." Fischer notes negative real short-term rates and an accommodative stance. This places conditions in the 28–38 range (loose to somewhat loose). Score: 33–36.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The **unanimous action** is to hold at this meeting (alternative B, no rate change). The **forward lean** is toward continued gradual tightening — most participants expect 2–3 more hikes in 2017, starting with June. The meeting is clearly in the tightening cycle. However, there is notable dovish resistance from Bullard (skeptical of further hikes given weak data and flat Phillips curve) and Kashkari (inflation not progressing; June hike inappropriate without inflation progress). The large majority favor continued gradual removal of accommodation. Per rubric: 70–75 (strong hawkish: consensus for continued tightening; "further increases needed"). The hold decision + strong forward tilt toward June hike ≈ 68–72 range. Adjust slightly downward given Bullard and Kashkari dissent, but majority is firmly on a hiking path.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote on alternative B is unanimous (no formal dissents). However, there are notable differences in emphasis and forward views: Bullard is highly skeptical of further tightening; Kashkari conditions June on inflation progress; Brainard is cautious and concerned about inflation undershooting; Fischer questions the "long march" framework. These are genuine substantive differences, not mere tactical quibbles. The rubric anchor for Dec 2015 liftoff (similar unanimous vote with some reservations) is 25–35. Here, the breadth of expressed reservations — Bullard, Kashkari, Brainard each articulating distinct and strong concerns — pushes toward 35–45. Score: 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Day 1 opens with a dedicated financial stability session (Fischer, George, Brainard) covering asset valuations, leverage, FHLB, shadow banking, money market funds, housing, CRE, equity valuations. Multiple participants devote substantial time to it. Several more mention it in the economic go-round (Bullard on bond market, Powell on risk premiums, George on eased financial conditions). This is clearly more than passing mention. It does not dominate the meeting but receives prominent attention. Score: 42–50. Rubric: 20–35 typical for 2013–19. This meeting is notably above typical given the dedicated session and cross-discussion. Score: 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is **mild tightening — further rate hikes anticipated, likely June, plus balance sheet normalization later in 2017**. Most participants explicitly say June hike is likely if data cooperates. Williams: "three more rate increases this year and the start of normalization of the balance sheet"; Rosengren: "a path of either three or four increases per year"; Mester: "I anticipate that the Committee will raise the funds rate in June unless the data come in significantly weaker than expected." Yellen closes by keeping "door open for a hike in June." This is firmly in the 70–80 range: "clear tightening signal: further rate increases or balance sheet reduction ahead." Adjustment: some hedging ("data dependent," "not baked in the cake") and Bullard/Kashkari's skepticism moderate to ~72–76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Participants express **moderately elevated uncertainty**, centered on: (1) Q1 weakness — is it transitory? (2) Inflation trajectory — March decline: signal or noise? (3) Fiscal policy — large range of possible outcomes. (4) Natural rate of unemployment and r* uncertainty. Evans: "my uncertainty over all of these forecasts is a bit higher than it was in March"; Powell: "outlook as broadly unchanged, albeit less certain"; Kashkari: "there's a lot of uncertainty regarding domestic policy." This is above routine but not extreme; multiple specific uncertainties named. Rubric anchor: 25–35 (2013–14 stable expansion). This period has somewhat more uncertainty than average 2016–17 (fiscal policy unknown, Q1 surprise, inflation miss). Score: 38–44.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Brainard: "the trailing 12-month change in the case of core PCE prices is now 1.56 percent, substantially below our objective." Kashkari: "we still have not reached our 2 percent target for inflation, and we may not even be making progress toward that goal." Bullard: "core PCE inflation rate… now at 1.6 percent… suggesting that there's little inflation pressure in the U.S. economy."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Virtually all participants acknowledge core PCE at 1.6%, unchanged from a year ago, below target with no clear near-term progress; a minority (Mester, George, Fischer) argue alternative measures are near 2% and expect convergence by year-end, but the dominant tone is "below target and concerning."
- **Minority views**: Mester cites Cleveland median CPI at 2.5% year-over-year and expects sustainable 2% by end of year; Fischer says "I view inflation as being very close to our target." These views push the upper uncertainty band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Williams: "We are currently about as close as we've ever been to simultaneously achieving our inflation and full employment mandates, and yet the economy appears on track to outpace potential for the foreseeable future." George: "According to the Tealbook, current economic output is above its potential." Rosengren: "above-potential GDP growth."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite a weak Q1, the consensus view is that the economy is at or modestly above potential (for the medium run), with the Q1 miss deemed transitory; the bright-line trigger of "above potential" language from multiple participants requires a score above 50.
- **Minority views**: Bullard argues the economy is in a "balanced growth steady state" of ~2% and sees no meaningful overheating; Kashkari notes it is "difficult for me to take too seriously the concerns that the economy may be overheating."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 ±5 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Fischer: "unemployment rate… 4½ percent, almost ½ percentage point below the staff's estimate of the natural rate." Williams: "We have now reached or exceeded everyone's longer-run estimate of full employment from the March SEP." Evans: "I continue to hear many of the same old complaints about shortages of skilled workers."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is described as tight to very tight, with unemployment clearly below most NAIRU estimates and multiple participants reporting ongoing labor shortages; the consensus is that labor markets have overshot full employment, though wage growth remains muted.
- **Minority views**: Kashkari sees ongoing scope for labor force participation gains, implying more room to run; Brainard notes part-time-for-economic-reasons and prime-age EPOP remain below pre-crisis levels, suggesting some residual slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Powell: "consumption rose at an annual rate of only 0.3 percent compared with 3 percent for 2016." Brainard: "residential construction posted a double-digit increase, drilling for oil and natural gas is rebounding sharply… business spending on equipment and intangibles… rebounded at a strong annualized rate." Mester: "Business fixed investment picked up in the first quarter."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending was exceptionally weak in Q1 (attributed to transitory factors), but business investment and residential construction were strong, with nearly all participants expecting a consumption rebound; the net is slightly below trend for the current moment but with high confidence of return to ~trend pace.
- **Minority views**: Dudley raises structural concerns about motor vehicle sector demand going forward; Bullard worries Q1 data may have more signal than others believe.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 34 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: George: "financial conditions appear to have eased over the past six months despite our actions to remove monetary accommodation on two occasions… low levels of implied volatility and compressed risk spreads." Dudley: "our recent moves to remove accommodation have not tightened financial conditions." Brainard: "financial conditions have eased significantly, on net."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Multiple participants explicitly note that the rate hikes have not translated into tighter financial conditions, with eased conditions described by multiple speakers; the consensus is that conditions are accommodative/somewhat loose.
- **Minority views**: Fischer notes negative real rates but frames this through the lens of r* uncertainty rather than explicit looseness concern. Powell mentions compressed risk premiums in equities and corporate debt as a mild concern.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 69 ±8 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Williams: "I expect economic conditions, particularly the outlook, to support three more rate increases this year and the start of normalization of the balance sheet also by late this year." Rosengren: "a path of either three or four increases per year is quite gradual by historical standards." Mester: "I anticipate that the Committee will raise the funds rate in June unless the data come in significantly weaker than expected."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The strong majority view supports continuing gradual tightening, with June hike expected; policy consensus is firmly on a hiking path with balance sheet normalization also approaching; this places the meeting firmly in the strong hawkish range of the rubric (70–75 for 2017–18 hiking), moderated slightly by Bullard's skepticism and Kashkari's conditions on June.
- **Minority views**: Bullard believes the Committee is "somewhat overcommitted" to a sequence of rate moves and would prefer to delay further hikes; Kashkari says June hike inappropriate without more inflation progress.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 ±8 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Unanimous vote for alternative B (no formal dissents). However: Bullard: "I do not think the current constellation of real GDP growth and inflation data provides a rationale for 200 basis points of policy moves in the near future." Kashkari: "it will not be appropriate to raise the funds rate unless we see more progress toward our inflation objective." Fischer raises fundamental questions about the "long march" strategy in his extended policy remarks.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: No formal dissents, but the committee shows a clear spectrum from Rosengren/Williams (hawkishly concerned about overshoot) to Bullard/Kashkari (skeptical of further tightening), with substantive disagreements about the inflation outlook and appropriate pace; this places it in the moderate-differences range.
- **Minority views**: Most participants are aligned on holding now and hiking in June conditionally; the disagreement is mainly about the longer-run path and the weight to put on weak Q1/inflation data.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 48 ±8 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Fischer (lengthy): covers asset valuations "high across a range of markets, including corporate debt, equities, and commercial real estate," shadow banking, FHLB maturity transformation risks, international financial stability matrix. George: leverage, capital ratios, interconnectedness. Brainard: "rich asset valuations as a source of notable concern while noting that financial sector leverage and maturity transformation are well contained."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: A dedicated financial stability session opens Day 1 with three substantial presentations, followed by brief mentions in the economic go-round by George, Powell (risk premiums), and Yellen (stock market valuations somewhat "rich"); this goes well beyond the 20–35 typical for this era but does not dominate the meeting overall.
- **Minority views**: N/A — this is a measure of attention, not direction.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 74 ±6 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Yellen: "if economic conditions develop as I expect, a further increase in the target range at our next meeting will probably be appropriate"; Williams: "three more rate increases this year and the start of normalization of the balance sheet also by late this year"; Dudley: "if real GDP growth supported by a strong consumer spending looks like it's bouncing back in the second quarter… the case for tightening monetary policy at the June FOMC meeting will be quite strong."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish — June hike widely expected, multiple further hikes in 2017, and balance sheet normalization plan to be released at June meeting; this is solidly in the "clear tightening signal" range (76–90), moderated to ~74 by data dependence language and the hold decision itself.
- **Minority views**: Bullard: "we should be willing to consider using the July meeting instead, should that become a more appropriate timing"; Evans: "I still see one or two additional rate increases this year."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 ±7 | Confidence: High**

- **Evidence**: Evans: "my uncertainty over all of these forecasts is a bit higher than it was in March… uncertainty over fiscal policy is even higher now than before"; Powell: "I see the outlook as broadly unchanged, albeit less certain"; Kashkari: "there's a lot of uncertainty regarding domestic policy, tax reform, and health care in particular."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants name several genuine uncertainties: whether Q1 weakness is transitory, whether the March inflation miss signals a trend, what fiscal policy will deliver, natural rate of unemployment, r*; this is somewhat elevated above the 2017 baseline but well short of crisis-level uncertainty.
- **Minority views**: Mester and Harker express relatively high confidence in their outlooks; George views risks as "broadly balanced."
