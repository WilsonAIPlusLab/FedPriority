### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the June 19–20, 2012 FOMC meeting. Speakers are individually identified (MR. ROSENGREN, MR. PLOSSER, MR. KOCHERLAKOTA, MR. WILLIAMS, MR. LACKER, MR. LOCKHART, MR. FISHER, MR. EVANS, MR. BULLARD, MS. GEORGE, MS. PIANALTO, MS. YELLEN, MS. DUKE, MR. TARULLO, MS. RASKIN, MR. STEIN, MR. POWELL, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE). The transcript covers the policy go-around (individual statements) and the subsequent policy discussion/wrap-up by the Chairman. The document is well-segmented, with speakers clearly labeled.

### 2. Era Context
June 2012: The U.S. economy is in a slow, uneven recovery from the Great Financial Crisis (GFC). Unemployment remains high (~8.2%), well above NAIRU estimates. GDP growth is below trend (~2%). The primary external risk is the European sovereign debt crisis (Greece near default, Spain banking problems). Domestically, the U.S. fiscal cliff looms at year-end. The federal funds rate has been at zero since December 2008. The Fed has conducted QE1 and QE2 and initiated Operation Twist (Maturity Extension Program, MEP) in September 2011. The meeting decides to extend MEP through year-end. This is firmly in the QE/zero-lower-bound era, where discussions of "more accommodation" are the center of gravity.

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as BELOW target (slightly to meaningfully below 2%). The headline decline is attributed to lower oil prices, with core also expected to dip. Inflation expectations are described as "well-behaved" but 10-year breakevens have been declining. The majority view is that inflation will be at or slightly below 2% medium-term, with some risk of disinflation. A few (Evans, Yellen, Raskin) express concern about inflation running persistently below 2%. Lacker sees the dip as temporary; Plosser and Bullard are less worried about downside. Center of gravity: mildly below target. Score should be in the 32–40 range (below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation risk emerging). Not deeply deflationary (no 20s language), but clearly below target.

Specific evidence:
- Plosser: "the dip in inflation will be temporary"
- Williams: "the drop in oil and other commodity prices and the absence of wage pressures have shifted the outlook for inflation lower... we will be falling short on both employment and inflation for years"
- Yellen: "Absent further policy action, inflation seems likely to come in somewhat below our 2 percent objective"
- Stein: "sees inflation as subdued, likely coming below our 2 percent target"
- Evans: "our medium-run inflation forecast continues to be below our inflation objective throughout our forecast horizon"
- George: "inflation will remain at or slightly below our 2 percent target"
- Pianalto: "there is a risk that inflation could fall below our inflation objective over the medium term"

Score: ~36. Most participants see inflation as somewhat below 2%; disinflation is the primary concern. Not yet alarming (no talk of deflation spiral) but clearly below target and declining.
