# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2001-09-17 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an emergency FOMC meeting held on September 17, 2001, six days after the September 11 terrorist attacks. The discussion is extremely brief and narrowly focused -- essentially Chairman Greenspan proposes a 50bp cut, every participant expresses agreement, and then discussion turns to operational/infrastructure matters (market reopening, Bank of New York connectivity, building safety in lower Manhattan). There is no traditional go-around or economic assessment.

### Era Context
September 2001: The economy was already in a mild recession (the NBER later dated it March-November 2001). The 9/11 attacks created an enormous shock to confidence, financial infrastructure, and the economic outlook. The Fed had already been cutting rates aggressively through 2001 (from 6.5% to 3.5% before this meeting). This is explicitly flagged as an emergency meeting in the rubric.

### Speaker Count and Views
Speakers who express views on the policy action:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Proposes 50bp cut, cites "heightened degree of fear and uncertainty," "considerable downward pressure on asset prices," "probability of an asset price deflation." Balance of risks toward weakness.
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "Absolutely the right thing to do." 50bp is the right amount.
- MS. MINEHAN: Supports 50bp. Notes "if necessary to reverse our action in a month or two, we will."
- MR. PARRY: "Enthusiastically support."
- MR. BROADDUS: "Support your recommendation fully."
- MR. STERN: "This is what we need to do."
- MR. MOSKOW: "I agree completely."
- MR. SANTOMERO: "I concur."
- MR. GUYNN: "I concur."
- MR. HOENIG: "I'm on board with this completely."
- MR. POOLE: "I absolutely agree."
- MR. MCTEER: "I concur."
- MR. FERGUSON: "I fully support." Notes importance of reiterating long-term economic optimism.
- MR. KELLEY: "I concur."
- MR. MEYER: "I strongly support."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I support it."

That is 16 speakers, ALL supporting the 50bp cut unanimously. Zero dissent. Zero reservations.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Inflation is not discussed at all. No participant mentions prices, inflation, deflation in the consumer price sense. Greenspan mentions "asset price deflation" but that is about financial markets, not consumer inflation. Given the emergency nature and the exclusive focus on crisis response, this is extremely thin. However, the rubric says core dimensions should almost never be N/A. The only indirect signal is the unanimous support for a 50bp cut with zero inflation concern, suggesting inflation pressure was perceived as a non-issue. In September 2001, with the economy in recession, inflation was already low and receding. The complete absence of any inflation concern, combined with willingness to cut 50bp aggressively, implies participants see inflation pressure as low. I will score this low but with low confidence. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Greenspan states "the economy was showing some mixed signs... the most one could have said was that it might be stabilizing. There was hope for a recovery in growth but that recovery obviously had yet to emerge." The press statement says "employment, production, and business spending remained weak." Balance of risks "weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness." This clearly signals the economy is below potential. Direction: below midpoint. The language "remained weak" and the need for emergency action suggest meaningfully below potential but not catastrophic. Score ~30-32.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The press statement mentions "employment... remained weak" but no participant discusses labor markets specifically. Very limited evidence. The mention of weak employment places it below the midpoint. Score ~32, low confidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: The press statement says "business spending remained weak" and "last week's events have the potential to damp spending further." No participant discusses consumer spending or investment in detail. The signal is clearly below trend and weakening. Score ~28.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a key focus. Greenspan: "heightened degree of fear and uncertainty... placing considerable downward pressure on asset prices, increasing the probability of an asset price deflation." The Fed is "supply[ing] unusually large volumes of liquidity to the financial markets" and the funds rate "may be below its target on occasion." Ferguson discusses infrastructure problems (Bank of New York connectivity issues, building concerns). McDonough describes operational challenges. The financial system is under severe stress -- not from normal credit tightening but from physical disruption and fear. This is clearly in the high/crisis range. Score ~82.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous 50bp cut in an emergency meeting. Balance of risks toward weakness. Willingness to supply "unusually large volumes of liquidity." This is strongly dovish. The committee is in emergency easing mode. Score ~10-12. Not quite the extreme bottom because the 50bp cut is large but not the largest conceivable action, and there is no QE discussion.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. Every single speaker expresses full, unqualified support. Not a single reservation or hesitation. This is the lowest possible disagreement. Score ~5.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The meeting is dominated by financial stability concerns. Greenspan's opening is about asset price deflation risk and market functioning. A large portion of the meeting discusses market infrastructure (NYSE connectivity tests, Bank of New York problems, physical building safety, liquidity provision). This IS the meeting. Score ~90.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The press statement says "risks are weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness" -- a clear easing bias. Greenspan says "for the foreseeable future." Minehan notes they may need to "turn on a dime" if things improve but that's a hypothetical positive scenario. The forward signal is strongly dovish/easing. Score ~12.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Greenspan: "heightened degree of fear and uncertainty." "We are in a highly unstable situation." "It's not exactly self-evident how all this will turn out." Minehan: "if necessary to reverse our action in a month or two" -- implying the path is highly uncertain. Ferguson: connectivity tests went well but "it's not clear exactly how everything is going to work with heavy volume." Greenspan: "we can inadvertently create a major problem." This is extreme uncertainty -- "uncharted territory" given an unprecedented terrorist attack on financial infrastructure. Score ~85.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (31) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both below 50, consistent -- recession/weakness.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-10 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: No participant explicitly discusses inflation. The only price-related comment is Greenspan's "increasing the probability of an asset price deflation" (referring to financial assets, not consumer prices). The unanimous support for aggressive easing with zero inflation concern implies participants view inflation pressure as low/non-threatening.
- **Center of gravity**: Inflation is a non-issue at this meeting; participants are focused entirely on downside economic and financial risks, implying inflation pressure is perceived as well below target.
- **Minority views**: None expressed.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 31 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the economy was showing some mixed signs... the most one could have said was that it might be stabilizing. There was hope for a recovery in growth but that recovery obviously had yet to emerge." Press statement: "employment, production, and business spending remained weak, and last week's events have the potential to damp spending further."
- **Center of gravity**: Economy clearly operating below potential, already weak before 9/11, with the shock expected to push it further below.
- **Minority views**: None. Unanimous agreement on weakness.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-10 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Press statement: "employment... remained weak." No participant discusses labor markets in any detail.
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market is weak, consistent with the recession context, but extremely limited direct evidence in the discussion.
- **Minority views**: None.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 28 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (via press statement): "business spending remained weak, and last week's events have the potential to damp spending further." No participant discusses consumption or investment in any specificity.
- **Center of gravity**: Spending and investment were already weak and the 9/11 shock is expected to weaken them further -- clearly below trend and deteriorating.
- **Minority views**: None.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 82 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "heightened degree of fear and uncertainty... placing considerable downward pressure on asset prices, increasing the probability of an asset price deflation." Press statement: "The Federal Reserve will continue to supply unusually large volumes of liquidity to the financial markets as needed until more normal market functioning is restored." MR. FERGUSON describes Bank of New York connectivity problems, infrastructure stress, noting "it's not clear exactly how everything is going to work with heavy volume."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are severely stressed -- physical infrastructure disrupted, markets closed for days, asset price deflation risk, massive emergency liquidity being provided. This is a crisis-level financial tightness environment.
- **Minority views**: None; all participants share the crisis assessment.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 10 +/-3 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN proposes 50bp emergency cut. VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "Fifty basis points is the right amount; 25 basis points would be too little." Every single participant (16 speakers) expresses full support. Press statement commits to continued generous liquidity provision. Balance of risks "weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness."
- **Center of gravity**: Unanimous emergency easing -- strongly dovish. Not quite the absolute floor because the action is 50bp (not larger) and there is no QE or unconventional policy discussion.
- **Minority views**: None. Unanimous.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 5 +/-3 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Every participant expresses unqualified support: "enthusiastically support" (PARRY), "I agree completely" (MOSKOW), "I'm on board with this completely" (HOENIG), "I absolutely agree" (POOLE), "I strongly support" (MEYER). Zero reservations, zero alternative proposals, zero dissents.
- **Center of gravity**: Near-perfect consensus. This is as close to unanimity as FOMC meetings get.
- **Minority views**: None whatsoever.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 90 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: The entire meeting is about financial stability and market functioning. Greenspan's opening focuses on asset price deflation risk and liquidity. Ferguson provides an extended update on NYSE/NASDAQ connectivity, Bank of New York infrastructure, building safety. McDonough discusses dial-up lines, physical infrastructure, and operational continuity. The economic discussion is minimal compared to financial stability/infrastructure discussion.
- **Center of gravity**: Financial stability IS the meeting. It overwhelmingly dominates all other topics.
- **Minority views**: None.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Press statement: "risks are weighted mainly toward conditions that may generate economic weakness." CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: proposes "keeping that language... for the foreseeable future." MS. MINEHAN: "Should it be necessary to reverse our action in a month or two, we will take the appropriate steps" -- suggesting the baseline is continued accommodation unless positive surprise.
- **Center of gravity**: Strong forward easing bias. The committee signals continued accommodation ahead with risks weighted to the downside.
- **Minority views**: None.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "heightened degree of fear and uncertainty," "We are in a highly unstable situation," "it's not exactly self-evident how all this will turn out," "we can inadvertently create a major problem." MR. FERGUSON: "it's not clear exactly how everything is going to work with heavy volume." MS. MINEHAN: acknowledges possibility of reversing course in "a month or two." CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "There is no way to discuss the outlook for the American economy" -- literally saying the future is too uncertain to discuss publicly.
- **Center of gravity**: Extreme uncertainty. An unprecedented terrorist attack has made the economic and financial outlook fundamentally unknowable. Multiple participants express deep uncertainty about market functioning, economic trajectory, and appropriate future policy.
- **Minority views**: None; uncertainty is universally acknowledged.
