# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — June 12–13, 2018

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from the June 12–13, 2018 FOMC meeting. It is a two-day session with individual participant statements clearly attributed by name (MR. WILLIAMS., MS. MESTER., CHAIRMAN POWELL., etc.). The document contains:
- Day 1 (June 12): Economic go-around by all participants, followed by a Q&A on yield curve analysis from staff (Thomas)
- Day 2 (June 13): Policy go-around with each participant stating their vote and reasoning

Speakers identified: Williams, Rosengren, Harker, Bullard, Barkin, Mester, Evans, Kaplan, Brainard, Quarles, George, Bostic, Kashkari, Dudley (Vice Chairman), and Powell (Chairman).

### 2. Era Context

Mid-2018 hiking cycle. The FOMC had been raising rates since December 2015. Tax reform (TCJA) had just passed, providing fiscal stimulus. Unemployment had fallen to 3.8%, a near-18-year low. Core PCE inflation was approaching 2% from below. Trade policy uncertainty (tariffs, NAFTA) was a growing downside risk. The yield curve was flattening and multiple participants discussed inversion risk. The meeting ends with a near-unanimous 25bp hike (Bullard dissenting, preferring to hold). This is a **standard, well-attended** meeting with extensive economic discussion.

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as *at or very slightly below target*. Williams: "closing in on our 2 percent goal," expects "modest overshooting by 2020." Mester: "inflation to be sustainably at 2 percent by the end of this year." Brainard: "core PCE prices rose 1.9 percent" and "after seven years of below-target inflation." Powell: "core inflation moved up close to 2 percent." Bullard is the main dovish outlier: Dallas trimmed mean at 1.7% is "not showing inflationary pressure." Kashkari: "close to target—not quite there yet." Center of gravity: near 2% PCE, slightly below on some measures, at or slightly above on others. This maps to ~50, but given that headline was touching 2.2%, most expected near-term achievement of goal and modest modest overshoot in the out-years — score ~52.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as *above potential* or rapidly approaching it. Williams: GDP 2.8% "well above the pace of potential, which I still put at 1.7 percent." Rosengren: "GDP growing much faster than potential." Mester: "economy is operating beyond maximum employment," "above-trend real GDP growth." Powell: "real GDP growth with a 'three' handle." Bostic is cautious — sees Q2 strength as partly transitory — but still expects above-trend growth. Quarles is most optimistic on potential. Bullard is the key dissenter: current growth is temporary, warns against permanent response. Nevertheless, the clear center of gravity is *above potential*. Score: 62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as *very tight*. Williams: unemployment at 3.75%, heading to 3.5%, "historically low, not seen since the 1960s," "quite tight." Rosengren: already at 3.8% where March SEP expected year-end. Mester: "economy is operating beyond maximum employment," unemployment gap "larger than we saw at any point during the expansions of 1982–1990 and 2001–2007." Brainard: falling to lowest since 1969. George: 3.8% "well below my assessment of its longer-run level." Kashkari is the main dissenter: "not quite at full employment," wage growth modest, prime-age participation still low. Bostic: "labor markets tight but plans to raise wages have not risen to critical mass." Clear center of gravity: extremely tight. Score: 73.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending and investment as *above trend* but with nuance. Williams: "robust economic expansion." Mester: "personal incomes are rising, household balance sheets healthy," "business fixed investment increased at robust pace of 6 percent." Brainard: "consumer spending growth bounced back," BFI "robust pace of 6 percent." Harker: "consumer spending has firmed," "manufacturing activity accelerated." George: "solid consumption growth." However, Bostic: strips out utility spike from consumer spending, capital spending held back by trade uncertainty; Kaplan: trade policy uncertainty deferring investment. Q1 was acknowledged as "soft." Net assessment: robust but with forward uncertainty. Score: 60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see financial conditions as *modestly loose to neutral*, with some tightening at the margins since the year started. Mester: "monetary policy and financial conditions remain accommodative." Powell: "still-accommodative financial conditions." Brainard notes equity prices up 5% since May, but dollar appreciated and 10-year yield up 50bp since year-start; calls net effect "about a wash" over the intermeeting period, "some net tightening" since beginning of year. Bullard: near-neutral given global context. Kashkari: "we're only going to be modestly accommodative" after this hike. George: "financial conditions become less accommodative" — forward looking. The statement language says "accommodative." Score: 35 (loose to modestly loose territory).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a **hiking meeting** with a near-unanimous 25bp hike. Support: Williams, Rosengren, Harker, Barkin, Mester, Evans, Kaplan, Brainard, Quarles, George, Kashkari, Bostic, Dudley — all support Alt. B (25bp hike). Bullard alone supports Alt. A (hold/softer). Additional hawkish dimension: Rosengren thinks the SEP path is "somewhat too accommodative." Mester wants to go faster than SEP median. On the other side: Kaplan leans toward 3 hikes in 2018, not 4. Evans: 3 or 4. Bullard dissents. Most participants favor continuing the gradual hiking path, with majority expecting 3–4 hikes total in 2018. Score: 71. This is in the "strong hawkish: consensus for continued tightening; further increases needed" range. The one dissent (Bullard preferring to hold) pulls it slightly below "very strong" territory.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal voting dissent (Bullard prefers Alt. A over Alt. B), plus meaningful policy disagreement. Rosengren wants faster pace than SEP; Mester explicitly wants steeper path; Evans and Kaplan suggest 3 hikes not 4; Kashkari wants to pause at neutral; Bostic wants to avoid signaling too much precision. Also notable theoretical disagreement on yield curve inversion interpretation across many speakers. But the vote itself is 11-1 (Bullard dissenting). One dissent + notable reservations from several = moderate disagreement. Score: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Multiple participants discuss financial stability topics. Rosengren: extensive discussion of Deutsche Bank stress, Italian political risk, EME stress as tail risk scenarios. Brainard: yield curve, term premium, EME capital flow vulnerabilities, Italian political developments. Dudley: Argentine peso depreciation, Italian spread widening, EME discrimination. Kashkari: mentions Deutsche Bank, Italian risks. Powell: broad EME vulnerability, dollar strength, Italian risk. Mester: asset valuations "elevated," recession indicator model. Bullard: yield curve inversion discussion. Several participants discuss the yield curve as a financial stability/recession signal. This is a meaningful topic but not dominant — it competes equally with labor and inflation discussion. Score: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The statement removes old forward guidance language ("likely to remain below levels expected in longer run") — that language is explicitly described as having "grown stale." The new forward-guidance signal from the majority is: gradual further rate increases toward neutral and perhaps modestly above. Williams: "four rate increases this year and further increases in 2019." Dudley: "four 25bp hikes in 2018, three in 2019." Mester: will support further rate increases. However: Kaplan leans toward 3 hikes; Evans: 3 or 4 (gradual); Kashkari: pause at neutral; Bullard: hold now. Removal of dovish forward guidance + consensus for continued gradual hiking = tightening signal. Score: 75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants explicitly note uncertainty about: neutral rate estimates (r*), natural rate of unemployment, yield curve interpretation/causality, trade policy outcomes (very prominent), EME contagion risk, inflation dynamics (flat Phillips curve, whether inflation will pick up). Bullard, Williams, Barkin, Kashkari, Bostic all discuss yield curve/r* uncertainty at length. Rosengren raises "financial fragility" scenarios. Dudley raises trade/immigration policy effects as very uncertain. Brainard notes term premium uncertainty. Kaplan discusses fiscal drag timing uncertainty. Multiple participants note this is "unusual" uncertainty. Score: 45.

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (75): Both clearly in hawkish territory. 25bp hike today + signal for continued gradual hikes. Consistent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (52) relatively modest despite very tight labor market: Consistent with the explicit flat Phillips curve discussion — many participants note inflation hasn't accelerated despite tightness, which is why they feel less urgency for aggressive hikes.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "Turning to inflation, we're closing in on our 2 percent goal...I expect nominal wage growth and price inflation to pick up somewhat further, producing a modest overshooting of our 2 percent target by 2020."
- MS. MESTER: "year-over-year total PCE inflation at 2 percent, and core PCE inflation is 1.8 percent...I expect inflation to be sustainably at 2 percent by the end of this year."
- MS. BRAINARD: "core PCE prices rose 1.9 percent over the latest 12 months...after seven years of below-target inflation, it will be important to see inflation around target on a sustained basis."
- MR. BULLARD (dissenter): "Dallas Fed trimmed mean rate year over year, that's 1.7 percent today...the market is really not saying that we're going to see a lot of inflation pressure ahead."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants collectively see inflation as essentially at or just touching the 2% PCE target for the first time after years of undershooting, with modest expected overshoot ahead; the center is ~50-53, reflecting the "at target" milestone rather than concerning overheating.

**Minority views**: Bullard emphasizes 1.7% trimmed mean and market-implied below-target miss over next decade. Brainard and Evans worry about re-anchoring still incomplete after prolonged undershoot. These pull slightly dovish on inflation.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "I expect real GDP to increase 2.8 percent this year, well above the pace of potential, which I still put at 1.7 percent."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "my forecast has real GDP growing much faster than potential through the rest of the year."
- MS. MESTER: "The economy is already beyond maximum employment...above-trend real GDP growth will push it further beyond sustainable levels."
- CHAIRMAN POWELL: "information from the second quarter now points to real GDP growth with a 'three' handle or perhaps even higher."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The strong majority of participants describe the economy as clearly running above potential output, with GDP growth well above trend and fiscal stimulus providing further tailwind; this maps to the 59–70 "clearly above potential; running hot" range.

**Minority views**: Bullard argues the growth is temporary/fiscal-driven and the committee should not treat it as permanent. Quarles believes higher potential growth partially offsets the apparent gap. Bostic discounts some of the Q2 pickup as transitory. These warrant a modest uncertainty band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 73 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "Last month the unemployment rate dipped to 3.75 percent...That is obviously a historic low, not seen since the 1960s...labor markets are quite tight."
- MS. MESTER: "unemployment gap is larger than we saw at any point during the expansions of 1982 to 1990 and 2001 to 2007, and is nearly as large as we saw in the 1991–2001 expansion."
- MS. GEORGE: "unemployment rate, now at 3.8 percent, is well below my assessment of its longer-run level. Anecdotes given by business contacts lament labor shortages."
- MR. KASHKARI (dissenter): "we're probably not quite at full employment but getting closer...prime-age labor force participation still appears low."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-universal agreement that the labor market is very tight—unemployment well below any natural rate estimate, widespread reports of worker shortages, record job openings—placing this firmly in the 66-80 "very tight" range; Kashkari's dissent about residual slack creates modest upward uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Kashkari and Brainard both note prime-age participation below pre-crisis levels, suggesting potentially more slack than the headline rate implies. Quarles also believes prime-age participation can continue rising. These nuances pull the score slightly below the high-70s.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 60 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MESTER: "business fixed investment is currently estimated to have increased at a robust pace of 6 percent at an annual rate."
- MS. BRAINARD: "The latest data suggest consumer spending growth has bounced back after a soft first quarter...strong fundamentals should support that momentum."
- MR. BOSTIC: "I am not inclined to read too much into the recent acceleration in business investment...excluding energy and oil investment, investment growth is still below 5 percent on a year-over-year basis, a bit lower than the typical expansion average."
- MR. KAPLAN: "General Motors told us that they were delaying any major capital spending decision that they could" due to trade uncertainty.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending and business investment are broadly above trend and robust, consistent with fiscal stimulus and strong confidence; however, trade policy uncertainty is actively chilling forward cap-ex plans, creating a moderately wide uncertainty band and pulling the score below "clearly strong momentum" territory.

**Minority views**: Bostic discounts Q2 consumer spending strength (utility spike), and multiple participants (Evans, Bostic, Barkin, Kaplan) flag deferred or canceled investment due to trade uncertainty as a meaningful headwind.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MESTER: "monetary policy and financial conditions remain accommodative."
- CHAIRMAN POWELL: "still-accommodative financial conditions" among the positive fundamentals cited.
- MS. BRAINARD: "equity prices are up about 5 percent since the May meeting...the net effects of these forces is likely to be about a wash. But...some net tightening in conditions has occurred since the beginning of the year."
- MR. KASHKARI: "after this meeting we're only going to be modestly accommodative...I could argue we might not have much accommodation at all."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants describe financial conditions as still accommodative overall, despite some tightening (dollar up, 10-year yield up ~50bp YTD), placing this in the "somewhat loose to neutral" range; the consensus phrase "accommodative" and references to supportive equity markets anchor the score below 50.

**Minority views**: Brainard is most nuanced, arguing net tightening since January is non-trivial. Kashkari questions whether accommodation remains meaningful at all given proximity to neutral.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 71 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. ROSENGREN: "I support alternative B...the path forward...my sense is that, at present, we are headed for a policy rate path that is somewhat too accommodative...the primary risk...is that our baseline forecast expects an economy that is running 'hot' and, quite likely, 'too hot.'"
- MS. MESTER: "My policy rate path is somewhat steeper than the SEP median...there's a nonnegligible risk we could be falling further 'behind the curve.'"
- MR. BULLARD (dissenting): "I support alternative A for today...I think we're in some danger of coming off as too 'hawkish' at this meeting."
- VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: "I support alternative B as written...The case for continuing to remove monetary policy accommodation gradually, I think, remains very compelling."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Thirteen participants support a 25bp hike (Alternative B or stronger intent); the majority signal continued gradual tightening ahead toward and possibly beyond neutral; Bullard's single dissent (prefer to hold) prevents a score above 75, but the dominant tone is "strong hawkish: consensus for continued tightening, further increases needed."

**Minority views**: Bullard (Alt. A, hold), Kaplan (leans 3 hikes vs. 4), Evans (3 or 4 hikes; wants to ensure gradual approach), Kashkari (pause at neutral) form a soft dovish minority within the hiking consensus.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Bullard formally supports Alt. A rather than Alt. B — 1 formal dissent on direction of policy.
- MR. ROSENGREN: believes the SEP path is "somewhat too accommodative," implying he wants more than the median.
- MR. KASHKARI: "once we get to neutral, we should pause effectively" — differs from Mester/Rosengren on forward path.
- Multiple participants disagree about yield curve interpretation (causal vs. correlational), although this is a methodological, not voting, disagreement.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: One formal voting dissent (Bullard, Alt. A) plus meaningful policy-path disagreements (Rosengren vs. Kaplan/Evans on pace; Mester vs. Brainard on forward path) and extensive yield curve interpretive debate; this sits in the "moderate differences: notable differences about pace/risk; 1 dissent or 2–3 reservations" range.

**Minority views**: Despite the policy-path disagreements, the meeting is notably cohesive on the key vote: 13 to 1 for hiking. The dissent is over alt. A vs. B wording, not the direction.

**Minority views**: The financial stability discussion is clearly secondary to the labor/inflation/output assessment in most speakers' remarks; it is elevated above typical meetings but not the dominant topic.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 75 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. WILLIAMS: "my SEP projection is four funds rate increases this year and further increases in 2019." "I continue to expect a total of four rate increases for this year."
- VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: "we continue to have four 25 basis point rate hikes in 2018, three in 2019, and two in 2020."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The consensus forward signal is clear tightening continuation — hiking at this meeting and continuing gradual normalization toward and possibly above neutral — consistent with "clear tightening signal: further rate increases ahead"; the dovish end of the consensus (Evans, Kaplan) takes the score modestly below 80.

**Minority views**: Bullard would stop here; Kashkari would pause at neutral. These dissenting voices are present but outnumbered. The statement language change (removing "below long-run level" phrase) is itself a hawkish forward signal from a language perspective.

**Minority views**: Dudley explicitly elevates his uncertainty assessment; Kaplan flags asymmetric tools and lag uncertainty; Rosengren raises tail risks about Deutsche Bank and soft-landing difficulty. These pull toward the upper end of the range.
