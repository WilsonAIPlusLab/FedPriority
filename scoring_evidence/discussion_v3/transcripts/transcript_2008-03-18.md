# FOMC Transcript Discussion Scoring — March 18, 2008

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of individual participant statements from the FOMC go-around and policy discussion. Speakers are clearly identified (e.g., MR. HOENIG., MS. YELLEN., MR. LACKER.). The meeting covers both the economic go-around and the policy vote discussion. The transcript runs roughly pages 25–112 of the full meeting record, which is a long, substantive document with many participants speaking at length. Confidence will generally be High for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
March 18, 2008. This is mid-GFC — Bear Stearns has just collapsed/been rescued over the preceding weekend. The economy is entering recession (participants broadly agree this is the case). Housing is in severe contraction. Financial markets are under acute stress. Commodity prices (oil, food, agriculture) are surging. The dollar is weakening significantly. The FOMC is in an active easing cycle — at the prior meeting the rate was cut 50bp to 3.00%; today the decision will be a further 75bp cut to 2.25%. This is a genuinely crisis-adjacent meeting with sharp ideological disagreement about whether to ease aggressively or hold to preserve inflation credibility.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as ABOVE target. Nearly every speaker acknowledges elevated headline inflation driven by energy, food, and commodity prices. Core PCE also above 2% (Hoenig notes ~2.1% average over four years; Yellen raises her 2008 core PCE forecast to 2.25%; Plosser notes prices-paid indexes at highest levels since early 1980s; Fisher describes 5–15% food price pass-throughs; Lacker says overall CPI up over 4% year-over-year). At the same time, a majority (Yellen, Kohn, Rosengren, Mishkin, Evans, Kroszner) argue that recession-driven slack and commodity price moderation will bring inflation down, and they view inflation expectations as not yet unhinged — "reasonably well contained" (Mishkin) with only a "slight" rise (~10bp). A minority (Lacker, Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, Warsh) argue credibility is eroding and inflation risks are serious/persistent. The center of gravity is that inflation is clearly above target, driven heavily by global commodity shocks, with non-trivial but still-manageable expectation risk. Score directionally in the 63–68 range: clearly above target, with broadening commodity-driven pressures, but not yet "substantially above" or "highest in decades."

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as BELOW potential — most agree recession has started or is imminent. Rosengren: "we have entered a recession"; Lacker: "compelling evidence that a recession is in train"; Yellen: "economy is essentially in recession during the first half"; Bernanke: "I thought in January that we were in recession. That was my view at that time, and I certainly believe it now"; Mishkin: "the possibility that we could have a severe recession is uncomfortably high." The go-around is essentially unanimous that the economy is below potential. Debate is only about severity — mild 2001-like vs. more severe 1990-91-like. Score: clearly below potential, economy entering recession. Not yet at GFC trough levels (which would be 15–22 for 2009). For March 2008, near the beginning of the recession with risks of deepening: score around 35–38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see labor as LOOSENING rapidly but not yet at extremely high unemployment. Rosengren: "private payroll employment fell 101,000 in February"; unemployment expected to "peak somewhere between 5½ and 6 percent"; Lacker: "payroll employment peaked at the end of '07"; Bernanke summary: "Payroll employment growth has turned negative." The labor market is deteriorating from a previously near-full-employment position — unemployment was still below 5% recently (Kroszner notes "unemployment rate below 5 percent or 4¾ percent"). Labor is now moving below NAIRU but has not yet reached high unemployment. Score around 37–40: below full employment, slack emerging, job losses underway, but not yet deep recessionary slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see spending and investment as BELOW trend and deteriorating. Yellen: "spending has softened further"; "declines in sales that haven't been experienced for almost fifteen years"; capital spending cuts of "at least a third." Lockhart: "entered recession territory." Pianalto: "most manufacturers have seen a slowing in business activity"; "pessimism over economic prospects now prevalent." Evans: "Manpower's CEO told me that their business had deteriorated." Bernanke summary: "consumption growth has flattened out"; Greenbook revised personal consumption from 2.3% to zero. Fisher: UPS volumes down 2%. However, there is one partial offset — exports remain a source of strength (Yellen, Pianalto, Evans all mention export demand supporting growth). Evans notes "spending is not collapsing at this point." So it is clearly below trend but not yet in freefall. Score around 28–32: contracting/sharp pullback, with some cushion from exports.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see financial conditions as VERY TIGHT — acute crisis-level stress. Rosengren: "credit spreads have widened significantly over the past six weeks, with many spreads more than 50 basis points higher"; "potential for a further episode of financial market dysfunction and for runs on additional financial firms is significant." Yellen: "financial conditions have unambiguously tightened"; "significant credit crunch is under way." Kohn: "illiquid markets, extreme volatility, deleveraging, margin calls, forced sales, especially in mortgage-backed securities, wider spreads, equity prices falling." Warsh: "counterparty risk has become the dominant concern"; "broker-dealers with widening CDS spreads and falling share prices." Mishkin: "worst financial crisis that we've experienced in the post–World War II era." Bear Stearns rescue is happening in real time. Score: 82–87 — extremely tight, near crisis-level, systemic stress, credit effectively frozen for many classes of borrowers.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The policy decision is a 75bp cut (Alternative A). Majority support Alternative A (Bernanke proposes it; Yellen, Kohn, Evans, Stern, Pianalto, Warsh, Mishkin, Kroszner, Geithner all support it). Lockhart came in preferring 50bp but can support 75bp (soft support). Hoenig explicitly objects and will dissent — he prefers holding. Lacker prefers much less aggressive easing. Fisher will not vote for further cuts and essentially dissents or reluctantly concedes at most 25bp. Plosser prefers a pause with Alternative D language. So the center is strongly dovish — the majority is implementing one of the largest single-meeting cuts in the easing cycle (75bp), with only 2–3 hawks resisting. This is clearly in the "strong dovish" range: large-scale easing, majority favors 75bp cut, with a vocal minority (3 people) wanting much less. Score: 13–18 (strong dovish, not emergency level since there was debate about 50 vs. 75 rather than "whatever it takes").

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
There is significant disagreement at this meeting. Hoenig will formally dissent (prefers hold). Fisher explicitly states he cannot support 75bp and will not vote for further cuts (dissents). Lacker strongly advocates for much less aggressive easing. Plosser prefers a pause. Lockhart prefers 50bp but joins the majority. This meeting has at least 2 formal dissents (Hoenig and Fisher based on the text), with Lacker and Plosser expressing strong reservations but joining Alternative A or something close. The disagreement extends to fundamental framework questions — whether credibility is being lost, whether rate cuts are efficacious, whether the Fed should "disappoint markets." Score: 62–68 — significant division, clear camps, multiple dissents, sharp substantive exchanges (Fisher-Mishkin exchange about Japan; Lacker's credibility argument vs. Geithner's pushback).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability is an overwhelming dominant theme. Every single participant discusses financial conditions at length. Mishkin: "worst financial crisis in the post–World War II era." Warsh: extended discussion of counterparty risk, investment bank business model impairment, capital raising challenges. Kroszner: detailed discussion of credit cards, HELOCs, SIVs, mark-to-market issues. Kohn: detailed financial conditions discussion. The Bear Stearns collapse framing dominates the policy discussion. Multiple new facilities (TAF, TSLF, PDCF) are discussed. This rivals the description at any pre-2008 meeting. Score: 88–92 — financial stability dominates the meeting; it is the dominant topic rivaling or exceeding economic assessment in emphasis.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The statement language is Alternative A with 75bp cut. The forward guidance in alternative A signals: "further policy actions," risks to the downside, attention to inflation risks. Several participants (Rosengren: "even with this move, further easing will be necessary"; Mishkin: r* has dropped more than 75bp so more may be needed; Yellen: "as far as we can go sensibly today" but wants to move more; Evans: wants to know where "the bottom of the funds rate cycle is"). Majority lean toward further easing ahead. Hawks (Lacker, Hoenig, Plosser, Fisher) want to stop or slow. The net forward signal embedded in Alternative A language adds inflation attention language but also includes a downside risk sentence — it is a dovish forward lean (further cuts expected) tempered by inflation signals. Score: 20–25 (mild easing bias, "low for long" territory, further cuts expected by majority, but with more explicit inflation caveats than in prior meetings).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Uncertainty is extremely high throughout. Mishkin: "policy environment as complicated as I possibly could have imagined"; "worst financial crisis...in the post–World War II era." Kohn: "extraordinary uncertainty"; "really difficult to know how financial markets will evolve." Stern: "I don't know that it is easy to distinguish how this is likely to play out." Bernanke: "I just don't have much confidence about the timing of that." Warsh: "it is increasingly difficult to separate liquidity issues from solvency issues." Geithner: "the most challenging set of pressures facing the central bank in 20 or 30 years." Kroszner: "very uncertain situation." Multiple speakers note standard models may not capture the dynamics (Rosengren: "difficult to capture in statistical models based on historical data"). Score: 78–82 — very high uncertainty; most participants emphasize deep uncertainty; "uncharted territory" language present; wide scenario ranges discussed.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence shows elevated CPI/PCE, commodity surge, broadening cost pass-through. Score 65 → matches (above 50, more inflation).
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence shows below potential/recession. Score 37 → matches (below 50, below potential).
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter labor. Evidence shows deteriorating labor market, job losses beginning. Score 38 → matches (below 50, loosening).
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence shows contracting spending. Score 30 → matches (below 50, contracting).
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence shows extreme tightness/crisis. Score 85 → matches (well above 50, very tight).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence shows majority strongly dovish, 75bp cut. Score 15 → matches (well below 50, strong dovish).
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows 2+ dissents, sharp exchanges, fundamental framework disputes. Score 65 → matches (above 50, significant division).
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Higher = more attention to financial stability. Evidence shows dominant topic throughout. Score 90 → matches (very high, dominant topic).
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence shows further easing expected. Score 22 → matches (below 50, easing bias).
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows very high, pervasive uncertainty. Score 80 → matches (above 61, very high).

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38): Both below 50, roughly aligned — economy below potential, labor market loosening. Consistent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (15) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (22): Both well below 50, dovish. Consistent — current action is a large cut and forward signal is for more easing. The slight difference (15 vs. 22) reflects that the current action is even more dovish than the forward guidance signal, which now includes some inflation-language caveats.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (90) is very high, consistent with FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (85) — both reflect the extreme crisis conditions.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (65) vs. OUTPUT_GAP (37): These diverge, which is explained in the text — the stagflationary supply shock (commodity prices up, demand down) creates exactly this tension. Many speakers address this paradox explicitly (Mishkin calls it "double whammy"; Kroszner calls it "a bit of a paradox"). This divergence is expected and documented.

### 6. Speaker Count for Center of Gravity

**On policy (dovish vs. hawkish):**
- Strong dovish (support 75bp or more): Yellen, Mishkin, Kohn, Evans, Stern, Pianalto, Kroszner, Warsh, Geithner, Bernanke = ~10
- Moderate dovish (prefer 50bp but can accept 75bp): Lockhart = 1
- Hawkish/resistant (oppose 75bp, want hold or 25bp): Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Plosser = 4
Center of gravity: strongly dovish.

**On inflation:**
- Manageable/will fall: Rosengren, Yellen, Mishkin, Kohn, Evans, Kroszner, Geithner = ~7
- Serious/credibility at risk: Lacker, Plosser, Hoenig, Fisher, Warsh, Lockhart, Pianalto (partial) = ~7
Center of gravity: inflation is elevated and concerning, but majority lean toward manageable.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 65 ±8 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Hoenig: "core PCE inflation has averaged about 2.1 percent, considerably above the numbers that this Committee has put forward in its long-term projections"; "rapidly rising commodity prices worldwide and a weaker dollar" as upside risks.
- Lacker: "overall CPI is still up over 4 percent year over year"; "substantial rise in the five-year, five-year-ahead inflation compensation"; "inflation expectations no longer qualify as well anchored."
- Plosser: "prices-paid and prices-received indexes over the past three months are at very high levels relative to the last twenty years"; "future-prices-paid index is at its highest level since the early 1980s."
- Yellen (moderating view): raised core PCE forecast "about ¼ percent in 2008, to 2¼ percent" but "prospects for core inflation this year have worsened a bit."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation is clearly above target, driven heavily by commodity, energy, and food price pass-throughs, with broadening cost-plus pricing behavior and some upward drift in inflation expectations. The majority view is that it will moderate as slack builds and commodities level off, but it is not dismissed.

**Minority views**: Lacker and Plosser argue the situation is more alarming — credibility at risk, expectations unmoored. This pushes the upper uncertainty band toward 73.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 37 ±6 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "we have entered a recession"; "risk of a more severe downturn is uncomfortably high."
- Bernanke: "I thought in January that we were in recession. That was my view at that time, and I certainly believe it now."
- Yellen: "economy is essentially in recession during the first half"; "financial market implosion...have now spread to most corners of the economy."
- Stern: recession likely to "resemble that of 1990-91, which, while brief, wasn't that mild."

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous assessment that recession has begun or is imminent. The economy is clearly below potential. The main uncertainty is whether it is mild (2001-like, Greenbook baseline) or more severe (1990-91-like or worse, with tail risk of something deeper). Score of 37 reflects below-potential but not yet catastrophic (2009 GFC trough was 15–22).

**Minority views**: Fisher and Hoenig have "one of the most pessimistic forecasts" on growth but do not push for a deeper-recession score than others.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 38 ±6 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "private payroll employment fell 101,000 in February"; "unemployment rate peaking somewhere between 5½ and 6 percent."
- Lacker: "payroll employment peaked at the end of '07"; "expecting a period of declining employment."
- Bernanke summary: "Payroll employment growth has turned negative."
- Kroszner: "unemployment rate below 5 percent or 4¾ percent" recently; noting uncertainty about whether loosening labor will dampen wages.

**Center of gravity**: Labor market is deteriorating — from recent near-full-employment, payrolls are now contracting. Unemployment rising toward 5.5–6%. Below full employment but not yet severely depressed (2009 level, which was 15–22). Score of 38 reflects clear slack emerging, consistent with a recession onset.

**Minority views**: None material — near-consensus on labor deterioration.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 30 ±7 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "CEO of a large high-end national retail operation reports that for January and February he has seen declines in sales that haven't been experienced for almost fifteen years"; nonresidential construction "financing spigot has been shut."
- Lacker: "expecting a period of declining employment, declining nonresidential construction, and flat-to-sluggish consumption. Equipment and software spending may decline as well."
- Plosser: Greenbook revised personal consumption from 2.3% to zero; "significantly weaker than I anticipated."
- Evans (partial offset): "spending is not collapsing at this point" and "exports of capital equipment in the agricultural sector continue to do well."

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending has sharply slowed to near-zero or slight contraction; business investment is pulling back across the board (CapEx cuts, commercial real estate financing gone, nonresidential construction falling); only exports provide partial offset. This is in "contracting/sharp pullback" territory. Not yet freefall (15–20) since exports and some residual spending persist.

**Minority views**: Evans provides modest offsetting view that spending is not collapsing. Upper band accordingly extends to 37.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 85 ±7 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "credit spreads have widened significantly over the past six weeks, with many spreads more than 50 basis points higher"; "potential for a further episode of financial market dysfunction and for runs on additional financial firms is significant."
- Mishkin: "worst financial crisis that we've experienced in the post–World War II era"; "credit markets have been deteriorating."
- Kohn: "illiquid markets, extreme volatility, deleveraging, margin calls, forced sales...wider spreads, equity prices falling, and lending and funding tenors collapsing toward the overnight."
- Warsh: "counterparty risk has become the dominant concern in markets...increasingly difficult to separate liquidity issues from solvency issues."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are extremely tight across all dimensions — credit spreads far above normal, counterparty risk acute, major institutions under existential stress (Bear Stearns collapse ongoing), credit channels closing down. This is a systemic stress event. The 85 score reflects conditions slightly below the absolute peak of September 2008 (credit freeze/Lehman, 90–95), since interbank credit has not yet fully frozen.

**Minority views**: Geithner cautions that average losses relative to capital are "relatively manageable" and resists characterizing the whole system as undercapitalized — this tempers the upper band slightly.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 14 ±8 | High confidence**

**Evidence:**
- Bernanke: "I would recommend the 75 basis point reduction."
- Yellen: "I support your proposal for a 75 basis point cut...I don't believe in gradualism in circumstances like these."
- Mishkin: supports Alternative A; argues r* has dropped 125–150bp and 75bp is actually insufficient insurance.
- Hoenig (dissent): "I think this is a mistake...I would much prefer to hold at this period."
- Fisher (dissent): "75 basis points, Mr. Chairman, is way too much"; "I'm not going to vote for further cuts."
- Lacker: "we need to adopt a strategy of easing much less aggressively going forward."

**Center of gravity**: Clear majority implements 75bp cut — among the largest single-meeting rate cuts in this cycle. Approximately 10–11 participants support the 75bp action with only 2–3 formal dissents (Hoenig, Fisher at minimum; Lacker and Plosser voice strong objections). This is solidly in the "strong dovish" range (11–25). Score of 14 reflects the large majority executing a very large cut, tempered from the emergency level (1–10) by the fact that a substantial minority is vocally resisting further easing.

**Minority views**: Hoenig, Fisher, Lacker, Plosser form a hawkish bloc (4 out of ~16) arguing for hold or minimal cut. This widens the uncertainty band.

- Fisher: explicit resistance — "75 basis points, Mr. Chairman, is way too much...I'm not going to vote for further cuts."

**Center of gravity**: This meeting has 2 formal voting dissents (Hoenig and Fisher) plus 2–3 strong reservations expressed explicitly (Lacker, Plosser, Lockhart preferred 50bp). The disagreement extends beyond tactics to fundamental framework questions (credibility, strategy, efficacy of rate cuts). This meets the 61–75 range (significant division, clear camps, multiple dissents, sharp exchanges).
