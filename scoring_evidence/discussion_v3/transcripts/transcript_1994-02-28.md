# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 1994-02-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an intermeeting telephone conference call on February 28, 1994. Individual speakers are identified. The discussion is relatively short and focused primarily on policy, with limited economic go-around. Speakers include Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, and Presidents Broaddus, Jordan, Parry, Hoenig, and Melzer. The latter portion covers G-7 meeting recap and banking regulation -- not scoreable economic/policy content.

### Era Context
February 1994: The Fed had just raised rates 25bp at the February 4 meeting (the first tightening since 1989). The economy was emerging from a slow recovery, with growth accelerating. Real short-term rates were near zero. Long-term rates were rising globally. This was the beginning of the 1994 tightening cycle.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants do NOT discuss current inflation readings at length. The discussion centers on whether rising long rates reflect real rates or inflation expectations. Greenspan argues "more real than inflation expectations" based on global co-movement of rates. Parry worries "inflationary prospects longer term are not as positive." Broaddus mentions real rates at zero pose "unacceptable risks that would generate future problems." The discussion is forward-looking about inflation RISK rather than current inflation pressure. Current inflation is low; the concern is that accommodation could generate future inflation. Direction: slightly below neutral -- inflation not yet a problem but risks are tilting up. Score around 42-45.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says current inflation subdued but risks rising. 43 fits -- slightly below midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Greenspan notes growth expectations are being revised upward: "average expectation for the first quarter clearly has increased." Fourth quarter GDP was "quite a good deal higher than originally expected." Jordan says "overall they'll see 1994 as stronger than they previously projected." The economy is growing above expectations but participants frame the funds rate as "still close to zero" in real terms, and policy as "too accommodative" -- implying the economy hasn't yet overheated but is moving toward potential. Direction: approaching or modestly above potential. Score around 52-55.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says growth revised up, economy strengthening, but no "overheating" language. 53 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very little direct discussion. Greenspan notes "measured labor input soft" and Phillips mentions unemployment insurance claims due to weather. No direct discussion of labor market tightness. Direction: near neutral, limited evidence. Score around 46-48 (labor input described as "soft" suggests slightly below full employment).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Soft" labor input suggests not tight. 47 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: No direct discussion of consumer spending or business investment components. The discussion is about aggregate GDP growth being revised upward. Direction: above trend given strong GDP revisions. Score around 55-58 based on GDP momentum.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Upward growth revisions support above-trend reading. 56 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Significant discussion. Greenspan: "we partially broke the back of an emerging speculation in equities." Long-term rates have risen substantially. McDonough: "markets overreacted somewhat," "very nervous markets," "very significant losses" in exchange and fixed-income markets. Greenspan notes "we may be imparting...more restraint to this recovery than I think we would have anticipated." The prior 25bp move has had outsized tightening effects through markets. Direction: conditions have tightened notably from the rate action. Score around 55-60.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Markets nervous, long rates up, losses in multiple asset classes, more restraint than expected. 57 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Clear hawkish lean. Broaddus: "current stance of policy is too accommodative...real rates are...still close to zero. And that's a problem." Jordan: "I wish we had gone ahead and done the full 1/2 point...I would like us to go ahead and do the other 1/4 point now." Melzer: "we're out of position and the sooner we get to...a neutral stance the better." Greenspan: "we're going to have to move again, obviously." McDonough and Parry agree further tightening likely but prefer waiting for the March meeting. NO participant argues against further tightening. The debate is purely about timing (now vs. 3 weeks). Direction: solidly hawkish. Score around 68-72.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous expectation of further tightening, debate only about timing. 70 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Jordan explicitly argues for immediate action ("I would like us to go ahead and do the other 1/4 point now"). Greenspan, McDonough, and Parry prefer waiting for March. Broaddus is "perfectly happy to wait" but could justify immediate action. The disagreement is about TIMING (3 weeks), not direction. All agree further tightening is needed. Direction: moderate differences about pace. Score around 35-40.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Timing disagreement only, unified on direction. 37 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Significant discussion of market reactions. Greenspan: "partially broke the back of an emerging speculation in equities," "pricked that bubble," "markets...clearly have been shocked." McDonough: "very significant losses," "very nervous markets." Hoenig asks about market self-correction. Greenspan discusses global long-rate co-movement. Financial stability/market functioning is a prominent topic. Direction: moderate-to-high emphasis. Score around 45-50 for 1994 context.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Multiple speakers discuss market reactions extensively. 48 fits.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan: "we're going to have to move again, obviously." Broaddus: "unless there's a big change...we will need to take a close look and move another notch." Melzer: "sooner we get to...a neutral stance the better." Clear forward signal of further tightening. Direction: hawkish forward signal. Score around 70-75.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Explicit statements about needing to tighten further. 72 fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Greenspan: "it's still too soon to know whether or not that's true," "the truth of the matter is that I don't think we can know at this particular point." Parry worries about decomposing long-rate moves. Melzer: "there will be distortions to the data." McDonough wants "additional information." Several participants express genuine uncertainty about what's driving rates and the outlook. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score around 38-42.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Genuine uncertainty about rate decomposition and data distortions. 40 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (47): Slight divergence. Justified because GDP growth is strong but labor input described as "soft" -- productivity gains bridging the gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Aligned. Both solidly hawkish.

### Speaker Count
Approximately 7 participants speak substantively (Greenspan, McDonough, Broaddus, Jordan, Parry, Hoenig, Melzer, Phillips). This is a telephone conference with fewer speakers than a regular meeting.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would be inclined to think it's more likely a real increase [in long-term rates]...it's hard to argue that inflation expectations are really beginning to pick up in Europe."
- MR. BROADDUS: "short-term real rates at zero posed...unacceptable risks that would generate future problems"
- MR. PARRY: "inflationary prospects longer term are not as positive as they were perhaps a month or several months ago"

**Center of gravity**: Current inflation is not elevated, but participants see forward risks from accommodative policy. The debate about whether long-rate rises reflect real rates or inflation expectations leans toward real rates per Greenspan.

**Minority views**: Parry more concerned about inflation expectations component; Greenspan and others see it as mostly real rates.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the average expectation for the first quarter clearly has increased" and "the revised number for the fourth quarter of 1993 is quite a good deal higher than originally expected"
- MR. JORDAN: "overall they'll see 1994 as stronger than they previously projected just a few months ago"

**Center of gravity**: Economy is growing above expectations with upward revisions, suggesting modestly above potential. No "overheating" language but clear above-trend momentum.

**Minority views**: None explicitly below-potential.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 47 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "at least the measured labor input soft"
- MS. PHILLIPS: mentions unemployment insurance claims related to weather effects

**Center of gravity**: Very limited discussion. The one direct reference ("soft" labor input) suggests labor market not yet tight. Weather distortions add noise.

**Minority views**: None expressed.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +/-8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the average expectation for the first quarter clearly has increased"; upward revisions to Q4 and Q1 GDP
- MR. JORDAN: "1994 as stronger than they previously projected"

**Center of gravity**: No direct discussion of consumption or investment components. Inferred from strong and upwardly revised GDP growth. Above-trend aggregate activity.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we probably have imparted or have been associated with a larger degree of restraint than we had anticipated"; "markets...clearly have been shocked"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "very significant losses--not just that by Mr. Soros and company in the exchange and fixed-income markets"; "very nervous markets"
- MR. HOENIG: asks about "uncertainty of this other shoe hanging over the market"

**Center of gravity**: The 25bp move had outsized effects on financial conditions. Long rates rose sharply, equity speculation was curbed, and market losses were significant. Conditions meaningfully tighter than before the move.

**Minority views**: None disagree that conditions tightened.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we're going to have to move again, obviously"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the current stance of policy is too accommodative...real rates are...still close to zero. And that's a problem"
- MR. JORDAN: "I wish we had gone ahead and done the full 1/2 point at the last meeting. I would like us to go ahead and do the other 1/4 point now"
- MR. MELZER: "we're out of position and the sooner we get to what we perceive to be a neutral stance the better"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: agrees further tightening likely, prefers waiting for March

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous agreement that further tightening is needed. Multiple participants characterize current stance as too accommodative. Debate is only about timing (now vs. 3 weeks), not direction. Jordan advocates immediate action.

**Minority views**: Jordan (and arguably Broaddus) lean toward immediate intermeeting action; majority prefers waiting for scheduled March meeting. But ALL favor further tightening.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 37 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "I would like us to go ahead and do the other 1/4 point now and not leave it until the next meeting"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I think it would be very much more advisable for us to wait"
- MR. BROADDUS: "I'm perfectly happy to wait until the March meeting"

**Center of gravity**: Disagreement is about WHEN to act (today vs. 3 weeks), not WHETHER. Jordan pushes for immediate action while Greenspan, McDonough, Parry, and others prefer the March meeting. This is a moderate tactical disagreement, not a fundamental policy split.

**Minority views**: Jordan is the clear outlier favoring immediate action.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we partially broke the back of an emerging speculation in equities"; "We pricked that bubble as well"; "we also have created a degree of uncertainty"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "very significant losses"; "very nervous markets"; "markets are oversold"
- MR. HOENIG: asks whether "markets...will self-correct between now and the next meeting"

**Center of gravity**: Financial market behavior is a central topic of this call. Greenspan discusses speculation, bubble-pricking, and market shock. McDonough discusses losses and nervousness. Market stability concerns are a meaningful factor in the decision to wait rather than act immediately.

**Minority views**: None -- all acknowledge significant market impact.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we're going to have to move again, obviously. I don't know when that will be"
- MR. BROADDUS: "unless there's a big change in the economic and financial climate by then, I believe we will need to take a close look and move another notch"
- MR. MELZER: "we're out of position and the sooner we get to what we perceive to be a neutral stance the better"

**Center of gravity**: Very clear forward signal that further tightening is coming. The only question is timing. Greenspan explicitly says they will "have to move again." This is a strong hawkish forward lean.

**Minority views**: McDonough notes the next move "could be a change of policy or not" -- a slight hedge -- but even he indicates tightening is likely.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it's still too soon to know whether or not that's true"; "the truth of the matter is that I don't think we can know at this particular point"
- MR. MELZER: "there will be distortions to the data"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: wants "additional information that we could get on the economy in the next few weeks"

**Center of gravity**: Participants express genuine uncertainty about what's driving long-rate movements and how weather/earthquake are distorting data. This motivates the "wait and see" approach. Uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not dramatically so -- participants are confident about the direction of policy, uncertain about specifics of timing and data interpretation.

**Minority views**: Jordan shows less uncertainty, wanting immediate action regardless.
