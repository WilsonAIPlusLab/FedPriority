# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2000-05-16 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions from the go-around and policy discussion. Speakers include Broaddus, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Guynn, Poole, Stern, McDonough, Gramlich, Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 16 participants speak.

### Era Context
May 2000: Late stages of the dot-com boom. The economy has been growing well above trend for several years, labor markets are historically tight, and the Fed has been in a tightening cycle since mid-1999. The funds rate is at 6% coming into this meeting. Inflation had been quiescent but is beginning to show signs of picking up. This is the meeting where the FOMC moved 50bp (up from 25bp increments).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the comfortable zone and rising, though not yet at alarming levels. Broaddus: "at least some evidence that we are experiencing some increase in actual inflation," core CPI at 2.2-2.3%. Minehan: "the expected pickup in inflation seems to have begun in earnest." Parry: "the news on inflation itself has been worrisome," core PCE "rose by an average of 1.9 percent in the past two quarters versus 1.3 percent." Guynn: "the inflation outlook has clearly deteriorated." Meyer: "we are seeing, I believe, the beginning of a pickup in inflation rates." Ferguson provides a counterweight: "all is not lost on the inflation front." Greenspan: "there is no evidence at this stage that we are experiencing a real underlying deterioration in inflation of the kind that we typically have seen in the past." The center of gravity is that inflation is modestly above comfortable levels and rising -- concerning but not yet out of control. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" to "clearly above" -- approximately 60-65. The evidence is building but Greenspan and Ferguson temper with more nuanced views. Score: 62.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is rising, becoming more worrisome. Score 62 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly every participant describes the economy as running above potential. McDonough: "reducing the imbalance between the unsustainable growth in demand and the capacity...to produce goods and services is essential." Meyer: "a large imbalance between the levels of actual and potential output." Greenspan: "overall demand forces are becoming very strong." Multiple districts describe "booming" conditions. Gramlich: "aggregate demand and aggregate supply are just not balanced. There is still tremendous momentum on the up side." The language is clearly "above potential" and "unsustainable." Bright-line rule: must be > 50. The language is strong -- "unsustainable," "imbalance," demand outpacing supply. Score: 65.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly above potential. Score 65 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is one of the most prominently discussed topics. Every single district president mentions extreme labor market tightness. Broaddus: "labor markets remain exceptionally tight." Minehan: "near impossibility of finding labor." Moskow: record hiring intentions in Manpower survey, 1.9% unemployment in Columbus IN. Guynn: "extremely tight," raffle for a car, $250K signing bonus. Poole: UPS "struggling," adding incentives. Jordan: "no unemployed construction workers," hospitals shutting operating rooms due to nurse shortages. McTeer: "Labor shortages are still the most common anecdote." Ferguson: "capacity utilization...reached its highest reading in two years." This is clearly in the 65-80 range given the "severe shortages" and "unsustainable" language. The era anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score: 73.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence uniformly says extremely tight. Score 73 matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is described as very strong though with scattered hints of possible moderation. Moskow: "consumption sector remained red-hot through the first quarter." McDonough: "demand in the economy is simply not sustainable." Parry: "real spending--as measured by real final sales--actually picked up." Guynn: "Consumer spending not only continues to be strong," "Investment spending bounced back markedly." Some early signs of cooling: Broaddus notes "a few signs of a possible moderation in consumer spending." McTeer: housing slowing in some areas. Greenspan: housing starts slowing marginally, motor vehicle sales coming off peaks. The center of gravity is well above trend but with tentative early signs of moderation. Score: 66.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says very strong. Score 66 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants note financial conditions are beginning to tighten. Ferguson: "corporate bond rates have increased noticeably since the start of the year," "issuance of corporate debt has also been cut back," banks tightening. Greenspan: "Real long-term BBB rates are up over 50 basis points," business borrowers moving from long to short end. Stern: "construction financing is getting harder to find." McDonough: banks "tightening in credit standards." Stock market volatility is noted. Poole: "NASDAQ has been more volatile this year than in the entire history of the index." These conditions are moderately tight and tightening further. Score: 58.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions have tightened notably. Score 58 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The vast majority supports the Chairman's recommendation of 50bp with asymmetric (inflation-tilted) risk statement. Only Hoenig explicitly preferred 25bp. McTeer "would have preferred 1/4 point" but "can also agree to 1/2." Jordan supports 50bp but would have preferred balanced risk language. Everyone else enthusiastically supports 50bp + asymmetric toward inflation. This is a clear hawkish consensus for acceleration of tightening. The 50bp move itself is a step-up from the 25bp gradualism. Multiple members say more tightening is needed. Score: 72.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. A 50bp hike with continued tightening bias is clearly hawkish. Score 72 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Despite the strong consensus for 50bp, there is meaningful variation. Hoenig is clearly a dissenter favoring 25bp. McTeer reluctantly agrees to 50bp. Jordan wants balanced risk language. Some like Gramlich and Meyer arrive at 50bp from a more alarmed view of inflation (Meyer: "our challenge is much more demanding than slowing the expansion to trend"). Stern expresses uncertainty about inflation. Greenspan himself is more sanguine about inflation than most. So there's broad agreement on the action but notable differences in reasoning and forward outlook. Score: 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions receive moderate attention. Stern discusses stock market significance. Poole discusses NASDAQ volatility and vulnerability. Ferguson discusses corporate bond markets and credit tightening. McDonough raises international financial stability concerns (emerging markets). Greenspan discusses real long-term rates. However, financial stability per se is not the dominant frame -- the meeting is primarily about inflation and demand/supply imbalance. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. The asymmetric risk statement tilted toward inflation signals more tightening ahead. Poole: "I don't think we want to give the impression that we see the end of this process." Moskow: "I personally think we are going to have to do more." Meyer: "the threat of inflation would become more of a reality" if they don't continue. Jordan provides counterweight: warns about the need to stop "long before the commentators...say we will." Greenspan is cautious about the future but still recommends asymmetric. Score: 73.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence clearly points to further tightening. Score 73 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is moderate uncertainty. Stern: "I continue to be uncertain, perhaps even more uncertain than usual" about inflation. Jordan raises questions about how far tightening needs to go and when to stop. Greenspan: "it is too soon to make that judgment." Ferguson: "I will let time tell on that one." But overall, most participants seem relatively confident in their assessment that inflation risks have increased and action is needed. The uncertainty is more about how far to go than about the current diagnosis. Score: 40.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (65) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (73): Both well above 50, consistent with an overheating economy. Labor tightness is scored higher because the labor shortage language is more extreme than the general output gap language. This makes sense -- labor markets are the tightest input.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (73): Well aligned. Both reflect a strong hawkish stance with continued tightening expected.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "it seems to me that there is at least some evidence that we are experiencing some increase in actual inflation... the 12-month change in the core CPI was 2.2 percent... approaching a rate that is 1/2 point higher than it was just a few months ago"
- MR. PARRY: "the news on inflation itself has been worrisome. With inflation coming in on the high side in the first quarter, we now have had two straight quarters with inflation rates that were above the previously established level"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN (counterweight): "there is no evidence at this stage that we are experiencing a real underlying deterioration in inflation of the kind that we typically have seen in the past"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly see inflation as modestly above target and trending upward, with rising compensation costs as a key driver, though Greenspan and Ferguson argue the situation is not yet dire.

**Minority views**: Ferguson and Greenspan see less inflation urgency than the majority, while Guynn and Meyer see the situation as more alarming. This informs the +/-7 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "reducing the imbalance between the unsustainable growth in demand and the capacity...to produce goods and services is essential"
- MR. MEYER: "another critical and persisting element is a large imbalance between the levels of actual and potential output"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "aggregate demand and aggregate supply are just not balanced. There is still tremendous momentum on the up side"

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-universal agreement that the economy is operating above potential with demand growing faster than supply can sustain.

**Minority views**: Some note incipient signs of slowing (McTeer, Hoenig), but even they acknowledge the economy remains above potential. Narrow uncertainty band reflects strong consensus.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 73 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "it is just not possible to have a conversation about local economic conditions anywhere in the District without discussing the near impossibility of finding labor"
- MR. JORDAN: "A Columbus hospital said that it has shut down three operating rooms in the face of a 100-nurse shortage"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Manpower's latest survey of hiring intentions set a new record: More firms than ever before in the survey's 24-year history indicated that they plan to increase employment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Virtually every participant describes labor markets as extremely tight with severe shortages, creative compensation schemes, and constraints on business expansion.

**Minority views**: None dissent from the tight labor market assessment. Narrow band reflects unanimity.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 66 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "consumption sector remained red-hot through the first quarter"
- MR. GUYNN: "Consumer spending not only continues to be strong... Investment spending bounced back markedly in the first quarter"
- MR. PARRY: "real spending--as measured by real final sales--actually picked up from the very strong fourth-quarter results"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment remain well above trend, though scattered early signs of moderation in housing and autos are emerging.

**Minority views**: McTeer, Hoenig, and Jordan note more signs of slowing in their districts, while most others emphasize continued strength. Band of +/-7 reflects this range.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FERGUSON: "corporate bond rates have increased noticeably since the start of the year... issuance of corporate debt has also been cut back... conditions on both loan rates and terms or availability do seem to be tightening"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Real long-term BBB rates are up over 50 basis points... a very large number of business firms are moving out of the long end of the borrowing market to the short end"
- MR. STERN: "construction financing is getting harder to find for both residential and commercial construction"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened moderately from prior loose levels, with higher corporate bond rates, reduced issuance, bank credit tightening, and stock market volatility -- but not yet at restrictive/stress levels.

**Minority views**: Poole emphasizes stock market vulnerability more; Greenspan sees conditions as functioning normally to restrain demand. Band reflects moderate range of emphasis.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "my own judgment...is that we have an opportunity now to move the funds rate up 50 basis points, remain asymmetric"
- MR. MEYER: "with a more aggressive policy action of 50 basis points today the risk of a policy mistake is much smaller than with a more gradual policy response"
- MR. HOENIG (minority): "everything you said convinced me that a 1/4 point seems right... the gradualist approach with quarter-point increases is still appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: Overwhelming support for 50bp hike, stepping up from prior 25bp pace, with continued inflation-tilted risk statement signaling more tightening to come.

**Minority views**: Hoenig preferred 25bp; McTeer reluctantly agreed to 50bp; Jordan wanted balanced risk language. These soften the band slightly.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "a 1/4 point seems right... the gradualist approach with quarter-point increases is still appropriate"
- MR. MCTEER: "While I would have preferred 1/4 point, I can also agree to 1/2"
- MR. JORDAN: "My preference would be to say that with such a move the risks are balanced"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the 50bp action with only minor tactical differences -- Hoenig as a clear dissenter, McTeer reluctantly on board, and Jordan wanting different communication. No fundamental framework disagreements.

**Minority views**: The disagreements are about pace and communication, not direction. This is a typical one-dissent meeting with a couple of reluctant supporters.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. POOLE: "the stock market is still very elevated by historical standards and the volatility in the market is really unprecedented. The NASDAQ has been more volatile this year than in the entire history of the index"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: extensive discussion of international financial stability risks in emerging markets
- MR. STERN: "there is a chance that we may be seeing something significant going on in the stock market"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives limited-to-moderate attention -- several participants mention stock market vulnerability and international risks, but the meeting is primarily framed around inflation and demand/supply balance, not financial stability per se.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 73 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. POOLE: "I don't think we want to give the impression that we see the end of this process"
- MR. MOSKOW: "I personally think we are going to have to do more"
- MR. MEYER: "until this process is much further along, there's a threat of continued upward pressure on inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- asymmetric risk statement tilted toward inflation, most participants expect further tightening, and no one (except possibly Jordan) sees the end of the tightening cycle.

**Minority views**: Jordan warns they're "closer to that point" of stopping than markets expect; Stern urges open-mindedness about next steps. These moderate the forward signal slightly.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "I continue to be uncertain, perhaps even more uncertain than usual" about inflation; "we can't find evidence since about 1984 that labor market variables make a significant contribution to forecasting inflation"
- MR. JORDAN: "I don't know if we have 'sweaty palms'... the question of when to pause cannot be avoided"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think it is too soon to make that judgment" about whether demand will slow sufficiently

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate uncertainty -- participants are fairly confident about the current diagnosis (strong economy, rising inflation risk) but express genuine uncertainty about how far tightening must go, whether inflation will actually accelerate, and when to stop.
