# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-07-01 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually identified speakers from the June 30 - July 1, 1998 FOMC meeting. It includes a full go-around of regional and national economic assessments plus a policy discussion on Day 2. Speakers include: Minehan, Parry, Jordan, Boehne, Broaddus, Moskow, Guynn, Stern, Hoenig, McDonough, Poole, Meyer, Rivlin, Gramlich, Kelley, Ferguson, McTeer, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a large, well-attended meeting with extensive discussion.

### Era Context
Mid-1998: The U.S. economy is in the late stages of a long expansion. Unemployment is around 4.3%, well below most estimates of NAIRU. The Asian financial crisis is deepening (Japan, Russia, emerging markets). The economy is experiencing a "two-track" dynamic: strong domestic demand vs. weakening international sector. Productivity is accelerating. The Fed has held rates steady for some time with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly picking up but still contained. Minehan: "core CPI picking up, as it clearly has in the first months of 1998." Meyer: "rebound in the underlying rate of core inflation from near 2 percent to about 2-1/2 percent." Parry: "core CPI staying between 2 and 2-1/2 percent." Greenspan: "Inflation probably is rising, but not at a rate that one would consider to be a matter of considerable concern." Core CPI is running around 2.5-2.7% annualized over the first 5 months. Multiple participants see upside inflation risks from tight labor markets, but actual inflation remains modest. Many note favorable temporary factors (Asia, dollar, commodity prices) holding inflation down. Direction: modestly above neutral but not alarming. Score should be in the 51-58 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly operating above potential. Minehan explicitly: "our economy is operating at levels beyond its potential, even though trend productivity may have increased." Growth has been above trend. Multiple participants see domestic demand as unsustainably strong. Broaddus: "the growth of final demand... well above trend." Rivlin: "The pace of growth last year and the acceleration in the first quarter of this year were clearly unsustainable." However, a slowdown is expected/beginning. Direction: above potential. The anchor for late 90s boom is 57-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Virtually every participant describes labor markets as very tight. Twin Cities at 1.7% unemployment; national rate 4.3%. Multiple anecdotes: prison work-release programs, BMWs for employees, 4,000 openings at Fidelity, construction workers being bused in. Stern: "unemployment rate in the Twin Cities metropolitan area has now dropped to 1.7 percent." Ferguson: "unemployment rate averaged about 4.5 percent, which is probably below anyone's reasonable estimate of the NAIRU." Wages picking up but not explosively. Direction: very tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is strong, business investment is robust. Rivlin: "Growth has been very strong and well balanced." Multiple participants describe consumer spending as pacing the economy. Construction spending at records. Capital investment strong. Guynn: "consumer spending continues to pace the domestic economy." Some signs of slowing but underlying strength persists. Direction: above trend, robust. Score in 60-68 range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described as accommodative/loose. Kelley: "credit is readily available on reasonable terms." Jordan: bank loan demand exceeding budgets. Broaddus: "extraordinary amount of money that is looking for a home... credit standards may be slipping." Greenspan: "continued supportive financial market conditions." Minehan: "credit continues to be available, though spreads have widened a bit, particularly for anything involving Asia." Some widening in spreads related to Asia, but overall conditions are loose. Direction: loose/accommodative. Score in 25-35 range.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The decision is to hold rates steady with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Many participants explicitly say they would prefer to tighten but international risks prevent it. Broaddus: "I still think that an increase now in the funds rate would be prudent." Minehan: "reluctantly because I think our policy may well be behind the curve." Poole: "it would be best if we were to raise the funds rate now." Jordan argues for tightening. McTeer is the sole voice for removing the tightening bias. The center of gravity is hold-with-hawkish-bias, with several wanting immediate action. Direction: lean hawkish, held back by international concerns. Score around 55-60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents on the vote. McTeer alone favors removing the tightening bias. Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, and Jordan express views favoring tightening now but accept the hold. There is broad agreement on the hold despite differing underlying views. The range of views is moderate -- most agree on the diagnosis but differ on timing. Score around 30-40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: International financial stability is a major theme. McDonough discusses the potential for a wider world financial crisis at length. Greenspan: "The danger of a wider world financial crisis, while still very small, has risen beyond the point of being de minimis." Asia, Russia, Latin America extensively discussed. Broaddus mentions concerns about credit standards slipping. Jordan worries about "imbalances in some of our markets--assets markets in particular and especially real estate." This is a prominent topic but not the dominant one. Score around 55-65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric tilt toward tightening is maintained. Multiple participants expect they will need to tighten this year. Moskow: "we are likely to have to tighten this year." Broaddus and Gramlich suggest being ready to take advantage of "windows of opportunity" for tightening. But international uncertainty creates caution. Direction: mild tightening bias with data dependence. Score around 58-65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is a dominant theme. Boehne: "a lot of uncertainty... both upside and downside risks have increased." McDonough: "the word that comes to my mind is 'uncertainty.'" Moskow: "upside and downside risks we face are much the same... they now seem to be more severe in both directions." Greenspan: "forecasting financial implosions and vicious cycles is virtually impossible." Multiple participants emphasize both-sided risks intensifying. Direction: elevated uncertainty. Score around 55-65.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modest pickup, some concern. Score ~55. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score ~58. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score ~70. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score ~28. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says lean hawkish, constrained by international. Score ~57. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor market is tighter than output gap score suggests, but this is consistent with the late-90s dynamic where productivity gains kept output gap from being as overheated as labor markets alone would suggest. Multiple participants note this explicitly.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance is somewhat more hawkish than the current lean, which makes sense -- participants are holding now due to international risks but signaling a tightening bias going forward. Coherent.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- **Want to tighten now but accept hold**: Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan (~4)
- **Hold with tightening bias, risks tilted up**: Parry, Moskow, Guynn, Hoenig, Meyer, Ferguson, Kelley, Stern, Greenspan (~9)
- **Hold, more balanced risks**: Boehne, Rivlin, Gramlich, McDonough (~4)
- **Remove tightening bias**: McTeer (~1)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "Inflation probably is rising, but not at a rate that one would consider to be a matter of considerable concern... core CPI over the last three months have averaged about 2.6 percent at an annual rate."
- Meyer: "I interpreted the May CPI report as incrementally reinforcing my view that there may already have been a rebound in the underlying rate of core inflation from near 2 percent to about 2-1/2 percent."
- Minehan: "inflation as measured by the core CPI picking up, as it clearly has in the first months of 1998."
- Greenspan notes core PCE is "still under 2 percent."

**Center of gravity**: Participants see a modest pickup in inflation from very low levels, with core CPI in the 2.5% range. This is modestly above target but not alarming. Favorable temporary factors (Asia, strong dollar, falling commodity prices) are restraining inflation, and there is widespread concern these will unwind.

**Minority views**: Meyer and several Bank presidents (Hoenig, Parry, Minehan) are more concerned about inflation trajectory, widening the uncertainty band. Greenspan and Gramlich are somewhat more sanguine.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Minehan: "I do not think there is much doubt that our economy is operating at levels beyond its potential, even though trend productivity may have increased."
- Rivlin: "The pace of growth last year and the acceleration in the first quarter of this year were clearly unsustainable by anybody's measures."
- Broaddus: "the growth of final demand... still going to be well above trend."
- Multiple participants note expected slowdown from inventory correction and net exports, but underlying final demand remains strong.

**Center of gravity**: The economy is operating modestly above potential, with very strong domestic demand partially offset by weakening net exports. A slowdown is expected but many doubt it will be sufficient.

**Minority views**: Some participants question whether the slowdown will materialize, pushing upside risk. A few see the slowing as potentially sharper than expected due to Asia.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Stern: "The unemployment rate in the Twin Cities metropolitan area has now dropped to 1.7 percent."
- Ferguson: "4.3 percent unemployment rate was not a fluke... probably below anyone's reasonable estimate of the NAIRU."
- Moskow: "The unemployment rate for our five states dropped... to an average of 3.3 percent."
- Jordan: firms advertising for workers with no applications; prison work-release programs; construction unions lobbying for immigration increases.
- Guynn: firm leased BMWs for all 40 employees as retention.

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are extremely tight by any measure. Unemployment is well below NAIRU. Anecdotal evidence of severe shortages across sectors and regions. Wage pressures are building but remain spotty rather than generalized.

**Minority views**: Very little disagreement on the level of tightness. Some debate about whether wages will break out more broadly.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Kelley: "a very, very strong economy... job formation continues strong... business and consumer sentiment is sky high."
- Guynn: "consumer spending continues to pace the domestic economy... Corporate profits remain strong."
- Jordan: "commercial loan demand is very strong, exceeding their loan budgets for 1998."
- Construction spending heading for records in multiple districts.
- Poole: investment outlook will remain strong as firms substitute capital for scarce labor.

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment are both well above trend. Construction is booming. Consumer confidence is high and income growth is supporting spending. Some signs of moderation in retail sales in June, but underlying momentum is strong.

**Minority views**: Some participants see signs of slowing in retail and manufacturing, but the consensus is that underlying demand remains robust.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Kelley: "credit is readily available on reasonable terms."
- Broaddus: "extraordinary amount of money that is looking for a home. Potential investors, including notably REITS, are competing very aggressively for deals... credit standards may be slipping."
- Jordan: "commercial loan demand is very strong, exceeding their loan budgets."
- Minehan: "credit continues to be available, though spreads have widened a bit, particularly for anything involving Asia."
- Hoenig: "continued strong growth in the monetary aggregates... suggests to me that monetary conditions have become more accommodative."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. Credit is freely available, monetary aggregates are growing rapidly, and the stock market is strong. There is modest widening in spreads for Asia-related exposures, but domestic conditions are very supportive.

**Minority views**: Some note spread widening for emerging market and Asia-related credits, but domestic conditions are broadly described as loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "I would propose that if we decide to stay where we are today, we at least also retain an asymmetric directive to the upside."
- Broaddus: "I still think that an increase now in the funds rate would be prudent."
- Minehan: "reluctantly because I think our policy may well be behind the curve as far as our domestic economy is concerned."
- Poole: "I continue to believe that it would be best if we were to raise the funds rate now."
- McTeer (lone minority): "I would suggest a removal of the bias toward tightening."
- Boehne: "If there ever was a time to hold steady, it is now."

**Center of gravity**: The committee holds rates steady but with a clear tightening bias. Multiple participants would prefer to tighten now but accept the hold due to international risks. The center of gravity is lean-hawkish: hold with tightening tilt, waiting for a "window of opportunity."

**Minority views**: Broaddus, Poole, Minehan, and Jordan are more hawkish (wanting immediate action). McTeer is dovish (wants symmetric directive). This widens the band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- No formal dissents on the vote.
- McTeer is alone in preferring symmetric directive: "I guess I am willing to be a minority of one."
- Broaddus: "I still think that an increase now in the funds rate would be prudent... Having said that, I recognize..."
- Minehan: "I would agree with your proposal, in some sense reluctantly."
- Broad consensus for hold + asymmetric, but with meaningful differences in conviction.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate differences about timing and urgency. No formal dissents, but 4-5 participants express reluctance or would prefer different action. The committee coalesces around the Chairman's recommendation but with notable tension between hawks wanting action and the majority wanting patience.

**Minority views**: The hawkish wing (Broaddus, Poole, Minehan, Jordan) and the more balanced camp (McTeer) both accept the consensus.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: "The danger of a wider world financial crisis, while still very small, has risen beyond the point of being de minimis... None of us has ever experienced anything like that. Our parents did."
- McDonough: discusses Japan's cultural/financial crisis at length, potential contagion to Latin America, Russia, Turkey.
- Broaddus: "credit standards may be slipping and we may be digging a hole for ourselves."
- Jordan: "inflationary excesses and imbalances in some of our markets--assets markets in particular and especially real estate."
- Kelley: "the precarious state of the world economy."

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability/conditions receive substantial attention. The Asian financial crisis and its potential to become a broader global financial crisis is a major theme constraining policy. Domestic financial conditions (loose credit, asset markets) also receive attention. This is a prominent but not dominant topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Moskow: "I do think that, on balance, the risks remain definitely to the upside and that we are likely to have to tighten this year."
- Broaddus: "if we see that the strength of overall demand is persisting... and we see a window of opportunity, I believe we should take it."
- Gramlich: "if we see an opportunity for raising the federal funds rate... we should take advantage of that opportunity."
- Meyer: "I strongly favor the asymmetric directive."
- Greenspan: retains asymmetric directive toward tightening.

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- asymmetric tilt toward tightening maintained, multiple participants expect tightening will be needed, but timing is highly data-dependent and contingent on international developments.

**Minority views**: McTeer favors removing forward tightening bias. McDonough and Rivlin emphasize international risks that could delay or prevent tightening.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Boehne: "I have the sense that what we see as the most likely outcome is less likely than earlier and that both upside and downside risks have increased in recent weeks."
- McDonough: "the word that comes to my mind is 'uncertainty.'"
- Greenspan: "forecasting financial implosions and vicious cycles is virtually impossible."
- Ferguson: "The future is quite uncertain."
- Moskow: "the upside and the downside risks we face... now seem to be more severe in both directions."
- Rivlin: "the economy operating at levels that were... 'extremely interesting, unusual, and uncertain.'"

**Center of gravity**: Participants express elevated uncertainty on multiple fronts: the trajectory of Asia, whether the domestic slowdown will materialize, productivity trends, and the potential for global financial contagion. Both upside and downside risks are seen as having increased.

### Optional Dimensions

### O1. INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Parry: "consumers have been benefiting at least temporarily from the related declines in prices of imports."
- Greenspan: "general price weakness" in manufacturing.
- Moskow: "worsening in inflation readings" but "goods price inflation" held down by special factors.

Goods prices are restrained by Asia, strong dollar, and falling commodity prices.

---

### O2. INFLATION_SERVICES
**Score: 60 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Moskow: "consumer price inflation in the Midwest had accelerated... with prices for services being the driving factor."
- Parry: "some recent pickup in price increases for nontraded goods and services... consumer prices for services other than housing also are increasing at a fast pace."

Services inflation is notably outpacing goods inflation.

---

### O3. INFLATION_HOUSING
**Score: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Parry: "CPI housing shelter costs in the San Francisco Bay area have jumped about 7 percent relative to a year earlier, and rents also are picking up in Los Angeles."
- Minehan: "uptick in the core CPI recently, especially as it reflects the cost of shelter."
- Multiple participants describe tight housing markets with rising prices.

---

### O4. INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 47 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Rivlin: "the low inflation expectations and the pervasive belief of the business community that they have no pricing power... are keeping inflation in check."
- Parry: "diminished inflation expectations."
- Greenspan discusses anti-inflationary forces.

Inflation expectations remain well-anchored and are helping to restrain price pressures.

---

### O5. CREDIT_STRESS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Broaddus: "credit standards may be slipping and we may be digging a hole for ourselves."
- Jordan: bankers "raise increasing questions about the transaction prices they are seeing on income property deals."
- Multiple descriptions of very aggressive lending and credit freely available.

Credit stress is low; concern is about excessive ease rather than tightness.

---

Equity markets are mentioned (stock market strong, wealth effects) but not in terms of valuation stress or concern that would warrant scoring.

### O7. BANKING_STRESS
**Score: N/A**

No significant discussion of domestic banking stress. Japanese banking system discussed but in an international context.

---

### O8. TREASURY_STRESS
**Score: N/A**

Not discussed.

---

### O9. DOLLAR_CONCERN
**Score: 62 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Meyer: "when the trend in the dollar reverses, it will be more dramatic than the staff projects."
- Multiple participants note the strong dollar is holding down import prices but worsening the trade deficit.
- Greenspan: "deterioration in Asia clearly has altered portfolio preferences in favor of the U.S. dollar."

Concern is about the dollar being too strong -- hurting exports, widening the trade deficit, and the risk of a sharp reversal.

---

### O10. PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Greenspan: extensive discussion of accelerating productivity, rising returns on capital investment, synergies from technology.
- Stern: "business people do report... very favorable results in terms of the application of technology and their ability to raise productivity."
- McDonough: "We have been experiencing very nice productivity improvement since the last quarter of 1995."
- Multiple participants see productivity as a key variable for the outlook but are uncertain about its persistence.

Productivity growth is perceived as above historical trend but with uncertainty about duration.
