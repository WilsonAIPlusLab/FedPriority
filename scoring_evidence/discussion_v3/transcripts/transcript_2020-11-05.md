## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an FOMC meeting held over two days (November 4–5, 2020). Individual participant statements are clearly attributed by name (e.g., "VICE CHAIR WILLIAMS.", "MR. CLARIDA.", "MS. BRAINARD.", etc.). The document is fully segmented: Day 1 covers (a) an extended go-around on asset purchase policy/balance sheet strategy, (b) financial stability comments, and (c) economic outlook go-around. Day 2 covers the policy go-around supporting Alternative B. This is a rich, very long transcript (~340 pages, roughly 15–17 distinct FOMC participants speaking at length). Confidence will be High for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context
November 2020: COVID-19 pandemic is ongoing. A severe second wave is beginning in the United States and is already causing shutdowns in Europe. The economy recorded a historic 33% annualized Q3 GDP rebound but is still far below pre-pandemic levels (real GDP ~3.5% below Q4 2019 peak; payroll employment ~11 million below February 2020 levels; unemployment at 7.9%). Inflation is running below the 2% target (~1.5% core PCE YoY). The Fed is at the effective lower bound (0–0.25%) and conducting $120B/month in QE. The CARES Act has expired and fiscal uncertainty is high. The new FAIT framework was adopted in August/September 2020 and state-based forward guidance for rate liftoff was introduced in September 2020. This meeting is primarily a "holding" meeting — no policy change expected or executed; Alt-B (no change) supported unanimously.

### 3. Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below target**. Clarida: core PCE "running at a 1.5 percent annual rate on a year-over-year basis." Powell: "Inflation has moved up a bit but remains below our 2 percent objective." Evans: wants inflation reaching 2.5%, calls Tealbook's modest path "too slow." Daly notes "inflation is barely back to its pre-pandemic levels." Brainard: "even under the upside risk scenarios… inflation doesn't even reach 2 percent, let alone sustainably move above it, until 2023 or 2024." Bostic highlights unusual relative price movements (used car inflation, rent softness) but acknowledges these don't constitute monetary inflation. George notes inflation "particularly unsettled" with risks in both directions. Multiple participants note services price inflation "soft" and goods prices elevated but transitory. The center of gravity is clearly below target — roughly in the 26–40 range. Not extreme disinflation fears but firmly below target. Score: **33**.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential** — clear consensus language. Mester: "levels of output and employment are still far below their pre-pandemic levels." Brainard: "the recovery remains highly uncertain and highly uneven." Powell: "continued economic growth amid muted inflation, with risks to the downside." Quarles: "unemployment at the elevated level of 7.9 percent—that's the 90th percentile of the postwar monthly distribution." Rosengren expects unemployment "well above 6 percent by the end of next year." Even the most optimistic speaker (Bullard, who projects 6.5% unemployment at year-end 2020) acknowledges substantial slack. The economy is in recovery from the COVID trough (output gap closed from catastrophic April 2020 lows), but still well below potential. Score: **32** (below potential; recovering but gap persists; consistent with anchors for 2010-14 recovery range 35–42, but COVID adds slightly more severity given still-elevated unemployment and services depression).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**

BRIGHT-LINE CHECK: "far from full employment" → below 50 → confirmed.
