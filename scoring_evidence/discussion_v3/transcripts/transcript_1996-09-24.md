# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-09-24

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
Transcript. Individual speakers are identified by name in a go-around format followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: McTeer, Jordan, Broaddus, Guynn, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Hoenig, Boehne, Melzer, Stern, McDonough, Phillips, Meyer, Yellen, Rivlin, Kelley, Lindsey, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-attended, lengthy meeting with extensive discussion.

### Era Context
September 1996: mid-expansion period. GDP growth was strong in the first half (~3.3%), with expectations of moderation in the second half. Unemployment around 5.1%, considered below most estimates of NAIRU at the time. CPI inflation around 2.6-2.9%. The Fed had cut rates in early 1996 and had been on hold since. This is a classic "soft landing" debate period.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently contained but with clear upside risks from tight labor markets. The center of gravity is that actual inflation measures are benign (core CPI declining to 2.6%), but wages are accelerating and nearly all participants see upside risk. Yellen calls it an "inflationary danger zone." Kelley notes "no visible sign of rising price inflation yet." Multiple participants (Parry, Moskow, Melzer, Broaddus, Stern) see inflation likely to accelerate. However, current inflation readings are low and even declining. Direction: modestly above neutral -- risks are clearly on the upside but actual inflation data are benign. Score should be in the 51-58 range: pressures building but not yet manifest. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Wages accelerating plus tight labor markets plus upside risk language = modestly above 50. Score: 55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly universal view that the economy has been growing above potential. McTeer: "growth more in line with the economy's long-run potential seems more likely." Guynn: "GDP growth is averaging just under 3 percent on a year-over-year basis and is exceeding my earlier expectations." Parry: "economy has been operating at a level somewhat above its long-term potential for some time." Meyer: growth slowing toward trend but from above. Greenspan discusses economy in context of above-trend growth moderating. Most expect growth to slow to trend or slightly above. The economy is operating somewhat above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language is present from multiple speakers. Score must be > 50. Score: 55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant theme of the meeting. Nearly every regional president reports tight labor markets, difficulty finding workers, wage pressures. Unemployment at 5.1%, considered below NAIRU by most. McTeer: "tight labor markets and rising pressure on wages." Broaddus: "persistent and increasingly widespread reports of very tight labor market conditions." Moskow: "Labor markets remain tight." Yellen: "labor markets are tight" and in an "inflationary danger zone." Greenspan: "labor markets are getting increasingly tight." This is clearly tight -- not extreme/unprecedented, but firmly tight. Score: 63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending appears to be slowing in Q3 (Meyer, Greenspan note soft retail sales). But business fixed investment remains strong, housing surprisingly resilient, auto sales solid. Guynn: "strong spending on consumer durables." Phillips: "Business fixed investment also is poised for substantial growth." Greenspan notes retail sales softening but notes strong consumer confidence and wealth effects. Net position: slightly above trend but moderating. Score: 54.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited discussion of financial conditions per se. Phillips notes "A bit more monetary restraint is being provided by the markets since the last meeting, as evidenced by higher long-term rates, including mortgage rates." She also notes "Banks are continuing to support credit availability." Melzer mentions "readily available credit, and a booming equity market." Jordan mentions "rising delinquencies, late payments, bankruptcy filings." Overall, conditions seem roughly neutral to slightly loose -- credit available, equity market strong, but long rates have risen somewhat. Score: 40.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a pivotal dimension. The vote was for no change (alternative B) with asymmetric tightening bias. But the discussion reveals a significant hawkish lean among many participants. Those explicitly favoring a rate hike at this meeting: Parry (25bp), Broaddus (50bp), Minehan (25 or 50bp), Melzer (tightening), Stern (25bp). Those very close to favoring a hike but supporting the Chairman: Meyer, Yellen, Guynn, Hoenig. Those supporting hold: Greenspan, McDonough, McTeer, Jordan, Moskow, Boehne, Rivlin, Kelley, Phillips, Lindsey. Greenspan himself says probability of needing to move is "still above 50 percent" and the asymmetric directive signals tightening bias. Even the "hold" camp largely agrees upside inflation risk. The center of gravity is clearly a tightening bias with hold for now. Score: 57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: This is notable. At least 5 participants explicitly favored a rate hike (Parry, Broaddus, Minehan, Melzer, Stern), and several more were very close (Meyer, Yellen, Guynn, Hoenig). The Vice Chairman McDonough made an unusual plea for unity. Greenspan acknowledged it was a close call. No formal dissent in the final vote (the transcript doesn't record dissenting votes but Parry and others likely dissented or reluctantly agreed). The discussion was vigorous but respectful. This is a meeting with genuine disagreement about timing, though broad agreement on the direction of risk. Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Lindsey gives an extensive discussion of stock market bubble concerns, speculative activity, IPO valuations. He explicitly warns about "emerging bubble" and references late 1920s US and late 1980s Japan. Greenspan engages directly: "I recognize that there is a stock market bubble problem at this point." Hoenig mentions "a bit more of a speculative bent to the economy." Jordan mentions rising delinquencies and charge-offs. But this is mainly Lindsey driving the discussion, with Greenspan and Hoenig contributing. Most participants focused on real economy. Score: 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive toward tightening. Greenspan: "the probability of our having to move, though declining, is still above 50 percent." Multiple participants signal that tightening is coming if data don't improve. Yellen: favors "routine adjustment in response to changing pressures." Meyer: "I expect I will be moved to support a tightening." Guynn: "modest increase... is probably desirable now or sometime soon." The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. Score: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. The key tension is between benign inflation data and tight labor markets. Jordan lays out four distinct scenarios for how things could evolve. Greenspan: "it is virtually impossible to make a judgment as to whether productivity growth is accelerating." Meyer: discusses uncertainty around NAIRU and the "disparity between inflation performance and inflation forecasts." Parry: "I realize that there is some uncertainty associated with this forecast." Rivlin: "forecasting at this particular moment" is difficult. But this is more normal analytical uncertainty than deep structural uncertainty. Score: 42.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63): Labor tightness is higher, which makes sense -- participants emphasize labor markets are tighter than the overall economy is overheating, as output growth is moderating.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current action, consistent with "hold now but likely move soon."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- YELLEN: "I would characterize the economy as operating in an inflationary danger zone."
- KELLEY: "There continues to be... no visible sign of rising price inflation yet."
- PARRY: "With unemployment clearly below the natural rate, wages are accelerating and price pressures are building."
- GREENSPAN: "it is very difficult to get around the fact that hourly wages and salaries are accelerating" but "The performance on the price side, I think, is very impressive."

**Center-of-gravity**: Actual inflation is benign and even declining, but nearly all participants see upside risk from tight labor markets and accelerating wages. The tension between current good data and forward-looking concerns places this modestly above neutral.

**Minority views**: McTeer suggests "we may be worrying too much"; McDonough is "slightly more optimistic on inflation." A few like Parry and Melzer see more imminent inflation pressure. Band reflects this range.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- PARRY: "The economy has been operating at a level somewhat above its long-term potential for some time now."
- GUYNN: "GDP growth is averaging just under 3 percent on a year-over-year basis."
- MEYER: "growth has slowed much more sharply in the third quarter... moving down to a pace close to trend."
- GREENSPAN: growth moderating, inventory effects boosting Q3 but final sales weakening.

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy has been above potential but is moderating toward trend. Most see it as still somewhat above potential. "Somewhat above" = modestly above 50.

**Minority views**: Meyer sees Q3 possibly at or below trend already; Parry and Broaddus emphasize continued above-potential operation.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- BROADDUS: "persistent and increasingly widespread reports of very tight labor market conditions"
- YELLEN: "it is now 'impossible' to find qualified entry-level workers, whereas six months ago the word 'difficult' would have been used more frequently."
- GREENSPAN: "the labor markets are getting increasingly tight"
- Multiple District reports of worker shortages, signing bonuses, difficulty finding workers at all skill levels.

**Center-of-gravity**: Virtually unanimous that labor markets are tight and tightening further. Unemployment at 5.1%, below most NAIRU estimates. Broad agreement. Tight but not "severe shortages" or "unsustainable" -- more "low unemployment, hard to find workers, moderate wage pressure."

**Minority views**: Jordan notes "it is now easier to find people at the entry level" in some areas; Boehne notes "some labor markets are tight while others still have significant slack." Narrow band due to broad agreement.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MEYER: "I expect an increase of only 1 percent in final sales in the third quarter compared to 4 percent in the previous quarter."
- PHILLIPS: "Business fixed investment also is poised for substantial growth."
- GREENSPAN: retail sales softening but "capital goods establishments are quite robust."
- GUYNN: housing "surprisingly strong" with "strong spending on consumer durables."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending clearly slowing from a strong pace but business investment remains robust. Housing surprisingly resilient. Net: near trend, perhaps slightly above. The moderation from Q2 strength is the dominant narrative.

**Minority views**: Meyer sees sharper Q3 slowdown in consumer spending; Minehan sees risk consumer spending is stronger than Greenbook projects.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- PHILLIPS: "A bit more monetary restraint is being provided by the markets... higher long-term rates, including mortgage rates" but "Banks are continuing to support growth with credit availability."
- MELZER: "readily available credit, and a booming equity market."
- GREENSPAN: "consumer credit, which has been growing less rapidly and getting increasingly tight, is finally beginning to affect the consumer data."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. Credit available, equity market strong, but long rates have risen. Slightly below neutral toward the loose side.

**Minority views**: Greenspan notes consumer credit tightening; Jordan notes rising delinquencies. But overall conditions remain supportive.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- PARRY: "I believe it would be prudent to raise interest rates 25 basis points at this meeting."
- GREENSPAN: "I continue to believe that the probability of our having to move, though declining, is still above 50 percent" but recommends hold with asymmetric directive.
- YELLEN: "would also have been quite willing to support an upward adjustment of 25 basis points today."
- MEYER: "I could make the case... a cautious step toward restraint would be prudent" but supports hold.

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear tightening bias. At least 5 speakers explicitly favor a rate hike; several more are very close. The decision is to hold with asymmetric tightening directive. This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish stance.

**Minority views**: Rivlin and Kelley are more comfortable waiting; McTeer suggests less concern about inflation risk. Parry, Broaddus, Melzer, Minehan, Stern push for immediate action.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Five participants explicitly dissent from the hold recommendation: Parry, Broaddus, Minehan, Melzer, Stern.
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH makes unusual plea for unity, suggesting internal tensions.
- MEYER: "My decision at this meeting is more difficult than at the past two meetings."
- YELLEN: "I find myself very close to the margin."

**Center-of-gravity**: Substantial disagreement about timing of tightening, with roughly 5 explicit hawks wanting immediate action, 3-4 very close to that position, and the rest supporting hold. The Vice Chairman's unity appeal signals real internal tension. Not a deep philosophical split but genuine camps on timing.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- LINDSEY: Extensive discussion of stock market bubble, IPO activity, "excessive optimism," comparisons to "United States in the late 1920s and Japan in the late 1980s."
- GREENSPAN: "I recognize that there is a stock market bubble problem at this point."
- HOENIG: "there is a bit more of a speculative bent to the economy."

**Center-of-gravity**: Mainly driven by Lindsey's extended remarks with brief engagement from Greenspan and Hoenig. Most participants focused on real economy and inflation. Limited but notable discussion -- a few participants address it meaningfully, but it is not a major theme for the majority.

**Minority views**: Lindsey is clearly an outlier in emphasis; most participants give no attention to financial stability.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: asymmetric directive toward tightening; "the probability of our having to move... is still above 50 percent."
- GUYNN: "a modest increase... is probably desirable now or sometime soon."
- MEYER: "If the unemployment rate stabilizes near 5 percent and if the economy settles into trend growth, I expect I will be moved to support a tightening."
- YELLEN: favors "routine adjustment in response to changing pressures on resources."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal toward tightening, conditional on data. Asymmetric directive is the institutional expression of this bias. Most participants signal tightening is the next likely move.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- JORDAN: Lays out four distinct monetary policy scenarios, all "respectable views."
- GREENSPAN: "it is virtually impossible to make a judgment as to whether productivity growth is accelerating."
- MEYER: discusses "disparity between inflation performance and inflation forecasts."
- BOEHNE: "there is something for everyone, depending on what story one wants to tell."

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, mainly about the inflation/productivity puzzle and whether the Phillips curve relationship is changing. Not extreme uncertainty -- most have reasonable confidence in the broad outlook -- but genuine analytical difficulty with the wage-price-productivity nexus.

### OPTIONAL DIMENSIONS

**INFLATION_GOODS**: N/A. No specific goods inflation discussion by 2+ participants beyond scattered mentions of steel and building materials prices.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A. Not specifically discussed as a category.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A. Housing activity discussed but not housing inflation specifically.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- KELLEY: "Inflationary expectations are flat."
- MELZER: concern that "market participants may easily conclude that the 'acceptable' rate of inflation has moved up."
- YELLEN: "we have yet to see any evidence of an uptick in inflationary expectations."
Expectations anchored near current levels, with some concern about drift upward if Fed fails to act.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A. Jordan mentions rising delinquencies/charge-offs but no broad discussion.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A as stress per se, but notable: Lindsey's bubble concern is about overvaluation not undervaluation. Score would be on the low end (bubble concern). But only 1-2 participants discuss it as a concern, so N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A. Banks described as doing well and profitable.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A. Foreign currency management discussed but not dollar valuation concerns.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- GREENSPAN: extensive analysis showing corporate productivity at 2.6% annually, much higher than reported nonfarm business figures; "productivity is indeed rising a lot faster than our statistics indicate."
- JORDAN: "productivity is really quite good" citing NFIB survey evidence; reports of 5-7% annual productivity improvements at auto parts firms.
- Multiple participants note productivity as potential explanation for wage-price puzzle.
Above historical trend perception, though with significant uncertainty about measurement.
