# FOMC Transcript Scoring — June 17–18, 2014

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (June 17–18, 2014). Individual participant statements are clearly identified by speaker name (e.g., MR. BULLARD., MR. FISHER., MS. GEORGE., CHAIR YELLEN., etc.). The document is segmented into a normalization/operational discussion (Day 1, pages ~74–160), an economic go-around (Day 1 evening and Day 2 morning, pages ~160–217), and a policy discussion/vote (Day 2, pages ~220–256). Staff content appears in Q&A but participant-attributed views dominate. I score only participant-attributed content.

### 2. Era Context

June 2014 is mid-recovery, approximately 5 years after the GFC trough. The Fed is actively tapering QE (reducing asset purchases by $10B/meeting). The funds rate is at the zero lower bound. Unemployment stands around 6.3%. Core PCE inflation is running roughly 1.5–1.8% — below the 2% target. The era is characterized by: QE tapering ongoing, ZLB policy rate, below-target inflation, labor market slack still present but improving, and preparations for eventual "liftoff." The macro backdrop is "sluggish but improving recovery."

### 3. Directional Analysis for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below** the 2% target but with recent firming. There is wide agreement that inflation is currently below target. Evans: "I think inflation and inflation pressures remain low." Rosengren: concerned about persistent below-target outcomes. Kocherlakota: staff sees inflation not returning to 2% until late this decade. However, several hawks (Plosser, Fisher, Bullard) note firming readings — Plosser: "three-month core PCE increasing 1.8 percent... trending upward toward the Committee's target." George: "recent data releases show that the deceleration we saw last year has ended." Bullard forecasts inflation exceeding target. Chair Yellen notes "higher recent readings on core CPI and PCE." The center of gravity: inflation is below target, recently firming, most expect gradual return to 2%. This maps to ~35-42 range. The recent firming and some hawks pushing upward risk argues for ~40.

**Direction check:** Participants broadly see inflation as below target (26-40 range). The firming provides modest upward pressure to the upper end of this range. Score: 40.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential** — a weak Q1 (large negative GDP), bouncing back in Q2, but with the trajectory expected to return to above-trend growth. Multiple participants cite "substantial slack," "significant slack," "below potential." Williams: "substantial labor market slack persists." Dudley: "I still have considerable uncertainty." Q1 GDP was described as a "large negative." Yellen: "it looks like we'll see a pretty alarming decline in first-quarter GDP." However, most expect above-trend growth in the second half — most project ~3% growth for 2H, which is "somewhat above trend." The net assessment is: economy currently somewhat below potential, improving. This places output gap in the 35-42 range. The bounce-back expected but not yet realized. Score: 38.

**BRIGHT-LINE CHECK:** "Slack remains," "below potential" language is universal → score MUST be < 50. Score 38 satisfies this.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see labor markets as **improving but still slack**, with unemployment at 6.3%. Multiple participants cite significant remaining slack — involuntary part-time, elevated U-6, depressed participation rate. Rosengren: "I remain concerned about slack in the labor market." Williams: "substantial labor market slack persists." Lockhart: "improving but still-elevated level of involuntary part-time employment." Tarullo: "it's still hard to say just how much less slack there is." However, improvement is consistently noted — job gains ~230K/month, unemployment falling. Fisher/Texas provides a sharp contrast (5% unemployment, wage pressures). Plosser sees unemployment below his natural rate estimate. The **center of gravity** is: labor markets still below full employment but improving at a solid pace, with meaningful remaining slack. This maps to ~37-42 range. Score: 40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see spending and investment as **at or slightly below trend** in net. Q1 consumption came in disappointing (health care data revisions), and housing investment is significantly soft. But consumer spending is expected to rebound and business investment to strengthen. Multiple participants describe the outlook as "moderate," "gradual pickup," "good not great." Evans: "continued to be positive, though not bubbling over with enthusiasm." Tarullo: "cap-ex almost has to jump some this year." Lockhart: "run rate is a bit above 3 percent." Housing is widely described as soft and a persistent drag. Retail sales have been weak. Net: at-to-somewhat-below trend with recovery expected. Score: 44.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Multiple participants discuss financial conditions as **loose to very loose** — but this is the conditions direction, not the policy intent. Powell explicitly notes: "implied volatility for equities, currencies, and long- and short-term fixed income are at historically low levels." Dudley: low volatility, reach for yield, spreads compressed. Powell: "leveraged finance markets in the United States and Europe are extremely loose." Williams: "supportive financial market conditions." George: "financing conditions remaining supportive of growth." The dominant characterization is loose financial conditions / low volatility / reach for yield. This maps to the 25-35 range (somewhat loose to neutral). Score: 28.

**Polarity check:** Higher = TIGHTER. Evidence says conditions are LOOSE. Score should be LOW (below 50). Score 28 is consistent.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The meeting: unanimous support for Alternative B (continuing $10B taper). Zero formal dissents. The discussion shows a clear majority in the dovish-to-moderate camp — Evans wants liftoff in Q1 2016, Rosengren in early 2016, Williams aligned with Tealbook baseline. Kocherlakota and Evans are outright doves emphasizing inflation below target. Plosser, Fisher, Lacker, and Bullard hold more hawkish views — Plosser suggests liftoff possibly as early as Q4 2014 per Taylor rules; Bullard warns about calendar-based policy being too dovish. The **center of gravity** is: continue tapering, no liftoff signal yet, forward guidance remains "considerable period," QE still ongoing. This is solidly in the dovish portion — the QE taper is occurring but the policy stance remains accommodative. Anchors: 2014 pre-liftoff era aligns with 40-50 (lean dovish to balanced). With ongoing QE, taper, zero rates, and majority dovish liftoff projections, score: 42.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Zero formal dissents on Alternative B. The unanimity on the statement is clear — "overwhelmingly in the go-round, what I heard was support to basically leave alternative B as it is." However, there are substantive policy differences: Kocherlakota wants to add language defending the inflation target from below; Plosser/Fisher/Lacker are more hawkish on liftoff timing; Bullard wants reinvestment kept as a buffer step vs. Dudley who wants it after liftoff; Evans/Kocherlakota/Rosengren more worried about below-target inflation vs. Fisher/Plosser/Lacker who worry about being behind the curve. These are real substantive differences but not formal dissents. A meeting with 0 formal dissents but several strong reservations and a clear hawk-dove divide scores in the 30-40 range. Score: 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability receives **moderate to prominent** attention. Powell gives an extended analysis of low volatility, leveraged finance markets, reach for yield, CLO issuance, and the Carney speech. Dudley also devotes significant attention to low volatility and its implications. Williams mentions carry trade and reach for yield from global divergence. Yellen explicitly endorses the concerns: "I too am concerned about developments in financial markets." Lockhart makes a brief mention of Herstatt risk. The normalization session on Day 1 also addresses financial stability implications of the ON RRP facility and circuit breakers. This is moderately prominent — several participants discuss it meaningfully. Score: 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The current guidance is "considerable period after the end of asset purchases" — this is the existing language being maintained. Several participants discuss eventual liftoff language changes. Mester: "it will very soon be time to change the guidance indicating that liftoff will be a considerable period after the asset purchases end." Plosser wants to end reinvestment "at least some portion" before liftoff. Bullard favors reinvestment end as buffer before rate hike. Evans signals liftoff in Q1 2016 with shallow path. Williams: "maintaining the current course of policy... forward guidance that signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate." The overall forward signal is: accommodative stance maintained, tapering continues, liftoff still some way off, "considerable period" language preserved. This is an easing-lean / low-for-long signal, consistent with the 2014 pre-liftoff era anchor of 25-40. Score: 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Multiple participants explicitly discuss elevated uncertainty. Fischer: "incredible uncertainty about these things... high degree of uncertainty around these estimates." Rosengren: counseling flexibility because the exit is "experimental." Dudley: "considerable uncertainty about what the growth rate is going to be during the second half." Tarullo: "hard to say just how much less slack there is." Fischer quotes Tealbook's 70% confidence interval of -3/4 to 4-1/4 for the real funds rate. Williams cites uncertainty about inflation dynamics. The mystery of Q1 GDP adds uncertainty. Several specific factors "harder to assess than usual." However, the uncertainty is not extreme/Knightian — participants still have reasonable modal forecasts. This maps to 40-50 range. Score: 45.

### 4. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, both in the 35-45 range — consistent. Labor market seen as improving a bit faster (hence slightly higher score), output gap more depressed by Q1 GDP.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Both lean dovish/accommodative. The forward tone is slightly more dovish than the current stance because the "considerable period" language explicitly signals extended accommodation. This is coherent — current action is continuing taper (not yet hiking, hence 42), forward guidance is explicitly low-for-long (hence 30).
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below neutral and roughly symmetric. Consistent with a below-target economy with slack.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (28): Conditions are loose, consistent with accommodative policy and the QE era.

### 5. Speaker Count for Center of Gravity

**Economic assessment orientation:**
- More worried about below-target inflation / more slack: Rosengren, Evans, Kocherlakota, Brainard (implied), Williams (partial)
- Middle/balanced: Lockhart, Tarullo, Powell, Mester, Fischer (uncertain), Chair Yellen
- More hawkish / less slack / inflation rising: Fisher, Plosser, Bullard, George, Lacker

Roughly 5 more dovish, 6 middle/balanced, 5 more hawkish — a slightly right-of-center committee but held together by unanimous support for Alternative B.

### 6. Optional Dimension Assessments

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:** Multiple participants discuss inflation expectations at length — Kocherlakota, Bullard, Williams, Lacker, Evans all weigh in. The majority view is that long-run expectations remain **anchored near 2%** (survey measures stable, SPF at 2%), with some concern about possible drift below. This is near neutral-to-slightly-below: score 45.

**CREDIT_STRESS:** Powell discusses leveraged finance: "extremely loose," covenant-lite loans at all-time high, low spreads. This indicates very low credit stress (opposite direction). Score: 18 (low stress, loose conditions).

**EQUITY_STRESS:** Not specifically discussed in terms of equity market stress. Mentioned only in passing re: volatility. Insufficient discussion for 2+ participants. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS:** No discussion of banking system stress. Well-capitalized system is assumed. N/A.

**TREASURY_STRESS:** Not discussed by 2+ participants as a specific concern. N/A.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN:** Williams mentions global carry trade / capital flows from policy divergence, but not specific dollar strength concern. N/A.

**PRODUCTIVITY:** Fischer and Williams mention productivity growth tangentially; not specifically scored as a major topic. N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Evans (go-around): "I think inflation and inflation pressures remain low. I think they are low for the PCE. I think they are low for the CPI. I think they are low around the world."
- Plosser: "Inflation appears to be moving up, with the three-month core PCE increasing 1.8 percent... trending upward toward the Committee's target."
- George: "recent data releases show that the deceleration we saw last year has ended, and we're seeing a broad-based increase in inflation over the past few months."
- Chair Yellen (summary): "many of you noted the higher recent readings we've seen on core CPI and PCE inflation... as providing positive evidence that inflation is in fact moving back up toward our goal."

**Center-of-gravity:** Inflation is below target (~1.5-1.8% PCE) but recently firming; the majority view is slow, gradual return to 2% with notable dovish concern about persistent below-target outcomes and hawkish minority seeing faster convergence or overshoot.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota / Rosengren fear persistent below-target inflation; Bullard / Plosser / Fisher see inflation rising faster than the Tealbook suggests, potentially exceeding 2% by 2015-16. Uncertainty band reflects this hawk-dove spread on inflation trajectory.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "substantial labor market slack persists, and inflation remains below our longer-run target."
- Rosengren: "Even with the assumed strong second quarter, real GDP growth for the first half of this year has been quite disappointing... 2014 real GDP growth of only 2.1 percent."
- Chair Yellen: "it looks like we'll see a pretty alarming decline in first-quarter GDP."
- Plosser: "growth will be about 3 percent, which is a bit above trend, over the remainder of this year" (forward-looking, not yet achieved).

**Center-of-gravity:** Q1 GDP was sharply negative; Q2 rebound expected but only beginning; the economy is currently below potential with slack acknowledged broadly, though the recovery trajectory is expected to move back toward and modestly above trend. Bright-line rule confirms score < 50.

**Minority views:** Fisher (Texas) sees local economy at or near capacity; Plosser and Bullard see closer approach to full employment; Rosengren is most pessimistic about the recovery's pace.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "substantial labor market slack persists."
- Lockhart: "The question of the amount of remaining labor market slack will be central... the improving but still-elevated level of involuntary part-time employment is very important to the question of how much slack remains."
- Tarullo: "there certainly continues to be improvement in the labor market... [but] it's still hard to say just how much less slack there is."
- Chair Yellen: "several of you, including Vice Chairman Dudley, noted that you agree [the unemployment decline overstates improvement]... an unusually large share of the work force is involuntarily underemployed."

**Center-of-gravity:** Labor market improving steadily (~230K jobs/month), unemployment at 6.3% and falling, but broad measures (U-6, PTER, participation rate) indicate meaningful remaining slack. Well below the 46-52 "near NAIRU" range; consistent with 35-40 anchor (2011-14 recovery period).

**Minority views:** Fisher's Texas District has 5% unemployment and acute labor shortages — a clear outlier from the national picture. Plosser believes decline in participation is structural, implying less slack than headline U-3 suggests.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 44 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "I have lowered my forecast for residential investment and business fixed investment... The scars from the recession appear to be causing more cautious behavior by both households and firms than I expected earlier."
- George: "fiscal drag ending, labor markets improving, household wealth continuing to rise, and financing conditions remaining supportive of growth." (moderately positive outlook)
- Lockhart: "Consumer spending continues to rise... Large corporate contacts hinted at a thawing of their attitude toward capital investment or expansion."
- Chair Yellen: "I am also worried by the continued failure of real wages to grow in line with productivity... I do see it as a downside risk to the outlook for consumer spending."

**Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending at a moderate but not robust pace; housing investment notably soft; business investment expected to pick up but not surging. Net position is near trend but slightly soft — at the lower end of the "at trend" range. Housing consistently flagged as a persistent drag.

**Minority views:** Fisher and Bullard are more optimistic (strong business contacts, favorable sentiment); Rosengren and Evans more cautious about household spending momentum.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 28 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Powell: "implied volatility for equities, currencies, and long- and short-term fixed income are at historically low levels around the globe... Conditions in the leveraged finance markets in the United States and Europe are extremely loose."
- Dudley: "our gradual, predictable taper of our asset purchases and our forward guidance with respect to liftoff does help reduce uncertainty... this contributes to less volatility... low volatility also contributes to rising stock prices and lower term premiums that make financial conditions more accommodative."
- George: "financing conditions remaining supportive of growth."

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are loose — low volatility, compressed spreads, reach-for-yield behavior, supportive equity and bond markets. Multiple participants characterize conditions as "accommodative" or "extremely loose" in leveraged segments. Polarity check: Higher = tighter; evidence says loose → score well below 50.

**Minority views:** Powell (and implicitly Dudley) flags financial stability risks from this looseness, but this informs the FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS score, not the tightness direction.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "inflation remains below our longer-run target, and these circumstances call for maintaining the current course of policy. This includes a further measured reduction in the pace of asset purchases, combined with forward guidance that signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate."
- Evans: "My preferred policy rate liftoff is delayed until I have high confidence that one-year-ahead inflation is at least at our target, and is sustainable thereafter." (Q1 2016 liftoff)
- Plosser (dissenting view): "I expect we will need to begin raising the funds rate sooner than is implied by our previous statement language and perhaps as early as the fourth quarter of this year."
- Chair Yellen (summary): "overwhelmingly in the go-round, what I heard was support to basically leave alternative B as it is with minimal red ink today."

**Center-of-gravity:** The Committee unanimously supports continuing QE taper (~$10B/meeting) and maintaining "considerable period" language. QE ongoing, funds rate at zero, dovish majority on liftoff timing. Pre-liftoff era anchor of 40-50 confirmed. Hawks want earlier liftoff but accept the current decision. Score reflects continuing-QE-taper stance with accommodative bias.

**Minority views:** Fisher, Plosser, Lacker, and Bullard lean more hawkish on liftoff timing and balance sheet normalization, pulling center of gravity slightly toward 45 vs. pure dovish end.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Chair Yellen (vote summary): "overwhelmingly in the go-round, what I heard was support to basically leave alternative B as it is with minimal red ink today." (zero dissents)
- Substantive differences exist: Kocherlakota advocates adding language defending the inflation target from below; Plosser and Bullard want reinvestment ended before liftoff; Fisher and Plosser see faster liftoff appropriate per Taylor rules.
- Bullard: "current policy path is not state-contingent enough. It is too driven by calendar considerations." (reserved but supporting B)
- Evans: "liftoff in the first quarter of 2016 with a shallow path" vs. Plosser's Q4 2014 suggestion from rules.

**Center-of-gravity:** Zero formal dissents but notable substantive disagreements on inflation trajectory, liftoff timing, and balance sheet sequencing. A real hawk-dove spread with 4-5 participants on each tail. Consistent with 31-45 range (moderate differences, 1 dissent or 2-3 reservations). No dissent but multiple "reluctant" supporters on different sides.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 45 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Powell (extended): entire subsection of economic go-around devoted to low volatility, leveraged finance conditions, CLO issuance, dealer inventories, retail liquidity mismatches, and Carney speech as communication tool.
- Dudley: "low level of volatility that we have seen in currency, equity, and fixed-income markets... causes me some concern." (3+ paragraphs)
- Chair Yellen: "I too am concerned about developments in financial markets and the signs we may be seeing of complacency in some segments."
- Williams: "divergence in the policy direction across the globe will add an additional dimension... carry trade, reach-for-yield, and short-term capital flows with financial-stability implications."

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial stability receives meaningful dedicated attention from at least 4 participants (Powell, Dudley, Williams, Yellen), though it does not dominate the meeting in the way a crisis discussion would. Consistent with 40-55 "moderate discussion" range.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 30 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "forward guidance that signals a highly accommodative future path for the funds rate."
- Evans: "liftoff in the first quarter of 2016 with a shallow path of tightening thereafter."
- Mester: "rate increases anticipated sometime next year... it will very soon be time to change the guidance indicating that liftoff will be a considerable period after the asset purchases end."
- Yellen: policy is "data-dependent," but current "considerable period" language maintained.

**Center-of-gravity:** The forward signal is an easing-lean / "low for long" message — "considerable period" language preserved, liftoff projected for late 2015 or 2016 by most, gradual path anticipated. This falls in the 26-40 "easing lean with data dependence" range. Not quite as dovish as the full Evans Rule/QE3 era (which scored 3-8), but clearly accommodative forward signal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 45 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Fischer: "incredible uncertainty about these things, and we don't really know how to deal with it... 70 percent confidence interval around our estimate of 1¾ percent would run from negative ¾ percent to 4¼ percent."
- Dudley: "I still have considerable uncertainty about what the growth rate is going to be during the second half of the year."
- Rosengren: "uncertainty does not excuse inaction, but it is a reason to admit to ourselves and the public that this will be somewhat experimental."
- Williams: uncertainty about inflation dynamics (short- vs. long-term unemployed effects on wages); multiple participants cite "hard to assess."

**Center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants express genuinely elevated uncertainty — about growth trajectory post-Q1, about inflation dynamics and expectations, about normalization mechanics, about labor market slack measurement. This is real uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging. Consistent with 40-50 "elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty" range.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:**

**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kocherlakota: "Cogley-Sargent and Stock-Watson models... estimate of the trend component... is currently less than 1½ percent. Inflation expectations would now be materially below target."
- Williams: "SPF is exactly at 2 percent for the 10-year ahead... Dallas Fed trimmed mean... 2 percent over the past 15 years."
- Bullard: "SPF expectations of core PCE inflation remain at 2 percent, and other measures are also stable."

**Center-of-gravity:** Survey and market measures show expectations anchored near 2%, but econometric models suggest possible drift below. Overall: anchored near but possibly slightly below 2% target → score slightly below 50.

**CREDIT_STRESS:**

**Score: 18 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Powell: "Conditions in the leveraged finance markets in the United States and Europe are extremely loose. In the United States, leverage is high... Yield structures are historically weak with covenant-lite loans at an all-time high."
- Dudley: "credit growth is sluggish" but conditions are broadly accommodative.

**Center-of-gravity:** Credit conditions are very loose — compressed spreads, weak covenants, reach for yield. Polarity check: Higher = MORE credit stress; evidence says stress is VERY LOW (loose credit). Score well below 50.
