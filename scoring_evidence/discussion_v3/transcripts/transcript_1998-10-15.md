# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1998-10-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
Transcript of an **intermeeting conference call** (not a regularly scheduled FOMC meeting). Individual speakers are identified by name. This is an emergency-style call in which participants discuss financial market stress, real economy conditions, and whether to cut rates between meetings.

### Era Context
October 1998: The aftermath of the Russian debt crisis and the LTCM collapse. Global financial contagion, flight to quality, widening credit spreads. The U.S. domestic economy remains solid but there are fears of spillover from financial stress. The FOMC had already cut the fed funds rate 25bp at its September 29 meeting (just 16 days prior). This call results in another intermeeting 25bp cut.

### Speaker Count and Views

**Financial market stress** — discussed extensively by: McDonough (detailed spread data), Greenspan, Boehne, McTeer, Minehan, Guynn, Parry, Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson, Gramlich.

**Real economy still solid**: Stern, Hoenig, Guynn, Parry, Moskow, Minehan all report fundamentals largely unchanged. Boehne sees erosion in sentiment but not yet in activity. Greenspan agrees "very little evidence of underlying deterioration."

**Policy views**:
- Favor 25bp cut now: Greenspan, Boehne, Rivlin, Kelley (conditional), Meyer, Ferguson, Gramlich, Stern, Moskow, Minehan, Parry, McDonough, Hoenig (reluctant)
- Prefer 50bp cut: McTeer, Gramlich (could defend 50), Rivlin (if discount rate only 25bp)
- Prefer to wait: Guynn, Jordan (thinks policy too expansionary)
- No strong objection raised by anyone

### Dimensional Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Inflation is barely discussed. Poole asks about PPI; Greenspan dismisses the high PPI as "clearly a significant aberration." No participant raises inflation as a current concern. Late 1990s low-inflation environment. The entire focus is on financial stress and downside risks. Direction: well below midpoint. Participants perceive very low inflation pressure. Score ~30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Most participants report the economy is still strong — "fundamentals remain reasonably good" (Hoenig), "no fundamental change" (Guynn, Moskow, Parry), construction "booked up through 1999" (Stern). But there is concern about prospective weakening. The economy is roughly at or modestly above potential — late 1990s boom still intact but with emerging cracks. Greenspan: "reasonably good economy is a fairly objective observation." Score ~53-55 (modestly above potential, consistent with late 90s boom anchor of 57-63, but with acknowledged softening risk pulling it slightly lower).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Stern mentions "persistence of tight labor markets," Moskow says "labor markets are still very tight." Boehne mentions some layoff announcements. The labor market is clearly tight — late 1990s tightness. Score ~63-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is strong — Rivlin: "general strength in the domestic economy, especially in the consumer sector." Investment is mixed: some pullback in export-sensitive industries, capital spending plans for 1999 being trimmed (Moskow), some delays on commercial real estate deals. But autos strong, construction booked. Net: above trend but with softening signs. Score ~55-58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant topic. McDonough gives extraordinary detail on spread widening: Treasury on/off-the-run spreads at 27bp vs normal 4-5; swap spreads doubled; BBB spreads at 6.3% vs normal 3.2%; "a very dramatic increase in risk spreads"; "a rather serious drying up of liquidity"; "a very significant run into high quality instruments of the kind that has not been seen for a very long time." Greenspan: "the degree of liquidity in the overall financial system is clearly under stress." Banks tightening credit, issuance dried up. This is clearly tight/stressed conditions. Score ~78-82.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Nearly universal agreement to ease. This is an intermeeting cut — itself a dovish signal. Greenspan proposes 25bp, nearly everyone agrees. Some want 50bp. Only Jordan objects (thinks policy too expansionary) and Guynn would prefer waiting. The center of gravity is clearly dovish — cutting rates between meetings due to financial stress. Score ~25-28.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: While there is broad agreement on easing, there are notable differences: Jordan thinks policy is "too expansionary"; Guynn and Poole would prefer waiting; McTeer and Rivlin want 50bp; Kelley is cautious about messaging. But no one formally dissents from the 25bp cut. Moderate differences in degree, not direction. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability/conditions dominate the entire meeting. McDonough's lengthy opening on spreads, LTCM discussion, flight to quality, clearing system stress, rating agency anxiety. Most of the call is about financial market dysfunction. This rivals economic assessment in emphasis. Score ~82-85.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan says "a funds rate in the area of 4-1/2 to 5 percent seems close to where we want it to be at this stage" (from current 5.25%, implying more cuts ahead). Multiple participants talk about getting "ahead of the curve." The forward signal is clearly dovish — more easing expected. Score ~25-30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Many participants explicitly mention uncertainty: "a greater sense of uncertainty about the future" (Boehne), "enormous uncertainty and uneasiness" (Guynn), "a lot of increased uncertainty" (Moskow). Greenspan: "I have never seen anything like the current situation." But the uncertainty is somewhat directional — most see downside risks, not pure Knightian uncertainty. Score ~58-63.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (64): Both above 50, consistent. Labor is tighter than output gap because participants emphasize tight labor while noting output growth may slow — plausible lag.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (27) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (27): Both dovish, aligned. Despite the economy still being above potential, financial stress drives dovish policy.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says very low concern. Score 30 = below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy still solid but softening. Score 53 = modestly above. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Spreads dramatically widened, liquidity drying up. Score 80 = tight. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Cutting between meetings = very dovish. Score 27. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. POOLE: "Do we want to ease policy on a day when we have received bad PPI news?"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the latest PPI number is clearly a significant aberration, and I think the markets understood that."
- No participant raises inflation as a current concern; the entire discussion focuses on financial stress and downside growth risks.

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is essentially a non-issue at this meeting. The one mention (PPI) is dismissed as an aberration. The late 1990s were a period of subdued inflation, and participants show no concern about price pressures.

**Minority views**: Jordan implicitly sees policy as too loose, which could relate to future inflation concerns, but he does not make an explicit inflation argument.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "there do not seem to be any changes in the economic fundamentals... a number of people in construction, for example, commented that they are booked up through 1999."
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor markets are still very tight and there are still many areas of strength in our District economy."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "there is very little evidence that I can detect of an underlying deterioration in the economy."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is currently operating modestly above potential — the late 1990s expansion is still intact with tight labor markets and solid activity. But participants widely acknowledge prospective weakening from financial stress, pulling the score slightly below the era anchor.

**Minority views**: Boehne and McTeer see more immediate erosion; Stern and Guynn see fundamentals holding firm.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 64 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "the persistence of tight labor markets and squeezes on earnings"
- MR. MOSKOW: "the labor markets are still very tight"
- MS. MINEHAN: reports layoff announcements at Gillette, Polaroid, Raytheon, and some financial services firms

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market remains tight by historical standards — consistent with the late 1990s boom. Some scattered layoff announcements have appeared but are sector-specific, not broad-based.

**Minority views**: Minehan cites layoffs, suggesting early softening, but this is localized.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. RIVLIN: "general strength in the domestic economy, especially in the consumer sector"
- MR. MOSKOW: "they are all planning lower levels of capital spending, hiring, and expenses for 1999 than they had for 1998"
- MR. BOEHNE: "if people have not made a final decision or they have not made a final commitment, there is a tendency to wait and see"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending remains strong. Business investment is mixed — some pullback in export-sensitive and energy sectors, delays on uncommitted projects, but also ongoing major investments (Lucent $350M plant, heavy construction backlogs). Net position is above trend but softening.

**Minority views**: McTeer emphasizes that financial market stress will "break the link" to the real economy going forward.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 80 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "a very dramatic increase in risk spreads... the BBB spread is now at about 6.3 percentage points [vs. normal 3.2]... a rather serious drying up of liquidity."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the degree of liquidity in the overall financial system is clearly under stress... junk bond issuance has completely dried up, but even investment grade issuance has come down very markedly"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "Even in the most liquid market of the world, the U.S. Treasury market, there is an incredible broadening of spreads"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are severely tight. Spreads have roughly doubled across the board, issuance has frozen for non-investment-grade and slowed dramatically for investment-grade, flight to quality is extreme, and liquidity has dried up. This is a significant credit/financial stress event, though not at the level of systemic seizure (clearing systems still functioning).

**Minority views**: None disagree on the severity; Hoenig notes that absolute rates on some investment-grade are still reasonable due to Treasury rate declines.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 27 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would be inclined at this stage to move the funds rate down by 25 basis points."
- MS. RIVLIN: "we should get ahead of that by taking action as expeditiously as we can"
- MR. MCTEER: "I would prefer 50 basis points today rather than 25 on the fed funds rate"
- MR. JORDAN: "my position on policy, namely that it is too expansionary at this point"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong dovish consensus for an intermeeting 25bp cut, with some voices favoring 50bp. This is a preemptive easing to get ahead of anticipated real economy weakness from financial stress. Only Jordan explicitly opposes easing.

**Minority views**: Jordan (hawkish outlier), Guynn and Poole (prefer waiting but don't object), McTeer and Rivlin (prefer 50bp cut).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "my position on policy, namely that it is too expansionary at this point" — hawkish outlier
- MR. GUYNN: "I would prefer to hold off taking action until the next FOMC meeting" — but "I certainly would not dissent"
- MR. KELLEY: raises concerns about messaging but ultimately "concur[s] with the recommendation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the 25bp cut direction — no one formally dissents. However, notable differences in preferred magnitude (25 vs 50bp), timing (now vs next meeting), and discount rate (25 vs 50bp). Jordan's outright opposition and Guynn/Poole's reluctance create moderate disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 83 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- The meeting opens with McDonough's extensive review of credit spreads, clearing systems, LTCM fallout, and rating agency concerns — spanning roughly 3 pages of transcript.
- Nearly every participant addresses financial market conditions as the primary reason for the meeting.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "after 50 years of looking at the economy on almost a daily basis, I must say that I have never seen anything like the current situation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions dominate this meeting. It is the raison d'etre for the intermeeting call. Discussion of financial stress rivals or exceeds discussion of real economic conditions.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 27 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a funds rate in the area of 4-1/2 to 5 percent seems close to where we want it to be at this stage" — implying 25-75bp more cuts from the post-meeting 5% rate.
- MR. MEYER: "if the story is that we feel we need to have a lower federal funds rate in the first half of next year, it probably would be good to get there a little faster"
- MR. BOEHNE: "the risks are on the downside and... we need to stay on or ahead of the curve"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly dovish. Multiple participants signal more easing ahead, and Greenspan explicitly states the target zone is lower than the current rate. The intermeeting nature of this cut itself signals urgency and a dovish lean forward.

**Minority views**: Jordan implicitly signals rates will need to rise again eventually.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BOEHNE: "a greater sense of uncertainty about the future on the part of business people"
- MR. GUYNN: "enormous uncertainty and uneasiness"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I have never seen anything like the current situation"
- MR. MEYER: "it adds a higher degree of uncertainty and reinforces the sense of downside risks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated — participants repeatedly cite it. However, the uncertainty is somewhat directional (downside risks dominate), not fully Knightian. There is reasonable conviction about the direction of risk even if magnitude is unclear. Greenspan's "never seen anything like this" raises the score, but it is specifically about financial conditions, not about fundamental unknowability.
