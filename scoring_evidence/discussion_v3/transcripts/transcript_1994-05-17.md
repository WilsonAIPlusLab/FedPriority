# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-05-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Individual participant statements from a go-around and policy discussion. Speakers are identified by name. This is an early 1990s transcript with a traditional go-around structure.

**Era context**: May 1994. The Fed had already raised rates three times in 1994 (February, March, April) and was in the midst of a tightening cycle. The economy was expanding solidly after the sluggish 1991-93 recovery. This meeting resulted in a 50bp increase in the funds rate (to 4.25%), combined with a 50bp discount rate increase -- the first "aggressive" move after three 25bp increments.

**Speaker count and views (go-around)**:
- MR. PARRY: Strong district growth outside California; small unused capacity; inflation could worsen in 1995.
- MR. BROADDUS: Strongest anecdotal conditions in 20 years; rising inflation expectations; upside risks to GDP and inflation; policy still accommodative.
- MR. FORRESTAL: Very positive district; price pressures "practically nonexistent" except construction; national forecast stronger than Greenbook; additional policy move needed.
- MR. MCTEER: Moderate growth; first mention of upward wage pressure since 1985; economy stronger than GDP numbers suggest.
- MR. BOEHNE: Positive but slower than nation; capacity constraints appearing; wage pressures well contained; moderate growth outlook.
- MR. STERN: Very healthy district; labor shortages of skilled labor; discounts diminishing; inflation risks on upside.
- MR. JORDAN: Fairly uniform and getting stronger; tail winds building; policy well behind the curve; need to tap the brakes; economy like first year of classic expansion.
- MR. MELZER: Expanding rapidly; momentum building; not comfortable with Greenbook slowdown forecast; inflation concerns reinforced.
- GOV. KELLEY: Policy was too accommodative; but skeptical of imminent inflationary flashpoint; unit labor costs favorable; productivity could surprise upward; economy sustainable.
- VICE CHAIR MCDONOUGH: District advancing nicely; CPI forecast above Greenbook; approaching NAIRU; question marks larger than usual.
- GOV. LAWARE: Less comfortable with consensus; first quarter GDP weak; psychological fragility; but slack reduction and export strength suggest further restraint needed.
- GOV. PHILLIPS: Expansion deeper and wider; sustainable strength; countervailing forces to upside risks; PPI up ~9% annualized; capital market volatility and fragility.
- GOV. LINDSEY: More inflation talk than in 2.5 years; demand moderate but supply-side uncertainty; risks of worsening inflation/growth mix.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Economy probably stronger than suspected; bubble being deflated but not eliminated; errors on inflation side more costly; supports 50bp; symmetric directive.

**Policy discussion**: Unanimous support for 50bp increase. Debate only about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive and whether the statement signals "neutrality." Broaddus and Melzer wanted asymmetric (hawkish). Jordan wanted even more aggressive action. All others supported symmetric.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral/slightly concerning. Current measured inflation is still moderate (~3% core CPI), but multiple participants express rising concern. Broaddus: "increased concern about the outlook for prices and inflation." Melzer: "consumer and producer price increases have accelerated." Phillips: PPI "up about 9 percent on an annual basis." Jordan: inflation risk from nominal GDP not decelerating. Forrestal: "don't see inflation reaching a peak until 1995." Kelley and Forrestal note price pressures "practically nonexistent" in some areas. Lindsey: "I've heard more [inflation talk] than I've heard in all the meetings I've attended in 2-1/2 years." Center of gravity: inflation not yet elevated but pressures building and concern rising. This is above the midpoint but not dramatically so. Direction: somewhat above 50. Score ~58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy clearly growing above trend. Multiple participants describe "very strong growth," "momentum building," capacity constraints appearing. Jordan: "monetary policy is well behind the curve"; likens it to first year of classic expansion with 5-6% real GDP growth potential. Melzer: "national economy continues to expand rapidly." Parry: "small amount of unused capacity." McDonough: "by end of year, potential GDP will be fully used up." Kelley skeptical of overheating. LaWare notes weak Q1 GDP but still sees slack reducing. Center of gravity: economy approaching or modestly above potential. Not yet clearly overheating but above trend. Score ~55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple reports of labor shortages, especially skilled labor. Broaddus: "shortages of 'good' labor." Stern: "continued reports about labor shortages, particularly of skilled labor." McTeer: first upward wage pressure since 1985. Boehne: wage pressures "well contained." Kelley: unit labor costs declining for four years. McDonough: "very close to the NAIRU." Unemployment had fallen ~1 percentage point over past year. Center of gravity: tightening but not yet critically tight. Approaching full employment. Score ~53.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Strong across the board. Retail sales strong (Forrestal: "exceeded retailers' expectations by a sizable margin"). Auto sales strong. Construction booming in many districts. Capital goods booming (Jordan: "capital goods overall are booming"). Business investment strong. Some concerns about consumer debt sustainability (Kelley). Score: above trend, solidly. Score ~62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Significant discussion of long-rate backup and market turmoil. Greenspan discusses at length the "bubble" being pierced, increased implied volatility, yield curve steepening. Phillips: "capital markets remain volatile and fragile"; "turmoil is not over." McDonough: financial services industry "malaise." However, credit spreads remain tight -- Greenspan notes junk spreads not widening, BAA spreads small. Bank loan demand rising. So conditions have tightened from very loose to moderately tighter, but not restrictive. The tightening is largely in long rates and market volatility, not credit availability. Score ~52.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for 50bp hike -- a step up from prior 25bp moves. Jordan wants even more. Broaddus and Melzer want asymmetric (hawkish) directive. Greenspan frames it as removing accommodation, not reaching restrictive territory. Several express view that more may be needed. This is clearly hawkish -- consensus for continued tightening via a larger move. Score ~72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. All support 50bp. Minor disagreement on symmetric vs. asymmetric directive (Broaddus prefers asymmetric; Jordan wants even more). Kelley, LaWare, Phillips have slightly less alarmed economic views than the hawks (Jordan, Broaddus, Melzer). But action is unanimous. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Score ~25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan devotes extensive remarks to the "bubble," implied volatility, portfolio unwinding, risk premiums. Phillips discusses capital market volatility and fragility at length. McDonough discusses mortgage-backed hedging dynamics. Jordan discusses yield curve behavior in crisis atmospheres. This is more than passing mention -- it's a moderate-to-prominent topic. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The statement says "substantially remove the degree of monetary accommodation." Greenspan: "I find it unlikely that we are near the end of this pattern." Several participants worry the statement might signal a pause; they explicitly don't want that. Broaddus and Melzer want to keep tightening bias. But Greenspan prefers symmetric directive and notes "we probably will not have to move before the next meeting." Forward signal: data-dependent with mild tightening lean. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: McDonough: "question marks are rather larger than usual." Phillips: wide range of forecasts feeding uncertainty. Greenspan: two credible scenarios. Stern: uncertainty about inflation outlook. Lindsey: "level of uncertainty about the economy and about the financial markets." Multiple participants mention uncertainty about how rate hikes will affect economy. This is somewhat elevated. Score ~45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both modestly above 50, coherent -- economy approaching/at potential, labor market tightening. OK.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both hawkish, with current action more decisively hawkish than forward signal (symmetric directive tempers forward lean). This makes sense -- big move now but uncertainty about future path.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says building but not yet high. 58 = modestly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 55: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says approaching/modestly above. 55 fits.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 52: Higher = tighter. Conditions have tightened from very loose but credit still available. 52 = roughly neutral. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MR. LINDSEY: "What struck me most at the table today was the talk of inflation. I've heard more on that than I've heard in all the meetings I've attended in 2-1/2 years."
- MR. BROADDUS: "We polled them on their inflation expectations and the vast majority expect some worsening in that situation."
- MR. PARRY: "We do not see the prospect for improvement in inflation from this point on, and we certainly don't rule out the possibility that the inflation numbers could begin to look even worse in 1995."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Price pressures are practically nonexistent, with the exception of construction and perhaps a little bit now in textiles."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation is running around 3% core and participants see building pressures -- not yet elevated but clearly trending upward with upside risks widely acknowledged. The level of concern exceeds what measured data alone would warrant, reflecting forward-looking worry.

**Minority views**: Forrestal, Kelley, and Boehne see current price pressures as still well-contained. This informs the lower bound of the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "monetary policy is well behind the curve"; economy like "first year of a classical recovery from recession" with potential for "5 to 6 percent real GDP growth."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "certainly by the end of the year, potential GDP will be fully used up. We will be at or very close to the NAIRU."
- MR. PARRY: "small amount of unused capacity in the economy."
- MR. MELZER: "national economy continues to expand rapidly and its momentum appears, at least to me, to be building."

**Center of gravity**: Economy is growing above trend and approaching potential. Most participants see remaining slack being rapidly absorbed, with several believing the economy is at or just above potential. A small minority (Kelley, LaWare) see more room before overheating.

**Minority views**: GOV. KELLEY sees more capacity via productivity gains; GOV. LAWARE emphasizes weak Q1 GDP and fragile psychology.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "shortages of 'good' labor" reported by small business and agriculture advisory committee; "labor markets tightening."
- MR. STERN: "continued reports about labor shortages, particularly of skilled labor in many markets."
- MR. MCTEER: "This is the first time since 1985 that people in our area are mentioning upward pressure on wages."
- MR. BOEHNE: "wage pressures are well contained."

**Center of gravity**: Labor market tightening meaningfully -- skilled labor shortages emerging, unemployment falling -- but broad wage pressures not yet evident. Approaching but not yet at full tightness.

**Minority views**: Boehne and Kelley emphasize that wage pressures remain contained, pulling the score lower. Broaddus and Stern see tighter conditions, pulling higher.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "Capital goods overall are booming; motor vehicle plants are going flat out."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "retail sales...exceeded retailers' expectations by a sizable margin. Auto sales are still quite strong."
- MR. MELZER: "inventory-to-sales ratio fell sharply as sales boomed."
- MR. PARRY: Intel building multiple major new plants; construction company head says "strongest period he has ever seen" in 30 years.

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment both running well above trend. Capital goods booming, retail strong, construction very active across multiple districts.

**Minority views**: GOV. KELLEY questions sustainability of consumer spending given high debt levels; GOV. LAWARE notes weak Q1 GDP.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "What we see is a very large increase in implied volatility in the long end of the market...but only a very modest increase in the yield spreads...Junk bond yield spreads are not moving up."
- GOV. PHILLIPS: "Capital markets remain volatile and fragile...the turmoil is not over."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Bankers...are now reporting modestly rising demand. Business loans...consumer and real estate lending continue to pick up."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened from the very accommodative stance of 1993 -- long rates backed up significantly, market volatility elevated, portfolio unwinding underway. But credit spreads remain compressed, bank lending is growing, and credit is available. Net: roughly neutral, having moved from loose toward neutral.

**Minority views**: Phillips sees continued fragility; Greenspan sees residual bubble conditions (loose elements remaining).

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Proposes 50bp increase; "we have the capability...to move more strongly than we usually do without the risk of cracking the system."
- MR. JORDAN: "I no longer feel that it's a case of simply backing further off the accelerator but that we may have to start contemplating tapping on the brakes fairly soon."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I've been a proponent of gradualism, but I believe we're at the point where we need to take more aggressive actions."
- All participants support the 50bp move. Multiple participants note more tightening likely needed.

**Center of gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus. Unanimous support for a 50bp hike (doubling from prior 25bp moves). Several participants believe more is needed. This is active, consensus tightening with forward tightening bias.

**Minority views**: No dovish dissent. Disagreement only about whether to signal more (Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan want asymmetric) vs. data-dependent pause (Greenspan's symmetric preference).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- All participants support 50bp. No formal dissents.
- Minor disagreement on directive language: MR. BROADDUS prefers asymmetric; MR. JORDAN wants even more aggressive action.
- Differing economic assessments: GOV. KELLEY more sanguine about inflation risks vs. MR. JORDAN who sees policy "well behind the curve."

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on action with minor differences on tactics and risk assessment. The 50bp move was truly unanimous, with disagreement limited to directive language and degree of forward lean.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extended discussion of the "bubble," implied volatility, portfolio unwinding, generic risk premium, mortgage-backed hedging dynamics. "We've caused a good part of it to dissipate, maybe even most of it, but it's very obvious...these markets are not about to simmer down very quickly."
- GOV. PHILLIPS: Lengthy discussion of capital market volatility, portfolio unwinding, market liquidity constraints. "The turmoil is not over."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: Discussion of mortgage-backed securities hedging linkage to long rates; financial services "malaise."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions and stability are a meaningful topic -- Greenspan devotes substantial remarks to it, Phillips provides extended analysis, and several others touch on it. It is not the dominant topic (economic conditions and inflation are) but it is a significant secondary focus.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I find it unlikely that we are near the end of this pattern, but I do think we are now becoming more data dependent." Prefers symmetric directive. Statement: "substantially remove the degree of monetary accommodation."
- MR. BROADDUS: Prefers asymmetric directive because symmetric "would tend to create the impression that we have reached a plateau."
- MR. MELZER: "I don't think this move is overdoing it...if we don't keep up with sustained increases in market interest rates...we run the risk of a policy that continues to be accommodative."
- MR. STERN: "I'm concerned about...a view in the markets now...that we are more clairvoyant than we are."

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish but data-dependent. The statement frames the move as removing accommodation, and Greenspan explicitly says "probably will not have to move before the next meeting" while also noting "it's unlikely we are near the end." Net: tightening lean but not committing to further action.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "At this particular time the question marks are rather larger than usual."
- GOV. PHILLIPS: "We see a little more diversity of opinion than we ordinarily do on the forecast front, and this is feeding into the capital markets."
- MR. STERN: "It raises uncertainty in my mind, if nothing else, about the price outlook."
- MR. LINDSEY: "There's a level of uncertainty about the economy and about the financial markets."

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Multiple participants explicitly note unusual uncertainty about the inflation outlook, the transmission of rate hikes to the economy, and financial market dynamics. However, participants are fairly confident about the direction of the economy (strong) and the appropriate policy response (tighten). The uncertainty is about magnitude and timing, not direction.
