## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (June 28–29, 2006). Individual speakers are clearly identified. The document contains two distinct segments: a **go-around/economic discussion** on Day 1 (June 28), and a **policy discussion** on Day 2 (June 29). Both are included in this scoring file and are scored together as participant views. Staff content is excluded. Speakers include: Pianalto, Hoenig, Guynn, Yellen, Minehan, Poole, Lacker, Geithner (Vice Chairman), Kohn, Bies, Warsh, Stern, Moskow, Fisher, Pianalto (again), Minehan (again), and Chairman Bernanke (summarizing/directing). Governor Kroszner is mentioned but his remarks do not appear in the available text (pages missing). This is a well-populated discussion with approximately 15+ distinct participant voices.

### 2. Era Context
June 2006: The Fed is near the end of a tightening cycle that began mid-2004. The funds rate has been raised 17 times; at this meeting the decision is whether to add a 25 bp hike (to 5.25%) or pause. Core PCE inflation has recently printed at elevated levels (~2.4% annualized, with 3-month rates near 3.4–3.8%). Housing is clearly slowing. GDP growth has decelerated in Q2 2006. This is broadly a "late-cycle" moment with competing concerns: inflation overshoot vs. risk of overtightening into a housing-led slowdown. I will score strictly from the text, not from era priors.

### 3. Direction Statements for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants overwhelmingly describe inflation as **above target and elevated**. Pianalto: "core inflation numbers have been drifting up"; Guynn: "greatest concern… is the inflation outlook"; Yellen: "pushing core inflation slightly above my comfort zone"; Kohn: "inflation has been running higher"; Bies: "core CPI prices and core PCE prices have grown, respectively, at 3.8 percent and 3.4 percent, twice their rate of growth in the previous three months"; Bernanke summary: "everyone considered these recent developments to be unwelcome." Debates exist about whether recent elevation is transitory, but participants score the current level, not the forecast. Score should be clearly above 50 — in the 65–72 range consistent with rubric's "Clearly above target; broadening pressures; 'persistent,' 'concerning.'" Multiple participants note broad-based acceleration beyond OER. I will score **68**.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe growth as **slowing toward or slightly below potential**. Geithner: "real GDP growth to follow a path pretty close to potential"; Yellen: "growth a bit below potential"; Bernanke summary: "output is slowing to something close to potential… falling somewhat below potential." A few (Warsh, Fisher) are more optimistic, seeing growth possibly above potential. Hoenig: "below-trend growth." Minehan: "small to nonexistent output gap." The center of gravity places the economy near to slightly below potential, consistent with a score of 46–50. Minehan's "small to nonexistent output gap" and Geithner's "close to potential" tilt toward 48. No one calls this a boom; nobody calls it a recession. I will score **48**.

**Polarity check**: Higher OUTPUT_GAP = more above potential. Score of 48 = very slightly below potential. Evidence says economy slowing toward/slightly below potential. Score matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor market described as **tight but not severely so**. Bernanke summary: "labor market remains reasonably healthy, difficult to find skilled workers, but few signs of wage pressures." Minehan: "hard to find the skilled labor they need." Hoenig: "Labor markets in much of our District remain tight." Poole reports J.B. Hunt has a "dire shortage of drivers" yet is only paying 3–4% annual raises. UPS pilot contract shows 3.25–3.5% wage increments. Yellen: "modest increases in wages." Overall: tight labor market with low unemployment (~4.7%), limited wage acceleration. This is squarely in the 53–65 "tight; hard to find workers; moderate wage pressure" range. Score **60**.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending **slowing** (especially lower-income, housing-related), but business investment **solid/rising**. Poole: Wal-Mart "several hundred basis points below plan since Easter." Guynn: "Consumption appears to be less strong." Score **28** — loose, accommodative, with spreads widening a touch but still very narrow historically.

Nobody advocates 50 bp; nobody advocates easing. The forward lean is explicitly uncertain — many want to leave August open (50/50), but the overall tilt is hawkish given inflation concern. Kohn: "inflation will remain the greatest risk to our objectives"; Lacker: "we may need to raise rates beyond today." Bernanke: "we cannot ignore the inflation side." ### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 68 ±6 | High Confidence**

**Evidence**:
