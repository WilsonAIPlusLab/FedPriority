## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (September 19–20, 2017). Day 1 is the economic go-around; Day 2 is the policy go-round. Speakers are identified by name throughout. The document is well-segmented with clear speaker attribution. There is also a short Q&A/discussion section between Bullard/Evans/Dudley and a staff briefer (Thomas) regarding SEP-implied r* and inflation model scenarios.

### 2. Era Context
September 2017: mid-cycle hiking period. The Fed had raised rates three times (Dec 2015, Dec 2016, Mar 2017, Jun 2017). The labor market was strong with unemployment near 4.4%. Core PCE inflation had been running well below 2% (around 1.3–1.4% on a 12-month basis). Hurricanes Harvey and Irma had just hit. The balance sheet normalization announcement was the primary policy action at this meeting (no rate hike). Era anchor: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN ~70–75 for hiking cycle, but inflation concerns moderated hawkishness.

### 3. Center-of-Gravity Identification
Speakers present in go-around or policy round: Bullard, Williams, Rosengren, Mester, Fischer, Bostic, Powell, Brainard, Harker, Kaplan, Evans, George, Kashkari, Dudley, Yellen. That is approximately 15 substantive participants.

Direction: Participants perceive inflation as **below target** — a substantial number call it "persistently low," well below the 2% objective. Core PCE running around 1.3–1.4%. Multiple speakers stress the shortfall is not merely transitory. Yellen revised down her core inflation projection. Mester, Williams, Brainard, Powell, Harker, Evans, Kashkari, Bullard, Bostic — nearly all participants note inflation is below target. A small minority (Rosengren, George) are relatively less concerned about persistence, but nobody argues inflation is near or above target (except hurricane-related gasoline price spikes which are acknowledged as temporary). Score must be well below 50. The language across speakers — "well below target," "1.3 to 1.4 percent," "five straight years of undershooting," "very large shortfall" — is consistent with the 30–38 range of the rubric ("below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation"). The level of concern is significant and multi-year in character.

**Direction Rule**: Participants see INFLATION_PRESSURE as below the midpoint because core PCE is running 1.3–1.4%, described by virtually all participants as "well below" or "significantly below" the 2% target, with multiple speakers noting five consecutive years of undershooting.

**Magnitude Rule**: "Well below" and "persistently low" and "5 years of undershooting" suggest moderately to clearly below. Not catastrophic deflation (not in 10–20 range), but genuinely below target with concern. Score ~32–38.

**Polarity Check**: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low inflation → low score. Consistent.

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 34 ± 6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
- Kashkari: "core 12-month PCE continues to be low at 1.4 percent. This is a large shortfall in relation to our target of 2 percent. We've now experienced low inflation readings for the past six months."

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous view that inflation is meaningfully below the 2% target, with the shortfall now spanning multiple years, placing the score in the disinflation/below-target range.

**Minority views**: Rosengren and George view recent softness as more likely temporary; George notes "it has remained within a range consistent with the longer-run goal." This informs the slightly wider uncertainty band (±6) but does not shift the center of gravity.
