# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-02-05 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Individual speakers identified. This is a two-day meeting (Feb 4-5, 1997). The document includes both a monetary aggregate range discussion (first half) and the policy discussion (second half). I will focus scoring on participant views about the economy and policy.

**Era context**: Mid-1990s expansion. Unemployment near 5.3%, economy growing above trend, inflation low but concerns about future acceleration. The "jobless insecurity" thesis of Greenspan is in play -- wages not rising as fast as Phillips curve models would predict. The stock market is surging (this is shortly after Greenspan's "irrational exuberance" speech of Dec 1996).

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently contained but with rising risk. Greenspan: "I can see no credible evidence of increasing price pressures" and "the estimated implicit price deflator...is unchanged, meaning the price change is zero." However, the majority is concerned about *future* inflation. Moskow cites Greenbook forecast of core CPI accelerating from 2.6% to 2.8% to 3.2%. The "inflation dragon nibbling" metaphor suggests creeping concern. The center of gravity is: inflation currently low/contained, but risks tilted upward. Current perception is slightly below or near target with upside risk. Direction: slightly below midpoint to at midpoint. Score around 44-48. The rubric says to score what participants perceive NOW. Current inflation is contained; the worry is forward-looking. Score ~45.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Multiple participants describe the economy as operating above potential. Parry: "the recent surge in economic activity has raised the level of output, which already appeared to be above its long-run potential." McDonough: "at the very high capacity usage the economy is experiencing." Greenspan mentions "capacity pressures." Minehan: "an economy that clearly was expanding at a pace beyond the long-term trend." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "above potential" language requires >50. Score ~56-58. The late 90s boom anchor is 57-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very strong consensus on tight labor markets. Greenspan: "labor market tightness, the crucial issue." Minehan: "labor markets that are tight by any measure." Meyer: "unemployment rate...more than 1/2 percentage point below their estimate of NAIRU." Jordan: "most of the metropolitan areas in my District have unemployment rates of around 4 percent or lower." Multiple speakers reference tight labor as a key concern. Unemployment around 5.3% vs estimated NAIRU of ~5.5-5.6%. Direction: clearly above full employment tightness. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75; this is 1997 and slightly less extreme. Score ~60-63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Minehan: "an economy with a lot of life, driven by consumer spending and business investment." McDonough references "very strong fourth quarter." Greenspan expects some payback/slowing in Q1 but overall economy expanding above trend. Minehan: "We do not see the sluggish residential investment picture that we thought we would." Direction: above trend. Score ~58-62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described as very loose/accommodative. Greenspan: "the financial markets look just too benign," "risk spreads, which are really quite narrow," "junk bond yield spreads against Treasuries" narrowing. Minehan: "a financing environment that obviously was conducive to excess" and "financial markets that are too one-sided and frothy." Greenspan discusses potential equity overvaluation. This all points to loose financial conditions. Direction: well below midpoint (loose). Score ~20-25.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The decision is to hold at "B" (unchanged) with asymmetric toward tightening. Greenspan explicitly says "we have to be prepared to move" likely at March meeting. Several participants (Parry, Minehan, Hoenig) wanted to tighten immediately (25bp). McDonough: "I now read it as meaning that we will tighten soon and probably at the March meeting." Broaddus: "we are going to need to move to a tighter policy posture...almost certainly...at the March meeting." The center of gravity is hold now but tighten very soon. This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish posture. Score ~60-64. The anchor for pre-liftoff 2015 is 40-50; this is more hawkish -- they are priming for imminent tightening, with some wanting to move today. Score ~62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: A few participants (Parry, Minehan, Hoenig) explicitly wanted to move today. The majority supported holding with strong asymmetry. No formal dissent recorded in this transcript segment, but 3 participants clearly preferred immediate tightening vs the majority who preferred waiting. There's also some philosophical disagreement (Kelley cautioning against prejudging, Stern noting costs of delay are small). Moderate differences. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan devotes substantial discussion to equity market valuation, risk spreads, junk bond spreads. His "irrational exuberance" theme continues here. McDonough discusses market conditions. Guynn discusses complacency. Greenspan: "the sharp narrowing of junk bond yield spreads...the level of equity premiums in the stock market." This is a notable amount of attention for the era. Score ~40-48. Anchor for 1993-96 typical is 1-10, but this meeting is clearly well above that given Greenspan's extensive equity valuation discussion. Score ~45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Greenspan: "we have to be prepared to move if we have to at the next meeting." McDonough: "we will tighten soon and probably at the March meeting." Broaddus: "we are going to need to move...almost certainly...at the March meeting." The Humphrey-Hawkins testimony is explicitly being designed to signal coming tightening. Direction: clear tightening signal ahead. Score ~70-75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is genuine uncertainty about the inflation outlook. Greenspan: "I do not seriously believe that this pattern can hold very much longer" but acknowledges it has persisted longer than expected. Meyer: "Uncertainty about the forecast, about the structure of the economy or specifically about the value of NAIRU, justifies...a more cautious approach." Kelley: "I am a bit haunted by the fact that we all saw emerging pressures...a year or 18 months ago, and here we are." Moskow: "Given the uncertainty." The NAIRU uncertainty is a major theme. Score ~40-48. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~44.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Both above 50, consistent -- economy above potential with tight labor markets. Labor slightly higher because labor tightness is more emphasized than overall output gap. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action (hold today). This divergence is expected and explained: they are holding now but signaling imminent tightening. Coherent.

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score 57. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I can see no credible evidence of increasing price pressures" and "the estimated implicit price deflator for the gross domestic product of the nonfinancial corporate sector is unchanged, meaning the price change is zero."
- MR. MOSKOW: "If we believe the Greenbook forecast, we will see core inflation accelerate from 2.6 percent last year to 2.8 percent this year and to 3.2 percent next year."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "My uppermost concern is the emergence of gradual, drip-by-drip inflation pressures."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants perceive current inflation as contained/low, but the majority sees rising risks ahead, placing the score just below the neutral midpoint for current pressure with meaningful upside uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Some participants (Parry, Minehan, Hoenig) perceive inflation risk as more imminent than Greenspan's characterization, widening the band slightly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "the recent surge in economic activity has raised the level of output, which already appeared to be above its long-run potential."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "at the very high capacity usage the economy is experiencing, potential noninflationary growth is really limited."
- MS. MINEHAN: "an economy that clearly was expanding at a pace beyond the long-term trend."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad consensus that the economy is operating modestly above potential, with above-trend growth and high capacity utilization, consistent with the late-1990s boom anchor range.

**Minority views**: Guynn suggests his region has "topped off," providing a modest counterweight, but this is a regional outlier.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "We see labor markets that are tight by any measure."
- MR. MEYER: "a decline in the unemployment rate to 5 percent, more than 1/2 percentage point below their estimate of NAIRU."
- MR. JORDAN: "Most of the metropolitan areas in my District have unemployment rates of around 4 percent or lower, and this has been the case for a couple of years."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-universal agreement that labor markets are tight, with unemployment below NAIRU estimates, difficulty finding workers widely reported, and wage pressures beginning to appear though still moderate.

**Minority views**: Greenspan argues the job-insecurity factor is suppressing wage growth despite tightness, but does not dispute the tightness itself.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 59 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "We see an economy with a lot of life, driven by consumer spending and business investment. We do not see the sluggish residential investment picture that we thought we would."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: references "a very strong fourth quarter" with expected payback in Q1.
- MS. MINEHAN: "an economy that clearly was expanding at a pace beyond the long-term trend and operating with constrained labor resources in a financing environment that obviously was conducive to excess."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending and business investment are seen as running above trend, driving the above-potential growth. Some expected Q1 payback from the strong Q4 is anticipated but not expected to fundamentally alter the picture.

**Minority views**: Guynn notes his region has topped off; Greenspan expects some moderation. But the overall thrust is above-trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the financial markets look just too benign" and "risk spreads, which are really quite narrow."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the sharp narrowing of junk bond yield spreads against Treasuries, the level of equity premiums in the stock market."
- MS. MINEHAN: "a financing environment that obviously was conducive to excess" and "financial markets that are too one-sided and frothy."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are perceived as very loose and accommodative, with compressed risk spreads, elevated equity valuations, and easy credit availability -- conditions multiple participants view with concern.

**Minority views**: None dissent from this characterization; the concern is broadly shared.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would argue for staying at 'B' today but with an asymmetry that is unlike that at the previous couple of meetings...it is a real asymmetry."
- MR. PARRY: "it is time to take an initial modest step in tightening policy, and therefore I would prefer a 25 basis point increase in the funds rate to 5-1/2 percent."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I now read it as meaning that we will tighten soon and probably at the March meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The majority favors holding today but with a strong tightening bias; three participants (Parry, Minehan, Hoenig) preferred immediate 25bp tightening. The consensus is firmly on the hawkish side of neutral, signaling imminent action.

**Minority views**: Parry, Minehan, and Hoenig wanted to move today (more hawkish). Kelley and Stern caution against excessive prejudging (slightly less hawkish). The spread justifies the +/-8 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: explicitly preferred 25bp tightening today.
- MS. MINEHAN: "I would align myself with President Parry's suggestion of a 25 basis point increase."
- MR. HOENIG: "I have some concerns about waiting" and "we now see at least a reasonable consensus that higher inflation is in prospect, and that is what we should act on."
- MR. KELLEY: cautioned against "an excessive amount of prejudging" future action.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Notable disagreement on timing -- three participants wanted to move immediately versus the majority favoring a hold with strong asymmetry. The disagreement is primarily about timing/pace rather than fundamental direction, keeping it moderate.

**Minority views**: The 3 pro-tightening-now voices are distinct enough to create meaningful tension without rising to formal dissent level.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Extensive discussion of equity market valuation models, earnings-price ratios, equity premiums, and junk bond spreads. "the financial markets look just too benign."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "that complacency very nervous-making and in particular its reflection in the sharp narrowing of junk bond yield spreads against Treasuries."
- MR. GUYNN: "I have been running into more and more complacency among business groups."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives moderate-to-significant attention, primarily driven by Greenspan's extensive equity valuation analysis and concerns about complacency. Several other participants echo the theme. This is notably above the 1993-96 typical range.

**Minority views**: The emphasis is somewhat dominated by Greenspan, though Minehan, Guynn, and McDonough also contribute.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we would have to prime the markets to anticipate that we might be moving quite soon" and "we have to be prepared to move if we have to at the next meeting."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I now read it as meaning that we will tighten soon and probably at the March meeting."
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think we are going to need to move to a tighter policy posture. I believe we will almost certainly have to do so at the March meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- the Humphrey-Hawkins testimony is being explicitly designed to prepare markets for imminent tightening, and most participants expect a March move barring significant economic weakening.

**Minority views**: Stern and Kelley express slightly more caution about pre-committing, but even they acknowledge the likely direction.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MEYER: "Uncertainty about the forecast, about the structure of the economy or specifically about the value of NAIRU, justifies...a more cautious approach."
- MR. KELLEY: "I am a bit haunted by the fact that we all saw emerging pressures that were clearly going to lead to an outbreak of inflation a year or 18 months ago, and here we are."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Discusses job insecurity thesis as explaining why standard Phillips curve models are off; acknowledges "I do not seriously believe that this pattern can hold very much longer" but also that predictions have been wrong before.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, driven by genuine confusion about why inflation has not accelerated despite tight labor markets. The NAIRU debate and structural uncertainty about the wage-price relationship are central themes. This goes beyond rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: Some (Parry) express more conviction that tightening is needed now, reflecting lower uncertainty. Others (Kelley, Moskow) emphasize the uncertain track record of recent forecasts.
