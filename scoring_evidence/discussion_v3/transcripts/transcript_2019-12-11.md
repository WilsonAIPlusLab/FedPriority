# FOMC Transcript Scoring — December 10–11, 2019

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the December 10–11, 2019 FOMC meeting. Speakers are individually identified (e.g., "MR. CLARIDA.", "MS. DALY.", "CHAIR POWELL."). The document is fully segmented. It has two sessions: Day 1 covers a framework review discussion (Fed Listens / HANK models) and the economic go-around; Day 2 covers the policy vote go-around. This is a very long, rich transcript (~4,200+ lines). Confidence will generally be High.

### 2. Era Context
December 2019 is year 11 of the post-GFC expansion. The economy was in a "mid-cycle adjustment" phase after three 25bp insurance cuts in July, September, and October 2019. Unemployment was at ~3.5%, a 50-year low. Core PCE inflation was running around 1.6%—persistently below the 2% target. Trade uncertainty from US-China tariffs had been the dominant risk factor. The Fed held rates steady (no change at this meeting). Financial conditions were loose/accommodative.

### 3. Directional Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants almost universally describe inflation as running below target. Core PCE at 1.6% for 2019 is the focal number. Multiple speakers (Daly, Evans, Kashkari, Bullard, Brainard, Williams) emphasize persistent below-target inflation and the risk of declining inflation expectations. Rosengren projects 1.9% next year; Daly projects 1.6% for 2019 and 1.8% for 2020. Mester notes the median PCE at 2.7% as an upside outlier, but the Committee centers on core PCE ~1.6%. Participants see inflation as **meaningfully below target** → score well below 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: GDP growth is described as "near trend" or "slightly above potential." Rosengren: "growth slightly above that of potential." Brainard: GDP growth "almost 2 percent" for the second half. George: "above-trend growth." Chair Powell: "GDP growth for the year at 2.2 percent, a bit above trend." Manufacturing is "in a recession"; business investment weak; but consumer spending solid. The center of gravity is slightly above potential but with cross-sectional weakness → score slightly above 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Overwhelming emphasis on the tight labor market. "50-year record-low unemployment rate" (Rosengren), "3.5 percent" (multiple speakers), "very tight labor market" (Rosengren, Evans, Kaplan, Barkin, George), employers having "difficulty finding workers" (Harker, George, Mester). Prime-age EPOP at a new cycle peak. The labor market is clearly tight; center of gravity is well above 50 → score in the 63–70 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is described as strong/solid across virtually all speakers. Rosengren: "strong consumption growth." Daly: "solid gains in jobs and income continuing to support consumer spending." Evans: "the consumer continues to do quite well." Brainard: "Consumer spending increased at a robust 3 percent in the third quarter." Business investment is explicitly weak/declining across nearly all speakers. Kaplan: oil cap-ex down 15% expected. Daly: "weakness in business investment and capital goods production." This is a split picture: very strong consumer, very weak business investment and manufacturing. Net consumption-investment at moderate-to-slightly-above trend, dominated by consumer strength → score around 53–56.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple speakers describe financial conditions as loose/accommodative. Brainard: "Every financial conditions index I consult is near the low end of its historical range." Barkin: "Markets seem to have responded well to our recent moves." Kaplan: "substantial rally along the Treasury curve," "historically cheap debt." The yield curve has steepened (uninverted after the 2019 cuts). Financial conditions are described as loose → score well below 50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants support alternative B (hold at 1.5–1.75%). No one advocates a rate cut or a hike at this meeting. The posture is "hold and wait" after three 2019 cuts, with a data-dependent bias. Kashkari notes "a decent chance we will need to make further cuts." Daly is open to further accommodation. Rosengren, Bostic, and Barkin want to preserve policy space. George did not support the three prior cuts but supports holding today. The center of gravity is hold/neutral with a mild dovish lean forward (open to cuts if needed, bar is high for hikes) → score around 43–47.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All participants support alternative B. The vote is unanimous (no formal dissents). However, there is notable back-and-forth on statement language ("near" vs. "returning to" vs. "centered on" for inflation language; inclusion/exclusion of "global developments and muted inflation pressures"). This is a spirited debate about wording but not about the policy decision itself. There are clearly distinct camps on statement language and frameworks (Evans, Kashkari, Daly, Brainard more concerned about persistently below-target inflation; Bostic, Barkin, Rosengren somewhat less alarmed; Rosengren worried about financial stability from excessive accommodation). A unanimous vote with meaningful substantive disagreement about statement language and future path → score in the 28–35 range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants discuss financial stability concerns, though it is not the dominant topic. Rosengren notes risk of "inflated asset prices"; Kaplan notes tight credit spreads and P/E expansion; Brainard raises "financial imbalances," "elevated risky corporate debt," "valuations of risk assets"; Barkin discusses "repricing of highest-risk debt"; George warns about lower-for-longer creating "financial imbalances." There is also technical discussion of repo operations and money market conditions (G-SIB surcharges, year-end repo). Moderate emphasis from several participants, but not the dominant concern → score around 35–45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward guidance is clearly "hold for now, data dependent." Multiple speakers write down a flat rate path for 2020 (Clarida, Daly, Kashkari, Williams, others). No one signals near-term rate hikes; several are open to further cuts if needed. Kashkari explicitly advocates a commitment not to raise rates until inflation sustainably returns to target. The signal is mildly easing-biased or neutral—not "tightening is coming" but not "we're about to cut." → score around 42–47.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is elevated uncertainty in several dimensions: trade policy, global growth outlook, measurement of inflation, the true u*, r*, and potential growth. Multiple participants raise the Tealbook alternative view on potential overstatement of employment gains. Several discuss uncertainty about whether current accommodation is sufficient. China Phase 1 deal uncertain; Hong Kong risks; multiple trade war fronts. This is elevated but not extreme uncertainty—"risks to the downside but less than in September." → score around 45–55.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Brainard says conditions are "near the low end of their historical range" (i.e., very loose). Therefore score should be LOW (well below 50). Confirmed.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Holding after three cuts with mild dovish forward lean → score slightly below 50. Confirmed.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish. Flat rate path, open to further cuts → score below 50, around 42–47. Confirmed.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = above potential. GDP slightly above trend → score slightly above 50. Confirmed.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (~52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~67): Labor tightness scores substantially higher than output gap. This is coherent — the labor market has run ahead of what simple output gap models would predict (NAIRU has proven lower than expected). Multiple participants discuss this explicitly (Daly, Williams, Powell).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~43): Both slightly below 50, both indicating a dovish-to-neutral lean. Consistent.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (~54) and OUTPUT_GAP (~52): Near-aligned. Consumer is strong, dragging overall output slightly above potential despite weak investment. Consistent.

### 6. Speaker Count for Center of Gravity

**Inflation below target (strong concern)**: Evans, Daly, Kashkari, Bullard, Brainard, Williams, Clarida (all explicitly note 1.6% is not satisfactory) — approximately 7 out of ~15 speakers very concerned.
**Inflation below target (moderate/acknowledge but less alarmed)**: Bostic, Barkin, Quarles, Harker, Bowman, Mester — acknowledge but less hawkish on the urgency.
**Labor market tight**: Near unanimous.
**Economy in good place**: Near unanimous framing of hold decision.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 36 ±6 | High Confidence**

Participants see inflation as clearly below target. The focal data point cited repeatedly is core PCE at 1.6% for 2019. Multiple participants describe a "persistent" miss.

**Evidence:**
- Daly (MS. DALY): "I project core PCE inflation will rise just to 1.6 percent this year and 1.8 percent in 2020, reaching 2 percent only in 2021."
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Core inflation of 1.6 percent is not okay."
- Bullard (MR. BULLARD): "Pressure on inflation remains muted, with the Tealbook now projecting core PCE inflation of just 1.6 percent for 2019 and 1.9 percent for 2020. In my view, this inflation outlook is unsatisfactory."
- Chair Powell: "We now forecast an increase of only 1.6 percent for 2019...there is a rising risk that inflation will fail to reach, let alone move beyond, 2 percent even during this long expansion."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation is running persistently ~40 bps below the 2% target, with a multi-year track record of undershoots. The construct "below target" is well below 50. Not deflationary (no price declines), but clearly in the disinflation/below-target range.

**Minority views**: Mester notes median PCE at 2.7% and cyclical core at 2.9%, and the Dallas trimmed mean near 2%. These suggest some alternative measures point higher, warranting a slightly wider uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 52 ±5 | High Confidence**

Participants describe the economy as growing slightly above potential. GDP running at ~2.2% versus most estimates of potential at ~1.8–2.0%. Manufacturing is in recession but the consumer-driven growth is slightly above trend.

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "The economy appears poised to deliver a stable outcome of growth slightly above that of potential and tight labor markets."
- George (MS. GEORGE): "I expect the economy will end this year with above-trend growth."
- Chair Powell: "The forecast now has GDP growth for the year at 2.2 percent, a bit above trend."

**Center of gravity**: Slight positive output gap. The above-potential growth language triggers the BRIGHT-LINE RULE: score > 50.

**Minority views**: Daly and others note weakness in business investment and manufacturing pulling down potential growth estimates, meaning the net gap may be smaller. Barkin warns of Q1 2020 downside. This keeps the score close to 50.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 67 ±6 | High Confidence**

The labor market is characterized as historically tight. Unemployment at 3.5%, a 50-year low, is cited by almost every participant. Employers report difficulty finding workers. This is unambiguously in the tight range.

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "Last week's employment report continued to confirm a strong economy, with higher payroll employment than expected, steady wage growth, and a 50-year record-low unemployment rate...continued difficulty hiring workers in a very tight labor market."
- George (MS. GEORGE): "We continue to hear from our District contacts that tight labor market conditions and difficulty finding qualified workers are constraining their hiring plans. More contacts report difficulty hiring or retaining workers compared with the previous year, with upward of 70 percent of our contacts indicating they increased wages."
- Chair Powell: "With the unemployment rate at 3½ percent, prime-age labor force participation on the rise, and payrolls running at 200,000 a month, the labor market is strong."

**Center of gravity**: Very low unemployment, widespread employer difficulty hiring, wage gains at low end. Consistent with the 53–65 range (tight) but leaning toward the higher end of that band given the 50-year unemployment low and widespread difficulty finding workers. Anchors suggest 65–75 for 1999–2000 and 2022–23; this market is comparable to 1999–2000 in some respects but wage growth is more moderate, hence 67.

**Minority views**: Williams questions whether "tight" is the right term without clear upward wage/inflation pressure. Kashkari and Williams note possible hidden labor supply (underemployed, job-dissatisfied workers). This tempers the score from the 70s.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 54 ±6 | High Confidence**

Consumer spending is strong; business investment is explicitly weak. The net position is slightly above trend, driven by households.

**Evidence:**
- Evans (MR. EVANS): "The consumer continues to do quite well...All of the directors pointed to the strong labor market as being the key foundation for household spending."
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Consumer spending increased at a robust 3 percent in the third quarter, and the latest indicators...continue to point to solid growth."
- Kaplan (MR. KAPLAN): "We believe the consumer will continue to be the primary underpinning of the U.S. economy."
- Daly (MS. DALY): "Ongoing weakness in business investment and capital goods production...concerning, because it tempers capital deepening." Kaplan: oil cap-ex down 15% expected.

**Center of gravity**: Strong consumer (above trend) + very weak business investment (below trend) = net position slightly above trend. Score ~54.

**Minority views**: Barkin notes PCE only grew at 1% in October; George anticipates moderation in household spending. These suggest the consumer strength may be less durable.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 28 ±7 | High Confidence**

Financial conditions are described by multiple participants as very loose/accommodative.

**Evidence:**
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Every financial conditions index I consult is near the low end of its historical range...Financial conditions are easing, and risk assets have done particularly well in the intermeeting period, with U.S. equities increasing 3 percent on net and investment credit spreads tightening a little further."
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "Monetary policy and fiscal policy remain accommodative, and financial conditions are supportive of the economy, as evidenced, for example, by the rising stock prices since our October meeting."
- Kaplan (MR. KAPLAN): "We continue to see...substantial rally along the Treasury curve...historically cheap debt."

**Center of gravity**: Conditions are at the low (loose) end of historical ranges. This places the score clearly below 50, in the "accommodative/loose" range. Score 28 is consistent with "loose; credit freely available; generally supportive."

**Minority views**: Rosengren and Brainard raise concerns about elevated risky corporate debt and triple-B debt vulnerabilities. Kaplan notes P/E expansion. These are financial stability concerns, but conditions are still loose overall.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 46 ±5 | High Confidence**

All participants support holding rates at 1.5–1.75% (alternative B). The stance is hold/neutral after three 2019 insurance cuts. The lean is mildly dovish forward (open to more cuts; bar for hikes is high).

**Evidence:**
- Kashkari (MR. KASHKARI): "I think there's a decent chance we will need to make further cuts." Also advocates "a commitment that we're not going to raise the federal funds rate until we get core inflation back to target on a sustained basis."
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "I would prefer to leave rates unchanged unless there's a substantial change in the forecast...I'm a bit concerned that any further easing will place us in a precarious position whenever the next downturn arrives."
- George (MS. GEORGE): "Although I did not support this year's rate cuts as the most appropriate path of policy, today's decision to hold rates steady is a prudent one."
- Chair Powell: "I see it as appropriate to stand pat at this meeting. I have written down a flat path to 2020."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold, but with a forward lean that is mildly dovish (open to cuts, bar for hikes is very high until inflation clearly returns to target). Kashkari at the dovish end; George/Rosengren most neutral/willing to preserve space.

**Minority views**: No one advocates a hike. George's concern about the prior cuts introduces a very mild hawkish minority tilt.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 28 ±7 | High Confidence**

The vote is unanimous. There is substantive disagreement about statement language ("near" vs. "returning to" for inflation), about the inclusion of "global developments and muted inflation pressures" in the statement, and about the framework going forward. However, these are tactical/communication disagreements rather than fundamental disagreements about the rate decision.

**Evidence:**
- Chair Powell: "It splits the Committee right down the middle" [on the statement language].
- Evans (MR. EVANS): "I will vote 'yes' on the current language...I don't like the 'near 2 percent' language."
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "I read this [language] as being very 'dovish'"—suggesting different interpretation of the same language.
- George (MS. GEORGE): "Although I did not support this year's rate cuts..." (retrospective dissatisfaction with prior decisions, but supports today's hold).

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous vote with meaningful wording debates and distinct policy-preference groups. No formal dissent. Consistent with 25–35 range ("broad agreement: minor emphasis differences; 0–1 dissents").

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 40 ±8 | High Confidence**

Several participants discuss financial stability. The Day 1 framework discussion is dominated by HANK models and distributional considerations (which includes some financial stability overlap). Day 2 has explicit financial stability comments from Brainard (most detailed), Rosengren, Kaplan, and George. Also, extensive technical discussion of money markets, repo operations, and G-SIB surcharges.

**Evidence:**
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "I am increasingly concerned about financial imbalances in this late-cycle environment...We see signs of this, clear signs, in current elevated risky corporate debt and the valuations of risk assets."
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "Allowing inflated asset prices or, more generally, an economy with rising imbalances can increase the likelihood of incurring the high-unemployment periods that are so costly to communities."
- Kaplan (MR. KAPLAN): Concern about P/E expansion and tight credit spreads; Brainard references triple-B rated investment-grade debt vulnerabilities.
- Year-end repo and money market discussions (Rosengren, Kashkari, Kaplan, Williams, Brainard) are substantive but operational rather than stability-crisis focused.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate discussion — several participants address it meaningfully, but it is not the dominant topic. Score 40 reflects "moderate discussion; several participants address it meaningfully."

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 43 ±5 | High Confidence**

Forward guidance signals "hold for now, patient, data-dependent." No one signals imminent tightening. The median SEP has a flat rate path for 2020. Several participants are explicitly open to further cuts if inflation disappoints.

**Evidence:**
- Chair Powell: "I have written down a flat path to 2020...We believe the current stance of monetary policy is appropriate and will remain so as long as incoming information about the economy remains broadly consistent with our outlook."
- Daly (MS. DALY): "I've written down a flat path of the policy rate—no change over the next two years."
- Kashkari (MR. KASHKARI): "We're going to be patient and actually let inflation climb to target." Advocates commitment not to raise rates until inflation sustainably at target.
- Vice Chair Williams: "A current assessment of appropriate policy sees us staying at the current target range through next year."

**Center of gravity**: Flat rate path forward; dovish lean. Not "easing is coming" but "we won't raise until inflation clearly improves." This is in the 41–50 range (lean dovish to balanced/hold with mild easing bias). Anchor reference: 35–45 for 2019 mid-cycle cuts; this meeting is at the end of that episode, slightly toward hold but not fully neutral.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 50 ±8 | High Confidence**

Uncertainty is elevated relative to normal but is not described as extreme. Key uncertainties: trade policy trajectory, global growth, inflation path, u* and r* estimates, potential employment data revision, Hong Kong/UK election.

**Evidence:**
- Chair Powell: "We simply lack sufficient data to reliably estimate the persistence of the positive effects we are clearly seeing."
- Williams (VICE CHAIR WILLIAMS): "The analysis...points to a potential fragility of the labor market...this would be another signal that the economy may be somewhat more vulnerable to an adverse shock than we think."
- Bullard (MR. BULLARD): "I see tariff uncertainty as likely to remain high over the forecast horizon." Multiple participants note global uncertainty.
- Daly (MS. DALY): "The risks to my global outlook, including trade policy and soft global growth, which I now see as tilted still to the downside but less so than in the time of my September projections."

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty on several fronts (trade, inflation path, employment data), but less acute than the September meeting when trade war fears peaked. Multiple sources of uncertainty balance out to elevated but not extreme. Score 50 is slightly generous; the evidence leans toward 46–52.

## Optional Dimensions

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS

**Score: 38 ±7 | High Confidence**

Multiple participants discuss inflation expectations explicitly, noting they are "at or near record lows" for survey measures and that market-based measures remain low.

**Evidence:**
- Clarida (MR. CLARIDA): "The CIE index is now at an all-time low level...it's running somewhat below that [pre-crisis anchor] now." "Expected CPI inflation five years forward is running at 1.9 percent, which would imply...a PCE inflation rate of 1.6 percent."
- Kashkari (MR. KASHKARI): "Survey measures of expectations remain at or near record lows. Market-based measures also remain low and, at face value, point to future inflation below 2 percent."
- Williams (VICE CHAIR WILLIAMS): "Expectations could be coming anchored at a level below our longer-run objective."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation expectations are anchored but at the low end — slightly below target. Score 38 reflects "somewhat below target anchor" without being unmoored.

### INFLATION_HOUSING

**Score: 55 ±8 | Moderate Confidence**

Discussed by a few participants but primarily in the context of housing as a positive economic indicator (rates sensitive recovery) rather than inflationary concern.

**Evidence:**
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "The decline in mortgage rates since late 2018 appears to have spurred a welcome rebound" in housing.
- Daly (MS. DALY): "Residential investment picked up in the previous quarter."
- Bowman (MS. BOWMAN): "Activity in the housing sector has begun to pick up more noticeably."
- Day 1 framework: Daly mentions "housing inflation" as a cost of hot economy discussed in Fed Listens; Harker notes high house prices make it harder for new buyers.

**Center of gravity**: Housing is recovering due to rate cuts, with prices somewhat elevated but not characterized as surging. Score 55 reflects modest above-neutral housing price pressure with improving activity. Only moderate confidence as most discussion is about housing activity rather than housing inflation explicitly.

### CREDIT_STRESS

**Score: 30 ±8 | Moderate Confidence**

Investment-grade credit spreads are tight, but concerns about triple-B rated debt are mentioned.

**Evidence:**
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Investment credit spreads tightening a little further. Market participants continue to highlight risks associated with triple-B-rated investment-grade debt. With a triple-B segment now 2.8 times the size of the high-yield market, market participants are concerned about the capacity of that market to absorb so-called fallen angels without dislocations."
- Kaplan (MR. KAPLAN): "Credit spreads for more highly-rated debt are very tight."

**Center of gravity**: Credit conditions are generally loose (spreads tight) but with vulnerability in lower investment-grade. Score 30 reflects "loose but with pockets of concern." Below 50 = loose/low stress, consistent with the evidence.

### EQUITY_STRESS (N/A)
Participants note equity markets have rallied and P/E ratios are elevated but do not express particular stress/crash concern. The discussion is more about valuations being high (which would score low) without specific crash-concern commentary. Not meeting the threshold for scoring.

### BANKING_STRESS (N/A)
Evans notes "credit quality is good" with banks seeing some increase in delinquencies but "levels are still very low." Not prominently discussed.

### TREASURY_STRESS

**Score: 35 ±10 | Moderate Confidence**

Repo market operations and year-end funding concerns are discussed at length by Rosengren, Kashkari, Kaplan, Brainard, Williams, and others. The September 2019 repo market spike is in the background. Year-end liquidity is a concern but not an acute crisis.

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren (MR. ROSENGREN): "People can't finance other assets—how comfortable we are with that kind of outcome over year-end...if large hedge fund portfolios have trouble rolling over the year-end, that we'll once again be in another cycle of hearing about concerns about liquidity."
- Brainard (MS. BRAINARD): "Funding conditions over year-end remain a key focus. Several contacts indicated dealers have been proactively warning their clients to expect significant reductions in balance sheet availability."
- Williams (VICE CHAIR WILLIAMS): "There is some small probability that we would start seeing illiquid conditions in the T-bill market."

**Center of gravity**: Year-end money market stress is elevated relative to normal but operations are currently working well. Score 35 = "somewhat elevated; technical stress monitoring but not severe."

### DOLLAR_CONCERN (N/A)
Not prominently discussed as a distinct concern by 2+ participants.

### PRODUCTIVITY

**Score: 48 ±8 | Low Confidence**

Clarida mentions productivity has "picked up to a 1.5 percent pace over the past three years—not great, but up about ½ percent." Daly discusses potential downside risks from weak capital goods/investment sector. Limited direct discussion.

**Evidence:**
- Clarida (MR. CLARIDA): "Real wages continue to rise broadly in line with underlying productivity, which itself has picked up to a 1.5 percent pace over the past three years—not great, but up about ½ percent from the previous three years."
- Daly (MS. DALY): Research suggesting durable goods sector decline could shave ½ pp off potential growth over the next decade.

**Center of gravity**: Modest productivity pickup but below historical average. Score 48 = slightly below historical trend. Low confidence given limited discussion.
