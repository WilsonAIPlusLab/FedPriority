# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1994-08-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers the go-around economic discussion and the policy discussion. Speakers include: Broaddus, Forrestal, Parry, Hoenig, Boehne, Melzer, McTeer, Stern, Lindsey, Phillips, Blinder, Kelley, McDonough, LaWare, Jordan, Minehan, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
August 1994. The Fed had already raised rates 125bp since February 1994 (from 3.00% to 4.25%). The economy was in a mid-cycle expansion with strong growth, near capacity, and inflation around 3% CPI. This meeting resulted in a 50bp hike. The 1994 tightening cycle is one of the classic preemptive tightening episodes.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above desired levels but not yet accelerating sharply. Current CPI around 3%. Multiple participants describe upside risks: Forrestal notes businesses "for the first time in a long time" are able to pass through price increases. Melzer notes the GDP deflator rose 2.8% in H1 1994 vs. 1.2% in H2 1993. Phillips notes core CPI "edged up to 3.1 percent." Parry sees "inflation accelerates in 1995." Blinder argues inflation is roughly contained at 3.1% and the NAIRU may be lower, so less pressure than others think. Kelley notes unit labor costs still very low. The center of gravity is: inflation at or slightly above 3%, with risks skewed to the upside, but not yet clearly accelerating. Direction: modestly above target (where "target" in 1994 was implicitly around 2-3%). Score should be in the 55-62 range. The language is "risks on the up side," "pipeline pressures," "beginning to pass through price increases" -- this is "somewhat elevated" territory.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly every participant describes the economy as at or slightly above potential. Phillips: "growth in the last four quarters has been above potential," "at low unemployment, perhaps at the NAIRU." Parry: "most if not all of the unused capacity in labor and product markets has been used up." Greenspan: "few signs of aging" in the recovery, "considerable set of momentums," strong orders, net business formation. Blinder is the main dissenter, arguing "not quite fully employed, though close" with a lower NAIRU estimate. Kelley: "we are either at or very, very near capacity." The bright-line rule: multiple speakers say "above potential" or "at capacity" -- score must be >= 50. Center of gravity is slightly above potential with strong momentum. Score: 55-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Forrestal reports labor shortages "not only for skilled people but for semi-skilled and unskilled" with signup bonuses for unskilled workers. Jordan: companies report "difficulty of hiring unskilled or semi-skilled workers," temp agencies have unfilled positions. Phillips: "at low unemployment, perhaps at the NAIRU." Stern: "jobs are plentiful in many, many areas." McTeer's economists say "we are at full employment." Blinder argues the NAIRU might be as low as 5.6-5.8% (old basis) so current 6.1% may not be quite full employment. The center of gravity: at or slightly above full employment, with emerging shortages at the lower skill levels. Score: 55-60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending showed some weakness in Q2 (Broaddus: "slowing in growth of real consumer spending" after three strong quarters). But this weakness is partly attributed to supply constraints (autos). Business investment is strong: Phillips notes "continued strength in business investment," "order books are showing considerable depth." Greenspan: capital goods markets expanding, unfilled orders accelerating. Lindsey argues consumer cash flow is stretched and consumer spending will slow. Inventories are being built intentionally. On net, the picture is solid but with consumer side moderating and business investment very strong. Score: 55-60 (above trend but consumer side tempering).

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Phillips discusses financial markets at some length: "stock market showing a great deal of resilience," bond market "withstood considerable volatility," "no major accidents." Banks are "aggressively financing the expansion." Hoenig reports strong loan demand, loan-to-deposit ratios rising sharply, banks easing credit standards, heavy competition for loans. McTeer's bankers report aggressive competition. Melzer notes business and consumer loans up 17% and 23% annualized in July. Jordan reports double-digit C&I loan increases. The picture is one of easy, loose financial conditions with aggressive bank lending. Score: 25-30 (loose conditions, credit freely available, banks competing aggressively).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Near-unanimous support for 50bp hike. Greenspan proposes 50bp. Every single participant who speaks in the policy round supports it. Even Blinder, the most dovish voice on economic assessment, "enthusiastically" supports 50bp. No one argues for no change or just 25bp. Several came in favoring "D" (presumably a larger move). The package includes a symmetric directive and a statement signaling a possible pause. Direction: clearly hawkish, consensus for significant tightening action. Score: 70-73. Not higher because the 50bp comes with a signal to pause and assess, and the symmetric directive is intentionally less hawkish than asymmetric.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Remarkably broad consensus. Every participant supports 50bp. No formal dissents. The differences are minor: Blinder has a somewhat different economic assessment (lower NAIRU) but still supports the action; Jordan came in "on the fence" but agrees; Melzer came in favoring a potentially larger move but supports. The range of disagreement is narrow -- it's about nuances of the accompanying statement, not the action. Score: 12-18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Phillips provides the most extended financial market discussion (stock market resilience, bond market volatility, currency markets). Greenspan mentions the bubble being "defused" after the May move, securities moving from "weaker hands to stronger hands." Hoenig and McTeer discuss bank lending aggressiveness. But financial stability is not a major anxiety -- it is mentioned as context, not as a threat. Score: 20-28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan explicitly crafts a statement: "these actions are expected to be sufficient at least for a time." Blinder emphasizes "oars are out of the water." Phillips says "our next move may be next spring and may even be an easing move." McDonough: "This may well be all we have to do." The symmetric directive reinforces this. But Melzer and others note the need to retain flexibility. The forward signal is: we've done a lot, probably pausing now, data-dependent. This is mildly hawkish in that they just hiked 50bp and are in a tightening cycle, but the forward signal itself is "wait and see" / neutral. Score: 55-60. They are not signaling further hikes, but not signaling easing either. The 50bp hike itself is the current action; forward is a pause signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Some uncertainty but not elevated. Jordan questions the output-inflation relationship. Stern notes capacity-price relationships have deteriorated since the 1980s. Greenspan acknowledges uncertainty about whether 50bp or 100bp more is needed (probability estimates of 1/3 to 40%). Blinder emphasizes NAIRU uncertainty. But most participants express relatively confident views. The tone is more "risks on the upside" than "we don't know." Score: 35-40.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both slightly above 50, consistent -- economy at/slightly above potential with tight labor markets. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Diverge somewhat -- current action is strongly hawkish (50bp hike) but forward signal is "pause and assess." This divergence is explained and intentional: the Committee is doing a large move now specifically to avoid needing to do more later.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modestly elevated with upside risks. Score 58 = modestly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says at or slightly above. Score 56. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are loose, banks lending aggressively. Score 27 = loose. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous 50bp hike. Score 71. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FORRESTAL: "most of them are reporting some input price pressures and, for the first time in a long time, I've heard that now to some extent they are able to pass those price increases through."
- MR. MELZER: "the implicit GDP deflator rose by about 2.8 percent at an annual rate in the first half of 1994 compared to 1.2 percent in the last half of 1993, which is a marked increase indeed."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The core CPI for the second quarter actually edged up to 3.1 percent... at best, we are stalled out on inflation."
- MR. KELLEY (minority/moderating): "the forecast for unit labor costs is still very low. Even out through 1995 it never gets much above 2 percent."

Center of gravity: Inflation around 3% CPI, somewhat above participants' comfort level, with pipeline pressures building and upside risks dominant. Not yet accelerating sharply but "somewhat elevated."

Minority views: Blinder sees less pressure (NAIRU lower, so less inflationary gap). Kelley notes low unit labor costs as restraining factor. These widen the uncertainty band slightly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The growth in the last four quarters has been above potential... We are at low unemployment, perhaps at the NAIRU if it really exists."
- MR. PARRY: "most if not all of the unused capacity in labor and product markets has been used up."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "despite the longevity of this recovery, there really are few signs of aging... this recovery has a considerable set of momentums."
- MR. BLINDER (minority): "not quite fully employed, though close" -- argues NAIRU is lower (5.6-5.8% old basis), so economy may be slightly below full employment.

Center of gravity: Economy operating at or modestly above potential, with strong momentum. Most participants see capacity as fully utilized.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FORRESTAL: "they are experiencing labor shortages, not only for skilled people but for semi-skilled and unskilled. In our District at least, it's across the board and some firms are actually paying signup bonuses even for unskilled people."
- MR. JORDAN: companies report "difficulty of hiring unskilled or semi-skilled workers"; temp agencies "have a lot of available positions that nobody applies for."
- MR. STERN: "Jobs are plentiful in many, many areas both geographically and across different industries and skills."
- MR. BLINDER (minority): argues unemployment at 6.1% may still be slightly above a NAIRU of 5.6-5.8%.

Center of gravity: Labor market at or slightly beyond full employment. Shortages emerging especially at lower skill levels, but not yet severe enough to drive broad wage acceleration. Forrestal: "It hasn't translated so far into a lot of wage increases, but I'm sure that will come."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "slowing in growth of real consumer spending, after all, follows three consecutive quarters of very robust growth... at least some of this weakness reflects supply constraints."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "continued strength in business investment. The order books are showing considerable depth... significant additions to payroll."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "expansions in the capital goods markets... unfilled orders are accelerating on the up side."
- MR. LINDSEY (bearish on consumer): "the increase in household spending was much more than the increase in the actual cash that they are seeing" -- argues consumer spending unsustainable.

Center of gravity: Consumer spending moderating from very strong levels but still supported by employment; business investment very robust with deep order books. Net position is above trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "loan growth of about 14 percent in our District... banks want to make loans. We see some heavy competition throughout the District for loans."
- MR. MELZER: "business and consumer loans up at annual rates in July of 17 and 23 percent respectively."
- MR. MCTEER: "competition for loans is getting very aggressive and the large banks are so aggressive on pricing and credit standards, that they wonder if they aren't making some loans that should not be made."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Banks are aggressively financing the expansion."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose. Bank lending is growing rapidly, credit standards have been eased, competition for loans is aggressive. Despite 125bp of rate hikes already, credit conditions remain very accommodative.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 71 +/-4 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "another upward notch in rates is clearly called for... I think we clearly demonstrated that the bubble for all practical purposes had been defused, and that we needn't worry about larger increases at this stage."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I'm completely supportive of what you are suggesting" (50bp + discount rate).
- MR. BLINDER: "I'm also supportive of the package... I not only support but support enthusiastically the suggestion of the Chairman."
- MR. MELZER: "I came in favoring alternative D coupled with at least an increase of 50 basis points in the discount rate."

Center of gravity: Near-unanimous support for a 50bp hike with a matching discount rate increase. This is a decisive hawkish action, moderated by the accompanying pause signal and symmetric directive.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Every participant who speaks in the policy round explicitly supports the 50bp proposal. No dissents, no reservations.
- Minor differences in emphasis: Blinder's lower NAIRU estimate, Jordan's skepticism about output-inflation causation, Melzer initially favoring alternative D.
- The discussion about the accompanying statement shows nuanced differences but not about the action itself.

Center of gravity: Near-consensus. Broad agreement on both the economic assessment (strong economy, upside inflation risk) and the policy action (50bp hike). Differences are about nuance and forward positioning, not the decision.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The markets have been buffeted significantly this year. The stock market... is showing a great deal of resilience... The bond market has withstood considerable volatility... we haven't had any major accidents."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the bubble for all practical purposes had been defused... the moving of securities from weaker hands to stronger hands has now made it far less likely that a 50 basis point move at this point would shake the markets."
- MR. HOENIG and MR. MCTEER discuss bank lending aggressiveness as a developing concern.

Center of gravity: Financial conditions discussed as context for the policy decision (can markets handle 50bp?) rather than as a primary worry. Phillips gives the most attention to markets. Bank lending aggressiveness noted by several but treated as an economic signal, not a stability concern.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "these actions are expected to be sufficient at least for a time to meet the objective of sustained noninflationary growth."
- MR. BLINDER: "our oars are out of the water. That does not mean that we have thrown the oars overboard."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "This may well be all we have to do in the adjustment of policy that began the fourth of February."
- MR. MELZER: cautioning to retain flexibility: "We really can't know today what could transpire, and we have to have flexibility to evaluate incoming data and move further if necessary."

Center of gravity: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- they are in a tightening cycle and just hiked 50bp, but the explicit forward signal is "pause and assess." The symmetric directive and accompanying statement aim to signal that the tightening may be sufficient. Not neutral (they just acted forcefully) but not signaling further imminent hikes.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 36 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I frankly don't know whether or not the staff's estimate of a 100 basis point increase is the right number. I do know that there is a not insignificant probability that 50 basis points may be enough... I think it's a third, maybe 40 percent."
- MR. STERN: "I'm uncertain what this means for inflation because the relationships between capacity and price or wage pressures don't seem to me to be as robust as they were in the early 1980s."
- MR. PARRY: "estimates of slack are subject, as we all know, to considerable uncertainty."

Center of gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty about the amount of additional tightening needed and about the inflation-output gap relationship, but participants are fairly confident in their economic assessment. The uncertainty is more about calibration of policy than about the direction of the economy.
