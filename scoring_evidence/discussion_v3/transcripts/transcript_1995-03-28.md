# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-03-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker statements from a go-around and policy discussion. Speakers include: McTeer, Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Hoenig, Jordan, Boehne, Stern, Melzer, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips, Lindsey, Yellen, Blinder, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-populated meeting with roughly 17 participants contributing.

### Era Context
March 1995: The Fed had been tightening through 1994 (300bp of hikes from Feb 1994 to Feb 1995). The economy was growing above potential through 1994, with capacity utilization at highs. By early 1995, signs of slowdown were emerging in interest-sensitive sectors (housing, autos, retail). CPI inflation was around 2.6-2.8% in 1994 but core CPI had ticked up to about 4.2% annualized in Jan-Feb 1995. The dollar was weakening. The Mexican peso crisis was ongoing.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target and a continuing concern. Multiple speakers note core CPI has "crept up" (McDonough), with Jan-Feb core inflation averaging "about 4.2 percent, up substantially from the 2.8 percent rate we saw in 1994" (Melzer). Minehan sees "upside risk in the degree of inflationary pressure." Parry says "inflation will increase a bit this year and next." Hoenig expects "core level of the CPI is likely to move up to the 3-1/4 percent range." Boehne notes "some increases in inflation in 1995" but contextualizes them as possibly cyclical. Phillips says "pressure at the commodity and intermediate materials levels." However, Yellen notes "surprising and continued moderation in compensation growth and subdued growth in unit labor costs." Boehne says wage and price pressures in his district "remain subdued and are a nonproblem." Center of gravity: inflation is modestly elevated and expected to rise further, but wage pressures remain contained. This is above target but not alarmingly so -- in the 55-63 range. Direction: participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Score ~60.

Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated and rising. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy was running above potential through 1994 but is now slowing. Parry: "the economy most likely will remain above levels of labor and capacity utilization that are consistent with steady inflation, despite a slowing of growth." Jordan: "The Fourth District is operating at a very high level of economic activity, though clearly the rate of change has slowed." McDonough: "Capacity utilization is still above 85 percent." Broaddus: "capacity utilization index back up again... highest level since 1979." Phillips: "the economic slack currently is either significantly diminished or perhaps the economy is operating over capacity." But multiple speakers note clear slowing: retail sales down, housing weakening, auto sales declining. Greenspan: "growth of the economy is moving down in the direction that we had hoped." The center of gravity is that the economy is still above potential but the gap is narrowing as a slowdown takes hold. Score ~55-57. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language present, so score must be >50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Several districts report very tight labor markets. Moskow: "labor markets remain very tight... Virtually all of our contacts in the personnel supply industry report difficulties finding workers." Melzer: "Parts of the District continue to report very tight labor market conditions" with unemployment at 4.5%. Stern: "labor remains in quite short supply in most of the District." Hoenig: nonfarm jobs up 4.2%. McDonough: "employment below 5-1/2 percent." Phillips: "both product and labor markets are fairly taut." However, wage pressures are not materializing strongly -- Boehne says wages are "a nonproblem," Yellen notes "continued moderation in compensation growth." Center of gravity: labor markets are tight but wage growth is restrained. Score ~60-63.

OUTPUT_GAP (55-57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60-63) -- these are reasonably coherent; labor tightness a bit higher because labor market lags the output cycle and some districts report acute shortages even as the economy begins to slow.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending is clearly slowing: retail sales down, auto sales down, housing starts declining. Multiple speakers emphasize this. But business fixed investment remains strong: Broaddus notes "continued strength of business fixed investment," Phillips says "the climate remains favorable for continued investment," and capital spending has momentum. Manufacturing remains robust in many districts. Net: consumer side is below trend, investment side above trend. Weighted together, the economy is at or slightly below trend on spending. Score ~48-50. The slowdown in consumption is offset by continued investment strength.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Bond rates have come down (Broaddus: "bond rates have come down"), stock market is "very strong indeed" (McDonough), mortgage rates declining. Greenspan worries about "significant increases in stock and bond prices" creating wealth effects. The market rally suggests conditions have loosened. McDonough: "strong stock market and the strong bond market would seem to reflect the notion in these markets that we have in fact achieved the soft landing." Lindsey compares it to 1993 bubble conditions. This points to conditions being on the loose side -- markets are easing despite the Fed's tightening. Score ~35-38 (somewhat loose). The dollar weakness is a concern but financial conditions domestically are loosening via markets.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for "alternative B" (no change in rates). But the key debate is symmetric vs. asymmetric toward tightening. Many favor asymmetry toward tightening: McDonough, Hoenig, Moskow, Boehne, Broaddus, McTeer, Phillips, Kelley (preference), Minehan, Parry (whose first preference was actually to tighten), Jordan. Those favoring symmetry: Stern, Yellen, Blinder, Lindsey (argued symmetric for strategic reasons). Greenspan himself leans marginally toward asymmetry. Minehan "did not come to this meeting with a predisposition in favor of staying pat" -- she wanted to tighten. Parry's "first preference would be for a small increase in rates." The center of gravity is: hold but with a tightening bias. This is a lean hawkish posture -- "hold steady with tightening bias." Score ~57-60.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. The debate over asymmetry toward tightening IS the forward signal. Most participants expect that the next move, if any, will be up. Greenspan: "I think the likelihood of moving them higher is sufficiently greater than moving them lower." Multiple speakers express concern that further tightening may be needed. But no one is calling for immediate action (except Parry/Minehan implicitly). Score ~60-63 -- mild tightening bias forward.

HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62) -- these align reasonably. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because the discussion of asymmetry specifically concerns the forward posture.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: While the vote was unanimous for alternative B, there was notable disagreement on symmetry vs. asymmetry. Parry preferred to actually tighten. Minehan came in wanting to tighten. Blinder/Yellen/Stern/Lindsey preferred symmetry while the majority preferred asymmetry. The policy action itself commanded consensus, but there were genuine differences about the appropriate forward lean. Score ~35-40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan explicitly discusses bubble risks: "the real danger is that things may get too good... we were beginning to get wealth effects that were unsustainable and potentially creating bubbles." Lindsey references the 1993 bond market bubble. McDonough worries about market euphoria. Melzer mentions the weak dollar and "very severe financial repercussions." Lindsey discusses Japanese banking problems, German economy, debt ceiling risks. This is more than passing mentions -- several participants engage meaningfully with financial stability themes. Score ~30-38.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple speakers emphasize uncertainty. Boehne: "we need more than the usual amount of humility about our assessment of the outlook." Kelley: "I am agnostic about the course of the upcoming quarters." Lindsey: "I am a scared agnostic." Parry: "there is always considerable uncertainty in such forecasts." Stern: "the confidence bands around these forecasts... are very, very wide." Blinder notes soft landings are "rarely achieved." But several speakers are fairly confident in the broad contour of a slowdown. Score ~45-50 -- elevated but not extreme uncertainty.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Labor tightness somewhat higher -- explained by labor market lagging the output slowdown. Districts report acute shortages even as GDP growth slows. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward tone slightly more hawkish -- the asymmetry debate is specifically about the forward signal while the current action (hold) is consensus. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "In January and February core inflation averaged about 4.2 percent, up substantially from the 2.8 percent rate we saw in 1994."
- MR. HOENIG: "I agree that the core level of the CPI is likely to move up to the 3-1/4 percent range this year. But if the economy comes in stronger than expected and if the dollar continues to weaken, I think inflation could move higher."
- MS. MINEHAN: "we see a gradual uptick... We believe that the core trend is rising... there is a bit of upside risk in the degree of inflationary pressure."
- MS. YELLEN (minority): "surprising and continued moderation in compensation growth and subdued growth in unit labor costs."

**Center-of-gravity**: Most participants perceive inflation as modestly above target and likely to rise further in 1995, though wage pressures remain contained, preventing alarm.

**Minority views**: Yellen and Boehne are more sanguine about wage restraint keeping inflation in check; Melzer and McDonough see higher inflation risk (CPI 3.4-3.7% in 1996).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "the economy most likely will remain above levels of labor and capacity utilization that are consistent with steady inflation, despite a slowing of growth in real GDP."
- MR. BROADDUS: "industrial production was strong enough in February to push the capacity utilization index back up again. It is now at the highest level since 1979."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "growth of the economy is moving down in the direction that we had hoped... The extent of the weakness clearly is not pervasive; it is still relatively spotty."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is still modestly above potential -- capacity utilization high, employment strong -- but the gap is narrowing as interest-sensitive sectors slow.

**Minority views**: Blinder and Yellen shade risks slightly to the downside (more slowdown than expected); Kelley/Hoenig see upside risk to growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor markets remain very tight. Help wanted ads continue to climb... Virtually all of our contacts in the personnel supply industry report difficulties finding workers to meet the needs of their customers."
- MR. MELZER: "at 4.5 percent, unemployment in major District states remains well below the national average. Parts of the District continue to report very tight labor market conditions."
- MR. STERN: "labor remains in quite short supply in most of the District."
- MR. BOEHNE (counterpoint): "there really is an absence of wage and price pressures. They just remain subdued and are a nonproblem."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are tight with low unemployment and worker shortages in many districts, but wage growth remains surprisingly subdued.

**Minority views**: Yellen suggests the NAIRU may be lower than 6%, implying tightness is overstated; Moskow and Hoenig see acute shortages.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 49 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "retail activity clearly has weakened since the beginning of the year; manufacturing also has moderated... continued strength of business fixed investment, which is a forward-looking indicator."
- MR. MOSKOW: "overall retail sales growth in the District has slowed from the fourth-quarter pace... The District's manufacturing sector outside autos continues to expand at a robust pace."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the climate remains favorable for continued investment at least in the near term... considerable momentum from 1994."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending has clearly weakened (retail, autos, housing), but business fixed investment remains robust and manufacturing is still expanding. Net position is approximately at trend.

**Minority views**: Blinder sees component-by-component downside risk; Kelley/Hoenig see continued upside strength.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the downside risks are basically coming from the possibility of significant increases in stock and bond prices... we were beginning to get wealth effects that were unsustainable."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "The stock market is very strong indeed and the long bond rates have declined... the strong stock market and the strong bond market would seem to reflect the notion in these markets that we have in fact achieved the soft landing."
- MR. KELLEY: "Nominal rates all along the yield curve are well off their highs, and real rates are certainly down from where they were."

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite 300bp of tightening in 1994-95, financial conditions have loosened considerably via rallying bond and stock markets, creating accommodative conditions that concern several participants.

**Minority views**: Melzer notes the weak dollar as a constraint; Minehan notes loan growth moderating in some areas.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "my first preference would be for a small increase in rates. However, I could support alternative B because of the significant uncertainties."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I did not come to this meeting with a predisposition in favor of staying pat... most of the work we have done... suggested that further interest rate increases were necessary."
- MS. YELLEN (dovish side): "the economy looks to me like it is not too hot, it is not too cold, it is just right... the risks are balanced and that tends to call for symmetry."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "there is no alternative to 'B'... I lean marginally, tentatively, unsurely toward asymmetry."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold, but majority favors asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Two participants (Parry, Minehan) would have preferred to actually tighten. Overall lean is mildly hawkish.

**Minority views**: Yellen, Blinder, Stern, and Lindsey prefer symmetric (balanced) posture; Parry/Minehan want to tighten now.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Policy action (no change) was unanimously supported, but the symmetry/asymmetry debate revealed genuine disagreement about the forward lean.
- MR. PARRY preferred to actually raise rates.
- MS. MINEHAN came wanting to tighten.
- MR. BLINDER: "I am certain that I am not certain" about what asymmetry even means -- fundamental disagreement about the framework itself.
- MR. LINDSEY warned asymmetry without follow-through could backfire (1993 precedent).

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates, but moderate differences on the forward posture. The split on symmetric vs. asymmetric reveals genuine philosophical differences.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the real danger is that things may get too good. When things get too good, human beings behave awfully... we were beginning to get wealth effects that were unsustainable and potentially creating bubbles."
- MR. LINDSEY: "the fear of a bubble was mentioned at this table. We actually had a pretty good bubble develop in the bond market, and we paid a fairly significant price for it in financial market instability later."
- MR. LINDSEY: "the financial sector is in much more precarious shape around the world... Japanese banking industry... German economy."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives meaningful but not dominant attention. Greenspan and Lindsey discuss bubble risks and international financial fragility. A few others reference dollar/market euphoria. It is a secondary theme, not primary.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think the likelihood of moving them higher is sufficiently greater than moving them lower."
- MR. HOENIG: "I would very strongly support asymmetry toward tightening and would encourage us to move sooner rather than later."
- MS. MINEHAN: "I really do think that there is more room to move interest rates up before we stop doing so."
- MS. YELLEN (counterpoint): "over this next intermeeting period... the risks are balanced and that tends to call for symmetry."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- the majority of participants indicate the next move is more likely to be up than down, though data dependence is emphasized.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 48 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "we need more than the usual amount of humility about our assessment of the outlook... we could be surprised either on the up side or the down side."
- MR. KELLEY: "I am agnostic about the course of the upcoming quarters."
- MR. LINDSEY: "I am a scared agnostic."
- MR. STERN: "the confidence bands around these forecasts, if one does it rigorously, are very, very wide."
- MR. BLINDER: "the reason soft landings are rarely achieved has to do with... People understate swings, forget about lags."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated -- several participants explicitly call out unusual difficulty in reading the outlook, with genuine two-sided risks. But there is reasonable confidence in the broad slowdown contour.
