# FOMC Scoring: 1995-02-01 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. The meeting spans two days (January 31 - February 1, 1995). The first major section is a lengthy debate on inflation targeting legislation (Neal/Mack bill), which is a theoretical/institutional discussion, not an assessment of current conditions. The second section covers the Mexico peso crisis and ESF/swap line arrangements. The economic go-around and policy discussion occur on Day 2, starting with President Parry.

### Era Context
February 1995: The Fed has been in a tightening cycle since February 1994, raising the funds rate from 3% to 5.5%. The economy grew about 4% in 1994. Unemployment has fallen to 5.4%, well below the staff's 6% NAIRU estimate. CPI inflation is running around 2.7-3%, considered somewhat above the implicit target. The Mexican peso crisis is unfolding. The committee is debating whether to raise 50bp further.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target and rising. Parry: "the economy now has attained levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation" and expects "a deterioration in the inflationary environment in 1995 and 1996." Moskow: "CPI inflation to rise perceptibly to ranges in the neighborhood of 3-1/2 percent by the second half of 1995." Minehan: "our own expectations are that inflation will be a bit more of an issue than the Greenbook suggests." Melzer: "our forecast envisions...the CPI inflation rate increasing to about 3-1/2 percent." Stern: "uncomfortable about the inflation outlook." McDonogh: "CPI at 3.3 percent Q4-to-Q4." Greenspan: participants are worried about inflation being above acceptable levels. Blinder is less worried, noting "we have just experienced a good inflation surprise in the fourth quarter." Yellen acknowledges both scenarios. The center of gravity sees inflation currently somewhat contained but with clear upward risks given the tight economy. Direction: above the neutral midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" language, concerns about future pickup. Score range: 57-63.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Overwhelming view that the economy is operating above potential. Parry: "levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation." Moskow: "economy continues to grow faster than its potential." Melzer: "more rapidly than can be sustained, with a four-quarter growth rate through the end of last year of about 4 percent." Hoenig: "momentum...leaves overall growth well above potential." Greenspan: "the data are uniformly very strong" in capital goods, though some "tentative signs" of slowing. Phillips: "the economy is operating at or above capacity." McDonogh: "rate of economic growth in the system that is going to create more inflation." Blinder and Yellen see possible slowdown but acknowledge economy is above potential. Direction: clearly above potential. Magnitude: "above potential," "unsustainable pace," 4% growth vs ~2.5% potential. Score range: 58-64.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very tight. Unemployment at 5.4%, well below the 6% NAIRU. Moskow: "labor markets remain quite tight. In many metropolitan areas, the reported unemployment rate is below 4 percent, including 3-1/2 percent in metropolitan Detroit." Stern: "labor shortages in many parts of the District." Melzer: "labor markets are the tightest they have seen in years." Broaddus: "some labor shortages and at least a few signs of upward pressure on wages." Boehne: "labor markets in the District have tightened noticeably." Jordan: "average workweek last year was about 2 hours longer." Counterpoint: Jordan notes manufacturing wages rose only 1.1%. Phillips: ECI shows "little wage pressure." But level of tightness is clearly above full employment. Direction: above the 50 midpoint. Score range: 60-66.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Business investment is very strong (Greenspan: "backlogs continuing to rise," "forward commitments continue to stretch out"). Consumer spending is showing some signs of weakness (Yellen: "surprising and unanticipated slowdown in retail sales in November and December"; Lindsey: "consumer is simply overextended"). Housing showing some softening. Auto sector showing early moderation. Overall the economy grew at 4% in 1994. Most participants expect moderation but from a very strong level. Direction: above trend. Score range: 57-63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Despite 300bp of tightening, financial conditions are not perceived as tight. Greenspan: "there is very little evidence that monetary tightening is constraining debt flows," "C&I loans are just extraordinary," "we are still getting some easing in credit terms." Bond market functioning normally. Credit card solicitations increasing. Banks not capital constrained. Blinder notes the real funds rate is in a reasonable range. Yellen references Taylor rule suggesting funds rate should be ~5.1%. Quality spreads are modest (Greenspan). Direction: slightly below neutral to neutral. Score range: 38-45.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Strong consensus for a 50bp hike. Greenspan proposes 50bp on both funds rate and discount rate with symmetric directive. Melzer, Minehan, Broaddus, Moskow, Boehne, Hoenig, Stern, Parry, Phillips, McTeer, Kelley, McDonogh, Jordan all support. Blinder reluctantly agrees (would have preferred to wait 2 weeks but does not dissent). Yellen also reluctantly supports (associates with Blinder's analysis but votes yes). Lindsey supports despite domestic reservations, citing international risks. Parry would prefer asymmetric (hawkish) language. McDonogh suggests this may be last rate increase. Several emphasize need to be "forward-looking" on the way down too. Direction: clearly hawkish - hiking 50bp. But with symmetric directive and many noting this may be last hike. Score range: 65-70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. Blinder and Yellen expressed strong preference to wait but explicitly chose not to dissent. Parry wanted asymmetric language but could "live with symmetry." The policy vote was unanimous. On the Mexico vote, Melzer, Broaddus, Lindsey, and Jordan expressed significant reservations. On the inflation targeting discussion, views were sharply divided ("split down the middle" per Greenspan). For the policy action itself: broad agreement with tactical differences. Score range: 28-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The Mexico crisis dominates a huge portion of the meeting. Extended discussion of ESF, swap lines, warehousing, systemic risks. Greenspan discusses dollar vulnerability, exchange market vigilantes. Lindsey discusses consumer debt overextension. Phillips mentions Orange County, Bankers Trust. Greenspan: "one of the successes of our policy to date is that we have taken the degrees of instability that one can envisage in stock prices down to a much reduced level." McDonogh: securities industry earnings down 50-80%, layoffs. The Mexico discussion is arguably the single biggest topic of the meeting. Direction: prominent attention. Score range: 55-65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan explicitly proposes symmetric directive, suggesting this could be the last hike: "it is by no means evident that...there will be another tightening of policy." McDonogh: "reasonably likely in my view that this will be the last interest rate increase." Minehan: "I hope when we see things begin to slow down that we will be as forward-looking on the way down as we have been on the way up." Yellen: envisions "only this 50 basis point increase" and does not see another 150bp as the market expects. Parry: "additional tightening probably will be needed." Jordan: "we are forced to err on the side of tightness whether this is to be the last tightening move or not." Center of gravity: current action hawkish but forward signal is near-neutral to mild tightening bias. Score range: 55-62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Notably elevated. Yellen: "my level of uncertainty about where things are headed is higher than at any previous time over the last six months," "the potential forecast error at this point is extremely high." Greenspan notes "tentative signs, not necessarily persuasive" of slowdown. Kelley outlines a wide spectrum of possible outcomes. Boehne: "moderating growth is still a forecast with the usual amount of uncertainty." Mexico adds geopolitical uncertainty. Blinder discusses wide ranges of model estimates. Multiple participants discuss whether the slowdown is real or wishful thinking. Direction: clearly elevated above normal. Score range: 50-58.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Both above 50, both indicating above-potential economy. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Current action is hawkish (50bp hike) but forward tone is more balanced/symmetric. This is explained by the symmetric directive and multiple participants suggesting this may be the last hike. The divergence is reasonable and explicitly discussed.

### Speaker Count
Economic go-around speakers: Parry, Minehan, Moskow, Boehne, Broaddus, Stern, Melzer, Hoenig, McDonogh, Jordan, McTeer, Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, Yellen, Greenspan (~17 speakers). Very well-covered.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Parry: "the economy now has attained levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation, to say nothing of progress toward lower inflation"
- Moskow: "we expect CPI inflation to rise perceptibly to ranges in the neighborhood of 3-1/2 percent by the second half of 1995"
- Melzer: "most private forecasters expect little improvement on inflation over the next two years...Blue Chip report predicts 3-1/2 percent inflation"

Center of gravity: Participants see inflation as modestly above an acceptable level (running ~2.7-3%) with significant upward risk given the tight economy. Not yet accelerating at the consumer level, but widely expected to rise.

Minority views: Blinder notes a "good inflation surprise in the fourth quarter" and sees inflation outlook as "pretty satisfactory." Phillips questions whether capacity measures are correct.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Moskow: "the economy continues to grow faster than its potential"
- Melzer: "the U.S. economy continues to grow more rapidly than forecasters had expected and likely more rapidly than can be sustained, with a four-quarter growth rate...of about 4 percent"
- Greenspan: "the economy has not shown very much in the way of a slowdown...the data are uniformly very strong"

Center of gravity: Virtually unanimous view that the economy is running above potential, with 4% growth in 1994 vs ~2.5% potential. Some tentative signs of slowing but not yet convincing.

Minority views: Blinder and Yellen see greater possibility of imminent slowdown, but even they acknowledge the economy appears above potential currently.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Moskow: "labor markets remain quite tight. In many metropolitan areas, the reported unemployment rate is below 4 percent"
- Stern: "labor shortages in many parts of the District"
- Melzer: "contacts throughout the Eighth District report that labor markets are the tightest they have seen in years"

Center of gravity: Unemployment at 5.4% is well below the 6% NAIRU. Multiple districts report shortages, tight labor markets, below-4% unemployment in metro areas. However, wage pressures remain surprisingly contained (ECI shows little pressure, Jordan notes manufacturing wages up only 1.1%).

Minority views: Some note the puzzle of tight labor markets without matching wage growth, suggesting possible structural changes.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 59 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Greenspan: "backlogs are continuing to rise...forward commitments continue to stretch out...profit figures remain quite extraordinary"
- Yellen: "surprising and unanticipated slowdown in retail sales in November and December"
- Lindsey: "the consumer is simply overextended. Of the roughly $250 billion increase in personal consumption expenditures...44 percent was put on installment credit"

Center of gravity: Business investment is uniformly strong with rising backlogs and robust profits. Consumer spending is showing mixed signals -- strong through most of 1994 but softening at year-end. Housing beginning to weaken. Autos showing moderation. Net position is above trend but with some cracks appearing.

Minority views: Lindsey and Yellen emphasize downside consumer risks. Greenspan and others emphasize sustained capital goods strength.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Greenspan: "it is very difficult to find any evidence that monetary tightening is constraining debt flows...C&I loans are just extraordinary...we are still getting some easing in credit terms"
- Greenspan: "quality spreads within the financial markets also attest to the fact that the forward risk premiums implicit in capital investment are quite modest"
- Minehan: First District banks showed "no relaxation of credit standards...relatively stable loan demand"

Center of gravity: Despite 300bp of rate hikes, financial conditions remain relatively accommodative. Credit is flowing freely, bank lending is robust, credit terms are actually easing. The bond market is reasonably well-behaved. Conditions are not yet restrictive.

Minority views: Minehan notes First District banks are more cautious. McDonogh reports financial sector stress in New York (securities earnings down 50-80%, layoffs).

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Greenspan: "raising both the funds rate and the discount rate by 50 basis points makes the most sense"
- Unanimous vote for 50bp hike
- Greenspan: "I think we will be truly symmetric if we raise the federal funds rate to 6 percent"

Center of gravity: Strong consensus for 50bp hike. Most participants see continuing inflationary risks warranting action. However, the symmetric directive and signals this may be the last hike moderate the hawkishness somewhat.

Minority views: Blinder and Yellen would have preferred to wait 2-3 weeks but chose not to dissent. Parry wanted asymmetric (more hawkish) language.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Blinder: "I have been wrestling a great deal over the question of whether this is an important enough difference to merit a dissent. I finally decided it is not."
- Yellen: "I do not intend to dissent...the differences that we have largely concern tactics and not strategy"
- Unanimous vote on rates; contested votes on Mexico swap/warehousing

Center of gravity: No formal dissents on monetary policy. Notable tactical disagreements (Blinder, Yellen prefer to wait; Parry wants asymmetric language) but strategic agreement. Deeper disagreements on Mexico involvement (Melzer, Broaddus, Lindsey, Jordan express concerns).

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Extended Mexico crisis discussion dominates large portions of both meeting days (ESF, swap lines, warehousing, systemic risk)
- Greenspan on dollar: "The United States is just barely investment grade...I don't think we have much leeway on the down side"
- Greenspan: "we have taken a lot of the bubble out of the market...we have taken the degrees of instability that one can envisage in stock prices down to a much reduced level"

Center of gravity: Financial stability concerns are prominent, driven primarily by the Mexico crisis and its potential contagion effects. Dollar vulnerability is a key policy consideration. The Orange County situation and financial sector stress in New York also discussed.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Greenspan: "it is by no means evident that if this cracking that we have seen continues...there will be another tightening of policy"
- McDonogh: "reasonably likely in my view that this will be the last interest rate increase"
- Yellen: "on the basis of current information, I am envisioning only this 50 basis point increase"
- Parry: "additional tightening probably will be needed in the future"

Center of gravity: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- the committee just hiked 50bp and chose a symmetric directive. Most participants suggest this may be the last hike, but data dependence is emphasized. A few (Parry) see more tightening ahead.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Yellen: "my level of uncertainty about where things are headed is higher than at any previous time over the last six months...the potential forecast error at this point is extremely high"
- Greenspan: "tentative signs, not necessarily persuasive but definitely beginning to appear"
- Kelley outlines wide spectrum from "economy slowing now" to "roaring along unimpeded at 4 percent plus"

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven by conflicting signals -- strong data vs. early hints of slowdown, Mexico crisis, unknown NAIRU. Multiple participants explicitly flag high uncertainty about the growth trajectory.
