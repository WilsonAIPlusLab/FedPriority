### 2. Era Context

May 4, 2004. The FOMC has been holding the federal funds rate at 1% for nearly a year (since June 2003). Prior statements had used "considerable period" and then "patience" to signal accommodation would continue. By this meeting, data have improved enough that participants expect imminent tightening — the debate is whether to telegraph this with "measured pace" language. This is the pivotal meeting where the easing-bias forward guidance is replaced with a mild tightening bias.

I state this macro context ONLY to orient the reading. I will score solely from what participants say in this document.

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants describe inflation as **below target but with risks shifting toward balance**. Ferguson: "inflation is relatively low but it might be picking up, though we aren't really sure." Gramlich: accommodation removal conditioned on "inflation being low and resource use being slack" — confirming current inflation is low. Kohn: "Inflation is lower" [than in 1994, justifying less aggressive tightening]. Broaddus: "I believe that we still have credibility for low inflation. This is not late 1993 or early 1994." McTeer: notes the risk assessment moved from "asymmetric to the downside" (deflation risk) and now shows "risks to price stability have moved into balance." The statement language read into the record: "risks to the goal of price stability have moved into balance." Net assessment: inflation is still low/below target but the deflationary fear has been set aside and upside risks now acknowledged. Score will be in the 40–44 range — below 50 but rising toward balance.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Economy still **below potential with slack, but the gap is closing**. Kohn: "the output gap is disappearing but is disappearing slowly." Ferguson: "economy that is still working hard to get rid of slack." Greenspan: notes a downside risk scenario involving "more slack in this economy than we now anticipate" — treating it as a low-probability tail, not the base case. Gramlich: statement conditioned on "resource use being slack" — confirms slack is present. The Bright-Line Rule: "slack remains" language → score MUST be < 50. Score: approximately 38–41.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor market still **below full employment, slack remains**. Ferguson: economy "still working hard to get rid of slack." Kohn (inferred from available text): awaiting convincing labor market improvement before tightening. Minehan: "I think we may need to move sooner than the markets now expect" — implying data are improving but the market's current expectation is not yet for very soon. Multiple references to "resource use slack" confirm labor market has not yet tightened. Score: approximately 36–40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Economy characterized as on **solid expansion path** — above trend, which is why tightening is being contemplated. Minehan: "I think we may need to move sooner than the markets now expect. But that is going to depend largely on the strength of the incoming data." Guynn: "I wish we were in a position to move today" — this implies current data already justify moving, though the timing is not yet ripe. Greenspan: discusses whether intermeeting move might be warranted if data accelerate. General atmosphere: the economic expansion has proceeded enough to trigger the tightening debate. Score: approximately 54–57.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are **loose / accommodative**. The rate is at 1% (emergency low). Kohn: concerned markets will "build in a 1994-type tightening" if "measured" not used — implying current conditions are loose and the worry is an abrupt tightening shock. Ferguson: markets have priced in only "a modest tightening probably starting sometime in August" — modest expectations consistent with current looseness. Greenspan: if the statement causes markets to tighten MORE, that would be inadvertent "collateral damage" — meaning market tightening from a low baseline is the risk. The policy rate at 1% with accommodative stance = loose conditions. Score: approximately 25–32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: **Lean hawkish** — hold at 1% but the entire discussion is about signaling imminent tightening. Key evidence:
- Rate held at 1% (no change → not hawkish action)
- "Patience" dropped → removes easing commitment
- "Measured pace" language added → signals tightening ahead
- Risk assessment shifted to balanced

The formal statement language, read into the record: "At this juncture, with inflation low and resource use slack, the Committee believes that policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured." This is the forward signal. Multiple participants comment on it:

- Geithner: "overt asymmetric bias" in the statement, conditioned in four ways

This is a clear shift from easing bias ("patience") to a mild tightening bias. Rubric range 60–75 = "mild tightening bias or 'begin normalization' language." Score: approximately 58–63.

However, the DIRECTION of tightening is fairly clear. The uncertainty is about magnitude and timing. Score: approximately 40–46.
