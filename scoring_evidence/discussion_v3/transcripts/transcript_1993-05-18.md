# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1993-05-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified by name. It covers both a go-around of regional/national economic reports and a policy discussion. The structure is characteristic of early 1990s FOMC transcripts.

### Era Context
May 1993: The U.S. economy is in a sluggish, uneven recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate has been at 3% since September 1992. Unemployment is around 7%. The key surprise is a recent uptick in inflation (core CPI running near 4-4.5% annualized in early 1993), which puzzles participants given the slack economy. The Clinton administration's fiscal proposals (tax increases, health care reform) are creating substantial business uncertainty.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above what they expected and above target. Nearly every speaker addresses it as the dominant concern. Broaddus: "core CPI rate...has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate over the first months of the year and that really bothers me." Stern: "my principal concern...is the inflation situation. I am not as sanguine as the Greenbook that inflation is going to decelerate." Mullins: "the inflation bear has been sighted." Lindsey argues inflation could be even more persistent due to redistribution effects. However, several participants (Keehn, Forrestal, Boehne, Phillips) note that anecdotally they don't see price pressures at the business level, suggesting the CPI numbers may overstate the underlying trend. The center of gravity is that inflation is moderately above target and no longer declining -- participants are troubled but split on whether it's a blip or a trend. Direction: above midpoint. CPI running at 3-3.5% (above the 2-2.5% goal participants had), with core CPI spiking to 4.5% annualized early in the year. This is "somewhat elevated" to "clearly above target." Score: 58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants broadly describe the economy as growing at a moderate pace but below potential. Multiple speakers note "sluggish" growth, "modest" recovery, manufacturing operating "below capacity" (Hoenig). Kelley describes "eight quarters...basically positive" but "moderate upturn." Mullins says growth is between 2.5-3.5%. The first quarter was weak (~1% GDP). Nobody describes the economy as running hot or above potential. Multiple references to "slack in the economy" (Stern, Kelley). Boehne: "growth is very modest, very modest indeed." Greenspan: "slack markets." Direction: below midpoint. Score: 38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 7%, down from 7.7% peak but still elevated. Every speaker who addresses employment notes continued layoffs, restructuring, and reluctance to hire. Keehn: "Major companies...say they will continue to reduce their employment and will produce more products with fewer people." Phillips: "The economy is growing enough to produce some jobs but hardly enough...to accommodate even the population growth." Jordan: "no sentiment to increase employment." Mullins notes payroll growth improving but from a low base. Direction: below full employment. Score: 35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Auto sales are described as holding up well (Keehn, Syron, Mullins). Housing starts are positive. But retail sales are "uneven" (Keehn), "negligibly" up (Boehne). Business investment is being held back by fiscal uncertainty -- heard from nearly every president. Jordan: business loan demand picking up but driven by precautionary motives. Equipment sectors mixed -- agricultural equipment strong, defense contracting. Overall: slightly below trend, moderate. Direction: slightly below midpoint. Score: 43.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants note improving credit conditions. Keehn: "a decided change in the attitude about lending by banks. People tell me...banks have become more aggressive." Forrestal: "Banks are reporting higher loan demand." However, long-term rates have been rising (25 basis points on the long bond). Mullins notes "the only component of the current environment that is stimulative or conducive to growth is the capital market environment and in particular the long bond rate." Syron notes strong inflows into bond and junk bond funds. The fed funds rate is 3%, a historically low level. Overall conditions are accommodative to neutral, not tight. Direction: somewhat loose. Score: 35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the key policy debate. The vote was 10-2 for alternative B (no change) with asymmetric tightening bias. Angell and Boehne dissented (Angell wanted tightening, Boehne wanted symmetric). Among all speakers:
- Favor immediate tightening: Angell, Lindsey, Broaddus, Jordan, Melzer (leaning) = ~5
- Favor no change but asymmetric (lean hawkish): Greenspan, Mullins, Stern, Kelley, McTeer, LaWare, Corrigan, Syron (persuaded), Keehn (preferred symmetric but accepted asymmetric) = ~8-9
- Favor no change symmetric (cautious): Boehne, Forrestal, Hoenig, Phillips (preferred symmetric, could live with asymmetric) = ~4

The center of gravity is hold with a clear tightening bias. Greenspan: "I think Governor Mullins is right that a 3 percent funds rate is too low...the issue has never been that. The issue is: When do we move?" This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish stance. Direction: above midpoint. Score: 56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote was 10-2 (Angell no, Boehne no -- from opposite directions). There was substantial debate about symmetric vs. asymmetric and whether to act now. Several participants argued passionately for immediate action (Angell, Mullins, Lindsey, Broaddus) while others (Forrestal, LaWare, Boehne) cautioned against moving. The Chairman needed extended discussion to forge consensus. However, the ultimate vote showed broad agreement on the asymmetric hold. Two formal dissents, significant discussion range. Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very little discussion of financial stability per se. Syron briefly mentions concern about mutual fund investors not understanding bond fund risks. Some discussion of the long bond market and gold prices as indicators, but these are discussed in the context of inflation expectations, not financial stability. No systemic stress concerns raised. Direction: low. Score: 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan explicitly states "there is a remote possibility that we may not want to move at some point in the future, it has to be pretty remote" -- meaning a tightening is very likely coming. The asymmetric directive signals tightening bias. Promise of telephone conferences before action. Nearly all participants agree the 3% funds rate needs to rise eventually; the debate is only about timing. Forward signal is clearly toward tightening. Direction: above midpoint. Score: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Extremely high uncertainty expressed. Kelley: "we now have before us about the widest spectrum of fundamentally conflicting possibilities that I can remember." Syron: "an enormous amount of uncertainty." Greenspan: "great certainty at this particular stage about what any of these variables is doing is probably ill advised." Multiple participants express confusion about the split between weak growth and rising inflation. Political/fiscal uncertainty is described as pervasive by nearly every president. Phillips: "we have considerable difficulty interpreting these numbers." Score: 65.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says inflation above expected/above target. Score 58 = modestly above. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential, slack. Score 38 = below potential. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says elevated unemployment, slack. Score 35 = below full employment. Matches.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence says moderate/sluggish. Score 43 = slightly below trend. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions loosening, accommodative. Score 35 = somewhat loose. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with tightening bias. Score 56 = lean hawkish. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says tightening signal. Score 62. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor market slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance is somewhat more hawkish than current action. This makes sense -- the Committee held but signaled tightening is coming. The gap is explained by the decision to wait for more data before acting.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "The core CPI rate, as I calculate it, now has risen at a 4-1/2 percent annual rate over the first months of the year and that really bothers me...inflation is no longer coming down."
- MR. MULLINS: "I think we have 4 percent inflation...the inflation bear has been sighted."
- MR. KEEHN: "although the recent numbers are a little unnerving, I do think it's far too soon to conclude that we are experiencing a basic shift in the outlook for prices."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "in spite of the inflation numbers that have been reported, I don't hear anybody talking about price increases at all."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants are collectively disturbed by the recent inflation uptick (core CPI spiking to 4.5% annualized), which has derailed the expected disinflation path, but are divided on whether it represents a genuine trend or temporary aberrations. The center of gravity sees inflation as modestly above target and no longer improving.

**Minority views:** Keehn, Forrestal, Boehne, and Phillips see anecdotal evidence contradicting the CPI data, arguing prices can't stick. On the other side, Angell, Lindsey, and Mullins see a more serious inflation problem driven by expectations. This range widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "Manufacturing in our area remains sluggish and it is consistently operating below capacity."
- MR. KELLEY: "the fundamentals of the slack in the economy" and describes "sluggish growth."
- MR. MULLINS: "my sense continues to be that we're on a path of moderate growth...between 2-1/2 and 3-1/2 percent."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN references "slack markets" repeatedly.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is growing moderately but clearly remains below potential, with substantial slack still evident in manufacturing capacity and broader economic activity. First quarter GDP was notably weak.

**Minority views:** Melzer notes the economy is in "fundamentally...better shape now" than in late 1992, suggesting the output gap may be narrowing. Most others see persistent slack.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KEEHN: "Major companies--and I must say everyone I have talked to in the manufacturing sector--say they will continue to reduce their employment and will produce more products with fewer people."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The economy is growing enough to produce some jobs but hardly enough, really, to accommodate even the population growth."
- MR. JORDAN: "no sentiment to increase employment."
- MR. MULLINS: "unemployment has come down from the peak last summer of 7.7 percent to 7 percent."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market has significant slack with 7% unemployment. While job growth is occurring, ongoing corporate restructuring, downsizing, and reluctance to hire permanent employees keep the labor market well below full employment.

**Minority views:** Mullins notes payroll growth has been accelerating and overtime is at record levels, suggesting some tightening from the worst. But no participant describes the labor market as tight.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KEEHN: Auto industry confidence "somewhat stronger than it has been," production schedules up 10% year-over-year; steel orders at 110% of capacity. But truck prices "really have not been increased."
- MR. BOEHNE: "Retail sales are going up negligibly...sluggish loan demand, or flat loan demand really."
- Multiple presidents report business investment on hold due to fiscal uncertainty: MR. HOENIG: "businesses are focusing now more than ever on cost containment."
- MR. LAWARE: "Consumers and businesses continue to be more interested in debt reduction than in re-leveraging."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and business investment are growing modestly but unevenly. Auto and housing sectors show strength, but retail sales are sluggish and business investment is constrained by fiscal policy uncertainty and ongoing balance sheet deleveraging.

**Minority views:** The auto/steel/housing strength (Keehn, Forrestal) contrasts with weakness in retail and defense-related sectors (Boehne, Parry). Investment is particularly dampened by political uncertainty.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KEEHN: "a decided change in the attitude about lending by banks...banks have become more aggressive. They are now actively seeking loans."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Banks are reporting higher loan demand...the credit situation seems to be improving."
- MR. SYRON: "very, very strong inflows into bond funds, junk bond funds" -- suggesting ample liquidity.
- MR. MULLINS: "the only component of the current environment that is stimulative or conducive to growth is the capital market environment."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. The fed funds rate is at a historically low 3%, banks are becoming more aggressive lenders, and capital markets are accommodative. The credit crunch that characterized the early recovery has eased significantly.

**Minority views:** Some concern about long bond rates having risen 25 basis points and the possibility of further deterioration if inflation expectations are not addressed.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 +/-10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think Governor Mullins is right that a 3 percent funds rate is too low...My inclination for today would be to go to a tilt toward tightness."
- MR. ANGELL: "I want to raise the rate today. I'd be satisfied with 25 basis points."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "a tightening action could well abort the expansion...I would not be in favor of any move at this time."
- Vote: 10-2 for Alternative B asymmetric toward tightening. Angell dissented wanting immediate tightening; Boehne dissented wanting symmetric.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The Committee leans hawkish, agreeing the 3% funds rate is too low and will need to rise, but the majority prefers to wait for more data before acting. The asymmetric directive signals a clear tightening bias without immediate action.

**Minority views:** A vocal group (Angell, Lindsey, Broaddus, Mullins, Jordan, Melzer) favored immediate action. Another group (Forrestal, Boehne, Hoenig, LaWare) expressed serious concerns about moving prematurely given economic weakness. This creates a wide uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 48 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Two formal dissents from opposite directions: Angell (wanted tightening) and Boehne (wanted symmetric/no bias).
- MR. LINDSEY: "I would again say that the best thing to do...is to [vote for] an increase at this meeting."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "I would not be in favor of any move at this time...I hope if we have to take action that we do it very, very deliberatively."
- Extended debate over symmetric vs. asymmetric directive required multiple rounds of discussion.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Notable disagreement with distinct camps. One group wanted immediate tightening, another wanted to hold with tightening bias, and a third wanted symmetric hold. Two formal dissents from opposite directions is an unusual configuration reflecting genuine philosophical differences. The Chairman needed significant persuasion to build consensus.

**Minority views:** The intensity of Angell's and Mullins' advocacy for action and Forrestal's and LaWare's pushback on tightening risks reveals deep divisions beneath the 10-2 vote.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. SYRON: Mentions concern about "sophistication of the investors, particularly that in a lot of cases people are not really aware that they can lose some capital in those bond funds."
- Brief mentions of gold prices, bond market, and exchange rates as indicators, but in the context of inflation expectations, not systemic stability.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is essentially not discussed as a topic. The few references to financial markets are about inflation signaling (gold, long bond) rather than systemic risk. Typical for 1993.

**Minority views:** None meaningful.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "there is a remote possibility that we may not want to move at some point in the future, it has to be pretty remote" -- strongly implying tightening is coming.
- MR. MULLINS: "I rather doubt that anyone parachuting into this economic environment would choose a 3 percent federal funds rate."
- MR. STERN: "we seem to be drifting away from [price stability]. I don't think it's fatal if the drift goes on a little longer."
- The asymmetric directive itself is the forward signal.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. Nearly all participants agree the 3% funds rate is unsustainably low, with debate only about timing. The asymmetric directive, combined with Greenspan's explicit statement that a move is very likely, sends a mild-to-moderate tightening signal.

**Minority views:** Forrestal and Boehne argued against committing to a tightening direction given economic uncertainty, but even they did not argue for easing.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: "we now have before us about the widest spectrum of fundamentally conflicting possibilities that I can remember."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "great certainty at this particular stage about what any of these variables is doing is probably ill advised. We are in an unstable environment."
- MR. SYRON: "an enormous amount of uncertainty; and to some extent...it's hard to have very strong convictions about the intermediate-term [outlook for] either output or prices."
- Nearly every president cited pervasive business uncertainty driven by fiscal policy/health care reform.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is high and explicitly acknowledged. Participants are genuinely puzzled by the combination of slack growth and rising inflation. Political/fiscal uncertainty is a dominant theme from district reports. Multiple participants express low confidence in any particular outlook scenario.

**Minority views:** Mullins and Broaddus express more confidence that inflation is the real concern; Corrigan suggests "a little perspective" may show things aren't as bad as feared. But the overall tone is one of high uncertainty.
