# FOMC Scoring: 1997-12-16 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the December 16, 1997 FOMC meeting. Individual speakers are identified. The transcript includes a go-around of economic assessments followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Vice Chairman McDonough, Presidents Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Boehne, Guynn, McTeer, Minehan, Stern, Hoenig, Jordan, Governors Rivlin, Ferguson, Phillips (brief/no text), Meyer, Kelley, Gramlich (brief/no text), and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 16-17 substantive speakers.

### Era Context
December 1997: The U.S. is in the late 1990s expansion. The economy is growing robustly, labor markets are very tight, and inflation remains remarkably subdued. The Asian financial crisis (Thailand, Korea, Indonesia) is a major new factor introducing significant uncertainty. The fed funds rate is at 5.50% (unchanged since March 1997 hike).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as clearly below normal concern levels -- "subdued," "dormant," "benign," "low and stable." Multiple participants note declining CPI, falling commodity prices, declining inflation expectations. Parry: "inflation continues subdued." Hoenig: "inflation has been quite stable. Core inflation has been stable or declining, pipeline inflation is basically nonexistent." Greenspan: "We cannot keep getting such numbers and continue to say that inflation is about to rise." Several note the stronger dollar and Asian crisis will further suppress prices. Boehne notes people asking about *deflation*. However, several note upside risks from tight labor markets and accelerating wages. The center of gravity is that inflation is low and falling, though some worry about future risks. Score direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "subdued," "dormant" = 10-15 points below midpoint. Score ~35-38.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very low current inflation. Score below 50 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Participants see the economy operating above potential. Greenspan: "an economy growing at a pace near 4 percent and operating at a 4.6 percent unemployment rate." Meyer: "an economy already operating beyond its point of sustainable capacity." Parry: "the unemployment rate...is now well below the natural rate." Multiple participants describe "strong," "robust" growth. Broaddus and others note GDP growth well above trend. Many expect slowing from Asia but from a position of above-trend activity. Score direction: above 50. Magnitude: "above trend," "beyond sustainable capacity" but not extreme -- the late 90s boom anchor is 57-63. Score ~58.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly above potential. Score >50 matches.
Bright-line rule: "above potential" language present; score >50. Confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Virtually every participant describes labor markets as very tight to extremely tight. Parry: "very tight labor markets, particularly in urban areas." Moskow: "labor markets are very tight in our District, with the unemployment rate at or below 4 percent since May." Broaddus: "record-breaking job vacancy backlogs at a number of temp agencies." Boehne describes extraordinary creative measures to find workers. McTeer: extremely tight with $20K salary jumps for engineers, recruiting from India. Stern: "wage pressures have become pervasive both geographically and by job and skill categories. It is very, very hard to find workers." Hoenig: "almost all the firms we have contacted are reporting labor shortages." Jordan: workers can't be disciplined because they'll leave. This is among the tightest descriptions in any era. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score ~70.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Consumer spending described as "reasonably well" to solid for holidays. Moskow notes some softness -- retailers used "sluggish" and "soft." Boehne: holiday sales "doing reasonably well." McTeer: "ho-ho year" for retail. Business investment remains strong. Commercial real estate very strong in many districts. McTeer: Houston building first downtown office tower since 1986. Minehan: speculative commercial RE construction increasing. Rivlin: "consumer spending may prove stronger than projected." Overall: above trend but not booming. Score ~57.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says above trend. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Financial conditions are described as not tight -- loose to neutral. Ferguson: "credit conditions are not overly tight" and notes "loosening of lending standards." Minehan: commercial real estate lending booming, banks lending at 2-3x national pace. Hoenig: "Long-term rates have fallen." Greenspan: "It is very hard to make the case that we have tight money when the housing market is behaving as well as it is." Capital inflows from Asia are pushing rates down. Broaddus: "the capital reflow...has some of the effects of an easing of monetary policy." Score direction: below 50 (loose). Magnitude: "not overly tight," loosening standards, capital inflows = somewhat loose. Score ~30.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score below 50 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
Direction: The consensus is to hold rates steady (alternative B). Greenspan recommends B symmetric. Only Broaddus explicitly advocates for a 25bp hike. Several participants (Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Guynn) prefer asymmetry toward tightening but accept symmetric. McDonough, Rivlin, Stern, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, McTeer, Boehne all support B symmetric. Greenspan says "the most probable next move" is still up but not now. Several note that absent Asia they might have tightened. The lean is hold-steady with a mild tightening bias for the future. Score ~52 -- hold with slight hawkish tilt.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with acknowledgment next move likely up but not now = just above midpoint. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Direction: Very broad agreement on holding rates. Only Broaddus explicitly favors a rate increase, making him a clear outlier. The disagreement is mainly about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive -- a secondary issue. Several participants prefer asymmetry (Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Guynn) but can accept symmetric. No formal dissent expected. Moderate differences on tilt but agreement on action. Score ~30.
Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. One clear outlier (Broaddus) plus secondary disagreement on tilt. Score 30 fits "broad agreement with minor differences."

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Direction: Financial stability is a very prominent topic. The entire Asian financial crisis dominates discussion. McDonough devotes his entire remarks to international financial stability risks -- "the world economy is walking along a rather narrow ridge...to the left, a rather large precipice." Greenspan discusses equity premium, stock overvaluation risks, potential for "significant implosion." Minehan raises speculative commercial RE construction and loosening lending standards, calls for regulatory attention. Jordan references 1920s asset prices. Ferguson notes loosening lending standards. Multiple participants discuss contagion risks. Score ~60-65 -- prominent topic, many participants discuss at length.
Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Heavy discussion of financial stability. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Direction: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. Greenspan: "I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point. I see that as the most probable next move." Meyer: "without changing our view that the next move is still likely to be in a policy tightening direction." But uncertainty from Asia introduces flexibility and the possibility of easing. McDonough: "We may wish to return to asymmetry at some later date." The signal is: next move probably up, but very data-dependent and could go either way. Score ~55 -- mild tightening bias with significant data dependence.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Next move likely up" but heavy caveats = mildly hawkish. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Direction: Uncertainty is a dominant theme. Rivlin: "widened our confidence intervals quite a lot." Meyer: "a great deal of uncertainty about the dimension of the Asian turmoil." Stern: "more than enough uncertainty to go around." Greenspan discusses widening tails of distributions. McTeer: "increased uncertainty, both on the downside and the upside." Kelley: "remarkable time...very substantial risks, both up and down." Ferguson: "era of significant uncertainty." Almost every participant emphasizes elevated uncertainty. Score ~58 -- elevated, multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty.
Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Very frequently mentioned. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap suggests. This makes sense because the labor market descriptions are extremely vivid while GDP growth is expected to slow from Asia effects. The labor market is the leading pressure point.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Aligned -- both mildly above neutral. Current hold with mild tightening bias for the future. Coherent.

### Speaker count by view direction (policy):
- Hold, symmetric: McDonough, Boehne, Stern, Rivlin, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, McTeer, Hoenig, Jordan (~10)
- Hold, prefer asymmetric but accept symmetric: Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Guynn (~4)
- Favor 25bp hike: Broaddus (~1)
- Center of gravity: Hold, symmetric. Clear.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- PARRY: "inflation continues subdued" and "we have reduced our forecast since the November meeting"
- HOENIG: "inflation has been quite stable. Core inflation has been stable or declining, pipeline inflation is basically nonexistent, and various measures of short-term and long-term inflation expectations are stable or declining"
- GREENSPAN: "We cannot keep getting such numbers and continue to say that inflation is about to rise. As we keep projecting a higher rate of inflation, it keeps going down"
- BOEHNE: Notes business people asking about *deflation* rather than inflation

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is subdued and declining, well below any level of concern. Strong dollar and Asian crisis expected to further suppress prices. However, tight labor markets and accelerating wages represent a latent upside risk that widens the uncertainty band.

Minority views: Several participants (Moskow, Broaddus, Minehan) see upside inflation risks from tight labor markets, but even they acknowledge current readings are benign.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "an economy growing at a pace near 4 percent and operating at a 4.6 percent unemployment rate"
- MEYER: "an economy already operating beyond its point of sustainable capacity, with the prospect of a slow progressive updrift in inflation"
- PARRY: "real GDP growth remains robust in the current quarter" and "the unemployment rate...is now well below the natural rate"

Center-of-gravity: Economy is clearly operating above potential with robust growth and very low unemployment, though the Asian shock is expected to bring growth closer to trend.

Minority views: Some expect a sharper slowdown (Greenbook forecast of 1.7% GDP), but most participants see above-trend growth continuing.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- STERN: "wage pressures have become pervasive both geographically and by job and skill categories. It is very, very hard to find workers"
- BROADDUS: "record-breaking job vacancy backlogs at a number of temp agencies...seasonal workers...are now making $6.00 an hour or even more"
- MCTEER: "Sizable wage increases are reported for highly skilled technical workers. Salaries have soared as much as $20,000 in the last year for...software engineers"; Intel mailing 14,000 letters to poach engineers with Hawaii trip offers
- JORDAN: "it was impossible to discipline workers because of the hot job market. If an employer tried to tell a worker that there were policies about showing up on time...the worker simply left"

Center-of-gravity: Labor markets are extremely tight across virtually all districts and skill levels. Creative and extraordinary measures to attract workers are widespread. Wage pressures are pervasive and accelerating.

Minority views: None disagree on the tightness assessment; all participants confirm tight or very tight labor conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MOSKOW: retailers used "sluggish" and "soft" for recent sales trends but "light vehicle sales...tracking around the 15 million unit rate"; "demand for heavy trucks has been very strong"
- BOEHNE: "holiday retail sales seem to be doing reasonably well overall"; "commercial real estate market continues to tighten"
- GUYNN: "consumer sector is a little stronger than we expected"; "business investment spending has been maintained at strong levels longer than we had forecast"
- MCTEER: retailers expecting a "ho-ho" year; Houston commercial RE booming

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is solid and business investment remains strong. Commercial real estate is very robust. Net position is above trend but not dramatically so, with some mixed signals in retail.

Minority views: Moskow notes some softness in retail; Greenbook projects slowdown.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- FERGUSON: "credit conditions are not overly tight" and evidence of "some loosening of lending standards"
- MINEHAN: "Commercial real estate lending is seen as a growth opportunity for the first time since the late 1980s" with District loans "growing at better than twice the national rate" and real estate lending "at over three times the national pace"
- BROADDUS: "the capital reflow that is occurring has some of the effects of an easing of monetary policy"
- GREENSPAN: "It is very hard to make the case that we have tight money when the housing market is behaving as well as it is"

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are loose. Credit is freely available, lending standards are loosening, capital inflows from Asia are pushing rates down. Housing and commercial RE are booming, which signals accommodative financial conditions.

Minority views: McDonough notes Asian markets are thin and volatile, but domestic conditions remain loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "I would be marginally in favor of alternative B symmetric this time...I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point. I see that as the most probable next move"
- MCDONOUGH: "absent the international events, this might be a rather good opportunity for a 25 basis point move...But given the international considerations, I believe such a move would be very ill-advised"
- BROADDUS (outlier hawkish): "a modest 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today would be a prudent step for us to take"
- KELLEY: "upside risks seem to me to have moderated a bit while...significant downside concerns have now emerged...those risks seem roughly in balance"

Center-of-gravity: Strong consensus for holding rates. Most acknowledge that absent Asia, tightening might have been warranted. The next move is expected to be up, but Asia has effectively created the tightening they might have needed. Net lean is very slightly hawkish -- hold but next move probably up.

Minority views: Broaddus favors immediate 25bp hike. Several prefer asymmetric (tightening-biased) directive.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Broaddus is the sole advocate for a rate hike: "I still think, stubbornly no doubt, that a modest 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today would be a prudent step"
- Secondary disagreement on symmetric vs. asymmetric directive: Minehan, Parry, Moskow, Guynn prefer asymmetric but accept symmetric
- No formal dissent expected; broad agreement on holding

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the hold decision. Moderate differences on directive tilt but all can live with the outcome. One clear policy outlier (Broaddus). No heated exchanges or fundamental disagreements.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MCDONOUGH: Entire remarks devoted to international financial stability -- "the world economy is walking along a rather narrow ridge...to the left, a rather large precipice"; discusses potential "implosion of the financial systems in some of the Asian countries"
- GREENSPAN: "It is conceivable that we could get a significant implosion here. I believe the odds are small but not zero"; discusses equity premium and stock overvaluation
- MINEHAN: Raises alarm on speculative commercial RE construction -- "plans have been submitted for 40 new hotels in Boston proper...11.3 million square feet of speculative office space"; calls for regulatory follow-up on lending standards
- JORDAN: References 1920s parallel of being "too restrictive for goods prices and too expansionary for asset prices"

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a prominent topic. The Asian crisis dominates as a financial stability concern. Additionally, domestic concerns about asset prices, lending standards, and speculative construction receive meaningful attention.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "I continue to believe that we will have to move rates up at some point. I see that as the most probable next move"
- MEYER: "without changing our view that the next move is still likely to be in a policy tightening direction"
- KELLEY: "the short-term risks seem to me to be more on the downside" -- suggesting near-term caution
- MCDONOUGH: "We may wish to return to asymmetry at some later date"

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- next move expected to be up, but timing highly uncertain and data-dependent. Asia introduces significant possibility of easing. Move to symmetric directive signals true two-way flexibility.

Minority views: Kelley and McTeer note short-term downside risks may dominate, lowering the forward hawkish signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- RIVLIN: "The increased uncertainty about world events...has widened our confidence intervals quite a lot, even though we don't know exactly what our confidence intervals are!"
- MEYER: "there is a great deal of uncertainty about the dimension of the Asian turmoil"
- STERN: "more than enough uncertainty to go around. We do not know how severe the Asian problem is going to be. We do not know how far it is going to spread"
- GREENSPAN: discusses widening tails of distributions; "the tails of the various types of distributions...very clearly seem to be getting more extended"

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is elevated and frequently emphasized. Almost every participant highlights heightened uncertainty from Asia. However, this is not "uncharted territory" -- the domestic economy is familiar ground, and it is the international spillover that is uncertain.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 35**
Multiple participants note falling goods prices from Asian imports, stronger dollar, declining commodity prices. Greenspan: "the Journal of Commerce industrial price index...does indeed show prices going down significantly." Parry: "the continued appreciation of the dollar will help to hold down prices."

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
Ferguson briefly mentions "the dichotomy between goods inflation and services inflation" but no substantive discussion by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
Housing market discussed in terms of activity (strong) but not housing inflation specifically.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40**
Parry: "the results of the last Philadelphia Fed survey of inflation expectations were impressive. The sizable drop in long-term expectations provides an important confirmation." Hoenig: "various measures of short-term and long-term inflation expectations are stable or declining." Expectations anchored below target.

**CREDIT_STRESS: N/A**
No specific credit spread or stress discussion beyond general lending conditions (scored under FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS).

**EQUITY_STRESS: 30**
Greenspan: "At any realistic earnings forecast, stock prices will be substantially overvalued. It is conceivable that we could get a significant implosion here." Jordan references asset price concerns from the 1920s parallel. This is overvaluation concern direction (low score = bubble concern).

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
Domestic banking stress not discussed. Asian banking weakness discussed but not U.S. banking system stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
Not discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: 60**
Several participants note dollar appreciation and its effects. Parry: "the dollar's appreciation over the last year and the projected slowing in foreign demand." Dollar strength is reducing export demand but also suppressing import prices. Moderate concern about strength effects on trade.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 68**
Greenspan devotes substantial discussion to accelerating productivity: "productivity has been accelerating over the past several quarters." Parry: "possible favorable productivity shock." Ferguson: "increase in productivity above the anemic 1 percent trend." Greenspan argues productivity acceleration explains the disconnect between tight labor markets and low inflation.
