# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2001-05-15 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript with individual speaker attributions. This is a full go-around plus policy discussion. Speakers include Jordan, Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Broaddus, Guynn, Santomero, Stern, Poole, Minehan, McDonough, McTeer, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 17 participants.

**Era context**: May 2001 — the economy is in the midst of a pronounced slowdown following the bursting of the dot-com bubble and a sharp contraction in high-tech/manufacturing investment. The Fed had already cut 200bp since January 2001. Unemployment was rising from historically low levels. Energy prices were elevated. The question was whether this was a rolling sectoral recession or something deeper.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Direction — Participants see inflation as modestly above comfort but not alarming. Several participants (Hoenig, Moskow, Santomero, Broaddus, Minehan, Meyer, Stern) flag a "slow upcreep" in core inflation and rising energy pass-through. Greenspan downplays it, arguing pricing power is limited and service price measurement is flawed. Gramlich calls it "blips largely related to energy prices." McTeer notes "more reports of price declines than price increases" in his District. The center of gravity: core inflation is drifting up modestly but is not yet a primary concern — it is secondary to growth fears. The language is "upcreep," "gradual increase," "not dead" — suggesting modestly above comfortable levels. Score should be in the 51-58 range. Higher = more pressure. Evidence supports "somewhat elevated" but contained. Score: 55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Direction — Participants clearly see the economy as operating below potential and weakening further. Multiple speakers describe "sluggish" growth, "slowdown," economy "fragile," manufacturing "contracting," "growing well below potential" (Ferguson). Greenbook forecast has unemployment rising to 5.1-5.5%. Meyer notes "slowdown began with output above potential" but output is now falling below. Gramlich notes unemployment "well above the rates necessary to control inflation." The economy is clearly below potential but not in catastrophic contraction — housing strong, consumption holding up. Score should be in the 31-40 range. Score: 35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Direction — Labor markets are softening meaningfully but from very tight levels. Parry: "slackening labor markets" but wages still up 5% in the West. Broaddus: "labor markets have softened." Minehan: Connecticut still at 1.9% unemployment. McDonough: unemployment "at or near cyclical lows" in NY. Moskow: "Manpower survey... lowest since 1993." McTeer: "layoffs are currently swamping the demand for hiring." The center of gravity is that labor markets have loosened notably from extreme tightness but aren't yet slack. Unemployment at 4.5% nationally, still low historically. Score: 42. Markets are easing rapidly from tight conditions but still near NAIRU.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Direction — Mixed picture. Consumer spending holding up surprisingly well (retail sales, housing strong) but business investment is very weak and falling. Multiple participants describe investment as "sidelined," "cutting back," firms "hunkering down." Moskow: "almost all manufacturers reviewing, freezing, or scaling back capital spending." Guynn: investment "largely sidelined." On the other hand, consumption "surprisingly resilient" (Moskow), retail sales "robust" in April (Kelley), housing "very strong." Net position: the deep weakness in investment outweighs the moderate resilience in consumption. Below trend overall. Score: 33.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Direction — Financial conditions are mixed. Credit is available — lending standards tightening modestly (Poole's bankers). Long rates have risen ~50bp since March. Credit spreads have actually declined (Hoenig: "decline in risk spreads"). Equity markets stabilized and up 6-9% (Ferguson). Bond issuance active. McDonough: market "volatile" but not stressed. The picture is of somewhat loose to neutral financial conditions — the aggressive rate cuts have eased conditions, credit is flowing, but there's some tightening in lending standards. Score: 40. Somewhat loose side of neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Direction — The majority supports 50bp cut today (dovish action) but there is a very strong undercurrent of wanting to slow/stop the easing. The vote was for 50bp with asymmetric risk toward weakness. However, multiple participants (Jordan, Hoenig, Guynn, Santomero) preferred 25bp. Others (Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Meyer, Moskow) supported 50bp but explicitly said they think we're near the end of easing. Greenspan himself said he hopes 4% is as low as they'll go. This is a dovish action but with a clear lean toward stopping easing. The center of gravity is moderate dovish: ease further but approaching the end. Score: 32. Still cutting (dovish) but with strong signals of approaching the end.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Direction — Significant disagreement about pace. Jordan preferred no change or 25bp. Hoenig and Guynn explicitly preferred 25bp. Santomero "if I had my druthers" would do 25bp. Stern could "make a reasonable case for why we ought to pause today." Broaddus and Minehan could have supported 25bp. On the other side, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, McDonough supported 50bp strongly. The vote went for 50bp but with substantial expressed reservations. The debate about signaling the end of easing was also quite divided. This represents notable disagreement — distinct camps on pace if not direction. Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Direction — Financial stability gets moderate attention. Greenspan discusses stock market overvaluation risk at length. Minehan warns about "financial market excesses" from rates "too low for too long." McDonough discusses volatile markets. Broaddus mentions long-term Treasury rate increases and inflation expectations. But this is not a dominant topic — it's secondary to the growth/recession debate. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Direction — The forward signal is dovish but transitioning. The statement retained asymmetric risk toward weakness. However, the removal of any intermeeting move language is itself a hawkish shift. Multiple participants want to signal the end of easing is near. Greenspan plans to raise inflation concerns in a May 24 speech. McDonough: removing intermeeting language "is going to sound a big gong." The forward signal is: still easing bias but approaching the turn. Score: 35. Easing lean with growing data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Direction — Very elevated uncertainty. McDonough: "the market is conveying... it is extremely uncertain. So am I." Moskow: "economic situation this quarter continues to be highly uncertain." Greenspan: the risks "are really different from any I've ever seen." Multiple participants describe deep uncertainty about whether the slowdown will deepen or the recovery will take hold. Greenspan discusses long historical tail risks. The technology overhang duration is "extremely uncertain" (Minehan). Score: 65.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (42): Labor slightly higher because unemployment is still historically low (4.5%) even as output is clearly below potential. The gap reflects the lag — labor markets are softening but haven't fully adjusted to the output weakness. This divergence is explained and reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (35): Both moderately dovish. The forward guidance is slightly less dovish because the signal is clearly shifting toward the end of easing. Coherent.

### Polarity verifications:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "upcreep," "modestly elevated." Score 55 = modestly above midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly below potential. Score 35 = below potential. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions somewhat loose (rate cuts working, spreads narrowing). Score 40 = somewhat loose. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "The gradual increase in core inflation over the past year is a concern to me... our more accommodative policy may very well lead consumers, laborers, and businesses to ratchet up their inflation expectations."
- MR. MEYER: "The modest rise in core inflation and the upward adjustment to the staff's inflation forecast support this assessment. These developments confirm, in my view, that inflation risks were indeed building before the economy slowed so sharply."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the pricing power of business firms is very limited at this point... the capacity to raise prices in an environment such as this is extraordinarily restricted."

Center of gravity: Core inflation is drifting up modestly (from ~1.6% PCE to ~2%+), driven partly by energy pass-through. Multiple participants flag it as a rising concern, but pricing power is limited and the slowdown constrains further acceleration. Modestly above the comfortable range.

Minority views: McTeer ("more reports of price declines than price increases"), Gramlich ("blips largely related to energy prices") pull toward lower scores. Hoenig and Santomero see more risk, pulling higher.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FERGUSON: "an economy growing--and likely to continue to grow far below its potential"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "Overall industrial production is down, capacity utilization rates are at 10-year lows, and inventories outside of transportation and high-tech are building up."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Nearly half the respondents expect an outright recession... the risks continue to be skewed toward economic weakness."

Center of gravity: The economy is clearly below potential with manufacturing contracting, capacity utilization at decade lows, and growth expected to remain very weak for at least two quarters. But housing is strong, consumer spending resilient, and a recovery is expected in H2 2001 / 2002.

Minority views: Hoenig and Kelley see somewhat less dire conditions, noting encouraging data points. Broaddus and McDonough see larger downside risks.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "Slower employment growth has boosted unemployment rates... However, as of yet, slackening labor markets have not resulted in lower wage inflation... total compensation in the West grew by 5 percent."
- MR. JORDAN: "openings for over 1,000 registered nurses... many staff support positions continue to go unfilled... unemployment rate dropping to 3.8 percent and 3.5 percent" (Ohio).
- MR. MOSKOW: "the Manpower survey of hiring intentions... the lowest since 1993 and the two-quarter drop is the largest ever outside a recessionary period."

Center of gravity: Labor markets are loosening rapidly — layoffs spreading, hiring intentions plunging — but the level of tightness remains near historical norms (4.5% nationally). Several districts still report difficulty finding workers. Wage pressures have not yet abated. The labor market is transitioning from tight to balanced but hasn't reached slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. GUYNN: "[Investment] remains largely sidelined... he simply was sitting on several large and very strategic investment spending proposals... he just wasn't going to turn them loose right now."
- MR. MOSKOW: "almost all manufacturers... were reviewing, freezing, or scaling back capital spending plans."
- MR. KELLEY: "April retail sales were robust... Consumer sentiment showed an improvement" but also "record household debt burdens, over-investment by business firms."

Center of gravity: Business investment is in deep retrenchment — cutting across manufacturing, high-tech, and commercial real estate. Consumer spending shows surprising resilience and housing remains strong, preventing a score in the 20s. The net position is clearly below trend, with investment dragging the aggregate well down.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "The decline in short-term rates, the decline in risk spreads, and double-digit rates of growth in M2 are having beneficial effects."
- MR. POOLE: "bankers do say that lending standards are being tightened modestly, to some extent in response to pressure from regulators."
- MR. FERGUSON: "equity indices... have in fact experienced growth of 6 to 9 percent... businesses are issuing bonds to fill their coffers."

Center of gravity: Aggressive Fed easing has loosened financial conditions — credit spreads narrowing, equity markets recovering, bond issuance active. But some tightening in bank lending standards and long rates have risen ~50bp. Overall, conditions are somewhat loose to neutral, reflecting the large cumulative policy easing.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 32 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Recommends 50bp cut with asymmetric risk toward weakness. "I would hope that a 4 percent funds rate is as low as we will have to go."
- MR. HOENIG: "I think we should pull back on the throttle today... I'm of the view that a 25 basis point cut... is what we should do now."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the economy, both domestically and internationally, is currently in a very tenuous state, with still significant downside risks... I think we can safely lean harder against these recessionary winds."

Center of gravity: The majority votes for 50bp ease, maintaining a dovish stance. But the discussion is heavily laden with caution about going further — many participants explicitly say this should be the last or near-last large move. The action is moderate dovish; the lean is toward ending the easing cycle soon.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. JORDAN: Preferred no change or 25bp. "I'd be happier with no change or a 1/4 point reduction today."
- MR. HOENIG: "I'm of the view that a 25 basis point cut... is what we should do now."
- MR. GUYNN: "I actually would prefer to come out of today's meeting with some clear signal that we have slowed the pace of easing... a 25 basis point easing would be a cleaner, preferable action."
- Multiple others (Broaddus, Minehan, Santomero, Stern, Meyer) expressed sympathy for 25bp but ultimately supported 50bp.

Center of gravity: Notable disagreement on pace. At least three participants explicitly preferred 25bp, and several more said they could have supported it. Strong debate on forward signaling between those wanting to telegraph the end of easing (Broaddus, Minehan) and those warning against mixed signals (McDonough, Ferguson, Kelley). Distinct camps exist.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. MINEHAN: "even if higher inflation is not a concern, financial market excesses should be. Extended periods with interest rates that are arguably too low for too long... can produce a predilection for one-way market bets."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "stock market prices are still probably higher than what our models say would be consistent with their fundamental determinants. The trouble... is that it never stops where the models say it should."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: Extensive discussion of market volatility and uncertainty in interpreting market signals.

Center of gravity: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion. A few participants raise concerns about market excesses and the risks of extended low rates. Greenspan discusses stock valuation risk. But it is not a prominent topic relative to the growth/inflation debate.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would hope that a 4 percent funds rate is as low as we will have to go... we should begin in speeches or interviews to address the possibility that 250 basis points of ease... may be enough."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "There will be a very large signal received from this statement, which is that we've removed any notion of an intermeeting move."
- MR. MOSKOW: "we should start indicating that 250 basis points of ease may be enough."

Center of gravity: The forward signal still leans dovish — asymmetric risk statement retained toward economic weakness, rates cut further. But the removal of intermeeting move language and widespread discussion of "approaching the end" of the easing cycle signals a transition toward neutrality. The forward tone is easing lean with clear data dependence and approaching-the-turn signals.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the main message the market is conveying is that it is extremely uncertain. So am I... our models don't work in assessing the effects of a loss of consumer confidence."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the risks are really different from any I've ever seen... the risk distribution is not symmetric. Indeed, it has a fairly worrisome tail at the wrong end."
- MR. MOSKOW: "the economic situation this quarter continues to be highly uncertain."
- MS. MINEHAN: "the region's increasing concentration of information technology companies... does not bode well... this is the region's largest area of vulnerability and uncertainty."

Center of gravity: Uncertainty is high. Multiple participants explicitly describe the outlook as "extremely uncertain," "highly uncertain," or "fragile." Greenspan invokes historical tail risks. The interaction of international slowdown with domestic weakness adds additional layers of uncertainty. Models are acknowledged as inadequate. This goes beyond normal cyclical uncertainty.
