# FOMC Scoring: 1996-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers the economic go-around and the policy discussion. Speakers include: Hoenig, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Guynn, McTeer, Broaddus, Boehne, Stern, Melzer, McDonough, Rivlin, Jordan, Kelley, Phillips, Meyer, Lindsey, Yellen, and Chairman Greenspan. Approximately 19 participants.

### Era Context
December 1996: The U.S. economy is in the middle of a long expansion. GDP growth had been strong earlier in the year and slowed to near-trend in the second half. Unemployment is around 5.2-5.4%. CPI inflation is running near 3% (core under 3%). This is the period just after Greenspan's famous "irrational exuberance" speech (December 5, 1996). The stock market is a notable topic. Labor markets are tight but wage pressures have been restrained by job insecurity and strong productivity/profit margins.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as contained but with upside risks. The center of gravity: core inflation is running slightly below 3%, which with CPI bias adjustments may be closer to 2% true inflation. Multiple participants note rising core inflation projections for 1997-98 (Hoenig, Parry, Melzer). However, actual incoming data show "stable to still declining" core inflation (Meyer). McTeer finds dollar, gold, commodities "fairly reassuring." Broaddus notes true inflation may be near 2% after CPI bias. Greenspan notes "mild degree of acceleration" in wages but no price pass-through yet. The majority see current inflation as benign but risks tilting up. Direction: slightly below the midpoint for current perception, but upside risks noted. Score around 45-48 -- inflation is slightly below the old 3% threshold after technical adjustments, with broad agreement that risks are to the upside but no actual acceleration yet.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is currently well-behaved but with upward risk. Score ~47 fits: slightly below midpoint, acknowledging risks but no actual elevated pressure.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Multiple participants describe the economy as growing "at or near trend" or "near potential." Parry: "slight degree of excess demand." Moskow: "output growing at or slightly above potential." Meyer: "growth has now slowed to trend" and "beginning from full employment." Greenspan: "expansion is moving along at a reasonably good pace." Phillips: "sustainable zone." Boehne: "successfully made the adjustment downward from unsustainable growth rates... on a more sustainable track." The center of gravity is near potential with a very slight positive gap. Parry's "slight excess demand" and tight labor markets push slightly above 50.

Direction: at or very slightly above potential. Score ~52.

Bright-line check: Parry says "slight degree of excess demand" (above potential), Moskow says "at or slightly above potential" -- score must be > 50. Score 52 is consistent.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is one of the most discussed topics. Nearly every participant mentions tight labor markets. Hoenig: "labor markets continue to be tight, more reports of wage pressures." Parry: "judging resource utilization is particularly difficult right now, especially in labor markets." Moskow: "operating at a high level, unemployment rates below the national average." Minehan: mutual fund company giving 5-10% raises on top of merit, "lid has blown on salaries of engineers." Broaddus: "labor markets remain exceptionally tight... from virtually everyone." Boehne: "Getting qualified workers is becoming an increasingly familiar refrain." Stern: "how tight labor markets are and how difficult it is to find both skilled and unskilled workers... higher wages." Melzer: "labor markets remain tight." Guynn: "labor markets remain tight in many areas." McTeer: "upward pressure on wages has not abated." Rivlin: "tight labor markets and beginnings of accelerating wage increases." Yellen: "labor markets are undeniably tight." Meyer: unemployment rate in "low but narrow band" near 5.2-5.4%.

Direction: clearly tight. Multiple speakers use "tight" or "exceptionally tight." Wage pressures emerging but not yet broad-based. Unemployment around 5.2-5.4% is low by 1990s standards. Score ~60-63. This is tight but not yet "severe shortages" territory broadly; shortages are selective/occupational.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence = very tight. Score ~62 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending views are mixed. Greenspan: "Personal consumption expenditures appear to be running at a moderate pace." Phillips: consumer spending constrained by debt but labor income supports it. Yellen: "concern with the possibility that consumer spending may be too robust rather than too weak has increased." Moskow: "holiday sales somewhat better than expected." On investment, Phillips notes "business fixed investment has consistently outpaced Greenbook projections." Capital spending is strong. However, some housing softness noted (Broaddus, Guynn, Jordan). Auto sales moderate. Net: spending is solid, near or slightly above trend. Score ~54.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence = solid/moderate, slightly above trend on investment, moderate on consumption. Score 54 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are discussed primarily through the lens of the stock market boom and credit availability. Lindsey: "excessive credit expansion" continues, lenders making loans to bankrupt people. Minehan: "declining liquidity ratios," "higher degree of leverage in financial markets." Phillips: "external financing cost of capital has been relatively low. Profit levels and cash flow also permitted internal funding." Greenspan: consumer credit hitting a ceiling for lower-income households, but overall conditions are loose. Bond yields around 6.5%. The overwhelming signal is that financial conditions are accommodative/loose, not tight. Stock market is booming, credit is freely available.

Direction: loose conditions. Score ~25-30. Conditions are clearly on the loose side -- strong equity markets, easy credit, low financing costs.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence = loose. Low score fits. Score ~27.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The policy decision is to hold at "B" (unchanged funds rate) with asymmetric bias toward tightening. Nearly all participants support this. Greenspan: "far greater likelihood that the next move will be up rather than down." Melzer is the lone voice favoring an actual tightening ("I would favor a modest increase in the degree of reserve restraint"). Broaddus would have accepted a "slight snugging." Most others support hold with asymmetric tightening bias. No one advocates easing. The center of gravity: hold steady, tightening bias, next move likely up.

Direction: lean hawkish. Hold + tightening bias = ~55-58. Not moderate hawkish (no one except Melzer wants to actually move now), but clearly tilted toward tightening. Score ~56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Only Melzer dissents from the hold recommendation (preferring a tightening). Broaddus says his "opposition would have been less than heated" if a snugging had been proposed. Everyone else supports "B" asymmetric. Meyer notes it's "a closer call" on symmetric vs asymmetric. Jordan raises communication questions but supports the recommendation. Views are broadly aligned. One soft dissenter (Melzer), one near-dissenter (Broaddus).

Direction: broad agreement with minor differences. Score ~25-28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The stock market and financial conditions receive significant attention. Minehan devotes substantial discussion to leverage, mutual fund liquidity, and asset price bubble concerns. Lindsey's entire remarks revolve around "irrational exuberance" and three risks (budget illusion, excessive credit, dollar). Yellen discusses stock market as "significant continuing risk." Phillips mentions the Chairman's speech effect on markets. Jordan relays anecdotes about stock valuations. Greenspan discusses consumer credit and wealth effects at length. Rivlin mentions stock market as "most worrisome problem on the horizon." This is well above the 1993-96 typical level but not dominant.

Direction: moderate-to-prominent discussion. Score ~35-42. Multiple participants address it meaningfully, but it is not the dominant topic (economic conditions and labor markets get more weight). Score ~38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward tightening. Greenspan: "far greater likelihood that the next move will be up." Parry: "may be necessary sometime next year to tighten." McDonough: "very likely that our next move will be up." The asymmetric directive signals tightening bias. But no urgency -- "watchful waiting" (Kelley), "we can be patient" (Boehne). Several call for eventual discussion of inflation targets at February meeting.

Direction: mild tightening bias. Score ~60-63. Clear signal next move is up, but patient/data-dependent.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence = next move likely up, but patient. Score ~62 fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is moderate. Meyer: "risks related to growth have really become more balanced." Parry: "considerable uncertainty about prospective strength of economic activity." Guynn: "major uncertainties continue to be whether labor shortages will eventually begin to be reflected in inflationary wage pressures." Jordan discusses uncertainty about output measurement. Minehan's academics note "uncertainty made it difficult for monetary policy to be extremely forward-looking." But many participants express reasonable confidence in the near-term outlook (trend growth). The Greenbook is described as "bland" (Kelley) and there is narrow range of GDP forecasts (Meyer: 2.0-2.4%).

Direction: somewhat elevated but not high. Standard caveats plus genuine uncertainty about NAIRU and labor market dynamics. Score ~35.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Labor is tighter than the overall output gap. This is consistent with the transcript -- participants note capacity utilization is not strained in product markets but labor markets are tight. The divergence is explained by strong capital spending expanding industrial capacity while labor supply is constrained.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance is slightly more hawkish than current lean. This makes sense -- they hold now but signal the next move is up. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 47 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MEYER: "I read core measures of inflation as stable to still declining."
- MR. HOENIG: "both we and the Board staff are projecting rising core inflation, even allowing for the technical adjustments."
- MR. BROADDUS: "the underlying true inflation rate is in some sense closer to 2 percent"
- MR. PARRY: "only a very gradual upward trend in underlying inflation. Our forecast shows core CPI rising from under 2-3/4 percent this year to close to 3 percent in 1998."

Center of gravity: Current inflation is benign/slightly below 3%, and with CPI bias adjustments near 2% true inflation. However, most forecasts show a gradual uptick ahead. The present perception is of well-contained inflation with upside risk -- slightly below the midpoint.

Minority views: Melzer sees inflation risks as "somewhat greater now" and wants to act. McTeer finds price indicators "fairly reassuring."

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "there probably will still be a slight degree of excess demand in the economy next year"
- MR. MOSKOW: "we see output growing at or slightly above potential in 1997"
- MR. MEYER: "Economic growth has now slowed to trend."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the expansion is moving along at a reasonably good pace"

Center of gravity: Economy is operating very near potential, with a slight positive gap. Growth has decelerated from earlier in the year to near trend.

Minority views: None materially divergent. Very tight range of forecasts (2.0-2.4% GDP growth).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "labor markets remain exceptionally tight in our region. We hear that from virtually everyone"
- MR. STERN: "how tight labor markets are and how difficult it is to find both skilled and unskilled workers. This is translating into higher wages."
- MS. MINEHAN: "A mutual fund company just instituted an across-the-board salary increase of between 5 and 10 percent on top of a merit increase of 5 percent"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Getting 'qualified workers' is becoming an increasingly familiar refrain... wage increases still remain in the 3 to 4 percent area"

Center of gravity: Nearly unanimous view that labor markets are tight. Unemployment around 5.2-5.4%, possibly below NAIRU. Wage pressures emerging but selective/occupational, not yet broad-based. Worker shortages widespread.

Minority views: None dissent from the "tight" characterization. Debate is whether tightness will produce broad inflation.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 54 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Personal consumption expenditures appear to be running at a moderate pace"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "business fixed investment has consistently outpaced the Greenbook projections"
- MS. YELLEN: "my concern with the possibility that consumer spending may be too robust rather than too weak has increased"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Retailers generally report that holiday sales have been somewhat better than expected"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is moderate to solid, supported by strong labor markets and confidence. Business investment is strong. Housing is softening. Overall slightly above trend.

Minority views: Some concern about consumer debt constraining spending (Greenspan, Phillips). Yellen sees consumption risks as balanced.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LINDSEY: "I think we have probably another year to go on the excessive credit expansion"
- MS. MINEHAN: "declining liquidity ratios... higher and higher degree of leverage in financial markets"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the external financing cost of capital has been relatively low. Profit levels and cash flow also have permitted internal funding."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative. Stock market is booming, credit is freely available, financing costs are low. Leverage is rising. Conditions are loose.

Minority views: Jordan relays bankers tightening credit for retail construction space, but this is a narrow sector concern.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I believe we confront a far greater likelihood that the next move will be up rather than down. So, I would hope that we can stay asymmetric today and still remain at 'B.'"
- MR. MELZER: "I would favor a modest increase in the degree of reserve restraint"
- MR. BROADDUS: "if you had recommended a slight snugging, my opposition would have been less than heated"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I agree that it is very likely that our next move will be up"

Center of gravity: Unanimous hold on rates, near-unanimous asymmetric tightening bias. One participant (Melzer) prefers immediate tightening. No one advocates easing. Lean hawkish but patient.

Minority views: Melzer favors immediate action. Broaddus would have accepted a snugging.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MELZER: "I would favor a modest increase in the degree of reserve restraint" (sole dissenter from hold)
- MR. BROADDUS: "my opposition would have been less than heated" (soft near-dissent)
- MR. MEYER: "it has clearly become a closer call as to whether the directive should be symmetric or asymmetric"

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on holding rates with asymmetric tightening bias. One dissenter (Melzer) on the hawkish side. Minor tactical differences about symmetric vs. asymmetric. No deep divisions.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. MINEHAN: extensive discussion of mutual fund liquidity, leverage, asset bubble concerns, margin requirements
- MR. LINDSEY: entire remarks framed around "irrational exuberance" -- three risks from budget illusions, excessive credit, dollar
- MS. YELLEN: "I consider the stock market a significant continuing risk to the outlook"
- MS. RIVLIN: "Clearly, the most worrisome problem on the horizon is the stock market"

Center of gravity: Financial stability receives meaningful attention from several participants (Minehan, Lindsey, Yellen, Rivlin, Phillips, Jordan), but it does not dominate the meeting. Labor markets and the growth outlook receive more collective attention. Above typical 1993-96 level due to "irrational exuberance" context.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "far greater likelihood that the next move will be up rather than down"
- MR. PARRY: "our forecast suggests that it may be necessary sometime next year to tighten the stance of policy"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "very likely that our next move will be up"
- MR. BOEHNE: "we can also continue to be patient"

Center of gravity: Clear forward signal that the next move is a tightening. But patient and data-dependent -- no urgency for near-term action. Mild tightening bias.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "there is considerable uncertainty at this time about the prospective strength of economic activity"
- MR. MEYER: "the range [of GDP forecasts] is remarkably tight... 2.0 to 2.4 percent"
- MR. GUYNN: "the major uncertainties continue to be whether labor shortages will eventually begin to be reflected in inflationary wage pressures"
- MR. KELLEY: "We continue to be in a period of watchful waiting"

Center of gravity: Moderate uncertainty, primarily about the NAIRU and whether wage pressures will translate to inflation. But the near-term growth outlook is viewed with reasonable confidence (narrow forecast range). Uncertainty is somewhat elevated but not unusual.
