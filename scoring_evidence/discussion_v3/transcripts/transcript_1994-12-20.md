# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1994-12-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a go-around on economic conditions followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include Presidents Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Forrestal, Broaddus, Stern, McTeer, McDonough (Vice Chairman), Jordan, Melzer, Minehan, and Governors Lindsey, Kelley, Phillips, LaWare, Blinder, Yellen (brief), and Chairman Greenspan. This is a mid-1990s transcript with the characteristic "Greenspan fence" separating economic assessment from policy prescription.

### Era Context
December 1994: The Fed has been tightening aggressively throughout 1994, having raised the funds rate 250 basis points (from 3% to 5.5%). The economy is growing very rapidly — Q4 GDP estimated at 4.5-5%. The 75bp move in November was the most recent action. Inflation remains moderate but pressures are building at intermediate levels. Unemployment is around 5.6%.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the midpoint — pressures are building but have NOT yet materialized in final goods prices. The center of gravity is that intermediate/materials prices are rising, labor markets are tight, but final goods inflation remains contained. Multiple speakers note difficulty passing costs through. Hoenig: "continued inflationary pressures." Moskow: "threatens to generate a noticeable increase in CPI inflation later in 1995." Broaddus: "talk about passing intermediate-level price increases through to final prices." McTeer: "little evidence of price rises on final goods." Forrestal: "about 50 percent of manufacturers... rising costs of raw materials... difficulty in passing these costs on." Blinder defends moderate inflation outlook. Greenspan notes unit costs "very well contained." Direction: above neutral but not alarming. Score ~57-60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants overwhelmingly see the economy operating above potential. Hoenig: "the economy is currently operating beyond long-term potential levels... perhaps even an overheating economy." Moskow: "real GDP already slightly above potential output." Stern: "operating at full employment or maybe even beyond." Phillips: "Growth in three of those years is now indicated to have been above potential." LaWare: "burning along at a rate well above potential." Greenspan: Q4 GDP could be "3 percent or 7 percent." Blinder: "probably pushed a bit past the NAIRU." This is unambiguous — above potential. Score ~59-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Extremely tight labor market reports from almost every district. Hoenig: "shortages of labor, spanning both skilled and unskilled." Moskow: Michigan unemployment lowest since 1969, Illinois lowest since late 1970s. Broaddus: "labor market conditions... tightening very noticeably." Stern: "most of their talk was about labor shortages across the board." McTeer: "capacity constraints are strong." Jordan: "unemployment rates are 4 percent or less in most of our metropolitan areas." BUT wage pressures remain muted — multiple speakers note shortages without wage acceleration. Greenspan: "wages are not responding to what is a very rapidly tightening labor market." Score ~62-67. Very tight but wage response lagging.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Very strong. Holiday sales robust across districts. Auto sales strong (15.5 million unit rate). Heavy truck sales at record high. Capital spending strong. Moskow: "Consumer activity... quite robust." Forrestal: "retailers are now expecting they will exceed last year's strong levels by as much as 6 to 8 percent." McTeer: "strong growth in the consumer sector." Melzer: "nominal retail sales rose at a 15.1 percent annual rate." Some tentative signs of moderation (auto softness, housing). Score ~63-67.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Policy has tightened significantly (250bp) but financial conditions are not fully transmitting. Greenspan: "easing of credit terms in the banking sector... difficulty getting banks to notice that interest rates are up." Lindsey: "bankers... are now issuing credit cards to C and D class credits." Parry notes models show limited GDP impact so far. Orange County episode created some temporary market stress but was contained. Bond market has rallied recently (post-November move). Overall: conditions have tightened somewhat but remain more accommodative than policy stance would suggest. Score ~45-50 — near neutral, with tightening underway but not yet biting.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee voted to hold rates (alternative B) but with asymmetric directive toward tightening. Most participants expect further tightening will be needed soon. LaWare and Broaddus wanted to raise 25bp today. Parry wanted 50bp today. Blinder, Lindsey, Forrestal, Stern preferred symmetric (slightly less hawkish). Greenspan: "the odds that we are going to have to move shortly after the first of the year... are quite high." Kelley: "overwhelmingly likely that we are going to need to go up at least some more." This is a hawkish hold — no action today but strong consensus for more tightening soon. Score ~65-68.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents on the vote for B asymmetric. However, there was notable disagreement: LaWare wanted alternative C (raise now); Broaddus and Hoenig wanted 25bp now; Parry wanted 50bp now; Blinder, Lindsey, Stern, Forrestal preferred symmetric. Greenspan noted a "very sharp split on symmetric versus asymmetric." Score ~40-45 — moderate disagreement on timing/pace but broad agreement on direction.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Orange County bankruptcy was a significant topic. Parry gave an extended discussion. Phillips mentioned Bankers Trust, mutual fund mark-to-market risks. McDonough noted market thinness and Bankers Trust uncertainty. Kelley discussed consumer debt being "off the charts." Greenspan noted Orange County turmoil. More than passing references but not the dominant topic. Score ~30-38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Most participants expect further tightening at or before the next meeting. Greenspan: "the odds that we are going to have to move shortly after the first of the year, no later than our next meeting, are quite high." Moskow: "expectation that economic developments... will confirm the need for an increase at the January meeting, if not before." Parry: expects 50bp by early January. The asymmetric directive itself signals tightening bias. Score ~72-76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Somewhat elevated. McDonough: "there is great uncertainty about where the economy is going." Phillips: "considerable uncertainty as we go into 1995." Broaddus: "considerable risks in the forecast... greater perhaps than we have seen earlier." Several speakers question models (NAIRU uncertainty, lag uncertainty). Jordan questions whether growth is demand-driven or supply-driven. But this is not extreme uncertainty — most are fairly confident in the broad direction. Score ~42-48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (61) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (64): Both above 50, consistent — economy above potential with tight labor markets. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (66) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (74): Forward guidance is somewhat more hawkish than the current action (hold), which makes sense — they held today but signal strong likelihood of near-term tightening. The divergence is explained by timing (waiting past year-end).

### Speaker Count Summary
- Economy above potential / strong: Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Forrestal, Broaddus, Stern, McTeer, McDonough, Melzer, Kelley, Phillips, LaWare, Blinder, Greenspan (~14)
- More tightening needed soon: Most participants (~12-13)
- Tighten today: LaWare, Broaddus, Hoenig, Parry (~4)
- Wait/cautious on tightening: Blinder, Lindsey, Forrestal, Stern, Jordan (~5)
- New economy / lower NAIRU possible: Jordan, Forrestal, Kelley, Greenspan (as possibility) (~4)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "continued inflationary pressures that confront us as we enter 1995"
- MR. MOSKOW: "this robust pace of real activity threatens to generate a noticeable increase in CPI inflation later in 1995, moving inflation above the Greenbook forecast"
- MR. MCTEER: "we have seen little evidence of price rises on final goods and services, especially at the retail level"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "about 50 percent of manufacturers indicate that they are still being pressured by the rising costs of raw materials. They are noting difficulty again in passing these costs on to final goods."
- MR. BLINDER: "the recent news is good: To the extent that the errors are coming in not quite zero, they are coming in favorably"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as somewhat above neutral — intermediate/materials price pressures are clearly building, and the tight economy creates upside risk, but final goods inflation has remained surprisingly well-contained. The worry is prospective rather than realized.

**Minority views**: Stern and Hoenig see more inflation risk ("if we get an acceleration of inflation, we will not need a magnifying glass to discern it"). Jordan and Forrestal suggest structural changes may keep inflation lower than expected. This widens the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 61 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "the economy is currently operating beyond long-term potential levels... perhaps even an overheating economy"
- MR. MOSKOW: "With real GDP already slightly above potential output"
- MR. LAWARE: "burning along at a rate well above potential"
- MR. BLINDER: "the best guess is that the economy probably has pushed a little past the NAIRU, or noninflationary capacity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-universal agreement that the economy is operating above potential. Q4 GDP growth estimated at 4.5-5%. However, the overshoot is described as modest ("slightly above," "a little past") by the center of gravity, even as the growth rate is very rapid. Score reflects above-potential but not extreme overheating.

**Minority views**: Jordan questions whether the standard framework applies — perhaps higher potential growth means less overheating than traditional models suggest.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 64 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "In almost every part of the District, we continue to hear reports of shortages of labor, spanning both skilled and unskilled"
- MR. MOSKOW: "In Michigan, the unemployment rate in November was the lowest since the end of 1969... surveys of employer hiring plans in the Midwest are the highest in at least 18 years"
- MR. STERN: "most of their talk was about labor shortages across the board, from the skilled to the entry level jobs"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "wages are not responding to what is a very rapidly tightening labor market"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight — widespread shortage reports, unemployment at multi-decade lows in several states. However, the unusual feature is that wage pressures remain muted. The level of tightness is high but not yet producing the expected wage-price spiral.

**Minority views**: Phillips notes "there may be more flexibility in the labor market than we had thought." Jordan observes businesses paying bonuses but "not building pay increases into their wage bases."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "Indicators of consumer activity in the Seventh District remain quite robust. For most of the District, the holiday sales season is running very strong"
- MR. MELZER: "nominal retail sales rose at a 15.1 percent annual rate from July to November, the fastest four-month pace since the spring of 1987"
- MR. MCTEER: "The improvement of job prospects, consumer confidence, and credit availability are propelling strong growth in the consumer sector. The need to add to capacity is putting upward pressure on investment spending."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "retailers are now expecting that they will exceed last year's strong levels by as much as 6 to 8 percent"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are clearly above trend — holiday sales very strong, auto sales robust, heavy truck sales at records, capital spending firm. Some early tentative signs of moderation in autos and housing, but overall momentum very strong.

**Minority views**: Kelley worries consumer debt is "off the charts" and spending could stop. Blinder expects "considerable deceleration" in 1995 from lag effects.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 47 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are having difficulty getting banks to notice that interest rates are up. The interest rates on automobile installment paper are really lagging. Everybody is trying to protect market share"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "easing of credit terms in the banking sector"
- MR. LINDSEY: "bankers... are now issuing credit cards to C and D class credits, and they are doing so profitably"
- MR. PARRY: "the evidence suggests that the current pace of real economic activity has not been diminished very much by the tighter stance of monetary policy this year"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite 250bp of tightening in the fed funds rate, financial conditions have not tightened much in practice. Banks are easing lending terms, credit is freely available, and the economy shows little restraint from policy. The bond market has recently rallied. Conditions are near-neutral, perhaps marginally on the loose side given policy context.

**Minority views**: McDonough argues monetary policy may be tighter than it appears based on various indicators (real fed funds rate, monetary aggregates, yield curve).

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 66 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the odds that we are going to have to move shortly after the first of the year, no later than our next meeting, are quite high"
- MR. KELLEY: "it is overwhelmingly likely that we are going to need to go up at least some more"
- MR. PARRY: "I would prefer a 50 basis point increase in the funds rate"
- MR. BLINDER: "I for one have an open mind about future increases in short rates"
- MR. LAWARE: "I would prefer alternative C symmetric" (raise now)

**Center-of-gravity**: This is a hawkish hold. The overwhelming majority believes further tightening is coming, likely within weeks. Four participants wanted to raise rates at this meeting. The decision to hold was driven by: (1) the recent 75bp move needing time to work, (2) year-end market thinness, (3) Orange County uncertainty. But the forward lean is clearly toward tightening.

**Minority views**: Blinder and Lindsey urged patience and symmetric directive. Forrestal questioned whether much more tightening was necessary. Jordan warned against being seen as "anti-growth."

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "there is a very sharp split on symmetric versus asymmetric"
- Four participants (LaWare, Broaddus, Hoenig, Parry) preferred immediate tightening vs. the majority who preferred to hold
- Blinder, Lindsey, Stern, Forrestal preferred symmetric directive
- No formal voting dissents on the final "B asymmetric" outcome

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement. All agree the economy is strong and the direction is toward tightening, but there are distinct camps on timing (now vs. January) and on the asymmetric directive. The split is tactical, not fundamental. No formal dissents.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: Extended discussion of Orange County investment pool losses ($2 billion), impact on California municipal bonds, bank exposures
- MS. PHILLIPS: "I don't think we have seen all of that play out. I don't think we have yet seen all of the implications of the Bankers Trust situation play out. When people get their year-end mark-to-market statements on their mutual funds, there may well be some surprises."
- MR. KELLEY: "Consumer debt is off the charts on the up side in relation to consumer income or net worth"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: markets "very thin," Bankers Trust creating uncertainty

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability gets meaningful but not dominant attention. Orange County is discussed at length by Parry and referenced by several others. Bankers Trust and consumer debt are additional concerns. However, most participants treat these as contained risks rather than systemic threats.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 74 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the odds that we are going to have to move shortly after the first of the year, no later than our next meeting, are quite high"
- MR. MOSKOW: "expectation that economic developments in the coming weeks will confirm the need for an increase in the funds rate at the January meeting, if not before"
- MR. PARRY: "I am concerned that further delay beyond [mid-January] would lead to a deterioration of inflationary expectations"
- Asymmetric directive toward tightening adopted
- MR. KELLEY: "it is overwhelmingly likely that we are going to need to go up at least some more"

**Center-of-gravity**: Very clear tightening signal. The forward guidance is unambiguously hawkish — most expect to raise rates at the next meeting or before. The asymmetric directive codifies this lean. This is stronger than the current action (hold) because the hold is explicitly temporary.

**Minority views**: Lindsey: "I may even want to delay at our next meeting on January 31-February 1." Forrestal: "I am not convinced that any great further degree of tightening is necessary."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I feel that there is great uncertainty about where the economy is going, or at least I am very uncertain"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "there is considerable uncertainty as we go into 1995"
- MR. BROADDUS: "There are considerable risks in the forecast at this point, greater perhaps than we have seen earlier"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "whether or not there is something different going on in the economy... whether or not there can be greater growth in the economy with less inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Participants express genuine uncertainty about: (1) whether the economy will slow as forecast, (2) whether NAIRU has shifted, (3) the timing and magnitude of policy lags, (4) whether a "new economy" paradigm applies. However, most are fairly confident about the broad direction (economy strong, more tightening needed), so this is not deep uncertainty about fundamentals.
