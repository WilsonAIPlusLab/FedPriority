# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-03-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers a go-around of regional and national economic assessments followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Broaddus, Jordan, Hoenig, Parry, Moskow, Minehan, McDonough, Guynn, Boehne, Stern, Rivlin, McTeer, Meyer, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
March 1997: The U.S. economy is in the sixth year of an expansion. Unemployment is near 5.3%, and real GDP growth has been surprising to the upside. Inflation remains subdued (core CPI around 2.5%). The Fed has held the funds rate at 5.25% for over a year since the last easing in early 1996. This is the meeting where the FOMC decides to hike 25bp to 5.50% -- the first tightening since February 1994.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently low but with rising upside risks. The center of gravity is that actual inflation data remain benign -- "inflation is flat to down" (Kelley), "there is relatively little talk of imminent price increases" (Broaddus), "consumer and producer price increases have been subdued" (McTeer). However, nearly every participant flags upside risks due to tight labor markets and above-trend growth. Greenspan: "we still do not have concrete evidence to suggest that the inflation rate has stopped going down." Parry forecasts core CPI rising to 2.75%-3% in 1997-98. McDonough's staff expects 3.3% by 1998. The key tension is: current inflation is low, but future inflation risk is high. Per rubric: score what participants perceive NOW. Current inflation is at or slightly below target, but with strong concern about building pressures. Direction: slightly below to at target currently, with upside risk acknowledged by nearly all. Score should be around 48-53 -- inflation is not yet elevated, but participants perceive incipient pressure building. I'll score 50, reflecting "at target but building pressures recognized."

Wait -- the rubric says score what participants PERCEIVE NOW. Many participants explicitly state inflation is still declining or flat. But many also say the risks of higher inflation are "significant" or "considerably greater on the upside." The actual perception is that inflation is currently benign but risks are tilting up. This puts it around 48-52. I'll go 50 -- balanced current reading with acknowledged upside risk embedded in the uncertainty band.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Current inflation is benign but risks are tilting up. 50 is appropriate. The uncertainty band should be wide because the forward-looking risks skew higher.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants overwhelmingly describe the economy as operating at or above potential. Broaddus: "the economy is now operating at a very high level." Parry: "excess demand pressures most likely are building." Moskow: "full resource utilization." Kelley: "a fully utilized economy." Greenspan: "we are not at this stage moving into what I would describe as an overheated boom. We are short of that." Minehan: "above-potential growth." Multiple participants describe growth at 3.5%+ versus trend of ~2%, with unemployment around 5.3% (below many estimates of NAIRU at the time). The bright-line rule says "above potential" language requires >50. Nearly all participants use such language. Score: 55-60. Not overheating yet per Greenspan, but clearly above potential. I'll score 57.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. 57 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant theme. Broaddus: "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight." Moskow: "District labor markets are still very tight... labor shortages are constraining both employment and output growth." Minehan: labor tightness "broadening a bit." Hoenig: "contacts describe our labor market as tight." Stern: "labor markets are still tight, and there are widespread labor shortages." Greenspan: "the tightness in labor markets is increasing." Kelley: "strong and persistent job formation." Multiple districts report difficulty finding workers at all skill levels. Unemployment at ~5.3% was considered below NAIRU by many. However, wage pressures remain muted -- signing bonuses and targeted increases rather than broad wage acceleration. Score: 63-68. Very tight but wage pass-through is limited. I'll score 65.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence is clearly tight. 65 matches. Consistent with late-1990s anchor of 65-75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Nearly all participants describe strong consumption and investment. Broaddus: "Consumer spending clearly seems to have accelerated." McDonough: "levels of consumer spending, consumer confidence, and residential construction are so high" and "business fixed investment seems unusually robust." Rivlin: "Consumer spending is strong; inventories are lean; business investment, housing, and nonresidential construction, you name it, look good." Kelley: "sky-high consumer confidence with jobs plentiful, and supportive wealth effects." Greenspan notes the wealth effect is kicking in. Growth at 3.5%+ in the prior four quarters. Multiple mentions of strong retail sales, rising construction, robust business investment. Some mentions of potential slowing (Guynn, McTeer construction softening), but center of gravity is clearly above trend. Score: 62-67. I'll score 64.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence is strong. 64 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants describe financial conditions as accommodative/loose. Stern: "financial conditions remain very comfortable whether we look at interest rates, growth in the broad measures of money, equity prices... or credit availability in general." Jordan: "lending standards relating to the construction of commercial real estate have disappeared." Boehne: "Competition for bank lending is stiff, with the predictable squeezing of margins and slippage of underwriting standards." Multiple mentions of strong equity prices, wealth effects, easy credit. Greenspan: risk premiums declining, cost of capital low. Meyer discusses the horizontal LM curve accommodating demand. Direction: conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Score: 20-28. I'll score 24.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says loose. 24 matches (low score = loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous support for 25bp hike. Several participants (Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Hoenig, Guynn) would have preferred asymmetric (hawkish) tilt. Parry mentions Taylor Rule suggesting 100bp total tightening. Broaddus: "the case for a larger move is stronger than indicated." Greenspan: "the odds are better than 50/50 that the move we are considering will not be our last tightening move." Even the most dovish participants (Rivlin, McDonough) support the 25bp hike. No one argues for holding. The debate is between 25bp vs 50bp, and symmetric vs asymmetric. This is clearly in hawkish territory -- a consensus for tightening with several wanting more. Score: 63-68. I'll score 65.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Consensus for tightening, several want more. 65 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote is unanimous for 25bp. However, there are meaningful differences: several prefer asymmetric directive (Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Hoenig, Guynn), some think 50bp has a case (Broaddus, Parry), while Rivlin and McDonough emphasize uncertainty and caution. The differences are mostly about pace/degree, not direction. No formal dissents. Score: 25-32. I'll score 28.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Broad agreement on action, differences on degree. 28 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants mention financial conditions -- equity valuations/wealth effects (Greenspan, McDonough, Kelley, Stern), commercial real estate concerns (Jordan, Guynn, Broaddus), lending standards slipping (Jordan, Boehne). However, these are contextual mentions within the broader economic discussion, not a primary focus of concern. No one frames financial stability as a dominant risk per se. Score: 25-35. I'll score 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan: "the odds are better than 50/50 that the move we are considering will not be our last tightening move." Parry: Taylor Rule suggests 100bp total. Broaddus: "this move very likely will not be our last and that we will have to tighten further... tightening may happen sooner rather than later." The decision is for a symmetric directive, meaning no formal tilt. But the discussion clearly leans toward further tightening ahead. Score: 65-70. The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- further tightening likely but data-dependent. I'll score 67.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says further tightening likely. 67 matches. Coherent with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN of 65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Significant uncertainty is expressed throughout. Greenspan: "There is a wide range of opinions in the way our society is looking at this inflation phenomenon." McDonough: "what I find myself concentrating on is what we do not know." Meyer: extensive discussion of uncertainty about NAIRU. Boehne: "greater divergence between the person-in-the-street view and this macro framework than I have seen in a long time." Stern: "We are uncertain about the outlook; we are uncertain about the effectiveness of policy." Parry: "I emphasize the word 'risk' because we have not actually seen signs of rising inflation in the data. This fact obviously raises uncertainty about the future." Multiple participants note the puzzle of why inflation hasn't risen. Score: 48-55. Elevated uncertainty -- genuine puzzlement about the inflation-employment relationship. I'll score 52.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Labor tightness is higher than output gap, which makes sense -- labor was the binding constraint while capital utilization was more flexible (as Greenspan explicitly discusses).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Aligned -- hawkish current action with hawkish forward lean.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (24) and HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65): Coherent -- loose financial conditions motivate tightening policy.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. KELLEY: "inflation is flat to down according to many statistical series"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still do not have concrete evidence to suggest that the inflation rate has stopped going down"
- MR. PARRY: "I see a significant risk of an increase in underlying inflation in the years ahead... our forecast shows core CPI inflation of around 2-3/4 percent in 1997 and 3 percent in 1998"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "we have raised the rate of core inflation expected to be reached in 1998 to 3.3 percent"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Current inflation readings are at or slightly below target, but nearly every participant sees significant upside risk to inflation ahead due to tight labor markets and above-trend growth. The score of 50 reflects the current benign reading; the wide uncertainty band captures the strong forward-looking concern.

**Minority views**: McTeer notes "consumer and producer price increases have been subdued" and commodity/gold prices are not concerning -- the most sanguine view. Parry and McDonough are the most alarmed about future inflation.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "the economy is now operating at a very high level and it strikes me as being quite vulnerable to upside shocks"
- MR. PARRY: "excess demand pressures most likely are building in the economy"
- MR. KELLEY: "Growth for the past four quarters including this one is going to be somewhere in excess of 3-1/2 percent. That is certainly well beyond anybody's estimate of potential in an economy whose resources already seem to be fully utilized."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are not at this stage moving into what I would describe as an overheated boom. We are short of that."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is operating modestly above potential with growth well above trend. Not yet overheating but clearly beyond sustainable capacity, particularly in labor markets.

**Minority views**: Greenspan tempers the view slightly, noting the economy is "short of" an overheated boom and some indicators may be softening.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "Labor markets remain exceedingly tight throughout most of the District"
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor shortages are constraining both employment and output growth"
- MS. MINEHAN: "the definition of 'selected' [job categories that are tight] seems to be broadening a bit"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the tightness in labor markets is increasing... we are running out of space to expand, at least in terms of labor market resources"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are clearly tight across virtually all districts, with shortages broadening from skilled to unskilled categories. Wage pressure is present but contained by temporary factors (job insecurity, rising participation, strong dollar).

**Minority views**: Guynn notes "We still get very few reports of unusually large increases in wages" -- even in tight markets, wage acceleration is limited.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 64 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. RIVLIN: "Consumer spending is strong; inventories are lean; business investment, housing, and nonresidential construction, you name it, look good. It just does not look like an economy that is running out of steam."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the levels of consumer spending, consumer confidence, and residential construction are so high... business fixed investment seems unusually robust"
- MR. KELLEY: "sky-high consumer confidence with jobs plentiful, and supportive wealth effects"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "personal consumption expenditures are... significantly affected by the wealth effect"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumption and investment are running well above trend, driven by wealth effects, high consumer confidence, and strong business investment. Virtually every district reports stronger-than-expected spending.

**Minority views**: Some pockets of moderation noted -- McTeer reports construction slowing in some categories; Guynn sees some regional softening from earlier highs.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "financial conditions remain very comfortable whether we look at interest rates, growth in the broad measures of money, equity prices... or credit availability in general"
- MR. JORDAN: "lending standards relating to the construction of commercial real estate have disappeared in the last couple of months"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Competition for bank lending is stiff, with the predictable squeezing of margins and slippage of underwriting standards"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly loose -- equity prices are elevated, credit is freely available, lending standards are slipping, and risk premiums are compressed. Multiple participants describe conditions as accommodative or comfortable.

**Minority views**: None -- broad agreement that financial conditions are easy.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I do not think that there is any particular urgency to move in a very aggressive way. I do think we have to move... 25 basis points is enough for now"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the case for a larger move is stronger than indicated by others"
- MR. PARRY: "estimated Taylor Rule... suggests a gradual rise in the funds rate totaling about 100 basis points"
- MS. RIVLIN: "A small move to restrain growth and the future acceleration of inflation that seems increasingly likely could prolong this remarkable expansion"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous support for a 25bp tightening. Several participants make the case for 50bp or asymmetric directive (further hawkish lean). No one argues for holding. The debate centers on how much to tighten, not whether to tighten.

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Parry lean toward more aggressive tightening; McDonough and Rivlin emphasize caution and uncertainty but still support the hike.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote for 25bp with symmetric directive
- MR. BROADDUS: "I can accept your recommendation... the case for a larger move is stronger" (mild reservation)
- MR. PARRY: "I also would strongly prefer to have language in the directive that is asymmetric toward further tightening" (strongest procedural disagreement)
- Several others note preference for asymmetry but accept symmetry without dissent

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on direction and magnitude of action. Differences are about pace and forward signaling (symmetric vs. asymmetric directive), not about fundamental assessment or whether to act. No formal dissents.

**Minority views**: Parry and Broaddus represent the most hawkish wing; neither dissents formally.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "lending agreements with no takeouts being arranged, no permanent financing being locked up, no tenants being signed" (commercial real estate lending concerns)
- MR. GUYNN: "a few of my contacts in real estate and banking are expressing some concern about developers who seem to be forgetting the lessons of the 1980s"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: discusses declining risk premiums, wealth effects, and notes "What we are trying to avoid is bubbles that break"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives scattered mentions -- commercial real estate excesses, wealth effects, and lending standards -- but these are subsidiary to the main macroeconomic discussion. Not a primary focus of the meeting.

**Minority views**: Jordan gives the most attention to financial excesses in lending markets.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 67 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think the odds are better than 50/50 that the move we are considering will not be our last tightening move. We are very likely to have to move again"
- MR. BROADDUS: "this move very likely will not be our last and that we will have to tighten further... tightening may happen sooner rather than later, perhaps before the May meeting"
- MR. PARRY: Taylor Rule suggests 100bp total tightening through year-end
- MR. MEYER: "containing a threat to higher inflation is likely to require some further increase in short-term rates beyond the small change contemplated today"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward further tightening. The Committee chose a symmetric directive, but the discussion strongly suggests this is the beginning of a tightening cycle, not a one-and-done move.

**Minority views**: McDonough and Rivlin express more uncertainty about the need for further action, preferring to wait for data.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the best thing to concentrate on at the moment... is what we do not know... we are not, and certainly I am not, altogether sure why inflation and the cost-push causes of inflation have been so benevolent"
- MR. PARRY: "I emphasize the word 'risk' because we have not actually seen signs of rising inflation in the data. This fact obviously raises uncertainty about the future."
- MR. STERN: "We are uncertain about the outlook; we are uncertain about the effectiveness of policy; and because of those uncertainties, I think we ought to be cautious."
- MR. BOEHNE: "there is a greater divergence between the person-in-the-street view and this macro framework than I have seen in a long time"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express elevated uncertainty, particularly about why inflation has remained quiescent despite tight labor markets. The productivity puzzle, NAIRU uncertainty, and unpredictable temporary factors all contribute to genuine intellectual puzzlement about the outlook.

**Minority views**: Meyer and Parry express more conviction about the traditional Phillips curve framework, while others are more openly uncertain.
