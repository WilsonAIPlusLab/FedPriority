### 1. Document Type

The document covers two sections: the go-around (economic assessment) and the policy discussion (vote and summary remarks). Greenspan provides a comprehensive policy summary before the unanimous vote round. The policy vote captures all ~16 participants concurring.

### 2. Era Context
May 7, 2002. Early recovery from the brief 2001 recession (exacerbated by September 11 attacks). The Fed had cut rates aggressively to 1.75% (historically low for that era). Key backdrop: below-potential output, slack labor market, subdued/falling inflation, severe business investment collapse, resilient consumer spending, and uncertainty about the durability of recovery. NOT a deep recession — the economy is recovering — but output gap clearly negative.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bies: "inflation should remain well under 2 percent for the rest of the year"
- Kohn: "measures of underlying price and labor cost increases...dropped further from already low levels. In the staff forecast, inflation remains quite damped—and slightly lower than in the previous forecast"
- Greenspan: "They perceive an utter inability to raise prices. Pricing power, if anything, may be eroding, not expanding"

**Center-of-gravity:** Participants uniformly perceive inflation well below target with falling cost measures, no pricing power, and a larger-than-previously-estimated output gap exerting downward pressure on prices.

**Minority views:** Kohn notes upside risk factors (faster health care costs, higher oil, dollar decline) but the dominant framing is clearly disinflationary.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "the shortfall of actual output relative to its potential now is larger than previously thought"
- Kohn: "little progress is made on reducing the unemployment rate over coming months, and capacity utilization just edges higher"
- Bies: "given the level of excess capacity and the lack of pricing power by businesses"

**Center-of-gravity:** Output clearly below potential with gap recently revised larger. Economy in a recovery pause, not at potential. Consistent with 31–40 range (below potential, recovering, gap persists).

**Minority views:** Greenspan notes this is "exactly what we see in the annals of business cycle history" — a normal pause, not an alarming shortfall.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Bies: "employment growth will lag until business confidence is restored"
- Kohn: "little progress is made on reducing the unemployment rate over coming months; the greater growth of structural productivity going forward keeps the unemployment rate from falling as fast"
- Greenspan: businesses focused on cost-cutting, not capacity expansion

**Center-of-gravity:** Labor market clearly below full employment, unemployment elevated and not expected to improve materially near-term. Structural productivity gains delaying labor market recovery.

**Minority views:** None. Consensus on soft labor market.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "Personal consumption expenditures are high, and indeed they are holding up. The trouble is that, because they are high, the prospective rate of growth cannot be very large"
- Bies: "we have seen a lack of business investment for over eighteen months now"
- Greenspan: "nonresidential construction is not a sector that seems about to turn around and move up"

**Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending is a stabilizing floor but not a growth driver; business investment severely depressed for 18+ months with no near-term recovery expected. Net position is below trend, dragged by investment collapse despite consumption resilience.

**Minority views:** Bies characterizes consumer as "mainstay" — modest upward offset on spending side, but investment drag dominates the net assessment.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Bies: "how accommodative our monetary policy is at the moment, with real funds rates at zero"
- Kohn: "the real federal funds rate is far enough below a reasonable range of estimates of its equilibrium value to indicate that policy will have to be tightened at some point"
- Kohn: "interest rates have fallen appreciably all the way out to ten years"

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions are broadly accommodative. Real funds rate at zero, long-term rates declining intermeeting, policy described as well below equilibrium. Conditions clearly supportive rather than restrictive.

**Minority views:** Equity prices declined and dollar fell intermeeting — mild tightening of financial conditions via wealth and trade channels — but not dominant in participant discussion.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "My recommendation...is that we stay at 1¾ percent on the funds rate and retain the statement that we view the risks as balanced"
- All ~16 participants: immediate and unanimous concurrence with hold + balanced risks
- Bies: "We shouldn't be lowering rates. But it's not [the time for] raising rates either"

**Center-of-gravity:** Pure hold consensus with balanced risks statement. No participant advocates for easing OR tightening at this meeting. Lean is slightly dovish (holding at historically low rate with no tightening bias) but the balanced risks language places it near neutral (47).

**Minority views:** Kohn and Bies note tightening will eventually be necessary — forward-looking concerns, not current policy advocacy.

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions and markets present as contextual factors (equity decline, dollar move, corporate governance post-Enron, risk premiums) but not a major sustained discussion focus. A few passing references, not deep analysis. Score in 25–35 range.

**Minority views:** None — no participant devotes extended focus to financial stability per se.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 46 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Balanced risks statement explicitly retained: "retain the statement that we view the risks as balanced"
- Kohn: "the real federal funds rate is far enough below...its equilibrium value to indicate that policy will have to be tightened at some point to forestall increasing inflation pressures"
- Kohn: "the market expects you to view the risks as balanced, and so such a statement should have minimal effects on expected interest rates"

**Center-of-gravity:** Forward signal is neutral (balanced risks). Tightening is the eventual direction but explicitly framed as conditional on data, with significant uncertainty about timing. No date-based or threshold-based commitment. Near-neutral (46).

**Minority views:** Market and some commentators expect tightening in June or August; no participant explicitly endorses that near-term timeline.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 54 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan (terrorism): "we have no idea what that probability is. None of us has knowledge of that from our day-by-day reading of the daily newspapers...There's a lot of uncertainty, understandably"
- Greenspan: "There are obviously a significant number of risks to our forecasts"
- Kohn: disentangling structural vs. one-time productivity gains "has been especially difficult"

**Center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants identify genuine uncertainty about investment recovery, productivity trajectory, terrorism risk, and fiscal discipline. Greenspan explicitly identifies a major tail risk (terrorism) that is not priced by markets. Elevated above normal but not extreme (not "uncharted territory"). Score in 46–60 range.

**Minority views:** Greenspan also expresses confidence that the business cycle pattern is historically normal — slight moderating effect on uncertainty score.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 38 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**
Kohn: "surveys tend to confirm that the public's long-term inflation expectations remain damped, despite the gentler and later trajectory for policy tightening and the rise in energy prices." Expectations anchored but slightly below the comfort zone implied by the 50 anchor. Score below 50.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 72 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**
Bies: "much of the productivity growth that we're enjoying currently is due to the innovation-investment cycles associated with the extremely high levels of investment in the late 1990s." Greenspan at length: productivity driven by cost-cutting investment, compositional effects of high-tech spending, multifactor productivity explaining a significant proportion of labor productivity gains. Current productivity genuinely high/above historical trend, though Bies worries it will eventually slow. Score well above 50 (72).

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: 38 ±10 | Confidence: Low**
Kohn: "the dollar's fall over the intermeeting period seems at least partly a response to disappointment in the vigor of the U.S. expansion." Parry raises dollar depreciation assumptions as a sensitivity issue. Dollar weakening = concern about weakness, not strength → score below 50 (higher = concern about STRENGTH). Score: 38.
