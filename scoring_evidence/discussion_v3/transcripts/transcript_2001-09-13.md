# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2001-09-13 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an emergency FOMC conference call held on September 13, 2001, two days after the September 11 terrorist attacks. Speakers are identified by name. This is explicitly an emergency meeting -- the rubric notes that September 2001 is a listed emergency meeting. Discussion is relatively short and narrowly focused on crisis logistics and the immediate policy response. N/A should be used liberally.

### Era Context
September 2001: The economy was already in recession (the 2001 recession officially started in March 2001). The Fed had been cutting rates aggressively through 2001. Then the 9/11 attacks hit, creating an unprecedented shock. The fed funds rate had already been brought down from 6.5% to 3.5% through the year. This meeting is entirely dominated by the crisis response.

### Speaker Count and Views
Identified participant speakers with substantive economic/policy views:
- **Chairman Greenspan**: Economy was in "precarious balance"; shock is clearly negative; too soon to judge magnitude
- **Vice Chairman McDonough**: Focused on infrastructure/logistics; deferred on rate move timing/size
- **MR. POOLE**: Provision of liquidity most important; leans toward readiness to cut; suggests 25bp might suffice but wouldn't rule out 50bp
- **MR. PARRY**: Economy was weakening pre-Tuesday; favors move soon; opposes 25bp, wants something more substantive (implying 50bp)
- **MR. MOSKOW**: Manufacturing weak, consumer spending soft and softening; favors moving sooner, 50bp
- **MR. STERN**: Favors moving sooner rather than later; psychology is key; effects will be negative
- **MR. HOENIG**: Manufacturing slow, no improvement in business fixed investment; if moving, 50bp is appropriate
- **MR. SANTOMERO**: Data were on weaker side; favors 50bp move before markets open
- **MR. BROADDUS**: Manufacturing was stabilizing pre-crisis, now much less favorable; favors 50bp, warns against more than 50bp
- **MS. MINEHAN**: Was becoming more optimistic on manufacturing bottoming; now demand dried up; marginally favors 25bp but could go with 50bp
- **MR. GUYNN**: Growing concern pre-crisis; favors more measured move (25bp) to preserve ammunition
- **MR. MCTEER**: Emergency situation; 50bp is right amount; do it Monday morning
- **MR. JORDAN**: Liquidity already injected massively ($100B); rate cut mainly for Main Street messaging, not needed for Wall Street

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Virtually no discussion of inflation by any participant. The entire focus is on the demand-side shock and maintaining confidence. In the context of 2001, inflation was already subdued. No participant mentions price pressures. Direction: Below midpoint -- inflation is a non-issue. However, given near-zero discussion, confidence is low. Score ~35 based on era context (2001 disinflation environment) and complete absence of inflation concern.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy as below potential and weakening. Greenspan: "precarious balance" pre-crisis, now clearly negative shock. Parry: "economic situation was looking weaker." Moskow: "manufacturing sector was clearly weak," "consumer spending was soft and getting softer." Hoenig: "manufacturing slow and no signs whatsoever of any improvement in business fixed investment." Multiple participants describe an already-weak economy hit by a massive negative shock. Direction: Well below potential. Score ~25-30. Economy was already in recession, now hit by major shock.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Not directly discussed. No participant mentions unemployment, hiring, or labor conditions. Given the recession context and the shock, labor market was loosening. But without direct evidence, confidence is low. Score ~32 based on recessionary context.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Extensively discussed. Greenspan: consumer expenditures "holding up" pre-crisis but confidence declining sharply. Moskow: "consumer spending was soft and getting softer"; Sears sales down 50% Tuesday, down 10% Wednesday; Lands End calls off 75% and 60%. Minehan: directors say "demand had just dried up. People were not buying and in fact were not showing up in stores." Hoenig: "no signs whatsoever of any improvement in business fixed investment." Capital goods orders "remained weak." Direction: Well below trend, contracting further. Score ~22-25.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Significant discussion of financial conditions but focused on operational/infrastructure disruption rather than credit tightness per se. Greenspan notes corporate yields may have eased slightly but flight to quality widening spreads. McDonough describes massive infrastructure disruption. Massive liquidity injection ($100B). Stock exchanges closed. Direction: Conditions are clearly tight/stressed due to the crisis -- markets shut, infrastructure damaged, flight to quality. Score ~72-75.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Overwhelmingly dovish. Nearly all participants favor cutting rates. The debate is about 25bp vs 50bp and timing (now vs Monday). Rough count: 50bp camp: Parry, Moskow, Hoenig, Santomero, Broaddus, McTeer (6 clear). 25bp or measured: Poole (leans 25bp), Minehan (marginally 25bp), Guynn (measured/smaller). Stern favors moving sooner, doesn't specify size. McDonough and Jordan don't commit to specific action. Center of gravity is clearly toward 50bp cut soon. This is strong dovish in an already-easing cycle. Score ~15-18.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is broad agreement that a rate cut is needed. The disagreement is about magnitude (25bp vs 50bp) and timing (before Monday open vs Monday morning vs wait). No one opposes cutting. The range of views is modest -- tactical disagreement within a dovish consensus. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: This is an enormous topic. McDonough's entire lengthy discussion is about financial infrastructure, exchange reopening, clearing bank operations, building stability near exchanges. Greenspan discusses risk premiums, Dow estimates from European markets. Multiple participants discuss liquidity provision. Financial stability/conditions IS the meeting along with the rate decision. Score ~85-90.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Forward signal is clearly dovish/easing. Stern: "if it turns out...such an action proves to have been unnecessary...we can always reverse it." Broaddus: same sentiment. Multiple participants signal willingness to cut again at October meeting. Minehan: "having the October meeting available to move again." The forward lean is toward continued easing. Score ~12-15.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Extremely high. Greenspan: "How significant that deterioration is I think is exceptionally difficult to judge"; "I think it's much too soon to make judgments about how this will ultimately come out." Poole: "we may be faced with events that we can't predict." Stern: "I don't know how all this is going to play out." The entire meeting is suffused with deep uncertainty about what comes next. This is a genuine "uncharted territory" situation. Score ~82-88.

### Polarity Checks

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy clearly below potential and worsening. Score 27. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: No participant discusses inflation, price pressures, or price stability concerns. The entire discussion is about demand weakness, confidence, and crisis response.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is completely absent from the discussion; in the context of the 2001 disinflation environment and the massive negative demand shock, participants implicitly view inflation as a non-issue, well below concerning levels.
- **Minority views**: None expressed.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 27 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we were still in that very precarious balance with regard to which way the economy was going to go" and "the net effect from this tragic event, any way one can cut it, has to be negative." MR. PARRY: "the economic situation was looking weaker." MR. HOENIG: "manufacturing slow and no signs whatsoever of any improvement in business fixed investment."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was already in recession with weak manufacturing and softening consumer spending; the 9/11 shock added a large negative impulse to an already below-potential economy.
- **Minority views**: Greenspan and a few others (Minehan, Broaddus, Moskow) noted that manufacturing may have been stabilizing pre-crisis, but all agree the shock is clearly negative.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-10 [Low confidence]**
- **Evidence**: No participant directly discusses labor market conditions, unemployment, or hiring. The recessionary context and the overwhelming negative shock imply labor slack, but this is inferred.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market conditions are not discussed; scored based on the clear recessionary context and massive demand shock, implying below-full-employment conditions.
- **Minority views**: None expressed.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 23 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: MR. MOSKOW: "consumer spending was soft and getting softer...Sears reported that its sales were down 50 percent on Tuesday." MS. MINEHAN: "demand had just dried up. People were not buying and in fact were not showing up in stores." CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "The orders pattern in the capital goods area remained weak."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending was already softening pre-crisis and collapsed in the immediate aftermath; business investment was weak with no improvement; the net picture is sharply below trend.
- **Minority views**: Greenspan noted consumer expenditures were "holding up" and auto sales were "not bad" pre-crisis, but even he sees the net effect as clearly negative.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 73 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "corporate yields may have eased slightly but nowhere near the extent to which government yields have" (implying widening corporate spreads). VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: extensive discussion of infrastructure destruction, exchange closures, clearing bank problems ("The Bank of New York has been a considerable problem"). MR. JORDAN: "we've added about $100 billion worth of liquidity" (indicating massive stress requiring massive response).
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are severely stressed -- equity markets shut for days, critical infrastructure damaged, flight to quality widening spreads, clearing system impaired -- though massive Fed liquidity is partially offsetting.
- **Minority views**: Jordan suggests the liquidity injection has already had a "calming effect" on sophisticated financial players.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 16 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: MR. SANTOMERO: "I think we should move...a 50 basis point move would be appropriate." MR. PARRY: "a move on the funds rate would be called for...I would not favor a 25 basis point move." MR. BROADDUS: "I believe 50 basis points is the right move." MS. MINEHAN: "I would be marginally in favor of the smaller 25 basis point move rather than 50, but I could go along with 50 as well."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-universal support for an imminent rate cut, with the strong majority favoring 50bp; this is a strongly dovish meeting in the context of an already aggressive easing cycle.
- **Minority views**: Poole and Minehan lean toward 25bp; Guynn prefers a "more measured" move; but even these dissenters support easing.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: The disagreement is purely tactical: 25bp vs 50bp, and Friday/Monday timing. No participant opposes easing. MR. POOLE: "25 basis points would perhaps be enough." MR. GUYNN: "I'd make a more measured move now and leave ourselves some room." Most others clearly favor 50bp.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on the need to cut rates; modest tactical disagreement on magnitude and timing within a unified dovish framework.
- **Minority views**: The 25bp camp (Poole, Minehan, Guynn) vs the 50bp majority is the only axis of disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 87 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH's extended discussion of exchange infrastructure, power/telecom restoration, building collapse risks, clearing bank operations. CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: flight to quality, Dow proxy calculations, risk premium increases. MR. JORDAN: "$100 billion worth of liquidity" injected. Multiple participants reference financial system functioning.
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability and market functioning dominate the meeting; the operational integrity of the financial system is the central concern alongside the rate decision.
- **Minority views**: None -- all participants engage with financial stability as a primary concern.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 13 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: MR. STERN: "if it turns out with the benefit of hindsight that such an action proves to have been unnecessary...we can always reverse it." MS. MINEHAN: "having the October meeting available to move again, setting a steady pattern." MR. GUYNN: "leave ourselves some room to respond to developments...even more decisively."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is strongly dovish -- participants signal willingness to cut again at the October meeting and maintain readiness to act further; the only caveat is a willingness to reverse if conditions improve, which is pro forma.
- **Minority views**: Jordan and Poole imply the liquidity response may be sufficient, suggesting slightly less dovish forward lean, but they do not oppose further easing.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "How significant that deterioration is I think is exceptionally difficult to judge"; "I think it's much too soon to make judgments about how this will ultimately come out." MR. POOLE: "we may be faced with events that we can't predict, so it's going to be very important to be ready to move." MR. STERN: "I don't know how all this is going to play out."
- **Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express extreme uncertainty about the economic and geopolitical fallout from 9/11; the situation is genuinely unprecedented and multiple speakers acknowledge they cannot bound the range of outcomes.
- **Minority views**: None -- uncertainty is universally expressed.
