# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-07-20 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
Transcript -- individual speakers identified by name. This is an **extremely truncated** participant discussion segment. The transcript covers: (1) brief Q&A with staff presenters (Peter Fisher, Joan Lovett, Mike Prell, Don Kohn), (2) Chairman Greenspan's summary of his congressional hearing, (3) an extended discussion of Mexico swap arrangements and peso risk, (4) BIS membership expansion, and (5) Super 301 trade issues with Japan. There is essentially NO go-around on economic conditions and NO policy discussion regarding the funds rate.

### Era Context
Mid-1994: The Fed was in its 1994 tightening cycle, having begun raising rates in February 1994. The economy was growing above trend, and the Committee had raised rates several times. However, this transcript contains almost none of this substantive discussion -- what we have is the procedural/international portions only.

### Assessment of Scorability
This transcript is missing the core economic go-around and policy discussion. The only substantive economic signals are:
- **Greenspan**: "very little evidence of weakness in orders" (suggesting strong economy)
- **Greenspan**: mentioned telling Congress "we were open to the possibility of moving again" (hawkish forward signal)
- **Parry**: asked about benchmark revisions and implications for "slack" (suggesting slack was a live question)
- **McTeer/Parry**: humorous exchange about NAIRU at 6.5% (suggesting labor market tightness was a live topic)
- **Mexico discussion**: concerns about peso, systemic risk in Latin America -- financial stability topic

Given the extremely limited economic discussion content, confidence will be Low across nearly all dimensions. Many scores must be heavily caveated.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: No direct participant discussion of inflation. The 1994 context and the fact that the Committee was in a tightening cycle suggests above-neutral inflation concern, but the text itself provides almost no evidence. Greenspan's "open to moving again" implies concern about overheating/inflation, but this is indirect. Direction: likely somewhat above midpoint given tightening cycle context, but text evidence is near-zero. Score conservatively at 55 with very wide uncertainty and Low confidence.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Greenspan says "very little evidence of weakness in orders" -- suggesting economy is strong. Parry's question about benchmark revisions and "slack" implies the question of remaining slack was relevant. McTeer's joke about NAIRU implies labor market approaching or past full employment. Direction: modestly above potential. Score: 54, wide uncertainty, Low confidence.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The NAIRU joke (6.5% mentioned by Senator Sarbanes in context of Parry's view) implies tightness was a live question. No direct participant assessment of labor conditions in this text. Direction: likely near or modestly above NAIRU given the tightening cycle. Score: 53, very wide uncertainty, Low confidence.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Greenspan's "very little evidence of weakness in orders" and the inventory buildup question (Boehne asking about which industries had inventory buildup) suggest solid demand. But inventory buildup could signal either strong demand or unplanned accumulation. Direction: modestly above trend. Score: 55, wide uncertainty, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: The Mexico peso discussion and concerns about Brady bond markets and Latin American contagion suggest some emerging market financial stress. However, these are international, not domestic financial conditions. Greenspan references "the backup" (presumably referring to bond market selloff of 1994). Direction: somewhat tight given the 1994 bond market selloff context, but direct evidence is minimal. Score: 55, wide uncertainty, Low confidence.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Greenspan explicitly says he told Congress "we were open to the possibility of moving again" -- this is a hawkish forward signal from the Chairman. The Committee was in the midst of a tightening cycle. Direction: moderately hawkish. Score: 65, wide uncertainty, Low confidence (only Chairman's view is evident).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: On the Mexico swap issue, Broaddus expressed "strong reservation" about swap arrangements but did not formally object. No evidence of dissent on monetary policy in this transcript segment. Score: 25, moderate uncertainty, Low confidence.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The Mexico/peso discussion is substantial -- concerns about systemic contagion in Latin America, Brady bonds, exchange rate crisis. McDonough, Broaddus, Lindsey, Boehne all engage. This is a meaningful financial stability discussion, though focused on international rather than domestic. Score: 40, moderate uncertainty, Moderate confidence.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan's statement about telling Congress the Fed was "open to the possibility of moving again" is a clear hawkish forward signal. Score: 68, wide uncertainty, Low confidence (only one speaker's forward signal).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Greenspan mentions "we're not clear at this stage what part of those inventories are imported goods." The Mexico discussion has uncertainty about post-election dynamics. But no broad expressions of economic uncertainty from participants. Score: 35, wide uncertainty, Low confidence.
