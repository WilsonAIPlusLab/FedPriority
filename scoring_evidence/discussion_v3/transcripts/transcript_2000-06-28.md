# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-06-28 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a transcript with individual speaker attributions. It covers both the go-around (economic discussion on June 27) and the policy discussion (June 28 morning session). Speakers include: Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Guynn, Jordan, Broaddus, Stern, Poole, Hoenig, McTeer, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson, Vice Chairman McDonough, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-populated meeting with extensive discussion.

### Era Context
Mid-2000: The economy had been growing rapidly (above-trend), the Fed had been tightening through 1999-2000 (175bp of hikes over the cycle, including a 50bp move in May 2000). The dot-com bubble was deflating in parts. Unemployment was at historic lows (~4%). Oil prices were surging. This is late-boom, with early signs of deceleration emerging.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target with upside risks. Parry: "core PCE price index to increase 2.1 and 2.2 percent this year and next, well above last year's 1.5 percent rate" and "balance of risks for inflation is pretty clearly on the up side." Broaddus: "emerging trend of higher core inflation and deteriorating prospects for inflation." Meyer: "inflation is rising... whether one looks at overall inflation or at the core inflation measures." However, several participants see it as moderate or not alarming. Jordan: "not as worried... that inflation is definitely on a rising trend." McTeer: "the inflation trend thus far in 2000 does not seem alarming." Hoenig: "I don't see an acceleration in inflation." Gramlich: unemployment below NAIRU for 3.5 years yet "signs of acceleration in either prices or wages are still rather scant." The center of gravity is that core inflation has moved up modestly (from ~1.5% to ~2%), with upside risks acknowledged by most. This is "somewhat elevated" but not alarming territory. Direction: modestly above balanced, perhaps 55-58.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says moderate upward drift with upside risks. Score ~57 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy clearly operating above potential. Meyer: "I am at least as convinced as the staff that output is beyond potential." Broaddus references baseline scenario with NAIRU above 5% and economy operating above potential. Greenspan: "the evidence that the expansion is slowing to a certain extent is pretty much unambiguous" but also discusses the possibility that it has been "running at a seemingly unsustainable rate." The growth rate is slowing from unsustainably high levels but remains at or above potential. Parry projects 4.25% growth this year. Minehan: "growth rate for the first half of the year would be above nearly all estimates of potential GDP." Multiple participants note tentative signs of slowing but from very high levels. The economy is still above potential but the gap may be narrowing. Direction: above potential. Score should be >50. Given the "still above potential but signs of slowing from very elevated levels," approximately 58-62.

Bright-line check: "above potential" language clearly present. Score >50 confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Overwhelming evidence of very tight labor markets. Parry: "employment growth rate of 3.6 percent"; Minehan: "remains difficult to find workers of almost any type" with reports of 10-20% wage increases, "All the new college graduates were hired even before they graduated." Moskow: "our labor markets are still very tight." Broaddus: "Labor markets are still very tight in our District." Poole: UPS/FedEx reporting severe difficulty finding entry-level workers, with massive signing bonuses. Jordan notes some relief in construction but overall still tight. Hoenig: "District labor markets still are tight." Even those noting slight easing (Guynn: "some emerging relief" in construction trades) describe markets as very tight overall. Greenspan: discussion of labor markets as "very tight." Direction: very tight. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending is showing signs of moderation from very high levels. Greenspan: "consumption outlays... fairly conclusive" slowdown; chain store figures flat, motor vehicles declining. Multiple participants report housing slowing, auto sales down from record highs. Guynn: "some noticeable falloff in consumer spending for big ticket, high-end products." BUT business investment remains strong. Greenspan: "orders coming into the capital goods companies... have remained very strong, and backlogs continue to build." Poole: FedEx/UPS expecting very strong volumes. The net is above-trend but decelerating. Consumer side weakening, investment side still very strong. Center of gravity: still above trend but moderating. Score ~55-58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Several participants note tightening financial conditions are biting. Parry: "deceleration is due mainly to tightening financial conditions." Guynn: "more reports of tightening credit standards and more cautious and less accommodative lending." Greenspan: "rise in mortgage interest rates is beginning to exert an effect." Kelley: "a substantial additional impact still in the pipeline from tightening already in place." But Ferguson notes: "financial market conditions between this and our previous meeting have become, if anything, a little more accommodative. Net corporate borrowings, driven by bond issuance, seem to have picked up." The flat yield curve is noted. Several mention the wealth effect moderation (equity markets sideways to down for tech). On balance, conditions have tightened somewhat from prior periods but are not severely tight. The 50bp hike in May "got people's attention" (Guynn). Direction: somewhat tight, in the 55-62 range.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous agreement to hold rates steady at this meeting. However, near-universal agreement that the balance of risks remains toward higher inflation and that further tightening may be needed. Greenspan: "the probability that we are through tightening in this cyclical expansion has to be less than 50-50." Multiple participants explicitly say they support holding but expect more tightening may come. Meyer: "our work is not yet done." Broaddus makes the strongest hawkish case, saying "a solid case could be made for a further move today." Kelley wants strong language in the statement. On the dovish side, Gramlich raises the possibility the Fed "has already done enough," and McTeer, Hoenig, Jordan are more cautious about further tightening. The decision is hold + asymmetric tightening bias. This is a hawkish hold. Direction: lean hawkish. Score ~58-62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No dissents in the vote. Broad agreement to hold with tightening bias. However, there is a notable range of views: Broaddus would have preferred to tighten today; Gramlich/Jordan/Hoenig think the Fed may have done enough. Meyer was "struggling" with the decision. The range of views on the outlook (NAIRU debates, whether slowdown is real) is substantial, but the policy recommendation converged. Moderate differences in assessment, low differences in action. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited direct discussion of financial stability per se. Jordan discusses the healthy correction in attitudes (people learning stock prices can go down, IPOs can fail). Stern mentions business people expressing concern about financial market developments. Ferguson notes some market indicators. Guynn mentions tightening credit standards. But financial stability is not a major topic; it's discussed mostly in passing as context for the economic outlook. Score ~25-30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Clear tightening bias maintained. Greenspan: "utterly essential that we retain a statement... that the risks remain unbalanced toward conditions that may generate heightened inflation." Poole: "futures market has built in another 1/4 point at our August meeting. I believe that is a reasonable bet." Kelley wants "quite strongly worded" statement. Multiple participants note further tightening is likely needed. But it is conditional on data. Minehan: "I don't see any difference between moving now or moving at our August meeting." Direction: mild tightening bias with data dependence. Score ~63-67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Substantial uncertainty expressed. Greenspan frames two scenarios for productivity and says "I know of no evidence that conclusively would suggest which of those two scenarios is true." Kelley: "Maybe, maybe, maybe" extended passage about unknowns. Minehan: "range of uncertainty about a lot of things." Meyer discusses uncertainty about whether slowdown is real. Multiple participants express uncertainty about whether the deceleration is temporary or persistent, about NAIRU, about productivity trends. Gramlich explicitly notes uncertainty about NAIRU and recession risk. Direction: elevated. Score ~50-55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor tightness is higher because while output growth is moderating, the labor market remains very tight (level vs. rate distinction). This is coherent -- labor market conditions lag output changes.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both lean hawkish. Current action is a hold (not a hike), but forward signal is tightening-biased. The forward guidance is slightly more hawkish than the current action, which is coherent for a "pause with tightening bias" meeting.

### Speaker Count by Direction
On the slowdown: virtually all participants see some signs of slowing, but most view them as "tentative" or "preliminary."
On inflation: most see upside risks (Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Meyer, Greenspan), some are less worried (Jordan, McTeer, Hoenig, Gramlich).
On policy: all support holding, with all favoring keeping the tightening bias. Broaddus is the most hawkish (would have moved today). Gramlich/Jordan/McTeer/Hoenig most dovish.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "core PCE price index to increase 2.1 and 2.2 percent this year and next, well above last year's 1.5 percent rate... the balance of risks for inflation is pretty clearly on the up side."
- MR. MEYER: "the data on prices are clear and that inflation is rising. It is rising whether one looks at overall inflation or at the core inflation measures."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the signs of acceleration in inflation do not look so strong either... 'scant' is the operative word."

Center of gravity: Core inflation has moved up modestly from the very low levels of 1998-99 (around 1.5% PCE) to about 2%, with most participants seeing upside risks driven by energy pass-through and tight labor markets. This is somewhat above the balanced mark but not a breakout.

Minority views: Jordan and McTeer see less inflation pressure; Broaddus and Meyer see more definitive upward trend. This widens the band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MEYER: "I am at least as convinced as the staff that output is beyond potential."
- MS. MINEHAN: "growth rate for the first half of the year would be above nearly all estimates of potential GDP."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the evidence that the expansion is slowing to a certain extent is pretty much unambiguous" but discusses continued strong investment and productivity.

Center of gravity: Economy is operating above potential, though the gap is narrowing as growth decelerates from unsustainably rapid rates. Still clearly above trend.

Minority views: Hoenig projects growth close to trend with no further tightening; others see continued above-potential operation.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. MINEHAN: "remains difficult to find workers of almost any type... 10 to 20 percent increases for various classes of workers... 'All the new college graduates were hired even before they graduated.'"
- MR. POOLE: UPS entry-level workers receiving massive signing bonuses and attendance bonuses on top of base wage, with severe difficulty hitting hiring targets.
- MR. MOSKOW: "our labor markets are still very tight."

Center of gravity: Very tight labor markets across virtually all districts. Severe worker shortages persist. Some marginal easing in construction trades but overall extremely tight. Consistent with era anchor of 65-75 for 1999-2000.

Minority views: Jordan and Guynn note some emerging relief in specific sectors (construction), but acknowledge overall tightness persists.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "chain store figures have been flat since the second or third week of May... motor vehicles clearly is easing"; but also "orders coming into the capital goods companies... have remained very strong, and backlogs continue to build."
- MR. GUYNN: "some noticeable falloff in consumer spending for big ticket, high-end products" alongside "business investment spending, at least for equipment including computers and telecommunication equipment, is likely to remain strong."
- MR. POOLE: FedEx and UPS "expecting volume to continue very strong and, if anything, stronger than it has been."

Center of gravity: Consumer spending is moderating from very strong levels (housing, autos declining), but business investment remains robust. Net position is still above trend but decelerating. The investment/shipping evidence keeps the score above neutral.

Minority views: Some (Jordan, Hoenig) see more significant slowing; others (Poole, via shipping contacts) see continued strength.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. GUYNN: "more reports of tightening credit standards and more cautious and less accommodative lending... the 50 basis points of tightening that we did at our May meeting got people's attention."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the increase in fixed rate mortgages has been a crucial element in housing" and notes the flat yield curve eliminating the ARM option.
- MR. FERGUSON: "financial market conditions between this and our previous meeting have become, if anything, a little more accommodative. Net corporate borrowings... seem to have picked up."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions have tightened somewhat from the prior accommodative stance, particularly in mortgage rates and bank lending standards. However, corporate bond markets remain open and some easing in market conditions noted. Moderately tight.

Minority views: Ferguson sees conditions as somewhat more accommodative recently; Guynn and Greenspan emphasize tightening in credit and housing finance.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the probability that we are through tightening in this cyclical expansion has to be less than 50-50... we should be very careful about suggesting in any way that we might be at the edge."
- MR. MEYER: "our work is not yet done... I believe that the data are likely to show the wisdom of moving further perhaps as early as by the next meeting."
- MR. BROADDUS: "a solid case could be made for a further move today."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "It is at least possible that the Fed has already done enough tightening."

Center of gravity: Unanimous hold, but with strong tightening bias. Most participants believe further tightening is probable. This is a hawkish pause -- not hiking today but signaling more likely. Moderate hawkish lean.

Minority views: Broaddus wanted to move today (more hawkish); Gramlich, Jordan, McTeer, Hoenig more cautious about further tightening (less hawkish). Wide band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Unanimous vote to hold with asymmetric tightening bias.
- MR. BROADDUS: Strongly argued for tightening today but accepted the Chairman's proposal.
- MR. MEYER: "I did have to struggle more than most with this decision" -- wanted to tighten but accepted hold.
- Substantial philosophical disagreement about NAIRU (Gramlich, Poole, Stern vs. Meyer) but convergence on policy action.

Center of gravity: Policy action was unanimous, but notable differences in economic assessment (NAIRU debate, inflation outlook, whether slowdown is real). Several members were "struggling" or "reluctant." Moderate differences, no formal dissents.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. JORDAN: Discusses correction in risk attitudes -- "People have learned that stock prices can go down. They have learned that IPOs can fail."
- MR. STERN: "a number of business people I've talked to recently have expressed some concern about financial market developments."
- MR. GUYNN: Mentions tightening credit standards and financial institutions saying "their best days in terms of credit quality may be behind them."

Center of gravity: Financial conditions and markets mentioned in passing by several participants as context, but not as a focal concern. No extended discussion of systemic risk, bubbles, or financial stability per se. Limited emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "utterly essential that we retain a statement in our press release expressing our view that the risks remain unbalanced toward conditions that may generate heightened inflation."
- MR. KELLEY: "I hope the statement we release to the public will be quite strongly worded... We may indeed need to tighten more--perhaps quite soon and perhaps by a considerable amount."
- MR. POOLE: "The futures market has built in another 1/4 point at our August meeting. I believe that is a reasonable bet."

Center of gravity: Clear tightening-biased forward signal. The asymmetric risk statement is maintained, with several participants wanting even stronger language. Markets pricing in another 25bp at August. But conditioned on data. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening forward signal.

Minority views: Gramlich and others are more cautious about signaling further action, preferring "watchful waiting."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I know of no evidence that conclusively would suggest which of those two scenarios is true" (regarding productivity/inflation paths). "Owing to the uncertainty surrounding the potential outcome we have in front of us."
- MR. KELLEY: Extended "maybe, maybe, maybe" passage listing multiple unknowns about productivity, NAIRU, consumer behavior, and noting "all these maybes plus $3 will buy you a grande size latte at Starbucks!"
- MS. MINEHAN: "there is a range of uncertainty about a lot of things... trend productivity, as well as the NAIRU and the implications of the trade deficit for the dollar."

Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty about whether the slowdown is real and persistent, about NAIRU, about productivity trends. Multiple participants frame the situation explicitly in terms of competing scenarios and unknowns. This is notably above normal hedging.

- MR. MOSKOW: Directors reported "definite acceleration in productivity in their industries over the past three years."
- MS. MINEHAN and MR. PARRY reference accelerating structural productivity.
Productivity is perceived as well above historical trend -- the "new economy" productivity acceleration is a major theme.
