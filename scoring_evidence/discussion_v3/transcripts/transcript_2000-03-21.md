# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 2000-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript. Individual participant statements from a go-around and policy discussion. Speakers identified by name.

**Era context**: March 2000 — the late-1990s/early-2000s boom. The economy is experiencing strong growth driven by the tech/productivity revolution. The Fed has been gradually raising rates (already up 100bp over the prior year). Unemployment is near historic lows. Inflation remains contained but participants debate whether it will soon accelerate. NASDAQ is surging. Oil prices are rising.

**Speaker count and direction mapping**:

Speakers in go-around: Guynn, Minehan, McTeer, Moskow, Stern, Hoenig, Gramlich, Poole, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, McDonough, Greenspan (13 speakers).
Policy round: McTeer, McDonough, Poole, Jordan, Guynn, Hoenig, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Gramlich, Stern, Broaddus, Kelley, Meyer, Ferguson (15 speakers). Unanimous vote for 25bp hike.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as slightly below to at the concerning threshold. The center of gravity is: core inflation is currently well-contained/low, but there are emerging risks. Guynn sees "gradual upward creep." Minehan notes rising medical/fuel/shelter costs. Moskow forecasts "acceleration in core PCE inflation of 0.4 percentage point." Meyer says "core inflation remains well contained" but expects it "will soon begin to rise." Greenspan argues forcefully that unit costs are near zero and he doesn't see acceleration "yet." McTeer notes "core CPI remains on its long-term downward trajectory." Kelley sees "only the scantiest evidence of rising price pressures." The center of gravity: inflation is currently low/contained, but most expect it to rise. This is a forward-looking concern more than a current reading. Current inflation is near or modestly below target, but expected to move up. Direction: slightly above neutral — participants perceive emerging/incipient pressures but not actual elevated inflation yet. Score around 52-56. Polarity check: higher = more pressure perceived. Evidence says modest current + expected rise. Score ~54.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy operating above potential. Demand is outstripping supply growth. Meyer: "growth in aggregate demand continues to outpace the growth of aggregate supply." Moskow: "growth in aggregate demand will continue to outstrip growth in aggregate supply." Kelley: "extremely strong demand driving the economy toward overheating." McDonough: "demand side of the economy is simply too strong." Greenspan agrees growth is very high but questions whether it constitutes "overheating" given productivity. Multiple participants note above-trend GDP growth. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires >50. Nearly all participants describe demand exceeding supply. Score should be in the 59-65 range. The late 90s boom anchor is 57-63. This fits well. Score ~62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Nearly every participant describes extremely tight labor markets. Guynn: "Labor markets are as tight or even tighter--if that's possible--than ever." Minehan: "we are running out of workers." Hoenig: "Labor markets remain quite tight." Poole: labor supply constraints causing slowing employment growth. Moskow: "tight labor market was the major topic of discussion." McTeer: unemployment at lowest in 20 years. Stern: "Labor markets, of course, remain very tight." Ferguson: unemployment rate near lows. Direction: very tight. The 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75. Score ~72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Spending and investment are described as extremely strong by nearly all participants. Moskow: "current growth in household spending is breathtaking." Auto sales at 19 million unit pace. Ferguson: "Consumer spending is still outpacing GDP growth overall... capital spending also continues unabated." Minehan: "obvious in auto sales, in the continued strength of housing." Guynn: "retail... sales have continued to exceed expectations." Greenspan suspects first-quarter GDP will be much higher than forecasts. Direction: well above trend. Score ~68-72. Anchor: 2021 surge was 62-68. This late-90s boom spending is comparable or stronger in real terms relative to trend. Score ~70.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Conditions are mixed. Higher interest rates have had limited effect so far. Stern: directors report higher rates have had no effect on spending. Hoenig: "higher interest rates... have not really produced any slowdown." Guynn: "Loan demand remains quite high." Moskow: "Loans are readily available to businesses with reasonable prospects." Guynn notes unsolicited lending offers. Ferguson: "financial markets that are indeed somewhat accommodative to strong growth." The stock market is booming. However, rates have risen 100bp+. Net: conditions remain fairly accommodative/loose despite rate hikes. Direction: below neutral (loose). Score ~30-35. Polarity: higher = tighter. Evidence says still loose/accommodative. Score ~32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous agreement on 25bp hike. Balance of risks toward inflation. Several participants (Minehan, Gramlich, Meyer, Broaddus) express private interest in 50bp. McTeer is most dovish, questioning need for long series of hikes. Greenspan supports gradual 25bp path but signals continuing tightening ahead. Nearly all agree more tightening is likely needed. This is a clear moderate-to-strong hawkish consensus: hiking 25bp with strong tilt toward more. Anchor: 70-75 for 2017-18 hiking. This meeting has unanimous 25bp + strong expectation of more + several wanting 50bp. Score ~70-72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote for 25bp. No formal dissents. However, notable spread of views: McTeer/Hoenig lean cautious on further tightening; Minehan/Gramlich/Meyer/Broaddus lean toward 50bp; Greenspan occupies center. The range is between "25bp is enough for now" to "could justify 50bp." This is disagreement about pace, not direction. 0 dissents but several "confess" to wanting 50bp. Score ~30-35 (broad agreement with moderate differences about pace).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market discussion is prominent. Minehan reports CEOs obsessed with stock prices. Poole discusses unusual NASDAQ/S&P negative correlation at length. McDonough discusses wealth effect and stock market overpricing. Greenspan discusses NASDAQ vs Dow dynamics extensively. Guynn mentions unsolicited lending. Several participants mention wealth effects. This is more than passing reference but not dominant. Score ~35-42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward more tightening. Balance of risks toward inflation retained. Greenspan: "continue on a tightening path until there are problems with that path." Poole: "25 basis point increases until the data convince us that a further increase is no longer necessary." Meyer: if inflation rises, "a very good reason to move in a larger increment." Broaddus: "more forceful actions are going to be required sooner or later." Hoenig pushes back slightly wanting data-dependence. Net: clear hawkish forward lean. Score ~72-76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Greenspan: "something very unusual is going on in the economy." Gramlich: "What we may have to do in the long run does not seem clear at all." Ferguson: "a lot about this economy that is very difficult to understand." Hoenig mentions "level of uncertainty." Greenspan questions standard models. Productivity dynamics create genuine uncertainty. Several participants note difficulty predicting where rates will end up. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty — not crisis-level, but genuine puzzle about the new economy. Score ~42-48.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap because participants emphasize labor shortage more than general overheating. Greenspan tempers overheating language while everyone agrees labor is extremely tight. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (74): Both strongly hawkish. Aligned — current action is hawkish and forward signal is also hawkish. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 54 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "when I look at the various inflation measures, including the broader chain-weighted measures... I see the prospects of a gradual upward creep"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "unit costs for nonfinancial corporations were zero year-over-year... I find it hard to reconcile these cost numbers with a belief that there is any acceleration of inflation--yet"
- MR. MEYER: "core inflation remains well contained, I agree with the Greenbook forecast that it will soon begin to rise"
- MR. KELLEY: "we see to date only the scantiest evidence of rising price pressures"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants agree core inflation is currently contained but most expect it to begin rising due to tight labor markets, oil prices, and demand-supply imbalance. The perception is of incipient/emerging rather than realized pressure.

**Minority views**: McTeer sees little inflation risk ("core CPI remains on its long-term downward trajectory"). Guynn and Moskow see more definite inflation acceleration ahead. Greenspan anchors the center toward currently contained.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MEYER: "growth in aggregate demand continues to outpace the growth of aggregate supply at a time when labor markets are already very tight"
- MR. MOSKOW: "without significant action by this Committee, growth in aggregate demand will continue to outstrip growth in aggregate supply"
- MR. KELLEY: "extremely strong demand driving the economy toward overheating"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: acknowledges very high growth rates but questions whether it constitutes overheating given productivity acceleration

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-universal agreement that demand growth exceeds supply growth and the economy is operating above potential. Greenspan tempers this with productivity arguments but does not deny excess demand.

**Minority views**: McTeer is most optimistic about supply-side capacity (productivity, immigration, fiscal drag). Greenspan questions the overheating narrative but agrees on excess demand direction.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GUYNN: "Labor markets are as tight or even tighter--if that's possible--than ever. Wages are reportedly up 3 to 5 percent"
- MS. MINEHAN: "Nothing about current labor market data suggests that the assumption that we are running out of workers is any less true now"
- MR. MOSKOW: "District's tight labor market was the major topic of discussion by all three groups... average time to fill orders nationally had increased from seven days to ten days"
- MR. POOLE: "UPS also reports continuing pressure on the available supply of entry-level labor"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimously described as very tight. Every single district reports severe tightness. Unemployment at multi-decade lows. Worker shortages widespread across skill levels and regions.

**Minority views**: None dissent on tightness. Minor differences on whether wage pressures are accelerating (some report 3-4%, others 6-7%).

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "current growth in household spending is breathtaking and seems unlikely to abate any time soon... Light vehicle sales were unbelievable in February, reaching a 19 million unit pace"
- MR. FERGUSON: "Consumer spending is still outpacing GDP growth overall... capital spending also continues unabated. While we are used to seeing this in the tech sector, capital spending outside of the tech sector is also strong"
- MS. MINEHAN: "obvious in auto sales, in the continued strength of housing, in oil prices, in labor markets, in rising consumer and business debt"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment are both well above trend. Multiple participants use superlatives ("breathtaking," "unbelievable," "insatiable"). Capital investment booming especially in tech but also broadly.

**Minority views**: Some note early hints of moderation in certain sectors (Guynn: real estate modestly slowing; some interest-rate sensitivity beginning). But these are marginal.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: directors report higher interest rates "had no effect on spending or on future investment plans"
- MR. GUYNN: "Loan demand remains quite high... a large commercial real estate developer... said he and his competitors have recently been getting more unsolicited knocks on the door from people wanting to push money at them"
- MR. FERGUSON: "financial markets that are indeed somewhat accommodative to strong growth"
- MR. HOENIG: "higher interest rates... have not really produced any slowdown"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite 100bp+ of rate hikes, financial conditions remain accommodative. Credit freely available, loan demand strong, stock market surging, wealth effects powerful. Rate hikes have not yet meaningfully tightened conditions.

**Minority views**: Poole notes money growth hasn't slowed as expected, implying conditions are very loose. Broaddus notes long-term rates aren't supporting short-rate hikes. Minor disagreement on degree of looseness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 71 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we should be moving another 25 basis points today, retain the sentence... balance of risks remains tilted toward inflation, and continue on a tightening path"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I probably could have gone with 50 basis points, too"
- MR. MEYER: "a time may come when we are going to have to surprise the bond markets... Well, that was the 40 percent case" (for 50bp)
- MR. MCTEER: "I just hope that we will take it one step at a time" (most dovish)

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for 25bp hike. Strong consensus for continued tightening ahead. Several participants (Minehan, Gramlich, Meyer, Broaddus) actively considered 50bp. Only McTeer and Hoenig urge more caution on forward path.

**Minority views**: McTeer questions need for long series of hikes. Hoenig wants data-dependence, not precommitment. But even these two support the 25bp hike.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote for 25bp with balance of risks toward inflation
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I'll admit to Bill and others that I have had private mental dalliances with 50 basis points"
- MR. HOENIG: "our strategy is not necessarily that we are on a course to continually increase the funds rate"
- MR. BROADDUS: "I want asymmetry with a vengeance!"

**Center-of-gravity**: Zero dissents. All agree on direction (tighten). Disagreement is only about pace — some want faster (50bp), some want caution about committing to a long series. This is tactical disagreement within a unified directional view.

**Minority views**: The 50bp camp (Minehan, Gramlich, Meyer, Broaddus) vs. the cautious camp (McTeer, Hoenig) creates moderate spread but never threatens the consensus.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. MINEHAN: director reports "CEOs of major firms across the nation are now more concerned about the stock market than they ever have been... today's period of stock market ebullience has similarities to the period of the late '70s and early '80s"
- MR. POOLE: extensive discussion of NASDAQ/S&P negative correlation and "phenomenal commitment to investing in equities"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: wealth effect discussion, "most ridiculously overpriced stocks are those of new technology companies"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: lengthy analysis of NASDAQ vs S&P dynamics, tech capital flows

**Center-of-gravity**: Stock market conditions receive meaningful discussion from multiple participants — not as a stability threat per se, but as a key factor driving demand and creating unusual dynamics. Guynn's unsolicited lending anecdote raises a mild credit quality flag. This is a moderate level of financial conditions attention.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 74 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "continue on a tightening path until there are problems with that path"
- MR. POOLE: "we will have 25 basis point increases until the data convince us that a further increase is no longer necessary"
- MR. BROADDUS: "more forceful actions are going to be required sooner or later... asymmetry with a vengeance!"
- MR. MEYER: "further increases will be warranted over the course of the next several months"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal of continued tightening. Balance of risks toward inflation retained. Multiple participants signal that 50bp could come if data warrant. The forward lean is firmly hawkish.

**Minority views**: Hoenig wants to preserve optionality ("not a sure thing"). Gramlich notes long-run path is unclear. But even cautious voices accept near-term tightening bias.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "something very unusual is going on in the economy" and "I don't see how any of us can forecast that at this particular point"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "What we have to do in the short run seems pretty clear to me. What we may have to do in the long run does not seem clear at all"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "there continues to be a lot about this economy that is very difficult to understand"
- MR. HOENIG: "because of the uncertainty surrounding the projections, it would be of some concern to me if we began an aggressive tightening program"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express genuine uncertainty about the productivity revolution, how far rates need to go, and whether traditional models apply. The short-run action is clear (tighten), but the longer-run path and the underlying economic dynamics are genuinely puzzling. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty.
