## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type Identification

This is a **transcript** — a two-day FOMC meeting (January 28–29, 2020). Individual participant statements are clearly attributed by name. The document is well-segmented into: (a) a framework go-round on January 28 focused on inflation ranges and financial stability considerations; (b) a financial stability go-round; (c) an economic/outlook go-round; and (d) a policy go-round on January 29. All sections contain participant-attributed discussion. Staff material (Tealbook briefings, balance-sheet presentations by Lorie Logan/Patricia) precedes participant responses and will be excluded from scoring.

### 2. Era Context

January 28–29, 2020. The 11th year of economic expansion. Unemployment at 3.5% (50-year low). Core PCE inflation running approximately 1.6% on 12-month basis — persistently below the 2% target. The Committee had cut rates 75 basis points in mid-2019 (three cuts: July, September, October). Policy rate at 1.5–1.75%. The coronavirus (COVID-19/Wuhan) was only just emerging as a risk factor — mentioned by several speakers but not yet treated as a material shock. The economy was described as "in a good place" by most participants.

**Macro backdrop without letting it override text:** Early 2020 pre-COVID expansion. Strong labor market, sub-target inflation, accommodative stance, mixed business investment/consumer picture.

### 3. Directional Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below target** — below 50. The consistent refrain: core PCE at 1.6%, running below 2% for most of 8 years. Multiple speakers (Kashkari, Brainard, Williams, Daly, Clarida, Powell) describe inflation as below target. Clarida: "PCE inflation is running somewhat below our objective." Kashkari: "core PCE inflation still running at 1.6 percent on a 12-month basis." Brainard: "inflation remains below target." Some participants (Bostic, Quarles) argue alternative measures (trimmed mean, core CPI) are closer to 2%, but the center of gravity is: inflation is below target but not alarmingly so. No deflation risk discussed. Score: **35–38** range — below target, some slack in inflation but not a deep deflationary environment; consistent with "26-40: below target; slack keeping prices in check."

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target → score below 50. Correct direction.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see economy as **at or slightly above** potential. Powell: "solid growth… in the neighborhood of its estimated trend pace." Clarida: "real GDP growth at 2.3 percent is above most estimates of trend." Rosengren: "real GDP is still growing above its potential rate." Barkin: "economy continuing to perform above trend." However, Kashkari explicitly disputes this: "I don't see any evidence that the economy is operating above potential." Bostic: economy "continuing to grow slightly above its long-run trend rate." Center of gravity: most participants see growth near or slightly above trend, unemployment well below NAIRU. Score: **52–55** — slightly above potential, consistent with "51-58: modestly above potential; above trend."

Bright-line rule check: Most say "above trend" or "above potential" → must be > 50. Score 53 is correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see labor market as **tight**. Unemployment at 50-year low (3.5%), U-6 at series low. Multiple speakers say "tight labor market," "difficult to find workers," "50-year low unemployment." Daly argues structural factors may mean it's not as tight as it appears (declining u*). Kashkari also argues max employment not yet reached. But the dominant view is a very tight labor market — "tight" language appears from nearly every participant discussing the economy. Score: **65–68** — tight, "low unemployment; hard to find workers; moderate wage pressure." Consistent with anchors 65-75 for tight labor markets. The fact that wage growth is moderate (~3%) despite low unemployment is noted, but the level of tightness is still high.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending described as solid/moderate; business investment described as weak/sluggish. Housing rebounding. Powell: "strong household sector balances out weak manufacturing, business fixed investment, and exports." Mester: "The mix continues to be of strong consumer spending and weak investment." Bowman: "consumer spending growth has stepped down" in Q4 but solid fundamentals. Net: consumer side at or slightly above trend; business investment clearly below trend. Overall net position: **near trend but slightly below** given the drag from business investment. Score: **47–50** — "at trend; moderate pace; sustained." Consumer strong, investment weak, roughly offsetting.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending and investment. Net position is roughly at trend or just slightly below due to weak business investment. Score 48 is appropriate.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants describe financial conditions as **loose/accommodative**. Brainard: "financial conditions that are notably accommodative… every financial conditions index I consult is in the very low end of its historical range." Rosengren: "very accommodative financial conditions." Daly: "favorable financial conditions." Multiple participants note tight credit spreads, equity prices at all-time highs, easy financial conditions overall. Score: **22–28** — "loose; accommodative conditions; credit freely available." Consistent with anchors 45-55 being normal; current is clearly below that range.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter financial conditions. Evidence says conditions are very loose/accommodative → score well below 50. Score 25 is appropriate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
All participants support holding rates unchanged (Alternative B). Unanimously support no change. The lean is mild dovish-to-neutral: policy described as "accommodative," rates on hold, willingness to tolerate an overshoot. No one advocates for rate hikes. Kashkari preferred Alternative A (more dovish language) but accepted B. Clarida, Evans, Bostic favor tolerance of overshoot. Rosengren and George signal more balanced risk management (concerned about financial stability, but still support holding). Bullard notes possibility of needing to ease further. The current stance is described as "accommodative" by most. Score: **42–46** — "lean dovish to balanced: hold steady with mild easing bias; prepared to act if needed." The policy is explicitly on hold but with more explicit language about inflation returning to target (mild dovish lean).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
The vote was unanimous for Alternative B. Kashkari explicitly said he preferred A but would support B — a "reluctant" supporter. There are clear emphasis differences on financial stability (Rosengren, Brainard, Kaplan vs. Evans, Bowman, Bullard), on inflation targeting (Kashkari pushing for stronger forward guidance vs. majority content with current language), and on inflation range (wide disagreement in framework go-round but this is a framework discussion, not a policy disagreement). Policy vote: unanimous, with one near-dissenter (Kashkari) and some divergence in views. Score: **25–30** — "broad agreement: minor emphasis differences; 0-1 dissents." The zero formal dissents and one "reluctant" supporter puts this in the 25-30 range.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability received **substantial attention** across multiple rounds. The entire first half of Day 1 was a framework go-round focused on financial stability. A separate financial stability go-round occurred. In the outlook go-round, many participants returned to financial stability themes. Topics covered: asset valuations at all-time highs, corporate debt elevated, leveraged lending, bank capital declining, CCyB debate, financial conditions very loose. This is a prominent topic dominating the meeting. Score: **68–73** — "prominent topic; most participants discuss at length." Nearly every participant discussed financial stability in at least one round; the framework discussion made it the central theme of Day 1.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The Committee changed language from inflation "near" to "returning to" 2% target. Evans supported, Daly wanted "sustained" added. Kashkari noted preference for stronger commitment (no rate hike until inflation reaches 2%). Clarida: policy on hold "until and unless PCE inflation not only reaches 2 percent, but for some time also modestly exceeds our 2 percent objective." Most favor holding for an extended period, with the bar for rate hikes quite high. Market expectations also assign a high bar for hikes. This is a mild easing bias in forward guidance, but not a strong commitment. Score: **30–38** — "easing lean with data dependence." The signal is: we're on hold, we expect inflation to return to target, we'll tolerate an overshoot. This is a mild dovish forward lean, consistent with 26-40 range.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
The coronavirus is explicitly mentioned as a new risk by multiple participants (Rosengren, Bullard, Kaplan, Daly, Brainard, Williams). Downside risks to global growth remain. Trade uncertainty still present. Rosengren: "growing economic and human cost of the coronavirus… a greater risk than is currently priced in by markets." Bullard: "this'll be scary, but temporary." However, most participants present a fairly confident baseline — "economy is in a good place," risks "somewhat more in balance" (Powell), "recession probabilities have declined" (Brainard). The coronavirus adds some genuine new uncertainty, but overall the tone is not "extremely uncertain." Score: **42–48** — "elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty; particularly uncertain." The coronavirus is a new wildcard mentioned by many, plus ongoing global growth and trade risks, pushing uncertainty above normal but not dramatically elevated.
