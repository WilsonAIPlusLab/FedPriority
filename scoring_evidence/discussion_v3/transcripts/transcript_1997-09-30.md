# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-09-30 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers a go-around on economic conditions plus a policy discussion. Speakers include: Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Greenspan, McTeer, Minehan, Jordan, Hoenig, Boehne, Stern, Guynn, McDonough, Rivlin, Meyer, Kelley, and policy round contributions from most of the same participants.

### Era Context
September 1997: Mid-to-late 1990s expansion. The economy is experiencing strong growth with surprisingly low inflation. The "new paradigm" debate is active -- whether technology-driven productivity gains have changed the inflation-growth tradeoff. Unemployment is around 4.8% and falling. The stock market is booming. The Fed last raised rates in March 1997 (25bp). The Asian financial crisis is beginning but has not yet fully materialized in US discussions.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants universally note that actual inflation is low and falling. Broaddus: "we still are not hearing reports of significant price increases in final goods and services." Parry: "inflation has fallen further." Moskow: "the inflation data continue to roll in lower than expected." Boehne: "little or no upward pressure on prices." Guynn: "we still are not hearing any reports of significant wage increases." Greenspan: "we still have negligible inflation in the system." However, nearly everyone sees upside RISK to inflation from tight labor markets, resource utilization, and the unsustainability of favorable temporary factors. The center of gravity is: inflation is currently low (below target concerns are not present, but actual readings are benign), with upside risks acknowledged. Direction: below the midpoint -- current inflation is subdued. Score should be around 38-42. The "risk" language pushes slightly above pure "subdued" but participants are scoring current conditions, not forecasts. I'll score 40 -- at the low end of "slightly below target but not concerning."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly running above trend. Greenspan: "the rate of increase in overall demand is creating a demand for employment that cannot be satisfied indefinitely." Meyer: "growth is likely to remain above trend." Kelley: "growth has averaged 3.8 percent, which is very strong." McTeer: strong district performance. All districts report strength. Capacity utilization is high. Broaddus: "Capacity utilization, domestically at least, is now approaching the level that has been associated with rising inflation pressures." The bright-line rule: "above trend" language is pervasive. Score MUST be > 50. Multiple participants describe the economy as running well above potential with strong momentum. Score: 60. This is "clearly above potential" territory -- not quite overheating, but running hot.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is the most discussed topic. Virtually every speaker describes extremely tight labor markets. Broaddus: "labor markets in much of our District are as tight as at any time since I have been in the Federal Reserve." Moskow: "labor has become the limiting factor rather than capital." Minehan: "Many of our contacts characterized labor markets as tight." Hoenig: "unemployment... anywhere from 2 percent to 4 percent across the District." Greenspan describes a fundamental disequilibrium -- employment growing 2 million/year vs. working-age population growth of 1 million. Jordan: Wendy's tables now list "positions available" instead of "special of the day." The national unemployment rate is ~4.8%. This is very tight labor market territory. The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. At 4.8% unemployment in Sept 1997, tightness is high but not quite at the 1999-2000 extreme. Score: 67.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is strong across most districts. Moskow: "reports generally point to continued strength in August and September." Jordan: "customers crawled out of their caves and started to spend at a surprisingly strong rate." McDonough: "retailers are happy and report that sales are a little ahead of their plans." Capital investment is accelerating -- Greenspan: "the extraordinary acceleration in capital investment... a big increase in capital investment this late in the business cycle is really quite startling." Auto sales strong. Overall: above trend, robust. Score: 62 -- "above trend; robust; strong momentum."

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described as loose/accommodative. Jordan's bankers: "banks have plenty of money to lend and that everyone is driving their volume up... one banker commented that everybody is chasing deals." McTeer: "competition between bank and nonbank lenders have been putting some downward pressure on rates." Kelley: "very little pressure in the yield curve or in interest rate levels... household wealth continues to rise and there is abundant liquidity." Meyer: "supportive financial conditions." The stock market is booming. Credit is freely available. This is clearly loose territory. Score: 22 -- "Loose; accommodative conditions; credit freely available."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The committee unanimously votes to hold ("B" directive) with asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Greenspan: "I do not think we should move today, but I do think the probability that we will be forced to move at the next meeting has gone up quite considerably... easily a 50/50 chance that we will decide to move in November." Meyer: "a preemptive move might well be in order at the next meeting." Minehan: "I also would have supported a small preemptive move at this meeting." Jordan: "I think that we are probably late." Broaddus favors being ready to move sooner. McDonough: "we do not need to tighten quite yet. However, we think the time is coming." Rivlin: agrees with hold but "a little less convinced" about November. McTeer: "keep an open mind." The center of gravity is: hold today, lean strongly toward tightening soon. This is a clear lean-hawkish-to-moderate-hawkish stance. Score: 58 -- hold with strong tightening bias. Not 61+ because they didn't actually move.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. Broad agreement on holding steady with asymmetric directive. Some range in how urgently they want to move next: Minehan would have supported moving today, Jordan thinks they're "probably late," while Rivlin and McTeer are more patient. But all support the recommendation. Score: 25 -- broad agreement with minor differences in timing urgency.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market valuations are mentioned by several participants. Broaddus worries about a stock market correction: "a 20 percent decline in the stock market." Jordan's bankers note "everybody is chasing deals" and "skimpy pricing in C&I lending." Greenspan discusses the stock market in context of the wealth effect and policy. Kelley notes "household wealth continues to rise." Commercial real estate overbuilding is mentioned by Broaddus and Jordan. But financial stability is not the central focus -- it's mentioned as context for the economic assessment. Score: 30 -- limited discussion; a few mention as context.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. Greenspan: "50/50 chance that we will decide to move in November." Meyer explicitly calls for preparedness to tighten in November. McDonough wants Greenspan to publicly lay groundwork for a rate increase. Broaddus, Minehan, and others express similar forward-leaning sentiment. The asymmetric directive itself signals tightening bias. Score: 65 -- mild-to-moderate tightening signal, consistent with "approaching time to begin" or re-begin tightening.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: The "new paradigm" debate reflects genuine uncertainty about the inflation-growth tradeoff. Greenspan: "we are still guessing." Boehne: "we just do not know where in the middle at this point." Stern: "there may be some warning signs out there" but can't be certain. Rivlin discusses the limits of econometrics. Greenspan acknowledges "the possibility that we may be wrong is rising." Multiple participants express inability to know if traditional models apply. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 45 -- multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty about the new paradigm question.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (67): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap implies, which makes sense given the productivity puzzle -- output is strong but labor is even tighter. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action -- this is exactly the pattern described (hold now, signal likely move soon). Coherent and explainable.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low current inflation. Score 40 (below midpoint). Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above trend. Score 60. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score 67. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score 22. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with strong tightening bias. Score 58. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "we still are not hearing reports of significant price increases in final goods and services"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still have negligible inflation in the system... there is no evidence to suggest that there is any pipeline inflation or any other type of inflation"
- MR. MOSKOW: "the inflation data continue to roll in lower than expected"
- MR. BOEHNE: "little or no upward pressure on prices"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Nearly all participants acknowledge that current inflation is remarkably low and has continued to fall, but most see upside risks from tight labor markets and high resource utilization that could eventually push inflation higher.

**Minority views:** Meyer and Broaddus see greater upside inflation risk than others; Rivlin argues the economy may be structurally less inflation-prone. These inform the +/-7 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the rate of increase in overall demand is creating a demand for employment that cannot be satisfied indefinitely"
- MR. MEYER: "growth is likely to remain above trend for some time in the absence of a change in policy"
- MR. KELLEY: "During the past four quarters, growth has averaged 3.8 percent, which is very strong... Unemployment has come down over that period from 5.3 to 4.8 percent"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants uniformly describe the economy as growing above trend with declining unemployment, consistent with running clearly above potential.

**Minority views:** Some (Parry, Guynn) expect growth to slow toward trend, but even they acknowledge the economy is currently above potential. Narrow band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "labor markets in much of our District are as tight as at any time since I have been in the Federal Reserve... signing bonuses for middle level and even clerical entry-level workers... skyrocketing wages... the remaining labor pool consisted mostly of essentially unemployable people"
- MR. MOSKOW: "labor has become the limiting factor rather than capital... labor markets had tightened further since these advisory councils previously met last spring"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "employment is rising by 2 million each year and the population of people of working age who wish to work, including immigration, is going up 1 million annually"
- MR. JORDAN: "Wendy's Restaurants across the nation once had a notice in the center of every table that said 'special of the day.' Now every table has a list of 'positions available.'"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually every participant describes very tight labor markets with widespread shortages, signing bonuses, and difficulty filling positions across skill levels and regions.

**Minority views:** Guynn notes his district labor markets are "not tighter than a year ago." Otherwise near-unanimous assessment. Narrow band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the extraordinary acceleration in capital investment. A big increase in capital investment this late in the business cycle is really quite startling"
- MR. MOSKOW: "reports generally point to continued strength in August and September... August was the best sales month so far this year for most Michigan retailers"
- MR. JORDAN: "customers crawled out of their caves and started to spend at a surprisingly strong rate... Major retailers reportedly are growing more confident"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is robust across districts and investment spending is accelerating sharply, placing the combined picture well above trend.

**Minority views:** Some note consumer spending may slow (Parry, McDonough expect moderation). Band reflects this.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "banks have plenty of money to lend and that everyone is driving their volume up to maintain earnings in the face of slimmer margins. One banker commented that everybody is chasing deals"
- MR. KELLEY: "one can find very little pressure in the yield curve or in interest rate levels... household wealth continues to rise and there is abundant liquidity in the economy"
- MR. MEYER: "supportive financial conditions and the absence of signs of imbalances"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are broadly loose with abundant liquidity, easy credit availability, rising household wealth, and competitive lending driving down rates and terms.

**Minority views:** None significantly dissent from this characterization. Narrow band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I do not think we should move today, but I do think the probability that we will be forced to move at the next meeting has gone up quite considerably... there is easily a 50/50 chance that we will decide to move in November"
- MR. MEYER: "a preemptive move might well be in order at the next meeting"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I also would have supported a small preemptive move at this meeting"
- MR. JORDAN: "I think that we are probably late"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous support for holding today but with a strong asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Multiple participants signal likely action at the next meeting, with some wanting to move even sooner.

**Minority views:** Rivlin and McTeer are more patient; Minehan and Jordan lean toward earlier action. The +/-5 band reflects this spread around a hold-with-strong-tightening-bias center.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- All speakers support Greenspan's "B asymmetric" recommendation
- MS. MINEHAN: "I also would have supported a small preemptive move at this meeting" (would have preferred tighter but supports hold)
- MS. RIVLIN: "I am a little less convinced than you that we will have to move in November or December"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on the policy action with no dissents. Differences are limited to timing nuances -- how soon a tightening move will be needed.

**Minority views:** Minehan/Jordan slightly more hawkish, Rivlin/McTeer slightly more patient. Differences are modest.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: discusses risk of "a 20 percent decline in the stock market" and concern about "some sort of inflation scare"
- MR. JORDAN: bankers report "increasingly skimpy pricing in their C&I lending... everybody is chasing deals"
- MR. KELLEY: "household wealth continues to rise and there is abundant liquidity"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions and asset valuations are mentioned by several participants as context for the economic assessment, but financial stability per se is not a primary focus of discussion. The stock market is discussed more in terms of the wealth effect on consumption than as a systemic risk.

**Minority views:** Broaddus gives more attention to stock market risk than others.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "easily a 50/50 chance that we will decide to move in November"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I encourage you to seize an opportunity in a speech or Congressional testimony... lay out the line of thought... [to make] people understand... why we would choose to firm policy in the near future"
- MR. MEYER: "we should be prepared to tighten in November if the data confirm continued above-trend growth and rising utilization rates"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. The Chairman himself puts the odds of a November move at 50/50, and multiple participants urge public communication to lay the groundwork for a rate increase.

**Minority views:** Rivlin puts the odds slightly below 50/50; McTeer wants to wait for data. But the modal signal is a clear tightening lean.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are still guessing... Until we get a sense that this unusual behavior is coming to an end, I don't think we are going to understand fully what is happening"
- MR. BOEHNE: "We just do not know where in the middle at this point"
- MR. PARRY: "It is difficult, however, to know how much weight to put on these indicators"
- MS. RIVLIN: "it is very hard to defend the proposition that economists 'know' in some sense that an economy operating below 5-1/2 percent unemployment is taking on a serious risk"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple participants explicitly express uncertainty about whether traditional economic relationships (NAIRU, Phillips curve) still hold. The "new paradigm" debate reflects genuine analytical uncertainty about the inflation-growth tradeoff, though participants are not paralyzed by it.

**Minority views:** Some (Meyer, Broaddus) express more confidence in traditional models; Rivlin expresses more openness to structural change.

### PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 70 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "manufacturing productivity appears to be rising at an annual rate of about 7-1/2 percent for the third quarter... prospective internal rates of return must be rising"
- MS. RIVLIN: "the apparent increase in productivity growth at a time when the economy has been growing for a long period and the boom might be expected to be running out of steam"
- MR. JORDAN: reporting CEOs on "major increase in labor productivity averaging 4.4 percent per year" in steel; "a contact from a very large company... sees virtually unlimited opportunities for cutting costs and improving productivity"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly recognize that productivity growth appears to be unusually strong and possibly accelerating, though there is debate about whether this represents a lasting structural shift or transitory factors.
