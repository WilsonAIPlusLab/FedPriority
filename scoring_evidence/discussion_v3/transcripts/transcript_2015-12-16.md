## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the December 15–16, 2015 FOMC meeting. It contains two sessions: (1) the go-around economic outlook discussion from December 15, with individual participant statements identified by name; and (2) the policy discussion from December 16, again with individually attributed statements. The document is fully segmented and rich in participant-level content. Approximately 16–17 speakers contributed substantive remarks: Rosengren, Lacker, George, Lockhart, Bullard, Harker, Evans, Brainard, Fischer, Kaplan, Williams, Powell, Tarullo, Mester, Dudley, and Chair Yellen (in the Chair's wrap-up). This is a very long, content-rich transcript.

### 2. Era Context
December 2015 is the historic **liftoff** meeting — the first rate hike after more than seven years at the zero lower bound. The macro backdrop: unemployment near 5%, well below its post-crisis peak of ~10%; GDP growing modestly above trend at roughly 2–2.5%; but PCE inflation running around 0.5% headline and 1.3–1.5% core, persistently below the 2% target. Oil prices had declined sharply (~52% over the prior year in WTI). The dollar had appreciated substantially (~16% since mid-2014). International headwinds (China slowdown, weak EM, weak euro-area growth) were present but not acute. High-yield credit stress had emerged in the week prior (Third Avenue fund, energy-related defaults). This context sets the backdrop but I will score only what the text says.

### 3. Directional Analysis (Pre-Score)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as clearly BELOW target. Core PCE was running at 1.3–1.5%. Headline was below 0.5% due to energy. Almost every participant discussed the persistent undershooting. Brainard: "PCE inflation has averaged 1.4 percent" for six years. Tarullo: "both headline and core have been running below that target for nearly the whole period since the financial crisis." Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation is currently at 1.3 percent." The majority view is below target with considerable concern. Fischer and Bullard took a somewhat more sanguine view (trimmed mean at 1.7%, temporary energy effects), but the center-of-gravity is clearly below target, not deeply deflationary. Score should be in the 26–40 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy running at or modestly above potential on a GDP basis, but with some residual labor market slack. Chair Yellen: "real GDP growth will continue to run a bit ahead of potential output growth." Williams: "above-trend growth." Mester: "above-trend pace of 2.7 percent." Powell: "GDP growth continuing modestly above the potential rate." Tarullo: "economy is chugging along at a moderately above-trend pace." Evans: "resource gaps closing in 2017 and then the economy running only a bit above potential in 2018." Bright-line rule: majority use "above trend/potential" language → score MUST be > 50. However, margins are modest — not a hot economy, just slightly above. Score: 52–56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Unemployment at 5%, near or at many estimates of the natural rate. Williams: "labor market as very close to our maximum employment goal" with unemployment at 5% = natural rate. Mester: "at or nearly at full employment." Bullard: "labor markets are largely healed." However, Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, and Yellen all highlight residual slack: prime-age participation still below pre-recession, subdued wage growth (ECI at 1.9%). Chair Yellen: "economy is not quite back to full employment." Center of gravity: near NAIRU but with some residual slack. Most participants cluster near 46–53 range — "near full employment" with moderate qualification. Score: 52–55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Consumer spending characterized as solid/moderate: auto sales robust ("flying off showroom floor"), retail sales advancing, PCE up ~3%. But investment weaker: energy sector capex cuts, business investment "decelerated," inventory buildup concerns. Brainard: "domestic demand continues to increase at a moderate pace." Lacker: "consumer spending to continue to grow at around 3 percent." Lockhart: "mixed" consumer spending reports, concern about inventory. Kaplan: "consumer as healthier but as being notably cautious." Net assessment: spending at or modestly above trend, investment below trend. Net score near 50, tilted slightly above. Score: 51–54.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Financial conditions had tightened some but remained broadly accommodative. Evans: "credit conditions have tightened somewhat since the previous FOMC meeting." High-yield spreads had widened significantly, particularly energy-related. Kaplan: discussed high-yield widening in detail, energy-related ripple effects. Powell: "markets have been in a correction since about midyear, particularly for the most risky assets." Vice Chairman Dudley: "other than in the high-yield market… financial conditions have not tightened appreciably." Multiple participants noted Third Avenue fund disruption. But consensus was: this was a repricing of risky assets, not a systemic tightening. Broad conditions still broadly supportive/accommodative. Score: 42–47 (slightly below neutral/loose to neutral).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This is the liftoff meeting — a 25bp hike. The vast majority supported Alternative B (hike): Williams, Evans (reluctantly), Rosengren, Fischer, Lockhart, Lacker, Kaplan, Powell, Harker, George, Mester, Dudley, Bullard, Brainard (reluctantly), Chair Yellen. Only Tarullo expressed a preference for Alternative A (no hike) but "reluctantly" supported B. This is a 25bp hike with strong emphasis on gradualism going forward. Per rubric anchor: Dec 2015 liftoff = 60–65. The hike is unanimous (or near-unanimous) but forward lean is moderate-hawkish with strong "gradual" messaging. Score: 63–66.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Formally, near-unanimous vote for Alternative B. Tarullo is the only participant who explicitly preferred Alternative A but went along. No formal voting dissents. However, there were notable substantive disagreements: Tarullo's extended critique of the inflation hypothesis; Brainard's reluctance; Evans' "tough call"; Dudley's "not a slam-dunk case." Multiple participants expressed reservations about inflation progress. The rubric anchor says Dec 2015 liftoff is in the 25–35 range ("typical one-dissent meeting"). Here there are no formal dissents but several strong reservations and one participant who stated a different preference. Score: 32–40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability received substantial discussion but was not the dominant theme. Multiple participants discussed high-yield stress, leveraged finance correction, commercial real estate in the Twelfth District, energy sector credit concerns (Kaplan at length), and Third Avenue. Fischer mentioned "some signs of accumulating financial stability problems." Rosengren raised macroprudential concerns. Dudley discussed high-yield at some length. This is moderate to moderately prominent discussion. Score: 38–48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The meeting signals liftoff now but emphasizes a "gradual" path forward. The word "gradual" appeared twice in the statement. Most participants referenced gradual normalization. Evans: "very gradual path for rate increases." Brainard: "path is likely to be more gradual and move to a lower level than has been the case in past tightening cycles." Williams: four increases in 2016. Powell: three increases. Dudley: two increases in 2016. The median SEP dot was around four hikes in 2016. Forward signal = hiking cycle beginning, but flagged as very gradual. Per rubric: 60–75 for "begin normalization" / mild tightening bias. Score: 65–70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Considerable uncertainty, especially about inflation dynamics (Phillips curve inertness, inflation expectations, China, oil). Tarullo gave a lengthy treatment of why the "plausible hypothesis" for inflation returning was uncertain. Brainard: "hard to point to any developments… that have raised my confidence." Lockhart: concerns about inflation expectations. Evans: uncertainty arguments. Rosengren: inflation return not assured. Chair Yellen acknowledged "cautious approach" due to "heightened uncertainty." Multiple participants flagged the ZLB proximity as making errors costly. This is meaningfully elevated uncertainty, particularly around inflation. Score: 48–58.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- Labor tightness: ~8 participants said near/at full employment; ~6–7 said meaningful slack remains.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 34 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "PCE inflation has averaged 1.4 percent, while core PCE inflation has averaged 1.5 percent" over six years; "The Phillips curve remains inert, a tiny fraction of its former self."
- Rosengren: "Core PCE inflation is currently at 1.3 percent." Market-based inflation expectations "at or near 10-year lows."
- Tarullo: "both headline and core have been running below that target for nearly the whole period since the financial crisis, and this Committee has pretty consistently overestimated what inflation will be a year in advance."

**Center-of-gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as stubbornly below target with persistent disinflationary forces (energy, dollar, global); a minority (Bullard, Fischer) view it as close to target net of energy shock but do not move the majority view.

**Minority views:** Bullard argued trimmed mean PCE at 1.7% is "only three-tenths below the Committee's goal." Fischer: "largely temporary forces are keeping the inflation rate significantly below its target level." These shift the uncertainty band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 54 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I see growth continuing above trend in 2016"; "labor market overshooting our full employment target by a substantial margin."
- Chair Yellen: "real GDP growth will continue to run a bit ahead of potential output growth, thereby generating further improvements in labor market conditions."
- Powell: "real GDP growth continuing modestly above the potential rate."

**Center-of-gravity:** Economy is broadly running at a modestly above-trend pace, with most participants projecting continued above-potential growth. Not a heated overshoot — the language is consistently "modestly above."

**Minority views:** Evans noted "resource gaps closing in 2017 and then the economy running only a bit above potential in 2018," suggesting he sees the gap as small. Brainard emphasized residual slack still present in prime-age participation. These keep the score from rising further.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 53 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "With national unemployment at my estimate of the natural rate of 5 percent, I see the labor market as very close to our maximum employment goal."
- Mester: "I view the current economy at or nearly at full employment from the standpoint of our monetary policy goal."
- Chair Yellen: "the economy is not quite back to full employment. The unemployment rate is still ¼ percentage point above my own estimate of its longer-run level"; "wage growth…remains soft, suggesting not only that the labor market is not all that tight."

**Center-of-gravity:** Majority of participants see the labor market as near full employment, with unemployment at 5% at or near the natural rate. A meaningful group (Brainard, Tarullo, Powell, Yellen) cite remaining slack via participation, wages, and part-time employment. Score is slightly above 50, consistent with "near NAIRU" with modest residual slack.

**Minority views:** Bullard: labor market "largely healed"; Lacker: signs of increasing wage growth; Harker: "real economy remains healthy." These pull slightly higher but do not represent the majority nuanced view.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 52 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Chair Yellen: "Motor vehicle sales have been robust, retail sales are advancing at a solid pace…Housing continues its gradual recovery, and business fixed investment appears to be expanding steadily outside the drilling and mining sector."
- Brainard: "domestic demand continues to increase at a moderate pace. The recent data on consumer spending have been close to expectations—with auto sales, in particular, remaining strong—and real PCE looks to increase nearly 3 percent this year."
- Lockhart: "consumer spending reports were mixed"; concern about heightened inventory levels potentially weighing on production.

**Center-of-gravity:** Consumer spending solid/moderate, auto sales strong, but business investment weaker (energy capex cuts, cautious capex plans), and inventory concerns noted. Net position at or slightly above trend — the consumer side is above trend but investment weakens the aggregate.

**Minority views:** Kaplan noted consumers are "healthier but notably cautious." Evans: "the news about the consumer has been okay but not robust." These keep score from rising into the 55–60 range.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 43 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "credit conditions have tightened somewhat since the previous FOMC meeting" with spreads up; "a healthy repricing of riskier assets and not an unwarranted reduction in credit availability."
- Dudley: "other than in the high-yield market, which is undergoing a lot of stress, financial conditions have not tightened appreciably" even as probability of hike rose from 30% to 90%.
- Kaplan: "High-yield credit spreads have widened" with energy-related ripple effects; Third Avenue fund disruption; "we are just going to have to monitor it."

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial conditions remain broadly accommodative at the aggregate level, despite high-yield stress. Multiple participants specifically noted equity markets "little changed," modest bond yield rise, and only selective credit tightening. This puts conditions in the loose-to-neutral range, closer to neutral given the HY stress.

**Minority views:** Rosengren and Fischer raised financial stability concerns about conditions being "conducive to sowing the seeds" of problems — but framed as future risk, not current tightness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 64 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I unequivocally support alternative B as written. The data convincingly support this decision."
- Fischer: "I favor raising the target federal funds rate to a range of 25 to 50 basis points."
- Tarullo: "I will reluctantly go along with alternative B today" — the sole participant preferring alternative A.

**Center-of-gravity:** An overwhelming consensus in favor of hiking (Alternative B). 14–15 of approximately 16 speaking participants supported the hike. The hawkish lean is clear but tempered by strong emphasis on gradualism and data dependence going forward. Per the rubric anchor: Dec 2015 liftoff = 60–65.

**Minority views:** Tarullo's preference for Alternative A (no hike) is the only explicit dissent. Brainard and Evans expressed significant reservations but ultimately supported B.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 35 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Tarullo: "I don't think it's appropriate policy to raise rates today… My policy preference is captured fairly well by alternative A… I will reluctantly go along with alternative B."
- Evans: "I find today to be a tough call. I think the risk-management case for delay until mid-2016 remains strong."
- Brainard: "combination of…core inflation stubbornly below its target…leads me to place somewhat greater weight on the possible regret associated with tightening too early."

**Center-of-gravity:** Zero formal voting dissents, but one participant (Tarullo) explicitly preferred a different policy and one (Evans) called it a "tough call," and two (Brainard, Dudley) expressed substantial reservations. The rubric anchor places Dec 2015 at 25–35. The explicit reluctance and extended critiques from multiple speakers push slightly above the anchor lower end.

**Minority views:** The lack of formal dissent despite Tarullo's stated preference for Alternative A is notable — he cited institutional concerns, not a change of view.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 42 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kaplan gave an extended discussion of energy sector credit stress, high-yield widening, and Third Avenue ripple effects.
- Williams discussed commercial real estate booming in the Twelfth District; "potential for imbalances merits close monitoring."
- Fischer: "some signs of accumulating financial stability problems" used as one of three reasons to hike now; Rosengren raised macroprudential concerns.

**Center-of-gravity:** Financial stability received moderate discussion from multiple participants — more than a passing mention but not dominating the meeting. The high-yield/energy credit stress was a recurring theme across 5–6 speakers. Not dominant but meaningfully present.

**Minority views:** Most of the discussion treated the HY situation as a concern to monitor, not a crisis — consistent with a score in the moderate range rather than high range.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 67 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Evans: "I can support alternative B today, in large part because of its important state-dependent characterization of our gradual adjustment path."
- Chair Yellen: "our economic projections imply that the federal funds rate will likely have to rise gradually over the next few years."
- Brainard: "the path is likely to be more gradual and move to a lower level than has been the case in past tightening cycles."

**Center-of-gravity:** The forward guidance is clearly hawkish (hiking cycle has begun) but substantially modulated by "gradual" emphasis, data dependence, and multiple participants' relatively dovish SEP submissions (2–3 hikes in 2016 for many). The signal is "we are tightening but will be patient." Per the rubric: 60–75 for "begin normalization" / hiking cycle. The heavy "gradual" messaging keeps it from the upper end of that range.

**Minority views:** Williams had four hikes in 2016 and five in 2017 (the most hawkish forward path discussed), pulling the upper uncertainty band. Tarullo explicitly did not want stronger language committing to a gradual approach.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 52 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Tarullo gave an extended, multi-page argument on why inflation dynamics were uncertain, including possible secular changes: "it is precisely at times when received wisdom is breaking down that policy missteps look, in retrospect, most likely to occur."
- Brainard: "it would be difficult for me to point to any developments in the intermeeting period that have raised my confidence that core inflation will move to our 2 percent target in the next two to three years."
- Chair Yellen: "taking a cautious approach to tightening remains appropriate in light of the asymmetric risk posed by the effective lower bound"; "heightened uncertainty about the economic effects of higher interest rates."

**Center-of-gravity:** Elevated uncertainty, predominantly about inflation dynamics and the Phillips curve. Multiple participants explicitly questioned whether historical correlations would reassert themselves. The ZLB proximity made errors more costly. However, many participants remained relatively confident about the growth and employment outlook. The uncertainty is concentrated on inflation, not comprehensive.

**Minority views:** Lacker, Mester, and George expressed fairly balanced/contained uncertainty; Williams explicitly said the decision was clear. This keeps the score from the 60–75 range.

The concern is about dollar STRENGTH and its disinflationary and trade-drag effects. Score: clear majority expressing concern about dollar strength. Higher = more concern about dollar strength. Score 68.
