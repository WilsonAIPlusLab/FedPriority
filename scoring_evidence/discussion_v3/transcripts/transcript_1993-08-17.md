# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1993-08-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers the go-around discussion of economic conditions and a policy discussion focused on whether to move from an asymmetric (toward tightening) directive to a symmetric directive. Speakers include: Boehne, Broaddus, Syron, Hoenig, Stern, McTeer, Melzer, Jordan, Lindsey, Mullins, LaWare, Kelley, McDonough, Phillips, Angell, Forrestal, Parry, Keehn, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
August 1993: The economy is roughly two years into a slow, uneven recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate is at 3%, having been cut aggressively. Unemployment is around 6.8%. Core CPI has been running about 3-3.5%. A budget deficit reduction package has just been enacted. Long-term interest rates have declined sharply. The key policy question is when (not whether) the Fed will eventually need to raise rates.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as settling in the 3-3.5% range. Recent months have shown encouraging deceleration (core CPI 2.4% over last 5 months vs. 4.6% over previous 5 months). Boehne: "not likely to see an acceleration... just kind of stuck." Broaddus: core CPI "risen at an annual rate of about 2-1/2 percent over the last five months." Mullins: "inflation apparently has settled down in the 3 to 3-1/2 percent range" which is "somewhat higher... than we would have expected." Kelley: "inflation surge was temporary" but "at much too high a level." Stern: businesses "aren't seeing any inflationary pressures." Phillips: "more encouraged by the recent news" but "3 to 3-1/2 percent range, we can't be satisfied." Jordan's bankers: "inflation has already hit its low and is going to move up." Angell: concerned about dollar/yen and future inflation risks. The center of gravity is that inflation is not accelerating, recent readings are encouraging, but the level (around 3-3.5%) remains above what participants want. Direction: modestly below the midpoint of concern -- inflation is present but not worsening and recent data are encouraging. This is "below target but with slack keeping prices in check" territory -- though the 2% target wasn't formally established, participants clearly view 3-3.5% as too high but not alarmingly so. Score: ~38-42.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Participants see inflation settling at 3-3.5%, above desired levels but not accelerating, recent data encouraging. This is moderate, somewhat subdued pressure. Score of 40 fits.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is growing at about 2.5% -- "moderate," "muddling along." Boehne: "moderate growth... 2-1/2 percent." Mullins: "six quarters averaging 2-1/2 percent." Greenspan: "economy confronted with balance sheet problems." Phillips: "slow or moderate growth." Hoenig: "continues to grow modestly." LaWare: "risks to the economy are shifting toward the down side." McDonough: Second District "bumping along a trough." Multiple participants note manufacturing is sluggish, employment growth is slow. Unemployment at 6.8% (ex-California 6.4%). The economy is clearly still below potential with slack remaining. Direction: below potential, recovering but gap persists. Score: ~35-38.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential with significant slack. Score of 37 is below 50, consistent.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6.8%, "almost a percentage point lower than last year." Boehne: "slow employment growth... attitude of not adding workers if we don't have to." Mullins: "payroll employment running at a 2 million job pace" but "the jobs aren't great." Phillips: "labor market is seeing some improvement, but really only slightly more than handling the demographics... quality of much of that job growth has not been that good." McDonough: "labor markets have been weak." Multiple Districts report sluggish manufacturing employment. Direction: below full employment, improving but still slack. Score: ~33-37.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. 6.8% unemployment, slow job growth, slack -- below full employment. Score of 35 is consistent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mullins: "consumer spending advanced at almost a 4 percent annual pace; personal saving rate dropped again. Business fixed investment advanced at a 13 percent annual rate." But also: "auto sales have backed down a bit," "consumer and business confidence continue to recede." Boehne: "soft retail sales." Syron: "retail sector is mixed." Housing is improving. Multiple references to decent spending despite poor confidence. Direction: mixed but moderate -- spending is holding up reasonably well but not booming. Score: ~46-50.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Spending is near trend -- consumer spending 4%, BFI 13%, but offset by weak confidence, auto softening. Score of 48 is at trend, consistent.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term rates have declined sharply (30-year bond near 6.5% vs. expected 7.5%). Broaddus: "nice sharp decline in long-term interest rates... improved financial conditions are probably the main thing the economy has going for it." Mullins: "low quality spreads on bonds, very low spreads on junk bonds," "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices." Syron: "mutual fund industry continues to see very, very strong inflows." Banks "starting to see some slight improvement" in lending. Angell: "3 percent rate provides lots of liquidity in the financial markets... opportunity for certain bubble events." McDonough: 2-30 year spread at 235bp. Real short rates estimated 2-3.5% below equilibrium. Direction: conditions are loose -- low rates, tight spreads, strong equity markets, mutual fund inflows, early signs of speculative excess. Score: ~20-25.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are loose/accommodative. Score of 22 is consistent (low = loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The meeting consensus is to hold the fed funds rate at 3% and move from asymmetric (toward tightening) to symmetric directive. NO ONE advocates easing. Many note the eventual need to raise rates. Greenspan: "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher" but "we can afford at this stage to move back to symmetry." Mullins: "probability we will tighten in the intermeeting period is greater than the probability we will ease... two epsilons versus one epsilon." Angell: "very uneasy with the fed funds rate where it is" and wants to raise when possible (most hawkish). Broaddus: prefers staying asymmetric toward tightening. Jordan: questions whether Greenbook inflation forecast is "wrong or acceptable." But the majority sees no imminent action needed. This is a hold with medium-term tightening bias. Direction: lean hawkish -- hold steady but universal expectation of eventual tightening, no one wants to ease. Score: ~53-57.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Participants are holding but with a tightening bias for the medium term. Moving from asymmetric-toward-tightening to symmetric is slightly less hawkish tactically but the strategic lean remains. Score of 55 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The vote was unanimous (12-0) for "B" symmetric. Broaddus preferred asymmetric toward tightening. Angell was "very uneasy" and wanted higher rates but accepted symmetric. Jordan was "puzzled" but did not dissent. LaWare had reservations about signaling but supported symmetric. The debate was mainly about the directive language (symmetric vs. asymmetric) rather than the rate itself -- nobody wanted to change the funds rate. Direction: broad agreement on the hold, moderate differences on directive language. Score: ~25-30.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous vote, but 2-3 participants had meaningful reservations about going symmetric. Score of 28 is consistent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Mullins raised "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices... very low IPO volumes, the lowest in 15 years, low quality spreads on bonds, and very low spreads on junk bonds." Angell: "3 percent rate provides lots of liquidity... opportunity for certain bubble events." Syron mentioned mutual fund flows and overseas hedging activity. But these were secondary observations, not dominant themes. Most participants focused on growth and inflation. Direction: limited discussion, a few mentions. Score: ~15-20.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention to financial stability. Only 2-3 participants raised it as a meaningful concern. Score of 18 fits.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan: "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher... we have moved tactically, not strategically." Mullins: "I still have this viewpoint over the longer term" (rates need to rise). Multiple participants frame the symmetric move as tactical, not a change in strategy. The forward signal is mild tightening bias -- rates will eventually go up but no timeline. Direction: mild tightening lean forward. Score: ~58-63.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Forward signal is that rates will eventually rise, but timing uncertain. Score of 60 is consistent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Hoenig: "very uncertain future." Phillips: "a lot of these factors are contributing" to uncertainty. Multiple references to uncertainty about fiscal package effects, health care reform, flood impacts. But the overall tone is not one of deep uncertainty -- most are fairly confident in moderate growth around 2.5%. Boehne: "hard to see major risks... on the up side... on the down side." Direction: somewhat elevated but not extreme. Score: ~35-40.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Moderate level of uncertainty, elevated somewhat by fiscal package unknowns. Score of 37 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack. Aligned.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Both lean mildly hawkish. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish than current action, which makes sense -- they are holding now but signaling eventual tightening. Aligned.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (22, loose) is consistent with the discussion of very low rates and speculative signs.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "inflation apparently has settled down in the 3 to 3-1/2 percent range. That is somewhat higher, perhaps 1/2 percent or so higher, than we would have expected earlier this year"
- MR. BROADDUS: "Core CPI has risen at an annual rate of about 2-1/2 percent over the last five months, which is a pretty good hunk of time"
- MR. KELLEY: "more and more it seems that last winter's inflation surge was temporary... inflation now seems to be trendless, albeit at much too high a level"

Center of gravity: Participants see inflation settling around 3-3.5%, recent readings encouraging but the level remains above their comfort zone. No acceleration, some deceleration. Moderate inflation pressure -- present but contained.

Minority views: Jordan's bankers think inflation "has already hit its low and is going to move up." Angell warns of yen-related pass-through risks. These push the uncertainty band upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BOEHNE: "the moderate growth theme is the right one; 2-1/2 percent is the number... The District economy continues to muddle along"
- MR. MULLINS: "With growth in the first half at a rate of only a little over 1 percent, it's difficult to be too enthusiastic"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are dealing with an economy confronted with balance sheet problems, problems of excess debt from an earlier period"

Center of gravity: Economy growing at about 2.5%, well below potential, with persistent slack from balance sheet repair. Manufacturing weak, employment growth slow.

Minority views: Mullins notes some underlying strength (BFI up 13%, consumer spending up 4%). Jordan reports Fourth District conditions "better than they previously expected."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BOEHNE: "slow employment growth. This attitude of not adding workers if we don't have to is very strong"
- MR. MULLINS: "payroll employment... running at a 2 million job pace for the year... the unemployment rate is almost a percentage point lower than it was last year at this time. It's 6.8 percent"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The labor market is seeing some improvement, but really only slightly more than handling the demographics. And the quality of much of that job growth has not been that good"

Center of gravity: Unemployment at 6.8% is still elevated with significant slack. Employment growth is improving but reluctant hiring persists. Below full employment.

Minority views: Mullins offers a more positive read on job creation pace. Most others emphasize weakness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "consumer spending advanced at almost a 4 percent annual pace... Business fixed investment advanced at a 13 percent annual rate in the second quarter"
- MR. BOEHNE: "sluggish manufacturing, soft retail sales, some improvement in residential construction"
- MR. MULLINS: "both consumer and business confidence continue to recede on surveys"

Center of gravity: Hard data on spending are reasonably solid (consumer spending, BFI), but confidence is weakening, auto sales softening, and the outlook is tempered by fiscal drag concerns. Net position is approximately at trend.

Minority views: LaWare sees downside risks from tax increases; Mullins sees underlying strength.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "the nice sharp decline in long-term interest rates... the improved financial conditions are probably the main thing the economy has going for it right now"
- MR. MULLINS: "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices... very low IPO volumes, the lowest in 15 years, low quality spreads on bonds, and very low spreads on junk bonds"
- MR. ANGELL: "3 percent rate provides lots of liquidity in the financial markets and it provides an opportunity for certain bubble events"

Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose -- record low long-term rates, tight credit spreads, strong equity markets, mutual fund inflows, early speculative signs. Real short rates estimated 2-3.5% below equilibrium.

Minority views: None argue conditions are tight.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher... we have moved tactically, not strategically"
- MR. MULLINS: "probability we will tighten in the intermeeting period is greater than the probability we will ease... two epsilons versus one epsilon"
- MR. ANGELL: "very uneasy with the fed funds rate where it is... we ought to be fully conscious of the opportunity whenever forces are such that we can get the rate closer to... equilibrium"

Center of gravity: Unanimous hold at 3% funds rate. Move from asymmetric-toward-tightening to symmetric directive, framed as tactical, not strategic. Universal expectation of eventual tightening. No one advocates easing.

Minority views: Broaddus preferred staying asymmetric. Angell wanted higher rates. Both represent hawkish outliers within the hold consensus.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Vote: 12-0 for "B" symmetric
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would favor pretty strongly sticking with an asymmetric directive"
- MR. JORDAN: "I'm not sure where I come out" -- puzzled about declaring victory given Greenbook inflation forecast

Center of gravity: Unanimous vote, but meaningful debate about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive. Two to three participants had reservations about returning to symmetry. Disagreement was about tactics/communication, not the rate decision itself.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "signs of the beginning of speculative excess in stock prices, especially the weak stocks... very low IPO volumes, the lowest in 15 years, low quality spreads on bonds"
- MR. ANGELL: "3 percent rate provides lots of liquidity in the financial markets and it provides an opportunity for certain bubble events"

Center of gravity: Only 2-3 participants raised financial stability concerns, and these were secondary to the main discussion of growth and inflation. Brief mentions, not a focus.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we still expect that at some point short-term real rates will have to move higher... this is not a change in our policy"
- MR. MULLINS: "I still have this viewpoint over the longer term... we laid down some useful groundwork... without backing away from the groundwork we've laid concerning the importance of continued disinflation"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the most important thing is that our policy should promote price stability... our commitment to price stability"

Center of gravity: Forward signal is mild tightening bias. Participants are clear that rates will eventually need to rise, but timing is uncertain and data-dependent. The symmetric directive is tactical; the strategic lean remains toward tightening.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 37 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "we see a very uncertain future... the uncertainty has changed from what the fiscal package will be to what the effects of the fiscal package will be and then we add on health care uncertainty"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "a lot of these factors are contributing to the outlook for slow growth" -- lists multiple unknowns
- MR. BOEHNE: "hard to see major risks that would cause a significant break-out on the up side. Likewise, it's hard to see a break-out on the down side"

Center of gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty driven by fiscal package effects, health care reform, and floods, but most participants are fairly confident in the moderate growth baseline. Not deep uncertainty.
