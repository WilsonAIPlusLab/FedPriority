# FOMC Scoring: 1999-03-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions from the go-around and policy discussion. Speakers include: McTeer, Minehan, Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Guynn, Broaddus, McDonough, Stern, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, Poole, Gramlich, Jordan, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a late-1990s transcript with a traditional go-around structure.

### Era Context
March 1999 -- the late-1990s expansion/boom. The economy is growing rapidly, unemployment is very low (around 4.2% nationally, lower in many districts), the Asian crisis effects are fading, productivity growth is accelerating, and inflation is remarkably subdued despite tight labor markets. The Fed had cut rates three times in fall 1998 as insurance against the LTCM/Asian crisis spillovers. Kosovo conflict is underway. The stock market just crossed 10,000 on the Dow.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants universally note that current inflation is extremely low -- "virtually zero inflation" (Greenspan), "complete absence of inflation" (McDonough), CPI rose 0.1% in Jan and Feb, "no inflation in the pipeline" (McTeer). However, many participants express concern about future inflation acceleration in 2000 (Meyer, Moskow, Parry, Guynn, Broaddus). The CENTER OF GRAVITY is that current inflation is very low, well below target. Several note favorable supply shocks are suppressing prices. Even those worried about future inflation acknowledge it is absent now. Direction: clearly below midpoint. PCE/CPI near zero. But participants are not alarmed about deflation -- they see it as benign. Score should be in the 26-40 range: "below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation." Given "virtually zero" language and that even core measures show almost no inflation, I lean toward the lower end of this range, around 30.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very low current inflation. Score ~30 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Multiple participants describe the economy as operating above potential. Guynn: "our economy very likely is operating beyond sustainable levels at the moment." Broaddus: "U.S. economy already operating well above potential GDP." Greenspan: "labor markets are very taut" and aggregate demand is accelerating sharply. Meyer: "we have allowed the economy to move beyond its sustainable capacity." GDP growth is described as "surprisingly strong," with domestic final demand revised up dramatically. The consensus is clearly above potential. Direction: above midpoint. The language "well above potential" (Broaddus), "beyond sustainable levels" (Guynn), "beyond sustainable capacity" (Meyer) suggests clearly above potential. This is the late-90s boom. Score should be in the 59-70 range per anchors (57-63 for late 90s boom). Given the extremely strong language, I'll score 62.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score 62 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is one of the most discussed dimensions. Nearly every participant describes labor markets as very tight. Minehan: "almost every type of worker is said to be hard to find." Hoenig: District unemployment 3.3%. Jordan: "unemployment is less than 2 percent" in some metro areas, over 500 unfilled positions at one bank. Greenspan: "labor markets are very taut. Initial claims are unbelievable." Poole: ongoing problems recruiting entry-level employees. Multiple participants describe construction workers being lured out of retirement. The center of gravity is very tight labor markets. Direction: well above midpoint. Anchors say 65-75 for 1999-2000. I'll score 70.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score 70 matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is described as very strong by nearly all participants. Broaddus: "Consumer spending remains very strong... retail sales are now rising more rapidly than they have at any time in the last year." Greenspan: "The numbers for private domestic final purchases are truly awesome." Moskow: light vehicle sales "exceptionally strong," automakers revised up forecasts. Jordan: retailers reporting further increases, inventories below desired levels. Housing/construction is "extremely robust." Business investment also strong. Direction: well above midpoint. Score should be 65-70 range for "well above trend" / "unsustainably rapid."

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says very strong. Score 67 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Conditions have eased since the LTCM crisis of fall 1998. Minehan: "credit market conditions continue to be regarded as favorable, with spreads wider than a year ago but funding readily available." Venture capital contacts describe deals being made at "extremely rich prices." Moskow: "calmer financial conditions." McDonough implicitly: credit is plentiful. Minehan: "the stock market remains exuberant... credit is plentiful." Jordan: "strong loan demand." The consensus is conditions are quite loose -- credit available, stock market booming, spreads narrowed from crisis but still accommodative. Direction: below midpoint (loose conditions). Score around 25-30 for "loose; accommodative conditions; credit freely available."

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose. Score 28 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a nuanced meeting. Greenspan recommends hold + symmetric, and most agree. But there is significant hawkish sentiment:
- Explicit hawks wanting immediate tightening: Poole (would dissent for tightening), Hoenig (recommends tightening), Broaddus (wants asymmetric directive toward tightening)
- Hawks who accept hold reluctantly: Meyer (biased toward tightening going forward), Guynn (leaning toward tilt to tighter), Moskow (policy too expansionary but can wait), Minehan (would prefer asymmetric), Parry (inclined toward asymmetric), Jordan (frustrated by delays)
- Hold/balanced: Greenspan (hold symmetric, next move likely up), Kelley (pulled toward asymmetric but paused by Kosovo), McDonough (hold), Stern (hold), Ferguson (hold), Gramlich (hold, "asymmetric in my head"), McTeer (hold)

The center of gravity is: hold steady, but with strong recognition the next move is up. Many participants are "asymmetric in their heads." This is a lean-hawkish to moderate-hawkish meeting. The decision is hold + symmetric, but the forward lean is clearly hawkish. Score around 55-58 for "lean hawkish: hold steady with tightening bias."

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Most favor hold but lean hawkish. Score 56 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent at this meeting (Poole says he would dissent if voting, Hoenig recommends tightening). There is a notable range: from McTeer/McDonough (comfortable holding, no need to move) to Poole/Hoenig/Broaddus (want action now). Multiple participants express reservations about waiting (Meyer, Guynn, Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Jordan). This is broader than typical -- distinct camps exist (hold vs. tighten), though the hold camp prevails. Score around 40-45 for "moderate differences: notable differences about pace/risk."

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows notable differences but no formal dissent. Score 42 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants raise financial stability concerns. Minehan: venture capital contacts describe deals at "extremely rich prices," one firm "completely out of the market," investment community views conditions as "frothy, bordering on excessive." Jordan: extended discussion of financial sector risks from productivity reversals, parallels to 1980s lending mistakes. Greenspan mentions stock market. Jordan: "stock market finally closing above 10,000." Kelley: notes stock market. Minehan: parallels to Japan's bubble. This is more than passing mention -- several participants address asset valuations and financial stability at some length. Score around 35-45. Multiple participants address it meaningfully but it's not the dominant topic. Score 40.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Evidence shows moderate discussion. Score 40 matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan explicitly states: "it is exceptionally likely that the next move the Committee will be making will be on the upside." Multiple participants agree the next move is likely a rate increase. But the timing is uncertain and they are signaling patience for now. The forward lean is toward tightening but with data dependence. Score around 60-65 for "mild tightening bias."

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says next move likely up but patient. Score 62 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is genuine uncertainty about the productivity puzzle and its implications. Parry: "there are large uncertainties." Greenspan: the key question is whether this is "an aberration or the emergence of a new era." Gramlich: "the truth only becomes known many years after the fact." Ferguson: "we aren't really sure" what maximum sustainable growth is. Models have been consistently wrong. However, this is not panic-level uncertainty -- participants have a reasonable grasp of the economy, they just acknowledge model limitations. Score around 40-50 for "elevated" uncertainty. Multiple participants emphasize the difficulty of forecasting in this environment. Score 45.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows elevated but not extreme uncertainty. Score 45 matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with a boom. Labor tightness is higher because participants emphasize labor market extremes more than overall output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Both lean hawkish, with forward tone slightly more so because Greenspan explicitly states next move is up. The current action is hold, so the lean is moderate. This alignment makes sense.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "There is no evidence of which I am aware of any acceleration in prices... inflation effectively at zero"
- MCTEER: "Contemporary measures of inflation remain flat. The CPI rose 0.1 percent in both January and February; inflation in the pipeline and leading indicators of inflation continue to be favorable"
- MCDONOUGH: "with a complete absence of inflation"
- MEYER: "stronger-than-expected growth and lower-than-expected inflation"

Center-of-gravity: Participants universally see current inflation as very low, well below any reasonable target, with no signs of acceleration in the pipeline. However, several note concern about 2000 inflation forecasts.

Minority views: Meyer, Moskow, Guynn, and Broaddus are more worried about future inflation acceleration (2000), which widens the band somewhat, but even they acknowledge current inflation is essentially absent.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GUYNN: "It appears that our economy very likely is operating beyond sustainable levels at the moment"
- BROADDUS: "the U.S. economy already operating well above potential GDP"
- MEYER: "we have allowed the economy to move beyond its sustainable capacity"
- GREENSPAN: domestic demand acceleration "truly awesome"; manufacturing may be turning up

Center-of-gravity: Strong consensus that the economy is operating above potential, with domestic demand accelerating sharply beyond expectations. GDP growth well above trend.

Minority views: Some (McDonough, Ferguson) emphasize that the economy is performing exactly as desired (sustained growth + price stability), which moderately tempers the "overheating" framing.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "labor markets are very taut. Initial claims are unbelievable. The tightness in markets is such that... production in the First District is being restricted by a lack of workers"
- HOENIG: "District labor markets remain very tight... unemployment rate of about 3.3 percent"
- JORDAN: "southwest Ohio and central Kentucky metro areas where reported unemployment is less than 2 percent... one large regional commercial bank... currently has over 500 approved openings that it is unable to fill"
- MINEHAN: "almost every type of worker is said to be hard to find"

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous view that labor markets are very tight, with severe shortages in many areas. Unemployment far below NAIRU estimates. However, wage acceleration remains puzzlingly muted.

Minority views: Minimal. The puzzle is that wages are not accelerating as expected given the tightness, which somewhat complicates the picture but does not change the tightness assessment.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "The numbers for private domestic final purchases are truly awesome"
- BROADDUS: "domestic final demand... really have been extraordinary... raised to close to 7 percent"
- MOSKOW: "Light vehicle sales were exceptionally strong... housing remains robust"
- JORDAN: "Retailers... reporting that the first quarter will show further increases... inventories are below desired levels"

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and business investment are very strong, well above trend. Housing robust, auto sales exceptional, construction booming. This is clearly above-trend spending.

Minority views: Some manufacturing softness noted (steel, agriculture equipment), but offset by strength in motor vehicles and construction. Net assessment is firmly above trend.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MINEHAN: "credit market conditions continue to be regarded as favorable, with spreads wider than a year ago but funding readily available... The stock market remains exuberant, to say the least; credit is plentiful"
- MINEHAN: Venture capital contacts: "deals were being made at extremely rich prices"
- JORDAN: "Bankers continue to report strong loan demand"
- MOSKOW: "calmer financial conditions"

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative -- credit freely available, stock market exuberant, venture capital flowing. Spreads wider than pre-LTCM crisis but conditions are loose overall.

Minority views: Some note spreads are wider than a year ago and local lending growth has declined (Minehan), which provides a small offset. Stern mentions bankers expressing concern about credit quality of commercial loan portfolios.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "it is exceptionally likely that the next move the Committee will be making will be on the upside" but recommends hold + symmetric
- POOLE: "if I were a voting member I would, with respect, dissent... I believe it is time to unwind those cuts"
- HOENIG: "I would still recommend that we tighten"
- MEYER: "monetary policy should be biased toward tightening going forward"
- MCDONOUGH: "I not only support but applaud your recommendation" for hold

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement to hold rates, but with a clear tightening bias. Multiple participants describe themselves as "asymmetric in their heads" (Gramlich). The next move is widely expected to be up. However, the decision is hold + symmetric, restraining the score.

Minority views: Poole and Hoenig explicitly advocate tightening now. Broaddus wants asymmetric directive. McDonough/McTeer are most comfortable with the hold.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- POOLE: "if I were a voting member I would, with respect, dissent from your recommendation"
- HOENIG: "I would still recommend that we tighten"
- BROADDUS: "a step toward an asymmetric directive would be a good way" to act
- Multiple participants (Meyer, Guynn, Minehan, Parry) express reservations about waiting but accept the hold

Center-of-gravity: No formal dissents but notable differences. At least 2-3 participants would have preferred tightening or asymmetric directive. Several more express reservations. Two clear camps: act now vs. wait. The differences extend to the economic assessment (how seriously to take model forecasts of future inflation).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MINEHAN: Investment community contacts "all view economic conditions and financial markets as frothy, bordering on excessive"; venture capital firms "completely out of the market"; parallels to Japan
- JORDAN: Extended analysis of financial sector risks from productivity reversals, lending mistakes, "stability in various measures of average prices of consumer goods is not a sufficient condition for overall monetary and financial stability"
- STERN: "bankers are expressing a little concern about the credit quality of their commercial loan portfolios"

Center-of-gravity: Several participants address financial stability/asset valuation concerns meaningfully. Minehan and Jordan provide extended commentary. The stock market at 10,000 and venture capital froth are noted. However, it is not the dominant topic -- most focus is on real economy and inflation outlook.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "it is exceptionally likely that the next move the Committee will be making will be on the upside"
- STERN: "if I were a betting man... our next move is more likely to be an increase... than a decrease"
- MOSKOW: "it will be important to increase rates later this year"
- GRAMLICH: "it may be necessary to move sharply later, and we should recognize that"

Center-of-gravity: Strong forward signal that the next move will be a rate increase, though timing is uncertain. The signal is clearly toward tightening but tempered by data dependence and Kosovo uncertainty.

Minority views: McDonough and McTeer are more dovish about the forward outlook, questioning whether models forecasting inflation are reliable.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- GREENSPAN: "The interesting question, however, is how to project that trend of acceleration in productivity... whether we are looking at an aberration or at the emergence of a new era"
- PARRY: "there are large uncertainties. The economy has defied expectations of a slowdown for several years"
- GRAMLICH: "the truth only becomes known many years after the fact"
- FERGUSON: "we aren't really sure" what maximum sustainable growth is; "the forecasts have been inaccurate"

Center-of-gravity: Participants express elevated uncertainty about the productivity trend, the NAIRU, and the inflation outlook. Models have been persistently wrong. Multiple participants acknowledge genuine difficulty in assessing the economy's potential. However, this is not extreme or crisis-level uncertainty -- participants have a functioning framework, they just acknowledge its limitations.
