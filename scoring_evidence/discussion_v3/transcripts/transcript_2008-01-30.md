# FOMC Transcript Scoring — January 29–30, 2008

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of the January 29–30, 2008 FOMC meeting. It contains individual participant statements from the two-day go-around (economic assessment) and the policy round. Speakers are fully identified (e.g., MR. LACKER., MR. PLOSSER., VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER., CHAIRMAN BERNANKE.). The transcript is long and well-populated with substantive commentary from at least 15 identified participants. Confidence will be High for most dimensions.

### 2. Era Context

January 2008: The housing correction is in full force, the subprime crisis is widening into broader credit markets, and there is acute concern about recession. The Fed had already cut 175 basis points since September 2007. The meeting included an intermeeting cut of 75 bp on January 22, immediately preceding this meeting. The Committee is debating whether to cut another 50 bp. The macro backdrop features: rising inflation (headline driven by energy and food), softening real activity, tightening financial conditions, and monoline insurer stress. This context informs the anchor comparisons but does NOT override the textual evidence.

### 3. Core Dimension Pre-Scoring (Direction-First)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants broadly see inflation as **above target and elevated**, with significant concern. Multiple speakers (Lacker, Plosser, Fisher, Evans, Pianalto, Warsh) emphasize that core PCE accelerated in H2 2007 (Plosser: "core CPI advanced at 2.6% rate in H2 '07"; core PCE "up at 2.4% in H2"). Fisher provides extensive anecdotal evidence of price pass-through (Frito-Lay, Burlington Northern rail adjustment factor at 11%, Wal-Mart budgeting 2-7% food price increases). Lacker states "PCE price index has averaged 2¾ percent" over 4 years. However, a minority (Mishkin, Geithner, Rosengren) see inflation as likely to come down given slack, and regard inflation expectations as "grounded." The center of gravity: inflation is perceived as **meaningfully above target** but not spiraling — the key tension is whether it will self-correct. Score: **above midpoint, in the 62–70 range**. The "transitory" gotcha applies: Bernanke and Kohn acknowledge transitory supply-shock components, but nearly all participants describe current inflation as elevated. Score: **66**.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential** — the language is nearly uniform. Rosengren: "Growth well below potential for the first half." Bernanke: "economy is at risk of recession." Kohn: "a couple of quarters of very slow growth." Hoenig: "grow below our potential growth rate." Multiple recession probability estimates above 50%. Evans: "Chicago Fed National Activity Index... historically... associated with a recession about 70 percent of the time." Pianalto: "deteriorated significantly." Even the more optimistic participants (Fisher, Lockhart, Warsh) see slower-than-trend growth. Bright-line rule: "below potential" language is ubiquitous. Score MUST be < 50. This is not catastrophic yet but clearly below potential with significant downside risk. Score: **33**.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Labor is described as **softening but not yet in sharp deterioration**. Bernanke: "labor market has softened by a range of measures, with unemployment jumping in December." Lacker: "Labor market conditions in the Fifth District have deteriorated." However, Stern mentions "the labor market may be a bit better than the December household and payroll surveys depicted," and UI claims are cited as a mildly encouraging sign. Fisher mentions district employment growth of 2%. Several note "workers in some occupations remain in short supply" (engineers, technical workers). The overall picture is: labor market beginning to deteriorate, unemployment rising, but not at full recession-level slack yet. Score: **40**. This is consistent with below-full-employment but not deep recession territory.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see consumption and investment as **clearly below trend and weakening**. Pianalto: "January sales are shaping up to be the worst that they have seen in the past twenty years." Bernanke: "Consumer spending has slowed." Kohn: "deceleration in consumer spending late in the fourth quarter." Lacker: "Consumer spending appears to have slowed somewhat." Mishkin: "substantial downside risk to PCE." Business investment is also softening (Lacker: "delaying capital spending projects"). Housing investment is in freefall. The one offset is export strength and some manufacturing. Net position is clearly below-trend, bordering on contractionary for some sectors. Score: **30** (contracting/sharp pullback).

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
This is one of the most prominent dimensions. Participants describe **significantly tightened financial conditions** — credit spreads widening, bank capital impaired, monoline insurer stress, tightening lending standards. Kohn: "investors and lenders... demanding much greater compensation for taking risk in nearly every sector... lending caution spreading from housing to other sectors." Warsh: "financial institutions... undercapitalized"; "depleted credit availability and significantly tighter lending standards." Pianalto: "detecting the first signals of a credit crunch"; "deals that bankers would have done for creditworthy borrowers not long ago are simply not being done today." Mishkin: "Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey had substantial tightening." Rosengren: "credit risk for financial institutions has grown." This is well into "tight/restrictive" territory, but not yet the acute "credit frozen" of September 2008. Score: **74**.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The policy round shows a **strong dovish consensus for a 50 bp cut** (alternative B). The overwhelming majority support cutting 50 bp: Pianalto, Rosengren, Kohn, Yellen, Lockhart, Mishkin (wants 75 but accepts 50), Geithner, Warsh, Stern, Kroszner, Evans all support 50 bp. Bernanke recommends 50 bp. Only Fisher advocates no change; Plosser and Hoenig support 50 but want more hawkish statement language and no further cuts signaled. Lacker could have done 25 but accepts 50. The center of gravity is: near-consensus for 50 bp cut, with only 1 formal dissent (Fisher), several "reluctant" supporters who want hawkish language, and 1-2 wanting 75 bp (Mishkin, Rosengren). This maps to **strong dovish: majority favors easing** — fitting the 15–25 range. The meeting is part of a rapid 225 bp cumulative easing cycle since August. Score: **18**.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
The vote is described as nearly complete consensus on the action (50 bp), with Fisher the sole stated dissenter. However, there is significant disagreement about: (a) whether to go 50 or 75 (Mishkin/Rosengren lean 75, Hoenig/Plosser/Lacker/Fisher prefer staying); (b) statement language — notable camps between those who want to signal more easing possible (Geithner, Mishkin, Kohn, Yellen) vs. those wanting less forward easing signal (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker, Fisher). Bernanke explicitly says "several of you will be quite unhappy with the nature of that statement." This is more than a typical one-dissent meeting — there are clear camps with different economic frameworks (Phillips curve skeptics vs. demand-deficiency camp), real reservations, and extended debate. Score: **42** (moderate: notable differences about pace/risk; 1 dissent plus 2-3 reservations from multiple directions).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability is **a dominant topic** — arguably the central organizing concern of the entire meeting. Nearly every participant addresses it at length: monoline insurer stress, bank capital impairment, credit spread widening, ABCP, SIVs, potential adverse feedback loops, SocGen incident, European bank stress, credit default swap rates on major banks and Capital One. Bernanke devotes substantial discussion to it. Warsh, Kohn, Kroszner, Rosengren, Mishkin, Pianalto, Lockhart all address it prominently. Score: **83** (dominant topic; rivals economic assessment in emphasis — this is a GFC-onset meeting).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The Committee cuts 50 bp and uses alternative B language that retains "downside risks to growth remain" and "act in a timely manner as needed." The forward signal is clearly dovish — more cuts are possible, policy is data-dependent, the Chairman says they're "prepared to respond in a timely manner." Geithner says further action is "likely to require further action." Kohn assumes another 50 over Q2 in his forecast. While several participants (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker) want to reduce the dovish forward signal, the statement as adopted retains it. Score: **15** (strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation; multiple participants forecasting continued cuts).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Multiple participants emphasize **elevated to high uncertainty**. Bernanke: "exceptionally fluid." Lockhart: "uncertainty and fear continue to loom large." Kroszner: "I don't really understand what happened" (referring to Oct-Nov market reversal). Pianalto: "I realize that my outlook could change appreciably in the weeks and months ahead." Kohn: "a lot of uncertainty around" the inflation forecast; "extraordinary volatility in markets is indicative of underlying uncertainty." Bernanke: "confluence of circumstances that is extraordinarily difficult." Evans: "downside risks are large, and the recession scenarios are quite possible." This is clearly elevated uncertainty — multiple genuine unknowns about housing bottom, credit losses, feedback loops, and inflation trajectory. Score: **70** (High: many emphasize difficulty; standard models questioned re recession probability).

### 4. Polarity Verification

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence = below potential, recession risk. Less above potential (or below) = lower score. Score 33. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = labor softening, unemployment rising. Less tight = lower score. Score 40. Correct.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence = consumer spending slowing, investment declining. Weaker = lower score. Score 30. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence = credit tightening broadly. Tighter = higher score. Score 74. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence = near-consensus for 50 bp cut, strong dovish lean. Less hawkish = lower score. Score 18. Correct.
- DISSENT_INTENSITY: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence = 1 formal dissent + significant reservations + statement language debate. Moderate-notable disagreement = score 42. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: Higher = more attention. Evidence = dominant topic. Score 83. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence = dovish forward signal, further cuts signaled as likely. Dovish forward = lower score. Score 15. Correct.
- UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence = many participants cite unprecedented uncertainty about housing, credit losses, feedback loops. Score 70. Correct.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (33) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, consistent — economy below potential and labor softening. Labor slightly less depressed because labor market lags output; this is early in deterioration. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (18) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (15): Both strongly dovish. Coherent — current 50 bp cut plus dovish forward signal. Aligns well.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (74) and FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (83): Both high. The tight financial conditions are precisely why financial stability is the dominant topic. Coherent.
- OUTPUT_GAP (33) vs. INFLATION_PRESSURE (66): These are in tension, reflecting the "stagflationary" character of this meeting — supply shocks (energy, food) are keeping inflation elevated even as demand softens. Multiple participants explicitly note this tension (Plosser: "firms face increasing price pressures" despite activity softening). This is internally coherent and accurately captured.

- **INFLATION_GOODS**: Multiple participants discuss commodity price pass-through — food, energy, Wal-Mart, Burlington Northern. 2+ speakers. Score warranted.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES**: Mentioned incidentally (service sector price trends), but not a prominent standalone discussion. N/A.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 66 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PLOSSER: "Both the core CPI and the core PCE accelerated in the second half of '07, compared with the first half. The core CPI advanced at a 2.6 percent rate in the second half of '07... the core PCE was up at a 2.4 percent rate in the second half compared with 1.9 in the first half."
- MR. LACKER: "If you look back over the past four years, the overall PCE price index has averaged 2¾ percent. On a 12-month basis, the index has been below 2 percent only four months in that span."
- MR. FISHER: "every measure of inflation has turned up... 83 percent of the items therein experienced a price upswing"; Frito-Lay raising prices 6%, Burlington Northern rail factor up 11% ("highest rate in history"), Wal-Mart budgeting 6-7% food price increases.

**Center of gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as clearly above the 2% target, driven by energy and food pass-through plus some core acceleration, with the majority worried it may not self-correct quickly. A minority (Mishkin, Geithner, Rosengren) believe slack will bring it down, but even they acknowledge the current readings are elevated.

**Minority views:** Mishkin sees inflation risks as "balanced and actually somewhat to the downside." Geithner: "inflation outlook for the reasons laid out in the Greenbook is better than it was." These dovish-on-inflation voices shift the uncertainty band upward (i.e., there's a real chance of higher scores for those who focus purely on current data). Uncertainty band ±8 reflecting this dispersion.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Growth well below potential for the first half of this year results in additional slack in labor markets."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "the economy is at a risk of recession; in any case, it is likely to grow slowly for the first half of the year."
- MR. PLOSSER: "growth in the first half of the year to be quite weak, probably around 1 percent"; expects return "toward trend, which I estimate to be about 2¾ percent."

**Center of gravity:** Near-uniform agreement that the economy is below potential, with most forecasting Q1-Q2 growth of 0–1.5% against trend of ~2.5–2.75%. Recession probability cited at 45–70%+ by multiple speakers. The economy is distinctly below potential but has not yet entered the catastrophic contraction of late 2008/2009.

**Minority views:** Fisher is relatively less pessimistic ("none of the 30 CEOs... see the economy trending into negative territory"), and Warsh says "trends in the real economy may be a bit more positive coming into the first quarter from the fourth quarter than the Greenbook projections." These narrow the lower tail slightly, supporting a score of 33 rather than 25.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "The labor market has softened by a range of measures, with unemployment jumping in December. However, workers in some occupations remain in short supply."
- MR. LACKER: "Labor market conditions in the Fifth District have deteriorated in the last month. In a noticeable shift from previous surveys, numerous contacts told us they had begun to trim payrolls."
- MR. STERN: "the labor market may be a bit better than the December household and payroll surveys depicted, given the low level of initial claims."

**Center of gravity:** Labor is clearly softening — unemployment rose, payroll trimming has begun — but not yet at deep recession levels. Some pockets of tightness remain (engineers, Northern Virginia). This is consistent with a labor market beginning to deteriorate, sitting below full employment but not yet in substantial slack territory. Score of 40 places it in "below full employment; slack remains; unemployment elevated" range — appropriate for early-deterioration phase.

**Minority views:** Warsh and Stern are slightly more optimistic about near-term labor conditions, citing claims data.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PIANALTO: "The CFO of one of the nation's major department store chains told me last week that her company's January sales are shaping up to be the worst that they have seen in the past twenty years."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "Consumer spending has slowed. I think there's little doubt about that at this point... households... denied access to home equity... greater uncertainty in the economy and the labor market... natural tendency would be to be much more conservative in their spending."
- MR. LACKER: "Consumer spending appears to have slowed somewhat at the end of last year... carrying less momentum into this quarter... I am a bit less hopeful about business investment, particularly structures."

**Center of gravity:** Consumer spending is contracting or at best deeply below trend. Retail contacts universally report weakness; business investment also softening (though not collapsing). Export strength provides a partial offset. Housing investment is in freefall. Weighted net = clearly in the "contracting/sharp pullback" territory at 30, with some sectors (exports, energy, agriculture) providing marginal support.

**Minority views:** Fisher notes MasterCard December retail sales ex-auto, ex-gas up 5%; UPS up 2% YoY — slow but not negative, supporting a score slightly above the very bottom of the range.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 74 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KOHN: "investors and lenders seem increasingly concerned about the broader economic weakness... demanding much greater compensation for taking risk in nearly every sector... the spreading out from the housing sector of lending caution to other sectors... A substantial portion of banks reported tightening terms and standards for C&I loans... most banks—like 80 percent—tightened up on commercial real estate credit."
- MS. PIANALTO: "I'm now detecting the first signals of a credit crunch. Bankers in my District tell me that they're finding it much more difficult to issue debt... Deals that bankers would have done for creditworthy borrowers not long ago are simply not being done today."
- MR. WARSH: "financial institutions... are undercapitalized, even with the recent capital infusions... depleted credit availability and significantly tighter lending standards."

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions are significantly tighter than normal, with credit standards tightening across C&I, consumer, and commercial real estate. Monoline stress adds systemic risk. Spreads have widened substantially. This is firmly in "tight/restrictive; credit hard to obtain; stress emerging" territory. Not yet the acute freeze of September 2008 — some improvement in short-term funding noted (TAF effective) — but clearly elevated. Score 74.

**Minority views:** Geithner notes "some sign that the process of repair is starting... substantial early equity raising by major firms; pretty substantial improvement in market functioning; easing of liquidity pressure." This suggests conditions are tighter than neutral but not at crisis extremes, supporting 74 rather than 80+.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 18 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Overwhelming majority support Alternative B: 50 bp cut (Pianalto, Lockhart, Rosengren, Kohn, Yellen, Evans, Stern, Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Geithner, Bernanke). Plosser and Hoenig support 50 but want less dovish statement. Lacker prefers 25 but accepts 50. Fisher alone advocates no change.
- MR. MISHKIN: "I would actually advocate a 75 basis point cut at this meeting because I do think there is a need to take out insurance."
- VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER: "we're going to have to get them lower even with another 50 basis points tomorrow."
- MR. FISHER (sole dissenter): "I truly believe we have it right at 3½ percent right now."

**Center of gravity:** Near-consensus for 50 bp cut (strong dovish action), with 1 formal dissent on the dovish side from Fisher (who wanted to hold) and 1-2 pushing for 75 bp. This is firmly in the "strong dovish: majority favors easing or maintaining max accommodation" range (11-25). The 225 bp cumulative easing since August and the forward signal of further cuts if needed confirm the strong dovish posture. Score: 18.

**Minority views:** Fisher's no-change preference and Plosser/Hoenig/Lacker's desire for less accommodation signal slight rightward pull, but they still accept 50 bp. The uncertainty band ±5 reflects this dispersion.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FISHER advocates no change — explicit dissent.
- MR. PLOSSER: "we are on the verge of overshooting, and I worry about the broad range of consequences for our credibility." Pushes for more hawkish statement language, disagreeing with majority on communications.
- MR. MISHKIN: would prefer 75 bp but accepts 50; views inflation risks as "balanced and somewhat to the downside" — disagreeing with the center-of-gravity view on inflation.
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "I now understand that several of you will be quite unhappy with the nature of that statement."

**Center of gravity:** One formal dissent (Fisher), 2-3 strong reservations (Plosser, Hoenig, Lacker) from the hawkish end, and 1-2 preferences for more easing (Mishkin, Rosengren). The statement language produced real debate — two camps on how dovish the forward guidance should be. This is "moderate differences: notable differences about pace/risk; 1 dissent or 2-3 reservations" territory. Score 42 (upper end of 31-45 range given clear multi-directional tensions).

**Minority views:** Despite the disagreement, there is actually near-consensus on the 50 bp action itself; the dissent is mainly about degree and communications, which prevents the score from reaching 55+.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 83 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. LOCKHART: "The principal risk to the forecast in my view is the fragility of the financial markets. Uncertainty and fear continue to loom large." Discusses monoline insurer solvency, illiquid fixed-income markets, mark-to-market accounting problems.
- MR. WARSH: Devotes entire statement to financial sector analysis: undercapitalized financial institutions, "rolling capital calls," European bank stress, income statement vs. balance sheet issues, repair process "very fragile and quite incomplete."
- MR. ROSENGREN: Capital One CDS from <100 to >400 bp; monoline guarantor risk; bank balance sheet expansion.

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability is arguably the central organizing concern of this meeting. The economic assessment go-around is deeply intertwined with financial stability analysis. A special dedicated presentation on "policy issues raised by financial crisis" was given on Day 2. Multiple participants devote half or more of their remarks to financial conditions. This is "dominant topic; rivals economic assessment in emphasis" — score 83. Just below 85-95 range because it has not yet reached the acute panic-level emphasis of Sept 2008.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 15 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Statement (alternative B) adopted: "downside risks to growth remain" and "will act in a timely manner as needed to address those risks." — strong easing bias signal retained.
- VICE CHAIRMAN GEITHNER: "getting that strategy in place is likely to require further action. It's very hard to know when or how much."
- MR. KOHN: assumes further rate cuts in Q2 in his own forecast; "We just need to concentrate on the forecast... heading where we're heading, and maybe even moving a little lower if circumstances permit."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "I will not be surprised if we find further action is indeed needed."

**Center of gravity:** The forward signal is explicitly dovish — statement retains "downside risks remain" language with "timely action" phrasing. Multiple participants assume further cuts in their forecasts. Only Plosser/Hoenig/Fisher push back, and they lose the statement language debate. Score 15 = "strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation ahead."

**Minority views:** Plosser, Hoenig, and Lacker want to remove the forward easing signal — if their preferred language had prevailed, score would be ~30-35. The ±7 band reflects this.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE: "the situation is exceptionally fluid and... the financial risks, in particular... can be very hard to predict. There are a lot of interactions between the financial markets and the real economy that are potentially destabilizing... a confluence of circumstances that is extraordinarily difficult."
- MR. KROSZNER: "I don't really understand what happened" (re: October-November reversal of financial improvements); "I don't want to take too much comfort from what has happened so far... since I don't really understand what happened there."

**Center of gravity:** Participants across the spectrum express genuine uncertainty about recession probability, housing bottom timing, credit loss magnitude, feedback loop severity, and inflation trajectory. Standard models are explicitly questioned ("Our models have never been successful at assessing turning points" — Kroszner; "regime-switch models... suggest probability of recession at least 50 percent or more" but with wide bands). This is "High: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range; standard models questioned" = 61-75 range. Score 70.

**Minority views:** Some participants (Fisher, Lockhart) express more conviction in their views (Fisher is relatively confident recession is not imminent from CEO contacts). This pulls slightly against a higher score.

Score: 62 (elevated concern about equity market stress as a symptom of broader financial fragility, but not a primary equity crash narrative per se). Confidence is Moderate because equity stress is more of a secondary indicator in this meeting.
