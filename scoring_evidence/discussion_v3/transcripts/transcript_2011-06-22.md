# FOMC Transcript Scoring — June 21–22, 2011

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the June 21–22, 2011 FOMC meeting. Individual participants are identified by name (MR. ROSENGREN., MS. YELLEN., MR. PLOSSER., etc.). The document contains:
- A two-day meeting structure
- Day 1 (June 21): Procedural items (balance sheet/exit strategy), financial stability discussion, economic go-round
- Day 2 (June 22): Bernanke's summary, policy go-round
- Speakers are clearly attributed. President Hoenig's remarks are notably absent (his text was stripped/redacted in this decomposed file — the Chairman says "Thank you, President Hoenig" twice with no speech text appearing), suggesting those portions were excluded or omitted.

The scoring population is: Rosengren, Lockhart, Plosser, Pianalto, Bullard, Lacker, Williams, Vice Chairman Dudley, Kocherlakota, Evans, Fisher, Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin, Bernanke (Chairman). Hoenig was a voting member in 2011 but his text does not appear in the excerpted document.

---

### 2. Era Context
June 2011: Post-GFC recovery. QE2 ($600B Treasury purchases) was completing. Fed funds rate at 0–0.25%. Economy had hit a "soft patch" in spring 2011 — weak jobs reports (May payrolls disappointing), GDP revised down, supply chain disruptions from Japan earthquake. Headline inflation elevated from oil/commodity prices. Core inflation rising from very low 2010 levels but still modest. Unemployment ~9.1%. Greece sovereign debt crisis escalating. U.S. debt ceiling debate underway.

I will score from the text alone, treating this backdrop as context for sanity checks, not as overriding evidence.

---

### 3. Core Dimension Pre-Scoring (Direction First)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
The text shows a divided but consistent picture: headline PCE inflation rose over 1 percentage point year-over-year; core PCE running ~2.3% annualized over 3 months (Lacker). Most participants see inflation as having recently risen meaningfully above 2010 lows but expect it to moderate. Plosser projects ~2¼% in 2012 and considers the upward trend "persistent"; Pianalto treats the increase as "an upward shift in the path of inflation"; Kocherlakota sees "sharp acceleration" in core PCE. Evans/Williams/Yellen see the rise as partly transitory (autos, commodities) and expect inflation to settle below 2%. Bernanke sees inflation as having risen, with some comfort from TIPS moderation. The center of gravity: inflation has risen above the very low 2010 levels and is approaching or modestly above the 2% level, with dispersion between hawks who see it as persistent and doves who see it as transitory.

Direction: Participants perceive inflation as slightly-to-modestly above target currently, with majority expecting it to decline back below 2% over forecast horizon. The current reading is elevated relative to 2010 but not deeply concerning for most.
Score will be in the 55–65 range — somewhat elevated, not at extreme.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
This is unambiguous: universal "below potential" language. Rosengren: "significant slack in the economy." Bernanke: "output growth has been below what we think to be potential output growth." Unemployment remains at 9.1%. Multiple references to "significant slack," "substantial slack," "far from full employment." Lacker: "level of employment is almost 5 percent below its cyclical peak." Tarullo: "slogging through mud." The economy has grown "only slightly above potential" but with recent slowdown it is clearly running below potential. The gap is shrinking slowly but is still substantial.

Direction: Participants see economy as below potential, with substantial slack remaining. This maps to 31–40 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Unemployment at 9.1% — elevated, though down from 9.8% in November 2010. Rosengren: "unemployment rate to remain above 8 percent at the end of 2012." Kocherlakota: "labor markets are relatively slack...workers are widely available with muted wage pressures." Evans: "no robust hiring is planned." Pianalto: "With unemployment still very high, growth in labor costs is likely to remain low." Multiple references to labor market weakness.

Direction: Labor market clearly below full employment; elevated unemployment, slack remains. Maps to 31–40 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Mixed picture. Consumer spending recently disappointed: higher gas prices ate into discretionary spending, confidence fell, retail sales weak. Rosengren: putting off hiring/investments, "not seeing significant increases in demand." Yellen: "abnormally large" response of consumer spending to energy prices. However, business investment and equipment/software investment remained relatively solid. Exports strong. Overall consumption is below trend, dampened by energy prices and housing headwinds, though not collapsing.

Direction: Below trend; sluggish/cautious consumers. Range 31–43.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Participants describe financial conditions as having "weakened" during the intermeeting period. Bernanke's summary: "Financial conditions weakened during the intermeeting period, volatility increased. Equities, bond yields, and bond spreads all reacted." Williams: "lower Treasury yields, faltering stock market, rise in high-yield bond spreads." Duke: "Financing conditions in the dealer space tightened noticeably in recent weeks." However, for commercial banking: "Liquidity remains ample." Banks have excess cash, competition is "ferocious" for C&I lending. The overall financial conditions are mildly tighter than neutral due to the intermeeting developments, but not severely restrictive.

Direction: Conditions tightened modestly from accommodative levels; somewhat loose to neutral trending slightly tighter. Range ~40–50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Policy go-round is nearly unanimous for Alternative B (hold rates, maintain "extended period" language, end QE2 reinvestment as planned). Participants who support B: Lockhart, Williams, Kocherlakota (with caveat), Lacker, Yellen, Duke, Tarullo, Raskin, Vice Chairman Dudley, Fisher, Evans (all support B).

Minority hawkish lean: Plosser (explicitly wants to begin removing accommodation, signals action before year-end, says "may need to take action before end of year"), Kocherlakota (says he'll likely favor Alternative C later in year). Pianalto (support B but sees upside inflation risk).

Minority dovish lean: Rosengren and Williams explicitly worry about needing more accommodation if economy stalls; Evans open to alternative A (more dovish extended-period commitment); Yellen says risks shifted to downside; Raskin supports B "out of abundance of hope."

The center of gravity: broadly dovish-to-neutral hold, with the QE2 program completing and no rate hike on the table. The dominant position is to maintain maximum accommodation at 0–0.25% with "extended period" language intact. However, this is NOT a meeting where additional easing is proposed — just holding steady. Given the rubric anchor that "QE era 2010-12 = 15-25," and that no new QE is being discussed, the lean is slightly less dovish than peak-QE meetings. But with strong dovish undertones and no hawkish action proposed.

Direction: Lean dovish to balanced; majority favoring hold with "extended period" and no new tightening. A few members lean toward eventual tightening signals. Range 30–42.

**Polarity check**: Higher HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN = more hawkish. Participants are mostly dovish/hold. Score should be below 50. Correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**
The policy vote will be Alternative B with near unanimity. Hoenig's comments are absent from transcript text, but historically he was dissenting in 2011. However, within the participant discussion, the range of views is notable: Plosser explicitly signals he wants to tighten soon; Rosengren/Williams want to be prepared to ease more; Evans/Yellen support B but with dovish notes. The debate on statement language is active but collegial. This is a meeting with notable but not extreme disagreement — one potential formal dissent, multiple expressed reservations in different directions (some hawkish, some dovish).

Direction: Moderate disagreement; distinct views about pace but broad agreement on current action (Alternative B). Range 35–50.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
This meeting had an unusually prominent financial stability discussion — a dedicated session was added at the request of the Conference of Presidents. Rosengren gave detailed remarks on money market mutual fund risks. Vice Chairman Dudley discussed Europe and U.S. debt ceiling. Fisher, Kocherlakota, Tarullo all contributed. This is above typical for 2011. However, it didn't dominate the meeting to the exclusion of economic discussion.

Direction: Prominent topic, most participants discussed at length but didn't fully dominate. Range 55–68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
The Committee is maintaining "extended period" language for near-zero rates. Kocherlakota's amendment to add timing language ("3-6 months") was rejected by majority. Yellen explicitly interpreted "extended period" as meaning "very low probability of tightening in the next few months." Vice Chairman Dudley explicitly argues against any impression of "early exit." Rosengren/Williams mention contingency planning for more accommodation if needed. The forward guidance is firmly on the easing/holding side.

Direction: Mild easing bias to "low for long" emphasis. Extended-period language maintained. Range 20–35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Pianalto: "uncertainty surrounding this assessment is extraordinarily high." Yellen: "two words that crop up continually are 'uncertainty' and 'caution.'" Lockhart: "greater uncertainty around their cautious optimism." Williams: "worrisome" data, "downward modal revision...and downside risk assessment is a truly sobering development." Bernanke: "soft patch" with genuine uncertainty about temporary vs. lasting. Multiple participants note debt ceiling, Europe, Japan, China as tail risks. Multiple participants explicitly say uncertainty is "higher than at the April meeting." This is genuinely elevated uncertainty — not just rhetorical hedging.

Direction: Elevated uncertainty, multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty; "particularly uncertain." Range 52–65.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 60 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. LACKER: "Over the past three months, core PCE inflation has been running at an annual rate of about 2.3 percent...This looks like a fairly persistent step-up."
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "Over the past six months, annualized core PCE inflation was 1½ percent; this is a sharp acceleration...Such a rapid rate of increase in six-month PCE core inflation has occurred but twice in the past 15 years."
- MS. YELLEN: "core PCE inflation in the three months ending in April...were 1.9 percent and 2.5 percent, respectively...a large fraction of this increase was attributable to surging commodity and food prices and Japanese supply disruptions."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see headline and core inflation as having risen notably from 2010 lows to modestly above the 2% target range, with a real division between those who view this as persistent (hawkish minority) and those expecting transitory factors to reverse (moderate majority). The current reading is clearly elevated relative to prior period but expected by the majority to return below 2%.

**Minority views:** Plosser projects 2¼% in 2012 and sees a risk of "missing emerging signs that medium-term inflation is accelerating," pulling the band higher (+8). Evans/Williams expect core and total PCE at ~1½% in 2012–13, pulling lower (-8).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Since the beginning of the recovery, we have only grown slightly above potential, and our forecasts have been too optimistic...What accounts for the inability of this recovery to make significant headway in reducing the significant slack in the economy?"
- MR. LACKER: "Real GDP growth has averaged just 2¾ percent over the last two years, and the level of employment is almost 5 percent below its cyclical peak."
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "output growth has been below what we think to be potential output growth...Overall, downside risks to growth have increased as the slow pace of the recovery brings the economy near stall speed."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants agree the economy is running below potential with a persistent output gap; the dispute is only about whether the gap is structural/permanent (Lacker's "neoclassical" view) or cyclical (majority view). Score reflects consistent "slack remains, below potential" language across the full table.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota suggests potential output may be lower than staff estimates, which would imply a smaller gap — but even on his view, current activity is at/below potential, not above.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KOCHERLAKOTA: "respondents in our District...indicate...that labor markets are relatively slack...workers are widely available with muted wage pressures...unemployment in our District is well under 7 percent."
- MS. PIANALTO: "With unemployment still very high, growth in labor costs is likely to remain low."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "expecting the unemployment rate to remain above 8 percent at the end of 2012."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal agreement that labor markets remain substantially below full employment with unemployment at 9.1%, wage growth subdued, and hiring plans cautious. This is firmly in the 31–40 "below full employment, slack remains" range.

**Minority views:** Fisher is concerned about the trend of businesses investing in productivity rather than hiring, suggesting labor market improvement is being structurally impeded — directionally consistent with the low-tightness reading.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. YELLEN: "Higher food and gas prices have taken an obvious toll on cash-strapped consumers...the response of consumer spending to the increase in higher food and energy prices may have been abnormally large in recent months."
- MR. LOCKHART: "few of our contacts cited major changes to their plans for the remainder of the year...higher gasoline prices that reduced shopping trips or ate into consumer discretionary spending."
- MR. EVANS: "revisions to income, weak jobs and claims numbers in May, and the softness in second-quarter consumer spending throw cold water on my previously more optimistic forecast."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is below trend, dampened by energy prices, housing wealth effects, and labor market caution. Business investment in equipment/software provides partial offset. Net position is below trend but not collapsing — more "sluggish" than "contracting."

**Minority views:** Bullard and Lockhart note some sectors (energy, agriculture, high-tech, tourism) remain strong; Williams notes Silicon Valley boom. Business investment mentioned positively by several as a partial offset, which constrains the score from moving much lower.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 44 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (summary): "Financial conditions weakened during the intermeeting period, and volatility increased. Equities, bond yields, and bond spreads all reacted to the disappointing economic news."
- MR. WILLIAMS: "lower Treasury yields, the faltering stock market, and the rise in high-yield bond spreads since our last meeting."
- MS. DUKE: "Financing conditions in the dealer space tightened noticeably in recent weeks...all banks characterize the competition, in C&I lending especially, as irrational."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions tightened modestly from accommodative levels during the intermeeting period (equity weakness, spread widening), but commercial banking liquidity remains ample and C&I lending competition is fierce. Net position is slightly tighter than neutral but not at a restrictive level.

**Minority views:** Dudley suggests monetary policy may be less stimulative than believed (different point but suggests loose conditions may have less impact). Duke notes substantial populations of borrowers who cannot access lower rates, suggesting impaired transmission rather than tight overall conditions per se.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 36 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. LOCKHART: "I support alternative B...I think maintaining the current extent of accommodation will help mitigate some of the downside risks to the outlook."
- MR. WILLIAMS: "I favor alternative B...the current very accommodative stance of monetary policy remains entirely appropriate. Indeed, I would like to echo President Rosengren's comments: I worry that 2011 could become a replay of 2010."
- MR. PLOSSER: "Given inflation developments and the upside risk I see to inflation forecasts, I think we may very well need to take some action to withdraw some accommodation sooner than what's priced into the funds futures market...I believe we may need to take action before the end of the year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Overwhelming consensus (~11 of ~15+ visible participants) supports Alternative B — maintaining accommodation at 0–0.25% with "extended period" language and completing QE2 without extension. A small minority (Plosser explicitly, Kocherlakota conditionally) lean toward near-term tightening signals. A few members (Rosengren, Williams, Evans) advocate contingency planning for more accommodation. The center sits firmly in dovish-hold territory.

**Minority views:** Plosser's explicit call for rate action before year-end (+8 towards hawks). Rosengren/Williams readiness for more easing if needed (-8 towards doves). Uncertainty band reflects this spread.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 42 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Multiple participants support B while noting reservations: Kocherlakota ("I do believe that if the economy evolves according to the forecast in the Tealbook, it is likely that I will favor an alternative closer to C later this year").
- Plosser vs. Rosengren/Williams represent opposing reservations about the current stance.
- Fisher: "I'm getting increasingly close to alternative C, but I'm going to suggest at this meeting that we should play it up the middle."
- Multiple substantive language debates about "loss of momentum" sentence, downside risks language, etc.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on Alternative B action, but with notable reservations from both directions (hawkish: Plosser, conditional Kocherlakota; dovish: Rosengren, Williams contingency planning). The debate on statement language was active. Hoenig's text is absent but was historically a dissenter. This qualifies as moderate disagreement with 1–2 formal dissents possible plus several expressed reservations.

**Minority views:** If Hoenig dissented (consistent with his 2011 pattern), that would push intensity up. The band reflects this uncertainty.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN BERNANKE (opening financial stability session): "The Conference of Presidents requested that we have an opportunity for some discussion on this area."
- MR. ROSENGREN: Extended remarks on money market mutual fund risks, Dexia exposures, systemic risk structure.
- VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: Detailed discussion of European sovereign debt and U.S. debt ceiling risks.
- MR. FISHER, MR. KOCHERLAKOTA, MR. TARULLO: All contributed to financial stability discussion; shadow banking, Farm Credit System, TBTF also mentioned.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** An unusually prominent financial stability discussion was added as a dedicated agenda item. Multiple participants engaged meaningfully with specific risks (money market funds, Europe, debt ceiling). This clearly exceeds typical 2011 baseline but does not overwhelm economic discussion.

**Minority views:** The discussion was structured as an add-on item rather than the central focus — economic go-round and policy discussion retained their prominence. This keeps the score from reaching 65+.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 25 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. YELLEN: "When we include 'extended period' language in the statement, we are saying the FOMC sees very low probability that it will need to tighten in three to six months...The funds rate target could well...stay exceptionally low for a very long time."
- VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY: "It is really important that people don't think that spending so much time on this contingency plan means that exit necessarily lies close at hand."
- MR. ROSENGREN: "Should the economy start growing less than potential with the unemployment rate rising and the inflation rate declining, we would need to consider additional asset purchases."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward guidance firmly commits to extended near-zero rates ("extended period" language retained, Kocherlakota's proposal for timing signals rejected). The tone is "low for long" with several participants explicitly flagging readiness for more accommodation. This corresponds to the 16–25 "mild easing bias, low for long emphasis" range.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Kocherlakota's conditional signals of eventual tightening push the band upward (+7).

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PIANALTO: "The uncertainty surrounding this assessment is extraordinarily high. In my view, the level of uncertainty around my outlook is higher than it was at the April meeting."
- MS. YELLEN: "The two words that crop up continually are 'uncertainty' and 'caution'...survey measures of business confidence have declined, and a growing, albeit ill-defined, sense of malaise has surfaced."
- MR. LOCKHART: "Several directors mentioned greater uncertainty around their cautious optimism for the second half...shock risk has risen dramatically with the debt ceiling, Greece, and Europe."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple participants explicitly identify uncertainty as elevated and higher than at the prior meeting, citing domestic (debt ceiling), international (Greece/Europe, Japan, China), and structural (potential output debate) sources. This is genuine elevated uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views:** Bullard and Lacker take more confident positions that the soft patch is temporary, consistent with the "look through" approach. This constrains the score from moving higher.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**
Lockhart, Plosser, Williams, Fisher all discuss goods price pass-through, import prices from China, commodity prices in goods. Plosser notes half of firms passed on cost increases. Fisher discusses 5–8% planned price increases in staples. Score: 62 ±8 — goods prices elevated with commodity and import pass-through concerns.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**
Tarullo and Yellen discuss persistent inflation in services, particularly rents/shelter. Rosengren notes OER increases related to housing market dysfunction. Score: 57 ±8 — services inflation modestly elevated, primarily driven by shelter/rent dynamics.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**
Yellen: "stable longer-term inflation expectations, minimal cost pressure from wages." Bernanke: "TIPS spreads have come down to something close to the middle of their historical range...the mini inflation scare that we had has moderated." Kocherlakota (Plosser's camp) worry about unanchoring risk. Williams: "financial markets are not expecting an outbreak of inflation." Center: expectations reasonably anchored, modestly below 2% target. Score: 47 ±8 — near anchored, slight concern about upside risks from some participants.

**CREDIT_STRESS**
Duke's detailed discussion of commercial banking: "competition for C&I lending is ferocious," "banks do not yet have any stomach for easing credit standards," but also "banks are seeing slow but steady improvement in credit quality." Spreads widened somewhat (Williams notes high-yield spread widening). Score: 42 ±8 — modest stress from market spread widening; bank credit conditions moderately functional.

**EQUITY_STRESS**
Williams mentions "faltering stock market" and lower stock prices during intermeeting period. Fisher warns about potential 20% market selloff as a risk. Score: 48 ±10 — some concern about market weakness but not panic; treated as context rather than crisis signal. N/A would be too strict given multiple references.

**BANKING_STRESS**
Duke's discussion is detailed but describes gradual improvement in credit quality and no acute stress. Rosengren's financial stability focus is on money market funds, not banking per se. Tarullo discusses TBTF and regulatory issues but not acute banking stress. Score: 40 ±10 — some residual concern (repurchase reserves, CRE exposures) but banks not under acute stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS**
Debt ceiling discussed as a risk (Dudley calls it "very risky course"), but no actual Treasury market dysfunction at present. Williams notes lower Treasury yields (a flight-to-quality signal, not dysfunction). Score: N/A — participants discuss debt ceiling as a risk scenario, not current Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**
No significant discussion of dollar levels or dollar stress as a focal issue for participants. N/A.

**PRODUCTIVITY**
Fisher and Evans note businesses are investing in productivity in lieu of hiring. Lacker discusses regulatory/tax effects on potential output (structural). Not a sustained 2+ participant discussion of productivity growth levels per se. N/A.
