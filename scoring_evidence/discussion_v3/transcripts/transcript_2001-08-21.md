# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2001-08-21 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers the economic go-around and the policy discussion from the August 21, 2001 FOMC meeting. Speakers include: Broaddus, McTeer, Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Stern, Guynn, Santomero, Jordan, McDonough, Ferguson, Poole, Gramlich, Meyer, Kelley, Greenspan, Hoenig. This is a very well-attended meeting with extensive discussion.

### Era Context
August 2001 -- the economy is in the midst of the 2001 recession (though NBER dating was not yet official). The dot-com bust and capital spending collapse are dragging the economy. The Fed had already cut the funds rate by 275bp since January 2001. International weakness (Japan, Argentina, Asia) was intensifying. Housing and consumer spending were holding up as the main pillars of strength.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target and declining. Greenspan: "I find that prospect exceptionally unlikely [a recovery in profits]... there is no pricing power in markets across the nation... I would suspect that when this whole episode is over we will have a lower rate of inflation." Parry: "We are looking for inflation in the core PCE price index of just below 2 percent both this year and next" and notes disappointment that the slowdown may not reduce inflation much. Hoenig: "inflationary pressures continue to subside in our area." Guynn notes widespread discounting. McTeer notes the "slightly improved outlook for inflation." Multiple participants see inflation as well-contained and subdued, with discounting widespread. Direction: below midpoint. No one is expressing upward inflation alarm. Score range: 35-40. The language is "subdued," "no pricing power," "discounting" -- suggesting modest below-target, not alarming deflation risk.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly operating below potential. Multiple participants describe GDP growth well below trend, manufacturing in deep contraction, spillover to other sectors. Greenspan: "economic conditions are continuously eroding." Meyer: growth rates "marked down" and economy has persistent weakness. McTeer: "The slowdown is now a year old and shows few signs of coming to an end." Parry: "best guess is for growth of around 1 percent in the current quarter." Minehan notes growth "at more than 1 percentage point below potential" for multiple quarters. However, the economy has not collapsed -- consumer spending and housing are holding up. This is below potential but not catastrophically so. The output gap is negative but moderate. Score range: 30-35. Language suggests "below potential" clearly, with "sluggish," "weak," "anemic" growth but not freefall.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor markets are clearly easing. Moskow: "District employers are still reporting that it's less difficult to hire and retain workers... wage pressures have subsided further. Total payroll employment... has been below year-ago levels for two months." Minehan: "labor market tightness has eased... much less upward pressure on compensation." McDonough: "employment in New York City's critical financial services sector has now declined for four consecutive months." However, Greenspan notes "unemployment still relatively low," Poole's Wal-Mart contact says labor is still tight for sales associates, Jordan reports unfilled positions in some sectors. The center of gravity is that labor markets have loosened considerably from 1999-2000 tightness but unemployment is still not high. Score range: 35-40. Easing but not yet severely slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: This is a split picture. Consumer spending is holding up reasonably well (multiple participants note this) but business investment is in deep contraction. Greenspan: "household demand more generally that clearly is the source of strength holding this economy together." But on investment: "a capital spending bubble that has now collapsed" (Stern), "the outlook for capital spending is poor in a large number of industries" (Moskow), "our aggressive policy easing moves have had very little impact on business investment spending" (Guynn). The NET position: consumer spending is at/near trend, but investment is deeply below trend. Since investment collapse is the dominant discussion theme and the net picture is clearly below trend, score should reflect the combined weakness. Score range: 28-33. Investment deeply weak, consumption near normal -- net is clearly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants discuss tightening financial conditions. Minehan: "the availability of bank credit has become a concern... Big firms with lower credit ratings are reportedly shut out of credit markets." McDonough: "Bankers report... rising delinquency rates, and tighter credit standards." Meyer's FRB/US simulation: "on net financial conditions have actually become more restrictive rather than more stimulative" -- long rates haven't fallen, equity prices lower, dollar higher. Poole notes long rates haven't followed short rates down. However, housing finance is readily available, mortgage refinancing is active. Center of gravity: somewhat tight, with credit conditions tightening especially for business borrowers, but residential lending still flowing. Score: 57-62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: There is near-consensus support for a 25bp cut. Greenspan proposes 25bp with risks tilted to weakness; every participant who speaks in the policy round supports this. Parry calls it "a close call" and notes arguments for holding. Guynn and Jordan express reservations about over-easing but support the cut. Broaddus supports but wants forward language about approaching end of easing cycle. Ferguson makes an extensive dovish case. Gramlich calls for a "monetary booster shot." The center of gravity is clearly dovish -- cutting rates with risks tilted to weakness -- but the size of move (25bp not 50bp) and several participants expressing concern about lags and needing to reverse course soon moderates the dovishness. Score: 30-35. Moderate dovish: majority favors easing, but limited size and growing concern about exit strategy.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. All participants support the 25bp cut. However, there are notable differences in emphasis. Guynn makes a case for patience and expresses concern about over-stimulating housing/sub-prime. Jordan questions the efficacy of further cuts. Broaddus wants language signaling end of easing. Parry notes arguments for holding. Multiple participants (Stern, Minehan, Guynn, Broaddus) express concern about lags and exit strategy. This is more than pro forma agreement -- there are genuine philosophical differences about whether to continue easing, even though all end up supporting the action. Score: 30-35. Broad agreement with notable emphasis differences and reservations.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: There is meaningful discussion of financial conditions and stability concerns. Guynn discusses home equity lending, sub-prime lending to marginal borrowers, and potential housing overvaluation. Moskow mentions concern about overbuilding in Chicago. Minehan discusses credit market difficulties. McDonough provides extended discussion of Argentine crisis and potential for chaotic financial markets. Meyer discusses financial conditions simulations extensively. Poole analyzes the yield curve. Multiple participants address financial conditions as part of their transmission mechanism analysis. Score: 40-48. Moderate discussion -- several participants address it meaningfully, particularly around credit conditions, housing, and international financial risks.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mildly dovish with growing ambiguity. Greenspan: "we have not as yet completed our easing process" but also "at some point we are going to have to stop our easing process." The statement removes notion of intermeeting moves. Broaddus pushes for language that "easing cycle may now be approaching an end." Multiple participants express concern about needing to reverse course. The signal is: more easing may come, but we're approaching the end, and risks are toward weakness. Score: 28-33. Easing lean with data dependence, but signal that end of easing is approaching.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is elevated. Greenspan: "the outlook remains subject to a good deal of uncertainty" and "our forecasts at this stage essentially reflect hope." McTeer: "the risks to the forecast seem mostly to be on the downside." McDonough: "there is certainly a very great deal of uncertainty about this short-term growth outlook." Gramlich: "the forecast recovery has been pushed back and pushed back again." Meyer discusses tension between data and forecast. Multiple participants emphasize that the recovery keeps being pushed back. However, this is not "uncharted territory" language -- it's elevated uncertainty about timing and strength of recovery. Score: 55-62.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (32) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (37): Both below 50, consistent with recession/slowdown. Labor slightly higher because unemployment is "still relatively low" even as it rises -- labor is lagging the output gap decline. This makes sense.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Both dovish, aligned. The lean is to ease, and forward signal is continued easing bias with growing caution. Coherent.

### Speaker Count for Center of Gravity
- Support 25bp cut: All speakers (unanimous)
- Express concern about over-easing/exit strategy: Broaddus, Stern, Minehan, Guynn, Jordan, Parry, Meyer, Kelley (~8)
- Emphasize downside risks: Ferguson, Gramlich, McTeer, McDonough, Greenspan (~5)
- Most optimistic: Jordan (selective positive anecdotes)
- Most cautious on further easing: Guynn (explicit case for patience), Jordan (prefers dry powder)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "there is no pricing power in markets across the nation... I would suspect that when this whole episode is over we will have a lower rate of inflation, even in the PCE consumption deflator"
- HOENIG: "inflationary pressures continue to subside in our area. Only one of the employers we contacted said that it had granted above-normal wage increases recently"
- GUYNN: "All the anecdotal reports on retail sales, auto sales, tourism, hotel occupancy, cruise bookings, and airline traffic indicate that deep discounting is being used to bolster sales"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants perceive inflation as contained and declining, with widespread discounting and no pricing power, placing pressures somewhat below the 2% target but without alarm about deflation.

**Minority views**: McTeer raises the possibility that "supply may be cut back faster than demand," which could reverse disinflationary dynamics. Moskow notes "for the long term, I remain somewhat concerned about inflation." These inform the upward uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MCTEER: "The slowdown is now a year old and shows few signs of coming to an end"
- PARRY: "the recent data taken together show few, if any, signs of a pickup in activity. Our best guess is for growth of around 1 percent in the current quarter"
- GREENSPAN: "economic conditions are continuously eroding... We do not yet see any evidence of the beginnings of a turnaround"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is clearly operating below potential with manufacturing in deep contraction, capital spending collapsing, and overall growth around 1% -- well below trend. Consumer spending and housing provide a floor, preventing deeper contraction.

**Minority views**: Jordan reports several positive anecdotes from his district, including firms up 30% YoY. Broaddus sees "modest signs of improvement." These are minority and prevent the score from going lower.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MOSKOW: "District employers are still reporting that it's less difficult to hire and retain workers in most areas and that wage pressures have subsided further"
- MINEHAN: "labor market tightness has eased. This seems to be helping area firms keep costs in check, as there is much less upward pressure on compensation"
- POOLE: Wal-Mart contact "finds labor market conditions still tight for sales associates... just as difficult as it was a year ago to hire people"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets have loosened substantially from the very tight conditions of 1999-2000, with rising layoffs, declining payrolls, and easing wage pressures, but unemployment remains historically low and pockets of tightness persist in low-wage and specialized occupations.

**Minority views**: Poole's Wal-Mart anecdote and Jordan's reports of unfilled positions in specialized trades suggest tightness lingers at the lower end and in specific sectors, supporting the upward end of the uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 30 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "household demand more generally that clearly is the source of strength holding this economy together" but capital spending is in deep contraction with "no pricing power"
- STERN: "we clearly had a capital spending bubble that has now collapsed. And that's not something that monetary policy is inherently well positioned to deal with"
- MOSKOW: "the outlook for capital spending is poor in a large number of industries... no recovery is in sight for heavy truck sales"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is holding near trend supported by housing wealth effects and tax rebates, but business investment is in deep retrenchment -- the dominant theme of the meeting. The net position is clearly below trend, weighted heavily by the investment collapse.

**Minority views**: Jordan reports pockets of investment strength (e-business firms, healthcare, coal mining equipment). Housing construction is robust. These prevent the score from going lower.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MINEHAN: "the availability of bank credit has become a concern... Big firms with lower credit ratings are reportedly shut out of credit markets and smaller healthy firms are facing higher rates"
- MEYER: "on net financial conditions have actually become more restrictive rather than more stimulative if we begin the accounting from mid-2000"
- MCDONOUGH: "Bankers report steady loan demand, rising delinquency rates, and tighter credit standards"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite aggressive Fed easing, broader financial conditions have tightened: long rates have not declined, equity prices are lower, the dollar was stronger until recently, credit standards have tightened, and some borrowers are shut out of credit markets. Residential mortgage conditions remain favorable, tempering the overall tightness.

**Minority views**: The residential mortgage market remains well-functioning and Guynn notes that low rates are stimulating home equity lending aggressively. This produces the downward uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "the appropriate policy at this stage is to move the federal funds rate a notch lower. A 25 basis point reduction strikes me as appropriate" with risks "weighted toward economic weakness"
- FERGUSON: "the arguments against moving rates down at this policy meeting strike me as far weaker than the arguments in favor of moving them"
- PARRY: "While I'm concerned about these considerations, I do support a 25 basis point cut in the funds rate as some insurance against additional downside surprises"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous support for a 25bp cut with the balance of risks tilted toward weakness, reflecting a moderate dovish consensus. The move is limited to 25bp (not 50bp), and several participants express growing concern about the need to reverse course eventually, which keeps the score from being more deeply dovish.

**Minority views**: Guynn explicitly argues a "good case can be made for patience and caution" and worries about over-stimulating housing. Jordan would prefer to conserve dry powder for external shocks. These temper the dovish lean.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- All participants support the 25bp cut -- no formal dissents
- BROADDUS: pushes for language signaling approaching end of easing cycle; others (Stern, Minehan, Guynn) share concerns about exit strategy
- GUYNN: "a good case can be made for patience and caution... I remain bothered by the rapid growth in the monetary aggregates"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: No formal dissents and unanimous support for the action, but with notable philosophical differences about the wisdom of continued easing. Several participants (Guynn, Broaddus, Stern, Jordan) express meaningful reservations that go beyond minor emphasis differences, producing moderate disagreement within a consensus outcome.

**Minority views**: N/A -- the spread itself is the story.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GUYNN: "I'm also concerned that a lower federal funds rate and considerable liquidity in the banking system have added to the high proportion of loans leveraged by housing collateral... Lowering rates further may simply encourage more home equity lending and more sub-prime lending to marginal borrowers"
- MCDONOUGH: Extended discussion of Argentine crisis risks and potential for "very chaotic financial markets"
- MEYER: Extensive analysis of financial conditions via FRB/US simulations

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives moderate attention. Several participants discuss credit market conditions, housing-related risks, and international financial stability (Argentina, Asia) meaningfully, though it does not dominate the discussion -- the economic slowdown and capital spending collapse are the primary focus.

**Minority views**: Most participants focus on the real economy rather than financial stability per se.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 30 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "I would say at this point that the real-time surveillance of the economy... is one that says we have not as yet completed our easing process"
- GREENSPAN: "at some point we are going to have to stop our easing process" and the statement "eliminates any notion of an intermeeting move"
- BROADDUS: "our statement... should indicate clearly that there's at least a possibility that the easing cycle may now be approaching an end"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is continued easing bias with growing caution. Greenspan signals more easing likely but at a measured pace (25bp increments, no intermeeting moves). Multiple participants want to signal that the end of easing is approaching, though they are not yet ready to stop. The signal is dovish with increasing data dependence.

**Minority views**: Guynn and Jordan implicitly prefer stopping sooner. Broaddus explicitly wants hawkish forward language. These push the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- GREENSPAN: "the outlook remains subject to a good deal of uncertainty" and forecasts "essentially reflect hope. We do not yet see any evidence of the beginnings of a turnaround"
- MCDONOUGH: "there is certainly a very great deal of uncertainty about this short-term growth outlook"
- GRAMLICH: "the forecast recovery has been pushed back and pushed back again; it always seems to be just around the corner but never quite here"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express elevated uncertainty about the timing and strength of the recovery. Repeated forecast downgrades have undermined confidence in the baseline projection. Multiple participants describe the Greenbook baseline as "hope more than reality" (Ferguson, Greenspan). Uncertainty about the international outlook adds to the concern. However, this is not "uncharted territory" -- participants have frameworks for analyzing the slowdown.

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Jordan are somewhat more confident that a bottom may be near.
