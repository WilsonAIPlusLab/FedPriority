# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 1995-03-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an intermeeting/telephone conference call on March 10, 1995. It is NOT a regularly scheduled FOMC meeting. The discussion is extremely narrow in scope, focused on three topics: (1) the Mexican and Argentine financial crises and their market spillovers, (2) potential foreign exchange intervention to support the dollar, and (3) a Bankers Trust loss announcement. There is no go-around on domestic economic conditions, no policy discussion regarding the federal funds rate, and no discussion of inflation, output, labor markets, or consumption/investment.

### Era Context
March 1995: The Fed had been in a tightening cycle through 1994 (raising rates from 3% to 6%). The Mexican peso crisis ("Tequila Crisis") erupted in December 1994 and was creating contagion to Argentina and broader emerging markets. The dollar was weak against European currencies. However, NONE of these macro conditions are discussed by participants in terms of domestic economic assessment -- this call is purely operational/crisis management.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Assessment

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: No participant discusses U.S. inflation in any way. There is zero textual basis for scoring. Must be N/A.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: No participant discusses U.S. GDP, growth, capacity utilization, or any dimension of domestic output. Must be N/A.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: No participant discusses unemployment, labor conditions, wages, or hiring. Must be N/A.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: No participant discusses consumer spending or business investment. Must be N/A.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the one dimension with some (indirect) evidence. The discussion centers on dollar weakness, FX market conditions, and the Argentine banking crisis. However, the discussion is about INTERNATIONAL financial conditions and FX intervention, not domestic U.S. financial conditions (credit availability, spreads, lending standards). The Bankers Trust loss is mentioned but Vice Chairman McDonough explicitly says "This is not a direct FOMC matter." The Argentine banking stress and interbank credit concerns (Minehan mentions "interbank credit market" and "U.S. banks that might be involved") provide some peripheral evidence of international financial stress. This is very thin but present.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: No participant discusses the appropriate stance of monetary policy (federal funds rate). Ms. Phillips makes one remark -- "if we go in modestly and there is a chance that we are going to back it up with monetary policy, then we are at least going in with a chance of some success. I don't see us doing that, at least in the next week or so" -- which implies no near-term policy change is expected, but this is about intervention credibility, not a policy preference. Mr. Melzer warns that intervention "might create a perception that this was being done to try to avoid a possible monetary policy response" but immediately adds "I don't mean to suggest that I think any policy actions are appropriate right now." There is no substantive policy lean discussion.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is disagreement -- but it is about FX INTERVENTION TACTICS, not monetary policy. Some favor modest intervention (Minehan, Greenspan tentatively, Kelley reluctantly), some are skeptical (Blinder, Hoenig, Kelley, Phillips), and McDonough presents a nuanced "minority position." This is tactical disagreement about intervention, not policy dissent.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: This is the most clearly scorable dimension. The ENTIRE meeting is about financial stability concerns -- Mexican crisis contagion, Argentine banking system fragility, dollar weakness, Bankers Trust losses. However, the rubric measures attention to financial stability in the context of the Fed's monetary policy discussion. This call is an operational/crisis call, not a monetary policy meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: No discussion of future policy direction for the federal funds rate.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Greenspan explicitly notes "Foreign exchange forecasting is notorious for how good it is!" (sarcastic). There is general uncertainty about intervention effectiveness and market dynamics. But this is uncertainty about intervention, not about the economic outlook.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
Since virtually all core economic dimensions lack evidence, coherence checks are moot. The only dimensions with any textual basis are FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (high -- the entire call is about financial stress) and possibly FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (modest -- international stress is discussed but not directly domestic conditions).

### Key Judgment
This is an emergency/intermeeting telephone conference call with an extremely narrow agenda. The rubric's edge case rule states: "Emergency meetings: Discussion may be short/narrow. Use N/A liberally. Note in anomaly flag." I will apply N/A to most dimensions and only score those with genuine textual evidence. The rubric also says for Tier 1 dimensions "always score, never N/A unless truly zero discussion." For INFLATION_PRESSURE, OUTPUT_GAP, LABOR_TIGHTNESS, CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT, HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN, and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE, there is genuinely ZERO discussion by participants. I will use N/A for these per the "truly zero discussion" exception.

For FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS, FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS, DISSENT_INTENSITY, and UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED, there is some basis for scoring, though the context is unusual.
