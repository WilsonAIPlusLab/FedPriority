# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 1999-10-05 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers the go-around (economic assessment) and a full policy discussion. Speakers include: Broaddus, Minehan, Parry, Guynn, Moskow, Hoenig, Jordan, McTeer, Stern, Poole, McDonough, Ferguson, Gramlich, Kelley, Meyer, Greenspan (Chairman), plus brief exchanges from others. This is a well-attended meeting with extensive commentary from approximately 16 participants.

### Era Context
October 1999: Late-stage expansion, the longest peacetime expansion in U.S. history to that point. Unemployment at 4.2%, well below conventional NAIRU estimates. Two 25bp rate hikes already delivered earlier in 1999 (June, August). Y2K concerns in background. Asian crisis recovery underway. Strong productivity growth debate ("new economy"). Core CPI near 2%, but oil prices rising and temporary disinflationary factors fading.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently low but with rising risks. Core CPI at ~2% (price stability per McDonough). However, a strong majority sees upside inflation risks: fading temporary factors (cheap oil gone, dollar weakening, foreign recovery reducing excess capacity, health insurance costs surging). Meyer lists 9 factors pushing inflation higher. Greenbook projects core CPI rising to 2.7-3% by 2001. Minehan says "it would seem that all the temporary factors that acted to restrain price increases...are now slipping away." But McDonough pushes back strongly: "with a year-over-year rate of 1.9 percent in the core CPI...we surely have price stability." McTeer sees "no significant pickup in inflation." Center of gravity: current inflation is low/benign, but most participants see it as at or slightly below target NOW with meaningful upside pipeline pressures building. This is not elevated inflation -- it's concern about *future* inflation from a currently stable base. Score should be around 48-53: near target but with pipeline pressures starting. Direction: slightly above midpoint because of the pipeline/cost pressures noted by the majority, even though realized inflation is benign.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly universal view that economy is running above potential. Broaddus: "growth of aggregate demand is continuing to outstrip the growth of potential GDP." Minehan: "above-trend growth." Hoenig: "GDP growth well above 3 percent when...potential may be in the 3 percent range." Meyer: "near-term above-trend growth...aggravate already tight labor markets." Greenspan debates whether potential is higher than models suggest (positive second derivative of productivity), which would narrow the gap, but even he acknowledges demand is outrunning supply. Bright-line rule: clear "above potential/above trend" language from multiple speakers means score MUST be >50. This is the late-90s boom. Anchor: 57-63 for late 90s boom. Score ~58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Extremely tight labor markets are discussed by virtually every speaker. Broaddus: "labor markets remain very tight." Minehan: "labor markets in the region remain extremely tight," unemployment at 3% in New England. Parry: District unemployment falling, labor costs rising faster in the West. Guynn: "Labor markets remain very tight. Some say they are even a bit tighter." Moskow: "scattered reports of shortages." Hoenig: District unemployment at 3.4%. Stern: Twin Cities unemployment below 2%. Poole: "awfully hard to find workers." McDonough: 4.2% national unemployment. Multiple anecdotes of restaurants closing for lack of staff, signing bonuses, recruiting retirees. Anchor: 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Very strong across the board. Broaddus: "extraordinary strength of household spending," contacts call it a "binge." Minehan: "incoming data on the real domestic economy are considerably stronger than we and others had expected." Moskow: auto sales "quite robust," manufacturing at "very high levels." Jordan: motor vehicles "booming," luxury goods sales "took off like gangbusters" in September. Kelley: "consumption is surging," "auto sales continue to fly higher." Meyer: "investment soared in the third quarter." Greenspan: remarkable acceleration in capital investment. McTeer: "brisk pace." Score should be well above trend -- 65-68 range. Anchor: 55-65 "above trend/robust."

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. Corporate bond yields up significantly (Greenspan: "corporate bond yields...are up significantly more than Treasuries"). McTeer: "most major stock market indexes have fallen by about 10 percent since our last meeting and there has been a noticeable pickup in yield spreads." Minehan: "widening credit spreads." McDonough: "increasingly tight credit standards" at banks. But also: Stern notes banks still "chasing the marginal customer." Jordan discusses record asset prices and easy credit. Kelley: "interest rates have been rising." Overall, conditions have tightened modestly from very loose levels -- the two rate hikes and wider spreads are noted, but credit is still broadly available. Score ~47-50: roughly neutral, perhaps very slightly on the loose side given the stock market and credit boom context, but with recent tightening.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee voted for "B" (no change) with an asymmetric directive toward tightening. Several participants (Minehan, Guynn, Kelley, Hoenig, Broaddus) explicitly said they could have supported a rate hike today. Hoenig wanted to move up 25bp. Meyer pushed strongly for tightening bias. Gramlich: "I would be more comfortable with a higher funds rate." McTeer and McDonough are the clear doves -- McDonough says "declare victory," McTeer sees no inflation pickup. Stern favors B, is relatively relaxed. The center of gravity is: hold today but lean strongly toward tightening soon, with November as a live meeting. This is moderately hawkish. Score ~62-65: majority favors near-term tightening, held back only by wanting more data and Y2K timing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent on the vote. But there is genuine disagreement. Hoenig explicitly wanted to raise rates. McDonough and McTeer are notably dovish, arguing against concern over tight labor markets and inflation. Most others cluster around "hold but lean hawkish." The productivity debate (Greenspan's second derivative argument) creates intellectual disagreement about the framework. There's also disagreement on disclosure/asymmetry communication (5-4 split among voting members). Score ~35-40: notable differences in economic outlook and policy preference, but ultimately broad agreement on the hold-with-asymmetry outcome.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants mention financial conditions: Jordan discusses asset price inflation, unsustainable credit-financed asset prices, and potential credit crunch. Greenspan discusses wealth effect and potential stock market bubble ("a bubble that can't persist"). Minehan mentions "unrealistic asset prices and high and growing levels of consumer and business debt." Hoenig mentions record levels of consumer/corporate/margin debt. Stern notes deteriorating bank credit quality. Kelley: "negative saving rate...introduces the danger of fragility." This is moderate -- not the dominant topic but discussed meaningfully by several participants. Score ~35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Clear signal toward future tightening. Asymmetric directive adopted and announced. Greenspan: "I would prefer not to give a message that we are necessarily through for the year." Multiple participants emphasize November is a live meeting. Gramlich: "I believe we might have to act in November." Kelley: "delighted that the window still appears to be open for action in November." Forward signal is mildly to moderately hawkish. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Significant uncertainty about productivity trends (the entire Greenspan lecture on second derivatives is about what we don't know). Parry: "there is simply no way to determine the size or persistence of the current supply shock." Kelley: "a perfectly plausible case can be made for at least three different policies." McDonough: "forecasting models...have been consistently wrong." Greenspan: "we really don't know at this stage." Ferguson: "many things we don't know and our models have been inaccurate." This is elevated uncertainty -- not crisis-level, but genuine intellectual humility about the structural shift underway. Score ~50-55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor is tighter than the output gap score suggests, but this is consistent with the era -- the debate is whether potential output is higher than measured (Greenspan's productivity argument), which would narrow the output gap while labor markets remain demonstrably tight. The divergence is explained.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Aligned -- current hold with hawkish forward lean.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low realized but building pipeline. Score 52 = slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above trend. Score 58 = above midpoint. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says extremely tight. Score 70 = well above midpoint. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says modest recent tightening from loose levels. Score 48 = very slightly loose. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "the economy to sustain this boom situation to this point without any run-up in inflation" -- acknowledges no current inflation but risks
- MS. MINEHAN: "all the temporary factors that acted to restrain price increases...are now slipping away"
- MR. MEYER: Lists 9 factors contributing to higher inflation ahead; "the cards seem stacked"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "with a year-over-year rate of 1.9 percent in the core CPI at the end of August, I think we surely have price stability"
- MR. MCTEER: "I'm not foreseeing any significant pickup in inflation in the next year or so"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Realized inflation is at or slightly below 2% target (benign), but a clear majority sees pipeline pressures building from fading temporary factors, tight labor markets, and rising commodity/energy costs. The score reflects current near-target inflation with modest upward pressures emerging.

**Minority views:** McDonough and McTeer are more sanguine, seeing continued price stability; this widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "the growth of aggregate demand is continuing to outstrip the growth of potential GDP"
- MS. MINEHAN: "extremely tight labor markets and above-trend growth"
- MR. HOENIG: "GDP growth well above 3 percent when...potential may be in the 3 percent range"
- MR. MEYER: "near-term above-trend growth is projected to aggravate already tight labor markets"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Argues potential may be higher due to accelerating productivity, but acknowledges "actual demand growth is in excess of potential supply"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Nearly all participants view the economy as operating above potential, with demand growth outpacing supply growth. However, the vigorous debate about whether potential itself is higher than modeled (Greenspan's productivity argument) introduces some moderation to the score.

**Minority views:** Greenspan's argument that accelerating productivity raises potential output could narrow the gap; McTeer also sees less overheating.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "regional unemployment rate hit 3 percent in August...Beigebook contacts in every industry reported shortages of workers at all skill levels"
- MR. STERN: "the unemployment rate in the Twin Cities metro area has dropped below 2 percent"
- MR. GUYNN: "Labor markets remain very tight. Some say they are even a bit tighter than earlier in the year"
- MR. POOLE: "it's awfully hard to find workers, but they are finding them"
- MS. MINEHAN: Restaurants "forced to close a couple of days during the week...because there just was not sufficient staff"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually every participant describes extremely tight labor markets with widespread worker shortages, very low regional unemployment rates, and creative measures to attract workers. National unemployment at 4.2% with many districts well below that.

**Minority views:** Minimal -- even the doves (McDonough) acknowledge 4.2% unemployment and tight markets; they just don't view it negatively.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 67 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "extraordinary strength of household spending...a household spending 'binge'"
- MR. KELLEY: "consumption is surging, even as consumers continue to run with a negative saving rate...auto sales continue to fly higher"
- MR. MEYER: "investment soared in the third quarter"
- MR. JORDAN: "motor vehicles are booming"; luxury goods sales "took off like gangbusters" in September
- MS. MINEHAN: "incoming data on the real domestic economy are considerably stronger than we and others had expected"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and business investment are both described as remarkably strong, well above trend, with Q3 GDP growth revised up to ~4.5%. Multiple participants use language like "surge," "boom," and "binge."

**Minority views:** Some note a potential slowdown ahead (Greenbook forecast), but current momentum is universally acknowledged as very strong.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a very significant rise in corporate bond yields...they are up significantly more than Treasuries"
- MR. MCTEER: "most major stock market indexes have fallen by about 10 percent since our last meeting and there has been a noticeable pickup in yield spreads"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "increasingly tight credit standards" at banks
- MR. STERN: Banks "still chasing the marginal customer"
- MR. JORDAN: Record levels of margin credit, consumer debt, corporate debt

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions have tightened modestly from recent rate hikes, wider credit spreads, and a stock market correction, but credit remains broadly available and the boom in asset prices and debt accumulation suggests conditions are still relatively accommodative on balance.

**Minority views:** Some (McTeer) emphasize the tightening; others (Jordan, Stern) note continued aggressive lending and debt growth.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 63 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "I, too, could have been enthusiastic about increasing the fed funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting"
- MR. HOENIG: "my own view is still that we should move the funds rate up 1/4 point now"
- MR. KELLEY: "I found myself identifying with those who would have preferred to go ahead and tighten today"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "in my heart...I would be more comfortable with a higher funds rate"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "we should declare victory...and stop worrying ourselves into believing that we have to stand in the way of something...very positive"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The Committee voted unanimously for no change with an asymmetric (tightening) directive. Several members explicitly stated they could have supported a hike today. Only McDonough and McTeer were clearly dovish. The center of gravity is a firm hold with strong expectation of tightening at or shortly after the November meeting.

**Minority views:** McDonough argues forcefully for patience and declaring victory on price stability; McTeer sees no inflation problem. Hoenig wanted to move immediately.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: Explicitly favored a rate hike today
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: Argued strongly against the hawkish consensus, calling inflation concerns "like Don Quixote's windmills--a fiction of our own minds"
- 5-4 split among voting members on whether to announce asymmetry
- Fundamental intellectual disagreement about productivity (constant vs. accelerating trend)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissent on the vote, but genuine disagreement on economic outlook (productivity debate), urgency of action (several wanted to hike today vs. those content to wait), and communication strategy (5-4 split on disclosure). Disagreement is substantive but not divisive.

**Minority views:** The range spans from immediate tightening (Hoenig, Broaddus, Minehan, Guynn, Kelley) to declaring victory (McDonough, McTeer).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "I worry about asset price increases...financed in ways that make those price rises unsustainable. And...we will have a credit crunch"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the existence of a bubble that can't persist"
- MS. MINEHAN: "the very strong rates of near-term growth could exacerbate...the imbalances reflected in unrealistic asset prices and in high and growing levels of consumer and business debt"
- MR. HOENIG: "consumer debt is rising to record levels and corporate debt is increasing. Margin credit is rising to record levels"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Several participants raise financial stability concerns around asset prices, debt levels, and potential fragility, but it is not the dominant topic. The primary focus remains on the inflation/productivity debate.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would prefer not to give a message that we are necessarily through for the year"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I believe we might have to act in November"
- MR. KELLEY: "delighted that the window still appears to be open for action in November"
- Asymmetric (tightening bias) directive adopted and publicly announced
- MR. PARRY: "the time for raising rates is probably not that far off"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The asymmetric directive and language clearly signal that further tightening is likely. November is explicitly flagged as a live meeting by multiple participants. The forward lean is moderately hawkish.

**Minority views:** McTeer and McDonough would prefer no forward tightening signal, but they are a clear minority.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we really don't know at this stage" (regarding productivity second derivative)
- MR. KELLEY: "a perfectly plausible case can be made for at least three different policies. And there is a perfectly plausible rebuttal argument against each"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "The forecasting models...have been consistently wrong...these forecasts, since they have been consistently wrong, are likely to stay wrong"
- MR. PARRY: "there is simply no way to determine the size or persistence of the current supply shock"
- MR. FERGUSON: "we should be mindful of the fact that there are many things we don't know and that our models have been inaccurate for quite a while"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Elevated uncertainty driven by the fundamental question of whether the economy has structurally shifted (productivity acceleration) or is following traditional patterns. Multiple participants express genuine humility about model failures and the difficulty of forecasting in this environment.
