## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the December 16–17, 2014 FOMC meeting. It is segmented into two sessions: an economic go-around (December 16) and a policy discussion (December 17). Speakers are individually identified by name. The document covers a full, large committee with well over a dozen named participants. The total word count is very large (well over 30,000 words), providing high evidentiary richness for scoring.

### 2. Era Context
December 2014 is deep into the post-GFC recovery but well before the eventual liftoff in December 2015. The economy has been expanding for over five years. Unemployment has fallen sharply (from ~8% in 2012 to 5.8%), QE has just ended in October 2014, and the policy debate has shifted entirely to the timing of liftoff from the zero lower bound. The key macro features present in the text:
- Inflation persistently below 2% (running ~1.5% PCE core)
- A sharp drop in oil prices since mid-2014 is pushing headline inflation further down
- Strong payroll growth (~228–280K/month)
- Dollar appreciation and weak foreign growth (Europe, Japan) as headwinds
- Forward guidance transitioning from "considerable time" to "patient"

### 3. Directional Signals — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as **below target**. The center of gravity is that core PCE has been running around 1½%, has missed the 2% target for over 30 months, and is expected to remain below target for several more years. Multiple speakers (Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Brainard, Lockhart, Fischer) emphasize the persistence and troubling nature of below-target inflation. The hawkish end (Fisher, Lacker, Mester, Plosser, Williams) agree inflation is below target but characterize it as largely transitory (oil-driven), expect a return to target within 2–3 years, and argue expectations remain anchored. No one argues inflation is at or above target. Score: in the 28–35 range (disinflation / below target; slack keeping prices in check). Oil price shock pushes headline toward potentially negative territory near-term. This is solidly in the "below target" zone. I will score 32.

**Direction check**: Higher = more inflation pressure. Participants see inflation as clearly below target. Score below 50. ✓

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see the economy as **below potential but recovering/approaching**. The prevailing view is above-trend growth (~3%+ GDP in recent quarters), with unemployment nearing NAIRU (5.8% vs estimates of natural rate around 5.2–5.5%). Several speakers (Williams: "well above potential output," Bullard: "5 out of 6 quarters of 3.5%+ growth," Lacker, Fisher, Mester, Plosser) explicitly note above-trend growth. George estimates output gap around 1% (small remaining gap). Chair Yellen and others note growth running near 3.5% in H2 2014. However, some slack remains (Rosengren, Kocherlakota, Brainard note residual labor market slack and U-6 still elevated). The center of gravity: the economy is growing above trend, gap is narrowing rapidly, and may be near closing. Score: 45–48 (slightly below potential; approaching full capacity). The "above potential" language from Williams and Bullard is noted but the majority view is approaching, not yet above.

**Direction check**: Higher = more above potential. Gap is small and closing but not yet closed. Score approaching but below 50. Score: 46.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**

However, U-6 still elevated, long-term unemployment still above pre-crisis levels, wage growth still muted (2–2.25% ECI/AHE), part-time for economic reasons still somewhat elevated. Several participants debate whether U-3 understates slack. Majority view: labor market has made substantial progress and is near full employment, but residual slack remains. Score: 42–46 range. Unemployment still ~0.5–0.8 pp above estimates of NAIRU; wage acceleration largely absent; approaching but not at full employment.

**Direction check**: Higher = tighter labor. Approaching NAIRU but not there yet. Score ~44.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "current wage and price growth are significantly below levels consistent with our announced 2 percent inflation target... my own forecast does not have us reaching a core PCE inflation rate of 2 percent any time soon."
- Evans: "Since 2008, PCE inflation has averaged just 1½ percent per year... under my SEP forecast we will have missed our inflation target for ten years. I continue to see this outcome as highly unsatisfactory."
- Kocherlakota: "inflation has already been below target for over 30 months... the staff projection is that inflation will be below target in each of the next four years."
- Fisher (more optimistic end): "Dallas Fed trimmed mean PCE inflation has averaged 1.65 percent over the past year... will rise to just shy of 2 percent in 2015."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Essentially the entire committee agrees PCE inflation has been running ~1.5% for years and will remain below 2% for at least another year and likely longer; the only debate is whether this represents a serious problem (Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren) or a transitory/acceptable shortfall (Fisher, Lacker, Mester, Williams).

**Minority views:** Fisher and Lacker argue the below-target reading is supply-driven (oil) and transitory; they would score inflation pressure somewhat higher (maybe 38–40) if the question were about medium-term trajectory. This informs the ±7 band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 46 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee sees growth running above potential (3–3.5%+ vs. potential of ~2–2.5%), with a small output gap remaining (George estimates ~1%) that is rapidly closing; several say the economy is approaching or near potential, but not yet above it in level terms.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
