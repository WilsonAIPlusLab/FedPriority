# FOMC Transcript Scoring — June 29–30, 2004

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** from a two-day FOMC meeting (June 29–30, 2004). Individual participants speak in a go-around format followed by a brief policy discussion. Speakers are clearly identified by name. Note: Janet Yellen and Robert McTeer appear to have been introduced but their full statements are absent from this decomposed file — they are referenced by others (Kohn, Guynn refer to "President Yellen"). All scoring is based solely on content present in the document.

### 2. Era Context

Mid-2004: The economy was in a solid expansion after the 2001 recession and the 2003 job-loss recovery. The FOMC had held the fed funds rate at 1% since June 2003 and was widely expected to begin tightening at this meeting. Core PCE had accelerated noticeably in early 2004, raising inflation concerns while the output gap was still debated. This is the meeting at which the FOMC initiated its "measured pace" hiking cycle.

---

### 3. Directional Assessment — Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants broadly see inflation as having risen above comfortable levels, with core PCE running around 2%+. Multiple speakers note: Moskow sees "emerging inflationary pressures" and prices rising across sectors; Minehan notes a "substantial uptick" in CPI/PCE; Santomero sees a "rising trend in inflation"; Hoenig believes "core price indexes now have embedded in them some inflationary updrift"; Geithner says "significant acceleration in underlying inflation to a level somewhat above our range of comfort"; Bies notes "surprising strength" in inflation measures; Greenspan observes core market-based PCE at 2.1–2.4% annual rate. A minority (Gramlich, Ferguson, Bernanke) argue recent acceleration is likely temporary and inflation risks are roughly balanced, with Gramlich finding it "surprisingly hard to see much supporting macro evidence" for being behind the curve.

Direction: **Above target / modestly elevated.** The center of gravity sits above 50 but not at an alarm level. Most participants see elevated readings but expect moderation; a meaningful minority see them as temporary. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" but "expected to moderate" → score range 55–65. Placing at **60**.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants consistently use language pointing to an economy still below full potential but closing the gap. Lacker cites "output gap is still sizable." Minehan references "slack remaining in the economy." Kohn says "4 percent growth — only moderately above the rate of growth of the economy's potential" and references "remaining slack." Ferguson notes "unemployment rate, labor force participation rate, and capacity utilization rate have all remained stubbornly low." Gramlich explicitly argues 5.6% unemployment is not full capacity, referencing prior 4% unemployment experience. Greenspan notes a strong anecdotal picture but downward data revisions. Pianalto says growth not "poised to accelerate further." Santomero says economy "has left the recovery phase and is now in an economic expansion" — slightly more positive.

Direction: **Below potential but approaching it** — clearly below 50 by the bright-line rule (multiple "slack remains," "output gap still sizable" references). Magnitude: described as "sizable" by some but "not that large" by others — modest negative gap. Score range 41–47. Placing at **44**.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Labor market is improving meaningfully but still below full employment. Unemployment at 5.6%. Kohn: "labor force participation hasn't risen" and "workweek… has barely budged off a very low level." Ferguson: "unemployment rate is unchanged since January, and labor force participation is low." Gramlich: "5.6 percent is above the NAIRU" (i.e., labor market still has slack). At the same time, several regional contacts mention difficulty finding qualified workers (Minehan, Santomero, Lacker), and Poole's UPS contact anticipates part-time worker problems. Multiple speakers note wage rates are contained but beginning to tick up.

Direction: **Below NAIRU / still slack but tightening** — below 50. Multiple references to unemployment above NAIRU-estimates (~5%), limited wage acceleration, low labor force participation. Yet anecdotal reports of selective tightness. Score range 40–48. Placing at **44**.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Business investment described as strong and accelerating (Santomero: "business investment is accelerating"; Geithner: "ample reason and room to continue to grow at a rapid rate"; Bies: "capital expenditures…are strong"). Consumer spending described as moderate to solid (Geithner: "reasonably healthy growth in consumption"; Poole notes Wal-Mart June slightly weaker, 2–4% vs. expected 4–6%, attributing to weather). Retail sales generally on upward trend across Districts. FedEx, UPS, trucking companies describe demand as extraordinarily strong. Overall, spending is above trend.

Direction: **Above trend** — clearly above 50. Business investment strong, consumption solid-to-above. Score range 55–63. Placing at **60**.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants note financial conditions remain accommodative overall despite recent market adjustments anticipating rate hikes. Kohn: "Financial conditions remain supportive of above-trend growth, but they have tightened in recent months as markets have come to anticipate our actions." Geithner: "financial markets seem reasonably well prepared for the beginning of this transition" — suggesting conditions haven't seized up. Greenspan warns about carry trades and balance sheet maturity mismatches but sees conditions as still supportive. Olson: corporate balance sheets strong, asset quality at banks "best ever." Real rates still negative. The context is one of accommodative-to-loosening conditions (real fed funds rate negative), though nominal rates are about to rise.

Direction: **Loose / accommodative** — below 50. Policy rate at 1% with inflation at 2%, implying strongly negative real rates. Kohn describes conditions as "supportive of above-trend growth." Score range 20–32. Placing at **28**.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This meeting ends with a unanimous (or near-unanimous) 25bp hike. Every speaker present supports beginning tightening. Multiple participants (Minehan, Santomero, Hoenig, Guynn, Lacker) explicitly push for the rate increase and emphasize need to move toward neutral. The dovish voices (Gramlich, Ferguson, Bernanke) agree with the hike but urge gradualism and caution about overshooting. Greenspan himself supports 25bp and leaves open 50bp for August. There is broad consensus for tightening initiation, but views diverge on pace — most favor "measured" (25bp per meeting), while a few push for potentially faster adjustment.

Direction: **Hawkish** — clearly above 50. Unanimous support for first rate hike; forward lean toward continued tightening. Magnitude: "moderate hawkish" — 25bp hike with measured pace signal; some want flexibility for faster moves. Score range 62–70. Placing at **65**.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
No formal dissents recorded (the roll call yields unanimous support for 25bp). However, there are meaningful differences: Gramlich/Ferguson/Bernanke lean toward caution and see inflation risks as roughly balanced; Santomero/Hoenig/Minehan/Guynn push harder for normalization and worry about being behind the curve. A separate debate exists over statement language — whether to keep "measured pace" language (Kohn, Bernanke, Gramlich support keeping it; Minehan, Poole want to remove it; Santomero wants to simplify). These differences are substantive but resolved without formal dissents.

Direction: **Moderate disagreement** — no formal dissents but clear camps on pace and language. Score range 30–40. Placing at **35**.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial stability is not a dominant topic. Brief mentions include: Guynn on consumer debt levels and current account deficit; Greenspan's warning about carry trades and balance sheet maturity mismatches; Geithner noting uncertainty about how the world will react to tightening initiation; Olson on bank asset quality. These are contextual mentions, not a sustained financial stability discussion. No crisis language, no market stress concerns.

Direction: **Limited discussion** — below 40. Score range 20–30. Placing at **25**.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Participants debate the forward guidance language extensively. The adopted statement retains "measured pace" language but adds a sentence about responding to incoming data. Multiple speakers (Minehan, Poole, Santomero, Hoenig) want to move away from the "measured pace" commitment and toward data-dependence. Kohn argues for retaining "measured pace" as it conveys useful information. Greenspan frames the evolution: "considerable period" → "patience" → "measured pace" → eventual return to balance-of-risks formulation. The forward signal is: tightening will continue at a measured pace, but the Committee reserves flexibility for faster moves.

Direction: **Mild-to-moderate tightening bias** — clearly hawkish forward signal, but "measured pace" qualifier is deliberate. Score range 65–75. Placing at **68**.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Uncertainty about inflation dynamics is repeatedly emphasized. Bernanke: "inflation forecasts…are always hazardous and seem particularly so today." Ferguson: "models…have an r2 of somewhere around 0.5 or 0.6, suggesting that humility is very important." Kohn: "there is much we do not understand about the inflation process." Greenspan notes a disparity between strong anecdotes and downward data revisions — "something very interesting going on." However, uncertainty about growth is more limited — most participants confident in self-sustaining expansion. The uncertainty is concentrated in inflation dynamics and pace of output gap closure, not in whether expansion continues.

Direction: **Somewhat elevated uncertainty** — above normal but not extreme. Focused on inflation dynamics and policy path. Score range 38–48. Placing at **42**.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative → score should be low (below 50). Score 28 → consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says still below potential → score below 50. Score 44 → consistent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence = unanimous hike with ongoing tightening bias → score above 50. Score 65 → consistent.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Measured pace" of tightening with flexibility for faster moves → above 50. Score 68 → consistent.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (44) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44): Both slightly below 50, consistent with an economy approaching but not yet at full potential. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Both above 50 and closely aligned — hawkish current action with mild tightening forward signal. The slight difference reflects that the "measured pace" qualifier softens the forward tone relative to pure current hawkishness. Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (60) and OUTPUT_GAP (44): Somewhat divergent — inflation elevated despite output gap still negative. This is consistent with the textual debate: participants note that some of the inflation acceleration appears supply/commodity-driven rather than demand-pull, explaining why inflation readings are elevated even with remaining slack. Flagged but not surprising given era context.

### 6. Speaker Count by View Direction

**Hawkish / inflation-concerned / push for faster normalization**: Minehan, Santomero, Hoenig, Guynn, Lacker, Poole, Bies (~7)
**Centrist / measured / gradual approach**: Kohn, Geithner, Moskow, Olson, Pianalto (~5)
**Cautious / inflation risks balanced**: Gramlich, Ferguson, Bernanke (~3)
**Chairman Greenspan**: favors 25bp + measured pace + flexibility language (centrist-hawkish)

Center of gravity: moderately hawkish, broadly supportive of tightening, with debate concentrated on pace and communications language.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 60 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Geithner: "We have now seen significant acceleration in underlying inflation to a level somewhat above our range of comfort."
- Greenspan: "the underlying inflation rate, using the market-based core PCE measure, is at an annual rate of 2.1 percent for April and May, and for the latest three months it goes up to 2.4 percent."
- Hoenig: "I still think that the core price indexes now have embedded in them some inflationary updrift that is due to our earlier accommodative monetary policy."

**Center of gravity:** Participants broadly perceive inflation as having moved modestly above a comfortable level (core PCE above 2%), with the majority expecting moderation but acknowledging upside risk. A vocal minority (Gramlich, Bernanke, Ferguson) find the acceleration likely temporary.

**Minority views:** Gramlich sees "substantial doubts" about further pass-through and finds macro evidence for being "behind the curve" surprisingly hard to find. Bernanke remains "inclined to the view that inflation…is likely to moderate in the second half." These views widen the uncertainty band but do not shift the center of gravity below 55.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 44 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Lacker: "The output gap is still sizable. The markup of prices over unit labor costs…is well above its long-run average."
- Kohn: "4 percent growth—only moderately above the rate of growth of the economy's potential—doesn't seem all that strong" and "continuing though ebbing margins of slack."
- Minehan: "This forecast is shaped by the assessment of slack remaining in the economy and the belief that growth will not be constrained by resources for some time, perhaps not until year-end 2005."

**Center of gravity:** The economy is solidly expanding but participants broadly acknowledge a remaining output gap. The gap is described as "sizable" by some and "not that large" by others; the Greenbook baseline closes it by early 2006. All references point to below-potential operation.

**Minority views:** Santomero says the economy "has left the recovery phase and is now in an economic expansion" and projects somewhat stronger growth, implying a faster gap closure. This narrows the uncertainty band from below.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 44 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "In the household survey, the unemployment rate is unchanged since January, and labor force participation hasn't risen…the workweek…has barely budged off a very low level."
- Gramlich: "the present 5.6 percent rate of employment does not represent full capacity economywide…I remember a few years ago when the unemployment rate went to 4 percent with no real hint of acceleration in inflation."
- Ferguson: "unemployment rate, labor force participation rate, and capacity utilization rate have all remained stubbornly low or high — depending on how you think about it."

**Center of gravity:** Unemployment at 5.6%, labor force participation depressed, and wages not accelerating — indicating the labor market is still below NAIRU by most participants' estimates, though it is improving and some Districts report spot tightness. Score consistent with OUTPUT_GAP at 44.

**Minority views:** Minehan, Santomero, and Lacker all report anecdotal labor shortages in specific sectors/regions, keeping the uncertainty band from being narrower.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 60 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Geithner: "Investment seems likely to have ample reason and room to continue to grow at a rapid rate, with corporate balance sheets strong, profit margins strong, inventories low, and orders running high."
- Bies: "Capital expenditures…are strong. Employment growth in the last several months has really come back to life."
- Poole (FedEx contact): "incredibly strong" earnings; FedEx "operating at capacity in its freight business"; UPS says demand is "well above expectations everywhere."

**Center of gravity:** Business investment clearly strong and accelerating; consumer spending solid if slightly moderated recently (Wal-Mart June softness attributed to weather). Overall above-trend spending picture, especially on the investment side.

**Minority views:** Ferguson flags that "consumer spending, while also strong, was noticeably weaker than expected" and that "the economy's path may be shallower than originally thought." This tempers the score from the upper range of the above-trend band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 28 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "Financial conditions remain supportive of above-trend growth, but they have tightened in recent months as markets have come to anticipate our actions."
- Olson (banking contacts): "asset quality exceeded that suggested in Steve's chart show…at a level that also is better than what could be achieved simply by the application of good credit standards."
- Greenspan: concern about carry trades and maturity mismatches, but frames these as reasons to move gradually — not signs of stress.

**Center of gravity:** Conditions remain fundamentally accommodative with a real fed funds rate deeply negative. Recent tightening in anticipation of rate hikes noted but described as orderly and manageable. Credit quality at banks described as exceptional.

**Minority views:** Guynn mentions elevated consumer debt levels and current account deficit as imbalances. Geithner flags uncertainty about how markets will react to the start of tightening. These do not reach "tightness" in current conditions but warrant a slightly wider band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 65 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Minehan: "I think the time has come to start moving the fed funds rate up and to get real short-term rates first to a positive position and then to a more neutral level."
- Hoenig: "I would join those who encourage us to have in mind moving back toward neutral at a more deliberate pace and in a very systematic fashion…avoid having to go above neutral later to contain inflation."
- Gramlich (most dovish present): "I'm still comfortable with the main strategy of a gradual increase in rates starting now and at every meeting or so."

**Center of gravity:** Universal support for a 25bp hike at this meeting with unanimous vote. The debate is about pace and language — most favor "measured" (25bp per meeting); a few want flexibility or faster moves. No participant argues for holding steady.

**Minority views:** Gramlich, Bernanke, and Ferguson urge caution and gradualism, arguing against rushing normalization. Their presence in the discussion prevents the score from reaching the 70+ range.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 35 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Formal vote: unanimous (no dissents recorded in this transcript).
- Substantive differences: Gramlich finds it "surprisingly hard to see much supporting macro evidence" for being behind the curve, while Hoenig believes "inflation…is now threatening."
- Language dispute: Minehan and Poole want to remove "measured pace" language; Kohn argues for retaining it; Gramlich cautions against over-reliance on balance-of-risks formulation.

**Center of gravity:** No formal dissents but clearly distinct camps — inflation-hawks vs. cautious gradualists, and a language debate that consumes a notable portion of the policy discussion. Consistent with "broad agreement on action, moderate disagreement on framing and pace."

**Minority views:** The range of views is genuine and substantive, placing this above a low-disagreement meeting (10–20) but well below the multiple-dissent territory (60+).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 25 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "we have to be careful not to move too fast and risk destabilizing the financial system by enforcing maturity mismatches and other idiosyncrasies in the financial system."
- Guynn: "Consumer debt and household financial obligations, while a bit off their peak levels, are still much greater than in the 1990s…we have a growing and unsustainable current account deficit."
- Geithner: "there's a lot we cannot measure and cannot know about how the world will react to the initiation of what will have to be a substantial increase in the fed funds rate."

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability issues are raised as context for policy pace decisions, not as a primary topic of concern in their own right. Discussions are brief and episodic, not a sustained theme. Banking system is healthy (Olson). The emphasis is clearly secondary.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 68 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan (adopted statement framing): the statement retains "measured pace" language but adds a sentence emphasizing Committee will respond to changes in economic prospects — keeping open possibility of 50bp moves.
- Minehan: wants "to remove from our statement any characterization of how we will act in the future" — arguing for less commitment.
- Kohn: "the combination of words that reflects both the central tendency of expectations of a measured pace of policy tightening and the recognition that…the Committee must be alert to the possibility that it could need to take more-decisive action."

**Center of gravity:** The forward signal is a clear tightening direction ("measured pace" of further increases) with explicit preservation of flexibility for faster moves if needed. Hawkish forward lean, moderated by the "measured pace" qualifier and the data-dependence caveat. Score just above the lower bound of "clear tightening signal" range.

**Note:** The adopted language — "measured pace" + flexibility clause — is carefully calibrated to be hawkish in direction but moderate in pace commitment.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 42 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bernanke: "inflation forecasts as well as output forecasts are, of course, always hazardous and seem particularly so today."
- Ferguson: "models that we have on price level changes I think have an r2 of somewhere around 0.5 or 0.6, suggesting that humility in this regard, as with other things, is very important."
- Kohn: "there is much we do not understand about the inflation process…and the pickup in core inflation has been a surprise to me."

**Center of gravity:** Uncertainty is genuinely elevated, particularly around inflation dynamics (pass-through, persistence, output gap estimates). Multiple participants express explicit uncertainty. However, uncertainty about the growth outlook is moderate — expansion is "self-sustaining" by broad consensus. The uncertainty is concentrated and domain-specific rather than pervasive.

**Minority views:** Greenspan and several Presidents express considerable confidence in the growth trajectory. This prevents the overall uncertainty score from rising above 50.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS:**
**Score: 65 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**
Bernanke explicitly decomposes the inflation acceleration: "more than 100 percent of the acceleration in core PCE inflation between 2003:H2 and 2004:H1 can be accounted for by higher inflation or less deflation in goods prices, particularly durable goods prices." Multiple speakers cite steel, concrete, commodities as sources of price pressure. Score reflects clearly elevated goods price pressures.

**INFLATION_SERVICES:**
**Score: 45 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**
Bernanke: "inflation in services excluding energy…has actually decelerated in the past six months." Services inflation is relatively contained relative to goods. Slightly below neutral given deceleration signal.

**INFLATION_HOUSING:**
**Score: 58 ±8 | Confidence: Low**
Multiple speakers note strong homebuilding and home price appreciation (Santomero, Hoenig, Lacker), but housing inflation is not discussed as a standalone concern. Score reflects generally elevated housing market activity.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:**
**Score: 55 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**
Lacker: "short-run inflation expectations have moved up in recent months together with actual inflation, and financial market measures of inflation compensation at longer horizons have drifted higher." However, Greenspan counters: "measures of long-term inflation expectations are remarkably contained" — the forward rate on 1-year maturities nine years out "has barely moved" and is actually below August 2003 levels. Short-term expectations up modestly; long-term anchored. Score reflects modest upward drift in near-term expectations, with long-term still well-anchored.

**BANKING_STRESS:**

Banking system discussed briefly by Olson (asset quality "best ever") but not framed as stress; no 2+ participants discuss banking stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS:**

**DOLLAR_CONCERN:**
**Score: 35 ±10 | Confidence: Low**
Guynn and Gramlich both mention the current account deficit and dollar vulnerability, and Greenspan references dollar-related dynamics. However, the concern is about dollar weakness/depreciation risk (Guynn: "falling behind the curve we risk the likelihood of additional pressure on the dollar"). Score below 50 reflects mild concern about dollar weakness, not strength. Only marginally eligible given limited focus.

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
**Score: 65 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**
Pianalto cites numerous examples of strong productivity gains from lean manufacturing, with one CEO citing 10% annual rates. Bernanke: "structural productivity is probably still advancing rapidly." Greenspan discusses productivity slowdown as a near-term concern but structural productivity still high. Score reflects continued above-trend productivity perception, moderated by Greenspan's note about actual productivity slowing in April–May.
