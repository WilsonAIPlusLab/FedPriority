# FOMC Scoring: 1999-08-24 Transcript

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions from the August 24, 1999 FOMC meeting. It includes a go-around (economic assessment by district presidents and governors) followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Broaddus, Guynn, Hoenig, McDonough, Jordan, McTeer, Stern, Poole, Gramlich, Meyer, Ferguson, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
Late 1990s boom. The economy is in the midst of a long expansion with strong productivity growth, tight labor markets, low unemployment, and rising equity markets. The Fed had cut rates 75bp in fall 1998 due to the LTCM/Asia crisis, then began reversing those cuts in June 1999. This is the second tightening of that cycle. Oil prices are rising, the dollar is weakening, and global growth is recovering. Inflation remains low but there are widespread concerns about future acceleration.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently contained but with rising risks. The center of gravity is that core inflation is still low (Greenspan: "core CPI excluding tobacco...is well under 2 percent"; McDonough: "we are enjoying price stability"; McTeer: "Core inflation is about as low as it was last year"). However, many see upside risks: oil prices rising, commodity prices increasing, favorable supply shocks dissipating (Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Meyer, Guynn, Ferguson). Several note wage pressures are beginning to appear. The balance is: inflation is currently at or near target, but participants perceive early signs of upward pressure and dissipation of favorable factors. Direction: modestly above the midpoint -- current inflation is not elevated, but the perception of emerging pressure tips it above 50. Score around 52-55.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is low now but pressures are building. A score of 53 captures "slightly above neutral with emerging concerns." Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants overwhelmingly describe the economy as strong, growing at or above trend, with very tight resource utilization. Greenspan: "we have a very strong economy"; Kelley: "growth of 3.5 percent or likely more than that, which would mark a return to a pace above a sustainable level"; Meyer: "continued momentum in aggregate demand...support growth at or above trend"; Moskow: "this moderation in growth is not likely to be enough to alleviate increasing inflationary pressures." Multiple participants describe the economy as running above potential. Several view the Q2 slowdown as an "aberration." Direction: clearly above potential. Score around 58-62.

Bright-line rule check: "above trend" and "above sustainable level" language --> must be > 50. Score of 60 is consistent. Era anchor for late 90s boom: 57-63. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is one of the most extensively discussed topics. Nearly every participant describes labor markets as "very tight" or "extremely tight." Minehan: "unemployment rates in all six states are quite low"; Moskow: "labor markets are very tight...labor shortages are constraining business activity"; Hoenig: "labor markets are extremely tight and our unemployment levels are very low"; McTeer: "Labor markets are extremely tight in Texas"; Jordan describes acute shortages in health care ("worst she has seen in 20 years"); Broaddus: "Skilled construction workers are still in very short supply." Wage pressures are noted but not sharply accelerating yet. Direction: very tight, well above full employment. Score around 70-73.

Era anchor for 1999-2000: 65-75. Score of 72 fits well.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is described as robust/strong by nearly all participants. Broaddus: "consumer spending had been especially robust...Car sales remain at an exceptionally high level"; Guynn: "robust District retail sales"; auto sales expected to be record high (Moskow). Construction is very strong in most districts though showing some moderation. Business investment is solid. Some manufacturing weakness (Boeing, agriculture, textiles) but these are sectoral. Overall demand is described as having "substantial forward momentum" (Guynn). Direction: clearly above trend. Score around 62-65.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence supports above-trend. Score of 63 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants note recent tightening in financial conditions. Greenspan: "the BAA real rate is up just about a full percentage point since the beginning of the year. That's a fairly substantial amount of tightening." Minehan: "financial markets have been fragile and volatile of late...some widening of spreads is not unhealthy." Poole: "over the last two years...financial conditions were quite accommodative...but that situation has changed. Rates are up." Bond rates rising ahead of policy. Banks report "tightening credit standards" (McDonough re: NY district). But conditions are not severely tight -- they've moved from accommodative toward neutral/slightly tight. Direction: modestly tighter than neutral. Score around 52-55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The vast majority favor a 25bp rate increase. Only McTeer explicitly dissents ("I would prefer no rate increase"). Jordan suggests he'd prefer 50bp or a larger discount rate move. Broaddus wants an asymmetric (tightening) tilt. Most support 25bp + symmetric directive. Greenspan recommends 25bp + symmetric. The consensus is clearly hawkish but moderate -- one more 25bp hike with an expectation that more may follow. Direction: moderate hawkish. Score around 63-66.

Anchors: 60-65 for Dec 2015 liftoff-type environment. This is a hiking meeting with broad (not unanimous) support and forward signal of possible further moves. Score 64 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: McTeer is the lone voice against a rate increase. Broaddus wants asymmetric tilt but accepts symmetric. Jordan proposes 50bp or larger discount rate move but supports the 25bp consensus. There is broad agreement on the direction but some spread on tactics (tilt debate, Poole/Moskow want to drop tilt entirely). One effective dissent (McTeer), a few tactical differences. Direction: moderate. Score around 30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability is discussed but is not a dominant topic. Minehan mentions fragile/volatile markets and spread widening briefly. McDonough discusses international risks at length (Japan, China, Argentina, Brazil, Russia, Ecuador) -- but this is more international macro risk than financial stability per se. Greenspan discusses Y2K liquidity concerns in some detail. Meyer mentions equity correction risk. Ferguson mentions Y2K market skittishness. Broaddus discusses stock market correction risk. Overall, financial conditions/stability receive moderate attention as a secondary theme. Score around 35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish but deliberately restrained. Greenspan: "if it turns out that we have to move again in October, symmetry does not preclude us from doing so." Stern: "I will be mildly surprised if we don't wind up deciding that we need to move the funds rate up a bit further before too long." Meyer: bond market expects "further, though modest, increases." But the deliberate choice of symmetric directive and Y2K caution suggests a desire to pause or proceed cautiously. Gramlich: "leave financial markets alone for a while, at least until the end of the year." Direction: mildly hawkish forward lean, tempered by Y2K caution. Score around 60-63.

Coherence check with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (64): Forward guidance (62) is slightly lower because of the explicit desire to signal patience/pause after this move. Makes sense.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Several participants express uncertainty, particularly about: productivity trends (Greenspan, Stern, Parry), the international outlook (McDonough, Jordan, Greenspan on political risks), whether inflation will actually materialize (Guynn: "with some humility...we have had such forecasts for some time now, and measured inflation has continued to defy those forecasts"), and Y2K effects. Meyer notes "uncertainties about the structure of inflation dynamics and the poor forecasting record of recent years." But this is not extreme uncertainty -- most are fairly confident about the economic outlook, with uncertainty focused on inflation timing and international risks. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score around 40-45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): Labor tightness scores higher, which makes sense -- the labor market discussion is more emphatic ("extremely tight," "worst in 20 years") than the output gap discussion, which acknowledges strong growth but also notes Q2 slowdown was temporary. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (64) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Aligned -- current action is hawkish (hiking), forward signal is mildly hawkish but deliberately cautious due to Y2K. Coherent.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- Favor 25bp hike: Minehan, Moskow, Parry, Broaddus, Guynn, Hoenig, McDonough, Stern, Poole, Gramlich, Meyer, Ferguson, Kelley, Greenspan (14)
- Favor no hike: McTeer (1)
- Favor more aggressive action: Jordan (leans 50bp or larger discount rate move) (1)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the twelve-month change for the core CPI excluding tobacco...is well under 2 percent. I think it is 1.7 percent...I agree with the Vice Chair in that I believe we have arrived at price stability"
- MOSKOW: "this moderation in growth is not likely to be enough to alleviate increasing inflationary pressures...the recent data on wages, the further increase in oil prices, the rise in other commodity prices, and the weakness of the dollar all suggest less optimism about cost pressures"
- MINEHAN: "we see a bit more inflation risk, largely driven by wage costs, which do seem to be turning up a bit"
- McDONOUGH: "our view is that the inflation pressures...are in fact much better contained...I think what we have now should be called price stability"
- McTEER: "Core inflation is about as low as it was last year--significantly lower by some measures"

**Center of gravity**: Participants acknowledge current inflation is at or near price stability, but the majority perceive that favorable supply factors are dissipating and upside risks are building. The score reflects low current inflation (pulling toward 45-50) offset by widely perceived emerging pressures (pulling toward 55-60).

**Minority views**: McDonough and McTeer see inflation as well-contained with little reason for concern (dovish wing). Minehan, Moskow, Parry, and Guynn see more definitive upside inflation risks. This spread widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- KELLEY: "the consensus expectation for the second half is for growth of 3.5 percent or likely more than that, which would mark a return to a pace above a sustainable level"
- MEYER: "continued momentum in aggregate demand that is likely to support growth at or above trend"
- GUYNN: "the economy is maintaining substantial forward momentum"
- GREENSPAN: "we have a very strong economy with very marginal indications of any slowing"
- MINEHAN: "We view the second-quarter slowdown as an aberration, not the beginning of a trend"

**Center of gravity**: Near-universal view that the economy is operating above potential with growth above trend. The Q2 slowdown is dismissed as temporary. Score of 60 reflects "clearly above potential" without extreme overheating language.

**Minority views**: Some note the Q2 slowdown and agricultural/manufacturing weakness, but these are clearly minority sectors. McTeer notes GDP may come in below 2% for Q2, but even he acknowledges "the economy remains very strong."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MOSKOW: "labor markets are very tight...labor shortages are constraining business activity, either in the form of scaled-back expansion plans or business lost"
- HOENIG: "Our labor markets are extremely tight and our unemployment levels are very low"
- JORDAN: "The hiring coordinator at the Cleveland Clinic said the shortage is the worst she has seen in 20 years"
- McTEER: "Labor markets are extremely tight in Texas...in Austin and Dallas there are almost 9 jobs for every 10 residents between the ages of 16 and 65"
- GRAMLICH: "labor markets are very, very tight and...wage gains may finally be accelerating"

**Center of gravity**: Virtually every participant describes labor markets as very tight to extremely tight, with widespread shortages across industries (construction, health care, technology, retail, trucking). Unemployment rates in many districts well below 4%. Wage pressures are present but not dramatically accelerating yet -- contained partly by productivity and creative compensation strategies.

**Minority views**: Minehan notes some labor force growth easing concern; Greenspan notes the pool of job seekers is "continuing to erode" but the rate of decline has slowed. These are minor qualifications to the overwhelming consensus of extreme tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- BROADDUS: "consumer spending had been especially robust in May and June, and all reports suggest that that is continuing. Car sales remain at an exceptionally high level"
- MOSKOW: "Industry contacts continue to expect 1999 to be a record high year for sales of both light vehicles and heavy-duty trucks"
- GUYNN: "robust District retail sales. Prospects for the Christmas season look especially bright"
- GREENSPAN: "the question that is still up in the air is whether, in fact, it is an overheating economy"
- HOENIG: "Construction activity remains brisk...we do see strong domestic demand"

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending is robust across most districts, with record auto sales and strong retail. Construction is very active though showing some early moderation. Manufacturing is mixed (autos strong, textiles/agriculture weak). Business investment remains solid. Overall demand is clearly above trend.

**Minority views**: Poole reports FedEx saw "clear sign of some slowing in volume" though UPS reported "booming." McDonough's research group expects "growth recession in 2001." These are modest qualifications.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the BAA real rate is up just about a full percentage point since the beginning of the year. That's a fairly substantial amount of tightening that has taken place"
- MINEHAN: "financial markets have been fragile and volatile of late...some widening of spreads is not unhealthy"
- POOLE: "over the last two years, until the beginning of this year, financial conditions were quite accommodative...But that situation has changed. Rates are up. Money growth is slower this year"
- McDONOUGH (re: NY district): "Banks report...tightening credit standards"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have moved from accommodative to roughly neutral or slightly tight. Long rates have risen, spreads have widened modestly, and credit standards are tightening. But there is no stress -- this is viewed as a healthy normalization. The stock market remains elevated.

**Minority views**: Kelley notes the "sky-high stock market" as a risk, suggesting conditions are still loose in equities. The spread between views on financial conditions is moderate.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 64 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "I think we have to tighten by 25 basis points...we should make that move and stay with symmetry"
- McDONOUGH: "I fully support your recommendation...a move of 25 basis points is certainly a bit of insurance"
- GUYNN: "I see this as a unique opportunity to take out some additional inflation insurance at little or no cost"
- JORDAN: "My first choice...25 basis points on the funds rate and 50 basis points on the discount rate" [more hawkish]
- McTEER: "I would prefer no rate increase at this meeting" [dissent]

**Center of gravity**: Strong consensus (14 of 16 speakers) for a 25bp rate hike with symmetric directive. The action is clearly hawkish. However, many frame this as "insurance" rather than responding to realized inflation, and the symmetric directive signals no urgency for further immediate action. One dissent (McTeer) and one calling for more (Jordan).

**Minority views**: McTeer opposes hiking; Jordan and Broaddus want more aggressive posture. This creates a moderate spread.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- McTEER (dissent): "I would prefer no rate increase at this meeting"
- JORDAN (more hawkish): favors 50bp or larger discount rate increase
- BROADDUS: wants asymmetric tilt toward tightening but accepts symmetric
- Debate over tilt language (Poole, Moskow want to drop it entirely; Meyer wants "market neutral" approach)

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on the 25bp hike (14-1-1 split: 14 for 25bp, 1 for no move, 1 for more). The main disagreement is tactical -- the tilt/directive language debate. One effective dissent. Score reflects "broad agreement with minor differences."

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: extended discussion of Y2K liquidity risks and potential illiquidity problems
- McDONOUGH: lengthy discussion of international political/financial risks (Japan, China, Argentina, Brazil, Russia, Ecuador)
- BROADDUS: discusses possibility of "sharp decline in the stock market" and lessons from 1987
- KELLEY: "the sky-high stock market and the still fragile world economy and exchange rate structure"
- MEYER: "a correction in equity prices" as important contingency

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability receives moderate attention. Y2K liquidity concerns are a notable sub-theme. International financial risks are discussed by several participants. Equity market overvaluation is mentioned. But these are secondary to the main inflation/growth discussion. Not a dominant topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "if it turns out that we have to move again in October, symmetry does not preclude us from doing so"
- STERN: "I will be mildly surprised if we don't wind up deciding that we need to move the funds rate up a bit further before too long"
- GRAMLICH: "do what it has to do at this meeting and then leave financial markets alone for a while, at least until the end of the year"
- MEYER: "bond markets today still have the expectation built in that if we move today, it won't be our final move...I don't think it would be particularly useful to...remove that expectation"
- MINEHAN: "make a move and give some indication of then standing pat for a while"

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. Most expect further tightening will be needed, but the deliberate choice of symmetric directive and Y2K concerns suggest no urgency. The signal is "we've moved, we may need to move again, but not right away." This is a restrained hawkish lean.

**Minority views**: Gramlich and Minehan explicitly want to signal a pause. Stern and Parry expect further moves soon. Creates moderate uncertainty band.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GUYNN: "with some humility...we have had such forecasts for some time now, and measured inflation has continued to defy those forecasts"
- MEYER: "uncertainties about the structure of inflation dynamics and the poor forecasting record of recent years"
- GREENSPAN: "the increase in the rate of growth of productivity is going to slow down, but I don't know when"
- McDONOUGH: "the global situation is still highly problematic" with extensive discussion of political risks
- GREENSPAN: "the hardest thing to forecast is the American exchange rate"

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, focused on: (1) whether inflation will actually materialize given repeated failed forecasts, (2) productivity growth trajectory, (3) international political/economic risks, (4) Y2K effects. However, participants are fairly confident about the domestic economic outlook and the appropriate policy response. This is not deep uncertainty -- more like acknowledged unknowns within a confident framework.
