## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of an emergency intermeeting conference call held on January 21, 2008 (Martin Luther King Jr. Day, a federal holiday). Individual participants are identified by name. The document is segmented: staff-attributable content (Bill Dudley's briefing) is followed by participant statements beginning with Chairman Bernanke's framing and then a round of individual participant comments. Pages 5–31 of 32 are captured. The document is substantive (~695 lines), so confidence is generally Moderate-to-High for most dimensions.

**Edge case flag**: This IS an emergency intermeeting meeting (not one of the canonical Sept 2001/Sept 2008/March 2020 trio, but it is explicitly an unscheduled intermeeting action). The discussion is focused narrowly on the single question of whether to cut 75bp now vs. waiting 9 days for the scheduled meeting. Normal go-around economics discussion is largely absent; the meeting jumps directly to the rate action debate. This will force several dimensions to rely on brief embedded remarks rather than full economic assessments.

### 2. Era Context

January 2008, early GFC unfolding. The housing downturn is spreading to credit markets. Equity markets globally have fallen sharply (S&P 500 down ~16.5% since the prior FOMC meeting; German DAX down ~8% on the day of the call). Monoline insurance downgrades are imminent. The Fed cut 100bp from September through December 2007. The question at this meeting is whether to add 75bp more on an emergency basis, with a regularly scheduled meeting nine days away. This is the moment just before the acute phase of the GFC.
