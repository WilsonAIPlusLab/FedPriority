## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the FOMC September 25–26, 2018 meeting. Day 1 (September 25) contains the economic go-around with individual participant statements. Day 2 (September 26) contains the policy go-round. Speakers are identified by name (MR. ROSENGREN, MS. MESTER, MR. BULLARD, MS. BRAINARD, MR. QUARLES, MR. CLARIDA, MR. KAPLAN, MR. HARKER, MS. GEORGE, MR. BOSTIC, MR. KASHKARI, VICE CHAIRMAN WILLIAMS, CHAIRMAN POWELL, MR. EVANS, MR. BARKIN). The document is segmented with the policy discussion clearly labeled. Content is participant-sourced throughout.

### 2. Era Context
September 2018 is mid-to-late hiking cycle. The economy is in a strong expansion: fiscal stimulus (Tax Cuts and Jobs Act) is boosting near-term growth; the unemployment rate is near or below 4 percent; PCE inflation has just reached/returned to the 2 percent target after years of undershooting; the Fed has been hiking quarterly. The Committee is now approaching its estimate of the neutral rate. Known landmarks: 2018 Q2 GDP growth was very strong (~4%+ annualized), tariff uncertainty is mounting, emerging market turbulence (Turkey/Argentina) is noted, and financial conditions remain broadly accommodative despite hikes. I will not let this background override text evidence, but it helps interpret ambiguous passages.

### 3. Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as at or near the 2% target — neither alarming nor deflationary. Multiple speakers (Mester, Kashkari, Williams, Brainard, Clarida, Powell, Evans, Harker) describe inflation as having firmed to 2% or "around" 2%. Upside risks are noted (tariffs enabling price pass-through, tight labor market) but no one describes a clear outbreak or dangerous acceleration. Bullard and Kashkari still note lingering downside risk and an incomplete anchoring of expectations symmetrically at 2%. The center of gravity is slightly above 50 — inflation is AT target and participants perceive mild upside risk, but do not see significant pressure yet. Score: ~53–56.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Multiple participants describe growth as "above trend," "above potential," with real GDP expected around 3–3.25% in 2018 against potential of ~1.75–2%. Williams says economy "operating above potential for the remainder of this decade." Powell: "growth is strong." Mester: "growth will be above trend over 2018–2020." Brainard: "economy expanding rapidly at a time when we're already beyond most estimates of full employment." This is clearly above potential — BRIGHT-LINE: score MUST be > 50. The language "well above trend" / "growing at 3%+ vs. 1.75–2% potential" corresponds to the 59–70 range ("clearly above potential; running hot"). Score: ~63–65.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants describe the labor market as very tight. Unemployment near or below 4%, described as below NAIRU by most (Mester: District unemployment 4.4%, below natural rate; Williams: unemployment edging to below 3.5% next year — "lowest in 50 years"; George: unemployment at 20-year low; Mester: below 4% since April; widespread reports of worker shortages across skill levels and regions). This maps to 65–75 range (very tight; severe shortages mentioned by multiple participants; truck driver shortages, restaurant shortages, construction shortages, school bus driver shortages). Score: ~68–70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Multiple participants describe consumption as robust and business investment strong. Mester: "both consumption and business investment remain strong." Brainard notes "consumption and business fixed investment expected to post solid gains." Williams: growth 3% this year "well above potential." Quarles: real business fixed investment jumped 10% in first half, fastest in 6 years. Kaplan and others note consumer broadening. Caveats: housing sector weaker, some capex on hold due to tariff uncertainty, Bostic notes muted capex intentions. Net assessment: above-trend but not "boom/unsustainably rapid." Score: ~60–62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants consistently describe financial conditions as still "accommodative" or "quite accommodative" despite rate hikes. Mester: "Financial conditions remain accommodative despite the gradual path of rate increases." Brainard: "easy domestic financial conditions"; "financial conditions have remained highly accommodative." Williams: "overall financial conditions remain quite supportive of growth." George: "financial conditions overall have actually eased" even as policy tightened. Multiple indices confirm this. The rubric: 31–45 = "somewhat loose to neutral; generally supportive." Score: ~35–38. Higher = tighter; conditions are loose, so score is below midpoint.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The overwhelming majority (13+ speakers: Mester, Rosengren, Harker, Kaplan, Brainard, Quarles, Clarida, Barkin, Evans, George, Bostic, Kashkari, Williams, Powell) all support a 25bp hike at this meeting. No formal dissent from the hike. The division is about the FORWARD path: most support continued gradual increases (Mester, Rosengren, Williams, Evans, Brainard, Powell signal ongoing gradual hikes); Bullard and Harker are slightly less hawkish forward (Quarles wants only 3 hikes total in 2018, no December move; Harker has no December hike and only ~2 per year after). Kashkari supports today's hike but is the most dovish forward (argues against going beyond neutral; worried about overtightening). Bullard has historically not penciled in increases but "went along" with recent ones. Center of gravity: unanimous 25bp today + continued gradual increases ahead, with meaningful minority wanting caution as neutral approaches. This is in the 70–75 range (strong hawkish: consensus for continued tightening). Score: ~72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: The vote appears unanimous (no speaker mentions a dissent intention; Powell says "broad support" and "consensus"). However, there is notable divergence on the FORWARD path: Bullard/Harker/Kashkari/Kaplan/Quarles all want either fewer hikes than the modal view, or to pause at neutral, while Rosengren wants to go somewhat above neutral, and Mester/Williams/Evans want gradual continued increases. The divergence is primarily about "how far beyond neutral" rather than whether to hike today. This meets the 31–45 range ("moderate differences: notable differences about pace; 0–1 dissents or 2–3 reservations"). Score: ~38–42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Financial stability gets meaningful but not dominant attention. Mester devotes substantial discussion to leveraged lending, CLOs, corporate bond spreads, CRE valuations, asset prices elevated above historical norms. Brainard extensively discusses financial vulnerabilities (corporate sector low spreads, leveraged lending). Bostic echoes concerns about leveraged lending in nonbank sector. Kashkari discusses financial stability costs of rate hikes vs. capital requirements. George mentions risk to financial system. Barkin mentions "financial excesses." This is discussed by at least 6–7 participants with some depth. Rubric 40–60 = "moderate discussion; several participants address meaningfully." Score: ~48–52.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly tightening-oriented. All participants remove the "accommodative" language from the statement. The statement retains "further gradual increases in the target range" language (Evans, Mester, Rosengren, Brainard, Barkin, Williams all support this). Powell signals we are "feeling our way forward" as rates approach neutral — which is a mild moderation from the pure hawkish signal, but still pointing toward further hikes. The forward signal is: continued gradual increases expected, now approaching neutral, will reassess as data evolve. This maps to 76–85 range (clear tightening signal: further rate increases ahead), moderated by the "gradual/patient/data-dependent" emphasis and the first hint of "approaching neutral." Score: ~78.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Uncertainty is elevated but not extreme. Multiple participants cite trade/tariff uncertainty (widespread), EM contagion risks (Mester, Brainard, Williams, George, Bostic, Quarles), Brexit risk, yield-curve uncertainty (Kashkari, Bullard, Brainard), and uncertain r* estimates (universal). Bullard specifically about Taylor rule uncertainty. Powell: "risks to this outlook roughly balanced" suggests no single dominant uncertainty, but multiple risks cited. Kashkari: r* uncertainty "elevated." Mester discusses "considerable uncertainty" around r*. This is moderately elevated uncertainty — typical of a late-cycle environment with geopolitical risks. Rubric 31–45 ("somewhat elevated: certain factors harder to assess than usual"). Score: ~43–47.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 54 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester: "The rates of both headline and core PCE inflation are at our longer-run goal of 2 percent... Inflation expectations are well anchored near target."
- Kashkari: "Core PCE inflation is now at 2 percent on a yearly basis. This is a milestone; I welcome it."
- Rosengren: "Suppliers may now feel they have more price flexibility — posing an upside risk to the inflation forecast." [tariffs giving firms pricing power]
- Williams: "Inflation appears to be settling in at our 2 percent longer-run objective... there's little if any evidence of substantial inflation pressures building."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Inflation has just returned to target after years below; participants welcome it, perceive mild upside risks from tight labor markets and tariff pass-through, but no outbreak. Score is slightly above 50 reflecting the "at target with upside risks" framing rather than the "below target" framing that dominated earlier in 2017–2018.

**Minority views:** Bullard and Evans note inflation expectations not yet fully symmetrically anchored, implying some residual downside risk. Brainard emphasizes importance of sustainably achieving 2%, acknowledging uncertainty. These shade slightly toward a lower score.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 64 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I expect real GDP growth to come in around 3 percent this year and 2½ percent next year, well above my estimate of potential growth of 1¾ percent... the economy operating above potential for the remainder of this decade."
- Mester: "I'm projecting growth will be above trend over the 2018 to 2020 period, a tad over 3 percent this year."
- Brainard: "The economy is continuing to expand rapidly at a time when we're already beyond most estimates of full employment... fiscal stimulus in the pipeline will likely keep the economy growing at an above-trend pace into next year."
- Powell: "Growth is strong... both the staff projections and our SEP submissions show an upward revision for growth to a bit above 3 percent this year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Essentially universal agreement that the economy is running clearly above trend/potential, with growth of 3%+ against potential of ~1.75–2%, warranting a score in the 61–68 range.

**Minority views:** Kashkari argues the economy may not be as far above potential as it seems, given limited wage/price acceleration. Harker cites GDPplus showing ½pp lower growth. Bullard notes historical context ("3% is just average historically"). These moderate the score toward the lower end of the above-potential range.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 69 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester: "More than half of the District contacts are reporting their demand for workers is outstripping available supply... The unemployment rate has been below 4 percent since April. Across a range of measures, there is less slack in the labor market than at the peak of the previous expansion."
- Williams: "Even with the labor market already reaching or even surpassing most measures of full employment, job growth has averaged over 190,000 jobs per month... roughly double the 75,000–100,000 job estimates of trend labor supply growth."
- Barkin: "Quits are back to 2000 levels... a chicken plant actually reduced the number of shifts because of inability to find workers... Trucking shortages are everywhere."
- George: "District unemployment is at its lowest level since 2000... job openings hit a new record high and outnumbered the unemployed for the fifth consecutive month."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal description of an exceptionally tight labor market with widespread worker shortages across industries, geographic areas, and skill levels; unemployment well below NAIRU estimates; consistent with a score in the upper portion of the 66–80 range.

**Minority views:** Kashkari and Brainard note prime-age LFP still ~1pp below pre-recession peak, suggesting some remaining slack. Kashkari: "ongoing strong job growth and modest nominal wage growth suggest we have yet to reach full employment." This moderates the score away from extreme tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 61 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester: "Both consumption and business investment remain strong. Indeed, despite uncertainty about trade and tariff policies, consumers and businesses are quite optimistic."
- Quarles: "Real business fixed investment jumped 10 percent in the first half of this year, the fastest pace in six years... leading indicators of capital spending are strong."
- Kaplan: "We've been struck since the previous meeting about reports of broader strength of the U.S. consumer among retailers... consumer strength is now across a much broader range of stores."
- Evans: "Activity is robust, and businesses are optimistic about their near-term prospects."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumption and business investment are described as strong and above trend. Offsetting factors: housing slowdown (higher mortgage rates), some capex on hold pending tariff resolution, and Bostic's observation that capex plans are not materially ramping up. Net: solidly above trend but not "booming unsustainably."

**Minority views:** Bostic: "businesses in general are not yet in the mindset to take on aggressive expansion plans"; capex plans "maintained" rather than increased. Housing weakness (Harker, Bullard, Mester) acts as a partial offset. Harker notes GDPplus growth about ½pp weaker than GDP. These pull the score away from the 65+ range.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester: "Financial conditions remain accommodative despite the gradual path of rate increases the Committee has put in place... lending terms and standards have eased in many markets."
- Brainard: "Since we began the tightening cycle, financial conditions have remained highly accommodative, according to most of the major indexes."
- George: "As policy accommodation has diminished, financial conditions overall have actually eased, as illustrated by a number of financial condition indexes."
- Williams: "Overall financial conditions remain quite supportive of growth."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Despite eight rate hikes in the current cycle, financial conditions are described uniformly as still accommodative to very accommodative — equity markets near highs, credit spreads tight, lending terms loose. This is clearly below midpoint (loose conditions).

**Minority views:** No participant argues conditions have tightened materially. Kashkari and Bullard note EM stress but as contained. The only upward pressure on this score is the passage of time and rising rates, not participant assessments of current conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 72 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester: "I support increasing the federal funds rate target range 25 basis points today... I expect further rate increases will be warranted to sustain the expansion."
- Rosengren: "My baseline forecast has quarterly increases in the federal funds rate for the next year and a half... rates will need to continue to gradually rise until we are mildly restrictive."
- Powell: "I see a strong case for a 25 basis point increase at this meeting... the further gradual increases in the federal funds rate are envisioned in our draft statement."
- Bullard: "I support alternative B for today... the removal of the 'accommodative' language sets the Committee up to pause if desired, but not necessarily."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants support the 25bp hike today; the vast majority support continued gradual increases through at least neutral. A meaningful minority (Harker, Quarles, Kashkari, Bullard, Kaplan) want to pause at or near neutral and are more cautious about going further. The "strong hawkish" range (71–80) fits: consensus for continued tightening with some voices urging patience.

**Minority views:** Kashkari is most dovish on the forward path: argues against overshooting neutral absent inflation uptick; wants to drop "further gradual increases" language. Harker also only pencils in ~2 hikes/year going forward. Bullard argues "pause" may be appropriate. These pull toward 65–68 range; the majority's stance keeps it at 72.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Powell at close: "I hear broad support for a 25 basis point increase... and also consensus for removing the 'accommodative' language."
- No participant states a dissent intention on the vote.
- Quarles explicitly wants only 3 hikes in 2018 (not 4): "my utopia... three rate increases this year and not one in December."
- Kashkari: actively argues against moving beyond neutral — "I would encourage us to drop the language soon about 'further gradual increases.'"
- Bullard: argues for considering an alternative monetary policy strategy (price-level targeting, asymmetric loss function); has historically not penciled in rate increases.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Zero formal dissents from the 25bp hike; strong consensus on removing "accommodative" language. However, meaningful divergence on the forward path exists between those who want continued regular quarterly hikes (Mester, Rosengren, Williams) and those who want to pause at or near neutral (Kashkari, Bullard, Harker, Kaplan, Quarles). This places the score in the 31–45 range.

**Minority views:** Bullard's broader argument that the Taylor rule is no longer appropriate — and his advocacy for price-level targeting and the asymmetric loss function — represents a framework-level disagreement that elevates the dissent intensity slightly.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 50 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mester [extensive]: "Increased attention to financial stability risk is warranted... Asset valuations and equity are elevated above historical norms... leveraged lending is growing. The value of institutional leveraged loans has risen 20 percent over the past year and now exceeds the total value of the high-yield bond market. Underwriting standards are loosening."
- Brainard: "Financial vulnerabilities are indeed building, especially in the corporate sector, in which low spreads and loosening credit terms are mirrored by rising indebtedness. Leveraged lending once again on the rise."
- Bostic: "I want to echo concerns raised by President Mester and Governor Brainard regarding the increase in leveraged lending, particularly in the nonbank sector."
- Kashkari: Devotes extended remarks to financial stability tradeoff: "raising rates is many times more costly to the real economy than raising bank capital."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives explicit, substantive attention from at least 5–6 participants, with particular focus on leveraged lending, loose credit conditions, and asset valuations. This is moderate-to-prominent discussion, consistent with the 40–60 range. It does not dominate the meeting (real economy and policy path take more airtime) but is a recurring theme.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 78 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Statement language (supported by all): Retains "further gradual increases in the federal funds rate will be consistent with sustained expansion" language.
- Mester: "I expect further rate increases will be warranted... my policy rate path includes further federal funds rate increases this year and next, with the policy rate holding at a level somewhat above my longer-run estimate."
- Rosengren: "My baseline forecast has quarterly increases in the federal funds rate for the next year and a half... rates will need to continue to gradually rise until we are mildly restrictive."
- Williams: "I anticipate that it'll be appropriate to raise the target funds rate to a level moderately above my assessment of its longer-run value."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly tightening — further hikes to and beyond neutral are the dominant expectation. The "gradual" and "data-dependent" qualifiers, and the removal of "accommodative" language (designed to allow flexibility), prevent the score from going higher (above 85). Score sits in the 76–85 range.

**Minority views:** Kashkari wants to remove "further gradual increases" language entirely to signal true data dependence. Quarles and Harker want fewer hikes. This pulls away from 85+ territory.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Minority views:** Most participants describe their own outlooks as broadly unchanged or having modest upward revisions — suggesting the uncertainty is about tail risks, not the central forecast. Williams explicitly says "uncertainty associated with my projections is aligned with historical norms."
