## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the May 1–2, 2018 FOMC meeting. It is well-segmented with individually identified speakers (MR. WILLIAMS, MS. MESTER, MR. BULLARD, etc.). The meeting covers three distinct segments: (1) a financial stability go-round led by Gov. Quarles, Pres. Rosengren, Pres. Kaplan, Pres. George, and Gov. Brainard; (2) an economic go-round with all participants; and (3) a monetary policy discussion. The document is rich and long (~185 pages), with many participants speaking at length.

### 2. Era Context
May 2018 is late in the 2015–2018 gradual tightening cycle. The Fed had raised rates multiple times since December 2015. The labor market is near or beyond full employment by most measures (U-rate ~4.1%). Inflation (core PCE) has recently risen to ~1.9–2.0%, nearly hitting the 2% target for the first time since 2011. Fiscal stimulus (tax cuts + spending) is a significant new tailwind. Trade policy uncertainty (tariffs, potential trade war with China) is a prominent new risk. The economy is growing above trend. This is NOT a crisis, recession, or emergency context.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Core Dimensions

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as essentially AT target (just arriving), with some upside risk.
- Core PCE at 1.9%, headline at 2.0% (Williams, Quarles, Mester, Bullard, many others).
- Bullard: "this is about the best that can be achieved" at 2% headline PCE.
- Evans, Kashkari: inflation expectations "remain somewhat below levels consistent with our symmetric 2 percent target" — a downside concern.
- Brainard, Dudley: caution about declaring victory; some transitory factors inflating the recent print.
- Dudley: stripping out transitory components leaves core PCE unchanged year-over-year at ~1.51%.
- Bullard: Dallas trimmed mean at 1.8%, flat year-over-year, suggesting underlying inflation may be below the headline 2%.
- Input cost pressures (steel, aluminum, freight, oil) widely cited; some pass-through beginning.
- Center of gravity: inflation is AT target but just barely arriving; not clearly entrenched above it; risks noted both ways. Score around 50–53. The text strongly supports a "near target" reading — slightly above 50 because inflation is arriving at target after years below, with upside input cost pressures, but Dudley/Bullard/Brainard cautions prevent going much higher.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as operating ABOVE potential.
- Williams: "GDP growth this year and next to average about 2½ percent, well above potential."
- Rosengren: "Q1 GDP growth was faster than my estimate of potential growth... real GDP for the remainder of this year and all of next year will grow more rapidly than potential GDP."
- Barkin: "Output growth is above trend."
- Brainard: "economy is at above-trend economic growth."
- Powell summary: "above-trend growth."
- Bostic: "economy is close to or at full employment" — slightly more cautious, but doesn't say below potential.
- Kashkari dissent: questions whether "well above full employment" is plausible given modest wages — but still says economy near full employment.
- BRIGHT-LINE RULE: "above potential" language cited by Williams, Rosengren, Barkin, Brainard, Powell → score MUST be > 50. Multiple participants describe GDP running 2.3–3% vs. potential of ~1.75–2%. Score 57–62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as clearly TIGHT.
- Williams: "worker shortages have become a binding constraint"; unemployment rate heading to 3.5%.
- Rosengren: unemployment rate "more than a full percentage point below my estimate of full employment."
- Evans: "Labor markets, obviously, are tight."
- Mester: "District unemployment rate fell to 4.4 percent... its lowest level since 2001."
- Harker: "Labor markets remain quite healthy."
- Kashkari: minority view — still thinks there may be slack, prime-age EPOP still 1–2pp below prior peaks; "I don't find plausible the Tealbook view that we are running well above full employment."
- Bullard: Phillips curve effects very weak, suggesting labor market tightness doesn't translate to inflation pressure as expected.
- Center of gravity: broadly tight (U-rate 4.1%, below NAIRU), shortages widely reported. Multiple participants say 3.5–3.6% unemployment expected. Score: 62–67 range — clearly tight but with meaningful minority skepticism about extent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed — business investment strong, consumer spending soft in Q1 but seen as temporary.
- Williams: "Consumer and business confidence is high."
- Brainard: Q1 consumption posted "anemic growth of 1.1 percent"; business investment "continued last year's impressive gains."
- Harker: "Consumption activity continues to be somewhat lackluster" but sees it as temporary.
- Barkin: "underlying growth in this economy really is strong" despite soft Q1 PCE.
- Most participants look through Q1 consumer weakness as temporary; expect rebound in Q2.
- Business investment cited as solid; cap-ex plans strong except where trade uncertainty is paralyzing pipeline projects (Bostic: firms "moving to the sidelines").
- Kaplan: consumer spending bifurcated — top 20% healthy, rest "standing pat."
- Overall NET assessment: at-to-above trend, with consumer softness being temporary. Score ~53–57 — solid but not booming.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Conditions have tightened modestly from very accommodative, but remain broadly supportive.
- Brainard: "financial conditions have tightened somewhat... moving more in line with expectations of continued gradual increases." 10-year yields up 60bp, crossed 3%; equities flat/down marginally since March.
- Quarles: "Financial conditions have tightened a little bit since our previous meeting, but not in a way that's really particularly surprising."
- Powell summary: "financial conditions have tightened modestly."
- Dudley: "Our tightening moves have not appreciably tightened financial market conditions. They've tightened a little bit."
- Brainard financial stability: "asset valuations across a range of markets remain elevated... Corporate bond yields remain very low. Spreads on junk bonds and leveraged loans... near the low end of their historical range."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Broad consensus for holding rates steady at THIS meeting with strong expectation of June hike; most support continued gradual hiking.

- Mester: "gradual rate increases... remains appropriate"; "burden of proof will lie on delaying a rate increase, not on taking one."
- Rosengren: wants "at least three more tightenings this year, not two" — more hawkish than median.
- Harker: "becoming more confident that a policy that raises the funds rate four times this year may be appropriate."
- Evans: "two or three additional rate hikes this year"; data-dependent on pace; gradual.

- Brainard, Kashkari: most cautious — gradual, data-dependent, not accelerating; Kashkari comfortable "moving to neutral" but not beyond.
- Bullard: explicitly more dovish — supports Alternative B "for quite a while into the future"; inflation expectations still too low; wants to let expectations firm before tightening further.

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence**:
