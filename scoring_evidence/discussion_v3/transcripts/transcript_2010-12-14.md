## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of the December 14, 2010 FOMC meeting. It contains clearly identified individual speakers (President, Governor, Chairman) giving go-around statements followed by a policy discussion. The transcript is segmented by speaker. The document is long (~4,000+ lines) and covers a full two-round discussion with numerous participants. Confidence can be High for all well-discussed dimensions. Staff content (Mr. English, Mr. Moore acting as First VP San Francisco stand-in) is excluded from member scoring where it presents policy alternatives; however, First VP Moore's economic go-around remarks are treated as participant remarks.

### 2. Era Context

December 2010 is in the early QE2 era. The FOMC had launched the second round of large-scale asset purchases (QE2: $600 billion) at the November 2010 meeting. The economy was recovering from the Great Recession (officially ended June 2009) but unemployment was elevated near 9.8%. Core PCE inflation was running around 0.6–1.0% — well below the 2% target. The Fed funds rate was at the zero lower bound (0–0.25%). This is textbook QE-era deep-dovish context, but with important nuances: there is meaningful internal dissent (Hoenig, Plosser, Lacker, Fisher) about the QE2 program, and the near-term data had improved modestly from the prior meeting.

### 3. Directional Signals by Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants overwhelmingly describe inflation as below target and still declining. Multiple speakers: Rosengren — "core CPI inflation rate for the last 12 months, at 0.6 percent, is the lowest since 1957"; Pianalto — "core PCE inflation measures fell sharply"; Evans — "underlying trend in core PCE inflation is likely closer to 1 percent"; Moore (SF First VP) — "measures of core inflation…running below 1 percent…inflation is on a clear downward trend"; Yellen — "inflation still seems to be moving downward"; Bernanke — "Core inflation measures continue to drift down." There is near-unanimity that inflation is meaningfully BELOW target. A few note commodity price increases and rising inflation expectations (TIPS breakevens recovering), but explicitly argue pass-through is limited due to slack. Hoenig is the outlier noting more concern about future inflation risks from QE2. Direction: well BELOW 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is described as being in recovery but still far below potential. Bernanke: "A remarkable statistic — unemployment is, after all, 9.8 percent, 0.3 percentage point higher than it was in June 2009 when the recession officially ended"; "we are starting from a very deep hole"; "we remain far from our objectives." Evans: "inflation is too far below our mandate and resource slack remains very large." Pianalto: "employment-to-population ratio…fallen from 63 percent" substantially. Multiple speakers note "substantial slack." Output growth in 2010 was under trend. Direction: well BELOW 50, consistent with 2010-era anchor of 35–42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 9.8%. Participants emphasize: "labor markets are still struggling for sustained gains" (Pianalto); "quit rate still nearly 20 percent below previous levels" (Tarullo); "employment-to-population ratio for teenagers is now 27 percent" (Bernanke); Tarullo — "it seems hard to imagine a sustained or increasingly strong recovery without a significant decrease in unemployment." Hoenig acknowledges improving, but consensus is labor market remains slack. Direction: well BELOW 50, around 30–35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but leaning below trend. Positive signals: strong holiday retail (several district reports), consumer confidence improving, positive auto sales, equipment and software spending up. Negative: housing in "deep hole," business investment cautious ("no business wants to overcommit"), credit card debt falling, loan demand weak. Bernanke summarizes: "A moderate and apparently more sustainable recovery is continuing and may accelerate in 2011 and 2012." The tone is cautious improvement — below trend but recovering. Direction: slightly BELOW 50, around 38–42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This requires careful parsing. Financial *conditions* are described as: accommodative policy (zero rates), but with a notable backup in longer-term Treasury yields since November 3 FOMC. Several note that loan spreads remain "unusually wide" for small businesses (Duke), credit availability still constrained for smaller/weaker borrowers, small business loan demand weak. However, broader financial markets are recovering: equity prices up, risk spreads narrowed, improving CMBS market. The overall tone is that conditions are somewhat loose/accommodative (QE in place, zero rates), but not uniformly easy — credit for small businesses and mortgages remains difficult. Net position: financial conditions are broadly accommodative/loose. Direction: BELOW 50 (somewhat loose), around 30–38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is an active QE2 meeting — the vote is on staying the course at $600 billion. 10 of 11 voting members voted Yes for alternative B (continue QE2 at current pace). Only Hoenig dissented. Many participants express the view that more easing could be warranted if conditions deteriorate, and some (Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren) discuss tools for additional accommodation. Plosser, Fisher, Lacker, and Warsh express reservations (some want C or D) but accept B. Direction: Firmly DOVISH. During QE debates, the rubric specifies the presence of QE discussion IS the dovish signal (15–25 range). With 10/11 supporting QE continuation and some open to more: around 18–22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Hoenig, voting No). But there is notable disagreement beyond that: Plosser "strongly" believes the program carries more risk than benefit; Lacker supports alternative C; Fisher "not a fan of QE2," accepts B reluctantly; Warsh accepts B but with reservations; Dudley publicly criticizes colleagues' communications. Additionally, there is notable disagreement about the *mechanism* of QE (Bullard vs. others), conditionality (Kocherlakota), and exit strategy. The rubric anchor for 2010–11 QE2 debates is 55–65. With 1 formal dissent but 3–4 clear reservations and meaningful camps: around 50–58.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability receives moderate-to-prominent discussion: Hoenig extensively discusses agricultural land price boom and asset price concerns; multiple participants discuss European sovereign crisis (nearly every speaker mentions it); Dudley covers municipal bond market, strategic mortgage defaults, bank credit rating risks; Bernanke notes agricultural land prices may indicate broader asset valuation concerns; Duke covers banking conditions in detail. This is well above passing mention. Direction: MODERATE-HIGH, around 55–65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee is maintaining "extended period" language for the federal funds rate (near zero) and staying the course on $600B QE2. Evans advocates for even stronger forward guidance (calendar date on "extended period," explicit inflation objectives). Bullard advocates removing explicit $600B reference to allow more flexibility. Bernanke supports current "extended period" language. Overall signal: maintaining strong easing commitment, with some discussion of strengthening it. Direction: strongly DOVISH, around 10–18 (strong easing signal, "extended period" language).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants note elevated uncertainty: European sovereign crisis (mentioned by essentially all speakers), housing outlook uncertain, exit strategy uncertainty, uncertainty about QE2 transmission mechanisms, Warsh emphasizes uncertainty about longer-term Treasury yields. However, the near-term economic outlook has clarified since summer — data improving. The uncertainty is concentrated around tail risks (Europe) and policy mechanism uncertainty rather than the entire outlook. Direction: ELEVATED but not extreme, around 52–60.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 22 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "core CPI inflation rate for the last 12 months, at 0.6 percent, is the lowest since 1957 when the index began. The diffusion index and a wide variety of inflation measures provide ample evidence of continued disinflation."
- Moore (SF First VP): "measures of core inflation…running below 1 percent…inflation is on a clear downward trend, and measures of underlying inflation are running below 1 percent. Many of our business contacts report rising prices of materials…but the economic climate does not appear to support much pass-through."
- Yellen: "the data on core inflation have once again surprised to the downside, and wage increases remain very well contained. Even if temporary factors are partly responsible…the pace of core inflation still seems to be moving downward."
- Bernanke summary: "core inflation measures continue to drift down with the slowdown in price increases quite well spread across categories."

**Center of gravity:** Near-unanimity that inflation is well below the 2% PCE target and still declining. Core PCE at ~0.6–1.0%, lowest in decades. Inflation expectations have risen somewhat (TIPS breakevens) but from very low levels and not alarming. The score of 22 is consistent with the "meaningful deflation risk; well below target; subdued, muted" band (11–25) and fits the 2009–2010 anchor range (15–20 for 2009 deflation fears, somewhat higher here as deflation risk has receded somewhat since summer but still substantial).

**Minority views:** Hoenig and Plosser express concern about future inflation from QE, but their concern is *prospective* and about the risk of policy being too accommodative, not about current inflation being elevated. The rubric instructs to score what participants perceive NOW — current inflation is viewed as extremely subdued by all participants.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 32 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bernanke: "unemployment is, after all, 9.8 percent, which is 0.3 percentage point higher than it was in June 2009 when the recession officially ended…we are still missing our objectives by a wide margin…we are starting from a very deep hole, and we remain far from our objectives."
- Evans: "inflation is too far below our mandate and resource slack remains very large, and I don't see either of these conditions changing very soon."
- Kocherlakota: "my preferred estimate of the output gap is near minus 4 percent, rather than the Tealbook estimate of minus 6½."

**Center of gravity:** The economy is firmly below potential. Recovery is ongoing but modest, with Tealbook projecting unemployment remaining above 9% through at least 2011. The 35–42 anchor range for 2010–14 recovery fits; the December 2010 meeting is at the earlier end of recovery where the hole was still very deep. Score 32 (closer to the lower end of 31–40 "below potential; recovering but gap persists; slack remains") reflects that the gap was still quite large — participants describe being "far from objectives" despite improving data.

**Minority views:** None argued the economy was at or above potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 30 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bernanke: "employment-to-population ratio…has fallen from 63 percent to 58 percent…about a 7 percent decline. And that low point was in October of 2010. We have not made any progress in that particular statistic."
- Tarullo: "quits [are] still nearly 20 percent below previous levels…Until the gap grows a good deal more, it will be hard to conclude that the labor market is returning to some semblance of normality."
- Pianalto: "employers are inclined to hire on a temporary, part-time, or special project basis…the unemployment rate, even among young college graduates, is still nearly double its pre-recession levels."

**Center of gravity:** Unemployment at 9.8%, employment-to-population ratio at multi-decade lows, quit rate severely depressed, employers reluctant to hire full-time. Labor market is clearly in the "high unemployment; significant slack; far from full employment" territory (16–30). Score 30 reflects modest improvement from trough (~2009 at 15–22) but still well in depressed territory. The 35–40 anchor for 2011–14 applies to the later recovery; December 2010 was still near the worst levels.

**Minority views:** None argued labor markets were tight.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 40 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Positive signals: Lockhart — "Recent reports from contacts in the Sixth District were a little more optimistic…post-Thanksgiving receipts beat retailer expectations, in some cases by a good margin." Lacker — "steady expansion in real personal consumption expenditures…[growth rate] has averaged more than 3 percent over the last four months." Bullard — "business contacts in the Eighth District are cautiously optimistic about prospects for 2011."
- Negative signals: Pianalto — "recent progress in retail sales has been in the high end of the market…many of the mid-market households are shopping down in the value chain." Moore — "homebuilders in the West remain very downbeat…no signs of pent-up demand." Dudley — "the housing sector is under significant pressure, and most house-price indexes are showing renewed weakness."
- Bernanke summary: "consumer confidence and spending, including discretionary spending, have been stronger…purchases of durable goods are up. But the pace of hiring remains slow…deleveraging in the household sector is continuing."

**Center of gravity:** Consumer spending is recovering but below trend — concentrated at upper-income households, mid-market cautious. Business investment in equipment/software up, but no broad capex surge. Housing is a major drag. The overall picture is "below trend; sluggish; cautious consumers" (31–45), with the improving holiday data putting this toward the upper end of that range. Score 40 reflects genuine recovery momentum but incomplete and uneven.

**Minority views:** Bullard was somewhat more optimistic, seeing 2011 as potentially stronger. Rosengren was more cautious, concerned recovery remains "quite fragile."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 35 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Duke: "Survey of Terms of Business Lending indicates that interest rates have come down, but that spreads are still unusually wide. Every community bank I called was using floors on most new and renewed loans…small business borrowers are likely facing pricing equivalent to a 2 percent fed funds rate."
- Raskin: "equity prices are higher, the yield curve is steeper, risk spreads have narrowed. I read these financial indicators as implying that financial markets could be recovering from their nervous conditions of this spring."
- Dudley: "credit availability continues to improve and that households are more willing to open up their wallets."
- Bernanke: "Financial conditions during the intermeeting period have been mixed…there has been a significant backup in longer-term rates arising from a number of factors."

**Center of gravity:** Broad financial conditions are accommodative: zero federal funds rate, ongoing QE, equity prices rising, risk spreads narrowing since the summer. But conditions are not uniformly easy: small business credit remains constrained with wide spreads, mortgage refinancing difficult for many, housing credit very tight. The rise in longer-term Treasury yields since November 3 is a partial tightening. Net: somewhat loose to neutral, closer to the "somewhat loose to neutral; generally supportive" range (31–45). Score 35 reflects the broadly accommodative QE/zero-rate environment modestly offset by the yield backup and credit constraints at the margin.

**Minority views:** Hoenig sees excessive accommodation potentially stoking asset price bubbles (farmland, others). Duke and others note the credit availability improvement.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 20 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Vote: 10 Yes (continue QE2 at $600B), 1 No (Hoenig). Not a single voting member supported scaling back or stopping.
- Evans: "Inflation is too far below our mandate and resource slack remains very large…I do support alternative B." Open to stronger forward guidance and additional tools.
- Rosengren: "I remain concerned that disinflationary pressures may not abate quickly…I can easily envision circumstances in which we would want to expand our large-scale asset purchases next year."

**Center of gravity:** The center of gravity is firmly dovish. QE2 is ongoing, rates are at zero with "extended period" guidance, 10/11 vote to maintain. Several participants are explicitly open to more accommodation if needed. Per the rubric, "participants arguing 'how much more to buy' are in the 15–25 range." Score 20 reflects strong dovish lean with maintenance of QE2 and openness to expansion, but not quite at the emergency-launch level (2–5).

**Minority views:** Hoenig (formal dissent), Plosser (accepts B reluctantly, "strongly believes" QE carries more risk), Lacker (prefers C — scaling back), Fisher (accepts B but not a fan), Warsh (accepts B, focused on post-June question). These 4–5 participants would score closer to 40–50, but they represent the minority.

**Score: 14 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 57 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Warsh: "The most important development since we last met is the change, level, and uncertainty with respect to longer-term Treasuries. The 10-year Treasury seems to have awakened from its slumber, and I suspect that we will be talking about the uncertainty in the rate increasingly going forward."

Score 57 reflects "elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty" — European tail risks are prominent but the main recovery track is clearer than during 2009.
