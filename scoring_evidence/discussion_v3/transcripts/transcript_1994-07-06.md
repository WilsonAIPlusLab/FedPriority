# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1994-07-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers both the go-around economic discussion (July 5 evening) and the policy discussion (July 6 morning). Speakers include: Parry, Boehne, Forrestal, Hoenig, Melzer, McTeer, Broaddus, Stern, McDonough, Jordan, Lindsey, LaWare, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, and Chairman Greenspan. A lengthy mid-section discusses monetary aggregate ranges and 1996 projections (procedural), which I weight less for economic scoring.

### Era Context
Mid-1994: The Fed began tightening in February 1994 from a 3% funds rate, having raised 125bp by this meeting. The economy was in a vigorous expansion following the 1990-91 recession. Unemployment had fallen to 6%. Inflation was around 2.7-3% CPI. This is a classic "approaching capacity" period — the economy was growing above trend and the question was whether slack remained.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfortable levels but not dramatically so. The center of gravity acknowledges: (1) commodity/input price pressures are rising (construction materials, chemicals, metals), (2) CPI has accelerated from ~1.8% to ~2.7% since September '93, (3) firms cannot generally pass through input costs to final goods prices yet, (4) wage pressures are contained except in pockets (construction, entry-level). Melzer is the most alarmed ("very uneasy about possible path of future inflation"), Broaddus cites purchasing managers' price index doubling. But Stern says "no widespread signs of building wage and price pressures," Blinder sees a "three bears economy," and Boehne says "price pressures appear to be contained." The center is that inflation is somewhat above desired levels with upside risks, but final goods prices are still contained. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score ~57. Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate pressure with upside risk. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is growing above potential according to most participants. Melzer: "economy is still growing at an unsustainably high rate," capacity utilization at 83.5%, unemployment down to 6%. Hoenig: "growth should remain slightly above potential in the second half." Multiple districts report "very good" or "very strong" conditions. Broaddus: "upside" risks. Greenspan: economy "still moving ahead at a fairly good pace" but slowing. Phillips and Kelley note approaching full utilization. Blinder sees it as balanced — "not too hot, not too cold." The bright-line rule says "above potential" language requires >50. Most participants say above or at potential with some slowing. Direction: modestly above potential. Score ~55. Several participants note slowing but from above-potential rates.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6%, which multiple participants note is at or near the bottom of NAIRU estimates. Scattered labor shortages reported (construction, truck drivers, entry-level workers, skilled workers). Melzer: District unemployment at 4.8%, lowest in 20 years. Jordan: apparel workers recalled, Toyota adding 800 workers. Stern: "shortages of labor—in construction trades clearly." But Blinder: "fewer reports of tight labor markets than one would expect if we are sitting exactly at the natural rate." Wage pressures are contained except in spots. Phillips: "significantly different labor conditions in different parts of the country." Center of gravity: labor market is near full employment with emerging tightness in spots but not broadly tight. Score ~52-54.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending is moderating — several districts report slowing retail sales, trimmed expectations. Forrestal: "retailers disappointed by May performance." But auto sales constrained partly by low inventories (supply, not demand). Business investment is strong — capital spending announcements, manufacturing capacity expansion, construction vigorous. Housing moderating due to higher mortgage rates. Jordan: "explosive growth in high-tech." McTeer: capacity constraints in several industries. Overall: spending is solid to moderately above trend, with consumer side softening but investment robust. Score ~55-57.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term rates have risen substantially (30-year up 200bp from October lows). Mortgage rates rising, affecting housing. Phillips notes "significant turmoil" in markets since February tightening but says "market performance has been quite good" with "no major U.S. casualties." Commercial real estate financing "not particularly easy to obtain" (Forrestal). Credit has expanded overall. Refinancings dried up. Conditions are somewhat tighter than neutral due to the rate backup. Score ~56-58.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The decision is alternative B (no change) with asymmetric toward tightening. Greenspan recommends holding but with asymmetric directive. Broaddus and Melzer want immediate 50bp hike. Most support holding for now but acknowledge more tightening likely needed. McTeer, Boehne, Forrestal prefer pause. Blinder sees risks as balanced but supports asymmetry. Jordan prefers symmetric but won't dissent. The center: hold now, but lean hawkish — most expect to tighten further. This is a moderate hawkish lean — not acting now but with clear bias. Score ~60-63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broaddus and Melzer clearly want to move now (50bp). LaWare wants to pause ("play the role of Cassandra"). Blinder sees balanced risks. Stern prefers symmetric. There's a range from "move 50bp now" (Broaddus, Melzer) to "risks are balanced" (Forrestal, Blinder) to "take a breather" (LaWare). The vote achieves "B asymmetric" with a "modest majority" for asymmetry (Greenspan's words), suggesting notable but not extreme disagreement. Score ~40-45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Phillips gives significant attention to financial market conditions and turmoil. The dollar is discussed extensively — Greenspan devotes much of his policy statement to exchange rate considerations. But the focus is more on exchange rates as a policy signal than systemic stability per se. Phillips notes markets "weathered" turmoil well. Overall moderate attention. Score ~30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric directive signals likely further tightening. Greenspan: "I have a suspicion that if we were to do nothing until August, we might possibly want to do 50 basis points in August." McDonough: "entirely possible...we would in fact wish to make a tightening move before the next meeting." Multiple participants expect further tightening. The forward signal is clearly hawkish — further tightening expected. Score ~68-72.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Several participants express genuine uncertainty. Phillips: "we all seem to be struggling with this question as to whether we are below, at, or above capacity." Greenspan: the data are consistent with "either going back down or going up." Boehne: "transition period" with "more than the usual amount of conflicting data." Phillips: "significantly different labor conditions" making it hard to read. But uncertainty is not extreme — most have a view, just with caveats. Score ~42-48.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both modestly above midpoint — coherent. Economy near/slightly above potential, labor near full employment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action — coherent because they held but signaled future tightening. The gap is explained by the pause-but-plan-to-tighten posture.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- Clearly hawkish (want to move now or soon): Melzer, Broaddus, Hoenig, McTeer (future), Parry, McDonough = ~6
- Moderate/hold-but-lean-hawkish: Greenspan, Kelley, Phillips, Boehne, Minehan, Blinder = ~6
- More balanced/cautious: Forrestal, Stern, LaWare, Jordan = ~4
- The center of gravity is hold-now-but-tighten-further — lean hawkish.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "most inflation measures...have accelerated, including broad inflation measures...since September the CPI has risen at a 2.7 percent annual rate, up from the 1.8 percent registered from April to September 1993"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Price pressures appear to be contained. There is more talk of rising input prices, but most people still feel it's quite difficult to pass on those increases"
- MR. PARRY: "I have been impressed pleasantly by recent inflation results...I think it's possible that CPI inflation will come in at or even a little below 3 percent...I do, however, think that most if not all of the slack in the economy probably has been used up and that the longer-term inflation outlook is a significant concern."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see inflation as modestly above desired levels — input/commodity prices are rising and CPI has accelerated, but final goods prices are still contained by competition, placing current inflation pressure somewhat above neutral with upside risks.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Broaddus see inflation risks as very clearly tilted upward (would push toward 65-70). Stern and Blinder are more sanguine (would push toward 48-52).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "the economy is still growing at an unsustainably high rate...capacity utilization at a relatively high level of 83-1/2 percent...civilian unemployment rate is down...to a relatively low 6 percent"
- MR. HOENIG: "we expect growth should remain slightly above potential in the second half"
- MR. BLINDER: "I think the anecdotes are consistent with the view that we have, not a lot, but a little bit further to go" [i.e., a little slack remains]

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants see the economy operating modestly above potential or at least at potential, with growth slowing from above-trend rates but still positive; capacity utilization and unemployment are near threshold levels.

**Minority views:** Melzer sees clear overheating (would score 60+). Blinder and LaWare suggest some remaining slack or balanced risks (would score 50-52).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "The unemployment rate has already fallen to the bottom of the range of current estimates of the natural rate"
- MR. STERN: "There are some shortages of labor—in construction trades clearly...Despite this healthy economy having been in place for quite some time, there really are no widespread signs of building wage and price pressures"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "we are experiencing significantly different labor conditions in different parts of the country...spot labor shortages"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is near full employment with unemployment at the lower end of NAIRU estimates and scattered shortages in construction, trucking, and entry-level positions, but broad-based wage pressure has not yet materialized.

**Minority views:** Melzer's District at 4.8% unemployment (lowest in 20 years) suggests much tighter conditions regionally. Blinder sees "fewer reports of tight labor markets than one would expect."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Retailers in the District were disappointed by their performance in May...a number of industries in the District have recently announced plans to expand or to upgrade their facilities"
- MR. JORDAN: "43 percent now said they are going to increase capital outlays...explosive growth in the high-tech area"
- MR. BROADDUS: "shipments of manufactured goods and new orders at factories are still rising; tourism...is exceptionally robust"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is moderating from strong levels as interest rate hikes bite into housing and auto sales, but business investment remains robust with capacity expansion plans and strong manufacturing orders, keeping the net spending picture above trend.

**Minority views:** LaWare emphasizes consumer caution and replacement-only investment spending (lower). McTeer notes capacity constraints limiting output in several industries (higher).

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "The 30-year bond rate has risen two full percentage points from its low point back in October"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "markets have survived significant turmoil beginning with the Fed tightening in February...we have had no major U.S. casualties; money has moved; we do not have gridlock"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Financing is not particularly easy to obtain...but it is more available than it has been in the last few years"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions have tightened somewhat from the very accommodative state of 1993, with a substantial backup in long-term rates and mortgage rates dampening housing, but credit markets are functioning well without systemic stress.

**Minority views:** Limited divergence on this dimension.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would like to stay at 'B' unchanged, but I'd like to be asymmetric...I have a suspicion that if we were to do nothing until the August meeting, we might possibly want to do 50 basis points"
- MR. MELZER: "I would move at least as aggressively as we did last time. I'd move the funds rate up 50 basis points"
- MR. BLINDER: "I believe it would be useful to go asymmetric in order to cock the gun...it's modestly likely that we have to go further"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The Committee holds rates steady but with a clear tightening bias; the consensus is alternative B with asymmetric toward tightening, with most participants expecting further rate increases in the near future and some wanting to move immediately.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Broaddus want 50bp now (would push to 70+). Forrestal, Stern, and LaWare prefer symmetric/balanced stance (would push to 55).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan characterizes the outcome as "a fairly broad consensus for B and a modest majority for asymmetry"
- MR. MELZER explicitly advocates 50bp hike with discount rate increase — clearly dissenting from the hold recommendation
- MR. BROADDUS: "a good case can be made for increasing the funds rate 1/2 point this morning"
- Several prefer symmetric (Stern, Jordan, Forrestal would accept either)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** There is notable disagreement on timing and pace — a camp of 2-3 members wants immediate tightening of 50bp while others see balanced risks — but the broad majority agrees on holding with a tightening bias, making this moderate-level dissent.

**Minority views:** N/A (this dimension captures the spread itself).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: lengthy discussion of market turmoil, mutual fund flows, bank risk management, bond market volatility
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extended discussion of exchange rate dynamics and financial instability risks ("unless it becomes crucially necessary because of increasing financial instability")
- Multiple passing mentions of dollar weakness and bond market backup

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions receive moderate attention, primarily through the lens of exchange rate dynamics and the bond market backup, with Phillips providing the most substantive assessment of market resilience; this is above the typical 1993-96 baseline but not a dominant topic.

**Minority views:** Most participants focus primarily on real economy; Phillips is the main exception.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 70 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I have a suspicion that if we were to do nothing until the August meeting, we might possibly want to do 50 basis points in August if the economy starts to pick up"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "it's entirely possible...that we would in fact wish to make a tightening move before the next meeting. That tightening move could well be as much as 50 basis points"
- MR. STERN: "over time we may well have considerably more to do"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly hawkish — the asymmetric directive, explicit discussion of intermeeting moves, and broad expectation of further tightening (potentially 50bp) all point toward continued monetary tightening in the near term.

**Minority views:** Blinder notes the possibility that 125bp was enough. LaWare suggests a "breather."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "we all seem to be struggling with this question as to whether we are below, at, or above capacity...whenever we are at a change in a business cycle, we are likely to get a fair amount of conflicting data and mixed views"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: data are "consistent with...either going back down or going up"
- MR. BOEHNE: "We are in a transition from a higher growth rate to a lower growth rate...we could easily underestimate or overestimate the extent and smoothness of the adjustment"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty about the trajectory of the economy — whether the slowdown will persist or reverse, where capacity constraints truly bind, and how to read conflicting data — but most have directional views, placing uncertainty above normal but not extreme.

**Minority views:** Phillips expresses the most uncertainty. Melzer and Broaddus are quite confident in their hawkish view.
