# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1999-02-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually attributed speaker statements. It covers both a go-around discussion (focused on Bluebook simulations, NAIRU, and the inflation/output puzzle) and a policy discussion with Chairman Greenspan's summary and individual member responses.

### Era Context
February 1999: Late-1990s expansion. Economy growing well above trend for multiple years, unemployment at 4.4%, yet inflation remarkably subdued. The Fed had eased 75bp the prior fall (Sept-Nov 1998) in response to LTCM/global financial turmoil. Financial conditions had since normalized. This is the heart of the "New Economy" debate.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as remarkably low — below what models predict. Greenspan: "The inflation rate, as we measure it, is at zero currently; if we got the measurement right, it probably would be negative." "There is remarkably little evidence that inflation pressures are building. There is none in the data." Multiple participants note the puzzle of no inflation despite tight labor markets. Meyer and Minehan worry about latent/future pressures but acknowledge nothing is in the data. The center of gravity is that current inflation is well below target — but there is widespread concern that models predict it should be rising. Direction: below midpoint. Participants perceive very low current inflation pressure, though some worry about future risks. Score should be in the 30-38 range: below target, slack keeping prices in check despite tight labor markets.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Greenspan says "growth has been above trend" for several years. Multiple participants describe "unsustainable" demand growth. Greenspan: "5.6 percent annual rate of growth in the fourth quarter." Broaddus: "domestic demand is growing at an unsustainable pace." Meyer: "we are already operating beyond the point of sustainability in labor markets." McTeer: "three years during which economic growth has been faster than most people's estimate of potential." This is clearly above potential. The bright-line rule requires >50. The discussion centers on an economy running well above trend. Score: 58-63 range (late 90s boom anchor 57-63).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Greenspan: "the labor market has tightened, and tightened quite appreciably." Unemployment at 4.4%. Minneapolis at 1.5%, Boston at 2.2%, San Francisco at 2.5%. Poole: "very tight labor markets." Multiple anecdotes about worker shortages. Greenspan notes the number of job seekers "has been falling by almost one million annually." Yet wages are NOT accelerating — Greenspan: "gains in compensation per hour have slowed!" This is the central puzzle. The LEVEL of tightness is high (anchor 65-75 for 1999-2000). Despite the wage puzzle, participants universally describe the labor market as extremely tight. Score: 68-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Greenspan: "the economy has been exhibiting substantial, and what many analysts regard as unsustainable, strength. The strength is especially evident in interest-sensitive areas such as housing and light motor vehicles." Growth at 5.6% Q4. Broaddus: "domestic demand is growing at an unsustainable pace." This is well above trend — in the "robust/strong momentum" to "unsustainably rapid" range. Score: 65-70.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Greenspan notes "yields on junk bonds remaining significantly above Treasuries and even obligations rated A and AA still running spreads against Treasuries that we haven't seen for a very long time" — residual tightness from the fall 1998 turmoil. However, Minehan describes "credit conditions, monetary growth conditions, stock market conditions" as very stimulative. Poole: "Credit conditions and monetary growth are both very clearly on the stimulative side." Stock prices at record levels. The center of gravity is that financial conditions are loose/accommodative despite some residual spread widening. The loose conditions dominate the discussion. Score: 28-33 (somewhat loose, with some residual tightness noted).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants support holding rates unchanged (symmetric). But there is a notable lean toward future tightening. Greenspan recommends hold + symmetric, but says "the consensus is probably a shade in the direction of asymmetry" toward tightening. Multiple participants (Minehan, Meyer, Broaddus, Parry, Hoenig, Moskow, Guynn) express desire for asymmetric tightening bias. Broaddus: "one could make a case for undoing at this meeting some of the easing." Minehan: "I am a little more uncomfortable with not changing the symmetry." Yet no one pushes for an actual rate move today. The center: hold steady with a tightening bias forming. Score: 53-57 (lean hawkish: hold with tightening bias).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All support the Chairman's recommendation of no change + symmetric directive. But Minehan explicitly says "I can accept not changing policy but I am a little more uncomfortable with not changing the symmetry" and "I do not have a vote so I can't dissent, but I am really concerned." Broaddus "would strongly prefer an asymmetric directive." Meyer, Parry, Moskow, Hoenig all express reservations. Jordan reiterates his dissent from the November cut. There's a clear split between those comfortable with symmetry (McTeer, McDonough, Gramlich) and those wanting asymmetry (Minehan, Meyer, Broaddus, Parry). No formal dissent, but significant expressed reservations. Score: 38-43.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Jordan raises asset price concerns at length: "people have been purchasing equities in the expectation of selling them to somebody else at a higher price" and "real estate investments... in the expectation that prices can only go up." Greenspan: "There has been a legitimate concern about stock prices that, as an inadvertent side effect of our easing actions, have returned to record levels." Minehan: "stock market conditions, and asset market conditions more generally that seem to be stoking things." This is a moderate but not dominant topic. Score: 35-42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan explicitly says he may signal tightening bias in Humphrey-Hawkins testimony. Multiple participants want him to "put people on notice" (Guynn, Stern). Poole: "Over the course of this year, starting sometime this spring, I think it is going to be appropriate to be tightening up on the funds rate." The forward signal is mildly hawkish — not committing but clearly leaning toward tightening. Score: 58-63.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: This meeting is dominated by uncertainty about models, NAIRU, the inflation process. Greenspan: "Our great dilemma..." "we don't know where that is." Moskow: "there is a lot we don't know." Ferguson: "in periods when models do not seem to be working very well—and we seem to be in one of those periods." The entire discussion is about not understanding why inflation hasn't risen. This is elevated uncertainty — not about the data, but about the fundamental framework. Score: 55-62.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor tightness higher because unemployment is extremely low even as some output measures are debated.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Both lean mildly hawkish. Current action is hold, but forward signal tilts toward tightening. Consistent.

### Speaker Count Summary
Speakers in policy discussion: Greenspan, McDonough, Stern, Ferguson, Poole, Hoenig, Guynn, Minehan, Meyer, Gramlich, Broaddus, Parry, McTeer, Kelley, Jordan, Moskow (~16 participants).
- Hold + symmetric: broad support (all accept recommendation)
- Would prefer asymmetry/tightening bias: Minehan, Meyer, Broaddus, Parry, Moskow, Hoenig, Guynn (~7)
- Comfortable with symmetric: McDonough, McTeer, Gramlich, Stern, Ferguson, Kelley (~6)
- Could make case for rate hike now: Broaddus (~1)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "There is remarkably little evidence that inflation pressures are building. There is none in the data." "The inflation rate, as we measure it, is at zero currently; if we got the measurement right, it probably would be negative."
- GREENSPAN: "the CPI change for December, if we take out the tobacco price increase, was slightly negative for the total CPI and only slightly positive for the core CPI."
- MEYER: "We have all been over-forecasting inflation and under-forecasting growth for at least three years."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants uniformly acknowledge that measured inflation is very low and declining, well below what models predict. The score is below 50 because current inflation pressure is absent, but not deeply deflationary — it sits in the "below target, slack keeping prices in check" zone despite tight labor markets, reflecting the genuine puzzle participants describe.

**Minority views:** Meyer and some others believe latent excess demand pressures exist that models correctly identify, even if they haven't yet shown up in price data. This widens the band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "growth has been above trend... 5.6 percent annual rate of growth in the fourth quarter"
- BROADDUS: "domestic demand is growing at an unsustainable pace"
- MEYER: "we are already operating beyond the point of sustainability in labor markets"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal agreement that the economy is operating above potential, with growth well above trend for multiple years. Consistent with the late-90s boom anchor (57-63).

**Minority views:** McDonough and McTeer suggest the "new economy" may mean potential is higher than estimated, which would reduce the gap, but even they acknowledge above-trend growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the labor market has tightened, and tightened quite appreciably" with unemployment at 4.4%, and some cities at 1.5-2.5%.
- POOLE: "There are so many stories about the existence of labor market pressures and shortages"
- GREENSPAN: job seekers "falling by almost one million annually... ten million" remaining

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is described as very tight by virtually all participants, with unemployment well below historical NAIRU estimates. The puzzle is that wages aren't accelerating despite this tightness. Score reflects the level of tightness, not the wage outcome.

**Minority views:** None seriously dispute tightness; disagreement is over whether it will eventually produce inflation.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 67 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the economy has been exhibiting substantial, and what many analysts regard as unsustainable, strength. The strength is especially evident in interest-sensitive areas such as housing and light motor vehicles."
- GREENSPAN: "5.6 percent annual rate of growth in the fourth quarter, and the impression I have is that that number will be revised up"
- BROADDUS: "domestic demand is growing at an unsustainable pace... sustained and even boosted by the lingering and lagged effects of the easing actions"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and investment are well above trend, described as "unsustainable" and "substantial." Strong technology investment noted alongside robust consumer demand in interest-sensitive sectors.

**Minority views:** Some note weak energy/agriculture/manufacturing sectors (Greenspan), but these are overwhelmed by the strength elsewhere.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- POOLE: "Credit conditions and monetary growth are both very clearly on the stimulative side."
- MINEHAN: "credit conditions, monetary growth conditions, stock market conditions, and asset market conditions more generally that seem to be stoking things"
- GREENSPAN: "stock prices... have returned to record levels" but also "yields on junk bonds remaining significantly above Treasuries"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are predominantly loose/accommodative. Stock market at record highs, credit freely available, monetary growth strong. Some residual spread widening from the 1998 turmoil persists but is fading. The overall assessment is loose conditions.

**Minority views:** Greenspan notes residual credit spreads remain elevated, which prevents the score from going even lower.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "the consensus is probably a shade in the direction of asymmetry toward tightening... unless we see a pronounced slowing... some form of preemptive action may be called for"
- MINEHAN: "I can accept not changing policy but I am a little more uncomfortable with not changing the symmetry... I am really concerned."
- BROADDUS: "I would strongly prefer an asymmetric directive... one could make a case for undoing at this meeting some of the easing"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All support holding steady, but roughly half the committee wants a tightening bias. The lean is mildly hawkish — no one advocates easing, all acknowledge tightening may come soon, but Greenspan successfully argues for patience given the absence of actual inflation data.

**Minority views:** Broaddus favors an immediate rate hike; McDonough and McTeer are comfortable with full symmetry and patience.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MINEHAN: "I do not have a vote so I can't dissent, but I am really concerned."
- BROADDUS: "I would strongly prefer an asymmetric directive"
- JORDAN: "I still stand by my dissent on the cut in the funds rate taken in November"
- Multiple others (Meyer, Parry, Moskow) express reservations about symmetry

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissent at this meeting, but roughly half the participants express notable discomfort with the symmetric directive. Jordan references his prior dissent. The range of views spans from "could hike today" (Broaddus) to "comfortable holding symmetric" (McDonough, McTeer). This represents moderate disagreement.

**Minority views:** The split is roughly even between symmetry supporters and asymmetry advocates, creating genuine tension.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- JORDAN: "people have been purchasing equities in the expectation of selling them to somebody else at a higher price, with not a thought as to what the earnings potential or real value is"
- GREENSPAN: "There has been a legitimate concern about stock prices that, as an inadvertent side effect of our easing actions, have returned to record levels"
- MINEHAN: "stock market conditions, and asset market conditions more generally... The higher everything flies, the farther it has to fall."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Several participants raise asset prices and financial conditions as concerns, but it is not the dominant topic — the inflation/NAIRU puzzle takes center stage. Financial stability is a secondary but meaningful thread. Score reflects limited but genuine attention (a few participants raise it substantively).

**Minority views:** Jordan gives the most extended treatment; others mention it more briefly.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "a fairly strong signal in the Humphrey-Hawkins testimony that we might tighten would be wholly appropriate"
- GUYNN: "I hope... you will use the Humphrey-Hawkins setting to put people on notice that we are in fact prepared to tighten"
- POOLE: "Over the course of this year, starting sometime this spring, I think it is going to be appropriate to be tightening up on the funds rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is mildly hawkish. While no commitment to tighten is made, the consensus is that Greenspan should signal in upcoming testimony that tightening is possible. Multiple participants explicitly want markets "on notice." This is a clear tilt toward future tightening, data-dependent.

**Minority views:** McDonough and McTeer are more comfortable with pure data dependence without signaling.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "Our great dilemma is that although the labor market has tightened... gains in compensation per hour have slowed!" and "we don't know where that is; we only know that the number of job seekers has been reduced by almost a million in each of the last several years, and nothing has happened."
- MOSKOW: "there is a lot we don't know... We all have been over-forecasting inflation and under-forecasting growth for at least three years"
- FERGUSON: "in periods when models do not seem to be working very well—and we seem to be in one of those periods"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is elevated. The entire meeting revolves around the failure of standard models to explain the current environment. Greenspan's "missing variable" theme, the NAIRU debate, and the acknowledged inability to predict when wage pressures will emerge all signal genuine deep uncertainty about the inflation process. This goes beyond rhetorical hedging — participants are genuinely unsure about fundamental economic relationships.

**Minority views:** Meyer expresses more confidence in traditional frameworks, which slightly narrows the band.
