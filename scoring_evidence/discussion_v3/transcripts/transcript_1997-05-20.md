# FOMC Scoring: 1997-05-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers the go-around discussion of economic conditions and a full policy discussion. Speakers include: McTeer, Parry, Jordan, Broaddus, Boehne, Hoenig, Guynn, Moskow, Stern, Minehan, McDonough, Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, and Chairman Greenspan. This is approximately 15 participants, providing rich material.

### Era Context
May 1997: mid-to-late 1990s expansion. Unemployment had fallen to 4.9% (a 23-year low at the time). GDP grew 5.6% in Q1 1997. The Fed had raised rates 25bp in March 1997 to 5.50%. Inflation remained subdued despite tight labor markets -- the beginning of the "new economy" productivity puzzle. Strong dollar and falling commodity prices were restraining measured inflation.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently low/well-contained but with rising risks. Multiple speakers note "surprisingly well-behaved" inflation, low CPI, declining PPI, and commodity prices flat to down. However, most see upside risks building due to tight labor markets and high resource utilization. Parry says "risk of rising inflation in the future has increased to a serious level." Greenspan notes total unit costs near zero growth. The center of gravity is that current inflation is low but risks are modestly tilted upward. Direction: slightly below to near the midpoint -- inflation is contained NOW but not benign going forward. Score should be in the 42-48 range. Participants perceive modest, not alarming, current inflation pressure with upside risk.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Nearly universal view that the economy is operating at or above potential. Parry: "excess of real GDP over its potential level rose to about 1-3/4 percent" and "unemployment rate is now clearly in the inflation danger zone." Meyer: unemployment at "23-year low." Greenspan: "the amount of slack in the economy is not substantial." Broaddus: "considerable upside risk." McDonough: "real GDP is now somewhat above potential." Multiple participants describe Q1 growth at 5.6% as unsustainably fast. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language --> must be >50. Score: 57-62 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Extremely prominent topic. Nearly every speaker discusses tight labor markets in their district. Jordan: entry-level wages jumping from $5.50 to $8-9/hour; union settlements rising. Parry: "competition for employees is so fierce that employers are holding job fairs and offering finder's fees and signing bonuses." Hoenig: "labor shortages are pronounced for entry-level workers." Stern: "very, very tight labor markets." Minehan: NH unemployment at 2.1%. Moskow: District unemployment at 4.1%. Guynn: 600% turnover at fast food. National unemployment at ~4.9%, well below estimated NAIRU. This is clearly tight -- 63-70 range. Anchors: 1999-2000 at 65-75. This fits squarely there.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Q1 was extremely strong (5.6% GDP) but Q2 showing sharp moderation. Many report retail sales softening in April. However, fundamentals remain "very positive" -- consumer confidence at records, strong income growth, wealth effects from stock market. Business investment strong, especially high-tech (Greenspan's extensive discussion). Jordan: manufacturers revising up capital spending. Broaddus: "household fundamentals and general economic fundamentals remain exceptionally strong." Net: above trend overall but moderating. Score: 58-63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants describe conditions as loose/accommodative. Boehne: "more than ample liquidity available, and competition to make commercial loans is fierce." Jordan: "one banker said there are no standards. Bankers are just pirating each other's loans." Deals "skinnier" than ever, lending that "would not even have been considered last summer." Meyer: financial conditions "more favorable" than after March move -- long rates edged lower, stock market rallied. Minehan's investment managers focus on "availability of credit, the liquidity of the markets, and the excesses they see everywhere." This is clearly loose. Score: 18-25 range.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is nuanced. Several participants explicitly preferred a 25bp hike today: Meyer (preferred C), Parry, Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Guynn -- all expressed preference for tightening but most said they could accept the Chairman's recommendation to hold. Greenspan recommended no change but with asymmetric (hawkish) directive and stated >50% probability of moving in July, "very high" by August. McTeer, Boehne, Kelley, Rivlin, McDonough supported hold more comfortably. The consensus was hold-with-hawkish-bias. Several "reluctant" holders. Greenspan: "we will need another 25bp, more likely more, by the end of the year." This is lean-to-moderate hawkish: hold but with clear expectation of tightening. Score: 58-63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal vote dissent described, but substantial expressed disagreement about timing. At least 6 participants (Meyer, Parry, Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Guynn) preferred immediate tightening but acquiesced to the Chairman's hold recommendation. Meyer gave an elaborate case for "C" (tighten). The debate was genuine -- not just tactical differences but somewhat about framework (preemptive vs. reactive). However, all ultimately could live with the Chairman's proposal. Score: 40-48 -- notable differences but contained.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate-to-significant discussion. Multiple participants raise concerns about speculative building in commercial real estate (Jordan, Broaddus, Boehne, McDonough, Minehan). Jordan extensively discusses farmland speculation, aggressive bank lending, "no standards." Boehne: lenders "willing to lend up to 30 percent more than a new acquisition is worth." Minehan: investment managers see "excesses everywhere, particularly when it comes to commercial real estate financing." Greenspan: "elements of speculation, especially in the commercial real estate area." McDonough: "frothy asset markets." Score: 40-50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Clearly hawkish forward lean. Greenspan: "probability that we will have to move and should move in July is better than 50/50" and "probability of a move by August is very high." "Odds of a further move in the fall are better than 50/50." Asymmetric directive adopted. Multiple participants explicitly expect further tightening. Score: 68-73.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated. McDonough explicitly invokes Brainard uncertainty principle -- "the greater the uncertainty about the economic model and its forecast, the more cautious we should be." Greenspan: "this hypothesis... is only a hypothesis and by no means a certainty." Boehne: "uncertainties on the demand side are greater now." Meyer identifies two specific sources of increased uncertainty. Minehan: "agnostic" about both growth slowdown and inflation. But Broaddus pushes back: "we need to be very careful not to let uncertainty paralyze us." Score: 45-52.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low current but rising risks. Score ~46 fits -- near midpoint, slightly below.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence clearly says above potential. Score ~59 fits.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence overwhelmingly tight. Score ~66 fits.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: Higher = stronger. Evidence says strong but moderating from very high. Score ~60 fits.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says very loose. Score ~20 fits.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with strong hawkish bias. Score ~60 fits.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Greenspan signals likely move in July/August. Score ~70 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (59) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (66): Both above 50, coherent. Labor slightly higher because labor tightness discussed more emphatically than general output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Forward guidance more hawkish than current action -- makes sense because they held today but explicitly signaled tightening ahead. Divergence explained by strategic timing choice.

### Speaker Count by Policy Preference
- Preferred immediate tightening: Meyer, Parry, Broaddus, Minehan, Stern, Guynn (~6)
- Preferred hold (comfortable): McTeer, Boehne, Kelley, Rivlin, McDonough, Hoenig (~6)
- Greenspan: recommended hold but framed it as tactical delay, not disagreement with tightening need
- Net: roughly evenly split on timing, but near-unanimous agreement that further tightening would be needed

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 46 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MCTEER: "Producer prices have declined for 4 consecutive months... CPI increases also have moderated as energy prices have reversed last year's buildup."
- MR. PARRY: "the risk that inflationary pressures are building seems to me to have increased since our March meeting... underlying inflation would show a modest upward trend this year and next"
- MR. GUYNN: "Recent inflation remains low by all conventional measures, lower I think than almost all of us expected... a gradual upward drift in inflation at the end of 1997 and into 1998"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "total unit costs... the estimated rate of increase... is close to zero"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is well-contained and below what historical models would predict given labor market tightness, but nearly all participants see risks tilting upward. The score reflects contained-but-not-benign -- slightly below the midpoint with upside risk acknowledged.

**Minority views**: McTeer sees very little inflation concern; Parry sees the risk as "serious." This widens the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 59 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "the excess of real GDP over its potential level rose to about 1-3/4 percent in the first quarter... the unemployment rate is now clearly in the inflation danger zone"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "real GDP is now somewhat above potential and likely to stay that way"
- MR. MEYER: "the economy is likely over the forecast horizon... to remain at the high level of labor resource utilization rates reached in April"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating modestly above potential. Q1 growth was 5.6% (far above trend), and even with expected Q2 slowing, the level of output remains above potential. Multiple participants cite the GDP gap and unemployment below NAIRU.

**Minority views**: Greenspan's productivity hypothesis suggests potential GDP may be higher than estimated, which would narrow the gap. This contributes to uncertainty.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 66 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: "Entry-level wages are jumping throughout the District... starting wage for a teller was $5.50 per hour, whereas it is now $8.00 at one bank and $9.00 at another"
- MR. PARRY: "competition for employees is so fierce that employers are holding job fairs and offering finder's fees and signing bonuses to recruit new hires"
- MR. GUYNN: fast food operator "had issued over one hundred W-2s for tax year 1996 for a staff of 13; that reflects a 600 percent turnover rate"
- MR. STERN: "very, very tight labor markets... employment gains over the past 12 months outpaced the increase in the labor force by a significant amount"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight across nearly all districts, with unemployment at ~4.9% (well below most NAIRU estimates), widespread shortages of both skilled and unskilled workers, and emerging wage pressures. However, broad wage acceleration has not yet materialized -- pressures are building but not yet "severe" or "unsustainable."

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on the level of tightness; debate is more about whether productivity gains offset the inflationary implications.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: "household fundamentals and general economic fundamentals remain exceptionally strong... I see considerable upside risk in the staff forecast"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discussion of high-tech capital investment "tilts up sharply and progressively" since 1993
- MR. JORDAN: manufacturers "stepping up their capital spending for the year from what they had planned earlier"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Retailers generally report that sales were sluggish last month... light vehicle sales in April were well below the first-quarter level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment were very strong in Q1 and are moderating in Q2, but fundamentals (income growth, wealth effects, consumer confidence at records, strong business investment plans) point to above-trend activity continuing. The net position is above trend but with near-term softening.

**Minority views**: McTeer emphasizes "reassuringly weak" recent monthly numbers; others see the softening as temporary payback.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BOEHNE: "more than ample liquidity available, and competition to make commercial loans is fierce. One executive... told me that some lenders are willing to lend up to 30 percent more than a new acquisition is worth"
- MR. JORDAN: "one banker on our board said that there are no standards. Bankers are just pirating each other's loans... deals are now being done that would not even have been considered last summer"
- MR. MEYER: "Financial conditions either have been sufficiently stimulative to promote this strength or not sufficiently restrictive to prevent the resurgence... more favorable financial market conditions today than was the case immediately following the March move"
- MS. MINEHAN: investment managers focus on "the availability of credit, the liquidity of the markets, and the excesses that they see everywhere"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Credit is freely available, lending standards are declining, the stock market has rallied, and long-term rates edged down even after the March hike. Multiple participants describe conditions in terms consistent with excessive ease.

**Minority views**: Boehne notes some caution in consumer lending/credit cards, but this is a minor offset.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I recommend that we not move today... the probability that we will have to move and should move in July is better than 50/50... we will need another 25 basis points, more likely more, by the end of the year"
- MR. MEYER: "I prefer 'C'... some further tightening appears justified by current initial conditions and forecasts... I could live with 'B/go slow'"
- MR. PARRY: "I would prefer to raise the funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting. However... I am willing, somewhat reluctantly, to support a policy of no change"
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think we need to move today... a 1/4 percentage move would be appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee is leaning hawkish but chose to hold for tactical/timing reasons. Roughly half explicitly preferred an immediate hike, while the other half supported holding but acknowledged likely need for tightening. The decision was hold with asymmetric directive -- a clear tightening bias.

**Minority views**: McTeer was the most dovish, seeing little urgency. Meyer was the most explicitly hawkish in framework, insisting on preemptive policy.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MEYER: Gave an elaborate multi-page case for alternative "C" (25bp hike), explicitly distinguishing "B/reactive" from "B/go slow" from "C," and only reluctantly accepting B
- MR. PARRY: "somewhat reluctantly" supports no change
- MR. BROADDUS: "I think we need to move today"
- MS. MINEHAN: "I believe the right policy now is a further 25 basis point move. But... I could support your recommendation"
- MR. STERN: "I would certainly prefer a modest tightening now"

**Center-of-gravity**: Notable disagreement about timing and framework. At least 6 participants preferred immediate tightening. Meyer raised fundamental framework questions (preemptive vs. reactive). However, all ultimately acquiesced to the Chairman, and the disagreement was about timing (now vs. soon) rather than direction. No formal dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. JORDAN: extensive discussion of farmland speculation, aggressive bank lending with "no standards," speculative residential construction, Federal Farm Credit Banks returning to risky 1970s-era financing
- MR. BOEHNE: lenders "willing to lend up to 30 percent more than a new acquisition is worth. Some REIT activity also looks shortsighted"
- MS. MINEHAN: investment managers see "excesses that they see everywhere, particularly when it comes to commercial real estate financing"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "elements of speculation, especially in the commercial real estate area"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "frothy asset markets... speculative real estate development in New York"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a meaningful secondary theme. Multiple participants discuss speculative excesses in commercial real estate, aggressive bank lending, and frothy asset markets. It is not the dominant topic (economic outlook and policy timing dominate) but receives substantial attention from several participants.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the probability that we will have to move and should move in July is better than 50/50... the probability of a move by August is very high. I would add that the odds of a further move in the fall are better than 50/50"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we will need another 25 basis points, more likely more, by the end of the year... adopt an asymmetric directive"
- MR. MOSKOW: references Greenspan's "high probability of a tightening move... 50/50 probability for July and a very high probability for August"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. The Chairman explicitly laid out a tightening trajectory (likely July/August move, possibly more in fall). Asymmetric directive adopted. Near-unanimous expectation of further tightening. This is a clear tightening signal: further rate increases ahead.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: invokes Brainard uncertainty principle -- "the greater the uncertainty about the economic model and its forecast, the more cautious we should be in changing policy instruments"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "this hypothesis, which I would stress is only a hypothesis and by no means a certainty"
- MR. BOEHNE: "uncertainties on the demand side of the economy are greater now than when we last met... uncertainties remain... the economy moves further into territory not charted for nearly three decades"
- MS. MINEHAN: "agnostic about whether growth will slow in line with the Greenbook forecast"
- MR. BROADDUS (pushback): "we need to be very careful not to let uncertainty paralyze us... there is always a lot of uncertainty. There may be a little more of it than the average at this meeting but in my experience not that much more"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated. Multiple participants cite unusual difficulty in assessing the outlook due to the productivity puzzle, unprecedented low unemployment without inflation, and questions about structural change. However, this is not "deep" or "extreme" uncertainty -- participants still have frameworks and reasonable forecasts. The uncertainty is more about the supply side (productivity, NAIRU) than about direction.

- MR. MCTEER: "Producer prices have declined for 4 consecutive months... Commodity and metals prices, including gold, also reveal little buildup of inflationary pressures"
- MR. MOSKOW: "steel price increases of 3 to 5 percent announced for April and May are not sticking and in some cases are now being publicly rescinded"
- Strong dollar restraining import/goods prices. Goods inflation clearly below normal.

- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the rise in core services inflation which we think is the sector most susceptible to wage pressures being passed through to prices"
- Services showing slightly more pressure than goods, consistent with labor cost pass-through beginning.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A
- Housing construction and real estate discussed in terms of activity/speculation, not shelter/rent inflation specifically.

- MR. KELLEY: "There appears to be no fear of inflation embedded in the yield curve"
- MR. BROADDUS: assumes "inflation expectations are anchored at 2 percent"
- MR. MEYER: consumer sentiment data shows consumers "no longer expect those rate increases to damp income gains"
- Expectations appear well-anchored, slightly below midpoint.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]
- Extensive evidence of extremely easy credit conditions, aggressive lending, declining standards. This represents compressed spreads and underpriced risk.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Score: 25 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "frothy asset markets"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: concern about "elements of speculation"
- The concern leans toward overvaluation/bubble territory (lower score = more overvaluation concern). Several participants note wealth effects and booming financial markets with implicit concern.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Score: 20 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. JORDAN: "one banker on our board said that there are no standards... deals are now being done that would not even have been considered last summer"
- Concern is about excessive risk-taking/leverage, not stress. Banks are sound but taking too much risk (low end of scale).

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Score: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- MR. MCTEER: "the dollar remains on the strong side and is probably putting direct downward pressure on import prices"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: strong dollar restraining wages and investment, but "the other side of that coin, of course, is the additional inflationary effect of a weaker dollar through that channel in the future"
- Dollar discussed as strong and helpful currently, with some concern about future reversal. Net: mild strength-concern direction.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Score: 68 +/-10 [High confidence]
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive multi-page discussion of productivity acceleration, ~3% in nonfinancial corporate sector, Paul David hypothesis about technology synergies
- MS. RIVLIN: "The anecdotes are much more positive than the staff forecast of a return to trend would indicate"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the trend rate may have moved up to 2 percent or so"
- Strong perceived productivity gains, well above historical trend.
