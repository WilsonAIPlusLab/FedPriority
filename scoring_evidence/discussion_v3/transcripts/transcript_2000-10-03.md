# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-10-03 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. The go-around includes roughly 17 speakers: Parry, Guynn, Broaddus, Santomero, McTeer, Moskow, Jordan, Hoenig, Minehan, Stern, McDonough, Poole, Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan, plus a policy vote round. This is a long, substantive discussion.

### Era Context
October 2000: The late-1990s boom is maturing. The Fed has been tightening since June 1999, bringing the fed funds rate to 6.5%. The economy is showing signs of moderation from above-trend growth. Oil prices have spiked. The "new economy" productivity debate is central. The dot-com bubble has begun to deflate. Unemployment is near 4%.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the midpoint. The center of gravity is that core inflation has ticked up modestly but remains contained. Parry: "core inflation hasn't accelerated in recent months despite higher oil prices" but "core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent this year and slightly more next year." Guynn: "generally benign inflation data" but "upward tilt in core prices that should not be discounted." Moskow: "increasing pressure on profit margins from higher costs." Minehan relays employee concerns about rising costs. Meyer: "balance of risks is toward rising core inflation." Santomero: "core inflation has also shown a moderate acceleration." Nearly all see inflation risks tilted to the upside, but actual core inflation readings are only modestly above desired levels. Energy is the wild card. Direction: modestly above target, with upside risk. Score should be in the 55-62 range. Multiple participants use "modest" and "moderate" language about core, but almost universally flag upside risks.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants broadly see the economy as having been above potential and now decelerating toward potential. Meyer explicitly: "economy appears to be making a transition from above-trend to below-trend growth" and discusses the economy "operating somewhat above some notion of our national potential." McDonough puts potential at 4.5% and sees growth "below potential." Greenspan: "nothing terribly significant going on in the economy excluding energy." Multiple participants describe "moderation" and "slowing" but from high levels. The economy is still operating at or slightly above potential, with growth decelerating. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language from Meyer and others means score MUST be >50. Score around 55-58 -- still above potential but closing.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Nearly every participant discusses very tight labor markets. Parry: "labor markets will remain tight." Broaddus: "labor markets are still very tight" with 10% across-the-board wage increase anecdote. Moskow: "continued tight labor markets" with extensive anecdotes about difficulty finding workers. Minehan: "very low rate of regional unemployment, which is fully 1-1/3 percentage points below the national rate." Jordan: "it is easier to get a new customer than it is to get a new employee!" Hoenig sees "a little loosening" but still tight. Poole: unemployment around 2% in Jefferson City. McDonough: economy can operate at 4% unemployment. This is a very tight labor market. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score around 68-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending is showing signs of softening -- multiple participants report disappointing retail sales (Stern, McDonough, Jordan report significant retail weakness). Investment remains strong, particularly in tech. Moskow: "slowing in activity" across many industries. Jordan: "considerably more softening" in retail. But Broaddus: "demand may be strengthening." McTeer: "economy... has begun to strengthen modestly." Overall, spending has moderated from very strong levels but is not weak. Net position: slightly above trend but decelerating. Score around 52-55.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants note tightening financial conditions. Parry: "modestly lower equity values and a higher dollar in combination with our past tightening actions." Guynn: "larger banks report that loan demand has declined noticeably... financing terms are now more stringent, especially for second-tier borrowers." Jordan: banks "proposals are now being turned down that would have been accepted a year ago... interest margins narrowed considerably." Minehan: "credit markets seem to be distinguishing among borrowers, with spreads rising for lower quality credit." McDonough: "local banks report... tightening credit standards." Ferguson: "credit conditions tighten." These are conditions, not just policy. Conditions are somewhat tight. Score around 57-62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous decision to hold rates at 6.5% with inflation bias retained. Nearly all participants support the current stance and the inflation tilt in the balance-of-risks statement. Hoenig is the lone voice suggesting moving toward balanced risks ("I think the risks are more balanced than they have been"). Poole supports the hold but notes "about equal probability that the next move would be up or down." The dominant view is: hold steady, retain inflation bias, too early to shift. This is a lean hawkish hold -- not actively tightening but maintaining tightening bias. Score around 58-62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. Broad agreement on holding rates and maintaining inflation bias. Hoenig mildly dissents in discussion (would prefer balanced risk statement) but accepts the recommendation. Poole has some nuance but supports. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Score around 18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Some discussion of equity markets (Santomero: "violent reaction to earnings disappointment"; Ferguson: negative earnings surprises from Cisco, Apple, Intel), credit tightening, and banking conditions. But financial stability is not a primary focus -- it is discussed as context, not as a systemic concern. The SOMA study at the end is about balance sheet operations, not financial stability per se. Score around 25-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is maintaining an inflation bias -- the balance of risks remains toward inflation. Multiple participants: "premature to adjust our balance of risks statement" (Guynn), Gramlich: "it may not be too long before I join Tom Hoenig in thinking that the risks have become evenly balanced. Yet at this point I think that such a change is still premature." This signals a mild tightening lean going forward, but no one is arguing for actual further rate increases at this point. Some even hint the next move could be a cut eventually. The forward signal is a mild hawkish hold. Score around 58-63.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated uncertainty. Minehan: "this forecast seems surrounded by more than the usual sense of uncertainty. I think that is reflected in the fact that it took 13 alternative simulations!" Greenspan spends extensive time on oil uncertainty. Ferguson: multiple risk scenarios. Kelley: "many scenarios have a certain plausibility." Jordan: "we would have had a very hard time imagining just how far a number of these things would go." The productivity/NAIRU uncertainty is widely discussed. Score around 48-55.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor tightness is higher than output gap because labor markets are a lagging indicator -- the economy has begun to slow but labor markets remain very tight. This divergence is explicitly discussed by participants.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Aligned -- both reflect a lean-hawkish hold with inflation bias.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- Inflation risks tilted upside: Parry, Guynn, Broaddus, Santomero, Moskow, Minehan, Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Greenspan, McDonough, Poole, Kelley (~13)
- Risks more balanced: Hoenig, McTeer (~2)
- Jordan is more nuanced/idiosyncratic but generally cautious on inflation

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "core inflation hasn't accelerated in recent months despite higher oil prices" but "core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent this year and slightly more next year" and "the balance of risks for inflation going forward still is on the up side"
- MR. MEYER: "the balance of risks is nevertheless toward rising core inflation. And this risk not only persists throughout the forecast period; it will likely remain for a considerable period of time thereafter"
- MR. GUYNN: "when one decomposes the current inflation path into the energy component and other components, there remains an upward tilt in core prices that should not be discounted"

Center-of-gravity: Participants perceive core inflation as modestly above comfortable levels (~2% PCE) with upside risks from energy, tight labor markets, and potential erosion of productivity-driven disinflation. Nearly universal agreement that inflation risks are tilted up.

Minority views: McTeer more optimistic on productivity containing inflation. Jordan skeptical about standard frameworks but not necessarily seeing less inflation pressure.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MEYER: "the economy appears to be making a transition from above-trend to below-trend growth" and "the prevailing unemployment rate is below the short-run NAIRU"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: economy showing "moderation" but "nothing terribly significant going on in the economy excluding energy"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "our growth forecast has the economy expanding below potential, which we put at 4-1/2 percent per annum"

Center-of-gravity: Economy has been operating above potential but is decelerating. Still slightly above potential given 4% unemployment and strong investment, but the gap is narrowing. The transition to below-trend growth implies above-potential levels are closing.

Minority views: Some (Minehan, Broaddus) see continued robust demand; others (Jordan, Hoenig) see more pronounced slowing.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "labor markets are still very tight in our area" -- anecdote of 10% across-the-board wage increase to stop "poaching"
- MR. MOSKOW: "continued tight labor markets" with anecdotes about cattlemen unable to find communities to build in due to labor shortages, and "retaining and attracting people" as most popular conference topic
- MR. JORDAN: manager said "It is easier to get a new customer than it is to get a new employee!"

Center-of-gravity: Nearly every participant describes very tight labor markets with severe shortages, difficulty finding workers, and rising compensation. Unemployment near 4%, well below standard NAIRU estimates. This is a very tight labor market by historical standards.

Minority views: Hoenig sees "a little loosening" in his district. Jordan notes some layoffs in trucking/auto sectors.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. STERN: "reporting a significant slowing in consumer spending or at least disappointing sales over the last several months"
- MR. JORDAN: "considerably more softening in our region than the data suggest for the nation"
- MR. MOSKOW: "the vast majority of our contacts continue to report a slowing in activity" but "home-related items like furniture and appliances remain strong"

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is decelerating with mixed signals -- some districts report disappointing retail, while back-to-school was strong in some areas. Investment remains robust, particularly in tech. Net: slightly above trend but clearly decelerating from earlier strength.

Minority views: Broaddus sees "demand may be strengthening." McTeer sees modest strengthening. Jordan and Stern report notable retail weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. GUYNN: "larger banks report that loan demand has declined noticeably from year-ago levels. And there is evidence that financing terms are now more stringent, especially for second-tier borrowers"
- MR. JORDAN: "proposals are now being turned down that would have been accepted a year ago. Bankers also tell us that not only have their interest margins narrowed considerably this year"
- MS. MINEHAN: "credit markets seem to be distinguishing among borrowers, with spreads rising for lower quality credit. Tighter financial conditions are likely a healthy development"

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened somewhat from the prior easy environment. Credit standards are firming, spreads widening for lower-quality borrowers, equity markets under pressure. Conditions are somewhat tight but not distressed.

Minority views: Minehan views the tightening as "healthy." No one describes crisis-level conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the general consensus around the table, and in my judgment the right one, is to do nothing today. But the risks clearly remain on the up side as far as inflation is concerned"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "I still believe that the fundamental risks of inflation are on the up side and I would like to retain our present policy stance at least for a while"
- MR. HOENIG: "I think the risks are more balanced than they have been. I would feel comfortable saying that with a balanced risk statement" (minority dovish lean)

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous hold at 6.5% with retained inflation bias in balance-of-risks statement. Strong consensus that current stance is appropriate but risks remain tilted toward inflation. No one advocates further tightening now, but most want to keep the hawkish tilt.

Minority views: Hoenig favors balanced risk statement. Poole notes equal probability of next move up or down but supports inflation language for communication reasons.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- All members support the recommendation in the vote round. MR. HOENIG: "I can accept your recommendation, Mr. Chairman, although I think we could go to a more balanced stance. But we can wait."
- No formal dissents. Broad agreement on hold + inflation bias.
- Minor differences on whether risks are truly still tilted or becoming balanced, but no sharp disagreement.

Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus with minor shade differences about the balance-of-risks assessment. Hoenig's mild preference for balanced language is the only notable deviation.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. SANTOMERO: "Equity market investors suggest that there is a fear of a slowdown and their violent reaction to earnings disappointment is noteworthy"
- MR. FERGUSON: "we've seen some negative earnings surprises from some of the major participants in the new economy, including Cisco Systems... Apple Computer, and Intel"
- MR. GUYNN: watching commercial real estate, FDIC concerns about overbuilding

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are mentioned by several participants as context for the economic outlook (equity declines, credit tightening, banking conditions) but financial stability is not a primary concern or dominant topic. Discussed as supporting evidence for economic slowing rather than as an independent worry.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. GUYNN: "I believe it would be premature to adjust our much-watched balance of risks statement"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "it may not be too long before I join Tom Hoenig in thinking that the risks have become evenly balanced. Yet at this point I think that such a change in our posture is still premature"
- MR. FERGUSON: "the balance of risk statement should continue to be focused on inflation"

Center-of-gravity: Forward signal maintains mild hawkish lean via retained inflation bias. However, several participants hint the bias could shift to balanced before long, suggesting the tightening cycle may be at or near its end. No one signals further rate increases are imminent.

Minority views: Hoenig and Poole suggest risks may be more balanced going forward.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. MINEHAN: "this forecast seems surrounded by more than the usual sense of uncertainty. I think that is reflected in the fact that it took 13 alternative simulations!"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discussion of oil market uncertainty -- "Those uncertainties have to be changing the structure of our capital stock" and "It is very difficult to forecast the price of oil"
- MR. KELLEY: "Many scenarios have a certain plausibility"

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty, driven primarily by oil price trajectory, productivity growth sustainability, and NAIRU estimation. The staff produced 13 alternative simulations, which multiple participants flag as reflecting unusual uncertainty. However, most participants express reasonable confidence in the broad outlines of the forecast (moderation, contained inflation).
