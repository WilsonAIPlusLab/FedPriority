# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2001-04-18 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Transcript of an intermeeting conference call. Individual speakers are identified. This is a relatively short discussion focused primarily on a proposed intermeeting rate cut.

**Era context**: April 2001 — the economy is in the early stages of what would become the 2001 recession. The dot-com bust has been underway, capital spending is weakening, and the Fed has already been cutting rates (January 3 and January 31 intermeeting cuts, and the March 20 regular meeting cut). The fed funds rate is being proposed to move to 4.5%.

**Speaker count and positions**:
- Speakers who explicitly support the 50bp cut: Chairman Greenspan, Vice Chairman McDonough, Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Stern, Guynn, McTeer, Santomero, Moskow, Ferguson, Kelley, Gramlich, Meyer (with reservations about intermeeting timing, not about the cut itself).
- Poole: Does NOT favor the intermeeting move but voted yes with reservations to avoid confusion. His objection is about timing, not direction.
- Near-unanimous support for the cut, with reservations about intermeeting timing from Meyer, Poole, Hoenig (mild), Stern (mild), Guynn (mild).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint. Very little inflation discussion. Parry notes the real funds rate is "rather high" given CPI estimates, implying inflation is low/benign. Greenspan references "yesterday's CPI numbers and the related outlook on prices" positively (Hoenig paraphrasing). Moskow mentions being prepared for inflationary pressures in the future but not currently. McDonough notes "somewhat higher inflation" in Europe, not the US. The entire focus is on economic weakness, not inflation. Direction: well below midpoint. Score ~30-35.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy operating below potential. Hoenig explicitly states growth of "1 percent or maybe 1-1/2 percent... against a potential of 3 percent or better." Greenspan notes "flat trend from January through March" with "no kick" going into Q2. Stern says "without this action the economy is likely to grow below potential for at least several more quarters." Gramlich notes unemployment rate rising to 5.2%. This is a clear below-potential economy, though not a catastrophic contraction. Score ~32-35.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct labor market discussion. Gramlich mentions unemployment expected to rise to 5.2%. Ferguson notes employment and unemployment data showed surprising job losses. The labor market is loosening but not yet in severe distress. Score ~38-40.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Clear weakness discussed. Greenspan: "capital expenditures are weakening." Meyer: consumer spending "has lost all its momentum" and "is going to be close to zero in the second quarter." Minehan: consumer strength "is eroding now." Santomero: "clear concern about the consumer sector" reinforced by "slowdown in capital goods expenditures." Moskow: manufacturing firms most likely cutting back on capex. Both consumption and investment are weakening. Score ~30-33.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Equity markets rose that morning (Intel/TI news). Poole notes "very benign outlook in the equity futures market." No discussion of credit stress, spreads, or lending conditions. The discussion of conditions is sparse but markets seem neutral to slightly supportive. Score ~45-48.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Near-unanimous support for a 50bp intermeeting cut with balance of risks toward weakness. This is clearly dovish. However, several participants (Hoenig, Stern, Guynn, Moskow) caution about being ready to reverse and not going too far. The center of gravity is strongly dovish with a mild cautionary note. Score ~22-25.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent — Poole voted yes with reservations. Meyer expressed reservations about timing. Several participants cautioned about not going too far. But on the actual decision, there's broad agreement. Poole's near-dissent and Meyer's reservations create moderate tension. Score ~30-35.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very little emphasis on financial stability per se. Brief mentions of equity markets and the trade data. No discussion of systemic risk, credit conditions, or financial stability concerns. Score ~10-15.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The balance of risks is maintained toward economic weakness — a clear easing bias. Several participants note more easing may be needed (Ferguson: "we'll have to go even lower"; Moskow references forecasts assuming "nominal fed funds rate will decline to a level of around 4 percent"). However, Hoenig, Stern, Guynn, and Moskow explicitly warn about caution going forward and being ready to reverse. The forward signal is easing-biased but with increasing caution. Score ~25-30.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty expressed. Hoenig: views "split about 50/50" on whether economy is at bottom. Ferguson: data are "quite mixed." Stern: "that's simply a forecast." Greenspan: recovery appears "somewhat less than robust" but not certain. Poole worries about market confusion. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~45-50.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says benign/low. Score 32 is below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says clearly below potential. Score 33 is below midpoint. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loosening, rising unemployment. Score 38 below midpoint. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says strongly dovish. Score 23 well below midpoint. Correct.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (33) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38): Both below 50, consistent with recession/weakening economy. Labor slightly higher because unemployment hasn't spiked dramatically yet.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (23) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (27): Both dovish, aligned. Forward guidance slightly higher because of caution notes about reversing later.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "it's quite likely that the real funds rate is rather high now relative to where we might want it to be, given such a weak economy" — implying inflation is low enough that the real rate is restrictive.
- MR. HOENIG: "given yesterday's CPI numbers and the related outlook on prices, I think the lower rate is appropriate" — CPI data seen as benign.
- No participant raises inflation as a current concern; the entire meeting is about downside growth risks.

**Center of gravity**: Inflation is not a concern; participants see it as contained and well below levels that would constrain easing.

**Minority views**: Moskow mentions being "prepared to act just as decisively on the up side if and when inflationary pressures become excessive," but this is a future hypothetical, not a current concern.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. HOENIG: "the economy in the first quarter was growing at only 1 percent or maybe 1-1/2 percent... against a potential of 3 percent or better"
- MR. STERN: "without this action the economy is likely to grow below potential for at least several more quarters"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a fairly flat trend from January through March, implying that we're probably going into the second quarter with no kick"

**Center of gravity**: The economy is clearly operating below potential, with growth well under the 3%+ potential rate. The gap is notable but not catastrophic — this is early-stage weakness, not a deep contraction.

**Minority views**: None disagree on below-potential assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. GRAMLICH: references unemployment "rising to 5.2 percent by the fourth quarter" — elevated but not severe.
- MR. FERGUSON: "employment and unemployment data were a surprise with respect to the job loss figures"

**Center of gravity**: The labor market is loosening with rising unemployment and job losses, but discussion is limited. The 5.2% unemployment forecast suggests slack is building but not yet deep.

**Minority views**: None express tightness concerns.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MEYER: "consumption has not been well maintained; it has lost all its momentum. It is going to be close to zero in the second quarter"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "capital expenditures are weakening" and "the snapback when the liquidation finally runs its course potentially will be only modest"
- MR. SANTOMERO: "increased concern about the consumer sector... being reinforced by the slowdown in capital goods expenditures"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are weakening notably. Consumption is losing momentum and capex is contracting, particularly in manufacturing. This is a clear below-trend reading.

**Minority views**: Moskow notes some retailers are not changing expansion plans, but the weight of evidence is toward weakness.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 46 +/-10 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "today's rise in the equity markets... pinned basically on both Intel and Texas Instruments saying that life is not quite as grim"
- MR. POOLE: "very benign outlook in the equity futures market this morning"
- No discussion of credit spreads, lending conditions, or financial stress.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are not a focus. Equity markets are rising on the day. No credit stress is mentioned. Conditions appear roughly neutral, perhaps slightly loose given rate cuts already undertaken.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 23 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: proposes "1/2 percentage point reduction in the funds rate and maintain the language indicating that the balance of risks is weighted toward economic weakness"
- MR. MCTEER: "I'm not worried at all that we're going to over-stimulate the economy by making a move now"
- MR. MOSKOW: "our outlook... assumes that the nominal fed funds rate will decline to a level of around 4 percent" — implying further cuts ahead

**Center of gravity**: Strong dovish consensus for a 50bp intermeeting cut with continued easing bias. This is active easing in the middle of a cutting cycle.

**Minority views**: Hoenig, Stern, Guynn caution about not going too far, but all support the cut. Poole objects to the intermeeting timing, not the direction. Meyer objects to intermeeting timing but agrees on the economics.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. POOLE: "I do not favor the move" but "I would not intend to dissent today because I think that could only add to the view that the FOMC is not clear about what it is doing"
- MR. MEYER: "I'll voice one last time my reservations about an intermeeting move" — supports the cut but not the timing
- Several others (Hoenig, Stern, Guynn) add caution notes about not easing too far

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on the cut, with moderate disagreement about the wisdom of an intermeeting move. Poole came close to dissenting. The disagreement is tactical (timing) rather than directional (whether to cut).

**Minority views**: Poole is the clearest dissenter-in-spirit; Meyer is the second.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Brief mentions of equity market movements (McDonough, Poole) as context rather than as a financial stability concern.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit conditions, or financial vulnerabilities.

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is essentially not a topic. The meeting is entirely focused on the growth outlook and the appropriateness of an intermeeting cut.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 27 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Balance of risks maintained "toward economic weakness" — clear easing bias
- MR. FERGUSON: "it may well be that... we'll have to go even lower on the funds rate before we've reached the end of this easing process"
- MR. MOSKOW: forecasts "assume that the nominal fed funds rate will decline to a level of around 4 percent" — implying at least one more cut
- MR. HOENIG: "I hope we will exercise care as we approach the May meeting... the risks of going one step too far always increase"
- MR. STERN: "we are getting to the stage where we have to be a little careful about how far we push"

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is easing-biased — more cuts likely ahead — but with growing caution about how much further to go. This is a mild easing bias with increasing data dependence.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 47 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. HOENIG: "views are split about 50/50 between those who feel we're at a bottom... and those who are still expecting some further weakening"
- MR. FERGUSON: "much of the recent information to be really quite mixed"
- MR. STERN: "that's simply a forecast... I think we are getting to the stage where we have to be a little careful"
- MR. POOLE: concerned about "market uncertainty and market volatility"

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty. Participants are unsure whether the economy has bottomed or will weaken further. Data are mixed. The path forward for both the economy and policy is unclear.
