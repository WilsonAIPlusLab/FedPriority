# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-02-04 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individually identified speakers from a two-day FOMC meeting (February 3-4, 1994). It includes a go-around on economic conditions, a lengthy discussion about announcing policy changes, and then the policy decision round. Speakers include: Parry, McTeer, Syron, Boehne, Forrestal, Hoenig, Lindsey, Broaddus, Stern, Jordan, Melzer, Kelley, LaWare, Phillips, McDonough, Greenspan, and others. This is a well-populated discussion with 15+ participants.

### Era Context
February 1994 is a pivotal moment in Fed history -- this is the meeting where the Fed begins its first tightening cycle in five years, raising the fed funds rate from 3% for the first time since September 1992. The economy is in a mid-cycle expansion following the 1990-91 recession, with growth accelerating to 4%+ in the second half of 1993. Unemployment is near NAIRU estimates. This is one of the first times the Fed publicly announced a policy change.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly below worrisome levels currently but with rising risks. Parry notes wage inflation "stuck at 3-1/2 percent" and says "we would not be able to expect any further progress in bringing inflation down." Melzer projects "CPI will move up sharply in 1994 and will be even higher in 1995." Kelley notes the "fourth-quarter CPI of 3.4 percent, which looks a bit ominous" and says "There's dust on the horizon!" Phillips says "we're now seeing some major risks on the inflation front." Multiple participants note commodity price increases, lumber, concrete prices rising. However, current inflation numbers "still look pretty good" (Phillips on PPI and deflator). The center of gravity is: inflation is currently contained but rising risks are significant and disinflation is ending. Direction: somewhat above midpoint -- not yet high inflation but clear upward pressure perceived. Score ~55-58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: McDonough says "the gap between actual and potential GDP is now quite small, and certainly that which remains will be used up in the course of 1994." Parry says growth "has narrowed the gap between actual and potential GDP and has brought unemployment down close to most estimates of the natural rate." Melzer says "both capacity utilization and unemployment are approaching levels that raise concerns." Multiple participants describe 3%+ growth, "momentum," and some use the word "boom." Kelley says he wouldn't take "a whole lot for me to begin to use the word... 'boom.'" Greenspan himself says growth "may be running over 5 percent." The economy is at or slightly above potential, with the gap nearly closed. Direction: at or modestly above potential. Score ~52-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants report labor shortages: McTeer (trucking, construction), Syron ("tight labor conditions in the building trades"), Stern ("growing reports of labor shortages"), Jordan ("shortages of certain classes of labor... under 4 percent unemployment"), Melzer ("labor shortages have curtailed industrial expansion"), Hoenig ("shortage of labor" in housing). Parry says unemployment is "down close to most estimates of the natural rate." McDonough says unemployment "coming down to what we think is a reasonable estimate of the NAIRU--in the low 6 percent area." Stern notes "not at the unskilled end... but at levels above that clearly there are growing reports." However, Boehne says "still is a reluctance to hire" and Phillips notes "labor market is still showing some signs of stress due to layoffs and re-engineering." Direction: near NAIRU with some tightness, especially in specific sectors. Score ~52-56.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Overwhelmingly positive. Forrestal: "retail sales have been quite good." Phillips: "Housing, autos, durables... People are spending and they're dissaving to do so." LaWare: "fundamentals of housing, autos, capacity utilization, new orders, inventory levels... all pointing toward continued growth, even stronger growth." Kelley: "very strong housing numbers and very strong new orders data." Greenspan: "very dramatic increase in residential construction and the big increase in motor vehicle sales." Broaddus: "economy is moving ahead nationally at a good clip." Multiple districts report strong growth. Direction: clearly above trend. Score ~60-65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Jordan: "All of the lenders in the District are talking about very strong credit demands... I haven't heard any reference to the notion of a credit crunch in some time now." Phillips: "strong financial markets are continuing to provide financing, and banks are better positioned to contribute." Kelley: "a rather euphoric stock market; it's up 11 percent in the last four months." LaWare: "euphoria in the financial markets probably has bloated stock and bond prices beyond rational values." Hoenig mentions "bandwagon effect" in fund flows into investment opportunities. Melzer notes "stance of monetary policy has been very expansionary for about the last three years." Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative. Score ~20-25. Lower = looser.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the meeting where the Committee unanimously votes for a 25bp rate hike -- the first tightening in five years. A significant contingent (Melzer, Jordan, Lindsey, Hoenig, Stern, Parry, Phillips, LaWare, McTeer, Broaddus) preferred 50bp. Greenspan pushed hard for 25bp with the announcement effect. Nobody advocated for no action (Forrestal came closest but ultimately supported 25bp). The debate was 25 vs 50bp, not whether to move. This is clearly hawkish. Direction: moderate to strong hawkish. Score ~65-70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The final vote was unanimous at Greenspan's strong urging ("I rarely ask this... I really request that we not do this"). But the debate was vigorous -- roughly half the Committee wanted 50bp vs 25bp. Melzer, Jordan, Lindsey, Hoenig, Stern, Parry, Phillips, LaWare, McTeer, Broaddus all expressed some preference for 50bp (though several said they could live with 25bp). Greenspan made an impassioned plea ("I have a pain in the pit of my stomach"). The unanimous vote masks real disagreement about magnitude. Direction: moderate differences -- substantive debate about magnitude, but unanimous on direction. Score ~38-45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan: "the stock market is at an elevated level at this stage by any measure." Kelley: "euphoric stock market... these kinds of developments give me concern that we could possibly be heading for something that might look distressingly like a bust." LaWare: "euphoria in the financial markets probably has bloated stock and bond prices beyond rational values." Greenspan explicitly says 50bp could "crack these markets." Hoenig mentions "bandwagon effect" in fund flows. Multiple participants discuss financial markets but primarily in service of the policy decision rather than as a standalone concern. Direction: moderate attention. Score ~30-38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee clearly signals more tightening ahead. Greenspan: "I would not mind moving again at the next meeting or the meeting after that." Jordan: "understanding that there may be a conference call if conditions seem to warrant another 25 even before the March meeting." Greenspan: "if this economy continues to move... I would be strongly supportive of much more rapid and, frankly, larger moves." McDonough frames 25bp as "the first of a series of moves." Lindsey: "the staff... said that to maintain not price stability but constant inflation we have to raise interest rates 150 basis points by mid-1995." Direction: clearly hawkish forward signal. Score ~70-75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Forrestal mentions "uncertainty in the economy" and lists several sources (income/debt ratios, trading partners, health plan). Kelley says "I am not sure whether we are early or just right or late." Phillips mentions "health care uncertainties are still unknown." But broadly, participants express fairly high confidence in the economic outlook and the need to move. The main uncertainty is about the magnitude and timing, not the direction. Direction: normal to somewhat elevated. Score ~30-35.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- **OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Both in the low 50s -- economy near potential, labor market near NAIRU with pockets of tightness. Coherent.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Both firmly hawkish (65-70 and 70-75). Forward guidance is somewhat more hawkish than current action because the 25bp was a compromise; the signal is "more to come." Coherent.
- **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS low (loose) + HAWKISH lean**: Loose financial conditions motivating hawkish policy. Coherent.

### Polarity Verifications
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows rising concerns. Score above 50. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy near/slightly above potential. Score slightly above 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose/accommodative. Score below 50. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Committee voted to tighten, many wanted 50bp. Score well above 50. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "Growth in the employment cost index... has been stuck at 3-1/2 percent for quite a while. At current interest rates... we would not be able to expect any further progress in bringing inflation down in 1994 and clearly not in 1995."
- MR. KELLEY: "that fourth-quarter CPI of 3.4 percent, which looks a bit ominous... just about all of the commodity price indexes... seem to have a rather persistent upward drift... 'There's dust on the horizon!'"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "we're now seeing some major risks on the inflation front... commodity price increases working their way into the PPI and CPI."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see current inflation as low but with the disinflationary trend ending and upward pressures emerging from labor tightness, commodity prices, and strong demand -- modestly above neutral concern level.

**Minority views:** Syron and Broaddus project slightly lower inflation than the Greenbook (conditional on prompt Fed action). No one sees deflation risk. The spread is between "current numbers look good" and "CPI will move up sharply."

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the gap between actual and potential GDP is now quite small, and certainly that which remains will be used up in the course of 1994."
- MR. PARRY: "strong growth in the second half of last year certainly... has narrowed the gap between actual and potential GDP and has brought unemployment down close to most estimates of the natural rate."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Growth "may be running over 5 percent. This is not sustainable growth."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy has nearly closed the output gap and is at or slightly above potential, with strong momentum suggesting it will clearly exceed potential soon if not already.

**Minority views:** Parry notes California still weak; Forrestal notes some remaining uncertainty. But nearly all participants agree the gap is small or closing fast.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 54 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "we have low unemployment rates in most of the District states now... growing reports of labor shortages... not at the unskilled end of the labor market, but at levels above that."
- MR. JORDAN: "shortages of certain classes of labor and the difficulty of hiring. Some of our major manufacturing areas are reporting under 4 percent unemployment."
- MR. MELZER: "in at least two parts of the District... labor shortages have curtailed industrial expansion."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor market is near NAIRU with growing pockets of tightness, especially in construction, skilled trades, and manufacturing, but not yet generalized severe tightness. Wage pressure is not yet widespread.

**Minority views:** Stern notes shortages "have not translated into discernible wage pressures yet." Phillips notes labor market stress from layoffs and re-engineering. Some regions still have slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Housing, autos, durables, clearly reflected in the IP index. People are spending and they're dissaving to do so. Inventories are lean."
- MR. KELLEY: "The new data... look quite strong to me... very strong housing numbers and very strong new orders data... it wouldn't take a whole lot for me to begin to use the word... 'boom.'"
- MR. LAWARE: "fundamentals of housing, autos, capacity utilization, new orders, inventory levels, and increased loan activity of banks are all pointing toward continued growth, even stronger growth than we previously expected."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Spending and investment are running clearly above trend, driven by housing, autos, and durables. Multiple districts report robust activity with strong momentum.

**Minority views:** California remains weak (Parry). Some commercial real estate still soft. But the overwhelming weight of discussion is on strength.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "All of the lenders in the District are talking about very strong credit demands--commercial, consumer, and residential. I haven't heard any reference to the notion of a credit crunch in some time now."
- MR. KELLEY: "we seem to me to be experiencing a rather euphoric stock market; it's up 11 percent in the last four months."
- MR. MELZER: "the stance of monetary policy has been very expansionary for about the last three years."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are clearly loose -- strong credit flows, euphoric stock market, accommodative monetary policy, no credit constraints. Participants see conditions as highly supportive and possibly excessively so.

**Minority views:** No participant describes conditions as tight. The only concern is that conditions may be too loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 67 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "There's a time to talk, a time for speculation, and a time to act. We've talked, we've had speculation, and I think now is the time to act."
- MR. LINDSEY: "the staff... said that to maintain not price stability but constant inflation we have to raise interest rates 150 basis points by mid-1995."
- MR. MELZER: "I favor 50 basis points rather than a more modest amount... we are late in acting; too small a policy shift may not convince markets of our resolve."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if this economy continues to move... I would be strongly supportive of much more rapid and, frankly, larger moves."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous agreement to tighten policy. Roughly half the committee preferred 50bp over 25bp. The debate was entirely about how much to tighten, not whether. Greenspan advocated 25bp due to market concerns, not economic disagreement.

**Minority views:** Forrestal was closest to not moving ("I would just like to interject the argument for waiting a little") but ultimately supported 25bp. No one advocated easing or holding unchanged.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Roughly 10 participants expressed preference for 50bp: Melzer, Jordan, Lindsey, Hoenig, Stern, Parry, Phillips, LaWare, McTeer, Broaddus.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I rarely feel strongly about an issue, and I very rarely sort of press this Committee... I have a pain in the pit of my stomach."
- Final vote was unanimous after Greenspan's strong plea: "I really request that we not do this... I rarely ask this."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The vote was unanimous but masked significant disagreement on magnitude. Greenspan had to make an unusually impassioned plea to achieve consensus on 25bp, with roughly half the Committee preferring 50bp. This is notable tension despite unanimity on direction.

**Minority views:** The "minority" here was actually the larger camp (50bp advocates) that deferred to Greenspan's judgment on market impact.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the stock market is at an elevated level at this stage by any measure we know of. We could set off a sequence of events here..."
- MR. KELLEY: "euphoric stock market... concern that we could possibly be heading for something that might look distressingly like a bust somewhere down the road."
- MR. LAWARE: "euphoria in the financial markets probably has bloated stock and bond prices beyond rational values."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Several participants (Greenspan, Kelley, LaWare, Hoenig) raised financial stability concerns, primarily about stock market euphoria and excessive risk-taking. This was a meaningful part of the policy discussion but not the dominant theme.

**Minority views:** Most participants focused on real economy and inflation rather than financial stability per se.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would not mind moving again at the next meeting or the meeting after that... if this economy continues to move... I would be strongly supportive of much more rapid and, frankly, larger moves."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: 25bp "would be interpreted as the first of a series of moves."
- MR. LINDSEY: "to maintain not price stability but constant inflation we have to raise interest rates 150 basis points by mid-1995."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly hawkish. The 25bp move was framed explicitly as the beginning of a tightening cycle, not a one-and-done. Multiple participants expected further moves and Greenspan agreed to a conference call if conditions warranted sooner action.

**Minority views:** Syron cautioned against "fairly fixed notions of where I'm going at the end of it in terms of rates" and preferred data dependence.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "the uncertainty is engendered by the income/debt ratios... the uncertainties surrounding the economies of our trading partners... the possible effects of fiscal policy--including the uncertainty surrounding the health plan."
- MR. KELLEY: "I am not sure whether we are early or just right or late; I don't know how history is going to judge that."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Rate of growth "will come down. And the way a 3 percent growth feels... is a lot different from the way the expansion feels now."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express normal-to-slightly-elevated uncertainty. There are standard caveats about fiscal policy and health care, but the broad tone is confident about economic trajectory and the need for action. The main uncertainty is about calibration (how much/how fast) rather than direction.

**Minority views:** Forrestal expressed the most uncertainty but was an outlier in that regard.
