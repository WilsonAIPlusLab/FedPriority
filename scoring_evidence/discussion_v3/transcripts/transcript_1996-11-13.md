# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-11-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. It covers a full go-around of District reports and national economic assessments, followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Parry, Moskow, Minehan, Broaddus, Boehne, Guynn, McTeer, Stern, Hoenig, Melzer, McDonough, Jordan, Meyer, Rivlin, Yellen, Lindsey, Phillips, Kelley, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
November 1996: Mid-expansion, unemployment at 5.2%, economy growing near trend after a strong first half. CPI running near 3%. The "New Economy" debate about whether NAIRU has shifted lower is underway. Stock market rising sharply (Dow at ~6,000). The Fed has held the funds rate at 5.25% since January 1996.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as contained but with upside risks. Nearly every speaker notes that despite tight labor markets, inflation has not materialized. Parry: "underlying inflation has shown a slight downward trend...despite the low unemployment rate." Moskow: "competitive pressures make raising prices extremely difficult." Minehan: "pressures on overall prices are considered to be low." Yellen: "every broad measure of inflation other than the total CPI has been trending downward since 1993." However, many note that CPI is near 3% and the Greenbook projects a modest upward creep. Melzer: "CPI inflation has been trending up, that 3 percent inflation is too high." Hoenig: "that still reflects an upward move in the inflation rate." The center of gravity is: inflation is currently well-behaved/contained but there are acknowledged upside risks from tight labor markets. Current inflation near 3% CPI is slightly above the implicit comfort zone. Direction: slightly above neutral but not alarming. Score should be around 48-52 -- current inflation is contained, but there's a modest upward drift acknowledged. The actual price data are benign (commodities falling, ECI moderate), but CPI is near 3%. I'll score 48. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "contained but with upward risks acknowledged." 48 fits -- just below midpoint, reflecting the benign current readings with acknowledged risks.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants broadly describe the economy as having slowed to trend or slightly below trend growth after a strong first half. Parry: "real GDP growth to fall below its 2 percent trend this quarter." Meyer: "the economy has slowed to trend growth." Broaddus: "the economy is now moving back to a more sustainable growth path." Greenspan: "the outlook may have shifted toward slightly slower growth." However, many note that the LEVEL of activity remains high. Broaddus: "the overall level of activity seems to remain high." Moskow: "resource utilization rates are high and slack is limited." The economy is near or slightly above potential in level terms but growth has moderated. The bright-line rule: multiple speakers say "high level of activity," "resource utilization rates are high," "slack is limited" -- this is above-potential language. Score must be >50. Around 52-55. I'll go 53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Virtually every speaker describes labor markets as tight. Moskow: "Labor markets remain tight in many parts of the District." Stern: "Labor markets remain very tight, and I have no doubt that there are outright labor shortages." McTeer: "many reports of labor market tightness." Melzer: "unemployment in several Eighth District states remains near 20-year lows." Greenspan: "labor markets unquestionably are still quite tight." Unemployment at 5.2%, near or below estimated NAIRU of 5.6%. Wage pressures are present but modest. This is clearly tight. Score around 60-65. The tightness is real and broad-based, with shortages reported, but wage pass-through to prices is limited. I'll score 62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending slowed notably in Q3 but fundamentals remain strong. Minehan: "slower growth in consumer spending in the third quarter." Yellen: "consumer spending stalled" in Q3. But most expect a rebound. Phillips: "retail surveys are pointing to a reasonably strong holiday season." Greenspan notes housing is weakening. Business investment is still solid. Broaddus: "continued, quite marked strength in commercial real estate." Phillips: "Business fixed investment...has been holding up better than most forecasts." The net picture is near trend with some Q3 softness. Score around 48-52. The Q3 weakness pulls it slightly below trend but investment is solid. I'll go 49.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described as generally accommodative. Long-term interest rates have fallen. Phillips: "the cost of external capital is low as a result of low interest rates and the rise in stock prices." Lindsey discusses the stock market at elevated levels. Bond rates have declined. Credit is available. Jordan notes some tightening in consumer credit (banks tightening standards, rising delinquencies), and Yellen mentions "lending standards tightened." But overall conditions are loose -- stock market surging, financing available, long rates declining. Score around 30-35, somewhat loose. I'll go 33.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The vast majority support holding steady ("B" asymmetric = unchanged rate with tightening bias). Broaddus and Melzer explicitly favor a 25bp hike. Minehan says she "could go with a 25 basis point increase" but supports the Chairman. Hoenig says he'd "be inclined to move now" but accepts B asymmetric. Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, and Kelley express less conviction about the asymmetric tilt. No one favors easing. The center of gravity is: hold steady with tightening bias, but the urgency for tightening has diminished. This is lean hawkish: 53-58. Two explicit dissenters for tightening, several more sympathetic. I'll score 55.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broaddus and Melzer favor a 25bp hike. Minehan and Hoenig lean that way but accept the hold. Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer feel less asymmetric. Most support B asymmetric but with varying degrees of conviction. No formal dissents in the vote. This is moderate disagreement -- distinct camps but broad agreement on the action. Score 35-40. I'll go 37.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Lindsey gives a lengthy discussion of stock market overvaluation and potential bubble. Minehan: "We are risking a major asset price bubble." Hoenig: "concerned about signs of a potential financial bubble." Yellen: "share Governor Lindsey's and President Minehan's concern about euphoria in asset markets." Rivlin: "the clearly overvalued stock market might head down, perhaps sharply." Jordan discusses consumer credit quality/banking concerns at length. This is more attention than typical for the mid-1990s. Several participants raise it substantively. Score 35-45. I'll go 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is a tightening bias. Greenspan: "it would be premature to pull back on the asymmetry." Parry: "these considerations make it likely that we will need to tighten policy in the future." Broaddus wants action now and signals more needed. But several note that the need feels less urgent. Jordan and Rivlin feel less asymmetric. Lindsey: "we are going to be there for quite a while." The forward lean is modestly hawkish -- maintain tightening bias but actual tightening not imminent. Score 57-62. I'll go 58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate uncertainty. Greenspan: "these developments leave us just about where we have been recently" -- not highly uncertain. Multiple speakers note uncertainty about consumer spending, the labor market/NAIRU relationship, and productivity measurement. Yellen expresses two-sided uncertainty about consumption. Meyer: "We should not...be lulled." Phillips: "there are some uncertainties." This is somewhat elevated but not extreme. Score 35-42. I'll go 38.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Labor is tighter than output gap suggests. This makes sense because the economy has slowed to near trend growth but the unemployment rate remains very low -- a legacy of the strong first half. The level of employment is tight even as growth moderates.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Aligned -- lean hawkish with tightening bias forward. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because the asymmetric directive explicitly signals readiness to tighten.

### Speaker Count by Direction (Policy)
- Favor 25bp hike now: Broaddus, Melzer (2)
- Lean toward hike but accept hold: Minehan, Hoenig (2)
- Support hold + asymmetric: Parry, Moskow, McDonough, Guynn, Phillips, Yellen, Lindsey, Meyer, Stern, Greenspan (10)
- Support hold but feel less asymmetric: Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, Kelley (4)
- Favor easing: none (0)

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. PARRY: "underlying inflation has shown a slight downward trend according to most measures despite the low unemployment rate"
- MS. YELLEN: "every broad measure of inflation other than the total CPI has been trending downward since 1993"
- MR. MELZER: "CPI inflation has been trending up...3 percent inflation is too high"
- MR. MOSKOW: "most firms continue to report that competitive pressures make raising prices extremely difficult"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation readings are contained and broad measures show stable-to-declining trends, though CPI is near 3% and the Greenbook projects a modest upward creep. The center of gravity sees benign current conditions with acknowledged upside risks from tight labor markets.

**Minority views**: Melzer and Hoenig see a clear upward inflation trend that is already problematic. Jordan sees no mechanism for acceleration. These widen the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. MOSKOW: "resource utilization rates are high and slack is limited"
- MR. MEYER: "the economy has slowed to trend growth"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the overall level of activity seems to remain high...labor markets are still quite tight"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the outlook may have shifted toward slightly slower growth, but only at the margin"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy has slowed from above-trend growth to approximately trend, but the level of resource utilization remains high. Multiple participants describe the economy as operating at a high level with limited slack, placing it modestly above potential.

**Minority views**: Jordan sees risks growing on the downside. Meyer acknowledges "more downside than upside risk with respect to GDP growth" in the near term.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. STERN: "Labor markets remain very tight, and I have no doubt that there are outright labor shortages that are restraining activity"
- MR. MELZER: "unemployment in several Eighth District states remains near 20-year lows"
- MR. MCTEER: "many reports of labor market tightness...restaurants offering signing bonuses for new employees"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "labor markets unquestionably are still quite tight"

**Center-of-gravity**: Nearly every participant describes labor markets as tight, with unemployment at 5.2% (near or below estimated NAIRU). Widespread anecdotal reports of shortages and difficulty finding workers. Wage pressures are moderate but real, though not yet passing through to prices.

**Minority views**: Minehan notes "anecdotal reports of labor shortages...appear to have abated in the last couple of months" in New England. Boehne describes "mixed" labor markets in Philadelphia.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 49 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. YELLEN: "consumer spending stalled, the growth of consumer credit plummeted, and lending standards tightened" in Q3
- MR. BROADDUS: "final demand especially for households was very weak in the third quarter"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Business fixed investment, including nonresidential construction, has been holding up better than most forecasts"
- MR. GUYNN: "evidence that growth in consumer spending and business investment may be slowing"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending weakened notably in Q3 (a puzzle to many), while business investment and commercial construction remained solid. Most expect a consumption rebound, but it remains a forecast not a fact. Net position is approximately at trend, with Q3 consumer weakness offset by solid investment.

**Minority views**: Yellen flags meaningful downside risk to consumption from debt burdens and potential stock market correction. Jordan cites banking concerns about consumer credit quality suggesting weaker demand ahead.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "the cost of external capital is low as a result of low interest rates and the rise in stock prices. Banks are continuing to provide financing"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the recent drop in long-term interest rates suggests that inflation expectations have diminished"
- MS. YELLEN: "lending standards tightened" (on consumer credit side)
- MR. JORDAN: community bankers reporting rising delinquencies and tightening credit quality standards

**Center-of-gravity**: Overall financial conditions are loose -- long-term rates have declined, stock market surging, corporate financing readily available. However, there is an emerging pocket of tightness in consumer credit (rising delinquencies, tighter lending standards, rising bankruptcies). The dominant signal is accommodative conditions.

**Minority views**: Jordan highlights significant consumer credit stress from bankers. Guynn notes banks "beginning to pay closer attention to credit quality."

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would recommend 'B' asymmetric...it would be premature to pull back on the asymmetry"
- MR. BROADDUS: "I would still favor a 1/4 point increase in the funds rate today"
- MR. MELZER: "I am going to advocate a modest increase in the funds rate"
- MS. RIVLIN: "I support 'B' asymmetric, but like President Jordan I do not feel nearly as 'asymmetric' as I did at the time of the last meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: The overwhelming majority supports holding the funds rate steady with a tightening bias. Two participants (Broaddus, Melzer) favor an immediate 25bp hike. Several others (Minehan, Hoenig) lean toward hiking but accept the hold. A meaningful minority (Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, Kelley) feels less hawkish than before and questions the asymmetric tilt. No one favors easing. This is a lean-hawkish hold.

**Minority views**: Broaddus/Melzer want immediate tightening (hawkish wing). Jordan/Rivlin/McTeer feel the tightening bias is becoming less warranted (less-hawkish wing).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. BROADDUS: favors 25bp hike, explicitly disagrees with hold
- MR. MELZER: advocates 25bp hike, explicitly disagrees with hold
- MS. RIVLIN: "do not feel nearly as 'asymmetric' as I did at the time of the last meeting"
- MR. JORDAN: supports asymmetry "only because we used it in the directives for the previous two meetings"

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissents in the vote, but two participants explicitly favor a different action (25bp hike). Two more lean toward hiking but acquiesce. Four others express reduced conviction about the tightening bias. Notable disagreement on degree/direction but consensus on the actual vote.

**Minority views**: The spread ranges from "hike now" (Broaddus, Melzer) to "feel less asymmetric" (Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer), a meaningful range of views.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- MR. LINDSEY: Extended discussion of stock market overvaluation -- "we are erecting buildings in Boston again...valuations on financial assets...pose a problem for the future"
- MS. MINEHAN: "We are risking a major asset price bubble unless some sense of caution...is injected into these financial markets"
- MS. YELLEN: "I also share Governor Lindsey's and President Minehan's concern about euphoria in asset markets"
- MR. JORDAN: Lengthy discussion of consumer credit quality deterioration, rising bankruptcies, banking concerns

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives moderate attention. Lindsey's extended stock market discussion is a centerpiece. Several participants (Minehan, Hoenig, Yellen, Melzer) echo bubble/euphoria concerns. Jordan raises banking/consumer credit stress. This is above the typical 1990s baseline but not a dominant topic.

**Minority views**: Most participants focus on the real economy rather than financial stability, so this is driven by a vocal minority of 4-5 speakers who discuss it at length.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "As long as the labor markets remain as tight as they are, inflation can turn up on a dime"
- MR. PARRY: "these considerations make it likely that we will need to tighten policy in the future"
- MR. LINDSEY: "we are going to be there [asymmetric] for quite a while because I do not see any signs that we are going to be removing inflation pressures"
- MR. GUYNN: "I only hope that we are prepared to move if in fact the upside risks...materialize"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is a maintained tightening bias -- the asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten. Multiple participants note tightening will likely be needed eventually. However, the urgency has diminished compared to the prior meeting. The signal is mildly hawkish: prepared to tighten if needed but no imminent action expected.

**Minority views**: Jordan sees no mechanism for inflation acceleration, implying the tightening bias is less warranted. Yellen considers it "perfectly possible" that demand could moderate enough to eliminate any need for tightening.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the wage structure is extraordinarily dynamic and not easy to read...our deficient understanding of the true structure of costs, benefits, and the like should create a degree of humbleness on our part"
- MS. YELLEN: extensive two-sided uncertainty about consumption -- "I could build a case" for either direction
- MR. BROADDUS: "I don't think we understand this weakness or the related increase in the saving rate very clearly at this point"
- MR. MOSKOW: "there is a good deal of uncertainty about these adjustments in the inflation forecasts"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about: (1) the NAIRU/wage-price relationship, (2) why consumer spending stalled in Q3 and whether it will rebound, (3) productivity measurement. Greenspan's extensive discussion of data limitations adds to the uncertainty tone. However, the overall outlook is not deeply uncertain -- most agree on the broad picture of moderate growth.

**Minority views**: Jordan raises distinct concerns about downside risks that others largely dismiss.
