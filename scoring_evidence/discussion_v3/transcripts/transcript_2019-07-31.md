# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring v3 — July 30–31, 2019

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of individual participant statements from the July 30–31, 2019 FOMC meeting. The document runs 329 pages and covers three distinct sessions:

- **Day 1 Morning (July 30)**: Framework review go-around — participants address the Policy Framework Review questions (strengths/weaknesses of current FIT, tools, potential changes to the SLRGMPS). This is largely a theoretical/institutional discussion and NOT an economic conditions assessment. Speakers: Clarida, Williams, Rosengren, Mester, Evans, Bullard, Daly, Barkin, Harker, George, Bowman, Bostic, Kashkari, Kaplan, Quarles, Brainard, Powell (framework closing remarks).
- **Day 1 Afternoon (July 30)**: Financial Stability go-around — participants comment on the QS (Quantitative Surveillance) assessment. Speakers: Mester, Rosengren, Evans, Barkin, Bowman, Quarles, George, Brainard.
- **Day 1 Evening / Day 2 Morning (July 31)**: Economic conditions go-around (policy prefaced remarks) — participants assess the current economic outlook and announce their policy preferences. Speakers include Daly, Bostic, Barkin, Harker, Evans, Kaplan, George, Bowman, Quarles, Williams, Powell (economic go-round closing), followed by the policy go-round.
- **Day 2 Policy Go-Round (July 31)**: All participants state their policy votes and rationale. This is the richest source for HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN, FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE, and DISSENT_INTENSITY.

**Scoring approach**: I score based ONLY on participant views expressed about CURRENT economic/financial conditions and policy preferences, drawing primarily from the economic and policy go-rounds. Framework review discussion is relevant for UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED and for background context on inflation and r* but should not be treated as statements about current conditions.

**Speaker count (policy go-round)**: ~17 voting participants + Powell. Key voices:
- **Pro-cut (Alternative B, 25bp)**: Clarida, Daly, Bullard, Evans (prefers A but accepts B), Kaplan, Brainard, Bowman, Harker (very reluctant), Williams, Vice Chair Williams, Quarles (reluctant), Powell
- **Anti-cut / Alt-C (hold)**: Mester, George, Rosengren, Bostic
- **Alt-A (50bp cut)**: Kashkari (formal dissent for more easing)
- **Harker**: supports 25bp cut reluctantly but opposes the balance sheet early-end language

### 2. Era Context

**Mid-2019**: The U.S. was in its longest expansion on record. The FOMC had been in a normalization phase (9 hikes from Dec 2015 through Dec 2018), then paused in early 2019, and was now executing a "mid-cycle insurance cut." The specific era backdrop:
- Unemployment near 50-year lows (~3.7%)
- Core PCE inflation persistently below 2% (running ~1.6% as of June 2019)
- Trade policy uncertainty (US-China tensions), weak global growth
- Inverted yield curve (3m/10y)
- Fed balance sheet runoff still ongoing
- r* and u* estimates declining

The text itself is the authoritative source; the era context confirms what I read.

### 3. Directional Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**:
Participants almost uniformly describe inflation as BELOW target. Core PCE is 1.6% as of June 2019. Multiple speakers reference persistent undershooting. Vice Chair Williams: "core PCE inflation is around 1½ percent. It's well below our goal." Clarida: "breakeven inflation rates remain some 30 basis points below the levels that prevailed before the financial crisis." Evans: "there simply isn't any meaningful evidence of building inflation pressures… they are still too low." Bowman: "12-month change in core PCE prices through the end of June was just 1.6 percent."
**Direction**: Clearly BELOW target. Score must be < 50. The language is "persistently below," "stubbornly below," "muted" — not alarming deflation risk, but meaningfully below 2%. This maps to the 36–42 range.
Minority views: Barkin and Bostic see it as closer to target, citing the Dallas trimmed mean at ~2% and recent monthly readings running above 2% annualized. Mester and Quarles are less alarmed. But the center of gravity is still below-target.

**Magnitude check**: "Running below," "persistently below," "stubbornly below" = 8–12 points below 50. Score ~38–42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**:
Participants describe growth at or slightly above trend. Growth in 2019 is ~2–2.25%, described as "near potential" or "above trend." Powell: "real GDP growth will edge down to roughly potential." Evans: "real GDP rising a bit under 2½ percent in 2019." George: "expect real GDP growth to stabilize close to its trend rate." Kaplan: "2 to 2¼ percent... close to the trend." Bostic: "economy is, and looks like it will remain, in a good place."
However, the business investment and manufacturing sectors are weak. But consumption is strong. The center of gravity is at or slightly above potential.
**Direction**: AT or slightly above potential. Score: 50–55.
The bright-line rule: some participants say "above trend" (Bostic, Barkin at points), some say "at trend" (Powell, George). Splitting: 52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**:
Near-unanimous view that labor markets are historically tight. Unemployment at ~3.6–3.7%, lowest in 50 years. Bowman: "unemployment rate remains at its lowest level in decades." Barkin: "entry hiring is very tight." Kaplan: "contacts continue to report difficulty finding workers as a top challenge." Quarles: "labor market continues to be the best in generations, with low unemployment, better-than-expected labor force participation." George: "Unemployment rates remain at historically low levels." Mester: "labor market conditions will remain strong."
**Direction**: VERY TIGHT. Well above the 53–65 "tight" range. Maps to 65–72.
Some dovish participants emphasize that this tightness is NOT generating wage/inflation pressure as historically expected (flat Phillips curve), so the tightness isn't creating imbalances. But the raw tightness level is undeniable.
Score: 68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**:
Mixed picture with a net assessment of moderate strength. Consumer spending is robust (PCE surged 4.3% in Q2), consumer confidence high. But business fixed investment is weak (-0.6% Q2), manufacturing soft, residential investment contracted. Powell: "PCE surged at an annual rate of 4.3 percent. Business fixed investment, however, was down 0.6 percent." Williams: "Robust consumer spending is balanced by weakness in housing, business and fixed investment, and exports." George: "expansion relies heavily on solid growth in household spending." The net sentiment is "consumer-led moderate expansion" with investment weakness.
**Direction**: Slightly above trend due to consumer strength, but offset by investment drag. NET moderate positive.
The rubric's 46–54 range covers "at trend/moderate pace." Given the strong consumer but weak investment, the center of gravity lands at about 52.
Score: 52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**:
Financial conditions are described as LOOSE/ACCOMMODATIVE. Powell: "Financial conditions improved and are now much more growth friendly." Brainard: "Financial conditions are very accommodative, with borrowing rates low and the stock market at all-time highs." Williams references "favorable financial conditions." Bowman: "financing conditions for both households and businesses appear favorable." The early-2019 tightening that followed December 2018 has largely reversed. Asset prices are near historical highs. Credit is widely available.
**Direction**: LOOSE. This is financial CONDITIONS (not policy tightness). Score < 50.
Rubric: 31–45 = "somewhat loose to neutral; generally supportive." The language "very accommodative" and "more growth friendly" and "stock market at all-time highs" pushes toward the lower end of this range, maybe 33–38.
Score: 35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**:
The Committee voted to cut 25bp (Alternative B). Counting positions:
- **Alt-A (50bp cut + forward guidance)**: Kashkari (1 participant)
- **Alt-B (25bp cut)**: Clarida, Daly, Bullard, Evans (with reservations), Kaplan, Brainard, Williams, Bowman, Quarles (reluctant), Harker (very reluctant on balance sheet), Powell (~10 participants)
- **Alt-C (hold)**: Mester, George, Rosengren, Bostic (~4 participants)

The center of gravity is a 25bp cut with a data-dependent forward signal — not aggressive easing nor aggressive hawkishness. The rubric says 26–40 = "moderate dovish: majority favors some easing; provide additional support." A 25bp cut where the majority wants data dependence going forward maps to approximately 32–38.

Kashkari's 50bp preference pulls slightly more dovish. The 4 "hold" dissenters pull slightly more hawkish. The actual decision is a 25bp cut with a deliberately ambiguous forward signal.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. A 25bp cut with data dependence is BELOW 50 (dovish). Score ~33.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**:
This is a high-dissent meeting. Formal/functional dissents:
- Kashkari: votes for Alternative A (50bp), NOT Alternative B. This is a formal dissent to the LEFT (more dovish).
- George: explicitly supports Alternative C (hold). This is a formal dissent to the RIGHT (more hawkish).
- Rosengren: "no need for additional monetary accommodation at this time" — also effectively Alternative C dissent.
- Mester: explicitly supports "no change." Also effectively Alternative C.
- Bostic: "I support the policy action as described in alternative C." Formal dissent.
- Harker: "very reluctantly" supports the 25bp cut but "cannot support alternative B as written" due to the balance sheet language. Partial dissent.
- Barkin: "today's move makes me uncomfortable" and implicitly sides with the Alt-C camp in spirit, though does not formally state Alt-C.

So we have: 1 dovish dissent (Kashkari, 50bp) + at least 3–4 hawkish dissents (George, Rosengren, Mester, Bostic) + 1 partial dissent (Harker). The disagreement extends to both direction AND the rationale for any action. Multiple clear camps.

Rubric: 61–75 = "Significant division: clear camps with different prescriptions; multiple dissents; sharp exchanges." This is firmly in that range given 4+ functional dissents.
Score: 65.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**:
A full session of Day 1 was devoted to the Financial Stability go-around. Multiple participants addressed it at length:
- Mester: high corporate debt, CRE valuations, leveraged lending concerns.
- Rosengren: detailed analysis of co-working (WeWork) models and CRE risks.
- Evans: seconded Mester's concerns.
- Barkin: asked for more forward-looking dynamic assessment.
- Bowman: detailed analysis of private credit markets and agricultural lending.
- Quarles: extensive remarks on CCyB, stress tests, CLOs, overall assessment of "moderate" vulnerabilities but with "frothy" conditions.
- George: G-SIB leverage concerns, capital falling while risks rising.
- Brainard: strong emphasis on valuations near peaks, capital ratios falling, countercyclical capital buffer needs.
- Powell: "overall, such vulnerabilities are moderate... asset prices elevated... leveraged lending bears close watching."
- Multiple participants in the policy go-round reference financial stability in their rationale.

This is well above typical for this era. Financial stability was a prominent topic, discussed at length by most participants.
Rubric: 65–75 = "Prominent topic; most participants discuss at length." Score: 68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**:
The forward guidance is deliberately ambiguous. The Committee cut 25bp but wanted to preserve optionality for September. Key signals:
- Powell: "I see the Committee as adjusting policy midcycle to a somewhat more accommodative stance, not beginning an extended loosening cycle."
- Williams: "a further rate cut may well be warranted this year."
- Bullard, Kaplan: support wait-and-see after this cut.
- George, Mester, Rosengren: would prefer no more cuts without more data.
- Kashkari: advocates explicit forward guidance committing not to raise rates until core inflation reaches target on a sustained basis.

The center of gravity: The cut signals an easing lean, but the Committee is explicitly trying to NOT lock in September. The rubric anchor "35–45 (2019 mid-cycle cuts)" is directly applicable here. The forward guidance is "easing lean with data dependence" = 35–45. Given the deliberate attempt to preserve optionality and the mixed signals, score: 38.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. The signal is moderately dovish (cut made, maybe more, data-dependent) = 35–40.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**:
Multiple participants express elevated uncertainty about the outlook. The key sources:
- Trade policy uncertainty (everyone references it as persistent and not likely to resolve soon).
- Global growth uncertainty (euro area, China, Brexit).
- Uncertainty about r* and u* (Clarida, Williams, Daly, Powell all note estimates have been revised down substantially).
- Flat Phillips curve making the inflation outlook uncertain.
- Uncertainty about effectiveness of unconventional tools.
- Powell: "We are not in complete agreement, but, to a large degree, we are seeing the same things."
- Bowman: "I acknowledge that the downside risks have remained prominent."

This is NOT extreme/unprecedented uncertainty (that would be 2008-09 or March 2020), but it is clearly elevated above baseline. Multiple participants explicitly note "elevated uncertainty" around trade, global growth, and structural variables. Maps to 46–60 range.

Rubric anchor: 40–50 (Brexit, 2015-16 trade tensions) — this period has comparable uncertainty. The trade uncertainty in July 2019 is significant but not catastrophic. Score: 52.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence Check

- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Divergence is intentional and well-documented in the transcript. Labor is very tight (50-year lows) but output growth is only near trend. This divergence reflects the flat Phillips curve environment and elevated u* uncertainty — exactly what many speakers discuss. Acceptable divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (33) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38): Both dovish (below 50). The slightly less dovish forward guidance reflects the Committee's deliberate attempt to avoid committing to September. They are aligned directionally. ✓
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (39) and HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (33): Low inflation pressure → dovish lean. Consistent. ✓
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (35, loose) and HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (33, dovish): Loose financial conditions typically would argue against easing, but participants argue the conditions are loose BECAUSE of expectations of easing, not fundamentally — and that not cutting would tighten conditions. The dovish cut is coherent with the argument that maintaining the rate level would de facto tighten. ✓
