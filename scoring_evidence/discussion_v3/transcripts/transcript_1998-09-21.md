# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 1998-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of a telephone conference call on September 21, 1998. It captures individual participant statements by name. This is NOT a regularly scheduled meeting but an emergency-style consultation call arranged by Chairman Greenspan on short notice to seek Committee endorsement for signaling a likely rate cut at the upcoming September 29 meeting.

### Era Context
September 1998 is the height of the global financial turmoil triggered by the Asian financial crisis (1997-98), Russia's default and devaluation (August 1998), and the LTCM near-collapse. Domestically, the U.S. economy was still relatively strong with low unemployment (~4.5%), but international contagion was hitting credit markets hard. This call is specifically about whether Greenspan can signal an easing move in upcoming Senate testimony.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Inflation is essentially not discussed by any participant. The entire conversation revolves around global financial turmoil, credit market stress, and the risk of economic deterioration. Nobody mentions price pressures, CPI, or inflation concerns. In late 1998, inflation was low and benign. The absence of discussion suggests it is a non-issue. I will score this low but not extremely low -- the silence signals no concern. Hoenig notes "it is unusual to be thinking about easing when we have an unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent," implying a tight labor market that could theoretically produce inflation, but nobody raises this concern. Score around 35-38 (below target, not a concern).

Direction: Participants see inflation as well below the midpoint because it is not mentioned at all; their entire focus is on financial stability and growth risks.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. No inflation discussion in a period of low inflation = low score. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Greenspan says "the economy has been holding up, but it is now showing clear signs of deterioration" and "indications of weakness are becoming more pervasive but by no means dangerous thus far." Boehne: "the economy is still operating at a relatively high level" but there's "a notable change in sentiment." Hoenig: "the domestic economy... remains relatively strong." Broaddus: "still high level of economic activity." The consensus is that the economy is currently at or slightly above potential but deteriorating. The economy hasn't fallen below potential yet.

Direction: Participants see output as currently still near or slightly above potential but with clear deterioration underway.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy still at a high level but weakening. Score around 50-53.
Anchor check: Late 90s boom anchor is 57-63. But this is the moment of recognized deterioration from that boom, so slightly below the boom anchor makes sense.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Hoenig specifically notes "it is unusual to be thinking about easing when we have an unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent." This is the only direct labor market reference. 4.5% unemployment in 1998 was below most estimates of NAIRU. The labor market is tight but it's not a focus of discussion.

Direction: Participants see labor as tight (low unemployment acknowledged) but it is not a major topic.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. 4.5% unemployment = tight. Score around 60-63.
Anchor: 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75; this is slightly before peak tightness. 60-63 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Greenspan notes "it is very hard if history is any guide to believe that growth above 3 percent is possible in the context of the types of decisions that are being made for capital investment and inventories." He mentions "anecdotal indications of some softening" and business pessimism. Boehne says softening "had been largely in the manufacturing area, but it now appears to have spilled out into broader sectors." Guynn's small business advisory group "shifted its overall sentiment substantially." But multiple members note the economy is "still at a high level." This suggests spending/investment is currently still decent but clearly softening.

Direction: Participants see consumption/investment as currently near trend but with clear deterioration emerging.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Still near trend but weakening = slightly below 50. Score around 46-48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant theme of the entire call. Greenspan: "sharp widening in yield spreads is suggesting significant tightening in private debt markets"; "junk bond yields in the United States going up very sharply against Treasuries and indeed the junk market is virtually shutting down"; "opening of spreads in the corporate area is the largest that we have seen in such a short period of time in at least a decade"; "BAAs are showing their highest spreads since 1991." McDonough: "tremendous widening of spreads even in the Treasury market"; "people are reducing their involvement in financial markets"; "cutting back on counterparty exposure lines"; "the market is... starting to feed on itself in a rather dangerous way"; "markets are no longer functioning very well." Equity prices also down.

Direction: Participants see financial conditions as very tight and rapidly tightening further, with market dysfunction.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Massive spread widening, market dysfunction = very high score. Score around 78-82.
Anchor: Sept 2008 credit freeze is 85-95. This is severe but not yet at the 2008 level of systemic crisis. 78-82 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Every single participant supports easing. The entire call is about whether to signal a rate cut. Greenspan proposes 25bp cut. McTeer wants 50bp. Nobody opposes easing. This is clearly dovish. The question is only about size and timing.

Direction: Participants are unanimously dovish, favoring an easing move.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous easing support = low score. Score around 28-32.
Anchor: This isn't emergency QE-level dovish (2-5), but it's a clear consensus for easing. 28-32 reflects "moderate dovish: majority favors some easing."

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is near-unanimity on the direction of policy (ease). Minor differences exist: McTeer wants 50bp vs. Greenspan's 25bp suggestion; Broaddus, Stern, Guynn, and Kelley express slight hesitation on purely domestic grounds but support the move; Stern says "it is not clear to me that a change in domestic monetary policy is the preferable response, but it may be the only available response." These are minor differences about degree and rationale, not fundamental disagreement.

Direction: Low dissent; broad agreement on direction with minor differences in emphasis.
Score around 20-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial stability/market functioning is THE dominant topic of this call. Nearly every speaker addresses it. Greenspan, McDonough, Hoenig, Boehne, Guynn all discuss market stress, credit conditions, international financial turmoil extensively. The call was convened specifically because of financial stability concerns. This is the meeting's raison d'etre.

Direction: Financial stability is the overwhelming focus.
Score around 85-90. This rivals the emphasis level of major crisis meetings.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The entire purpose of this call is to authorize Greenspan to signal a forthcoming rate cut. The forward signal is unambiguously dovish -- easing ahead. Greenspan will "signal even obliquely that we will be moving the federal funds rate lower next Tuesday." Parry calls it "a fairly riskless insurance policy." No one suggests this is a one-and-done; the tone suggests readiness for more if needed.

Direction: Forward signal is clearly dovish/easing.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Easing signal = low score. Score around 25-30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: There is significant uncertainty about the outlook. Greenspan: "element of disengagement, fear, uncertainty, and the like is really pervasive around the world." Rivlin: "significant, although we hope not an overwhelming, possibility that things might move south." Greenspan: "the probability of such deterioration over the next week is quite small, but its consequences are, of course, extraordinarily important." Stern is uncertain whether domestic monetary policy is even the right response. Multiple participants note the unusual nature of the situation. Hoenig: "it is unusual to be thinking about easing when we have an unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent."

Direction: Participants express elevated uncertainty about the economic and financial outlook.
Score around 62-67. There's genuine uncertainty about how bad things could get and whether the global turmoil will infect the real domestic economy.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

- **OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62)**: These are somewhat divergent -- labor market still tight but output gap near zero/slightly positive. This makes sense for this specific moment: the labor market is a lagging indicator, and the economy is at the inflection point of deterioration. The recognized weakening hasn't hit employment yet.

- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (30) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (27)**: These align well -- both dovish, reflecting consensus for easing and a forward signal of easing.

### Speaker Count
Speakers who addressed the committee: Greenspan, McDonough, Rivlin, Minehan, Parry, Boehne, Hoenig, Broaddus, Stern, Guynn, Poole, McTeer, Kelley, Gramlich -- approximately 14 participants. All support easing. Minor variation in enthusiasm level.

**Evidence**:

- MR. HOENIG: "it is unusual to be thinking about easing when we have an unemployment rate of about 4-1/2 percent" -- implying tight labor market conditions that historically would be associated with some inflation risk, but no one raises this.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is entirely absent from the discussion, consistent with the benign price environment of late 1998. The score reflects below-target inflation that is a non-concern.

**Minority views**: None expressed.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 80 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "sharp widening in yield spreads is suggesting significant tightening in private debt markets"; "the junk market is virtually shutting down"; "BAAs are showing their highest spreads since 1991"; "opening of spreads in the corporate area is the largest that we have seen in such a short period of time in at least a decade"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "tremendous widening of spreads"; "people are reducing their involvement in financial markets"; "cutting back on counterparty exposure lines"; "the market is... starting to feed on itself in a rather dangerous way"; "markets are no longer functioning very well"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are very tight and tightening rapidly. Credit markets are severely stressed with the junk market shutting down, spreads at multi-year highs, counterparty risk aversion rising, and signs of market dysfunction. This is clearly well above normal tightness but not yet a full systemic freeze.

**Minority views**: Some regional presidents (Hoenig) note "a lot of activity" still in financial markets despite the stress.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we will be easing our policy next Tuesday"; proposes 25bp cut.
- MR. PARRY: "I strongly support your suggestion... a fairly riskless insurance policy"
- MR. MCTEER: "I agree with your approach and it is very likely that I will argue for 1/2 point"
- All 14 participants who spoke support easing.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous support for easing at the upcoming meeting. The only debate is timing (this week vs. next week) and size (25bp vs. 50bp). This is clearly moderate dovish -- a consensus for easing driven primarily by financial market stress and international turmoil.

**Minority views**: McTeer wants 50bp (more dovish). Broaddus, Stern, Kelley express slight hesitation on purely domestic grounds but still support the move.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- All participants support easing; no opposition to the direction of policy.
- MR. STERN: "it is not clear to me that a change in domestic monetary policy is the preferable response, but it may be the only available response" -- mild reservation about rationale, not direction.
- MR. BROADDUS: supports but "maybe a little less strongly than some of the others"
- MR. MCTEER: argues for 50bp vs. Greenspan's 25bp suggestion -- disagreement on size, not direction.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement with minor nuances. Differences are about emphasis (domestic vs. international rationale), degree of enthusiasm, and the size of the cut -- not about whether to ease.

**Minority views**: The Broaddus/Stern/Guynn/Kelley camp that expresses domestic-economy hesitation is a mild minority viewpoint.

- Nearly every participant references financial market conditions or international financial turmoil.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability is the overwhelming focus and the sole reason for this call. It rivals economic assessment in emphasis and effectively IS the meeting. This is among the highest emphasis levels short of the actual 2008 crisis peak.

**Minority views**: None; all participants acknowledge the severity of financial conditions.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly dovish. The call is specifically about authorizing Greenspan to signal easing in upcoming Senate testimony. The reference to "holding powder" implies readiness for more cuts. The forward lean is easing with data dependence.
