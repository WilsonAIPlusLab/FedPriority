## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the June 18–19, 2013 FOMC meeting. It is a two-day meeting with a full economic go-around on day one (June 18) and a policy discussion on day two (June 19). Individual speakers are clearly identified by name. The document is well-segmented with explicit speaker labels throughout. This is a standard full-meeting transcript with rich participant content.

### 2. Era Context
Mid-2013: The economy was in a slow, grinding post-GFC recovery. The Fed was running QE3 ($85B/month in MBS and Treasuries). Unemployment stood at 7.6%. Inflation was running well below the 2% PCE target — total PCE at ~0.7%, core PCE at ~1.05%, described repeatedly as historic lows. The dominant policy debate concerned when and how to begin "tapering" asset purchases, which Chairman Bernanke was seeking to communicate at this very meeting via the press conference. I will score purely from what the text says.

### 3. Core Dimension Pre-Scoring Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as clearly BELOW the 2% target. Multiple speakers emphasize this is anomalously low — Rosengren: "core PCE index is now at a low for the historical series at 1.05 percent. Total PCE is now at 0.7 percent." Bullard: "Headline PCE inflation...is running at about 70 basis points measured from one year earlier." Williams: "inflation now running well below its 2 percent longer-run target." Yellen: "total and core PCE inflation through April were down 1.2 and 0.8 percentage points, respectively, from a year ago." Fisher notes "trimmed mean PCE ticked down to 1.3 percent." Even those less alarmed (Lockhart, Plosser) treat current low inflation as temporary — no one says inflation is at or above target. The center of gravity is clearly below target, in the disinflation/below-target range. The anchors say 38-42 is the range for 2013-19 below target. Given the extreme nature of the readings described (53-year lows mentioned, first-ever negative trimmed mean), this sits at the lower end of the disinflation range. Score: ~33. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very low pressure. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**

Yellen says "the economy appears to have expanded at roughly a 2 percent pace during the first half of the year despite considerable restraint from fiscal policy." Multiple speakers describe fiscal drag as the main headwind and expect a pickup in H2 2013. The text consistently places the economy below potential but recovering. Anchors for 2010-14 recovery: 35-42. Score: ~38. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Evidence says below. Score below 50. Confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**

Multiple speakers acknowledge improvement since September 2012, but most emphasize what is still missing: participation rate down (Yellen, Kocherlakota), quits still low, long-term unemployment high, no wage inflation visible, employment-population ratio "little changed from last fall" (Rosengren), job gains per month around 155K-200K. The center of gravity is clearly below full employment but somewhat improved from the 2009-2012 depths. Anchors: 35-40 for 2011-14. Score: ~37. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says slack remains but recovering. Score below 50. Confirmed.

However, the dominant discussion was about WHEN and HOW to begin tapering, and most participants appear to support beginning reductions "later this year" (September or December). The Chairman explicitly states "a pretty clear majority for starting today with communicating these contingent plans." Participants who favored Alt C (immediate taper): Plosser, Lacker, Duke, Stein (in ideal world), Fisher (ideal world), George. Participants who favored the B/B' approach (hold now, taper later in year contingent): Lockhart, Williams, Yellen, Evans (more reluctantly), Tarullo, Powell, Dudley (somewhat reluctantly). Participants wanting more accommodation: Rosengren, Bullard (wanted Alt A to signal inflation defense). Kocherlakota most dovish, wanted to wait until July. The meeting's dominant consensus was: maintain current purchases NOW, but signal tapering beginning "later this year" (September-ish) contingent on data. This is a lean-hawkish posture — the forward signaling was firmly about reducing accommodation, and most participants supported beginning tapering in H2 2013. The rubric anchor for 2010-12 QE era is 15-25. The 2014 pre-liftoff is 40-50. This meeting is in between — the QE program is still running, the forward lean is toward beginning exit. Score: ~47. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Evidence says lean toward tapering signaling, but current action held. Score near midpoint, slightly below 50 given QE still running and inflation concerns pulling dovish. Confirmed.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "The core PCE index is now at a low for the historical series at 1.05 percent. Total PCE is now at 0.7 percent."
- Bullard: "Headline PCE inflation...is running at about 70 basis points measured from one year earlier. Other inflation measures, including core PCE inflation, have been running below target. Perhaps most worrisome, all inflation measures have been trending down since the beginning of 2012."
- Fisher: "trimmed mean PCE ticked down to 1.3 percent...the first negative trimmed mean that we've seen in the history of the data series, which goes back to 1977."
- Yellen: "total and core PCE inflation through April were down 1.2 and 0.8 percentage points, respectively, from a year ago...you have to go all the way back to the Volcker era to see such a widespread disinflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually unanimous participant recognition that inflation is running historically low, well below the 2% target across all measures, with breadth of price deceleration across most PCE categories; most treat it as partly transitory but all acknowledge the unusual severity.

**Minority views:** Plosser was "not overly concerned" about inflation and cited upcoming data suggesting a turning point. Lockhart cited morning CPI data as "encouraging." These reflect less alarm but do not argue for inflation at or above target.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Bullard: "2013:Q2...tracking and nowcasting models indicate a growth rate of 1.5 to 2 percent at an annual rate."
- Lacker: "since the end of 2009, the level of real GDP has always been within 0.3 percentage point of a 2.1 percent trend line...I am no longer including a spurt of above-trend growth in my projection."
- Yellen: "the economy appears to have expanded at roughly a 2 percent pace during the first half of the year despite considerable restraint from fiscal policy...we need growth above potential in order to reinforce the employment improvement."
- Bernanke summary: "fiscal headwinds so far have not proved as serious as we were concerned they might be" — acknowledging below-potential growth despite positive surprises.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Growth consistently described as running at or below potential (~2%), with widespread expectation of a pickup in H2 2013 as fiscal drag eases; the economy is below potential but not in deep contraction.

**Minority views:** Williams saw "private spending...resilient" and housing "hot, hot, hot" but still placed overall growth below potential. Fisher was more optimistic on Texas and consumer but acknowledged the national picture.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kocherlakota: "The fraction of people over the age of 16 who have a job remains pretty much the same now as it was in late 2009...It's hard to see an improvement in those conditions."
- Dudley: "the hiring rate remains depressed and has not improved much...The improvement in net hiring has come almost exclusively through a slowdown in the pace of layoffs...the labor market is still far from being healthy."
- Yellen: "the labor force participation rate has fallen by roughly 0.4 percentage point...duration of unemployment remains remarkably high. Quits have edged up slightly but remain far below levels consistent with a normal labor market, and the number of people working part time involuntarily has barely declined."
- Tarullo: "There is quite likely a substantial amount of room for further improvement...There is a substantial amount of need for further accommodation...no evidence of wage inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Despite unemployment declining to 7.6% from 8.1% (progress acknowledged), the dominant participant assessment is of significant remaining labor market slack, with participation decline, elevated long-term unemployment, and absent wage pressure all pointing to substantial distance from full employment.

**Minority views:** George cited diffusion index at 70 (above 2004-07 levels of 60), quit rate moving higher, layoff rate near 10-year low. Fisher noted employment up 71% recovery of lost jobs. Powell cited unemployment 7.3% Tealbook forecast vs 8% September baseline. These reflect improvement but not tightness.
