## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the June 13–14, 2017 FOMC meeting. It is fully segmented: individual participant statements are clearly attributed by name (MR. ROSENGREN, MR. WILLIAMS, MR. BULLARD, MR. BOSTIC, MR. FISCHER, MS. BRAINARD, MR. EVANS, MR. HARKER, MR. POWELL, MR. KAPLAN, MS. MESTER, MS. GEORGE, MR. KASHKARI, VICE CHAIRMAN DUDLEY, CHAIR YELLEN). The document covers both Day 1 (economic go-around) and Day 2 (policy round). Staff-attributable content has been stripped from this decomposed file.

### 2. Era Context
Mid-2017: The expansion is in its 8th year. Unemployment has fallen to 4.3 percent. GDP growth has been running around 2 percent. Core PCE inflation has recently softened to 1.5 percent (below the 2 percent target) after a brief run-up in 2016. The Committee is in a hiking cycle that began in December 2015, with two hikes in 2016 and one in March 2017. Balance sheet normalization is a central discussion. I will score based solely on what participants say in this document.

### 3. Directional Signals by Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants across the board describe inflation as **below target** — core PCE at 1.5 percent, a "considerable shortfall" (Brainard). The dominant view is that recent weakness (cell phone prices, telecom) is transitory, but there is notable discomfort. Kashkari says "Core 12-month PCE inflation of 1.5 percent, in my view, is a significant shortfall." Evans describes "low core inflation... telling us that important pricing headwinds still persist." Brainard notes "58 straight months" of undershooting. Bullard worries about being 4.5 percent below the price-level path. Fischer notes the "slumbering giant" of inflation. The tone is: currently below target, expected to rise, but with real uncertainty. This is a **below-target, modestly deflationary signal** for current conditions. Score should be in the 35–42 range. The transitory language is noted but, per rubric gotcha, does not reduce the score — participants *perceive* inflation as currently below target. The May CPI also comes in soft on Day 2. Centering around **37**.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Most participants characterize the economy as growing at or slightly above trend (~2 percent growth vs. ~1.5–2 percent potential). Williams says GDP growth of 2 percent is "well above my estimate of trend real GDP growth of 1½ percent." Rosengren says "real GDP growth will be above its potential rate over the next two years." Fischer says 2.4 percent staff forecast is "well above estimates of potential growth." However, most participants do not characterize the output gap as dramatically positive — Bullard says growth is "at the trend pace" and sees "little reason to believe... output was growing at a pace meaningfully above the trend pace." Yellen says growth "will come into line with potential next year." The center of gravity is: **modestly above potential**, perhaps approaching but not dramatically overshooting. Score should be in the 52–58 range. Centering around **54**.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Unemployment is at 4.3 percent. Williams says the labor market has "overshot by a significant amount, our maximum employment mandate" and characterizes it as "hot." Mester says the economy is "at or somewhat beyond maximum employment." George notes labor market conditions are "strong." Rosengren worries about unemployment falling to 3.8 percent in the Tealbook forecast. Kashkari acknowledges "the labor market is clearly tighter than it was" but notes wage growth is not responding. Brainard notes 4.3 percent is "just below the cyclical low reached in 2006 and 2007." The center of gravity: labor market is **tight, at or beyond full employment**, with minor uncertainty about NAIRU. Score: 57–62 range. Centering around **59**.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Most participants characterize spending as moderate — "moderate pace" is the dominant phrase. Yellen describes "moderate overall growth." Q1 was soft but Q2 rebounding. Business fixed investment has accelerated (Mester, Fischer). Consumer spending is moderate. Bostic notes auto sector concerns. Kaplan is relatively positive on consumer and nonresidential investment. Overall: at-to-modestly-above trend, consistent with "solid pace." Score: 50–55 range. Centering around **52**.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Multiple participants emphasize that financial conditions have **eased** despite rate hikes. Fischer: "since we began tightening monetary policy in December 2015, financial conditions have eased." Dudley: "financial conditions, broadly defined, have continued to ease and are at a very accommodative level." George: "financial conditions also remain supportive of growth." Stock prices up ~20% since Dec 2015, long-term yields down, corporate spreads narrow. This means conditions are **loose/accommodative**, which is a LOW score on the FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS scale. Score: 25–33 range. Centering around **28**.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The overwhelming majority support raising rates 25 bp at this meeting (Alternative B). Only Kashkari dissents (supports Alternative A — no hike). Most: Fischer, Williams, Rosengren, Bostic, Powell, Harker, Mester, George, Kaplan, Brainard, Dudley, Evans all support the 25 bp hike. Bullard "can go with 25 basis points" but is skeptical. The meeting is a 25 bp hike with essentially one formal dissent (Kashkari). Forward lean is moderate — most foresee gradual further tightening (one more hike in 2017, then possibly in December), with balance sheet normalization starting in September. This is in the hiking cycle range. Rosengren is the most hawkish (wants faster tightening, earlier balance sheet reduction). Evans is most dovish (hold until December, worried about inflation). Overall lean: clearly hawkish with a measured, gradual path. Score: 68–72 range. Centering around **70**.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
One formal dissent (Kashkari votes for Alternative A). However, there is notable spread in views: Rosengren wants more aggressive tightening; Bullard is skeptical of the calendar-driven approach; Evans wants to delay next hike to December; Brainard is conditional. The disagreement is mainly about *pace* and *forward path*, not about whether to hike today. Most of the substantive debate is on balance sheet timing (July vs. September) and inflation patience. One formal dissent plus several "reluctant" or conditionally qualified supporters. Score: 32–40 range. Centering around **36**.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Multiple participants raise financial stability themes. Williams mentions "degree of complacency... elevated asset prices of equities, real estate, and corporate bonds." Fischer raises comparison to 2005. Mester: "I am also watching financial stability risks... the low level of market volatility, coupled with a low equity premium, suggests that financial stability risk could be building." Powell discusses "financial risks building" and "very high asset prices." Brainard discusses "elevated financial imbalances." Dudley: "lean against some of the 'frothiness' that at least I see in financial market valuations." Multiple participants mention VIX, equity valuations, subprime auto. This is a topic with moderate-to-notable emphasis. Score: 42–52 range. Centering around **47**.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The forward signal: gradual tightening continues; balance sheet normalization likely in September; one more hike likely in 2017 (December); data-dependent. Yellen's closing framing: "announcing the start of our portfolio normalization program in, say, September, allowing us the option to raise the target range again at our December meeting." This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias forward signal. Not emergency or strong hawkish, but clearly directional tightening. Score: 68–76 range. Centering around **72**.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Several participants express uncertainty about inflation dynamics (why is it low with tight labor market?), the natural rate of unemployment, neutral rate r*, and the fiscal policy outlook. Kashkari: "we don't really understand inflation dynamics very well." Dudley: uncertainty about which Phillips curve explanation is correct. Kaplan explicitly lists four issues he is "wrestling with." Fischer emphasizes uncertainty about political and economic policy. However, this is not crisis-level uncertainty — most participants describe "roughly balanced risks." The primary uncertainty is around inflation and the policy transmission mechanism. Score: 40–50 range. Centering around **44**.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Participants say conditions are **loose**. Score of 28 = loose. Check: 28 < 50, meaning conditions are on the loose side. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Committee is hiking; majority support continuation. Score of 70. Check: 70 > 50, hawkish direction. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter labor market. 4.3% unemployment, described as "at or beyond" full employment. Score of 59. Check: 59 > 50, tight direction. Matches.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more inflation pressure. Inflation at 1.5% core PCE, well below 2%. Score of 37. Check: 37 < 50, below target. Matches.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (59): Both above 50, both modestly above neutral. Labor is tighter than output gap because of the well-documented disconnect between tight labor markets and weak inflation — consistent with discussion. Slight divergence is expected and explained by participants (flat Phillips curve).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Both in the 70s, both firmly in the moderate-hawkish hiking range. Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (37) and HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70): Usually these move together (high inflation → high hawk), but here inflation is below target while the Committee is still hiking. This is explicitly discussed — participants are hiking preemptively/based on labor market tightness, not because inflation is high. The divergence is real and intentional.

### 6. Speaker Count for Key Dimensions

Pro-hike (hawkish): Rosengren, Williams, Fischer, Bostic, Powell, Harker, Mester, George, Kaplan, Brainard, Dudley, Evans (with caveats), Bullard (reluctant). Count: ~13 (all except Kashkari).
Against hike (dovish): Kashkari (formal dissent, Alt A). Count: 1.
Worried about inflation undershoot (dovish lean): Evans, Brainard, Kashkari, Bullard. Count: ~4.
More hawkish than median: Rosengren, Williams, Fischer. Count: ~3.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "core PCE prices had increased only 1.5 percent on a 12-month change basis. This marks a considerable shortfall below the Committee's 2 percent objective... shows no progress over the past year or so."
- Kashkari: "Core 12-month PCE inflation of 1.5 percent, in my view, is a significant shortfall below our 2 percent target... Since 2012, core PCE inflation has been very stable at around 1.6 percent."
- Evans: "Year-over-year core inflation has retreated to 1.5 percent. This is disconcerting."
- Bullard: "We remain below our inflation target as of today."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants broadly characterize inflation as running meaningfully below the 2 percent target, with recent softness attributed partly to transitory factors but with real unease that the miss may be more persistent.

**Minority views:** Mester and Rosengren are less alarmed, treating the shortfall as transitory. Rosengren stresses idiosyncratic drivers. This creates modest uncertainty band below 37.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "I expect real GDP growth to be slightly above 2 percent this year. This is well above my estimate of trend real GDP growth of 1½ percent."
- Fischer: "the staff are forecasting a Q4/Q4 growth rate of GDP of 2.4 percent, well above estimates of potential growth."
- Bullard: "I see little reason to believe that there are any remaining cyclical dynamics in the U.S. economy with respect to real GDP growth that would make me think that output was growing at a pace meaningfully above the trend pace." (This is a dovish outlier.)
- Yellen: "I anticipate that real GDP growth will come into line with potential next year."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The center of gravity is modest positive output gap — GDP slightly above trend, with most participants expecting it to converge toward potential over the forecast horizon.

**Minority views:** Bullard argues economy is essentially at trend, implying no positive gap. This nudges the uncertainty band slightly lower.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 59 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "We have reached, and indeed have overshot by a significant amount, our maximum employment mandate."
- Mester: "I view the evidence as suggesting that the economy is at or somewhat beyond maximum employment with respect to the cyclical dimension that monetary policy can address."
- Brainard: "At 4.3 percent, the unemployment rate is just below the cyclical low reached in 2006 and 2007."
- Evans: "most other indicators are also pointing to a healthy labor market."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal agreement that the labor market is at or past full employment, with unemployment at 4.3 percent and multiple measures of labor market tightness at or near historical highs.

**Minority views:** Kashkari and Brainard note the absence of wage acceleration as a puzzle, suggesting residual slack may exist. Bullard is more reserved about interpreting low unemployment as overheating.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 52 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "growth in consumer spending has bounced back after the Q1 dip... growth for the first half as a whole is still relatively modest at about 2 percent."
- Mester: "investment in equipment and intangibles has accelerated considerably this year... the rebound in consumer spending may not be as strong as it was in the second half of last year."
- Bostic: "the recent turnaround in business fixed investment over the past two quarters has been encouraging" but notes auto weakness and dependence on oil/gas sector recovery.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Spending is at or modestly above trend — business investment rebounding, consumer spending solid but not strong, overall growth moderate around 2 percent; net position is near-neutral with a slight positive tilt.

**Minority views:** Bostic and Kashkari are more cautious, noting auto sector weakness, inventory buildup, and soft Q2 tracking data.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Dudley: "financial conditions, broadly defined, have continued to ease and are at a very accommodative level. Long-term Treasury yields have declined, the dollar has weakened, and the stock market continues to rise."
- Fischer: "since we began tightening monetary policy in December 2015, financial conditions have eased... the equity risk premium has fallen, and stock prices are up almost 20 percent since December 2015. And corporate bond yields are down significantly."
- George: "Despite three increases in the funds rate and a fourth fully priced in at this meeting, broad financial conditions have eased since December 2015."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Despite three rate hikes, virtually all participants observe that broad financial conditions have eased, with accommodative equity markets, compressed spreads, low volatility, and still-low long rates; conditions are clearly loose/accommodative.

**Minority views:** No participant argues conditions are tight. The debate is only about whether this easing is structural (Bullard: tax reform expectations) or whether it signals a need for more policy action (Dudley, Rosengren).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 70 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Dudley: "I definitely favor raising our federal funds rate target by 25 basis points at this meeting."
- Rosengren: "I support alternative B for this meeting. Current economic conditions and my forecast for the economy are consistent with tighter policy... we should be laying the groundwork for moving this July to begin the gradual reduction in the balance sheet."
- Fischer: "I am comfortable with another 25 basis point rate hike at this meeting."
- Evans (most dovish, but still supports): "I do support alternative B today... my preference is to be particularly careful not to set expectations of an overly aggressive funds rate path."
- Kashkari: "I support alternative A at this meeting" (sole formal dissent — no hike).

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-consensus for a 25 bp hike with one formal dissent; forward lean is moderate hawkish (gradual normalization, balance sheet reduction in September, possible December hike); Rosengren represents the hawkish tail wanting faster balance sheet reduction and more hikes.

**Minority views:** Kashkari formally dissents. Evans and Brainard are conditionally supportive, wanting to slow if inflation doesn't improve. Bullard is skeptical of the forward path's steepness.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 36 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- One formal dissent: Kashkari votes for Alternative A (no hike) — the only dissenter.
- Evans: explicitly supportive today but "my preference is to be particularly careful not to set expectations of an overly aggressive funds rate path that would impede our progress on inflation."
- Bullard: "I don't think it's a great day for the Committee... it's a symptom of our tendency to do things on a calendar basis... I can certainly go with 25 basis points today" — reluctant support.
- Brainard: "If the tension between the progress on employment and the lack of progress on inflation persists, it may lead me to reassess the expected path of the funds rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** One formal dissent, several "reluctant" or conditionally qualified supporters, and ongoing substantive disagreement about pace and forward guidance; this is a low-to-moderate dissent meeting.

**Minority views:** The dissent is primarily about inflation patience, not fundamental economic assessment. The balance sheet timing debate (July vs. September) generates additional but less fundamental differences.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 47 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Williams: "very low levels of indicators of financial market volatility such as the VIX... suggests a degree of complacency that is hard to explain completely and may be contributing to elevated asset prices of equities, real estate, and corporate bonds."
- Mester: "I am also watching financial stability risks... the low level of market volatility, coupled with a low equity premium, suggests that financial stability risk could be building."
- Dudley: "from a financial stability perspective — to lean against some of the 'frothiness' that at least I see in financial market valuations."
- Brainard: "There are already areas that are under heightened monitoring, such as rising levels of corporate debt, CRE lending, and rising delinquency rates in subprime auto."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Multiple participants raise financial stability concerns related to low volatility, elevated equity valuations, compressed spreads, and specific credit sectors, but this is a secondary theme rather than a dominant one; several participants invoke it as a marginal reason to favor tightening.

**Minority views:** Brainard downplays systemic risk ("these risks don't appear to be a threat to the broader system"). Bullard cautions about over-interpreting financial conditions indices.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "we could announce the start of our portfolio normalization program in, say, September, allowing us the option to raise the target range again at our December meeting if a further increase looks to be warranted."
- Dudley: "We have one more rate hike this year—25 basis points—not counting this meeting's move, three in 2018, and three in 2019."
- Mester: "a gradual upward federal funds rate path as appropriate... I anticipate that we will change our reinvestment policy later this year to begin normalizing the balance sheet."
- Powell: "my base case is that the Committee will begin phasing out reinvestments in September... and will raise the federal funds rate again in December."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly in the continued-tightening direction — balance sheet normalization expected in September, additional rate hike likely in December 2017, gradual multi-year hiking path projected; this is a moderate-hawkish tightening signal.

**Minority views:** Evans wants to delay any further hike to at least December, with the possibility of only two hikes in 2017. Bullard questions whether the full 200+ bp of additional hikes is warranted.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 44 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kashkari: "we don't really understand inflation dynamics very well, and I don't think we should yet be confident that inflation will soon return to target."
- Dudley: "I don't know which of these explanations is the correct one" (re: natural rate vs. flat Phillips curve).
- Kaplan explicitly names four issues he is "wrestling with": neutral rate, 10-year yield, secular disruptive trends, and financial conditions signaling.
- Fischer: "increased uncertainty about government policy in many dimensions" and uncertainty about U.S. international commitments.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Several participants express genuine uncertainty about inflation dynamics, the natural rate, and the policy transmission mechanism, which is somewhat elevated vs. "normal" uncertainty; however, most still describe balanced risks and the level does not approach crisis-like conditions.

**Minority views:** Fischer and Dudley lean toward somewhat higher uncertainty, particularly about fiscal policy tail risks and potential abrupt market repricing.
