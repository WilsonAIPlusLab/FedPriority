# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2001-04-11 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of an informational (non-official) FOMC conference call on April 11, 2001. Chairman Greenspan frames it explicitly: "this is not an official meeting of the FOMC but an informational one." The discussion centers on whether to make an intermeeting rate cut. Speakers include: Vice Chairman McDonough, Poole, McTeer, Stern, Minehan, Guynn, Parry, Moskow, Broaddus, Santomero, Hoenig, Jordan, Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson, and Kelley. Approximately 16 participants speak substantively.

### Era Context
April 2001: The economy is in the early stages of what would become the 2001 recession (officially March-November 2001). The Fed had already cut rates 100bp in January (two intermeeting 50bp cuts) and another 50bp at the March 20 meeting, bringing the fed funds rate from 6.50% to 5.00%. The tech/telecom bust is unfolding, business investment is declining sharply, and there is growing pessimism about the near-term outlook. However, consumption and housing have held up reasonably well.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Inflation is essentially not discussed by participants. McDonough mentions "lack of pricing power" — implying low inflation pressure. No participant raises inflation as a concern. The entire discussion is about downside growth risks. Direction: well below midpoint. Given the absence of inflation concern and one mention of lack of pricing power, I score this around 32-35, reflecting below-target inflation that is not a primary concern.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says no pricing power, no inflation discussion. Low score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The discussion is dominated by slowing growth, business investment weakness, and downside risks. Ferguson: "even if growth were to pick up a bit in the second part of the year, there is still a notable output gap." Gramlich: "weakening is from a Greenbook forecast that already had the year-end unemployment rate at 5.2 percent." Santomero: "some stabilization in the real economy as the slowdown starts to slow down." Multiple participants describe the economy as having "stabilized" but with significant downside risks. The economy is clearly below potential. Direction: below 50. Ferguson explicitly says "notable output gap." Greenbook has unemployment rising to 5.2%. Score around 35-38.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential. Score below 50 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very little direct labor market discussion. Moskow mentions Rockford IL at 5.1% unemployment. Gramlich references Greenbook unemployment at 5.2% year-end. No one discusses labor shortages or tight labor conditions. The focus is entirely on the investment/profit side. The labor market is softening from the late-1990s tightness but not yet deeply slack. Direction: somewhat below midpoint. Score around 40-43.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence suggests softening labor market, no longer tight. Below 50 matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: This is the central topic. Business fixed investment is described as the "major issue" by both McDonough and Poole. McDonough: "the view is spreading among business executives that they must delay investment decisions." Parry: "the adjustment in the high-tech area...is going to be greater and more intense than many of the people in the industry thought." Hoenig: "technology sector is clearly in difficulty." Multiple participants report cutbacks in capital spending. However, consumer spending is holding up — Moskow notes "consumer travel...is very strong," Gramlich notes "housing, autos, and overall consumption have been fairly strong," Hoenig reports "almost strength in sales of nondurables." Net: investment is contracting sharply while consumption is resilient. Weighted by discussion emphasis (investment dominates), this is below trend. Score around 30-33.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Evidence shows sharp investment decline partially offset by consumer resilience. Below 50 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Moskow: "Motorola has been frozen out of the commercial paper market." Jordan discusses the yield curve — 3-month bill rate lowest on the curve, funds rate at 5% "probably too high." McTeer notes the stock market dynamics. Jordan: "we could become inadvertently restrictive relative to where the markets are." The discussion suggests conditions are tightening somewhat, with some credit stress in specific sectors. Score around 57-60.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence of some credit tightening (Motorola CP, yield curve inversion concerns). Above 50 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The entire meeting is about whether and when to cut rates further. No one argues against easing — the debate is purely about timing (intermeeting vs. waiting for May 15). Meyer: "I wouldn't be surprised if we need to move a full percentage point between now and the end of June." Gramlich: "my personal preference would be to do 50 basis points of easing as soon as we possibly can." Most participants agree further easing is needed. The doves (McTeer, Gramlich, Kelley, Ferguson) want to move now or very soon; the cautious group (Poole, Minehan, Guynn, Broaddus) prefers waiting for the scheduled meeting. But ALL agree rates need to come down. This is clearly dovish. Score around 22-26.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence shows unanimous dovish lean, debate is only about timing of cuts. Low score matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is genuine disagreement, but it is about tactics (intermeeting vs. scheduled meeting), not about the direction of policy. All participants agree rates should come down. The split is roughly: 4-5 favor moving today or very soon (McTeer, Gramlich, Kelley, Ferguson lean toward sooner), 5-6 prefer waiting for scheduled meeting (Poole, Minehan, Guynn, Broaddus, Hoenig), and several are in the middle (Stern, Santomero, Meyer, Parry, Moskow). This is tactical disagreement, not fundamental. Score around 35-40.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Moderate tactical disagreement with directional consensus. Mid-range score matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Modest attention. Moskow mentions Motorola's commercial paper access. Jordan discusses the yield curve. McTeer discusses equity market dynamics and the "Greenspan put." Parry mentions PG&E bankruptcy. These are scattered references, not a sustained focus. Score around 25-30.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Limited but present references. Below-midpoint score matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly dovish — all participants expect further easing. Meyer: "a full percentage point between now and the end of June." Gramlich: "50 basis points as soon as we possibly can." Ferguson: "over the next several meetings we are likely to be moving rates down." Santomero: "we clearly will be easing as we go forward." The forward signal is strongly easing-biased. Score around 15-20.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence shows unanimous expectation of further easing. Low score matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants emphasize uncertainty and downside risks. Stern: "The issue is whether this is a durable stabilization or whether there is another leg of weakness to follow. I don't have the answer to that." McDonough: "the downside risk to our economic forecast is extremely considerable." Parry: "the uncertainties have intensified." Meyer: "There's a lot of uncertainty in the outlook." Broaddus: mixed picture. Many express genuine uncertainty about whether the economy will stabilize or deteriorate further. Score around 55-62.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows elevated uncertainty among many participants. Above-midpoint score matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (36) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (42): Both below 50, coherent — economy weakening, labor softening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (24) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18): Both strongly dovish, coherent — cutting now and expecting more cuts ahead.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 33 ±7 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "Because of the uncertainties about the economic recovery and the lack of pricing power, corporate executives feel they must postpone investment decisions"
- No other participant raises inflation as a topic of concern.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Inflation is essentially a non-issue at this meeting; the sole reference (lack of pricing power) indicates below-target pressure, consistent with a disinflationary environment.

**Minority views:** None — inflation is simply not discussed.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FERGUSON: "even if growth were to pick up a bit in the second part of the year, there is still a notable output gap"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "the weakening is from a Greenbook forecast that already had the year-end unemployment rate at 5.2 percent. So the economy is weakening from a forecast that already is showing a weak economy"
- MR. SANTOMERO: "we are seeing some stabilization in the real economy as the slowdown starts to slow down"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see the economy operating clearly below potential, with the slowdown possibly stabilizing but with large downside risks to even that subdued baseline.

**Minority views:** Some participants (Broaddus, Hoenig, Moskow) report mixed or not-entirely-negative local conditions, but no one claims the economy is at or above potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 ±8 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence:**

- MR. PARRY: tech-sector layoffs intensifying; PG&E bankruptcy affecting suppliers

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is softening from the tight conditions of the late 1990s, with tech layoffs spreading, but has not yet reached deeply slack territory. Limited direct discussion.

**Minority views:** None explicitly — labor market is not a focus of debate.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 31 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the view is spreading among business executives that they must delay investment decisions... the downside risk to our economic forecast is extremely considerable"
- MR. PARRY: "the adjustment in the high-tech area...is going to be greater and more intense than many of the people in the industry thought as recently as a month or two ago"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "housing, autos, and overall consumption have been fairly strong. But that could be a danger sign as well"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Business fixed investment is in sharp decline — the dominant concern of the meeting — while consumer spending remains relatively resilient. The net position, weighted by the overwhelming discussion emphasis on investment weakness, is clearly below trend.

**Minority views:** Some local reports (Moskow's Rockford visit, Hoenig's consumer nondurables) provide modest positive offsets, but the center of gravity is firmly negative on investment.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "Motorola...has been frozen out of the commercial paper market"
- MR. JORDAN: "the funds rate is probably too high at 5 percent...we could become inadvertently restrictive relative to where the markets are"
- MR. MCTEER: concerns about equity market dynamics and incentives to sell stocks for bonds

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are somewhat tighter than neutral, with specific credit stress in the technology sector and a yield curve suggesting policy may be inadvertently restrictive. Not crisis-level, but conditions are tightening.

**Minority views:** No one argues conditions are loose or overly accommodative.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 24 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MEYER: "I wouldn't be surprised if we need to move a full percentage point between now and the end of June"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "my personal preference would be to do 50 basis points of easing as soon as we possibly can"
- MR. FERGUSON: "over the next several meetings we are likely to be moving rates down as opposed to staying pat"
- MS. MINEHAN, MR. POOLE, MR. GUYNN: Agree on further easing but prefer scheduled-meeting timing

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimously dovish — every participant agrees rates need to come down further. The only debate is whether to cut between meetings or wait for the next scheduled meeting. Several favor aggressive action (50bp soon); others prefer measured pacing.

**Minority views:** No hawks. The "hawkish" end of the spectrum is simply the preference to wait for a scheduled meeting before cutting (Poole, Minehan, Guynn, Broaddus).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MCTEER: "I was disappointed to hear that you didn't want to make a move today. I think we really should cut the funds rate today" (favoring immediate action)
- MR. POOLE: "I am very opposed to an intermeeting move in the absence of compelling new information" (favoring patience)
- MR. KELLEY: "I would like to support Ned Gramlich...there may be a positive benefit in moving ahead of the data" (supporting earlier move)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Moderate tactical disagreement about the timing and method of easing, but fundamental agreement on direction. The split between "move now" (McTeer, Gramlich, Kelley, Ferguson) and "wait for meeting" (Poole, Minehan, Guynn, Broaddus) creates genuine tension without deep philosophical division.

**Minority views:** McTeer is the strongest outlier favoring immediate action; Poole is the strongest outlier against any intermeeting move.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**

- MR. PARRY: PG&E bankruptcy affecting 11,000-15,000 suppliers
- MR. JORDAN: yield curve analysis, concern about inadvertently restrictive policy
- MR. MCTEER: equity market dynamics, "Greenspan put"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives scattered attention from several participants but is not a major focus. References are typically brief and made in service of the larger argument about growth risks and timing, not as standalone financial stability concerns.

**Minority views:** None — no participant makes financial stability a central theme.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 18 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MEYER: "I wouldn't be surprised if we need to move a full percentage point between now and the end of June"
- MR. GRAMLICH: "50 basis points of easing as soon as we possibly can"
- MR. SANTOMERO: "we clearly will be easing as we go forward"
- MR. FERGUSON: "over the next several meetings we are likely to be moving rates down"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is strongly dovish. Participants universally expect further substantial easing in the near term. Some anticipate as much as 100bp of additional cuts in the next two months.

**Minority views:** None argue against further easing; the debate is only about pace and method.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "The issue is whether this is a durable stabilization or whether there is another leg of weakness to follow. I don't have the answer to that"
- MR. PARRY: "there is no question that the uncertainties have intensified"
- MR. MEYER: "There's a lot of uncertainty in the outlook and a number of crosscurrents"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the downside risk to our economic forecast is extremely considerable"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express elevated uncertainty about whether the economy is stabilizing or facing further deterioration. The mixed anecdotal reports reinforce genuine uncertainty. Multiple participants use explicit uncertainty language.

**Minority views:** A few (Broaddus, Hoenig) describe a "mixed" rather than deeply uncertain picture, but even they acknowledge significant downside risks.
