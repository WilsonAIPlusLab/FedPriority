# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring - 2001-01-31 (Transcript)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the January 30-31, 2001 FOMC meeting. It includes individual speaker statements from a full go-around on economic conditions (Day 1 afternoon) and a policy discussion with vote (Day 2 morning). Note: the first ~1700 lines are a special topic discussion on the Fed's portfolio composition in a declining Treasury supply environment; the economic/policy discussion begins around line 1960. Speakers include: Parry, Minehan, Hoenig, Guynn, Santomero, McTeer, Stern, Moskow, Broaddus, McDonough, Jordan, Gramlich, Ferguson, Kelley, Meyer, Poole, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
January 2001: The economy had been growing above potential through mid-2000 but experienced a sharp deceleration in Q4 2000. The Fed had tightened through mid-2000 (funds rate at 6.5%) and made an intermeeting 50bp cut on January 3, 2001. This meeting would deliver another 50bp cut. The dot-com bubble had burst, NASDAQ was well off highs, manufacturing was contracting sharply, but services and construction were still holding up. Energy prices (natural gas, oil) were spiking. The 2001 recession officially began in March 2001 (per NBER), so this meeting was on the cusp.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint -- benign and not a constraint on easing. Parry: "Recent data on the CPI and ECI have been encouraging...we expect core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent both this year and next." Hoenig: "Incoming inflation data confirm a trend of moderate inflation pressures...inflation could ease some." Minehan: "our policy options are not greatly limited by the threat of inflation." Broaddus: "With inflation apparently well contained, at least for now." Kelley: "the outlook for inflation seems to give us a comfortable degree of room to maneuver." Greenspan does not flag inflation as a concern. Meyer notes "excesses" unwinding. Energy costs are mentioned as squeezing margins, but not as generating broad inflation pressure -- rather as a drag on purchasing power. Center of gravity: inflation viewed as moderate, near or slightly below 2% PCE target, not a concern. Score should be in the 40-48 range -- close to target but perceived as favorable/contained. Energy input costs create some upward pull but are not translating into broader price pressure. I'll score 42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is contained/moderate, near target but slightly favorable. 42 is slightly below midpoint. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as having sharply decelerated from above-potential to near or below potential. Greenspan: "a very rapidly changing economy, one that still has a distance to go on the down side." Parry: "the pace of economic activity appears to have slowed sharply in the current quarter." Minehan: "real GDP growth will be zero or close to it" in Q1. McTeer: GDP growth "slowing on average by more than 2-1/2 percentage points from the new higher rate of potential." Meyer: "near zero or even slightly negative growth in the first quarter." Multiple participants compare to recession conditions. However, several note that a quarter ago the economy was running well above potential and that services, construction, and labor markets still show residual strength. The economy is viewed as moving from above potential to below potential, currently near or slightly below. Bright-line rule: language like "slowing sharply," "below trend," "slack" emerging but labor markets "still tight" creates tension. The center of gravity is that the economy has moved to slightly below potential but hasn't collapsed. Score: 38 -- below potential, recovering aspects remain but gap is opening.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says economy moving below potential. 38 < 50. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants describe labor markets as still tight but loosening notably. Hoenig: "labor markets are still relatively tight but far less so than they were just a month ago." Moskow: "labor markets show further signs of easing. The unemployment rate for the five District states moved up to 3.8 percent...Contacts report less difficulty finding workers." Broaddus: "Labor markets overall remain tight; although not as tight as they were, they're still remarkably tight." Kelley: "Labor markets are still tight, with unemployment still very low." Stern: "labor market conditions appear to be a little less tight than they were earlier." Jordan: layoffs emerging, "65 percent increase" in unemployment claims in his District. Minehan: "layoffs at dot-coms, in manufacturing, and elsewhere have made finding scarce labor easier." The center of gravity is that labor markets were very tight and are loosening but still tight by historical standards. Score: 53 -- still tight but clearly easing.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says still tight but easing. 53 slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- Coherence with OUTPUT_GAP (38): Mild divergence explained by labor market being a lagging indicator -- economy has slowed sharply but unemployment hasn't yet risen much. This is typical at the start of downturns.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see both consumer spending and business investment weakening sharply. Parry: "declining consumer sentiment and weakness in consumer spending, especially for durables, certainly are noteworthy." Hoenig: "Consumption, especially durable goods expenditures, and business fixed investment continue to soften." McTeer: "The contraction has been concentrated in investment spending so far, but rapidly sinking consumer confidence doesn't augur well for consumer spending going forward." Kelley: consumer durables "fell to minus 5 percent," business fixed investment "was at or near zero." Moskow: "auto production...scaled back even more than indicated in the Greenbook." Meyer: "very sharp slowdown" in spending. Greenspan: "an obvious deceleration in business investment in durable equipment and consumer expenditures on durable goods." Jordan: "business has fallen through the floor," retail "drop in sales in December was so sudden." But housing is holding up (Poole, Hoenig). Score: 28 -- contracting, sharp pullback in durables and investment, though not free-fall across all categories (housing, some services still positive).
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says sharp weakness. 28 < 50. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants discuss financial conditions as having tightened in some dimensions. Ferguson: "commercial paper market could be described as anything but healthy...conditions and standards with respect to loans for medium size and larger companies continue to be tight." Minehan: "the high cost of financing for all but the most highly rated firms...tighter credit terms." Greenspan: "Baa-Aaa spreads" elevated, "skittishness in the banking industry." But several note the banking system is healthy (Ferguson, Kelley, Poole, Minehan: "banking sector remains a source of strength"). Equity markets partially rebounded after the January 3 cut. Poole notes long rates have fallen significantly. On balance, conditions have tightened somewhat -- credit spreads wider, lending standards tighter -- but not crisis level. Banking system sound. Score: 58 -- somewhat tight.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions have tightened but banking is sound. 58 slightly above midpoint. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Overwhelmingly dovish. Every single participant supports a 50bp cut. The Chairman recommends 50bp with balance of risks toward weakness. Multiple participants advocate "front-loading" easing (McTeer, Meyer, Minehan, Gramlich, Ferguson, Greenspan). Gramlich: "a part of me thought you might recommend going to alternative A'', which was a 75 basis point cut." Hoenig: "a 50 basis point reduction in the fed funds rate might be appropriate...the current federal funds rate continues to represent a tight policy." McTeer: "bold and immediate policy easing at this meeting, followed up as necessary both early and often." Greenspan: "monetary policy must also compress itself into a narrower timeframe...we have to move faster, sooner, quicker." The only note of caution comes from Jordan and Meyer who worry about market expectations of continued easing creating problems later. This is a strong dovish lean. Score: 20 -- strong dovish, majority favoring significant easing and more to come.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says strongly dovish. 20 < 50. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is near-unanimity. All participants support the 50bp cut. No formal dissents. Minor differences exist only about the pace of future easing (Jordan/Meyer urge caution about further cuts; Gramlich would have accepted 75bp). These are very minor tactical differences within a strong consensus. Score: 12 -- near-consensus.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence says very broad agreement. 12 < 50. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are discussed by several participants but are not the dominant topic. Ferguson discusses commercial paper market health and credit standards. Greenspan mentions Baa-Aaa spreads and banking industry skittishness. Minehan mentions tight credit. Kelley mentions S&P P/E ratios. Greenspan references the 1998 LTCM episode as analogy. Poole notes market rates have moved ahead of Fed. Several note the banking system is sound (a stability positive). This is moderate attention -- several participants address it meaningfully as context, but the dominant topic is the real economy slowdown and inventory adjustment. Score: 38 -- limited to moderate discussion.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. 38 is moderate-low. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly dovish -- easing bias. The balance of risks statement is retained toward weakness. Multiple participants explicitly call for continued easing. McTeer: "followed up as necessary both early and often." Greenspan: "we have to move faster, sooner, quicker, and complete the operation in a shorter period of time." Gramlich: "we should be mindful that policy should be front-loaded and operate faster." Ferguson: "it is far too early to suggest that the balance of risks is anything other than to the down side." The only cautionary notes come from Jordan and Meyer about not over-committing to future cuts. Score: 20 -- strong easing bias, signal of further accommodation ahead.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says clearly dovish. 20 < 50. Matches.
- Coherence with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20): Both strongly dovish, aligned. Good.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Many participants express significant uncertainty, particularly about the depth and duration of the slowdown. Minehan: "I find it difficult to judge with great certainty just where we are in that adjustment process." Guynn: "Like the Greenbook, I find it difficult to judge with great certainty just where we are." Stern: "I have so little confidence in forecasting how the economy will perform over the next two, three, or four quarters." Greenspan: extensive discussion of the inability to forecast recessions, the role of psychology, "We do not seem to be experiencing a significant acceleration in final sales in January" (uncertainty about the data). Ferguson: "circumstances surrounding us seem filled with great uncertainty." Santomero: "wide confidence intervals around these forecasts." But several also express confidence in a second-half recovery. The center of gravity is elevated uncertainty -- genuine, not merely rhetorical. Score: 58 -- elevated, multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence says elevated. 58 > 50. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Mild divergence. Labor is a lagging indicator; economy has slowed sharply but labor market hasn't fully adjusted. Typical for onset of downturn. Acceptable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (20): Perfectly aligned -- strong dovish current action and forward signal.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (28) and OUTPUT_GAP (38): Consistent -- weak spending/investment pulling output below potential.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "Recent data on the CPI and ECI have been encouraging...we expect core PCE prices to increase about 2 percent both this year and next."
- MR. HOENIG: "Incoming inflation data confirm a trend of moderate inflation pressures. Given that the economy is slowing, that energy price increases are showing some signs of unwinding, and that inflation expectations remain well anchored, inflation could ease some."
- MR. BROADDUS: "With inflation apparently well contained, at least for now."
- MR. KELLEY: "the outlook for inflation seems to give us a comfortable degree of room to maneuver."

Center-of-gravity: Participants view inflation as contained near the 2% target, with risks tilted to the downside given slowing activity. Energy costs are squeezing margins but not generating broad inflation pressure.

Minority views: Minehan notes New England "prices that are rising more rapidly than elsewhere," and some report energy-driven input cost pressures, but no one views inflation as a significant concern. Narrow band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "the pace of economic activity appears to have slowed sharply in the current quarter, and output from the manufacturing sector has been especially hard hit."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we've got a very rapidly changing economy, one that still has a distance to go on the down side."
- MS. MINEHAN: "If the Greenbook or we are at all right about the first quarter of 2001, real GDP growth will be zero or close to it."
- MR. MCTEER: GDP growth "slowing on average by more than 2-1/2 percentage points from the new higher rate of potential."

Center-of-gravity: The economy has moved from above potential to below potential very rapidly. Most see Q1 2001 growth near zero against 4%+ potential. Manufacturing is in sharp contraction, though services and construction show residual strength.

Minority views: Poole cautions about "optimal level of gloom" and notes housing holding up. McDonough's Second District is still strong. But Stern thinks "the probability of recession at this point is uncomfortably high." Wide band reflects uncertainty about how far below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "Labor markets overall remain tight; although not as tight as they were, they're still remarkably tight."
- MR. KELLEY: "Labor markets are still tight, with unemployment still very low."
- MR. MOSKOW: "unemployment rate for the five District states moved up to 3.8 percent...Contacts report less difficulty finding workers...a very sharp reduction of 20 to 25 percent in hiring plans."
- MR. HOENIG: "labor markets are still relatively tight but far less so than they were just a month ago."

Center-of-gravity: Labor markets are still tight by absolute standards (low unemployment) but clearly loosening. Layoffs are emerging broadly, wage pressures easing, finding workers easier.

Minority views: Jordan reports sharp deterioration (65% increase in unemployment claims). Moskow's Manpower data showing biggest drop in hiring plans in five years. But Broaddus, Kelley, Santomero emphasize continued tightness. Moderate dispersion.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 28 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. KELLEY: "Consumer durables...that rate fell to minus 5 percent in the most recent quarter...Business fixed investment...in the most recent quarter it was at or near zero."
- MR. HOENIG: "Consumption, especially durable goods expenditures, and business fixed investment continue to soften."
- MR. JORDAN: "A large producer of plastics said that business has fallen through the floor and is still falling."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "an obvious deceleration in business investment in durable equipment and consumer expenditures on durable goods."

Center-of-gravity: Both consumer spending (especially durables) and business fixed investment are contracting sharply. Auto sales, manufacturing investment, and high-tech spending in steep decline. Housing and some services partially offsetting.

Minority views: Housing holding up provides a floor. McDonough's District shows few signs of slowing. But manufacturing and durables weakness is overwhelming and broad-based.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FERGUSON: "the commercial paper market could be described as anything but healthy...conditions and standards with respect to loans for medium size and larger companies continue to be tight."
- MS. MINEHAN: "the high cost of financing for all but the most highly rated firms...tighter credit terms and other rising costs."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Baa-Aaa spreads" not back to where they should be, "skittishness in the banking industry."
- MR. POOLE: "the two-year constant maturity rate moved below 6 percent in October, dropped below 5-1/2 percent in early December, and then by early January it was down to about 4-3/4 percent."

Center-of-gravity: Credit conditions have tightened -- wider spreads, tighter lending standards, stressed commercial paper market. But banking system sound, long rates have fallen, and equity markets partially recovered after January 3 cut. Mixed but leaning somewhat tight.

Minority views: Some note accommodative signals (falling long rates, healthy banks). Ferguson and Minehan emphasize tighter credit. Moderate dispersion.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "moving by 50 basis points today...strikes me as the right move along with retaining the balance of risks statement to the down side...monetary policy must also compress itself into a narrower timeframe."
- MR. MCTEER: "bold and immediate policy easing at this meeting, followed up as necessary both early and often."
- MR. GRAMLICH: "A part of me thought you might recommend going to alternative A'', which was a 75 basis point cut."
- All participants explicitly support 50bp cut. Unanimous vote.

Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish consensus for 50bp easing (100bp in one month), with balance of risks toward weakness. Multiple calls for front-loading. This is decisive easing action.

Minority views: Jordan and Meyer caution about managing expectations for future easing -- tactical, not directional disagreement.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- All participants support 50bp cut unanimously. No dissents.
- MR. PARRY: "I enthusiastically support your recommendation."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "I fully support your recommendation and the reasoning for it."
- Minor tactical differences only (Jordan/Meyer on future pace; Gramlich would accept 75bp).

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous on both the action and the rationale. The broadest agreement is on the need for decisive easing.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FERGUSON: extended discussion of commercial paper market, credit standards, financing gap.
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Baa-Aaa spreads, banking sector skittishness, references 1998 LTCM liquidity episode.
- MS. MINEHAN: tighter credit terms.
- MR. KELLEY: S&P 500 P/E ratios, wealth effect.

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are discussed by several participants as important context, but the meeting is dominated by real economy (inventory adjustment, manufacturing contraction, consumer spending) and policy discussion. Financial stability is supporting context, not a central focus.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 20 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. FERGUSON: "it is far too early to suggest that the balance of risks is anything other than to the down side."
- MR. MCTEER: "followed up as necessary both early and often...front-load its policy moves as much as possible."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we have to move faster, sooner, quicker, and complete the operation in a shorter period of time."
- MS. MINEHAN: "front-loading policy right now seems to me the wisest course."

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is clearly dovish -- balance of risks toward weakness, strong signals of further easing ahead. Multiple participants explicitly advocate additional cuts.

Minority views: Jordan warns "the pressure for further cuts at the March meeting...is going to be huge" and implies the Committee should resist if the baseline forecast is correct. Meyer concurs on managing expectations. This widens the band somewhat.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. STERN: "I have so little confidence in forecasting how the economy will perform over the next two, three, or four quarters that I'm reluctant to [base policy on a forecast]."
- MR. FERGUSON: "circumstances surrounding us seem filled with great uncertainty."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: extensive discourse on inability to forecast recessions, "We may have a deeper problem facing us here, though I grant it has a low probability."
- MR. SANTOMERO: "wide confidence intervals around these forecasts."

Center-of-gravity: Participants express genuinely elevated uncertainty about the depth and duration of the slowdown, whether a recession will occur, and the speed of recovery. Greenspan's extended remarks about the impossibility of forecasting turning points set the tone. Multiple members highlight unusual difficulty in assessing the outlook.

Minority views: Some (Meyer, Poole) express measured optimism about second-half recovery. But even optimists acknowledge large confidence bands.
