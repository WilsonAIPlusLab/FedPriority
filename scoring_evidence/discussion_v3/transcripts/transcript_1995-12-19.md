# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a full go-around on economic conditions, a policy discussion featuring Don Kohn's presentation on "opportunistic vs. deliberate" disinflation strategies, and a policy go-around. Speakers include Presidents Boehne, Parry, Minehan, Broaddus, Melzer, Stern, Moskow, McTeer, Hoenig, Guynn, Jordan, and Governors Kelley, Yellen, Phillips, Blinder, Lindsey, and Chairman Greenspan, plus Vice Chairman McDonough.

### Era Context
Late 1995: The economy is in a mature expansion, with unemployment around 5.6%. The Fed had raised the funds rate to 6% in early 1995 and eased by 25bp in July 1995 to 5.75%. Long-term bond yields had fallen sharply over the year. Inflation was running near 3% CPI. The government shutdown/budget impasse was ongoing.

### Dimensional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as subdued/contained but not falling dramatically. The center of gravity is that CPI inflation is running around 3%, which many view as acceptable for now but some (Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan) view as too high for price stability. Boehne: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued." Kelley: "inflation continues to be remarkably well contained... unit labor costs continue to behave. Productivity is still growing very nicely, and the ECI is flat." Greenspan: "I am not worried about product price inflation." Stern argues inflation may come in below forecasts. However, Broaddus and Melzer are unhappy with 3% as a plateau. The consensus is inflation is well-contained at about 3% CPI, with expectations declining. This is below a worrisome level but not at target (no explicit 2% target in this era, but price stability advocates want lower). Direction: slightly below midpoint -- inflation is present at ~3% but not accelerating and expectations are declining. Score around 38-42. With CPI at 3% and no acceleration, participants see it as contained. Score: 40.

Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says contained/subdued, not accelerating, expectations declining. 40 is correct -- below the 50 midpoint (below-target concern zone), reflecting "present but contained."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating at or near potential. Hoenig: "economic resources will continue to be used at or near capacity levels." Greenspan: "the economy has more of a feel of driving with the parking brake partially engaged." Several participants note slowing growth in Q4 after strong Q3 (5%+ revised). Broaddus: "the economy already operating at least in the neighborhood of potential GDP." Moskow: "we see the economy growing near its potential." Yellen sees a bias toward below-trend growth going forward. McTeer fears the economy "could run out of momentum." Multiple districts report mixed/slowing conditions. The center of gravity is "at or near potential but slowing somewhat." Score: 49-51. Near potential but with a slight sense of slowing -- score 49.

Higher = more above potential. "Near potential" with slight slowing signals = 49. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants report tight labor markets. Parry: "labor markets remain tight in much of the District." Stern: "labor markets in the District are tight. There are shortages of skilled and unskilled workers." Melzer: "labor problems of various kinds, such as shortages, unqualified applicants, and retention are affecting many businesses." Moskow: "Labor markets in the District remain tight, with unemployment rates still well below the national average." Hoenig: "Labor markets remain tight in many parts of the District." Phillips: "At 5.6 percent unemployment, people are working." However, Blinder notes "labor lately has been taking it on the chin" with "real wages going nowhere." Guynn: "scattered labor shortages... appear to have abated." Wage pressures remain limited despite tightness. The center of gravity is: tight labor markets with scattered shortages but wages remarkably well-contained. Score: 55-58. Tight but not creating major wage pressures. Score: 56.

Higher = tighter. Tight markets, scattered shortages, low unemployment at 5.6%, but wages contained. 56 is correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is getting mixed-to-weak signals. Boehne: "Retail sales in particular reflect a cautious attitude... an otherwise dismal season." Minehan: "consumers remained wary and uncertain." Broaddus: "anecdotally, it's described as quite sluggish." McTeer: "sales in recent weeks have been absolutely terrible." However, business investment is seen as solid. Phillips: "fundamentals are reasonably strong for continued investment." Kelley: "Credit availability still seems to be quite ample." Housing mixed. Auto sales weak due to inventory shortages and softening demand. Overall, the picture is below-trend on consumption but reasonable on investment. Net: somewhat below trend. Score: 42-45. Score: 43.

Higher = stronger. Mixed-to-weak consumption, reasonable investment = 43. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term bond yields have fallen sharply ("remarkable decline" per Kelley). Credit is "readily available" (Melzer). Stock market is strong/surging. Greenspan worries about "bond and stock bubble." Phillips: "The markets, I think, are fairly supportive." Mortgage refinancing may boom. Blinder: "the real fed funds rate looks restrictive to me." However, several participants note that the real funds rate has risen due to declining inflation expectations, making policy tighter than intended. The discussion is about financial conditions being mixed: loose at the long end (low long rates, strong stocks, ample credit) but the real short rate perceived as restrictive. The center of gravity on conditions (not policy) is: loose to neutral. Long rates down, stocks up, credit ample. Score: 35-40. Score: 37.

Higher = tighter conditions. Low long rates, strong stock market, ample credit = loose conditions. 37 is correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The committee is easing -- voting for a 25bp cut. Greenspan proposes and most support it. Supporters of easing: Greenspan, McDonough, Blinder, Lindsey, Yellen, McTeer, Phillips, Kelley, Parry, Guynn, Boehne, Jordan (who wants more). Preferring no change but accepting 25bp: Minehan, Moskow, Hoenig. Preferring no change/opposing ease: Broaddus, Stern, Melzer (prefers B/no change but can accept). No formal dissents. The action is a 25bp ease with symmetric directive. This is a moderate dovish lean -- easing modestly with no forward signal of more. Score: 35-40. A 25bp ease with broad support but some reluctance. Score: 37.

Higher = more hawkish. Easing 25bp with majority support = dovish lean. 37 is correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. But there is notable disagreement: Minehan, Moskow, Stern, Broaddus, and Hoenig would prefer no change. Melzer also prefers no change but accepts. On the other side, Jordan and McTeer want more aggressive ease. The policy outcome is consensus at 25bp, but the economic discussion shows disagreement on the framework (opportunistic vs deliberate disinflation, Greenspan's "brave new world" hypothesis vs. traditional views). Score: 30-38. Notable differences about direction but no dissents. Score: 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Greenspan explicitly raises concern about "bond and stock bubble" and "the real danger is that we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble." He references October 1987 and equity premiums. McDonough apparently expressed concern about stock market overvaluation (referenced by Phillips). But most other participants do not dwell on financial stability. This is moderate attention -- the Chairman raises it prominently and it factors into the policy discussion, but it's not the dominant theme. Score: 30-38. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Greenspan proposes symmetric directive. The statement focuses on inflation improvement, not further easing. Several participants (Parry, Melzer) note the cut may need to be reversed. Greenspan: "It is conceivable that we may have to go lower. I do not think that we have to make that judgment." The signal is data-dependent/neutral after this ease. Score: 40-48. A mild easing lean (just eased, some think more may come) but no strong forward signal. Score: 43.

Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Just eased with symmetric/data-dependent posture. 43 is correct -- slightly dovish lean forward.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Several participants emphasize uncertainty. Boehne: "we are at one of those pivotal periods where people are less certain about where things are going." Broaddus: "considerable room for forecast error." Phillips: "mixed signals." Greenspan's hypothesis is explicitly framed as uncertain ("I keep mentioning the word 'if' because it is a hypothesis... something that we will not be sure is the appropriate assessment... for probably five to ten years"). Budget uncertainty is very high. However, many participants are relatively comfortable with the Greenbook forecast. Score: 40-48. Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 44.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (56): These diverge slightly -- labor market is tighter than the output gap would suggest. This is consistent with the text: unemployment is low at 5.6% with tight labor markets, but growth is near potential and slowing. The labor market is a lagging indicator, reflecting prior strength.

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (37) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (43): Both dovish-leaning, with the forward tone slightly less dovish than the current action, which makes sense -- they just eased but adopted symmetric directive with no commitment to further action. Coherent.

### Speaker Count Summary
- Favor 25bp ease: Greenspan, McDonough, Blinder, Lindsey, Yellen, Kelley, Phillips, Parry, McTeer, Guynn, Boehne, Jordan (~12)
- Prefer no change but accept 25bp: Minehan, Moskow, Hoenig (~3)
- Prefer no change: Broaddus, Stern, Melzer (~3, though Melzer says he "can accept some easing")

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- BOEHNE: "The outlook for inflation remains about as it has been, neither accelerating nor decelerating much during the next year or so... price pressures remain remarkably subdued."
- KELLEY: "inflation continues to be remarkably well contained. I am particularly impressed with the way that unit labor costs continue to behave. Productivity is still growing very nicely, and the ECI is flat."
- GREENSPAN: "I am not worried about product price inflation... the CPI is currently running under expectations or forecasts."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation around 3% CPI is well-contained and not accelerating; expectations are declining. Participants are generally comfortable that inflation is subdued, though some (Broaddus, Melzer) dislike 3% as a permanent state.

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Melzer are concerned that 3% inflation is inconsistent with price stability goals and want progress toward lower inflation. This informs the upper end of the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- BROADDUS: "the economy already operating at least in the neighborhood of potential GDP"
- HOENIG: "economic resources will continue to be used at or near capacity levels"
- GREENSPAN: "the economy has more of a feel of driving with the parking brake partially engaged"
- KELLEY: "growth near potential as the highest probability"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy at or very near potential, with Q4 growth slowing from Q3's torrid pace. Slight downside lean as multiple districts report softening.

**Minority views**: McTeer sees more downside risk; Broaddus notes upside risk from potentially strong Q4.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MELZER: "labor problems of various kinds, such as shortages, unqualified applicants, and retention are affecting many businesses... the shortage is so acute now that it is discouraging firms from expanding"
- STERN: "labor markets in the District are tight. There are shortages of skilled and unskilled workers"
- PHILLIPS: "At 5.6 percent unemployment, people are working"
- BLINDER: "labor lately has been taking it on the chin quite badly, despite apparently tight labor markets. Real wages are going nowhere"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are tight with unemployment at 5.6% and widespread reports of shortages, but wage pressures remain remarkably contained. Tighter than neutral but not generating inflationary wage increases.

**Minority views**: Blinder argues wages should be expected to catch up to prices, which would not necessarily be inflationary. Guynn reports shortages abating in some areas.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- BOEHNE: "Retail sales in particular reflect a cautious attitude on the part of consumers... an otherwise dismal season"
- MCTEER: "sales in recent weeks have been absolutely terrible, with general merchandise and apparel sales being at recession levels"
- KELLEY: "Retail sales are certainly unexciting... but the most recent monthly data... were really rather good"
- PHILLIPS: "fundamentals are reasonably strong for continued investment, though probably not at the strong pace that we saw in 1994"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is sluggish with disappointing holiday retail, cautious consumers, and rising debt concerns. Business investment remains supported by low capital costs but decelerating. Net position is somewhat below trend.

**Minority views**: Guynn reports quite good retail sales in the Southeast, especially high-end goods. Kelley notes November data were good.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MELZER: "Aggregate credit remains readily available... growth in M2 since the beginning of 1995 seems consistent with an economy growing at potential"
- KELLEY: "Credit availability still seems to be quite ample"
- GREENSPAN: "we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble" and "the sharp runup in stock prices is very heavily determined by the climb in long-term bond prices"
- HOENIG: "lower yields in long-term debt markets have been stimulative"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are loose -- long-term rates have fallen sharply, the stock market is surging, credit is readily available. However, the real short rate is perceived as restrictive due to declining inflation expectations.

**Minority views**: Several participants (Blinder, Jordan, Yellen) argue the real funds rate is too restrictive, creating a tension between loose long-end conditions and tight short-end policy.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "we rightfully should be moving the funds rate lower... I would go 25 basis points now with no change in the discount rate"
- YELLEN: "the current level of the real funds rate is on the high side... monetary policy should be forward-looking when we are lowering interest rates"
- BROADDUS: "I would come out with Cathy and Tom... our best move today is no move"
- STERN: "I favor no change in policy at this meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear majority supports 25bp ease, framed as an adjustment to declining inflation expectations rather than stimulus. Several prefer no change but acquiesce. No one advocates tightening. The lean is moderately dovish.

**Minority views**: 3-4 participants would prefer to hold (Broaddus, Stern, Minehan, Moskow), while Jordan wants more aggressive easing.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MINEHAN: "my basic inclination would be not to change policy at this meeting, but I can't debate 25 basis points... I don't think I will dissent"
- BROADDUS: "our best move today is no move"
- JORDAN: "I support the move today, but I think we need to be prepared to be a little more aggressive"
- Outcome: consensus for 25bp, no dissents, symmetric directive

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement on timing and direction. About a third of participants would prefer no change, but none dissent. Underlying disagreement about the inflation framework (opportunistic vs. deliberate) and Greenspan's technology hypothesis. But the action itself achieves consensus.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "The real danger is that we are at the edge of a bond and stock bubble... we are in the lower ranges" of equity premiums relative to October 1987
- GREENSPAN: "if we are perceived to be easing policy, it is conceivable that we could foster further problems in that regard"
- MINEHAN: "it is hard for me to think about easing credit in the face of the kinds of financial markets that we have right now"
- BOEHNE: "the asset inflation problem, the bubble effect, is one that we ought to take into account"

**Center-of-gravity**: The Chairman raises financial stability concerns prominently, and a few others echo it, but most participants do not focus on it. It is a meaningful constraint on the size of the easing (25bp not 50bp) but not the dominant theme.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 43 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- GREENSPAN: "It is conceivable that we may have to go lower. I do not think that we have to make that judgment... at this time"
- PARRY: "it is quite possible that we may have to reverse that reduction sometime in 1996"
- Outcome: symmetric directive -- no directional bias
- GREENSPAN proposed statement focuses on inflation improvement, not further easing signals

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly dovish -- they just eased and Greenspan acknowledges more may be needed, but the symmetric directive and some participants' warnings about reversal create a balanced forward posture. Data-dependent with a slight easing lean.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- BOEHNE: "we are at one of those pivotal periods where people are less certain about where things are going and attitudes can flow either way"
- BROADDUS: "there is considerable room for forecast error on either side"
- GREENSPAN: "it is a hypothesis... something that we will not be sure is the appropriate assessment of our changing world for probably five to ten years"
- BLINDER: "we had a big question mark about the budget, and now we have a huge question mark about the budget"

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty driven by: the ambiguous Q4 data, the budget impasse, the question of whether the NAIRU/productivity has shifted, and the inventory situation. Not extreme, but clearly above normal caveats.
