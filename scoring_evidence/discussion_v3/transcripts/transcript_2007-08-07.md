# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — August 7, 2007 (Transcript)

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of an FOMC meeting (August 7, 2007). Individual speakers are identified by name throughout. The document has two structural sections: a **go-around** (economic discussion) and a **policy discussion** (statement language debate). The go-around speakers include Presidents Pianalto, Stern, Hoenig, Plosser, Lockhart, Rosengren, Poole, Lacker, Vice Chairman Geithner, Governor Kohn, Governor Warsh, and Governor Kroszner, followed by Chairman Bernanke's summary. Governor Mishkin leads the policy discussion section. Total speaker count is approximately 13 distinct participants with substantive economic remarks, making this a high-evidence meeting. Note: President Moskow's remarks appear to be missing from the decomposition (the file jumps from Lacker's policy turn to Pianalto's policy turn without a Moskow text block), but this is not unusual for partial decompositions.

### 2. Era Context

August 2007 is a pivotal moment: the early phase of the Global Financial Crisis. The housing correction has been under way since 2006. In mid-2007, subprime mortgage market stress began spreading through structured-credit vehicles (CDOs, CLOs, commercial paper conduits). This is the meeting at which the Committee first explicitly acknowledged financial market turbulence as a policy-relevant risk. The baseline macro backdrop still showed moderate growth and improving (but still elevated) core inflation. The Fed funds rate had been held at 5.25% since June 2006.

### 3. Directional Assessment — Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target but declining. The recent trend is encouraging (core PCE below 2% for four consecutive months per Lacker; median CPI improving per Pianalto). However, most participants continue to flag upside risks: higher energy prices, slower productivity raising unit labor costs (Stern, Hoenig, Lacker, Kohn, Bernanke), tight labor markets, and potential reversal of transitory dampening factors (apparel, OER). Bernanke's summary: "risks to inflation remain to the upside." Only Mishkin argues risks are "balanced on inflation." Center of gravity: inflation is modestly above 2% target, trending down, but majority still see upside risks. Score: slightly above midpoint — approximately 57-60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as approximately at or very slightly below potential, with the key debate being whether potential itself has been revised downward. Several participants (Kohn, Stern, Plosser, Bernanke) refer to growth "near potential" or "returning to potential" by 2008. Kohn explicitly: "growth a little below potential for a few quarters." Hoenig: "Growth in 2008 and 2009 is likely to be near potential." Plosser forecasts return to near-potential real GDP growth in Q1/Q2 2008. No participant characterizes the current output gap as deeply negative. Mild negative gap with housing drag, but not recessionary. Score: approximately 45-48 (slightly below potential, but not far below).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants describe labor markets as healthy to tight. Stern: "The labor market overall, I would say, is strong." Plosser: "businesses continue to report tight labor markets." Lacker: "District labor markets are increasingly taut." Hoenig notes labor shortage causing slowdown, not demand weakness. Kohn: "Labor market conditions fairly firm." Bernanke summary: "consumption is likely to continue to grow as labor markets remain strong." Slight softening noted (Bernanke: "The unemployment rate is about 25 basis points above its recent low"), construction employment set to decline. But center of gravity is tight labor markets. Score: approximately 60-63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but with notable downside risks. Consumer spending is viewed as holding up but with headwinds. Lacker: "PCE was fairly soft last quarter." Stern raises savings-rate revision uncertainty. Housing investment is clearly weak and declining (multiple speakers). Business fixed investment is trimmed due to uncertainty (Pianalto, Plosser: "some risk of temporary weakness"). Positive elements: exports strong (Kohn), global demand robust, trucking soft but other sectors OK (Poole's mixed picture). The net view is moderately below trend due to housing drag and investment uncertainty. Score: approximately 43-47.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant topic. Financial conditions have tightened materially. Participants describe widened risk spreads, disruption in CDO/CLO/commercial paper markets, restricted securitization for nonconforming mortgages, tightening underwriting standards, illiquid structured products, and uncertainty about counterparty risk. Warsh: "pullback in liquidity — with considerably less trust in underlying valuations." Rosengren: "originate-to-distribute model has been disrupted." Kohn: "financial conditions have tightened noticeably in the past few months." Geithner: "broad based increase in risk premiums, decline in some asset prices, pockets of liquidity pressures." Bernanke: "markets right now are not functioning normally. Some conduits of credit are simply closed or frozen." However, several participants also note that traditional bank capital is sound, and conditions for high-quality borrowers have not changed much (Plosser, Poole, Kroszner). This is not yet a full credit freeze but is clearly elevated tightness. Score: approximately 68-72.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Near-unanimous support for holding rates at 5.25% (alternative B). Nobody proposes a cut at this meeting. The dominant lean is still focused on inflation as the primary concern, but with acknowledgment of increased downside growth risks. Fisher, Hoenig, Plosser, Poole, Lacker all maintain inflation as their focus. Geithner, Kohn, Yellen, Rosengren push toward softer language acknowledging growth risks. No one calls for a hike. The center of gravity: hold with mild inflation-hawkish bias, but with growing dovish undercurrent. This is a lean-hawkish "hold" position where inflation still "predominates" but with more balance creeping in. Score: approximately 53-57.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is notable disagreement in the **policy discussion** around statement language (whether to keep "predominant," where to place downside risk language), but there is broad agreement on the core action (hold rates). On the economic assessment, there is meaningful spread: Hoenig/Plosser/Lacker are more hawkish and less worried about financial market contagion; Geithner/Kohn/Rosengren/Yellen are more concerned about downside risks; Mishkin occupies a somewhat independent position on inflation balance. No formal dissent on the rate vote itself. The disagreement extends beyond tactics to the assessment of risks. Score: approximately 38-45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: This is the central topic of the meeting. Nearly every participant discusses financial market conditions at length — subprime contagion, CDO/CLO disruption, commercial paper rollover risk, credit rating agency credibility, leveraged loan pipeline, bank capital, hedge fund stress, originate-to-distribute model. Warsh devotes the bulk of his remarks to financial markets, running through each asset class. Geithner gives a detailed analysis. The policy discussion is almost entirely about how to communicate awareness of financial stability concerns. This is among the highest FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS meetings up to this point in the record — clearly dominant. Score: approximately 82-87.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee is explicitly holding rates and maintaining "inflation as predominant concern" in the statement. However, Geithner argues for softening asymmetry and preserving "flexibility." Kohn anticipates possible easing in 2008-2009. Yellen notes market expectations for rate cuts are "not inappropriate." Rosengren would prefer alternative A risk language. The statement retains the "predominant inflation concern" language but adds "downside risks to growth have increased." Forward signal: still slightly hawkish (inflation focus retained, no cut signaled), but with meaningful dovish tilt opening the door to future easing. Score: approximately 50-55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Extremely elevated. Multiple participants explicitly state they cannot assess the duration or magnitude of financial market effects. Stern: "it is very difficult, to put it mildly, to assess its effects." Lacker: "the outlook for housing is still awfully uncertain right now." Warsh: "even orderly repricings have fat tails." Kohn: "I also believe that there's a non-negligible chance of a prolonged and very messy adjustment period." Geithner: "The information we have now about what's happening in markets and about the implications for credit markets and ultimately for confidence and demand is very stale and uneven." Kroszner: "uncertainty has gone up dramatically." Bernanke summary: "greater uncertainty may constrain housing and investment spending." This is genuinely high uncertainty, not merely rhetorical — participants acknowledge they don't know how long the financial market disruption will last or what its magnitude will be. Score: approximately 70-75.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (46) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): These diverge somewhat, which is explainable. The output gap is mild because housing is dragging, but labor markets are still strong and the rest of the economy is holding. This split is consistent with the transcript — participants emphasize labor strength even as housing weighs on overall output.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Broadly aligned. Both reflect a "hold with inflation concern retained but flexibility opening for future easing" posture. Slight hawkish lean on both dimensions is appropriate.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (70) and FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (85): These align well — very tight conditions dominate the meeting's discussion.

### 6. Speaker Count Summary (for center of gravity)

- More worried, lean dovish on forward path: Pianalto, Rosengren, Geithner, Kohn (4)
- Moderately worried but see as transitory/contained: Stern, Lockhart, Hoenig, Kroszner, Warsh, Mishkin (6)
- Less worried, minimal impact expected: Plosser, Poole, Lacker (3)

Inflation assessment:
- Still predominant concern, upside risks remain: Fisher, Hoenig, Plosser, Lockhart, Lacker, Stern, Kroszner, Bernanke (~8)
- Balanced or more optimistic on inflation: Mishkin (1), Geithner (sees upside but modest/manageable)

Policy (rate action): Unanimous hold.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 58 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Pianalto: "I still think that inflation is the predominant risk we face today" despite recent favorable data; "I am not yet persuaded that this progress will be sustained."
- Bernanke summary: "risks to inflation remain to the upside...including the possible reversal of transitory factors, tight labor markets, the high price of commodities, and higher unit labor costs resulting from lower productivity growth."
- Lacker: "we've now had four months of readings below 2. But there are still abundant reasons for caution" and notes Greenbook cites transitory factors that "could well push core inflation back up."
- Kohn: "Risks around my inflation forecast remain to the upside...Utilization is tight. The recent run-up in energy prices could still feed through to expectations."

**Center of gravity:** The majority see core inflation as modestly above 2% and trending favorably, but still above their comfort zone with persistent upside risks. Only Mishkin argues risks are "quite balanced on inflation."

**Minority views:** Mishkin sees inflation risks as balanced, not skewed upward. This informs the modest ±6 uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 46 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "growth a little below potential for a few quarters, held down by the housing correction, and the unemployment rate rising a little further."
- Plosser (forecast): "the economy [will] return to near potential real GDP growth in the first or the second quarter of 2008"; currently below trend.
- Geithner: "Overall spending by households and businesses is weaker...growth risks have shifted a bit more to the downside around the lower expected path."
- Bernanke: "economic growth looks a little softer to me, mostly because of housing."

**Center of gravity:** Economy is slightly below potential, largely due to the housing correction, but not deeply negative. The output gap is mild. Return to potential is expected by mid-2008 in most forecasts.

**Minority views:** Hoenig is more optimistic ("second-half growth is likely to be around 2½ percent"), placing output closer to potential, informing the upward end of the uncertainty band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stern: "The labor market appears to be healthy. Job availability appears to be ample. The labor market overall, I would say, is strong."
- Plosser: "businesses continue to report tight labor markets...he cannot find labor." (Citing a builder unable to find workers due to immigration crackdowns.)
- Lacker: "District labor markets are increasingly taut, given steady hiring activity and slower labor force growth...contacts continue to report rising wage pressures and difficulty finding qualified workers."
- Bernanke summary: "consumption is likely to continue to grow as labor markets remain strong."

**Center of gravity:** Participants broadly view labor markets as tight by any standard, with low unemployment and reported labor shortages. Some softening at the margin (unemployment 25bp above its recent low), but the level remains firmly in "tight" territory.

**Minority views:** Poole notes his trucking contacts say "nobody is talking about any particular labor market pressures," and some employment growth has slowed. This informs the lower end of the range.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 44 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Lacker: "PCE was fairly soft last quarter" and notes "softness could be a harbinger of more-sustained weakness."
- Pianalto: contacts "have been reevaluating their capital formation plans...trimming their projections" for business fixed investment; "my projection for business fixed investment...may still be somewhat optimistic."
- Geithner: "Overall spending by households and businesses is weaker."
- Kohn: Expects "moderate growth in consumption" but notes "households postponed capital investments" as a risk.
- On the positive side, Stern notes inventory bounce-back and equipment and software spending rebounded in Q2.

**Center of gravity:** Consumption is holding at moderate pace supported by income and employment, but housing investment is clearly contracting and business fixed investment faces headwinds from uncertainty. Net position is below trend, largely due to housing drag.

**Minority views:** Hoenig sees "consumer spending seems to be holding well" and Plosser's nonresidential investment characterization remains "firm." These moderate the score upward at the margin.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 70 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Geithner: "broad based increase in risk premiums, decline in some asset prices, pockets of liquidity pressures, and some disruption in mortgage markets and high-yield corporate credit markets."
- Rosengren: "A number of financial instruments...are no longer readily available...The originate-to-distribute model has been disrupted...forced liquidation of collateral in illiquid markets has further depressed the market."
- Kohn: "financial conditions have tightened noticeably in the past few months, even abstracting from market disruptions of the last week."
- Bernanke: "markets right now are not functioning normally. Some conduits of credit are simply closed or frozen."

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions are clearly tight and tightening beyond normal levels. Key markets (CDO, CLO, nonconforming mortgage securitization, commercial paper conduits) are impaired. However, high-quality borrowers still have access to credit (Plosser, Poole, Kroszner on bank capital strength), preventing a score in the 80s-90s range.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Poole see limited real-economy credit crunch; Plosser: "cost of capital for high quality borrowers has hardly changed and remains relatively low." This informs the lower end of the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 54 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Unanimous support for alternative B (no rate change). Fisher: "I am in favor of keeping the rate where it is." Poole: "I haven't heard anyone suggesting that we should be changing the fed funds rate." Kohn, Kroszner, Mishkin, Warsh all support unchanged rates.
- Hawkish lean retained: Bernanke summary: "In all, the risks to inflation remain to the upside." Statement retains "predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate."
- Dovish undercurrent: Geithner argues for "some softening of the asymmetry in our assessment of the balance of risks." Yellen notes market expectations for cuts are "not inappropriate." Kohn assumes "an easing of policy in 2008 and 2009" in his projections.

**Center of gravity:** The Committee holds rates with an inflation-focused lean that is softer than previous meetings. The statement acknowledges increased downside risks to growth while retaining the inflation concern as "predominant." This is a "lean hawkish / hold" position where the door to future dovish action is visibly opening.

**Minority views:** Rosengren would prefer alternative A (more balanced risk language), representing the most dovish view. Lacker/Fisher prefer maintaining current inflation-first posture without softening.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 42 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- No formal voting dissent on the rate action. All participants support holding rates.
- Notable disagreement on risk characterization: Rosengren explicitly prefers alternative A language over alternative B. Multiple camps disagree about keeping "predominant" in the statement (Fisher, Yellen, Geithner want it removed; Warsh, Kroszner, Mishkin, Kohn want to retain it).
- Economic assessment divergence: Plosser/Poole/Lacker minimize financial market contagion risk; Geithner/Kohn/Rosengren treat it as a serious downside threat. This is not merely tactical disagreement.
- Bernanke acknowledges the range: "the risk of a bad inflation outcome has diminished...and the probability of an adverse growth outcome has increased" — framing both camps' views.

**Center of gravity:** Moderate disagreement. No formal dissents, but clear distinct camps on risk assessment and statement language, going beyond tactical differences to genuine differences in economic interpretation.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 85 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Warsh devotes essentially his entire statement to financial market analysis, covering leveraged loans, subprime, CDOs, broker-dealers, private capital, and transmission to the real economy.
- Geithner's entire statement is organized around financial market risk and its implications for aggregate demand.
- Rosengren: dedicated multiple paragraphs to originate-to-distribute disruption, rating agency credibility, CDO/CLO liquidity.
- Bernanke summary: "developments in credit markets received considerable attention...higher risk premiums, tougher underwriting, and greater uncertainty may constrain housing and investment spending."
- Policy discussion: almost entirely about how to communicate awareness of financial stability concerns without triggering a "Fed put."

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability is the dominant topic of this meeting. It rivals — and in time allocated, arguably exceeds — the traditional economic assessment discussion. This represents the highest financial stability emphasis in this Committee's recent history to this point.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 52 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Statement retains "predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate as expected" — still an inflation-focused forward signal.
- Simultaneously adds "downside risks to growth have increased somewhat" — a dovish opening.
- Kohn: "we could end up coming out of this and easing policy somewhere down the road, but not right away."
- Geithner: "a somewhat downward slope to the path of the fed funds rate going forward...even if financial market conditions stabilize."
- Poole: "we ought not to give any hint that we are suggesting a policy change at our next meeting."

**Center of gravity:** The forward signal is nearly balanced — inflation concern is retained as the stated priority, but the door to future easing is explicitly opened. The net forward guidance is slightly hawkish (inflation still predominates in the statement), but markedly softer than prior meetings. Score is just above 50.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 73 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Stern: "it is very difficult, to put it mildly, to assess its effects for reasons I alluded to earlier...we don't know how long this will last and what will follow in its wake."
- Geithner: "The information we have now about what's happening in markets and about the implications for credit markets and ultimately for confidence and demand is very stale and uneven."
- Warsh: "harder still is to understand the second- and third-order consequences; and perhaps most difficult is to determine whether any treatment is needed." Also: "I'd like to say that what we have witnessed over the past week is transitory, but for that set of asset markets, it is probably hardest for me to come to a broader judgment."
- Kohn: "I also believe that there's a non-negligible chance of a prolonged and very messy adjustment period...the tail of this distribution is a lot fatter than it was only a month or so ago."
- Lacker: "the outlook for housing is still awfully uncertain right now."

**Center of gravity:** Participants broadly acknowledge they are navigating genuine Knightian-style uncertainty about the duration, magnitude, and transmission of the financial market disruption. This is qualitatively and quantitatively beyond normal policy uncertainty — multiple speakers say they "cannot assess" outcomes. Approaches but does not reach the "uncharted territory" extreme of later 2008.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS:**
**Score: 52 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
Multiple participants explicitly discuss inflation expectations as anchored near 2%. Kohn: "inflation expectations as best we can judge are anchored at something like 2 percent PCE inflation." Mishkin: "key driver of inflation...are grounded around 2 percent...I have more and more confidence that inflation is gravitating strongly to this 2 percent level." Lacker: "inflation expectations remain above where I'd like them to be." Stern warns unit labor cost revision "flashes a bit of a warning light." Net view: anchored at target but not comfortably below, with some participants seeing slight upside skew. Score 52 (fractionally above 50 = anchored at target).

**CREDIT_STRESS:**
**Score: 72 ±8 [High confidence]**
Extensively discussed. Rosengren: "originate-to-distribute model has been disrupted...forced liquidation of collateral in illiquid markets...investors have lost confidence in rating agencies." Warsh describes loss of bid for mortgage-related paper, rollover risk in extendable commercial paper, fear of using financial institutions as counterparties. Kroszner: "more than 50 percent of senior loan officers reported they are tightening credit standards for subprime, and about 15 percent for prime." Spreads widening across investment-grade and high-yield. However, high-quality corporate borrowers largely unaffected. Score: clearly elevated stress, not yet extreme.

**EQUITY_STRESS:**
**Score: 62 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**
Warsh notes "We have lost about $1 trillion in market capital in the past twenty trading days." Consumer confidence likely to fall. Homebuilder equity prices at yearly lows (Rosengren). However, broader equity markets not characterized as in crash territory; participants focus mostly on credit markets. Concern level is moderate — equity price declines noted but not described as panic. Score: moderately elevated stress/concern.

**BANKING_STRESS:**
**Score: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
Multiple participants explicitly emphasize the soundness of banks as a stabilizing factor. Kroszner: "major banks have very high...capital-to-asset ratios in excess of required minimums...Tier 1 capital on the order of $80-90 billion...earnings for each over the last year are $25-35 billion." Poole: "bank capital is not impaired." Kohn: "well-capitalized banks and opportunistic investors will come in and fill the gap." The banking system is specifically contrasted with the early 1990s as being far sounder. Score: below 50 (more healthy/well-capitalized concern than banking stress) — specifically the discussion emphasizes absence of stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS:** N/A — not specifically discussed by participants as a distinct topic. Treasury yields mentioned briefly (Hoenig: "Treasury yields are likely to move back up") but not as a dysfunction/stress issue.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN:**
**Score: 28 ±10 [Low confidence]**
Brief mentions of a "lower dollar" as a factor offsetting financial tightening and supporting export growth (Kohn: "recent declines in the dollar will reinforce the effects of good foreign demand"; Rosengren mentions "falling dollar" as an upside inflation risk; Kroszner mentions "lower dollar" as inflation upside risk). The dollar is not a focus of concern — its weakness is generally described as either helpful or a minor inflation upside risk. Score: slightly below 50 (mild concern about dollar weakness, not strength; scored 28 to reflect concern about WEAKNESS side per polarity — higher = more concern about strength, so dollar weakness concern = below 50).

**PRODUCTIVITY:**
**Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
Multiple participants discuss the NIPA revision that revised down structural productivity growth. Stern: "the downward revision to productivity may have significant implications for the longer-term performance of the economy." Kroszner: "compounded growth rate of productivity is down to 1.4 percent from 1.8 percent." Plosser: discusses "rebenchmarking of the growth rate of structural productivity." Kohn: "damped increase in productivity growth implies greater pressure on business costs." Participants generally accept that trend productivity is lower than previously estimated (below historical trend). Score: 38 (below 50, below historical trend productivity).
