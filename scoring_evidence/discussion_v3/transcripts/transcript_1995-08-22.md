# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-08-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It covers the economic go-around and the policy discussion from the August 22, 1995 FOMC meeting. Speakers include: Hoenig, Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Boehne, McTeer, Broaddus, Melzer, Jordan, Stern, Lindsey, McDonough, Kelley, Phillips, Blinder, Yellen, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a mid-1990s transcript with the typical go-around structure.

### Era Context
August 1995: The economy had slowed in Q2 1995 due to an inventory correction following the 1994 tightening cycle (fed funds had been raised from 3% to 6% between Feb 1994 and Feb 1995). The July 1995 meeting had produced a 25bp cut to 5.75%. The economy was operating near capacity, unemployment around 5.7%, CPI inflation around 3%. The "soft landing" narrative was emerging.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as modestly contained but still around 3%, which is above what they consider price stability. Center of gravity: inflation is present but not accelerating, and recent data suggest the earlier uptick was temporary.
- Hoenig: "core inflation...I do not expect core inflation to move much below 3 percent" and "price pressures will remain firm"
- Moskow: "Reports on prices have been mixed but generally continue to indicate receding inflationary pressures"
- Parry: "CPI inflation come in at around 2-3/4 percent next year"
- Boehne: "inflationary pressures appear to be subsiding"
- Stern: "wage and price pressures will remain relatively restrained"
- McDonough: "slightly less sanguine on inflation but just slightly" — core inflation around 3%
- Melzer: Worried about inflation expectations exceeding 3%, "concerned about actual inflation and the high level of inflation expectations"
- Yellen: "news has also been quite favorable...lessened the concern that the uptick in inflation...could presage a higher inflation trend"
- Phillips: "the uptick in the first half of the year was really due to temporary cyclical pressures...outlook for inflation still seems to be in the vicinity of 3 percent"
- Kelley: "we still have to keep the inflation rate on a secular downward path"

Center of gravity: Inflation around 3% CPI, not accelerating, recent data favorable. Most see it as contained but not at price stability. This is modestly above what they want but not alarming. Score should be in the 51-58 range — modestly above target but with favorable trajectory. In 1995 terms, the implicit "target" was roughly 2-2.5% CPI; 3% is modestly above. Melzer is the inflation hawk outlier. Score: 53.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says modest inflation around 3%, not accelerating but not declining much. 53 > 50 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Participants see the economy as operating at or near potential, with a Q2 inventory correction mostly complete and growth resuming at around potential pace (2-2.5%).
- Hoenig: "most measures of the economy still operating at or above capacity"
- Jordan: "it is as good as it gets" for Ohio
- Melzer: District "operating at a high level," unemployment at 4.7%
- Broaddus: Economy near full capacity in key industries and labor markets
- Phillips: "The economy could well be set to resume its growth path at potential"
- Blinder: GDP gap of about 0.4% below potential per Greenbook
- Greenspan: "real pressure on the up side seems a good distance away"

The economy had slowed in Q2 but was rebounding. Most see it at or near potential, with a few noting operation at or slightly above capacity. Blinder notes a small gap below potential. Center of gravity is approximately at potential. Score: 50.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says roughly at potential, maybe slightly above in some districts but the Q2 slowdown created a tiny gap. 50 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Labor markets are relatively tight — unemployment at 5.7% nationally, which is historically low and near NAIRU estimates. Multiple districts report labor shortages, but wage pressures remain contained.
- Hoenig: "wage and price pressures remain subdued"
- Moskow: "Labor markets in the District remain relatively tight" but demand has tempered
- McTeer: "scattered reports of tight labor markets but little about wage pressures"
- Broaddus: "supply of both skilled and unskilled labor is quite tight" in NC
- Melzer: "pockets of labor shortages, especially for entry-level workers"
- Jordan: "people who are not working today in that area are people who don't want to work. Bonuses are being paid"
- Stern: "labor does remain in relatively scarce supply" but "not translated into...broad-based acceleration of wages"
- Phillips: "unemployment rate is historically low" but "more flexibility in the labor market than is implied by the 5.7 percent unemployment rate"
- Kelley: "employment is as strong as it is right now"

The picture is tight labor markets (shortages reported in many districts) but without the expected wage acceleration — a puzzle that many note. Unemployment near NAIRU. Score should be 53-58: tight but not severely so, given the absence of wage pressure. Score: 55.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Tight labor markets with shortages but muted wages. 55 matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Consumer spending and business investment are holding up well despite the inventory correction. Final demand has been sustained.
- Hoenig: "continued strength in consumption and business fixed investment"
- Moskow: Retail sales strengthening; housing starts picking up
- Boehne: "consumer lending is rising," retail underlying trend favorable, residential sales picked up
- Phillips: "Both consumer and business spending have resumed. The housing market has shown some renewed vigor."
- Greenspan: "not seeing a weakening in final demand"
- Yellen: "continued strength of consumption and investment spending in the face of the inventory adjustment"
- McDonough: "nonauto retail sales are strong; consumer confidence is strong"

Some concern about auto sales weakness, but broadly the picture is solid final demand. Score: 52 — at or modestly above trend. The auto sector weakness and inventory correction pulled things down a bit from where they were in 1994, but underlying demand is solid.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence is moderate-to-solid spending. 52 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Financial conditions are mixed. Long-term rates backed up 35bp since last meeting. Dollar strengthened. Stock market robust. Credit available.
- Broaddus: "recent upward adjustment in bond rates" reflecting concerns
- McDonough: Stock market robust, credit demand strong
- Phillips: "The stock market has paused but it didn't tank. Yield spreads have not widened...Credit demand appears reasonably strong."
- Melzer: "we have just cut short rates 25 basis points and have seen long-term nominal rates go up 35 to 40 basis points"
- Yellen: "significant backup in interest rates since our last meeting, coupled with a significant appreciation of the dollar...working to restrain"

The backup in long rates tightened conditions somewhat from the July easing, but overall conditions are not strained — credit is available, spreads are stable, equity markets strong. Score: 48 — roughly neutral, maybe marginally on the supportive side given strong equity markets and available credit.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions are roughly neutral with a small tightening impulse from higher long rates offset by strong equities. 48 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
Direction: Unanimous support for "B" (hold at current 5.75%) with symmetric directive. Greenspan frames the real funds rate as "probably somewhat higher than we are likely to want it to be somewhere down the track," signaling eventual easing but not now. No one argues for easing today; no one argues for tightening.
- Greenspan: "B and symmetrical seems to me the most sensible approach"
- All participants: unanimous support
- Blinder/Yellen: real rate above equilibrium, eventually need to ease, but not now
- Melzer/Broaddus: more concerned about inflation, but also support hold

The current action is hold with no bias, but the forward lean from Greenspan and several members is mildly dovish (real rate above neutral, eventual ease). Score: 47 — hold with a very mild dovish lean, but not an active easing bias at this meeting.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with mild dovish lean. 47 < 50 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Direction: Unanimous support for alternative B with symmetric directive. No dissents, no reservations expressed. Even the inflation hawks (Melzer, Broaddus) supported the hold.
- Every single participant voices support without qualification.

However, there IS disagreement on the economic assessment — Melzer is notably more worried about inflation while Blinder/Yellen see real rates as too high. These are real intellectual differences about the inflation outlook, but they converge on policy today. Score: 20 — broad agreement on policy despite some divergence in economic views.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous vote with moderate intellectual divergence. 20 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Direction: Very limited discussion. Phillips mentions stock market, yield curve, yield spreads briefly. McDonough briefly mentions Japanese banking sector stability. No sustained financial stability discussion.
- McDonough: concern about Japanese "basic financial stability...especially its banking sector"
- Phillips: brief market condition references
- Blinder: mentions potential impact on financial markets from fiscal train wreck

These are passing references, not sustained financial stability discussion. Score: 12 — brief mentions, 1-2 passing references.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Limited attention. 12 matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Direction: The forward signal is mildly dovish. Greenspan says the real rate is "probably somewhat higher than we are likely to want it to be somewhere down the track." Blinder and Yellen explicitly argue the real rate needs to come down eventually. But no urgency — data dependence and fiscal uncertainty dominate.
- Greenspan: real rate somewhat above equilibrium "over the longer run"
- Yellen: "the real funds rate is going to need to decline to keep the economy on track beyond the forecast horizon"
- Blinder: gap between equilibrium and actual real rate growing

The forward signal is mildly dovish — next move is likely an ease, but timing is uncertain and data-dependent. Score: 40 — easing lean with data dependence.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Mild easing lean forward. 40 < 50 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Direction: Elevated uncertainty, primarily around the fiscal/budget outlook. Multiple participants emphasize they cannot predict how the budget impasse will resolve.
- Kelley: lists many "very big questions" — deficit reduction, NAIRU, productivity, traumatized worker, consumer debt, inflation measurement
- Greenspan: "we don't know how the budget debate will be resolved"
- Minehan: "the risks are sizable on either side and there isn't a high probability of hitting the forecast on the head"
- Blinder: fiscal process could rattle financial markets; "none of us can predict what is going to happen"
- Lindsey: extensive discussion of political uncertainty around budget

Genuine uncertainty, not just rhetorical hedging. Multiple major unknowns are emphasized. Score: 48 — elevated uncertainty, not extreme but clearly more than normal caveats.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Multiple emphasized unknowns. 48 matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (55): Close together, with labor slightly tighter than overall output, which fits — labor markets were tight while the economy was at potential due to the Q2 inventory correction. Reasonable divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (40): Both mildly dovish, with forward guidance a bit more dovish. This makes sense — hold today but expect to ease later. The forward guidance is more dovish than the current action, as expected when participants say rates are above equilibrium.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "core inflation...I do not expect core inflation to move much below 3 percent, if at all. With most measures of the economy still operating at or above capacity...price pressures will remain firm."
- MR. MOSKOW: "Reports on prices have been mixed but generally continue to indicate receding inflationary pressures, mainly in input prices."
- MS. YELLEN: "Recent readings on producer and consumer prices along with the appreciation of the dollar have lessened the concern that the uptick in inflation that we saw in the first half of the year could presage a higher inflation trend."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants see inflation around 3% CPI — modestly above their desired trajectory but not accelerating, with recent data favorable and the earlier uptick appearing temporary.

**Minority views:** Melzer is notably more hawkish on inflation, worried about elevated inflation expectations (3-4% range in surveys) and lack of credibility. Parry is more optimistic at 2-3/4%. These inform the ±7 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 50 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. HOENIG: "most measures of the economy still operating at or above capacity and likely to do so for some time"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The economy could well be set to resume its growth path at potential."
- MR. BLINDER: "there is a small GDP gap at the forecast horizon...GDP is below potential by just a hair more than it is now...about .4 percent of GDP"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is operating approximately at potential — the Q2 inventory correction created a brief pause but final demand held up, and growth is expected to resume at 2-2.5% (potential rate).

**Minority views:** Hoenig sees economy at or above capacity; Blinder sees a tiny gap below potential. Narrow range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. JORDAN: "people who are not working today in that area are people who don't want to work. Bonuses are being paid and firms are competing for unskilled or trainable workers."
- MR. STERN: "labor does remain in relatively scarce supply. That combination is interesting because it still has not translated into anything resembling a broad-based acceleration of wages."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Although the unemployment rate is historically low, it is difficult to assess the longer-term impacts of the re-engineering binge...there may be more flexibility in the labor market than is implied by the 5.7 percent unemployment rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets are moderately tight with widespread reports of shortages across districts, but the puzzle is that this has not produced broad wage acceleration, suggesting the effective tightness may be less severe than headline unemployment implies.

**Minority views:** Some districts (Connecticut, Rhode Island, parts of Maryland) are sluggish, while others (Ohio, North Carolina) are very tight. The range of conditions informs the ±6 band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are not seeing a weakening in final demand despite all the evidence that clearly points to a far more rapid pace of inventory adjustment"
- MS. YELLEN: "The continued strength of consumption and investment spending in the face of the inventory adjustment, coupled with strong evidence of a rebound in residential construction, substantially mitigates what I had thought was one of the most serious downside risks."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "nonauto retail sales are strong; consumer confidence is strong; and the stock market is quite robust"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and business investment remain solid at approximately trend pace, with housing rebounding, though auto sector weakness is a notable headwind.

**Minority views:** Auto weakness and consumer debt concerns (Phillips, Kelley) versus broad-based strength reports. Narrow band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. YELLEN: "we have had a significant backup in interest rates since our last meeting, coupled with a significant appreciation of the dollar. I think that those two forces are working to restrain this upside risk."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The stock market has paused but it didn't tank...Yield spreads have not widened...Credit demand appears reasonably strong."
- MR. MELZER: "we have just cut short rates 25 basis points and have seen long-term nominal rates go up 35 to 40 basis points"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are roughly neutral — the 35bp backup in long rates provided some tightening impulse, but this is offset by a robust stock market, stable credit spreads, and strong credit demand.

**Minority views:** Some see the long rate backup as meaningful tightening; others emphasize supportive equity conditions. Moderate dispersion.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 ±4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "B and symmetrical seems to me the most sensible approach until the next meeting...the real federal funds rate is...probably somewhat higher than we are likely to want it to be somewhere down the track"
- All participants: unanimous support for hold with symmetric directive
- MS. YELLEN: "eventually the real funds rate is going to need to decline to keep the economy on track beyond the forecast horizon"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous hold at 5.75% with symmetric directive, but the intellectual lean is mildly dovish — Greenspan and several Governors explicitly note the real rate is above equilibrium and will eventually need to come down. No urgency due to fiscal uncertainty.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Broaddus have more inflation concern but still support the hold. The unanimity yields a narrow band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Every participant voices explicit support: "I support your policy proposal" (Hoenig, Lindsey, McDonough, Kelley, Minehan, Boehne, Parry, Melzer, Stern, Broaddus, Jordan, Phillips, Yellen, Moskow, Blinder)
- No reservations, qualifications, or alternative proposals mentioned

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Complete unanimity on policy action and directive. While there is some intellectual divergence on the inflation outlook (Melzer vs. Blinder/Yellen), it produces no policy disagreement at this meeting.

**Minority views:** None on policy. The economic assessment divergence is substantive but convergent on action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "the basic financial stability of that country [Japan], especially its banking sector"
- MS. PHILLIPS: Brief mentions of stock market behavior, yield spreads, credit conditions as contextual references
- MR. BLINDER: Mentions potential for fiscal train wreck to rattle "foreign exchange markets, the domestic bond market, and the stock market"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives only passing, contextual mentions. The meeting focus is overwhelmingly on the real economy, inflation, and fiscal policy outlook.

**Minority views:** None — this is consistently a low-emphasis topic across all speakers.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 40 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "that rate is probably somewhat higher than we are likely to want it to be somewhere down the track or over the longer run, with 'down the track' being on the other side of the fiscal train wreck"
- MR. BLINDER: "if the real interest rate is above the equilibrium real interest rate...the gap between potential and actual GDP...will grow bigger as we go forward — and at an accelerating rate"
- MS. YELLEN: "the real funds rate is going to need to decline to keep the economy on track beyond the forecast horizon"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is mildly dovish — the next move is likely an ease, but timing depends on the fiscal outlook and incoming data. No urgency or commitment.

**Minority views:** Melzer and Broaddus implicitly push back on the easing framing by emphasizing inflation concerns and the unreliability of real rate targeting. This widens the uncertainty band somewhat.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: Lists "many very big questions...whose answers are going to have to unfold over the coming months...it's rather awesome" — deficit reduction, NAIRU, productivity trends, traumatized worker, consumer debt, inflation measurement
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we don't know how the budget debate will be resolved...it is not terribly clear precisely how the fourth quarter is going to come out"
- MR. BLINDER: "none of us can predict what is going to happen...it now ought to be at the front of our screens, not at the back"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is elevated, primarily centered on the unprecedented fiscal/budget confrontation expected in Q4 and its unknown economic consequences. Multiple structural questions (NAIRU, productivity) add further uncertainty.

**Minority views:** Some participants are more sanguine (Stern: "we are in pretty good shape"), but the dominant tone is one of genuine uncertainty about the fiscal outlook.
