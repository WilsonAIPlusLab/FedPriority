# FOMC Scoring: 1996-01-31 Transcript

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the January 30-31, 1996 FOMC meeting. It contains individual speaker statements from the economic go-around (Day 1) and the policy discussion (Day 2). Speakers are identified by name. This is an early transcript (1990s era) with the traditional go-around structure.

### Era Context
January 1996: The U.S. economy is in the fifth year of the expansion that began in March 1991. The Fed had tightened through 1994-early 1995, raising the funds rate to 6%. It had begun easing in July 1995 (25bp) and again in December 1995 (25bp), bringing the funds rate to 5.50%. CPI inflation was running around 2.7-3.0%. Unemployment was approximately 5.6%. The economy was perceived as growing near potential but softening, with weather, government shutdowns, and inventory issues clouding the picture.

### Core Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as roughly at the ~3% CPI level, which is somewhat above what they'd like (price stability) but not accelerating dangerously. The center of gravity sees inflation as contained but not declining. Key views:
- MR. PARRY: "I remain skeptical that the recent dropoff in inflation will be maintained... I concur with the Greenbook in expecting CPI inflation this year to be at or somewhat above last year's rate."
- MR. BROADDUS: "faint, but nonetheless discernible, signs of a firming in wages and labor costs... increase in average hourly earnings was 2.8 percent in 1994 and 3.2 percent in 1995."
- MS. MINEHAN: "a clear lack of any significant inflationary pressure, although there could very well be some uptick in inflation."
- MS. YELLEN: "I see no intensification of underlying inflationary forces at this point."
- MR. MELZER: "virtually no gains were made on inflation during 1995."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "some arguments can be made for a more optimistic projection than the staff forecast of 3 percent."
- MR. HOENIG: "Wage and price pressures remain modest in the District despite overall strength."

Most participants see inflation at roughly 3% CPI, which is contained but not making progress toward price stability. Some see potential for modest uptick in wages. This is slightly below the rubric midpoint -- inflation is present but not intensifying. Score should be around 42-48: slightly below target by modern standards but not alarming. In 1996, there was no explicit 2% target; the discussion centers around 3% as the current trend, with aspirations for lower. Participants broadly see inflation as stable-to-slightly-firming, not declining. Score ~45.

Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Participants see moderate inflation pressure (~3% CPI), stable, possibly slightly firming. 45 fits -- slightly below midpoint, reflecting that inflation is contained but not zero concern.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Participants generally see the economy operating near potential, with some softening.
- MR. HOENIG: "real GDP likely to run at or perhaps a little below potential"
- MR. MOSKOW: "We see the economy expanding near its potential growth rate this year"
- MR. BROADDUS: "the economy is operating in the neighborhood of the NAIRU without a lot of head room"
- MS. YELLEN: "grew over the first three quarters of 1995 at a pace a bit shy of the likely growth of potential output"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: describing economy with "hand brake on," downside risks elevated
- MS. PHILLIPS: "best current projection for GDP growth is about 2 percent, or close to potential"
- MR. PARRY: "I think we can place a low probability on the economy going into a slump"

Multiple participants describe economy as "at or slightly below potential." The bright-line rule says "at potential" = 48-52. With several noting it's "a bit below" or "slightly below," I lean toward 47-49. Score ~48.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. "Near potential, slightly below" = just under 50. 48 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Labor markets are relatively tight but with mixed signals.
- MR. MELZER: "average unemployment rate stood at 4-1/2 percent in November" (his district); nationally 5.6%
- MR. MOSKOW: "Labor markets generally continue to be tight throughout the District"
- MR. MCTEER: "labor market tightness is pushing up wages or restraining the ability of some firms to expand production... contacts have mentioned labor market tightness in eight of the last nine Beigebooks"
- MR. HOENIG: "Labor markets remain tight in some parts of the District"
- MR. STERN: "labor markets remain tight, but there are only scattered signs of more rapid increases in wages"
- MS. YELLEN: "measured unemployment rate that is a tad below the staff's... point estimates of the natural rate"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Price and wage pressures remain subdued, with fierce competition and job insecurity acting as dampers"
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: forecasts unemployment rising to 5.9% in 1996

The unemployment rate is around 5.6%, near or slightly below NAIRU estimates. Multiple districts report tight labor markets. But job insecurity, downsizing announcements (AT&T), and worker anxiety moderate the picture. Score should be around 50-55: near full employment with some tightness. Score ~52.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Unemployment near NAIRU, multiple reports of tight labor markets, some wage firming. 52 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Mixed picture. Consumer spending is soft; business investment has been decent but moderating.
- Multiple speakers report weak holiday retail sales across districts
- MR. BOEHNE: "The retail sector has been soft. Holiday sales were below expectations"
- MR. BROADDUS: "anecdotal reports of weak holiday sales despite discounting"
- MR. MOSKOW: "January sales were coming in at or somewhat below expectations"
- MS. MINEHAN: "Retailing, in contrast, had a very weak holiday season"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "consumption is likely at best to track income growth"
- MR. STERN: "Retail spending has been lackluster"
- But: housing is holding up, PDE investment expected to be decent
- MR. STERN: "investment in producers durable equipment... will turn in another healthy year"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The fundamentals favor continued growth in capital investment"

Consumer side is clearly soft; business investment is moderate. Net position is somewhat below trend. Score ~40.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Weak consumer, moderate investment = below trend. 40 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Financial conditions are generally accommodative.
- MR. MELZER: "Longer-term bond yields have held their ground... the relatively low long-term rates should help growth prospects... The stock market, of course, continues to set new records... credit remains readily available"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The capital markets are not going to be a bottleneck... The stock markets have been strong... Declines in long-term interest rates should also contribute to keeping the cost of capital down"
- MR. HOENIG: "Capital and loans are still available and even plentiful in some cases"
- MS. MINEHAN: "the ebullience of financial markets"
- MS. YELLEN discusses strong dollar adding tightening equivalent of ~40bp

Financial conditions are loose: booming stock market, declining long rates, credit readily available. But some offset from dollar appreciation. Score ~30 (somewhat loose).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Loose conditions = low score. 30 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
Direction: The committee voted to cut the funds rate by 25bp. Chairman Greenspan proposed the cut. A significant minority (Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Melzer, Moskow -- 6 participants) would have preferred to hold. The majority supported the cut as "insurance."

Supporting 25bp cut: Greenspan, McDonough, Jordan, Boehne, Stern, Guynn, Phillips, Lindsey, Yellen, Kelley, McTeer (~11)
Opposing (prefer hold): Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Melzer, Moskow (~6)

The majority favors a modest easing move, framed as insurance, not stimulus. This is a mild dovish lean -- easing in an economy near potential. Score ~42: lean dovish, hold steady with easing bias realized.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Majority favors easing = below 50. 42 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Direction: Significant disagreement. Approximately 6 participants explicitly stated they would prefer no change, while ~11 supported the cut. Minehan and Melzer expressed strong opposition. No formal dissents recorded in the text, but the split was roughly 11-6 in sentiment. Additionally, the earlier discussion on inflation targeting/Mack Bill showed philosophical disagreements.

Score ~45: notable differences. The 6 dissenters represent a substantial minority with substantive objections, not just tactical differences.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. ~35% opposing with substantive arguments = moderate-to-notable disagreement. 45 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Direction: Very little discussion of financial stability risks per se. The stock market boom is mentioned but mostly as a positive for wealth effects, not as a concern. Governor Lindsey's extensive discussion of household balance sheets and debt is the closest, but it's framed as consumer vulnerability, not financial stability risk. No discussion of systemic risk, banking stress, or market dysfunction.

Score ~12: brief mentions, mostly passing references to stock market levels without stability concern framing.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Very little attention = low score. 12 fits. Anchor for 1993-96 typical is 1-10, but Lindsey's household balance sheet discussion and some credit quality mentions push it slightly above.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Direction: The directive was symmetric after the 25bp cut. Greenspan framed it as "insurance" that could be reversed. The forward signal is roughly neutral-to-mildly-dovish: several participants see further easing as possible if economy weakens, but others want to wait and see.
- MR. GUYNN: "the prospect that we may need to ease further in the period ahead"
- MS. YELLEN: sees risk of "growth recession with gradually rising unemployment" if rates stay put
- MR. BROADDUS: worried about difficulty of reversing cuts in election year
- Symmetric directive = no explicit bias

Score ~43: mild easing lean with data dependence. The insurance framing and symmetric directive suggest near-neutral forward posture with a slight dovish tilt.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Mild easing lean = below 50. 43 fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Direction: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources: weather disruptions, government shutdowns, data delays, structural changes in retail, unclear inventory situation, NAIRU uncertainty.
- MS. PHILLIPS: "There is more fog than usual in Washington and in the outlook"
- MS. YELLEN: natural rate confidence interval "disconcertingly wide"
- MR. MOSKOW: "A lot of uncertainty... A lot of questions are being raised about the short-term outlook"
- GREENSPAN: "it is hard for me to judge how long this cyclical advance will continue"
- Multiple speakers note data are "less reliable than usual"

This is somewhat elevated uncertainty but not extreme. Score ~42: somewhat elevated, specific factors harder to assess.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Elevated but not extreme. 42 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52): Close together, both near 50. Output gap slightly below, labor slightly tight -- consistent with economy at potential with some softening in output while labor markets remain relatively firm. Reasonable divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (43): Both lean mildly dovish. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "the CPI has risen about 2-3/4 percent in each of the last three years. The Greenbook is now projecting that inflation will move up slightly to about 3 percent in 1996 and 1997."
- MS. YELLEN: "I have not seen any reason to alter my view that it is unlikely to move higher... I see no intensification of underlying inflationary forces at this point."
- MR. MELZER: "virtually no gains were made on inflation during 1995... market participants and professional economic forecasters do not expect lower inflation in the foreseeable future."

Center-of-gravity: Participants perceive inflation as stable around 3% CPI -- contained but not declining, with some faint signs of wage firming. Not alarming but not making progress toward price stability.

Minority views: Broaddus and Melzer see more upside risk to inflation; Yellen and Phillips are more optimistic about benign inflation. Narrow band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. HOENIG: "real GDP likely to run at or perhaps a little below potential"
- MR. MOSKOW: "We see the economy expanding near its potential growth rate this year"
- MS. YELLEN: "an economy that grew over the first three quarters of 1995 at a pace a bit shy of the likely growth of potential output"

Center-of-gravity: Economy operating at or very slightly below potential. Multiple participants use "near potential" or "at potential" language, with some noting slight softening.

Minority views: Greenspan sees more downside risk; Guynn sees potential as higher than staff estimates. Narrow band.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MCTEER: "labor market tightness is pushing up wages or restraining the ability of some firms to expand production... contacts have mentioned labor market tightness in eight of the last nine Beigebooks"
- MS. YELLEN: "the economy is operating with a measured unemployment rate that is a tad below the staff's... point estimates of the natural rate"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Price and wage pressures remain subdued, with fierce competition and job insecurity acting as dampers"

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment near NAIRU (~5.6%), multiple districts report tight labor markets and skilled worker shortages. But job insecurity and downsizing moderate perceived tightness. Near full employment with slight tightness.

Minority views: McTeer and Hoenig report more pronounced tightness; Boehne and Yellen emphasize offsetting factors (insecurity, wide NAIRU confidence interval).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BOEHNE: "The retail sector has been soft. Holiday sales were below expectations, and January sales have been hurt by severe weather."
- MR. STERN: "Retail spending has been lackluster except for the consumer electronics area... investment in producers durable equipment... will turn in another healthy year"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The fundamentals favor continued growth in capital investment, though probably not at the 1994 and 1995 levels."

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending is clearly soft; holiday sales disappointing across many districts. Business investment is holding up better but moderating. Net position is below trend.

Minority views: Hoenig's district is strong; McTeer reports 1995 was a good year in 11th District. But these are minority voices.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MELZER: "The stock market, of course, continues to set new records on the up side on a daily basis, and credit remains readily available. The broad monetary aggregates are growing at the top of the Committee's target ranges."
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The stock markets have been strong; they appear to be supported by earnings. Declines in long-term interest rates should also contribute to keeping the cost of capital down."
- MR. HOENIG: "Capital and loans are still available and even plentiful in some cases."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative -- booming stock market, declining long-term rates, credit readily available, monetary aggregates growing rapidly. Some offset from dollar appreciation.

Minority views: Yellen notes dollar appreciation equivalent to ~40bp tightening. But overall conditions are loose.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I come to the bottom line that... there is an increasing element of downside risks... it would be wise in my judgment for us to move rates down 25 basis points."
- MS. MINEHAN (opposing): "I would buy a little insurance now that might enable inflation to move down... I would buy that insurance in the form of not moving now."
- MR. PARRY (opposing): "I would not favor reducing the federal funds rate... the Committee's forecasts speak very loudly and are in conflict with reducing rates at this time."

Center-of-gravity: Majority (~11 of 17) supports a 25bp cut framed as insurance against downside risks. Substantial minority (~6) prefers no change, citing inflation concerns and economy near potential. The action lean is mildly dovish.

Minority views: Melzer strongly opposed, sees easing as deliberately raising inflation. Minehan, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Moskow also prefer holding.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Approximately 6 of 17 participants explicitly opposed the Chairman's proposal (Minehan, Parry, Hoenig, Broaddus, Melzer, Moskow)
- MR. MELZER: "I would favor alternative B. I would favor it strongly... this action says that we are not interested in lowering inflation"
- The split extends beyond tactics to assessment of whether the economy needs easing at all

Center-of-gravity: Notable disagreement. The ~35% opposition is substantive, involving different economic assessments (not just timing). Some voiced strong philosophical objections.

Minority views: Range from Melzer's strong hawkish opposition to McTeer/Lindsey suggesting 25bp may not be enough.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LINDSEY: Extended discussion of household balance sheets, debt burdens, and financial asset distribution -- but framed as consumer vulnerability, not systemic financial stability.
- Stock market "ebullience" mentioned by Minehan and others but not as a concern per se.
- No discussion of banking stress, credit market dysfunction, or systemic risk.

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is not a meaningful topic of discussion. Mentions of financial conditions are incidental to the economic outlook discussion.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Symmetric directive adopted after the 25bp cut
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "implicit in our decision is the understanding that if the insurance turns out to be inappropriate... it will get reversed"
- MR. GUYNN: "the prospect that we may need to ease further in the period ahead"
- MS. YELLEN: "I think a growth recession with gradually rising unemployment is a distinct risk if we keep the funds rate where it is now"

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is near-neutral with a slight easing lean. Symmetric directive suggests no committed direction. But the insurance framing and several participants' comments about potential further easing tilt mildly dovish.

Minority views: Hawks like Melzer and Broaddus would want to reverse this move; Yellen sees potential need for more easing.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "There is more fog than usual in Washington and in the outlook. Some of this uncertainty is caused by the data situation."
- MS. YELLEN: "the natural rate... is very imprecisely estimated. A recent paper... applies every conceivable econometric bell and whistle to compute a confidence interval... It is hard to get it down below 2 percentage points."
- MR. MOSKOW: "A lot of uncertainty. A lot of questions are being raised about the short-term outlook"

Center-of-gravity: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated due to data disruptions (weather, government shutdowns), structural changes in retail, and difficulty reading current economic conditions. Not extreme, but clearly above normal.
