### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the December 15–16, 2009 FOMC meeting. It contains individual participant statements identified by name (MR. ROSENGREN., MS. YELLEN., MR. PLOSSER., etc.). The document includes: (a) a go-around economic assessment on Day 1 (December 15); (b) Chairman Bernanke's summary at the opening of Day 2; (c) a policy discussion (go-round on alternatives A/B/C); and (d) a special topic discussion on inflation dynamics and output gaps. This is a segmented, full-length transcript with rich multi-speaker content.

### 2. Era Context
December 2009: The U.S. economy is in the early phase of recovery from the deepest post-war recession. Unemployment is at ~10%. The Fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound (0–0.25%), and the Fed has deployed large-scale asset purchases (MBS, agency debt). The Greenbook projects no rate change through 2010. This is a classic post-GFC deflation-risk, demand-slack environment. Era anchors suggest: OUTPUT_GAP ~15-22, LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~15-22, INFLATION_PRESSURE ~15-20. However, the text must be read on its own to confirm or modify.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "there is little evidence of inflationary pressures. A variety of models used by the Boston Fed expect continued disinflation… the very high unemployment rate relative to any reasonable estimate of full employment suggests further disinflation."
- Yellen: "inflation is undesirably low, and, even with large monthly employment gains, the level of resource slack will remain high for an extended period."
- Kohn: "I expect intense competition among workers and businesses to keep costs low and the underlying trend of inflation stable at a low rate, that is, a rate below my objective and/or perhaps even trending lower. Market-based core PCE has continued to go lower to around 1¼ percent."
- Fisher (relative hawk): "the fraction of components in the PCE registering price declines remains elevated and sustained…I remain unalarmed."

**Center of gravity:** The overwhelming consensus is that inflation is meaningfully below target with disinflation risk ongoing. Market-based core PCE ~1.25%, unit labor costs falling, resource slack large. This maps to the 11-25 range (deflation risk, below target). Score of 20 reflects the presence of anchored expectations partially offsetting slack, preventing a score in the 1-10 deflationary spiral range.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Bullard see medium-term upside inflation risk (2-5 year horizon) from excess reserves, but explicitly place current inflation as non-alarming. Lacker: "case for further disinflation continues to weaken."

**Score: 52 ±10 | Confidence: Moderate**
