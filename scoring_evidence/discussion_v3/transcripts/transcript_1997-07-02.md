# FOMC Meeting Scoring - July 1-2, 1997

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It is a two-day pre-Humphrey-Hawkins meeting covering: (1) a go-around on economic conditions, (2) a lengthy discussion on price stability measurement, (3) long-run ranges for monetary aggregates, and (4) the policy directive discussion. Speakers include: Boehne, Guynn, Stern, Jordan, Rivlin, McTeer, Meyer, McDonough, Kelley, Parry, Hoenig, Minehan, Moskow, Broaddus, and Chairman Greenspan.

### Era Context
Mid-1997: The U.S. economy is in a prolonged expansion, unemployment at 4.8% (well below most NAIRU estimates of ~5.4%), yet inflation remains remarkably low. This is the period when the "New Economy" productivity puzzle was front and center. The stock market is surging. The FOMC had raised rates 25bp in March 1997 and has been on hold since.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below typical concern levels despite extremely tight labor markets. The center of gravity is that current inflation is remarkably subdued. Boehne: "Producer prices have fallen for five straight months...Consumer prices are rising at a slower pace this year than last." McTeer: "Neither is there a buildup of inflation in the pipeline." Meyer notes inflation is surprisingly low given the unemployment rate. However, virtually every participant acknowledges upside risks going forward -- Guynn, Stern, Meyer all see forecasted upward drift. Greenspan: "the inflation risks certainly remain on the upside." The consensus is: inflation is currently very low, puzzlingly so, but risks are tilted up. Direction: currently below target/stable, but risks lean up. This maps to roughly 38-44 -- below target now but with acknowledged upside risks pulling the score slightly toward midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly believe the economy is at or modestly above potential. Jordan: metropolitan areas "operating flat out." Moskow: "the economy is operating beyond its potential." Greenspan describes labor market tightness as "digging into the bottom of the barrel for workers." Growth moderated in Q2 but expected to rebound strongly in H2. Meyer: "above-trend growth over coming quarters." However, Meyer also notes the interesting divergence: labor markets tight but capacity utilization only a shade above its natural rate. The center of gravity is above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language present, score must be >50. Score: 55-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is one of the dominant themes. Stern: "the degree of tightness in our labor market cannot be overstated." Unemployment at 4.8%, lowest in 24 years, well below NAIRU estimates of ~5.4%. Boehne: "The unemployment rate is as low as it has been in 24 years." Jordan: "no unemployed construction workers." McTeer: "it is becoming increasingly difficult to find any pockets of weakness." Signing bonuses, retention bonuses, job fairs across cities. Wage increases are picking up but modestly. Direction: very tight labor market. The 1999-2000 anchor is 65-75; mid-1997 is slightly earlier but similar conditions. Score: 68-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Growth slowed in Q2 (Greenspan estimates ~2%), seen as payback for robust Q1. But fundamentals remain very positive. Meyer: "solid momentum in income and employment; financial conditions are generally favorable, and measures of consumer confidence have soared to new highs." Business investment is described as booming, especially high-tech. Guynn: "tourism and hospitality industries...continue their solid growth." Construction booming. McTeer: energy at full capacity. Q2 is a pause, not a fundamental slowdown; rebound expected in H2. Direction: above trend on net, temporarily soft but underlying strength is clear. Score: 57-62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are discussed as generally favorable/loose, not tight. Meyer: "financial conditions are generally favorable." McTeer: "The yield curve is lower and flatter, the dollar remains strong, and the bond vigilantes have eased long-term rates recently." Minehan raises concern about "booming capital markets, enormous liquidity, and incipient real estate booms." Jordan: "aggressive bidding for real estate deals...speculative residential and commercial projects are getting funded." McDonough: real fed funds rate is "rather tight; it certainly is not loose." But conditions in markets appear loose. The center of gravity: conditions are accommodative to neutral despite modestly restrictive policy rates. Score: 30-35 (somewhat loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants support holding rates steady ("B" asymmetric). But the lean is clearly hawkish. Nearly every speaker acknowledges that tightening will likely be needed soon. Meyer explicitly calls for 50bp of tightening over H2 1997. Guynn: "we probably will need to adjust policy further sometime soon." Broaddus: "I would still prefer to take out some insurance now." Parry: "we will have to tighten policy soon." Moskow: "we will have to increase interest rates eventually." Even the relative doves (Boehne, McTeer, Rivlin, McDonough) support asymmetric toward tightening. Greenspan: "I do not think we have the luxury of relaxing our vigilance." Direction: hold with clear tightening bias. Score: 55-58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissent -- all support "B" asymmetric. There is a range of views but remarkable consensus on the current decision. The spread is between those who would prefer to act now (Broaddus, Meyer, Guynn) and those more comfortable waiting (McTeer, Rivlin, McDonough, Boehne). But no one dissents. The disagreement is about timing (now vs. soon), not direction. Score: 25-30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market valuation is discussed several times. Rivlin: "the stock market is being described even more emphatically...as seriously overvalued." Jordan discusses speculative activity in real estate, farmland. Minehan warns at length about asset bubbles and boom/bust scenarios, comparing to late 1980s and Japan. Greenspan discusses financial stability vs. price stability in theoretical terms. Multiple participants mention real estate booms. This is more than passing mention but not the dominant topic of the meeting -- the economic conditions go-around and price stability measurement discussion take more time. Score: 35-42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward tightening. Greenspan: "I suspect that such a case [for tightening] will emerge by the time of the August meeting." Meyer calls for 50bp over remainder of year. Multiple participants expect tightening soon. The asymmetric directive signals bias toward tightening. But there is also genuine uncertainty about timing. Direction: mild-to-moderate tightening bias. Score: 60-65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Uncertainty is a major theme. Greenspan: "something different is going on in the economy...it is increasingly difficult to argue that we are dealing only with statistical noise." McDonough: "we know that for the last two years or so the forecasting models have not been working well." Rivlin: "We have not made a lot of progress toward solving the fundamental puzzles." Boehne: "our preconceived notions" diverge from "the real world." Kelley: "healthy humility...our lack of understanding." The puzzle of low inflation + low unemployment is front and center and genuinely unresolved. Direction: elevated uncertainty. Score: 50-55.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says currently low but risks up. Score ~42 (slightly below midpoint). Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score ~56. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Score ~70. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score ~32. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with tightening bias. Score ~57. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says tightening likely soon. Score ~62. Matches.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor tightness exceeds output gap. This divergence is explicitly discussed by Meyer -- capacity utilization is near natural rate while unemployment is well below NAIRU. The transcript supports this divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance is slightly more hawkish than current lean, consistent with "hold now, tighten soon." Coherent.

### Speaker Count by Direction
- Favor holding but expect near-term tightening (hawkish lean): Guynn, Stern, Meyer, Parry, Hoenig, Moskow, Broaddus, Minehan, Jordan, Greenspan (~10)
- Favor holding, more patient/wait-and-see: Boehne, McTeer, Rivlin, McDonough, Kelley (~5)
- All support "B" asymmetric -- no one favors easing or symmetric.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "Producer prices have fallen for five straight months; the last time that happened was in 1952. Consumer prices are rising at a slower pace this year than last."
- MR. MCTEER: "Neither is there a buildup of inflation in the pipeline--commodity prices, oil, gold, steers, wheat, whatever."
- MR. GUYNN: "our own forecast and most other forecasts show a gradual upward drift in inflation at the end of 1997 into 1998 and even into 1999."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the inflation risks certainly remain on the upside, although there is no urgency for a particularly aggressive move as yet."

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation readings are remarkably benign and below previous forecasts, but virtually all participants acknowledge the risk of upward drift in inflation given the tight labor market. The score sits slightly below the midpoint, reflecting good current data but recognized upside risks.

**Minority views**: Meyer and Stern are most concerned about future inflation acceleration; McTeer is most optimistic that something structural has changed. This informs the +/-6 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "I think the economy is operating beyond its potential."
- MR. MEYER: "in the absence of further policy changes I expect above-trend growth over coming quarters, a further decline in the unemployment rate."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "employers are digging into the bottom of the barrel for workers...The level of economic activity is getting to a point where something has to give."
- MR. MEYER (divergence point): "the prevailing capacity utilization rate is only a shade above its estimated natural rate" -- labor tight but product markets less so.

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential, driven primarily by labor market tightness. Q2 slowdown is seen as temporary with rebound expected. The capacity utilization vs. unemployment divergence prevents a higher score.

**Minority views**: Some like McTeer question whether traditional potential estimates apply; Meyer's capacity utilization point tempers the above-potential assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. STERN: "the degree of tightness in our labor market cannot be overstated. There were widespread reports of great difficulty in finding workers."
- MR. BOEHNE: "The unemployment rate is as low as it has been in 24 years."
- MR. JORDAN: "there are no unemployed construction workers. Resources in that industry are so tight that many people cannot get bids on construction contracts."
- MR. MCTEER: "it is becoming increasingly difficult to find any pockets of weakness to report about."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are very tight, with unemployment at 4.8% (well below estimated NAIRU of ~5.4%), widespread anecdotes of worker shortages, signing bonuses, retention bonuses, and rising wage settlements. However, wage acceleration has been surprisingly muted, which prevents scoring at the very highest levels.

**Minority views**: Jordan notes some smaller communities still have high unemployment (9-10%). But the overwhelming weight of evidence is for very tight conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MEYER: "the fundamentals supporting aggregate demand, in my judgment, remain very positive: there is solid momentum in income and employment; financial conditions are generally favorable, and measures of consumer confidence have soared to new highs."
- MR. GUYNN: "tourism and hospitality industries...continue their solid growth. Near-term bookings...are now reported to be up between 5 and 20 percent."
- MR. MCTEER: "The energy industry and all the industries that service it are running at full capacity."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the demand elements in the economy are essentially quite strong."

**Center-of-gravity**: Q2 showed a temporary slowdown (payback for robust Q1), but underlying demand fundamentals are strong. Business investment is booming especially in high-tech. Consumer confidence at new highs. Rebound expected in H2. Net position: above trend.

**Minority views**: Jordan notes retail spending "definitely softer." Some Q2 weakness in motor vehicles. The temporary pause moderates the score.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: "we have the reality of booming capital markets, enormous liquidity, and incipient real estate booms."
- MR. JORDAN: "Bankers are telling us that earnings are good...very aggressive bidding for real estate deals...speculative residential and commercial projects are getting funded."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "our policy is in fact rather tight; it certainly is not loose." (But this refers to the policy rate, not conditions.)
- MR. MCTEER: "The yield curve is lower and flatter, the dollar remains strong, and the bond vigilantes have eased long-term rates recently."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are generally accommodative despite a moderate policy rate. Capital markets are booming, credit is freely available, banks are lending aggressively, consumer confidence is at record highs. Some real estate speculation is emerging. This maps to somewhat loose conditions.

**Minority views**: McDonough argues the real fed funds rate is "rather tight," but this is about policy stance, not market conditions. The wider band reflects some ambiguity between policy rates and market conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I believe the wisest thing we can do today is to adopt 'B' asymmetric and continue to monitor the outlook." Also: "I do not think we have the luxury of relaxing our vigilance."
- MR. MEYER: "I believe that we should plan to raise the funds rate by 50 basis points over the remainder of the year in two 25 basis point increments."
- MR. BROADDUS: "If it were up to me, I would still prefer to take out some insurance now."
- MR. BOEHNE: "I feel comfortable with the wait-and-see approach...I support 'B' asymmetric."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for holding steady with an asymmetric (tightening bias) directive. Several participants explicitly expect or advocate tightening in the near future. No one favors easing. The lean is clearly toward tightening but with patience for now. This is a lean-hawkish hold, not a strong tightening signal.

**Minority views**: Broaddus and Meyer favor more immediate action; McTeer and Rivlin are more patient. The band is narrow because all agree on the current decision and direction.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- All participants support "B" asymmetric -- zero dissents.
- MR. BROADDUS: "I can support your recommendation, Mr. Chairman, but I would definitely want an asymmetric directive." (Mild preference for action but acquiesces.)
- MR. MEYER lays out a longer-term tightening plan but agrees with today's hold.
- Disagreements are about timing and analytical framework, not direction.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the policy decision with minor differences in urgency. The debate is about "when" not "whether" to tighten. Some disagreement exists on the analytical interpretation of the economy (productivity vs. traditional Phillips curve) but this does not translate into policy disagreement at this meeting.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. MINEHAN: Extended discussion of asset bubbles, boom/bust scenarios, comparison to late 1980s and Japan: "People begin to believe that the prices of their assets can only go up. Banks begin lending for any project, viable or not."
- MR. JORDAN: "growing signs of speculative activity in farm land, residential real estate, and commercial real estate...these symptoms of speculative excesses...may be the first early warning signals."
- MS. RIVLIN: "The stock market is being described even more emphatically and for good reasons as seriously overvalued."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: Raises financial stability vs. price stability as a conceptual question: "Is price stability really what we are after or are we after financial stability?"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives meaningful attention from several participants (Minehan, Jordan, Rivlin, Greenspan), with discussions of stock overvaluation, real estate speculation, and bank lending standards. However, it is not the primary focus of the meeting -- economic conditions and price measurement take more time. Moderate emphasis.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I suspect that such a case [for tightening] will emerge by the time of the August meeting." Also: "if we begin to see signs of a pickup in demand that threatens to exceed the available supply of workers, I think we will have no choice but to start to move."
- MR. MEYER: "we should plan to raise the funds rate by 50 basis points over the remainder of the year."
- MR. GUYNN: "we probably will need to adjust policy further sometime soon."
- MR. PARRY: "a clear implication of both our forecast and that of the Greenbook is that we will have to tighten policy soon."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is a mild tightening bias. Multiple participants explicitly expect tightening in the near term, and the asymmetric directive codifies this. Greenspan suggests August could be the meeting for action. But there is data-dependence built in -- not a firm commitment.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "something different is going on in the economy...we are observing something that is different from what we have experienced over the years, and it is increasingly difficult to argue that we are dealing only with statistical noise."
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "we know that for the last two years or so the forecasting models have not been working well, witness how bad our forecasts have been."
- MS. RIVLIN: "We have not made a lot of progress toward solving the fundamental puzzles."
- MR. KELLEY: "healthy humility is a virtue in the eyes of the Lord, and I think you have given voice to a certain level of humility that we all feel in the light of our lack of understanding."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated. The core puzzle -- why inflation remains low with such tight labor markets -- is genuinely unresolved. Models are not working. But participants are not paralyzed; they have a framework (watch, wait, be ready to act). The uncertainty is about the economic structure, not about imminent catastrophic risk. This is meaningfully above normal hedging but not "uncharted territory."
