# FOMC Transcript Scoring — July 31–August 1, 2012

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type

This is a **transcript** of individual participant statements from the policy go-round at the July 31–August 1, 2012 FOMC meeting. Speakers are identified by name throughout. The document is well-segmented — the policy go-round is clearly delineated. There is also a subsequent discussion of an experimental consensus forecast, which contains additional participant views on the economic outlook. The discussion is lengthy and substantive, with roughly 15–16 named participants providing detailed statements.

### 2. Era Context

August 2012. The US economy is approximately three years into the recovery from the Great Financial Crisis. The unemployment rate is ~8.2 percent — well above any reasonable estimate of NAIRU. GDP growth is sluggish (Q2 2012 advance estimate came in around 1.5–1.7 percent annualized). The Committee had just extended the Maturity Extension Program (MEP/Operation Twist) at the June meeting. The European sovereign debt crisis is a live downside risk. The fiscal cliff looms. The Fed is actively debating whether and how to launch QE3 (asset purchases). The ZLB is binding. Inflation is running close to but modestly below the 2 percent target.

### 3. Directional Assessment for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Participants broadly see inflation as running at or very slightly below the 2 percent target. Fisher: "the trimmed mean has been remarkably steady in the neighborhood just shy of 2 percent... the 12-month rate... currently running at 1.9 percent." Lockhart: "I expect... little inflationary pressure." Williams: "inflation remains below our 2 percent target." Plosser and Bullard note inflation is near target and not a near-term concern. Kocherlakota is an outlier, arguing inflation will rise to 2.5 percent by mid-decade due to supply shocks. The center of gravity is inflation at or just below target — neither deflationary alarm nor meaningful overshoot. Score: modestly below 50 but close — approximately 40–44.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
Strong consensus: the economy is below potential. Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable and does not close for the next two years." Powell: "there's a material output gap... I don't know whether it's 4 percent or 2 percent, but it seems highly likely to me that it is material." Tarullo: economy is "stuck." Kocherlakota is an outlier arguing the gap is smaller, equivalent to early 2004. Chairman Bernanke references "a big output gap." The majority view places the economy clearly below potential. Score: 35–40. Apply bright-line rule — "below potential" language → score MUST be < 50. Score: 37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Unemployment at ~8.2 percent. Multiple participants cite "missing on the employment side of the mandate," "unemployment gap remains sizable," "significant slack." Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable." Yellen: "almost no meaningful progress in lowering unemployment over the next few years." Rosengren: forecast "misses on both elements of the mandate." Kocherlakota argues there's less slack than the consensus, citing capacity utilization and average weekly hours equivalent to early 2004. But the overwhelming majority see significant slack in the labor market. Score: 30–35. Anchors confirm: 35–40 is the 2011–14 range; unemployment at 8.2% puts this at the lower end, around 32–35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Incoming data described as "weak" across the board. Lockhart: "despite the weak incoming data." Plosser: economy not materially worse but growth modest at ~3 percent projected for 2013–14. Fisher: economy is "cruddy." Duke: "the economy is cruddy." Yellen: "moderate economic growth over coming quarters." No participant describes spending/investment as strong. The prevailing tone is sluggish, below-trend conditions. Score: 35–40, toward the lower part of the "below trend" range given multiple references to weak data and subdued pace.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Financial conditions are described as generally accommodative — flight-to-safety flows have pushed Treasury yields down significantly. Bullard: "rates are already low. We're benefiting from an extreme flight-to-safety effect globally." Plosser: "save-haven flows have done far more to lower long-term rates than we could ever hope to do with any likely LSAP." Yellen: "safe-haven flows into Treasuries would likely push yields down a lot." Corporate credit is available cheaply (Fisher cites Texas Instruments issuing at 0.45% for 3-year). However, some discussion of potential tightening from European turmoil widening risk spreads. The net picture is loose financial conditions, not tight. Score: 30–35 (loose/accommodative).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
This is the most important dimension. The overwhelming majority supports alternative B (hold, no new LSAP at this meeting) but with a strong dovish forward lean — most expect/prefer action at the September meeting. Pro-action participants (Williams, Evans, Kocherlakota, Rosengren, Tarullo, Yellen) strongly favor alternative A (new LSAP) or are pushing for September action. Alternative B supporters (Lockhart, Bullard, Plosser, Pianalto, Fisher, Duke, Stein, Powell, Bernanke himself) favor waiting but most with a dovish lean. Only Plosser and Lacker express fundamental skepticism about further easing. Fisher is skeptical but not formally opposed. The anchor for 2010–12 QE era is 15–25 — "majority favors easing or maintaining max accommodation." This meeting's center of gravity is: QE3 is coming, just a question of when. That is a strong dovish signal. Score: 18–22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**
There is a clear divide: at least 4–5 participants (Williams, Evans, Rosengren, Tarullo, Kocherlakota) would have preferred to act at this meeting (alternative A), while several others support alternative B with reservations in both directions. The formal vote will not show a dissent (everyone is accepting B), but the expressed views reveal a significant split — a camp eager for immediate action vs. a camp wanting to wait vs. a skeptical minority (Plosser, Lacker, Fisher). Lacker expresses opposition even to B's forward guidance language. This qualifies as notable-to-significant disagreement with distinct camps. Score: 50–58. There are no formal dissents recorded but the depth of disagreement extends to fundamental economic assessment (supply vs. demand), efficacy of LSAPs, and the wisdom of forward guidance. I'll score 52.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Multiple participants discuss financial market conditions: market functioning for LSAPs (Rosengren, Dudley, Duke, Stein, Powell), European financial market stress (Fisher, Yellen, George, Bullard), IOER effects on money markets (Duke), and corporate balance sheets (Stein). However, financial stability is not the dominant topic — it is a supporting consideration rather than the meeting's focus. The focus is on economic weakness and QE3 design. Score: 35–42. Several participants address it meaningfully but it does not rival the economic/policy debate.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
The Committee settles on keeping "late 2014" forward guidance language unchanged — no shift to mid-2015. Most participants frame the path as: hold at this meeting, act in September (LSAP + potentially extend guidance to mid-2015 at that point). The current statement retains the dovish "at least through late 2014" language and adds new "will provide additional accommodation as needed" language. The forward signal is clearly dovish — the Committee is telegraphing that more easing (LSAP) is coming unless significant improvement materializes. This is a strong easing bias. Score: 8–12. Anchor: 3–8 is Evans Rule/threshold-based commitment; 8–15 is strong easing bias. This falls in 10–14 range — strong easing bias, signaling further accommodation.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Considerable uncertainty expressed throughout. Lacker devotes substantial time to: "a staggering amount of uncertainty... the 90 percent confidence band for real GDP growth in 2014 goes from 0 to 6 percent." Plosser: "uncharted territory." Duke: "uncharted waters... need to constantly reassess." Powell: "substantial uncertainty" about efficacy. Multiple participants note uncertainty about the output gap, LSAP efficacy, European developments, and the fiscal cliff. This is genuine and repeated uncertainty emphasis, not mere rhetorical hedging. Score: 58–65. Multiple participants emphasize unusual/elevated uncertainty. Anchor: 55–65 is China slowdown/elevated; 40–50 is Brexit/trade tensions. Given the number of participants citing fundamental uncertainty about models, policy efficacy, and external shocks, this falls in the 60–65 range.

### 4. Polarity Verification

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score 37 < 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack, high unemployment. Score 33 < 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish (easing expected/favored). Score 20 < 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says easing bias, further accommodation signaled. Score 12 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score 32 < 50. Correct.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says near but slightly below target. Score 42 — slightly below 50. Consistent.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence

- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, consistent with recovery-in-progress but well short of potential. Coherent. The slight gap (37 vs 33) reflects that the output gap debate is more nuanced than the clear unemployment slack.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both are dovish, consistent. The forward guidance score is slightly more extreme because it captures the signaled commitment to keep rates low through at least late 2014 and implied QE3 incoming — a strong forward easing signal.

### 6. Speaker Count for Key Dimensions

**Favor alternative A (immediate action/LSAP):** Williams, Evans, Rosengren, Tarullo — 4 participants
**Support alternative B now, strong lean toward A in September:** Yellen, Duke, Kocherlakota — 3 participants
**Support alternative B with moderate lean toward A:** Lockhart, Bullard, Powell, Vice Chairman Dudley — 4 participants
**Support alternative B with skepticism toward further action:** Plosser, Lacker, Fisher, Pianalto, George — 5 participants
**Chairman Bernanke:** recommends alternative B with forward lean

The center of gravity is clearly on the dovish side — the majority expects/favors near-term easing.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Fisher: "the trimmed mean has been remarkably steady in the neighborhood just shy of 2 percent... the 12-month rate has flattened out somewhat toward the end of 2011, but it's currently running at 1.9 percent."
- Mr. Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable and does not close for the next two years. And all the while, inflation remains below our 2 percent target."
- Mr. Bullard: "inflation and inflation expectations are nothing like they were in the fall of 2010... as far as I can read the inflation and inflation expectations data, they are really pretty close to our target."

**Center of gravity:** Participants broadly see inflation as running at or just under the 2 percent target — not a deflation threat but not at or above target either. Near-consensus on this assessment.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota is an outlier arguing inflation will rise above 2.5% by mid-decade under accommodative policy (upside risk view). Pushes uncertainty band modestly wider upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Williams: "the unemployment gap remains sizable and does not close for the next two years... Simply put, such an outcome is inconsistent with appropriate monetary policy."
- Mr. Powell: "there's a material output gap. I don't know whether it's 4 percent or 2 percent, but it seems highly likely to me that it is material and that we're not making progress toward closing it."
- Mr. Tarullo: "it is much more a matter of the 'stuckness' or the bogged-down quality of the economy... we're at just about the same level of GDP growth."

**Center of gravity:** Overwhelming majority views the economy as clearly below potential, with a sizeable output gap. The language is "slack remains," "gap not closing," "stuck in the mud." Consistent with 35–40 range.

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota argues slack is roughly equivalent to early 2004 (a smaller gap). Pushes uncertainty band slightly upward.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Rosengren: "with a forecast that misses on both elements of the mandate, there should be a strong presumption that we implement alternative A at the next meeting."
- Ms. Yellen: "I anticipate moderate economic growth over coming quarters and no meaningful improvement in the labor market."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "we are missing badly on only one side of our dual mandate" (employment).

**Center of gravity:** Unemployment at 8.2%. Participants consistently describe significant labor market slack, missing on the employment mandate. Score squarely in the 31–40 range for "below full employment, slack remains."

**Minority views:** Kocherlakota argues utilization measures suggest slack equivalent to early 2004 — less slack than consensus. Small upward pressure on uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 37 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Ms. Duke: "I term it as 'cruddy.'"
- Mr. Plosser: "the BEA's advance estimate for 2012 quarter-two real GDP came in a little stronger than what was projected in the July Tealbook... the economy does not look particularly worse."
- Mr. Lockhart: "despite the weak incoming data, my outlook has not worsened materially since the last meeting. I continue to expect some strengthening in economic growth over coming quarters."

**Center of gravity:** Weak incoming data described across the board; the economy is "cruddy" but not collapsing. Most expect modest improvement. Score in the "below trend, sluggish" range.

**Minority views:** Plosser is more optimistic, maintaining a 3% forecast for 2013–14. This pulls slightly upward.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 32 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Bullard: "rates are already low. We're benefiting from an extreme flight-to-safety effect globally."
- Mr. Plosser: "save-haven flows have done far more to lower long-term rates than we could ever hope to do with any likely LSAP."
- Mr. Fisher (citing TI issuance): "three-years going at 0.45 percent and the seven-years at 1.65 percent... the cheapest corporate security ever issued in history."

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Safe-haven flows suppressing Treasury yields; corporate borrowing costs at historic lows. Some European risk-spread concern but not dominating domestic conditions.

**Minority views:** Yellen notes that under a European shock, "overall financial conditions would be markedly tighter due to the widening of risk spreads" — flagging a tail risk but not current conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 20 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Williams: "I strongly favor alternative A... The news since then confirms that progress toward our policy goals will likely remain stalled unless we take further, more-significant action."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "my default would be to move in September along the lines of alternative A unless we see some meaningful improvement in the economic outlook."
- Ms. Yellen: "barring such improvements, I think we should definitely take action along the lines of alternative A... I would also strongly endorse the launching of an open-ended LSAP program at the September meeting."

**Center of gravity:** The dominant stance is dovish — even those supporting alternative B do so as a tactical delay, not a rejection of easing. The majority anticipates/favors QE3 at the September meeting. This is squarely in the QE-era dovish range (15–25).

**Minority views:** Plosser and Lacker are skeptical of further easing. Lacker opposes even the forward guidance language. Fisher expresses the costs of further balance sheet expansion. These hawkish outliers push the uncertainty band upward slightly.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 52 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Distinct camps are visible: Williams/Evans/Rosengren/Tarullo/Kocherlakota explicitly prefer alternative A (act now); Yellen/Duke/Raskin support B but lean strongly toward A in September; Lacker/Plosser are skeptical even of B's forward guidance.
- Mr. Lacker: "I'd oppose the inclusion of forward-guidance language in alternative B at this meeting."
- Mr. Kocherlakota: disagrees with the consensus forecast on normative grounds regarding the balanced approach, while also arguing there's less slack than most.

**Center of gravity:** No formal dissents recorded, but the meeting shows significant substantive disagreement — about economic assessment (supply vs. demand shocks), LSAP efficacy, forward guidance design, and urgency of action. Distinct camps with different prescriptions qualify as "notable disagreement" (46–60).

**Minority views:** The divergence between Kocherlakota (more accommodation needed for credibility reasons) and Plosser/Lacker (skeptics of further action) represents nearly irreconcilable frameworks, but both sides ultimately accept B, keeping formal dissent at zero.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Multiple participants discuss LSAP market functioning risks (Duke on MBS capacity, Stein on Treasury spread detachment, Pianalto on LSAP capacity memo).
- Ms. Duke: "I worry that many risks to market functioning identified in the memo from Board and Desk staff on LSAP capacity may not be as manageable now as in earlier LSAPs."
- Mr. Stein: "buying $1 trillion of long-term bonds at a time when term premiums are at historically negative levels represents a significant expected cost to taxpayers. It also removes what are apparently, according to the market, highly valued safe assets from the system."

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability and market functioning are discussed meaningfully by several participants, but primarily as considerations in the LSAP design discussion rather than as stand-alone topics. European financial stress is mentioned as a downside risk but not analyzed in depth. Score falls in "limited to moderate discussion" range.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 11 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- The Committee retains "at least through late 2014" forward guidance and adds language signaling willingness to provide "additional accommodation as needed."
- Vice Chairman Dudley: "my default would be to move in September along the lines of alternative A unless we see some meaningful improvement in the economic outlook."
- Ms. Yellen: "barring such improvements, I think we should definitely take action along the lines of alternative A" — framing September LSAP as near-certain absent improvement.

**Center of gravity:** The forward signal is unambiguously dovish — "at least through late 2014" at the ZLB, plus a strong conditional commitment to QE3 in September. This falls in the 8–15 range for "strong easing bias / signal further accommodation ahead." The meeting explicitly sets up September as the likely launch of QE3.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 63 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Mr. Lacker: "a staggering amount of uncertainty associated with point forecasts of the economy... the 90 percent confidence band for real GDP growth in 2014 goes from 0 to 6 percent... The uncertainty about future policy-settings is staggering as well."
- Ms. Duke: "We are in territory, as others have said, where we have never been before, and I would like to better understand the risks and costs that we could be facing."
- Mr. Powell: "I hold that view with substantial uncertainty" (re: efficacy of current channels).

**Center of gravity:** Many participants explicitly emphasize elevated uncertainty — about the output gap, LSAP efficacy, European contagion, fiscal cliff, and the appropriate policy path. This is genuine uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging. Score in the 61–75 range for "high: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range."
