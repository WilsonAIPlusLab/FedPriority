# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-08-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a go-around of regional/national economic assessments followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include: Hoenig, Broaddus, Moskow, Parry, Minehan, Boehne, Guynn, McTeer, Melzer, Stern, McDonough, Jordan, Kelley, Meyer, Phillips, Lindsey, Yellen, Rivlin, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-populated meeting with extensive discussion.

### Era Context
August 1996: Mid-expansion, sixth year of recovery from 1990-91 recession. Unemployment around 5.4%, economy growing above trend in first half, with debate about whether a slowdown was materializing. Inflation by CPI around 2.5-3%, with core measures lower. The fed funds rate was at 5.25% after three 25bp cuts in late 1995/early 1996. Key tension: strong economy with tight labor markets but benign inflation readings.

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Direction: Participants see inflation as slightly below to modestly above target, with risks to the upside. The center of gravity is nuanced. Most participants acknowledge current inflation is remarkably well-contained for this stage of expansion (Phillips: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued"; Meyer: "broad measures of price inflation are still declining"; Yellen: "inflation has been declining, not increasing"). However, many express concern about ECI wage data and the potential for future acceleration. Broaddus, Parry, Melzer see clear upward pressure. Guynn, McTeer, Meyer, Yellen, Rivlin see current inflation as benign. The center of gravity: current inflation is contained but risks are on the upside. This is a "slightly above neutral" reading -- inflation is not yet a problem but concern is mounting. Score around 52-55. Higher = more pressure perceived. Evidence supports modest concern, not alarm. Score: 53.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Direction: Participants see the economy as operating above potential. Broaddus: "the level of activity nationally is really quite high" and "above trend." Parry: "levels of labor and capacity utilization that are inconsistent with steady inflation." Moskow: "aggregate demand will exceed potential output." Rivlin: "economy clearly was growing above its potential." Greenspan: "premature to assume that the economy is backing off its unsustainable rate of expansion." However, there is widespread discussion that growth is moderating toward potential. Kelley notes capacity utilization declining slowly to 82%. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language clearly present. Score must be > 50. Most say "above potential but moderating." Score: 55-58. Score: 56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Direction: Near-universal agreement that labor markets are tight. Hoenig: "labor markets continue to be tight." Broaddus: "especially apparent in the tightness of labor markets." Moskow: "labor markets remain tight." Minehan: "unemployment rates in the region are low--a full percentage point below the nation." Melzer: "tight labor markets continue to be the rule." Stern: "labor markets continue to be tight and if anything they have tightened a bit further." Greenspan: "there exists a tautness in labor markets." Unemployment at 5.4% with discussion of whether this is at or below NAIRU. Clear consensus on tightness. Score: 58-62. Score: 60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Direction: Mixed but modestly above trend. Consumer spending has softened from strong pace (Moskow: "growth in consumer spending slowed considerably in July"; Boehne: "retail sales growth, quite healthy in the spring, has eased"). Business investment growth is positive but slowing from 1994-95 pace (Phillips: "growth in business investment is down from 1994 and 1995, but this sector is still a contributor"). Manufacturing strong in many districts. Auto sales softer but still solid. Overall the picture is above-trend activity that is moderating. Score: 52-55. Score: 53.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Direction: Conditions described as generally supportive of growth. Phillips: "Financial markets generally continue to support growth. With the decline in long-term rates, the yield curve has come into a flatter and more normal alignment." Phillips notes "banks generally appear to be providing ample credit." Minehan notes "buoyant equity markets." Jordan and Melzer note some loan growth slowing and credit quality deterioration. Stern discusses easy credit card availability. Long-term rates had declined recently. The picture is neutral to somewhat loose. Score: 35-40. Score: 38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Direction: The committee voted to hold at 5.25% with an asymmetric (tightening bias) directive. Counting views in the policy discussion:
- Hold (B asymmetric): Greenspan, Lindsey, McDonough, Boehne, Jordan, Guynn, Kelley, McTeer, Phillips, Yellen, Meyer, Moskow, Rivlin = ~13
- Tighten 25bp: Hoenig, Minehan, Parry, Stern = 4
- Tighten 50bp preferred (would accept 25bp): Broaddus, Melzer = 2
Many of those voting to hold expressed hawkish reservations (Moskow: "concerned about labor cost pressures"; Phillips: "my suspicion is that at some point we are going to need to tighten"; Yellen: "I continue to consider the inflationary risks biased to the up side"). The asymmetric directive is itself a hawkish signal. This is a "hold with tightening bias" meeting where a significant minority wanted to act now. Score: 55-58. Score: 56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Direction: Notable disagreement. Six participants (Broaddus, Stern, Parry, Hoenig, Minehan, Melzer) explicitly favored tightening -- a substantial minority. But no formal dissents recorded in this transcript excerpt (it ends before the formal vote count). The holders themselves were split between those relatively comfortable (Rivlin, Guynn, Meyer) and those holding reluctantly (McDonough, Phillips, Moskow). Score: 45-50. Score: 47.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Direction: Limited discussion. Jordan mentions speculative excesses concern in high-tech stocks had "pretty much vanished" after Nasdaq correction. Phillips notes equity market correction partially reversed. Yellen flags stock market as risk. Jordan and Lindsey discuss credit card/small business credit quality. Stern discusses credit card use. These are scattered mentions, not a dominant topic. Score: 20-25. Score: 22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Direction: Asymmetric directive toward tightening. Greenspan's own view: "I believe we can prudently hold off for a while" but "risks are and remain unquestionably on the high side." Phillips: "my suspicion is at some point we are going to need to tighten." Moskow: "waiting makes the September and November meetings very crucial decision points." Yellen outlines conditions under which she'd support tightening. The forward signal is a mild tightening bias -- not committing to tighten but clearly leaning that way. Score: 58-62. Score: 60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Direction: Elevated uncertainty. Phillips: "we have more data but not more information." Yellen: "the fog of uncertainty hovering above the forecast might lift... Unfortunately... the body of incoming data has provided only modest illumination." Meyer: long discussion of whether NAIRU has shifted, making the inflation outlook uncertain. Greenspan: "current forecast error ranges easily encompass either scenario." Kelley: extended discussion of whether inflation will emerge or whether "things have changed." Multiple participants express genuine uncertainty about whether the economy has structurally changed. Score: 48-55. Score: 52.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, coherent. Labor slightly higher because labor tightness is the most universally noted feature.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Both lean hawkish but moderate. Forward guidance slightly higher because the asymmetric directive and "when not whether" framing suggest future action is more likely than current. Coherent.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modest concern, upside risks. 53 = slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential but moderating. 56 = modestly above. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions supportive, credit available. 38 = somewhat loose. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with asymmetric tightening bias. 56 = lean hawkish. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Broaddus: "the pressure on resources is still likely to push the trend rate of inflation up a bit, perhaps to above 3 percent over the projection period"
- Meyer: "broad measures of price inflation are still declining... The inflation rate in each case is about 2 percent over the past year"
- Phillips: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued for this stage of an expansion... the risks remain on the up side"
- Boehne: "price pressures remain remarkably subdued... upward wage pressures will not likely bubble up into upward price pressures as much as one might think"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Current inflation readings are benign and broad measures still declining, but the majority of participants perceive upside risks from tight labor markets and rising ECI, placing the assessment slightly above neutral.

**Minority views:** Hawks (Broaddus, Parry, Melzer) see inflation already rising and forecast acceleration, pushing the upper band. Doves (Guynn, Meyer, Rivlin) see contained inflation with possible structural change, pulling the lower band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Broaddus: "the level of activity nationally is really quite high... labor markets are likely to tighten a bit further"
- Moskow: "aggregate demand will exceed potential output"
- Rivlin: "economy clearly was growing above its potential"
- Greenspan: "premature to assume that the economy is backing off its unsustainable rate of expansion"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-universal agreement that the economy is operating modestly above potential, though growth is expected to moderate toward trend, keeping the score only moderately above 50.

**Minority views:** Few dissent from the "above potential" assessment. Kelley notes capacity utilization at only 82% (declining), but unemployment is tight. Narrow band reflects consensus.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Melzer: "Tight labor markets continue to be the rule... District unemployment rate was less than 5 percent... the lowest since the 1970s"
- Minehan: "pressures in the job markets are growing as employers find it hard to hire skilled and in some cases even clerical workers. Wage increases are picking up, with increases of at least 3 percent the norm"
- Stern: "Labor markets continue to be tight and if anything they have tightened a bit further"
- Greenspan: "there exists a tautness in labor markets that has been there all year long"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Essentially unanimous agreement on tight labor markets with unemployment around 5.4% and widespread reports of difficulty finding workers, but not yet at "severe shortage" levels.

**Minority views:** Very narrow range of views; most agree on tight markets. Jordan notes "little talk of wage pressures in spite of continuous reports of tight labor markets."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 53 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Moskow: "Growth in consumer spending slowed considerably in July... light vehicle production reached an expansion high in July"
- Boehne: "Retail sales growth, quite healthy in the spring, has eased during the summer months"
- Phillips: "Growth in business investment is down from 1994 and 1995, but this sector of the economy is still a contributor to the expansion"
- Minehan: "risks that GDP growth will be stronger than is reflected in the Greenbook in light of... recent declines in long-term interest rates, manageable levels of business debt... buoyant equity markets"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Spending and investment remain above trend but are moderating from a very strong first half; the pace remains solid and supportive of expansion.

**Minority views:** Some see more softening ahead (Boehne), while others flag upside risks from inventories and financial conditions (Minehan, Yellen).

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Phillips: "Financial markets generally continue to support growth. With the decline in long-term rates, the yield curve has come into a flatter and more normal alignment... Banks generally appear to be providing ample credit"
- Minehan: "recent declines in long-term interest rates... buoyant equity markets"
- Melzer: "Loan growth at District banks has decelerated... the slowdown may be partly due to a tightening of lending standards"
- Stern: credit card teaser rates "available without any effort"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are generally supportive of growth with declining long-term rates, ample credit, and strong equity markets, though some bank lending standards have tightened modestly.

**Minority views:** Jordan reports some credit quality deterioration and bank lending softening, which nudges the score slightly toward neutral but does not override the broadly accommodative picture.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "if the economy is in the process of growing at an unsustainable pace... raising the federal funds rate only 25 basis points is not going to help much... I believe that we can prudently hold off for a while... I trust we will continue our asymmetric bias"
- Broaddus: "I still favor some tightening of monetary policy... a 50 basis point move today would essentially drop both shoes"
- Yellen: "I continue to consider the inflationary risks biased to the up side... I support your recommendation [to hold]"
- Rivlin: "I still believe it is possible that we will not have to move... The general view seems to be that it is a question of when rather than whether"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee chose to hold steady with a tightening bias directive; a significant minority (6 of ~19) favored immediate tightening, and most holders acknowledged tightening was likely needed soon.

**Minority views:** Broaddus and Melzer favored 50bp; Parry, Hoenig, Stern, Minehan favored 25bp. Rivlin and Guynn were most comfortable holding.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 47 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Six participants explicitly favored tightening (Broaddus 50bp, Melzer 50bp, Parry 25bp, Hoenig 25bp, Stern 25bp, Minehan 25bp)
- McDonough held but said: "When we move... the move should be 50 basis points"
- Moskow held but expressed concern: "waiting makes the September and November meetings very crucial decision points"
- Phillips held but noted: "my suspicion is that at some point we are going to need to tighten"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Notable disagreement with roughly one-third of participants favoring immediate tightening and many holders expressing reluctance, though no formal dissents are recorded in this excerpt.

**Minority views:** The hawkish camp was vocal and substantive, advocating for 25-50bp increases with clear reasoning, creating meaningful tension.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Jordan: "concern about speculative excesses, especially in the high-tech sector. But with the sharp overall adjustment in the prices of high-tech stocks on the Nasdaq earlier this summer, that concern has pretty much vanished"
- Yellen: "Present market valuations appear to be based on earnings expectations that are highly optimistic... setting the stage for a very significant correction"
- Stern/Lindsey: discussion of credit card debt and small business financing risks

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability received scattered mentions from a few participants -- equity valuations, credit quality, credit card use -- but was not a primary focus of the discussion.

**Minority views:** None elevated this to a major concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan: "risks are and remain unquestionably on the high side... it would not take much to put us back in a tightening situation"
- Phillips: "my suspicion is that at some point we are going to need to tighten"
- Rivlin: "The general view seems to be that it is a question of when rather than whether"
- Yellen: "I would certainly support a tightening of policy" if slack declines or inflation measures rise

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The asymmetric directive and widespread "when not whether" language on tightening point to a mild hawkish forward signal, though conditioned on incoming data.

**Minority views:** Rivlin suggests it is "possible that we will not have to move." Guynn sees potential to "sustain a pace of moderate growth without a pickup in... inflation."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "the fog of uncertainty hovering above the forecast might lift... Unfortunately... the body of incoming data has provided only modest illumination"
- Phillips: "we have more data but not more information"
- Meyer: "The problem we face in acting on this forecast is that the pattern of rising inflation that it projects going forward should have been under way for some time and in fact is not yet evident"
- Greenspan: "current forecast error ranges easily encompass either scenario"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Elevated uncertainty driven by two fundamental unresolved questions -- whether the slowdown would materialize and whether NAIRU had shifted -- with multiple participants explicitly noting the difficulty of interpreting the data.

**Minority views:** Broaddus and Melzer expressed more conviction about inflation risks; Meyer and Yellen emphasized the NAIRU uncertainty most strongly.
