# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-05-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It includes a go-around of regional/national economic reports from Reserve Bank presidents and governors, followed by a policy discussion led by Chairman Greenspan. The document is well-structured and lengthy, with approximately 15+ participants contributing substantive remarks.

### Era Context
Mid-1996: The US economy is in a mature expansion. Unemployment is around 5.4%, near or at estimated NAIRU. GDP growth has been surprisingly strong in Q1 1996. Inflation by core measures has been quiescent but food/energy prices are rising. The Fed cut rates 25bp in January 1996 and is now debating whether to reverse. Long-term rates have backed up ~80bp since January. The stock market (S&P 500) has been rising sharply.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfortable levels but NOT yet in a breakout. The center of gravity is that risks have shifted to the upside. Parry: "upward pressure on inflation...I find this forecast alarming." Moskow: "risks of higher inflation are greater today than at the time of our last meeting." Hoenig: "upside risks of inflation as noticeably higher." BUT Greenspan: "the 'smoking gun' is missing...we just do not find any significant evidence of cumulative pressures." Yellen argues the Greenbook inflation forecast may be "a little too pessimistic" and notes three "ifs" before wage pressures translate to core inflation. McTeer: "inflation is up overall, but its rise has been mainly in measures that include energy and not in core inflation." Boehne: "General price inflation also is still subdued." Multiple participants note food/energy shocks but question pass-through. The center of gravity: inflation risks are rising, core inflation is not yet accelerating but the environment is worrisome. This is above 50 but not dramatically so -- "somewhat elevated" concerns about future inflation, with current core still behaving. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score ~55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as operating "around full employment" (Parry), "at or near potential" (Kelley: "GDP is at or near potential"), "high level of resource utilization" (Parry), "neighborhood of capacity levels" (Broaddus). Greenspan: "the physical side of the economy is as close to balance as one can imagine." Moskow: "growth at or above potential." The economy grew strongly in Q1 (~3.5% in Q2 forecast), well above trend. Multiple references to above-trend growth but with the level near potential. The bright-line rule: "at potential" or "near potential" = 48-52, but many say "at or above" and growth is above trend. Risks are described as to the upside. I'll place this at 52-53 -- at potential with upside pressure. The late-90s boom anchor is 57-63; we're not there yet but approaching. Score ~53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Multiple participants describe tight labor markets. Moskow: "Labor markets remain tight throughout the District...economic growth in parts of the District...is being restrained currently by labor shortages." Minehan: "regional unemployment rate was 4.7 percent." Broaddus: "shortages of skilled labor...especially with respect to skilled construction labor." Hoenig: "business agents for workers...have not seen such strong demand for their workers since the late 1960s." Melzer: "District labor markets remained tight. The average unemployment rate is below 5 percent." National unemployment at 5.4%. BUT wage pressures are NOT broadly accelerating -- Moskow: "Despite the tight labor markets, we still have few reports of mounting wage pressures." Greenspan: job insecurity is suppressing wages. The level is tight -- near or slightly beyond full employment. Anchors: 50 (2016-17), 65-75 (1999-2000). This is tight but not severely so. Score ~57.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: First quarter showed "remarkable strength across the board" (Broaddus). Consumer spending, business investment, housing all strong. Yellen: "I was surprised by the strength of both consumption and investment spending in the first quarter." Phillips: "Housing has remained amazingly strong...Business capital spending has been a major factor." Multiple districts report strong construction, good retail sales, auto production rising. Greenspan notes the wealth effect from the stock market. Expectation is for moderation in H2 but current readings are clearly above trend. Score ~60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term rates up ~80bp since January. But participants do NOT describe conditions as tight. Phillips: "financial markets are continuing to support opportunities for additional investment...stock market is surprisingly strong...Bank credit is still available." Minehan: "the ebullience of the stock market and the continuing strength...suggest that credit and funding are both available and not overly costly." Melzer: "Lenders are still very actively seeking borrowers in every category." Yellen views the rate rise as an "appropriate antidote" but notes it reflects stronger demand, not restrictive conditions per se. McTeer: "Long-term rates are up substantially...we already have had some tightening in a sense." Overall: conditions have tightened somewhat from very accommodative, but are still broadly supportive. Score ~42 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning neutral).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a pivotal meeting. The vote was for no change (alternative B, symmetric). Among the policy discussion speakers:
- For tightening NOW (25bp): Melzer, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Minehan (5 speakers, mostly nonvoters)
- For no change but asymmetric: Moskow, Lindsey (2 speakers)
- For no change, symmetric: Greenspan, McDonough, Boehne, Kelley, Jordan, Stern, McTeer, Yellen, Phillips, Guynn (10 speakers)

The center of gravity clearly holds steady but with a recognized tilt toward tightening in the near future. Greenspan himself says "if I fully believed the outlook in the Greenbook...I would feel that we ought to be tightening policy sooner rather than later" but advocates waiting. McDonough: "watchful waiting." Almost no one is dovish -- the question is whether to tighten now vs. wait for July. This is a lean-hawkish hold. Anchor: 51-60 range for "hold steady with tightening bias." Score ~56.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents (unanimous vote for B symmetric). However, 5 participants explicitly advocated tightening, and 2 more wanted asymmetric. The hawks are quite vocal (Parry wanted 50bp). There's genuine disagreement about timing, not direction. But the vote itself is unanimous. Anchor: 25-35 for one-dissent typical. This has zero dissents but notable expressed reservations from ~7 participants. Score ~38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market valuation is mentioned by several participants. Yellen: "dangerously lofty level of stock prices." Greenspan discusses the wealth effect and notes "the stock market as best I can judge is high." Lindsey discusses OTC bubble concerns, IPOs at 20x sales, "this is the silly season." Jordan relays software industry bubble concerns. Phillips: "stock market is surprisingly strong, perhaps a bit on the rich side." But these are contextual mentions, not the dominant focus of the meeting. Financial stability is not the primary concern -- inflation and real activity are. Anchor for 1993-96: 1-10 typical. This meeting has more attention than typical for the era due to stock market discussion. Score ~20.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: No explicit forward guidance language, but the strong consensus is that tightening may be needed by July. Greenspan: "We probably will have to make a judgment by July." McDonough: "The next meeting will be a new ballgame." Multiple participants signal readiness to tighten if data confirm. Yellen: "If the current Greenbook forecast is accurate...I think we definitely will need to respond." The forward signal leans hawkish -- data-dependent but tilted toward tightening. Score ~58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Multiple participants express genuine uncertainty about the inflation-output linkage. Greenspan: "Something is going on that we do not yet fully understand." Stern: "I am not sure that I know enough with enough confidence right now." Kelley: "I don't have a view at this point concerning what way the water is going to flow." Boehne: "our dilemma really is one of being open-minded to the possibility that there are some structural changes going on versus getting caught up in wishful thinking." Yellen's three "ifs." Jordan: uncertainty about crop conditions. This is somewhat elevated -- a genuine puzzle about structural change in inflation dynamics. Score ~45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both slightly above midpoint, consistent. Labor tightness slightly higher because multiple districts report shortages even while output is only modestly above potential. Makes sense -- labor market is the leading indicator of tightness here.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Both lean hawkish, aligned. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because the committee signals readiness to act at next meeting even while holding today.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (55): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says rising but not yet in core. 55 = "modestly above target" range. Checks out.
- OUTPUT_GAP (53): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says at-to-slightly-above. 53 checks out.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (42): Higher = tighter. Conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. 42 checks out.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "with the economy operating around full employment, it will be difficult to make any progress in getting the inflation rate down. In fact, our structural model suggests that there will be a small amount of upward pressure on inflation"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the 'smoking gun' is missing. We are seeing a very pronounced set of pressures...Every time I get out in the business sector I get reports...that no one can raise prices"
- MS. YELLEN: "I think the Greenbook inflation forecast is a little too pessimistic...the sentence I just uttered contained three 'ifs'"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants perceive rising inflation risks, particularly from food/energy shocks and tight labor, but core inflation has not yet accelerated. The mood is wary, not alarmed.

**Minority views**: Hawks like Parry and Melzer see inflation as more alarming (would push toward 60-65). Yellen and Greenspan see structural reasons inflation may remain contained (would push toward 48-52). The uncertainty band reflects this spread.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. KELLEY: "GDP is at or near potential"
- MR. PARRY: "the economy operating around full employment...at a high level of resource utilization"
- MR. BROADDUS: "an economy that is in a mature stage of a business expansion and growing at a firm pace in the neighborhood of capacity levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating at or very slightly above potential, with above-trend growth in H1 1996 threatening to push it further above.

**Minority views**: Few disagree on the level assessment. Disagreement is about the trajectory (whether growth will slow to trend or continue above).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MOSKOW: "Labor markets remain tight throughout the District...economic growth in parts of the District...is being restrained currently by labor shortages"
- MR. HOENIG: "business agents for workers in our area tell us that they have not seen such strong demand for their workers since the late 1960s"
- MR. MOSKOW: "Despite the tight labor markets, we still have few reports of mounting wage pressures"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are tight with scattered shortages, especially in skilled trades and construction, but wage pressures remain surprisingly subdued. Unemployment at 5.4%, near or at NAIRU.

**Minority views**: Some districts report more acute shortages (Hoenig, Broaddus) while others report less pressure (Boehne).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BROADDUS: "aggregate final demand is considerably stronger currently than one might have anticipated...The first-quarter GDP report was very revealing. It showed remarkable strength across the board"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Housing has remained amazingly strong...Business capital spending has been a major factor in the continuing expansion"
- MS. YELLEN: "I was surprised by the strength of both consumption and investment spending in the first quarter"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are clearly above trend in early 1996, driven by consumer durables, business investment, housing, and the wealth effect from rising equities. Expected to moderate but currently running hot.

**Minority views**: The moderation camp (Greenbook, Yellen) expects deceleration. Greenspan notes the wealth effect could reverse.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "financial markets are continuing to support opportunities for additional investment...stock market is surprisingly strong...Bank credit is still available"
- MR. MELZER: "Lenders are still very actively seeking borrowers in every category"
- MS. MINEHAN: "the ebullience of the stock market and the continuing strength...suggest that credit and funding are both available and not overly costly"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite an 80bp backup in long rates, financial conditions remain broadly supportive. Credit is available, equities are strong, and banks are competing for borrowers. Conditions have tightened from very loose but remain on the accommodative side of neutral.

**Minority views**: McTeer and Broaddus emphasize the rate backup as meaningful tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if I fully believed the outlook in the Greenbook...I would feel that we ought to be tightening policy sooner rather than later...my general view at this stage...is alternative 'B,' that is, to do nothing"
- MR. PARRY: "these two considerations support a 50 basis point increase in the funds rate. However...it would seem prudent to me to go for a 25 basis point increase"
- MS. YELLEN: "My preferred policy at this stage is cautious inaction...If the current Greenbook forecast is accurate, we will soon see clear evidence...When we see that, I think we definitely will need to respond"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous vote to hold, but with a clear lean toward tightening. Five participants explicitly called for a 25bp hike now. Even those favoring no change acknowledge the next move is almost certainly a hike. "Watchful waiting" is the operative phrase.

**Minority views**: Melzer, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Minehan all favored immediate tightening (would score 63-68). No one advocated easing.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Five participants (Melzer, Parry, Broaddus, Hoenig, Minehan) explicitly called for a 25bp hike
- MR. PARRY: advocated 50bp, settled for 25bp recommendation
- MS. MINEHAN: "I am going to continue the revolt of the nonvoters!"
- Vote was unanimous for B symmetric

**Center-of-gravity**: Zero formal dissents, but substantial expressed disagreement on timing. The "revolt of the nonvoters" is notable -- most hawkish voices happened to be nonvoting members. Disagreement is about timing, not direction.

**Minority views**: N/A -- the disagreement IS the story here.

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity**: Stock market valuations receive moderate attention from several participants but are not the central focus. The discussion is primarily about inflation and real activity. Financial stability is a secondary concern, somewhat elevated for the era.

- MS. YELLEN: "If the current Greenbook forecast is accurate, we will soon see clear evidence of an increase in compensation growth...When we see that, I think we definitely will need to respond"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. The committee is signaling readiness to tighten at the next meeting if data confirm. No one signals easing. Data-dependent but with a clear tightening tilt.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Something is going on that we do not yet fully understand...Something different is happening"
- MR. STERN: "I just do not have the sense that I know enough with enough confidence right now to come out strongly on one side or the other"
- MR. BOEHNE: "our dilemma really is one of being open-minded to the possibility that there are some structural changes going on versus getting caught up in wishful thinking"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. The central puzzle -- why tight labor markets have not produced accelerating inflation -- is genuinely unresolved. Multiple participants acknowledge they do not understand the inflation dynamics fully. This is more than normal hedging but less than "uncharted territory."
