# FOMC Transcript Discussion Scoring — May 6, 2003

---

## Part 1: Thinking / Reasoning

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the FOMC meeting on May 6, 2003. Speakers are individually identified (e.g., MR. MOSKOW., MR. PARRY., CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN., MS. MINEHAN., etc.). The document covers both the economic go-around (District reports + national outlook) and the policy discussion, including a lengthy debate about the new "balance of risks" statement language. The text is fully segmented.

### 2. Era Context
May 2003 is the early post-Iraq-war period, following a prolonged soft patch in the economy. The U.S. was in a sluggish recovery from the 2001 recession, with deflation concerns mounting, labor markets weak, and core PCE inflation trending well below 2%. The funds rate was already at 1.25%. The era macro backdrop: output gap is firmly negative, labor market loose, inflation below target and falling, deflation risk being actively debated, and policy heavily accommodative but markets not expecting a further cut on this date.

### 3. Directional Signals Per Dimension (Pre-Score Reasoning)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants widely see inflation as BELOW target and falling. Multiple speakers cite core CPI falling from 2.4% to 1.7% year-over-year (Bernanke), core PCE deflator market component at 0.9% year-over-year (Bernanke), six-month rate of about 0.2%. Greenbook 35% probability of deflation (cited by Parry, Santomero, Broaddus, Gramlich, Bies, Bernanke). Greenspan says "deflation process may be sneaking up on us." Most agree inflation is "dangerously low" (Gramlich) or "verging on unattractive" (Ferguson). Guynn dissents somewhat, saying he is "not yet convinced that deflation is our foremost risk." But even Guynn acknowledges inflation is positive and falling. Direction: well below target, disinflation risk prominent. Score should be in the 15–25 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
All participants see the economy below potential. "Output gap appears to be widening further" (Broaddus). "Output gap persists for several quarters" (Ferguson). "Output gap is wider than expected" (Kohn). Gramlich: "continuing output gaps." Parry: growth of ~2¾% "below most estimates of the potential rate." Minehan: "nearly four years of below-potential growth and an output gap even into 2005." BRIGHT-LINE RULE: below potential → score must be < 50. The gap is significant ("widening"), not trivial. Score in 33–40 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Labor markets clearly weak. Moskow: "labor demand remains weak," Manpower hiring index fell to lowest since 1991. Minehan: "labor markets remain quite slack, labor force participation is down, unemployment is up." Santomero: "Regional labor markets also remain weak." Guynn: "Firms remain reluctant to hire." National unemployment rising. Rapid job losses referenced (New England losing 10% of national job losses). Not at Depression-levels, but clearly elevated unemployment and significant slack. Score in 28–35 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Consumer spending sluggish but not collapsing — Poole's Wal-Mart contact shows 2.8% YoY comparable sales, no postwar bounce; Minehan's CVS contact reports no pickup. Business investment is weak with excess capacity, firms postponing capex, used equipment glut cited by multiple speakers. Pianalto: "too much excess capacity in too many firms." Poole: UPS/FedEx giving up aircraft options. Housing is a bright spot. Auto sales steady with incentives. Overall: below trend across most components. Score in 33–40 range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Conditions notably improving per most speakers — credit spreads have narrowed (Kohn: "risk spreads in credit markets have more than retraced their run-up of last summer"), equity prices rising, implied volatilities down, BBB bond rates declining sharply (Greenspan). Dollar weakening but without adverse asset price effects. Multiple speakers note "accommodative financial conditions." Vice Chairman McDonough flags that equity market gains are "tentative" and debt spreads "still broad by historical standards." Overall, financial conditions are loose to moderately loose, consistent with the accommodative-policy era. Score in 28–38 range.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
The Committee's center of gravity is clearly dovish. The decision is to hold rates at 1.25% (no cut today) but virtually all speakers express comfort with or preference for easing. Parry explicitly advocates for an immediate cut. Minehan, Bernanke, Bies, Olson, Ferguson, Kohn, Poole, McTeer, Hoenig, Gramlich all explicitly say they "could have supported" or "would have been comfortable with" an easing. Greenspan himself says "we probably would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless we see a fairly substantial pickup in economic activity." The reason to hold is purely tactical (market surprise risk, fragility of nascent financial market gains, waiting for June data). The balance-of-risks statement signals weighted toward weakness. This is emphatically in the dovish zone — strong easing lean with merely tactical deferral. Score in 18–25 range (strong dovish: majority favors easing, current hold is reluctant and tactical).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
No formal voting dissents mentioned. But the policy discussion reveals meaningful disagreements: Parry explicitly argues for cutting today (strongest dovish outlier); Broaddus is closest to wanting a neutral bias; Moskow prefers "balanced risks" language. Multiple participants object to the "taken together" sentence in the new statement. Ferguson, Kohn, Hoenig, Gramlich, Minehan, Bies broadly in agreement with hold-now-cut-soon posture. Guynn and Vice Chairman McDonough more cautious on deflation risk framing. There is substantive heterogeneity in the policy statement debate (multiple camps: drop the "taken together" sentence vs. keep it; balance vs. tilt toward weakness). However, no formal dissents occur. Score: 35–45 — moderate disagreement, notably across the statement language and easing timing, with 0 formal dissents but multiple "reluctant" or "different preference" voices.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Financial conditions are discussed, but not as a primary crisis concern. Speakers mention improving credit spreads, equity market recovery, dollar weakness (McDonough flags speculative euro long positions), and the fragility of the nascent financial market improvement. This is discussed as context for policy timing rather than as a systemic concern. A moderate amount of discussion — multiple participants reference financial conditions. Score: 35–45 range (limited-to-moderate discussion, not a dominant topic).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The new "balance of risks" statement language is extensively debated. The Committee adopts language saying risks are "weighted toward weakness" in the combined sense, and explicitly flags that a "substantial fall in inflation would be unwelcome." Greenspan signals he expects to move rates lower unless the economy shows substantial pickup; multiple speakers interpret the statement as strong code for a likely June cut. This is an easing signal — forward guidance is clearly dovish/easing-biased. Score: 20–28 range (mild easing bias with "low for long" implication and strong expectation of imminent ease).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Uncertainty is a recurrent theme throughout: war's lingering effects on data ("hard to discern how much of the economic weakness was due to geopolitical uncertainty vs. underlying dynamics" — Santomero); Greenbook forecasts repeatedly marked down; wide disagreement on deflation probability (20–35%); whether postwar bounce will materialize is genuinely unknown. However, this is not "uncharted territory" extreme language — it's elevated, with multiple participants acknowledging they "can't yet discern" the true state of the economy. Score: 55–65 range (elevated uncertainty, multiple participants emphasize it, unusual difficulty in reading data).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 18 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Bernanke: "Inflation as measured by the chain version of core CPI has fallen from 1.8 percent last year to 1.2 percent this year... Excluding nonmarket prices, core PCE inflation fell from 1.3 percent in the year ending March 2002 to 0.9 percent in the year ending March 2003, and it has averaged 0.2 percent over the past six months."
- Parry: "We expect core PCE inflation to fall to just over 1 percent both this year and next. After adjusting for a reasonable estimate of measurement bias, the two forecasts imply true inflation of about ½ of 1 percent. An analysis by the San Francisco staff... suggests an uncomfortably high 20 percent probability that true inflation could fall below zero next year."
- Gramlich: "inflation has fallen to dangerously low levels, and both arrows are pointed down... The Greenbook probability of deflation has risen to 35 percent."

**Center of gravity:** The overwhelming majority of participants see inflation well below target and falling rapidly, with 20–35% probability of deflation discussed seriously across the meeting. Guynn partially dissents on deflation risk characterization (relative price declines vs. general deflation) but does not dispute the low inflation level.

**Minority views:** Guynn argues current relative price declines don't constitute deflation psychology and current preconditions don't parallel the Great Depression. This raises the upper bound slightly but does not shift the core reading above 25.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 35 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Broaddus: "Hours worked are still contracting, and the output gap appears to be widening further."
- Ferguson: "The output gap envisioned in the baseline persists for several quarters; it is not closed during the forecast period."
- Minehan: "we will have had nearly four years of below-potential growth and an output gap even into 2005. Unemployment rises through most of the forecast period."

**Center of gravity:** Unanimous that the economy is below potential, with the output gap widening or persisting, and recovery sluggish (2¾% growth below potential per Parry). Not a catastrophic contraction — GDP growing modestly — but well into negative gap territory.

**Minority views:** Greenspan sees some encouraging straws in the wind (NFIB index, chain store sales), but even he does not dispute below-potential operation.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 30 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Minehan: "labor markets remain quite slack, labor force participation is down, unemployment is up, and industrial production and business investment remain subpar."
- Moskow: "The preliminary calculations for Manpower's third-quarter hiring plan survey, and the index fell from 11 to 6, which is the lowest level since 1991."
- Santomero: "Regional labor markets also remain weak... employment in the region has continued to contract this year."

**Center of gravity:** All District presidents report weak labor markets — firms reluctant to hire, job losses continuing, unemployment elevated and rising. This is consistent with the 31–45 anchor range ("below full employment; slack remains"). Closer to the lower end (28–35) given explicit references to "worst since 1991" hiring surveys and continuing job losses nationally.

**Minority views:** McTeer notes Texas has positive employment growth, but the national picture is unambiguously weak.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 37 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Poole: "There is no sign at all of a postwar bounce in the information that we have... His [Wal-Mart contact's] said there is no evidence of any postwar bounce in consumer spending."
- Pianalto: "I continue to question business people closely about their capital spending plans, and I've yet to find any concrete evidence pointing to a firming of spending plans later this year. There is just too much excess capacity in too many firms."
- Minehan: "labor markets remain quite slack... there's a trend toward less spending."

**Center of gravity:** Consumer spending sluggish with no postwar bounce confirmed across multiple District contacts (Wal-Mart/Poole, CVS/Minehan). Business investment deeply subdued — excess capacity, balance sheet constraints, used equipment glut. Housing and auto sales (with incentives) are soft positives. Net: clearly below trend, matching 31–45 range.

**Minority views:** Guynn sees some optimism building in business sentiment; Greenspan notes NFIB survey surge and chain store sales increase. These are tentative and anecdotal, keeping the score from falling to 30.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 32 ±7 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "Risk spreads in credit markets have more than retraced their run-up of last summer... the cost of capital to businesses has decreased."
- Greenspan: "We are getting some fairly substantial improvement in the financial numbers — those for the stock market obviously and, indeed, for all the credit markets... the dramatic decline in the premiums for credit derivative default swaps."
- McDonough: "spreads have narrowed, but they're still broad by historical standards."

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions have improved substantially from their worst levels. Spreads narrowed, equities rising, implied volatility down, credit access improving. Conditions are accommodative/loose. Some acknowledgment that spreads remain historically wide and the market improvement is fragile. Score in the "somewhat loose to neutral" zone, leaning toward loose side.

**Minority views:** McDonough's caution about the fragility of market improvements and historical width of spreads warrants the wider uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 22 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Parry: "as insurance against downside surprises to both economic activity and inflation in the future, it would seem prudent to ease policy further."
- Bernanke: "there appears to be a prima facie case for easing policy. I tend to agree with that conclusion, and barring an exceptionally strong near-term turnaround in the economy, I hope that the Committee will adopt a posture of leaning toward ease."
- Greenspan: "we probably would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless we see a fairly substantial pickup in economic activity."

**Center of gravity:** The hold decision is unanimous (no dissent), but the overwhelming consensus is that easing is appropriate soon — most participants explicitly say they "could have supported" a cut today. The hold is tactical (market surprise risk, fragility of nascent recovery). This is firmly in the "strong dovish" zone (11–25 range): majority favors easing, current hold is reluctant, forward lean is strongly toward accommodation.

**Minority views:** Guynn and Broaddus are somewhat more cautious about characterizing deflation risk as imminent, but neither opposes an easing lean. Moskow and McTeer want to wait but acknowledge downside risks.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 40 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Parry explicitly advocates for cutting today (disagrees with the consensus hold).
- Broaddus: prefers "neutral bias" rather than the new statement's "tilted toward weakness" framing — "I'm not sure I'm comfortable with that."
- Moskow: "My preference would be... to say that the risks are balanced" — proposes an alternative statement structure.
- Multiple participants (Guynn, Pianalto, Santomero, McTeer) want to eliminate the "taken together" sentence; McDonough and Greenspan prefer to keep it.

**Center of gravity:** No formal voting dissents occur. However, there are clear and significant differences on: (1) whether to cut today (Parry vs. the field), (2) the wording of the balance of risks statement (3–4 alternative preferences expressed), and (3) the severity of deflation concern (Guynn's contrarian view). This warrants a 40 — moderate differences with one strong outlier and several reservations, but not formal dissents or sharp adversarial exchanges.

**Minority views:** The debate over statement language is substantive but collegial. No "sharp personal exchanges."

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 38 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn discusses credit market developments, spread tightening, implied volatility declines, lower dollar at some length.
- McDonough raises dollar weakness and speculative euro positions as a financial stability concern.
- Greenspan discusses credit derivative default swaps, BBB bond rates, equity market fragility.

**Center of gravity:** Financial conditions are mentioned by multiple participants but primarily as context for policy timing (fragility of financial market gains being a reason not to act today) rather than as a central preoccupation. The discussion is meaningful but secondary to the inflation/deflation and output gap themes. Fits in the "limited discussion; a few mention as context, not focus" to "moderate discussion" transition zone.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 22 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Greenspan (policy discussion): "we probably would be wise to move the funds rate lower unless we see a fairly substantial pickup in economic activity."
- The new statement language adopted signals risks "weighted toward weakness" and flags that "a substantial fall in inflation would be unwelcome."
- Poole: "I believe the market is going to interpret the language as code... that we are highly likely to cut rates at our next meeting."

**Center of gravity:** The forward guidance is unambiguously dovish. The statement adopted explicitly signals easing bias/intention. This is a "strong easing bias: signal further/extended accommodation ahead" — fitting the 11–25 range. Multiple members describe the statement as signaling imminent easing; the June meeting is explicitly flagged as the likely decision point for a cut.

**Minority views:** Moskow and McTeer would prefer a neutral balance-of-risks signal, but the adopted statement is dovish-leaning.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 60 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Santomero: "I don't receive the usual comfort from the staff's effort this time around... a manifestation of the time period we are in... forecasting generally depends somewhat on retrospection, [and] most of our real sector data are dated."
- Kohn: "we still don't have enough new information to sort out whether the marked weakening in February and March was almost entirely related to the close onset of war or also reflected more-persistent problems that will damp the upturn."
- Ferguson: "successive periods [of data] have been weaker and weaker, which does at least undermine to some degree the confidence one might have in the Greenbook forecast."

**Center of gravity:** Multiple participants independently emphasize the difficulty of reading postwar data, the unusual forecasting environment, and wide scenario uncertainty (will there be a postwar bounce or not?). This is elevated uncertainty — "elevated: multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty; particularly uncertain." The uncertainty is genuine (not rhetorical) given the structural break created by the Iraq war and the unusual deflation-probability debate. Score 60 is appropriate for this level of acknowledged uncertainty without reaching the "uncharted territory" extreme.

**Minority views:** Guynn and Kohn are relatively more confident in their assessments. Parry's call for insurance easing implies he has enough confidence in the downside to act.
