# FOMC Transcript Scoring — September 17–18, 2013

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the September 17–18, 2013 FOMC meeting. It is fully segmented with individual speakers identified by name (MR. LACKER., MS. GEORGE., CHAIRMAN BERNANKE., etc.). The document covers two sessions: (a) a financial stability round and economic go-round on Day 1 (September 17), and (b) a policy go-round on Day 2 (September 18). Total word count is very large (~50,000 words). Speaker coverage is high — nearly all FOMC participants make substantive contributions.

### 2. Era Context
September 2013 is mid-QE3 era. The Fed has been conducting open-ended asset purchases ($85B/month) since September 2012. The "taper" debate has been ongoing since Bernanke's May 2013 taper hint. Long-term Treasury yields rose ~100 bps from May to September 2013, creating concerns about financial tightening. Unemployment was around 7.3%; PCE inflation was running ~1.0-1.2%, well below the 2% target. The Committee ultimately voted for alternative B (no taper at this meeting). Era anchors suggest: INFLATION_PRESSURE in the 38-42 range, OUTPUT_GAP in the 35-42 range, LABOR_TIGHTNESS around 35-40.

### 3. Directional Signals for Each Core Dimension

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Participants see inflation as BELOW target. Bullard: "total year-over-year PCE inflation for 2013 is projected to come in close to 1 percent, nearly 1 full percentage point below our expectations." Lockhart notes that "core PCE inflation will have been below 2 percent for nine consecutive years" if the Tealbook forecast proves correct. Evans: "I think we will be too low over the entire forecast horizon. Even in 2016, in my opinion we will be lucky to be near 2 percent." Kocherlakota emphasizes that the medium-term inflation outlook runs below target and that this justifies more accommodation. Multiple participants treat current inflation softness as a reason for caution. Dudley: inflation is "somewhat below our objective currently." Pianalto notes "Recent inflation data appear to be firming" and "median CPI inflation measure came in at 2 percent." Fisher says PCE inflation "seems to have stabilized" and is "firm." Williams is "encouraged by the recent data that suggest that the decline in inflation earlier this year was, indeed, temporary."
Center of gravity: inflation is clearly below the 2% target but has recently stabilized after an earlier dip — broad consensus at 1.0–1.5% PCE, expected to gradually drift back toward target. The rubric anchor for 2013 "below target" is 38-42. Given 1% PCE (well below, not just slightly below), this leans toward the lower end of that range, perhaps 36-40.

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Participants see economy as BELOW potential with slack remaining. Lacker: "the expansion we've seen so far is the expansion we've got...real GDP growth at around 2 to 2¼ percent." Rosengren: "I now expect third-quarter GDP to expand at 2 percent." Evans: "We still appear to be muddling along without signs that a more vigorous recovery is building." Tarullo: "an economy still in need of greater aggregate demand." Dudley: "Unfortunately, there is not much evidence that the U.S. economy is breaking out above the 2 percent growth path that has prevailed since the beginning of the economic recovery." Bernanke's summary: "Modest to moderate growth is expected to continue." Williams: "Recent economic data have been somewhat disappointing on net."
Multiple participants invoke "headwinds," "slack," "fiscal restraint" holding back activity. Several mention GDI running higher (~2.7%) than GDP (~1.7%), which provides some upside nuance. Lacker and a few others see fundamentals as stronger and expect a pickup. But the majority center is below-potential with notable slack. Era anchor: 35-42 range for 2010-14 recovery. This meeting is 2013 mid-recovery with persistent slack → ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Participants see labor market as BELOW full employment, recovering but slack remaining. Unemployment at 7.3%; three-month payroll average of 148,000 — below the 200,000 threshold multiple speakers cite as needed. Rosengren: "the three-month average of payroll employment growth is only 148,000 jobs, much less than the 184,000 jobs we have averaged over the past year, and well below the 200,000-plus jobs that I think are needed at this stage." Kocherlakota: employment-to-population ratio "has remained basically the same" since October 2009; labor market improvement "has almost entirely taken the form of reclassification of nonemployed people." Yellen: "slowing pace of payroll gains." Tarullo: "pretty clearly, a good bit more can be done now." Evans: reports "not indicative of a vibrant, sustainable labor market." George (more optimistic): "labor market conditions index has shown a notable improvement to historically high levels."
Most participants see substantial remaining slack; a minority (Plosser, George, Pianalto, Williams) see meaningful cumulative progress. Center of gravity: clearly below full employment. Anchor: 2013 mid-recovery → 35-40 range. Unemployment at 7.3% with low participation confirms low-to-mid range. Score ~38.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Participants see consumer spending and business investment as BELOW trend, sluggish. Bernanke's summary: "Consumer spending has been a little bit on the soft side. Retail sales, soft." Evans: "Consumer expenditures have decelerated noticeably...the past few months of spending data are pretty worrisome." Williams: contacts "remain very hesitant to expand employment given the still uncertain economic outlook." Lockhart: business contacts "apprehensively optimistic," trajectory "very similar to the first half." Stein: economy is "stuck in the mud." Fisher presents a more positive picture: "we've seen sustained low real interest rates, abundant credit, improved household and strong corporate finances, pent-up demand for consumer durable and capital goods."
Auto sector: strong (multiple speakers); housing: moderating from rates; business investment: mixed with some encouraging ISM/NFIB readings. On net, consumer spending sluggish, business spending mixed. Era anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate." The weight of discussion leans toward sluggish/moderate, not robust. Score ~40.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
This is the most debated dimension. The key fact: 10-year Treasury yields rose ~100 bps from May to September 2013. There is genuine disagreement:
- Dovish interpretation (Rosengren, Evans, Yellen, Kocherlakota, Dudley): "significant tightening in financial conditions," mortgage rates up substantially, adversely affecting housing.
- Hawkish/skeptical interpretation (Fisher, Williams, Powell): "outside of real estate, credit conditions are, if anything, easing, not tightening"; SLOOS easing; junk spreads narrowed; equity prices up; credit freely available for investment-grade borrowers.
- Bernanke: acknowledges the rise in rates and mortgage tightening but notes offsetting factors (house prices, stock prices).
Center of gravity: The dominant narrative in the room treats the rate rise as a meaningful source of headwinds, especially for housing. However, credit conditions for non-housing borrowers are seen as relatively accommodative. This is a mixed picture: tighter than neutral in the mortgage/long-rate channel, but not broadly tight. Score: mildly tight — probably 52-58. The rise in long rates (+100 bps) is real and widely noted, pushing above 50, but the broad credit picture offsets this. Score ~55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
This is the defining policy dimension. The vote was for alternative B (no taper). Count of preferences:
- Alternative B (no taper): Rosengren, Evans, Lockhart, Bullard, Yellen, Tarullo, Stein (reluctantly), Powell (reluctantly), Dudley, Bernanke = ~10 speakers
- Alternative C (taper $10B): Plosser, Fisher, Lacker, George, Pianalto, Williams (preferred C but willing to accept B), Powell (preferred C but accepted B) = ~5-6 speakers
- Kocherlakota: favors no taper but from the most dovish angle (goals-oriented approach, more accommodation needed)
The majority clearly favor maintaining the full purchase pace. The discussion throughout is dominated by dovish-to-moderate language: concern about tightening financial conditions, below-target inflation, insufficient labor market progress, need for more accommodation. However, this is NOT a meeting where participants want to expand accommodation — they are debating WHEN to begin tapering, not whether to add more QE. The baseline is existing accommodation at $85B/month. The lean is: hold current accommodation with a bias toward tapering "later this year." This maps to the rubric's "lean dovish to balanced: hold steady with mild easing bias" (41-50 range). The fact that 5-6 members explicitly favored tapering now (alternative C) pulls the center modestly hawkish of a pure hold. But the no-taper majority keeps it in the 40-48 range. Score ~43. This is distinctly different from the 2010-12 QE debates (where "how much more to buy" was the question — 15-25 range). Here it's "hold steady vs. begin reducing" — the 40-50 range is appropriate.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
This is a highly divided meeting. Bernanke explicitly states "the Committee is nicely split." The vote was apparently for alternative B, with Rosengren favoring B (explicitly), Williams preferring C but accepting B, Plosser firmly C, Fisher firmly C, Lacker firmly C, George C, Pianalto C, Powell preferred C but accepted B, Stein preferred C but accepted B, Bullard B, Evans B, Lockhart B, Tarullo B, Yellen B, Dudley B, Kocherlakota B (from the dovish direction). The final vote appears to be alternative B with multiple clear dissents or reluctant supporters. Fisher: "I hope you will find some way...to pull Switzerland out of the Balkans." Tarullo: "genuine and good faith differences of perspective." Bernanke: "a very close decision." There appear to be at least 3-4 formal or near-formal dissenters (Plosser/Fisher/Lacker/George at minimum for preferring C). Score: this maps to the 55-65 range ("notable disagreement: distinct camps; 1-2 formal dissents; disagreement extends to assessment"). The disagreement extends to the economic assessment as well (how much tightening has occurred, how much labor market progress has been made). Score ~62.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Day 1 begins with a dedicated financial stability round. Kocherlakota gives extensive remarks on using monetary policy to mitigate financial instability. George discusses community bank interest rate risk, farmland prices, leveraged loan market. Evans discusses forward guidance credibility and financial stability interaction. Throughout the economic go-round, financial conditions/stability is a recurring theme (Fisher at length on credit conditions; Stein on housing vs. corporate credit; Williams on asset price channels). In the policy round, financial tightening/stability is explicitly cited by Rosengren, Yellen, Dudley, and Bernanke as a reason for no taper. This is a prominent topic, addressed by most participants. Score: 60-68 range. "Prominent topic; most participants discuss at length." Score ~65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
The meeting's forward guidance discussion is rich. The Committee chose not to taper but included "at its coming meetings" language signaling imminent taper. Bernanke explicitly states "all meetings are on the table" and that October could see action. Multiple participants discuss strengthening forward guidance (inflation floor, post-liftoff path, alternative A paragraph 5 language). The forward signal is: no taper now but taper very likely "later this year," probably October or December; rates will remain near zero well beyond 6.5% threshold; post-liftoff path will be very gradual due to headwinds.
This is an easing-biased signal (no taper, rates low for very long) but with a clear lean toward beginning tapering imminently. The rubric anchors: 3-8 for "Evans Rule/QE3 thresholds" (2012-13); 8-15 for "taper" era 2014. This meeting is right at the inflection point — the committee signaled both continued accommodation AND near-term tapering. The net forward signal is mild easing bias with a near-term taper lean. Score ~25-30: easing lean with data dependence — the "at its coming meetings" language and Bernanke's press conference forward lean toward taper pushes above pure accommodative anchor but still in easing territory given the no-taper decision and emphasis on sustained accommodation. Score ~28.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Uncertainty is pervasive throughout. Lockhart uses "persistently ambiguous" multiple times. Bernanke: "data continue to be quite mixed." Williams: "I've revised down my forecast for 2014 by a few tenths." Tarullo: "many of us...have less certainty around that projection than we had back in June." Bullard: uncertainty about whether inflation will pick up. Stein: extensive discussion of uncertainty about productivity growth. Dudley: "I don't have any clarity about how the Congress will deal with funding." Rosengren: "I have no confidence in my ability to predict the magnitude of the market's reaction." Yellen: "lingering doubts about the potential for further improvements going forward." Also uncertainty about fiscal outcome (debt ceiling), impact of higher rates on housing, productivity growth interpretation.
Multiple participants explicitly invoke uncertainty as the rationale for not acting ("wait for more data"). This is elevated but not extreme uncertainty. Rubric anchor: 40-50 for "elevated" (multiple participants emphasize unusual uncertainty). "Considerable uncertainty" about multiple factors (rate impact, fiscal, productivity, labor market interpretation). Score ~55.
