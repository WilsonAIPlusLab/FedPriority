# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-09-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. The format is a classic FOMC go-around followed by a policy discussion round. Speakers include Presidents Moskow, Parry, Minehan, McTeer, Boehne, Guynn, Stern, Jordan, Hoenig, Broaddus, Poole, Vice Chairman McDonough, Governors Ferguson, Gramlich, Rivlin, Kelley, Meyer, and Chairman Greenspan. This is a well-attended meeting with robust discussion. The latter portion includes an extensive discussion of Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM).

### Era Context
September 1998 -- the height of the LTCM/Russian crisis. The Asian financial crisis that began in 1997 has now spread to Russia (August 1998 default/devaluation) and is threatening Latin America, particularly Brazil. Domestic economy still strong but international financial contagion is the dominant concern. The Committee voted unanimously for a 25bp rate cut at this meeting.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint -- benign and declining. Moskow: "price pressures remain benign," "less risk of a significant increase in inflation." Parry: core CPI "falling from 2-1/4 percent this year to about 2 percent in 1999, and then going below 2 percent in 2000." McTeer: "the price picture has turned deflationary in several sectors." Guynn: "not seeing significant signs of price pressures," material prices negative for fifth month. Boehne: "Inflation is a word that is hardly used in the District." Stern: "very hard to see any inflationary momentum building." Multiple speakers note commodity prices falling, strong dollar, and ample capacity suppressing inflation. A minority (Broaddus, Minehan) note that tight labor markets still pose some residual inflation risk, and Minehan expects "some pickup in core inflation next year." But the clear center of gravity is that inflation pressures are low and declining.
Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/declining. Score below 50. Magnitude: "benign," "deflationary in several sectors" -- this is clearly below target, ~30-38 range.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as currently operating ABOVE potential but decelerating toward or below potential. Multiple speakers note the economy "remains at a very high level" (Broaddus), "remarkably robust" (Kelley), with "very tight labor markets" (many speakers). However, forecasts are for growth "somewhat below potential next year" (Moskow), "below trend" (Meyer). Greenspan: "very hard to find any indicators of significant softening." The current state is still above potential, but the forward outlook is for deceleration to below potential.
Score the current assessment: economy is still running above potential but decelerating. The bright-line rule says "above potential" language must score >50. Multiple speakers describe the economy at "high levels" with manufacturing softening. I'd place this at 52-55 -- modestly above potential, reflecting current strength but with clear deceleration underway.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants see labor markets as VERY TIGHT. Moskow: "Labor markets are still tight." Minehan: "Labor markets remain very tight despite August job losses." Stern: "labor markets remain very tight. The unemployment rate in the state of Minnesota has dropped to a new record low." Jordan: "Labor markets continue to be extremely tight. Turnover is rising." Broaddus: "extraordinarily tight labor market conditions... Workers with even minimal job skills are hard to find." Hoenig: "tight labor markets and unemployment rates that are extremely low." Greenspan: "labor market is exceptionally tight." Nearly universal agreement that labor markets are very tight.
Anchor reference: 65-75 for 1999-2000. Unemployment at 4.5% in this period. Score ~67-72.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Consumer spending remains solid but investment outlook is weakening. Jordan: "retail sales have been very strong." Kelley: "final sales remain strong... consumer sentiment remains high." Greenspan: "Retail sales are not terrific, but obviously they are not doing too badly. Motor vehicles... softening a little." On investment: Ferguson notes corporate financing gap at highest since 1982, vulnerability of credit-driven investment. Greenspan: "a decline in capital investment" is anticipated. Manufacturing investment clearly weakening. Housing still strong. Net position: currently at or modestly above trend, but forward-looking weakness building.
Score ~52-55, currently solid but with clear signs of deceleration.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants see financial conditions as having TIGHTENED significantly. This is a dominant theme. McDonough: "very wide credit spreads," warns of potential "credit crunch." Minehan: "widening of spreads and... a rather significant drying up of liquidity in all markets." Poole: "spreads... indicate a very abnormal state of affairs." Ferguson: "syndicated loan market appears to have become less accommodating." McTeer: "financing has dried up recently for a variety of commercial projects." Greenspan: "opening up of risk spreads is a very significant indication of increased risk aversion," "fairly pronounced weakening of cash flows... coupled with an ever-increasing difficulty of raising capital externally." The senior loan officers' survey showed "fairly widespread tightening." Multiple speakers note that while spreads were too narrow before, they have now overshot to too wide.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence clearly says much tighter. Score in the 65-72 range. Not full crisis (credit not frozen) but clearly well above normal.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: Unanimously dovish -- every participant supports a 25bp rate cut. McTeer wanted 50bp. Broaddus and Jordan reluctantly support 25bp as minimum. The center of gravity is clear: moderate dovish lean with 25bp cut, forward bias toward further easing. The debate is whether to ease 25 or 50, not whether to ease at all. Multiple speakers (Boehne, Ferguson, Rivlin, Gramlich, Kelley) emphasize acting "deliberately" and "judiciously."
This is a moderate dovish action: easing 25bp with asymmetric (easing) tilt. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. This meeting: they are actively cutting rates. Score ~33-37.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: There is broad agreement on the 25bp cut (unanimous vote). Disagreement exists on two margins: (1) McTeer wanted 50bp, (2) the directive tilt -- "smallest of majorities" for asymmetry (Minehan, Kelley, Broaddus, Hoenig, Stern, Guynn preferred symmetric). The spread of views is relatively narrow (all agree on direction, differ on magnitude by 25bp and on tilt). No formal dissents.
Score ~25-30. Broad agreement with minor tactical differences.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Financial stability/conditions are a MAJOR topic. Virtually every speaker discusses financial market conditions at length. The LTCM discussion at the end occupies enormous space. Credit spreads, risk aversion, potential credit crunch, equity market declines, contagion -- these dominate the meeting. McDonough's extensive LTCM presentation underscores the systemic concern. This rivals the economic assessment in emphasis.
Score ~75-82. Financial stability is a dominant concern but not the *only* topic (domestic economy also discussed at length).

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is dovish/easing. The Committee adopted asymmetry toward ease. Greenspan: "a tilt toward ease." Multiple speakers (Moskow, Parry, Meyer, Gramlich, Ferguson) say further cuts are "more likely than not." Greenspan himself notes the possibility this could be "the first in a series of moves." However, there is also language that this could be "an insurance premium" and that events will drive future action. Several speakers (Stern, Hoenig, Jordan) prefer symmetry, suggesting some restraint in the forward signal.
Score ~32-38. Mild easing bias with data dependence. Not a strong easing commitment (no explicit path), but clear lean toward further easing.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Participants express ELEVATED uncertainty. Moskow: "greater uncertainty about the future." Minehan: "this estimate is fraught with risk." Poole: "The variance of the outlook has certainly increased... a fairly wide range of perfectly plausible outcomes." Meyer: "asymmetric downside risks," "unpleasantly fat tail of the probability distribution." Greenspan: multiple scenarios discussed, "cannot presume that the process of deterioration is 100 percent irrevocable." Jordan: "fear factor is pervasive." Boehne: "more uncertain and in some cases apprehensive." The uncertainty is genuine and widely expressed -- the key question is whether financial disruption will spill over to the real economy.
Score ~58-65. Elevated but not "uncharted territory" -- participants have frameworks and models, just high variance.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): These diverge somewhat, which is explained by the fact that labor markets are a lagging indicator -- the economy is at high levels currently but expected to slow. The output gap reflects the beginning of deceleration while labor has not yet eased.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (35) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (35): These align -- dovish action with dovish forward lean.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (68) is coherent with high FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (78) -- the tightening of financial conditions is a major concern driving the policy discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MOSKOW: "price pressures remain benign... less risk of a significant increase in inflation"
- MR. PARRY: "our forecast shows inflation as measured by the core CPI falling from 2-1/4 percent this year to about 2 percent in 1999, and then going below 2 percent in 2000"
- MR. MCTEER: "the price picture has turned deflationary in several sectors"
- MR. BOEHNE: "Inflation is a word that is hardly used in the District"
- MR. GUYNN: "We still are not seeing significant signs of price pressures. Our manufacturing survey indexes for prices of materials remain negative for the fifth month in a row"

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is clearly below target and declining further, with commodity prices falling, the strong dollar, and ample capacity all suppressing prices. Multiple districts report deflationary conditions in manufacturing/commodities.

Minority views: Minehan and Broaddus note that tight labor markets still represent a residual inflation risk; M2 growth mentioned by several as a potential concern. These inform the upper end of the uncertainty band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it is very hard to find any indicators of significant softening... the labor market is exceptionally tight"
- MR. KELLEY: "our domestic economy remains remarkably robust. Though decelerating from earlier this year, final sales remain strong"
- MR. MOSKOW: "we do see a period of growth somewhat below potential next year"
- MR. MEYER: "Second-half GDP growth still looks to be close to trend... That is, of course, a significant slowdown relative to the rate of growth in the first half"

Center-of-gravity: The economy is currently operating modestly above potential with tight labor markets and solid activity, but decelerating. The forward outlook is for below-potential growth. The score reflects the current state (still above potential) weighted by the strong expectation of imminent deceleration.

Minority views: McTeer sees more current weakness; Kelley and Jordan see more current strength. Wide uncertainty band reflects the bifurcation between current strength and expected weakness.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. BROADDUS: "no perceptible easing in the extraordinarily tight labor market conditions... Workers with even minimal job skills are hard to find"
- MR. JORDAN: "Labor markets continue to be extremely tight. Turnover is rising and finding qualified replacements is taking longer and longer"
- MR. STERN: "labor markets remain very tight. The unemployment rate in the state of Minnesota has dropped to a new record low"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the labor market is exceptionally tight"

Center-of-gravity: Near-universal agreement that labor markets are very tight, with unemployment at 4.5%, widespread reports of worker shortages across districts, and rising wage pressures (Jordan reports teller wages up from $7 to $10/hour).

Minority views: Very narrow band -- virtually all speakers agree labor markets are very tight. Some note nascent softening in manufacturing employment.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 54 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. JORDAN: "Our retail sales have been very strong. Retailers say that sales and profits for the third and fourth quarters will be at record levels"
- MR. KELLEY: "final sales remain strong; labor compensation is rising; new jobs continue to be created; consumer sentiment remains high"
- MR. FERGUSON: "nonfinancial corporate debt grew at an annual rate of about 12 percent in the first half... The financing gap is I believe at its highest level since the recession of 1982"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Retail sales are not terrific, but obviously they are not doing too badly... I do anticipate a significant decline in capital appropriations, new orders"

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending remains solid to strong currently, supported by tight labor markets and still-elevated wealth. But business investment outlook is deteriorating as financing conditions tighten and risk aversion rises. Net position is modestly above trend but weakening.

Minority views: McTeer sees more current weakness; Jordan reports very strong retail and construction. The uncertainty reflects the gap between current strength and anticipated weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: "spreads in the credit markets were certainly unnaturally tight earlier this year, but the correction has carried these spreads well beyond a return to normalcy. The current spreads are very wide indeed"
- MR. POOLE: "the spreads that we have seen open up in lower quality credits indicate a very abnormal state of affairs"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the approximately $3 trillion capital loss in the aggregate value of equities... opening up of risk spreads is a very significant indication of increased risk aversion"
- MS. MINEHAN: "widening of spreads and what he sees as a rather significant drying up of liquidity in all markets"

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions have tightened dramatically from previously loose levels. Credit spreads are well beyond normal, syndicated loan market has pulled back, commercial real estate financing has dried up, senior loan officer survey shows significant tightening. Not yet a full credit freeze, but clearly restrictive and potentially heading toward crunch.

Minority views: Stern and Jordan note that some tightening was desired and expected ("spreads were far too narrow previously"). Boehne notes "lending terms remain looser than one might expect" among some smaller banks.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I would suggest a reduction of 25 basis points in the federal funds rate... I would add that my preference for a tilt toward ease"
- MR. MCTEER: "I thought it was time to get ahead of the curve by easing monetary policy... we need sooner rather than later, and we need more rather than less" (wanted 50bp)
- MR. BOEHNE: "there is a persuasive case for a decrease in the fed funds rate"
- MR. MEYER: "an easing is clearly justified today... providing insurance against a bad draw from the unpleasantly fat tail"

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous support for 25bp cut. The debate is not whether to ease but how much and how to frame it. Most favor "deliberate" and "judicious" easing. This is a clear moderate dovish action. Vote was unanimous for 25bp cut with asymmetric directive toward ease.

Minority views: McTeer wanted 50bp (more dovish). Broaddus and Jordan viewed the cut as minimal/symbolic and would prefer symmetric directive (less dovish forward posture).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 27 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "There is unanimity for a 25 basis point reduction and the smallest of majorities for asymmetry"
- Broad agreement on direction (ease); tactical differences on magnitude (25 vs 50bp -- only McTeer pushed for 50) and directive tilt (roughly half preferred symmetric)
- No formal dissents

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the policy action with minor tactical differences about the directive's forward lean. The disagreement on tilt is notable but not fundamental -- multiple speakers said they were "almost indifferent" about the tilt.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 78 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Nearly every speaker devotes substantial time to credit spreads, risk aversion, LTCM, equity market declines, and potential credit crunch
- VICE CHAIRMAN MCDONOUGH: Extensive presentation on LTCM intervention (the Fed's role in preventing LTCM collapse due to systemic risk)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "what we are observing is a development that is occurring at the fulcrum of a turn... in the psychology of anticipatory evaluations"
- MR. FERGUSON: Detailed analysis of corporate leverage and financing gap vulnerability

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is a dominant theme -- arguably the primary driver of the policy decision. The LTCM near-collapse and intervention are discussed at great length. Financial conditions (spreads, credit availability, equity declines, contagion) are the central concern motivating the rate cut.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a recommendation that we move to asymmetry toward ease... my preference for a tilt in that direction"
- MR. MOSKOW: "there is a better than 50 percent probability that we will have to lower rates again"
- MR. PARRY: "the current situation may require more than one 25 basis point cut"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it is conceivable that we may end up viewing this action not as the first in a series of moves but as an insurance premium"

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is a mild easing bias. The asymmetric directive signals further easing is more likely than not, but Greenspan explicitly hedges this by noting events will drive future actions and that reversal is possible. Several speakers prefer symmetric directive.

Minority views: Minehan, Kelley, Broaddus, Hoenig, Stern, Guynn all preferred symmetric directive, suggesting the forward easing signal is not strong.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. POOLE: "The variance of the outlook has certainly increased... a fairly wide range of perfectly plausible outcomes on either side"
- MR. MEYER: "asymmetric downside risks... unpleasantly fat tail of the probability distribution of outcomes"
- MR. JORDAN: "the fear factor is pervasive... investors are parking cash because of uncertainty"
- MS. MINEHAN: "this estimate is fraught with risk, and I must say that we are very humble about our ability to forecast"
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "cannot presume that the process of deterioration is 100 percent irrevocable"

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple participants emphasize unusual difficulty in forecasting, wide variance of outcomes, and asymmetric downside risks. The international situation and its transmission to the domestic economy are "particularly uncertain." However, participants do have frameworks and models -- this is not Knightian uncertainty.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 30**
Multiple speakers describe falling goods/commodity prices: McTeer lists gasoline, petrochemicals, semiconductors, computers, primary metals, paper, lumber. Steel imports flooding market. Clearly deflationary in goods.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
Not specifically discussed as a separate category by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
McTeer briefly mentions "housing costs are on the rise" but not discussed prominently by 2+ participants.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 45**
Gramlich: TIP premium reflects "a general downgrading of inflation worries." Parry notes "diminished inflation expectations." Broaddus worries about M2 growth as inflation risk but acknowledges bond rates show no increase in inflation expectations. Generally anchored but slightly below target direction.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 72**
Extensively discussed. McDonough: potential credit crunch risk. Spreads "very wide" and "abnormal." Senior loan officers survey shows widespread tightening. Syndicated loan market pulled back. Commercial real estate financing dried up. Not yet a freeze, but well above normal stress.

**EQUITY_STRESS: 62**
Stock market down ~15% from highs. Greenspan: "$3 trillion capital loss." Multiple speakers discuss wealth effects and impact on confidence. Concern about further declines. Some note the decline was expected/desired from overvalued levels (Stern, Jordan), moderating the stress score.

**BANKING_STRESS: 55**
Some stress emerging but banking system still fundamentally sound. Hoenig: rural banks anticipate loan problems. Minehan: banks tightening lending. Senior loan officers survey shows tightening. But Boehne notes banks still competitive with loose terms. LTCM is a hedge fund, not a bank, though the intervention involved major banks.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
Treasury market functioning not discussed as dysfunctional; Treasuries benefiting from flight to quality.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: N/A**
Dollar strength mentioned as a factor in trade deterioration and commodity prices, but not discussed as a standalone concern by 2+ participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 62**
Parry mentions "somewhat higher trend productivity growth" as a disinflationary factor. This reflects the late-1990s productivity boom narrative. Limited discussion but what exists suggests above-trend productivity growth is perceived.
