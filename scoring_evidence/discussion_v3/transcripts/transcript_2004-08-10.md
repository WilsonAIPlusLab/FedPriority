### 2. Era Context
August 2004. The U.S. expansion that began in 2001-2002 had been proceeding, but was encountering a "soft patch." The Committee had begun raising rates at the June 2004 meeting (first hike since 2000) from 1% to 1.25%. Energy prices had surged — oil at ~$45/barrel. The July payroll employment report released just before this meeting was very weak (32,000 jobs added). Core PCE had picked up earlier in 2004 but had begun to moderate. The Committee was debating whether the soft patch was temporary or more persistent. The default prior to this meeting was to continue "measured pace" tightening.

### 3. Dimension-by-Dimension Directional Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation pressure as *modestly above the baseline but contained/moderating*, leaning slightly above neutral. Most participants note that core inflation had picked up earlier in 2004 (surprising to the upside) but had recently moderated. Energy and commodity cost pass-through concerns persist but few indications of broad downstream price increases. Moskow: "core inflation remains well contained." Pianalto: "not hearing anything that would lead me to believe that substantial inflationary pressures are building across a wide range of final goods prices." Guynn notes ongoing cost pass-throughs and service price increases. Kohn sees "less upward risk on inflation." Ferguson: "significant upside outbreak of inflation from this level is unlikely." Bernanke: core measures have moderated, inflation expectations somewhat anchored. The center of gravity: inflation is not alarming but moderately elevated — call it slightly above target with pressures building on the commodity/energy side but core restrained. Score range: 48–55. The earlier upside surprise in core PCE and ongoing cost pressures push above 50; the recent moderation keeps it from being clearly elevated. Score: **52**.

Geithner: "growth moving back to a pace modestly above potential" (implying it is not yet there). Santomero: real GDP growth had slowed below expectations, "temporary lull." ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Moskow: "data suggest that core inflation remains well contained" — yet notes cost pressures (steel, energy, construction materials), "taking longer to resolve," and capacity constraints.
- Pianalto: "I have not been hearing anything that would lead me to believe that substantial inflationary pressures are building across a wide range of final goods prices" — but warns about contract expirations, potential pass-through, and directors losing patience with "transitory" framing.
- Guynn: "I am less willing than some to conclude that most of the price pressures we have seen...are going to turn out to be transitory" — cites trucking price increases of 5-6%, UPS fuel surcharges, rising temp employment rates.
- Kohn: "incoming data have left us with less upward risk on inflation" — inflation expectations well behaved, TIPS long-run compensation 50bp below May peak.

**Center-of-gravity:** Core PCE has recently moderated after earlier upside surprises; cost pressures persist but pass-through is limited; the committee views near-term inflation as contained but not entirely comfortable — slightly above 50 reflecting residual concern and ongoing cost pressures.

**Minority views:** Guynn is the most hawkish on inflation (not comfortable moving inflation to "back burner"); Gramlich notes a permanent energy shock could push core inflation up ~1/3 pp. These push the uncertainty band upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kohn: "the output gap has turned out to be larger than anticipated" and "if the staff is right, the output gap will have closed very little, if at all, since late last year."
- Minehan: "the baseline Greenbook forecast, and ours in Boston as well, sees excess capacity remaining at the end of 2005" — and notes this was before the weak July employment report.
- Geithner: "growth moving back to a pace modestly above potential — perhaps under 4 percent — for the balance of '04" (implying current growth is not yet above potential).

**Center-of-gravity:** Multiple participants explicitly confirm an output gap — below potential — with the gap having widened relative to prior expectations due to Q2 softness and weak payrolls. Not deep recession slack (GDP still growing at ~3.5%), but clearly negative output gap.

**Minority views:** Santomero sees the recent data as a "temporary lull" and expects rebound; Greenspan is more upbeat about July data signals. Both still acknowledge current below-potential conditions.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Santomero: "tri-state unemployment rate edged down to 5.1 percent in the second quarter, and we continue to receive reports that some firms are having difficulty finding qualified workers" — mixed signal; national unemployment at 5.5%.
- Pianalto: "companies indicate that they intend to remain disciplined about their hiring" — health care costs restraining hiring; firms preferring productivity gains over new workers.
- Kohn: "there is slack in the economy" — employment is a good indicator of "whether we're putting people back to work," and he would not ignore employment entirely.
- July payroll: 32,000 jobs created — very weak; multiple participants note this disappointment.

**Center-of-gravity:** Unemployment at 5.5%, payrolls growing slowly, firms still squeezing productivity before hiring — labor market has meaningful slack, below full employment. Not deep recession levels (unemployment not elevated dramatically), but clearly not tight.

**Minority views:** Household survey showed +629,000 in July; Greenspan notes jobs-are-becoming-plentiful Conference Board readings. These don't change the level assessment but provide uncertainty.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 ±7 | Confidence: High**
