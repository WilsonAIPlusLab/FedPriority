# FOMC Transcript Scoring — June 18-19, 2019

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** of the June 18–19, 2019 FOMC meeting (two-day meeting). Speakers are individually identified by name (e.g., MR. BULLARD., MS. DALY., CHAIR POWELL., etc.). The document is segmented and covers: (a) a Day 1 policy discussion on a proposed standing repo facility; (b) an economic go-round (afternoon Day 1); and (c) a policy go-round (morning Day 2). This is a long, rich transcript with numerous participants providing substantive views. Confidence on all scored dimensions will be High or Moderate given the volume of material.

### 2. Era Context
June 2019 sits in the late expansion phase of the post-GFC cycle. The unemployment rate was near 50-year lows (~3.6%), GDP growth had been solid in Q1 (3.1%), but trade tensions had sharply escalated (May 5 re-escalation with China; Mexico tariff threats). PCE inflation was running at ~1.5-1.6% core, well below the 2% target. Inflation expectations had been declining (Michigan survey hit record lows, breakevens fell ~30 bp). The Committee was debating a "patient" posture vs. cutting rates. The era anchor for OUTPUT_GAP would be 50-55 (near potential, late expansion); LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~60-65 (very low unemployment); INFLATION_PRESSURE ~38-42 (chronically below target).

### 3. Dimensional Analysis (Direction Before Score)

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as **below target** — closer to the 38-42 range given chronic undershooting. Evidence from multiple participants: Clarida ("inflation is muted—probably more muted than we would like"), Bullard ("Actual PCE inflation is running below target"), Williams ("core PCE inflation looks to come in at 1.5 percent in May"), Kashkari ("inflation is running below target"), Daly ("headline and core PCE inflation of only 1.5 percent over the past 12 months"), Brainard ("core PCE prices posted a 12-month increase of 1.6 percent, well short of our objective"). Only Bostic and Quarles note trimmed-mean measures near 2%. The center of gravity is clearly below target but not alarmingly so. Score: 35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as **near or slightly above potential**, with growth decelerating toward trend. Multiple participants explicitly discuss "above-trend" growth in H1 2019, with deceleration expected. Brainard: "real GDP growth should come in somewhat above its potential rate in the first half of this year." Quarles: "GDP growth will step down this year...but remain at a rate that's above my estimate of potential." However, Kashkari and Williams see little to no positive output gap given revised-down u* estimates. The median SEP output gap is slightly positive per staff (Williams noted staff estimate is "at the high end or near the high end of models"). Chair Powell: "growth near potential and unemployment near historic lows." Center of gravity: slightly above potential but close to 50. Several participants argued output gap is near-zero. Score: 52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants see labor markets as **tight** — unemployment at 50-year lows (3.6%), though some see it approaching or past full employment. Clarida: "unemployment rate is at 3.8 percent, the most recent reading, at 3.6, is near a 50-year low." Mester: "labor market readings are consistent with continued expansion...unemployment rate has fallen to 3.6 percent, its lowest level in nearly 50 years." Rosengren: "their major complaint is the scarcity of labor rather than the risks from trade" (small businesses). Nearly every participant acknowledges tight/strong labor market. However, Kashkari pushes back: "wage growth is essentially flat" — he sees remaining slack. Most participants are at or past full employment but wage growth not accelerating. Score: 63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: **Split picture** — consumer spending strong, business investment weakening. Clarida: "household consumption, which is 70 percent of the economy, is certainly robust." Brainard: "consumer spending remains robust." Barkin: "I see real strength in the consumer part of the economy." But: "business fixed investment looks to have stalled" (Powell), "we see an outright contraction in business fixed investment this quarter" (Williams). Most participants note the contrast strongly. Net: consumer near trend or above, BFI below trend/contracting. Weighted average tilts slightly below trend given investment drag and the discussion emphasis on business weakness. Score: 46.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants describe financial conditions as **broadly accommodative/neutral** with only marginal tightening since May 5. Brainard: "financial conditions overall remain supportive relative to their historical average and have tightened only marginally in recent weeks." Kaplan: "cost of capital is historically low...access to capital is historically high. Credit spreads are extremely tight." However, 10-year Treasury yields fell ~40 bp, yield curve inverted (3m/10y), which could be read as a loosening signal for long rates. The overall conditions are NOT tight — they are loose to neutral. Score: 35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: **Dovish lean** — majority supports no cut today (alt-B, hold) but with strong forward lean toward cutting. Formal vote: 17 for alt-B (hold), 1 for alt-A (cut — Bullard). Kashkari also voiced preference for alt-A (50bp cut + guidance). So only 1 formal dissent (Bullard), with Kashkari also supporting alt-A verbally. The majority holds but signals easing ahead: 8 participants wrote down rate cuts in their SEP dot. Williams, Evans, Daly, Evans project 2 cuts in 2019. Mester, Rosengren, George, Harker, Barkin, Bostic, Quarles: hold or very limited easing. Chair Powell: "many participants see that a more accommodative rate policy is likely to be appropriate at coming meetings." This is squarely in the 35-45 range: moderate-to-lean dovish. The Committee holds today but broadly signals accommodation ahead. Score: 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: **Moderate** disagreement. One formal dissent (Bullard for alt-A/cut). Kashkari also preferred alt-A (50bp cut). The 17 who voted for alt-B range from those who see cuts coming soon (Williams, Evans, Daly — 2 cuts in 2019 SEP) to those who are skeptical of any need to cut (George, Harker, Quarles, Bostic, Barkin, Mester, Rosengren). The economic assessments diverge: Bullard/Kashkari vs. the hold-and-watch camp. Clear camps exist but the disagreement is more about timing than direction — nearly everyone acknowledges downside risks and the hold was near-consensual in the vote. Score: 42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Financial stability is **discussed but not a dominant topic**. The Day 1 repo facility discussion involves financial stability as a sub-theme (broker-dealer access, moral hazard, resolution planning). In the go-round, Kaplan mentions rising corporate debt and leveraged lending. Brainard mentions "exuberance in financial and credit markets" and elevated risky corporate debt. Daly mentions moral hazard. But this is contextual, not an alarm. The yield curve inversion and recession probability models are discussed as financial signals. Moderate discussion. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: **Easing lean** — forward guidance signals accommodation likely ahead, but data-dependent. The statement removes "patient," adds "act as appropriate." Chair Powell says: "many participants see that a more accommodative rate policy is likely to be appropriate at coming meetings." 8 out of 17 SEP participants wrote down cuts. The forward signal is clearly dovish — pointing toward cuts — but not committing to a specific timeline. This is in the 30-40 range. Score: 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: **Elevated uncertainty** — multiple participants use strong uncertainty language about trade, the outlook, inflation persistence, u* estimates. Rosengren: "bimodal outcomes"; "assigning probability for these two outcomes is quite difficult." Mester: "the policy dot plot is close to bimodal." Daly: "higher probability of hitting the ELB has risen about 17 percentage points." Bullard: "The 'new normal' is persistently higher trade regime uncertainty." Multiple participants note wide error bands on u*, wide range of recession probability estimates (staff model: 67% next year). Williams: "heightened risks of a more significant slowdown...ongoing weakness in inflation indicators." Powell: "we don't have the luxury of waiting for anything approaching certainty." Score: 62.

### 5. Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63): Slight divergence but explainable — labor market is tight at 50-year-low unemployment, but many participants argue the output gap is minimal or zero given revised-down u* estimates. The labor market appears tighter than the output gap for a structural reason (u* revision debate). This is internally coherent with the meeting content.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (38) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (35): Both below 50 (dovish), aligned. The Committee held rates but strongly signaled future cuts. Consistent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (35, loose) vs. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (38, lean-dovish holding): Loose financial conditions would normally reduce the impetus to ease, but inflation shortfall + trade risk drives the dovish lean. Consistent with the discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE

**Score: 35 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Clarida: "wages are rising broadly in line with productivity and underlying inflation; there is no evidence of cost push pressure; and, obviously, inflation is muted—probably more muted than we would like."
- Williams: "the 12-month core PCE inflation looks to come in at 1.5 percent in May...The direction of travel in core inflation from 2 percent in December to 1½ percent today is far from encouraging."
- Kashkari: "Core PCE on a 12-month basis is only running at 1.6 percent. We can continue to point to possible transitory factors, but a range of signals suggests that low inflation is likely to be persistent."

**Center of gravity:** Participants overwhelmingly characterize inflation as running meaningfully below the 2% target, with growing concern that it reflects persistent rather than transitory factors and that inflation expectations are eroding.

**Minority views:** Quarles and Kaplan note that trimmed-mean PCE measures (Dallas, Cleveland) are running near 2%, arguing the headline weakness may be transitory. Mester says Cleveland median PCE at 2.6%. These views inform the ±5 band but do not shift the center.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP

**Score: 52 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Brainard: "real GDP growth should come in somewhat above its potential rate in the first half of this year, and incoming data provide some basis for expecting above-trend growth to continue."
- Powell: "despite some areas that bear close monitoring, the baseline outlook hasn't changed that much, with growth near potential and unemployment near historic lows."
- Clarida: "the economy does appear to be slowing, at least in GDP terms...transitioning from above-trend to trend growth."

**Center of gravity:** The majority of participants characterize the economy as transitioning from slightly above potential to at-trend growth. A minority (Kashkari, Williams) argue the output gap is effectively zero or very small given lower u* estimates.

**Minority views:** Kashkari: "I don't think there's an actual positive output gap." Williams: his u* of 4% implies an output gap "less than half of the staff estimate and could well be close to zero" (Clarida's words). These views bring the score close to 50.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 63 ±5 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Clarida: "unemployment rate is at 3.8 percent, the most recent reading, at 3.6, is near a 50-year low."
- Mester: "the broader U-6 measure fell to 7.1 percent, its lowest level of the cycle...These readings are well below my revised estimate of the longer-run unemployment rate."
- Rosengren: "their major complaint is the scarcity of labor rather than the risks from trade" (small businesses in district).

**Center of gravity:** Universally characterized as a tight labor market with unemployment near 50-year lows; nearly every participant uses language consistent with "at or past full employment." Some disagreement about remaining slack given stagnant wage growth.

**Minority views:** Kashkari argues wage growth is "essentially flat," suggesting slack remains. This shifts the band slightly but does not change the score, as the unemployment rate itself is near-unprecedented low.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

**Score: 46 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Clarida: "household consumption, which is 70 percent of the economy, is certainly robust, and the recent data do indicate a pickup from the soft Q1 data." But: "the drop in business fixed investment projected in this Tealbook is especially something to pay attention to: essentially no contribution from business fixed investment in the second half of the year."
- Williams: "While consumers enjoy their spending spree, the latest indicators suggest that we could see an outright contraction in business fixed investment this quarter, and that's a rare event during an economic expansion."
- Bowman: "Consumer spending has regained momentum following a brief slowdown early in the year." But: "data on orders and shipments of capital goods have also softened in recent months."

**Center of gravity:** Consumer spending robust and above-trend; business investment stalling or contracting. The net weighted assessment across both components lands slightly below trend, driven by the investment drag against strong consumption. Rubric range 46-54 represents "at trend/moderate pace."

**Minority views:** Barkin and Bostic are somewhat more sanguine on business investment momentum continuing. The uncertainty is large.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

**Score: 35 ±6 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- Kaplan: "the cost of capital is historically low, and that access to capital is historically high. Credit spreads are extremely tight."
- Brainard: "The repricing of risk that we have been seeing since the May 5 'pivot' has left overall financial conditions only marginally tighter than in April and still accommodative relative to historical norms."
- Kaplan: "Financial conditions are very strong. Markets remain near their all-time highs."

**Center of gravity:** Participants describe financial conditions as loose to neutral — credit spreads extremely tight, cost of capital historically low. Only marginal tightening since May 5 despite equity volatility and yield curve inversion. Conditions are broadly accommodative.

**Minority views:** The 40 bp drop in 10-year yields and yield curve inversion are noted, but participants frame these more as recession signals than as financial tightening per se.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN

**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Vote outcome: 17 for alt-B (hold), 1 dissent for alt-A (cut) — Bullard.
- Kashkari (verbally supporting alt-A): "I support alternative A at this meeting...My proposal is a 50 basis point cut combined with language that commits us not to raise rates until we actually achieve our 2 percent target."
- Williams: "Conditioned on a policy rate path that includes two rate cuts this year, my forecasts for GDP growth this year and next are little changed."
- Powell: "many participants see that a more accommodative rate policy is likely to be appropriate at coming meetings."

**Center of gravity:** Strong dovish lean with hold today but nearly unanimous recognition that cuts are likely upcoming. 8 of 17 participants wrote rate cuts in their SEP for 2019. The statement drops "patient" and adds "will act as appropriate." Score 38 reflects a moderate dovish lean: majority holds today, but broadly signals and anticipates easing.

**Minority views:** George, Quarles, Harker, Mester, Bostic, Rosengren, Barkin are skeptical of near-term cuts and warn against over-signaling. Their preferences for optionality keep the score from falling below 35.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

**Score: 42 ±8 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Formal dissent: Bullard for alt-A (25bp cut now); Kashkari also vocally preferred alt-A (50bp cut).
- Clear camps: (1) Cut now or soon (Bullard, Kashkari, Evans, Williams, Daly) vs. (2) Hold and wait (Mester, Rosengren, George, Harker, Barkin, Bostic, Quarles, Kaplan, Bowman) vs. (3) Hold today but lean dovish soon (Clarida, Brainard, Powell).
- Mester: "The policy dot plot is close to bimodal" — explicitly noting two distinct camps on the Committee.

**Center of gravity:** One formal dissent (Bullard) plus strong verbal disagreement from Kashkari (alt-A supporter). Clear economic disagreements on the appropriate policy response, inflation persistence, and output gap size. But most agree on holding today, limiting the dissent score. Three camps rather than two dissents keeps this in the 40-50 range.

**Minority views:** Some of the "hold" camp (Rosengren, Mester) are at pains to distance themselves from the easing-leaning members, signaling genuine policy disagreement beyond mere tactical timing.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

**Score: 33 ±8 | Confidence: Moderate**

**Evidence:**
- The Day 1 repo facility discussion extensively covers financial stability sub-themes: moral hazard, broker-dealer access, resolution planning, discount window stigma — but this is a technical/operational discussion, not an assessment of financial system stress.
- Kaplan: "I'm particularly concerned about the relative amount of corporate debt, particularly in triple-B and weaker segments."
- Brainard: "elevated risky corporate debt and associated asset valuations...financial market risk appetite and private-sector leverage are highly pro-cyclical."

**Center of gravity:** Financial stability is mentioned in multiple contexts but is not a dominant topic in the economic/policy discussion. The repo facility discussion is operational (not a distress assessment). Corporate debt concerns are raised by 2-3 participants. Score reflects "limited discussion; a few mention as context" (rubric range 25-35).

**Minority views:** Brainard and Kaplan give the most substantive financial stability commentary; without these it would score lower.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

**Score: 35 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- The statement drops "patient" and adds "will act as appropriate" — a clear dovish shift in forward signal.
- Powell: "many participants see that a more accommodative rate policy is likely to be appropriate at coming meetings."
- Williams: "If...my assessment of the overall outlook does not improve, I would expect that a 25 basis point rate cut will be appropriate at our next meeting in July."
- 8 SEP participants projected rate cuts in 2019; the statement language implicitly validates market expectations of a July cut.

**Center of gravity:** Forward signal is a clear easing lean — "data-dependent" but with a clear tilt toward accommodation. Rubric anchor for 2019 mid-cycle cuts is 35-45. Score of 35 reflects the easing lean while acknowledging data-dependence ("prepared to act if needed" phrasing).

**Minority views:** George, Mester, Harker, and Bostic explicitly caution against the statement being read as committing to a July cut, urging more optionality. This pushes the forward signal toward the 40-45 range rather than 25-30, hence 35.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Score: 62 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Rosengren: "the outlook seems more bimodal at this time...assigning probability for these two outcomes is quite difficult."
- Bullard: "The 'new normal' is persistently higher trade regime uncertainty. Future tariffs are unknown and could disappear or reappear at any time."
- Daly: "the probability of monetary policy being constrained by the ELB three years out has doubled over the past month." San Francisco Fed term structure models indicate "risk of hitting the ELB has risen about 17 percentage points."
- Mester: "The policy dot plot is close to bimodal, with one group of participants believing holding pat on rates is appropriate and another group seeing rate cuts as appropriate."

**Center of gravity:** Multiple participants explicitly characterize the outlook as unusually uncertain — bimodal, difficult to forecast, with wide scenario ranges on trade outcomes and their economic effects. Standard models questioned on u*, output gap. Rubric range 61-75 = "High: many emphasize difficulty; wide scenario range; standard models questioned."

**Minority views:** Bostic and Quarles describe their outlooks as "largely unchanged" — they experience less uncertainty. Barkin wants to be "patient." These moderate the score toward the lower end of the 61-75 range.
