# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1993-11-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speakers identified. The meeting has two main sections: (1) a lengthy procedural discussion about transcript release policy (roughly lines 1-3650), and (2) the economic go-around and policy vote (lines ~3770-4965). Only the economic discussion portion is relevant for scoring.

### Era Context
November 1993: The U.S. is in the early-to-mid expansion following the 1990-91 recession. Unemployment remains elevated (around 6.7-6.8%), GDP growth has been moderate averaging about 3% but with perceived volatility ("sawtooth" pattern). Inflation is around 3% CPI, slowly declining. The fed funds rate is at 3%, a historically accommodative level. Long-term bond rates recently backed up about 40 basis points.

### Speaker Count and Center of Gravity

**Participants providing economic/policy views**: Hoenig, Keehn, Parry, Forrestal, Stern, Broaddus, Boehne, Syron, Jordan, Melzer, Vice Chairman McDonough, McTeer, Mullins, Kelley, LaWare, Angell, Phillips, Lindsey, Chairman Greenspan (~19 speakers).

**Policy vote**: Virtually unanimous for "B" symmetric (hold at current funds rate, symmetric directive). Only Melzer explicitly favored asymmetric (bias toward tightening), and Angell stated the funds rate "really ought to be at 4 percent" but did not formally dissent. Broaddus expressed "a lot of sympathy" for Melzer but voted B symmetric.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Direction: Below midpoint. Participants overwhelmingly report no inflation pressure. Stern: "no inflationary pressure around whatsoever" from business contacts. Boehne: "no sense out there that there's an inflationary problem." Forrestal: "no signs of price increases at all or of wage pressures." Phillips: "continued progress on reducing inflation." Mullins: "Inflation still seems to be on a marginally downward path." Parry expects even less inflation than the Greenbook. CPI running about 3%, seen as slowly declining. Some minority concern: Jordan says inflation progress is "unacceptable" (too slow), Melzer notes 3% CPI "can hardly be viewed as representing price stability." But the center of gravity clearly sees inflation as subdued and declining. Score should be in the 30-38 range -- below target, slack keeping prices in check, disinflation ongoing. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low/declining inflation. Score below 50 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Direction: Below potential. Multiple references to "slack" in the economy. Kelley: "We still have slack in the economy." Mullins: "we still have our measure of slack" and "outstanding slack." Greenbook assumes "continued slack in labor and product markets." Growth is moderate at about 2.5-3%, with some participants seeing risk of stronger growth but none claiming the economy is above potential. Melzer says economy "appears poised for a significant period of average growth at or above potential" -- forward looking, not current assessment. Score should be around 41-44 -- slightly below potential, approaching but gap persists. Bright-line rule: "slack remains" language = must be below 50. Confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Direction: Below full employment. Unemployment around 6.7-6.8% (and may go to 7%+ with BLS measurement change). Boehne: "employment growth is flat to very modest." Phillips: "Job growth is not strong enough to lower unemployment." Forrestal: "no signs of wage pressures." Parry: "labor market slack of this size." However, Melzer notes some areas report "shortages of qualified workers" and unemployment rates in some states at or below national average. Center of gravity sees labor market as having significant slack. Score around 35-38. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Slack/high unemployment = below 50. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Direction: Moderately positive, near or slightly above trend. Multiple speakers note strong consumer spending, robust housing, strengthening business investment. Keehn: retail sales "fantastic" with 20% total gains. Forrestal: "Consumer spending has been quite good." Mullins: "a very traditional pattern of interest-sensitive spending emerging: business investment spending and consumer spending on durables and housing." But concerns that consumer spending outrunning income is unsustainable. Overall impression: solid momentum, somewhat above average trend. Score around 55-58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Direction: Loose to somewhat loose. Phillips: "The financial sector is strong and able to assist in financing so we're not having bottlenecks of credit unavailability either in the direct financing markets or through the banking system." McTeer: "not much talk of a credit crunch anymore" and "too many banks were aggressively seeking" loan demand. Mullins: policy stance is "accommodative." Long bond backed up 40bp but from very low levels. Fed funds at 3%. Credit expansion is modest but conditions are loose. Score around 28-33 -- loose/accommodative conditions. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Loose conditions = low score. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Direction: Balanced to very slightly hawkish lean. Current action is unanimous hold ("B symmetric"). But several participants flag the need to think about tightening ahead. Mullins gives extended discussion about "when to switch from accommodative to neutral." Melzer explicitly wants asymmetric bias toward tightening. Angell says funds rate "really ought to be at 4 percent." Broaddus says he has "a lot of sympathy" for Melzer. But the overwhelming majority votes B symmetric with no urgency. Greenspan calls it "a gimme putt." Score around 48-50 -- hold steady, very slight hawkish undercurrent but no action warranted. Actually slightly more hawkish given the forward-looking comments from several members. Score 50-52.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Only Melzer formally preferred asymmetric. Angell expressed preference for higher rates but didn't formally dissent. Broaddus sympathized with Melzer but voted B symmetric. Jordan had philosophical objections to the Greenbook framing. Very broad agreement on the action. Score around 20-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion. LaWare raises concern about potential market fragility: "an opportunity here for us to blow an aneurism in this economy through its most fragile part, and that's the financial markets." Mullins discusses bond market dynamics at some length. Angell discusses equity market vulnerability. But these are 2-3 speakers, and it is not a dominant theme. Score around 15-20 -- brief/limited mentions, consistent with 1993 era anchor of 1-10 but slightly more given LaWare's explicit concern. Score 18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive = neutral. But several speakers (Mullins, Angell, Melzer, Broaddus) signal that tightening may be needed in coming months/quarters. Mullins: "the current favorable outlook for stronger growth does provide a credible environment in which to consider such a move." Angell: funds rate "really ought to be at 4 percent." Greenbook itself assumes tightening later in 1994. Still, no explicit signal or commitment. Score around 53-57 -- very mild tightening lean in forward signal. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Mild tightening lean = slightly above 50. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Moderate level. Several note "sawtooth" patterns making forecasting hard. Stern: business contacts think "expansion is very fragile and could easily be disrupted." Boehne: sawtooth pattern, can't be sure of breakout. Syron: "how much of this is a long-term shift and how much a short-term variation." But overall, most are fairly confident in moderate growth continuing. Kelley reports FAC saw risks on the downside. Standard caveats. Score around 35-40.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (36): Both below 50, labor somewhat lower reflecting more discussion of labor slack. Consistent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (51) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Both near neutral with slight hawkish lean. Consistent -- hold now but thinking about future tightening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. STERN: "they were pretty much of one mind that there is no inflationary pressure around whatsoever. And they were quite vociferous about that."
- MR. BOEHNE: "There just is no sense out there that there's an inflationary problem or that prices are rising; it's quite to the contrary."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "we have no signs of price increases at all or of wage pressures"
- MR. MULLINS: "Inflation still seems to be on a marginally downward path. The fundamentals, including a disinflationary world economic environment, support the prospect for some additional progress."

Center-of-gravity: Participants overwhelmingly perceive inflation as subdued at around 3% CPI and declining, with broad slack preventing price pressures. This is a disinflation environment with no current inflation concern.

Minority views: Melzer and Jordan argue that 3% is not acceptable and that stimulative policy will eventually feed inflation, but they do not claim current inflation is elevated. Widens uncertainty band modestly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. KELLEY: "We still have slack in the economy, although capacity utilization may have to come off of that list with this latest report. But certainly [we have slack] in labor markets."
- MR. MULLINS: "we still have our measure of slack" and "outstanding slack"
- MR. PRELL (cited by Jordan): "With continued slack in labor and product markets, core inflation..."

Center-of-gravity: Economy is growing at moderate pace (2.5-3%) but still operating below potential with meaningful slack remaining. Capacity utilization at 82%, not yet binding.

Minority views: Melzer sees economy "poised for a significant period of average growth at or above potential" -- a forward-looking view, not a current assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. PARRY: "the unemployment rate is likely to remain at roughly the present level" and "labor market slack of this size"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "Job growth is not strong enough to lower unemployment"
- MR. BOEHNE: "employment growth is flat to very modest"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "no signs of... wage pressures"

Center-of-gravity: Unemployment around 6.7-6.8%, well above full employment. Slack in labor markets is the consensus view. Job growth is weak and not generating wage pressure.

Minority views: Melzer's district reports "some areas report shortages of qualified workers" but this is localized, not national.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. KEEHN: "total sales were up 20 percent and on a [same] store basis they were up 12 percent... the level of confidence of retailers and consumers is building"
- MR. MULLINS: "a very traditional pattern of interest-sensitive spending emerging: business investment spending and consumer spending on durables and housing"
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Consumer spending, for example, has been quite good... Residential construction is quite strong"
- MS. PHILLIPS: "We have low interest rates; we're seeing pretty good business investment... Consumers are spending"

Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending and investment are moderately above trend, driven by housing, durables, and business investment. Multiple speakers report robust retail and housing activity. However, concerns about sustainability since spending outpaces income growth.

Minority views: Concerns that consumer spending cannot be sustained without stronger income growth temper the score slightly.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MS. PHILLIPS: "The financial sector is strong and able to assist in financing so we're not having bottlenecks of credit unavailability either in the direct financing markets or through the banking system."
- MR. MCTEER: "not much talk of a credit crunch anymore" and bankers noted "too many banks were aggressively seeking to fill that demand"
- MR. MULLINS: "we maintain an accommodative stance" and "the initial force of monetary ease clearly went to financial repair and it's increasingly evident that we're turning the corner"

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. Credit is available, banks are competing for loan demand, financial sector is strong. Fed funds at 3% is historically accommodative. Long bond backed up 40bp but still at relatively low levels.

Minority views: LaWare worries about potential financial market fragility, but this is about a possible future shock, not current tightness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 51 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "a symmetrical, no change directive until the next meeting is a 'gimme putt.'"
- MR. MULLINS: "when considering when to switch from accommodative to neutral... the current favorable outlook for stronger growth does provide a credible environment in which to consider such a move"
- MR. MELZER: "I favor alternative 'B' with a bias toward a greater degree of reserve restraint"
- MR. ANGELL: "the fed funds rate is at 3 percent and it really ought to be at 4 percent"

Center-of-gravity: Near-unanimous support for holding steady at current rates with symmetric directive. But a meaningful minority (Melzer, Angell, with sympathy from Broaddus and Mullins) signals that tightening should be considered soon. The center of gravity is balanced with the slightest hawkish tilt.

Minority views: Melzer and Angell explicitly hawkish; most others are comfortable with the current stance for now.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Only MR. MELZER preferred asymmetric: "I favor alternative 'B' with a bias toward a greater degree of reserve restraint"
- MR. ANGELL expressed preference for higher rates but did not formally dissent from B symmetric
- MR. BROADDUS: "I have a lot of sympathy for what Tom just said but for now 'B' symmetric"
- All others voted B symmetric without reservation

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on the policy action. One member wanted asymmetric bias, one expressed desire for higher rates, one expressed sympathy. No formal dissent in the vote. Minor emphasis differences on the outlook.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. LAWARE: "I think there is an opportunity here for us to blow an aneurism in this economy through its most fragile part, and that's the financial markets... we could have a serious contraction in both the equity market and the bond market"
- MR. MULLINS: extended discussion of bond market dynamics and the 40bp backup in long rates
- MR. ANGELL: brief discussion of equity market potential and bond market behavior

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability is not a major topic. LaWare gives the most explicit warning about market fragility, and Mullins discusses bond market moves, but most participants do not address financial stability as a concern. Consistent with the 1993 era where this was typically a low-emphasis topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. MULLINS: "when considering when to switch from accommodative to neutral I would note that the current favorable outlook for stronger growth does provide a credible environment in which to consider such a move"
- MR. ANGELL: "the fed funds rate is at 3 percent and it really ought to be at 4 percent... I'd like to have a little better opportunity for timing than doing it today"
- MR. MELZER: "the funds rate must at a minimum be allowed to move up with market interest rates over time"
- MR. STERN: "I have some sympathy for the issue of how we are going to know when the right time to deviate from this path is"

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is mildly hawkish. While the current decision is firmly hold, several participants signal that tightening is on the horizon, perhaps in early-to-mid 1994. The symmetric directive means no explicit bias, but the discussion tone leans toward eventual tightening.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 37 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- MR. STERN: business contacts "continue to think that the expansion is very fragile and could easily be disrupted"
- MR. BOEHNE: "sawtooth pattern" makes it difficult to distinguish trend from noise
- MR. JORDAN: "when I look at the nominal and the real GDP [forecast]... I think the Greenbook is wrong. I just have no idea in which direction or how big"
- MR. KELLEY: FAC members "were almost unanimous in seeing the risks on the down side, not the up side"

Center-of-gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty reflecting the "sawtooth" recovery pattern and difficulty distinguishing signal from noise. However, most participants are reasonably confident in moderate growth continuing, and Greenspan calls the policy decision "a gimme putt." This is somewhat above normal hedging but not notably elevated.
