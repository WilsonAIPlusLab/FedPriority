# FOMC Transcript Scoring — June 22–23, 2010

---

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### 1. Document Type
This is a **transcript** from the June 22–23, 2010 FOMC meeting. Individual participants speak in their own voice, identified by name (MR. PLOSSER, MS. YELLEN, CHAIRMAN BERNANKE, etc.). The document contains three segments: (a) a balance-sheet/asset-sales discussion from Day 1, (b) the economic go-around from Day 1 and Day 2 (participants' District and national assessments), and (c) the policy go-round from Day 2. This is a full, two-day meeting with extensive participant coverage — well over 1,000 words.

### 2. Era Context
June 2010 is mid-post-GFC recovery. The US is approximately one year into economic recovery from the 2008–2009 trough. Unemployment stands near 9.7%. The European sovereign debt crisis (Greece, Portugal, Spain) has intensified over the intermeeting period. Rates are at the ZLB (effective zero), and the FOMC holds a large balance sheet from prior LSAPs. The expected macro backdrop: meaningful output gap, substantial slack, below-target inflation trending lower, no tightening imminent.

### 3. Core Dimension Pre-Scoring Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE:**
Direction: Participants see inflation as clearly *below* target and trending lower — closer to disinflation territory than to target.
Evidence: Pianalto states median CPI dropped to 0.5% y/y in May, "an all-time low." Yellen expects core PCE to rise "about ¾ percent this year." Kohn says "inflation has continued to decline, with deceleration across a broad array of categories." Rosengren explicitly raises deflation risk, citing Spain, Ireland, Japan. Fisher notes trimmed mean index "continuing to diminish and show less inflationary pressure." Bernanke summarizes: "Inflation remains low and trending down." Plosser notes "prices remained subdued" and prices paid index turned negative. The center of gravity is firmly in disinflation territory, with a minority (Plosser, Bullard) worried about medium-term upside from low rates, and a minority (Rosengren, Dudley) worried about deflation risk. Warsh notes benign near-term data and sees no deflation spiral. The **center of gravity** is clear disinflation / significantly below target.
Score direction: Well below 50. Using anchor: 2009 deflation fears ~15-20; this is 2010 recovery with below-target but not crisis-level deflation. Range 20–28.
Score: **24**

**OUTPUT_GAP:**
Direction: Participants see the economy as *significantly below* potential. Recovery is proceeding but at a moderate pace; unemployment is ~9.7% and all participants describe substantial slack.
Evidence: Kohn: "substantial margins of slack and associated highly competitive conditions." Yellen: "The economy is operating far below maximum sustainable employment." Bernanke summary: "labor market is recovering, but painfully slowly." Multiple participants note unemployment won't fall below 8% until end of 2011 or later. Everyone expects recovery to "proceed" but below trend for years. Kocherlakota: "output gap created by wage and price rigidities." Warsh: "low level of output and very, very slow job creation."
Bright-line rule: "below potential" / "substantial slack" language → score MUST be < 50.
Era anchor: 2010-14 recovery period ~35-42.
Score: **33**

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Labor market is deeply loose — unemployment ~9.7%, slow hiring, little wage pressure, massive long-term unemployment.
Evidence: Yellen notes 46% of unemployed out of work 27+ weeks; expects unemployment to end year at ~9.5% declining to 8.5% at end of 2011. Kocherlakota: "I do not see unemployment falling below 8 percent until after the end of 2011." Bernanke summarizes: "new hires and UI claims were disappointing." Plosser: only 30% of firms expect to hire; employment index turned negative in June. Kohn: "substantial margins of slack." No participant mentions wage pressure from tightness (only health care and taxes as cost drivers, which are structural, not cyclical tightness).
Era anchor: 2011-14 range ~35-40. This is 2010, slightly worse.
Score: **28**

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT:**
Direction: Below trend, sluggish. Recovery in some sectors (autos, manufacturing, software) but overall moderate and fragile. Consumer spending shows mixed signals; housing weakening post-tax credit; equipment/software strong but expected to slow.
Evidence: Yellen: "most recent data suggest some slowdown in consumer spending... major retailers report spending is flat or growing at only a very modest pace." Fisher: "inventory correction... legs are tired... pace has slowed down." Dudley: "soft employment and consumption figures indicate that this positive multiplier story hasn't taken hold with any vigor." Kohn: "bank credit continues to be extraordinarily weak." Bernanke: "residential investment... not going to be repeated... housing is going to continue to be a drag." Some strength in autos, steel, manufacturing, but equipment/software likely to slow. Tarullo: "consumer spending, manufacturing, and investment in equipment and software remain solid" but hedged by "it seems less clear that self-sustaining economic activity will fully replace the waning effects of stimulus."
Net assessment: below trend, sluggish, but not contracting. Range 35-42 fits.
Score: **38**

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS:**
Direction: Conditions have tightened over the intermeeting period — specifically due to European sovereign debt concerns, risk-off sentiment, wider spreads for some borrowers. However, conditions are not crisis-tight; bank credit quality is improving and U.S. money market stress is a risk, not a reality yet.
Evidence: Dudley: "financial conditions have tightened" due to greater risk aversion and European banking exposure. Rosengren: U.S. money market funds "increasingly concerned about exposure in Europe." Yellen: "turmoil in Europe with resulting deterioration in financial conditions." Bernanke: "lower stock prices, the stronger dollar, and somewhat tighter corporate credit are negatives." But also: Duke notes credit quality improving, delinquencies peaking; Evans says funding availability increased for better-quality borrowers; Yellen notes "no signs of such excessive leverage or speculation."
Overall: somewhat tight/elevated risk aversion, but not crisis-level. The move over intermeeting period was toward tighter. Range 52-62.
Score: **57**

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN:**
Direction: Strongly dovish. Near-unanimous support for maintaining current accommodative stance (alternative B, hold rates at ZLB, keep "extended period" language). The key debates are about whether to do *more* accommodation vs. beginning normalization, with the large majority firmly against any tightening and a small minority (Hoenig, Plosser, Lacker, Bullard with caveats) pushing for eventual normalization signals.
Evidence: Rosengren: "I view any tightening through language or asset sales as moving in the wrong direction." Yellen: "risks associated with tightening policy prematurely as extremely high." Kohn: "I'm not any closer to tightening than we were two months ago." Dudley: "would not want to do anything that would be or could be construed as a monetary policy tightening." Kocherlakota, Evans, Pianalto, Warsh, Duke, Tarullo all favor alt B. Chairman Bernanke aligns with status quo.
Hoenig dissent: "I think it's very important that we take this 'extended period' language out" — advocating for normalization signals. This is a solo minority position.
Plosser/Lacker: "can live with" alt B but express concern about too-long accommodation.
Rubric anchor: QE era 2010-12 ~15-25; this is a hold meeting with strong dovish lean, slight consideration of more QE. Score ~20-25.
Formal vote: Hoenig dissents (he does push for removal of extended period language). This is the only formal dissent clearly articulated in the policy go-round.
Score: **22**

**DISSENT_INTENSITY:**
Direction: Moderate disagreement. There is clear separation into camps: a large majority favoring maintaining accommodation with strong dovish bias, versus a small but vocal minority (Hoenig primarily, with Plosser/Lacker/Bullard less intensely) favoring normalization signals and more active balance sheet management. There is also internal debate within the dovish camp about whether to consider more QE.
Evidence: Hoenig explicitly calls for removing "extended period" language — a substantial dissent. Lacker/Plosser push for balance sheet normalization independent of rate policy. Bullard argues LIFO policy and that QE should be used actively. Kocherlakota stands between camps. On the opposite dovish flank, Rosengren explicitly considers alt A language.
Rubric: "clear camps with different prescriptions; multiple dissents; sharp exchanges" = 61-75. But this is mostly one formal dissentor (Hoenig), with 3-4 others expressing reservations — more like "notable disagreement: distinct camps; 1-2 formal dissents" = 46-60.
Formal dissent: Hoenig. Reservations: Plosser, Lacker, Bullard, Warsh.
Score: **48**

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS:**
Direction: Moderate-to-prominent. Financial stability gets substantial discussion: the European sovereign debt situation is central to the outlook, the financial stability memos are praised repeatedly, stress test discussions for European banks, money market fund exposures, bank capital/Basel discussions, retail investor behavior. Multiple participants address it meaningfully and at length.
Evidence: Bernanke explicitly asked for a financial stability section in the presentation. Yellen: "macro prudential surveillance." Warsh gives detailed financial market analysis (institutional investors "lurching," retail investor retreat, bank profits, volatility of volatility). Dudley discusses Basel III capital, regulatory reform, rating agency risks. Duke provides extensive banking sector assessment. Tarullo describes "sharply renewed vulnerabilities and an associated flight to quality." Multiple mentions of European bank stress tests.
This clearly exceeds "limited discussion" (25-35) and reaches "moderate discussion" to "prominent" (40-65). Given the length and breadth of financial discussion, and the chairman explicitly commending the financial stability memos, score: **55**

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE:**
Direction: Strong easing / "low for long" signal. The "extended period" language is explicitly retained with strong support. Multiple participants argue for not even appearing to tighten. Rosengren considers whether alt A language (strengthening the "will" vs "likely to warrant") would be preferable. The forward signal is very dovish.
Evidence: Yellen: "I strongly support maintaining the 'extended period' language." Dudley: "want to reinforce" the sequential language (language, then IOER, then asset sales). Bernanke: "we should remain at the zero bound at least for now." The formal outcome is retention of "extended period" language with slight moderation of first paragraph language.
Rubric anchor: 2010-12 QE era with "extended period" language = 8-15. Strong easing bias forward signal.
Score: **12**

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED:**
Direction: Elevated uncertainty. The European sovereign debt crisis has materially increased uncertainty over the intermeeting period. Multiple participants note the outlook is harder to forecast, tail risks have increased, and the range of scenarios has widened significantly.
Evidence: Lockhart: "quite remarkable change since the last meeting—risk is higher, and uncertainty is a whole lot greater." Kocherlakota: "the level of uncertainty around my forecast has increased since the last FOMC meeting." Pianalto: "the uncertainties are too great to adjust our policy in either direction." Bernanke: "increasing... downside risks." Warsh describes "bouts of financial turmoil, mixed data, and new questions about the future of the European project." Kohn notes "greater uncertainty and downside risks."
This is more than "somewhat elevated" (31-45) — it is distinctly "elevated" with multiple participants specifically citing unusual uncertainty beyond normal. Range 50-65.
Score: **58**

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 24 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Pianalto: "Core inflation, as measured by the Cleveland Fed's median CPI, continues to drift lower. It dropped to 0.5 percent on a year-over-year basis in May, and that's an all-time low."
- Yellen: "I expect core PCE prices to rise about ¾ percent this year and a little below 1 percent next year." / "inflation is undesirably low and trending lower."
- Rosengren: "I am worried that we may be underestimating the risk of deflation and that we are placing too much weight on well-anchored expectations at a time when we are seeing disinflation in wages and declines in a surprising number of components of the CPI."
- Kohn: "inflation has continued to decline, with deceleration across a broad array of categories." / "I had headline inflation staying at about 1 percent for the next three years."
- Bernanke (summary): "Inflation remains low and trending down... Concerns about disinflation, or even deflation, have increased somewhat in the Committee."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Virtually all participants agree inflation is well below target (sub-1% core PCE), trending downward, with growing concern about disinflation risk and explicit deflation risk warnings from several participants.

**Minority views:** Plosser expects inflation to increase gradually and believes the Committee needs to start raising rates in H2 2010 to prevent future inflation. Warsh says "it doesn't seem to me as though we are all that close to a downward price spiral." Bullard worries about medium-term inflation from prolonged ZLB. These widen the uncertainty band but do not change the center of gravity.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 33 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Yellen: "The economy is operating far below maximum sustainable employment." / "significant downside risks to the outlook have once again materialized."
- Kohn: "substantial margins of slack and associated highly competitive conditions in labor and product markets."
- Bernanke (summary): "Most participants, however, continued to expect a moderate recovery into next year, although downside risks to growth have increased somewhat."
- Dudley: "The bottom line for me is that growth is likely to be moderate, with substantial risk to the downside at a time that inflation is already too low rather than too high."
- Kocherlakota: "Unemployment is likely to be unacceptably high for at least two to three years."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants place the economy well below potential, with moderate recovery under way but substantial slack persisting for years; era anchor of 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery applies, with a 2010 score at the lower end given the early stage.

**Minority views:** Plosser and Lacker are somewhat more optimistic, expecting GDP growth of ~3.5% and a faster return to trend — they score the gap as somewhat smaller, but still below potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±6 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**
- Kocherlakota: "In May, national unemployment was 9.7 percent, and private sector employment growth was small. I do not see unemployment falling below 8 percent until after the end of 2011."
- Yellen: "I now anticipate that the unemployment rate will end this year at about 9½ percent, declining only to 8½ percent at the end of 2011 and will take a couple of additional years to return to full employment." / "46 percent of the unemployed had been without a job for at least 27 weeks."
- Plosser: "only 30 percent expected to hire additional workers... Important reasons cited by firms for not hiring were concerns about future employment costs."
- Bernanke: "We have noted some changes in job-finding rates, as reflected, for example, in the Beveridge curve, which has shifted somewhat adversely."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants confirm unemployment at ~9.7% with a multi-year trajectory of only gradual improvement; labor market is far from full employment and hiring is constrained by both cyclical slack and structural concerns.

**Minority views:** Plosser is more optimistic about employment trajectory; he expects growth to pick up. This does not alter the tightness assessment but slightly narrows the uncertainty band.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 ±7 | Confidence: High**

**Evidence:**

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending and investment have been moderate but mixed; leading indicators suggest deceleration ahead, with housing clearly weakening post-tax credit, inventories exhausted, and business investment uncertain; overall below trend.
