# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 1993-02-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It is an early-era (1993) transcript with a somewhat unusual structure: the first ~800 lines are almost entirely devoted to a debate about M2 target ranges for the Humphrey-Hawkins testimony, followed by a brief policy go-around (lines ~828-1130), a short policy discussion/vote (lines ~1131-1300), and then a lengthy discussion about FOMC transparency/confidentiality procedures (lines ~1300-1864). The economic substance is relatively thin compared to later meetings.

### Era Context
February 1993: The U.S. is in the early stages of a sluggish recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The "jobless recovery" theme is prominent. Inflation has been declining (disinflation era). The fed funds rate is at 3%, a historically low level. Commercial real estate is a major drag. GDP growth has been around 3-3.5% in Q3-Q4 1992, but job growth is weak. The Clinton administration has just taken office.

### Speakers in the Economic/Policy Discussion
Key speakers on economic conditions and policy: Chairman Greenspan, Governor Angell, President McTeer, President Forrestal, President Boehne, President Parry, President Stern, President Broaddus, Governor LaWare, President Syron, President Jordan, President Keehn, President Melzer, President Hoenig, Governor Phillips, Governor Mullins, Governor Lindsey, Governor Kelley, Vice Chairman Corrigan.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as below target / declining (disinflation). The entire M2 discussion is framed around lowering ranges consistent with "progress that has been made in bringing inflation down" (Melzer, line 218-219). Parry notes the adjustment is "consistent with our prior inflation goals and doesn't represent any attempt on our part to increase the pace of disinflation" (line 196-198). Forrestal wants to "stress the performance of the economy and the improvements in inflation" (line 634-635).

However, Governor Angell is the notable outlier raising alarm about commodity prices: "you will see on that bottom chart for all commodities ex-food and ex-oil a price move that is somewhat of a preliminary indication" (lines 947-948), lumber up 43%, and warns about "getting behind the curve" (line 977). Melzer has "sympathy for what Wayne said" and notes "if the Blue Chip forecast is right, it's probably a big problem" regarding inflation (lines 1081-1082). Broaddus also has "a lot of sympathy for Governor Angell's comments" (line 1039).

Center of gravity: Most participants view inflation as subdued/declining. A minority (Angell, with Melzer and Broaddus sympathetic) see early warning signs. Score should be in the 30-38 range — below target, disinflation, but not deflationary concern.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says mostly subdued with a minority worried. Score ~35.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as below potential but recovering. Greenspan: "we are beginning to see some evidence that overall investment is beginning to move" (lines 832-833) but "the commercial real estate problem is having a larger macroeconomic effect than I think we fully realized" (lines 858-859). He calls it "the one remaining major factor holding the recovery back" (line 899). The discussion about weak job growth is prominent — Angell references "the lack of job growth" (line 43). Mullins notes "we're still going to hear the criticism... that we are holding back the pace of this recovery" (lines 310-311). The recovery has had "two quarters of 3-1/2 percent GDP growth" (line 313) but it's a "jobless recovery." Corrigan says "a soft landing in the economy has been achieved with a degree of success and precision that is almost beyond the human mind's capacity to imagine" (lines 728-729).

The economy is growing but below potential — slack remains, commercial real estate drag, weak employment. Score should be in the 38-42 range.

Bright-line check: Slack language present ("holding the recovery back"), so must be < 50. Score ~40.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor market is slack. The "jobless recovery" is a recurring theme. Angell: "the lack of job growth that politically is desired" (lines 42-43). Greenspan: "the employment issue might be taking hold" (line 912) — tentative and forward-looking. Unemployment at 7.1% in early 1993. No participant describes labor tightness; all references are to insufficient job growth.

Score should be in the 30-35 range — below full employment, slack remains, unemployment elevated.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor. Evidence says slack. Score ~32.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Mixed but improving. Greenspan: "overall investment is beginning to move. We're seeing it obviously in capital goods; we're seeing it in residential construction" (lines 832-834). Also: "the turnover in existing homes and the realized capital gains... coupled with the refinancing, are making a major contribution. But that can't go on indefinitely" (lines 850-853). He notes low saving rate questions and says growth must be "investment-led" (line 840). Commercial real estate is dead. GDP has been growing at 3-3.5% for two quarters but there are headwinds.

Center of gravity: Moderate/improving but not robust. Below trend but recovering. Score ~42-45.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says moderate, somewhat below trend but picking up. Score ~43.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are mixed. On one hand, rates are very low (fed funds at 3%), mortgage refinancing is booming, and there's liquidity. On the other hand, Greenspan devotes significant attention to commercial bank lending being "dull" (line 871), banks voluntarily raising capital ratios due to commercial real estate uncertainty, and credit not flowing to small/medium businesses. Lindsey notes "more bank aggressiveness, not less, going on" (line 170-171). The yield curve is steep (discussed in context of M2 velocity).

Center of gravity: Conditions are somewhat loose on rate/market side but credit availability is constrained by bank balance sheet issues. Net effect is roughly neutral to slightly tight on the credit side, but accommodative on the rate side. Score ~45.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence is mixed — low rates but impaired credit channels. Score ~45.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: Overwhelmingly in favor of holding steady ("B" symmetric = maintain current 3% fed funds rate). Nearly every participant votes "B symmetric." The only hawkish voices: Angell says "I'm inclined to believe that we ought to be thinking about tightening at this stage. But I just don't have the stomach for doing it" and prefers "asymmetric toward tightness" (lines 938-939, 974). Melzer favors "B asymmetric toward tightening" but "could support symmetry" (line 1077). Jordan wants to "find a way to do something to avoid a contraction of M2" (which is arguably dovish). The vote is unanimous for "B" symmetric.

Center of gravity: Hold steady, symmetric. The tiny hawkish minority (Angell, Melzer) prefer asymmetry toward tightening but don't dissent. This is a balanced/hold-steady meeting with a very mild hawkish lean from a few. Score ~50-52.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold steady with faint hawkish undercurrent. Score ~51.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Very low disagreement on the policy decision. The vote on "B" symmetric appears unanimous. On the M2 ranges, there was some variety of views (Angell wanted 1-5%, Lindsey wanted 2.5-5.5%, Syron wanted wider range, Keehn preferred no change, Broaddus wanted full point) but they all deferred to the Chairman. No dissents.

Score ~20-25. Broad agreement with minor variations on the M2 range question.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Greenspan's discussion of commercial real estate as a drag on financial intermediaries (lines 858-910) is the main financial stability content. He discusses bank lending impairment, nonperforming loans, uncertain collateral values, insurance companies. But this is framed as an economic drag rather than systemic risk. No other participants really pick up this theme substantially. Broaddus mentions "the bond market would punish us severely" (line 433) regarding M2 ranges — market reaction concern but not financial stability per se.

Score ~15-20. Some discussion by Greenspan about bank/CRE issues, but not a widespread financial stability focus among participants.

Anchor check: 1993 typical is 1-10. Greenspan's CRE/banking discussion warrants slightly above the era typical. Score ~18.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: Symmetric directive = no directional signal. Greenspan: "I find it hard to think of any reason why we would want to [change the stance of policy] over the next 6-week period" (lines 916-917). This is neutral/hold. Angell and Melzer hint at eventual need to tighten, but the consensus forward signal is neutral/data-dependent.

Score ~50. Neutral.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says neutral with faint hawkish undercurrent from 2 members. Score ~50.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Significant uncertainty expressed, mainly about M2/velocity relationships. Multiple participants: Greenspan notes "statistical risks of the first-quarter GDP forecast are on the up side" (line 418). Angell: "there's just too much uncertainty here" (line 38-39). Kelley: "large uncertainties, vitally important unknowables" (line 750). Keehn: "the uncertainties are very, very significant" (lines 507-508). The entire M2 discussion is about the unreliability of monetary aggregates. But the uncertainty is more about the M2/velocity relationship than about the fundamental economic outlook.

Score ~40-45. Somewhat elevated uncertainty, mainly around money/velocity, with standard economic uncertainty as well.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both below 50, consistent — economy below potential with slack labor market. Labor scored lower because the "jobless recovery" is a stronger theme than the output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (51) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50): Both near neutral, consistent — holding steady with no strong directional signal.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. MELZER: "I think the way to deal with Bob's point on inflation may simply be to say something along the lines of: 'Just recognize the progress that has been made in bringing inflation down.'" (lines 217-219)
- MR. PARRY: "the adjustment is really consistent with our prior inflation goals and doesn't represent any attempt on our part to increase the pace of disinflation" (lines 196-198)
- MR. ANGELL (minority): "you will see on that bottom chart for all commodities ex-food and ex-oil a price move... I do want to call the Committee's attention to what I think is a possibility of our getting behind the curve" (lines 947-948, 976-977)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Most participants view inflation as well-behaved and declining, with disinflation progress acknowledged. A small minority (Angell, with sympathy from Melzer and Broaddus) sees early commodity price signals of potential future inflation pressure.

**Minority views:** Angell's commodity-price warnings (lumber up 43%, broad commodity index rising) push the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are beginning to see some evidence that overall investment is beginning to move. We're seeing it obviously in capital goods; we're seeing it in residential construction" (lines 832-834)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the commercial real estate problem is having a larger macroeconomic effect than I think we fully realized... this [sector] seems to me to be the one remaining major factor holding the recovery back" (lines 858-859, 898-899)
- MR. MULLINS: "even if the economy performs well... if real GDP is 3 percent, we're still going to hear the criticism... that we are holding back the pace of this recovery" (lines 305-311)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is recovering (two quarters of 3.5% GDP growth) but still operating below potential, with significant headwinds from commercial real estate and weak job creation.

**Minority views:** Corrigan is more optimistic ("soft landing... achieved with a degree of success and precision that is almost beyond the human mind's capacity to imagine"), while Greenspan emphasizes ongoing CRE drag.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ANGELL: "the lack of job growth that politically is desired" (lines 42-43)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the employment issue might be taking hold" (line 912) — tentative, forward-looking
- MR. MULLINS: references to "the kind of job growth that politically is desired" and ongoing criticism about insufficient recovery pace (lines 310-313)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is clearly slack with the "jobless recovery" a recurring theme. Unemployment remains elevated and job creation is insufficient, though Greenspan tentatively suggests employment may be starting to improve.

**Minority views:** None substantially diverge — all acknowledge weak labor conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "overall investment is beginning to move. We're seeing it obviously in capital goods; we're seeing it in residential construction. And I suspect inventories may be preparing to move" (lines 832-835)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the turnover in existing homes and the realized capital gains... coupled with the refinancing, are making a major contribution. But that can't go on indefinitely" (lines 850-853)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we really cannot continue to get moderate real growth unless it's investment-led" (lines 840-841)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Investment is beginning to pick up (capital goods, residential construction) and consumption is sustained by refinancing/home turnover, but the overall level remains somewhat below trend with commercial real estate dead and sustainability questioned.

**Minority views:** Limited disagreement on economic assessment; discussion focused more on M2 technicalities.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the uncertain values in these markets are having a major impact on commercial banks' lending patterns... a major factor stunting the growth in lending, despite the obvious very heavy liquidity of the commercial banks" (lines 863-870)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "There has to be a reason why lending is as dull as it is" (line 871)
- MR. LINDSEY: "there's more bank aggressiveness, not less, going on. Restructuring has occurred more, not less, than a year ago" (lines 170-172)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are mixed — rates are very low (3% fed funds), refinancing is booming, and overall liquidity is ample, but bank credit channels remain impaired by commercial real estate exposure and uncertain collateral values. The net is slightly loose of neutral but with significant credit friction.

**Minority views:** Lindsey suggests banks are becoming more aggressive; Greenspan emphasizes ongoing lending impairment.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Unanimous vote for "B" symmetric (hold fed funds rate at 3%, symmetric directive)
- MR. ANGELL: "I'm inclined to believe that we ought to be thinking about tightening at this stage. But I just don't have the stomach for doing it... I would prefer the alternative of asymmetric toward tightness" (lines 938-939, 973-974)
- MR. MELZER: "I favor 'B' asymmetric toward tightening, but I could support symmetry" (line 1077)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous support for holding steady with a symmetric directive. The meeting tilts essentially neutral with a very faint hawkish lean from Angell and Melzer, who prefer asymmetry toward tightening but do not dissent.

**Minority views:** Angell (asymmetric toward tightening, commodity price warnings) and Melzer (asymmetric) provide the slim hawkish tail.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I think there's a fair consensus on 'B' symmetric" (lines 1128-1129)
- MR. ANGELL: "You have unanimous consent!" (line 1145)
- Differences on M2 ranges were aired but all deferred to the Chairman (Angell: "I'm not going to vote against... it seems to me that what we're doing is backing you up as the Chairman," lines 79-81)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Broad agreement on the policy decision with zero dissents. Some variety of views on M2 range technicalities but all converged. Angell and Melzer expressed mild hawkish preferences but explicitly chose not to press them.

**Minority views:** The M2 range debate featured multiple alternatives (Angell 1-5%, Lindsey 2.5-5.5%, Broaddus full point cut, Keehn no change) but all deferred to the Chairman's 2-6% proposal.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "the uncertain values in these markets are having a major impact on commercial banks' lending patterns... the fact that a lending officer... cannot presume that commercial real estate mortgages can be unloaded expeditiously at a price that is reasonably forecastable has been a major factor stunting the growth in lending" (lines 863-869)
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "it is a very large part of the collateral for the financial intermediary system and a horrendous part of the nonperforming loans" (lines 903-905)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Greenspan devoted meaningful attention to commercial real estate as a drag on financial intermediaries and bank lending, but this was primarily one speaker's focus framed as an economic headwind rather than a systemic stability concern. Other participants did not substantially engage with this theme.

**Minority views:** N/A — mostly a Greenspan monologue on this topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 50 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "I find it hard to think of any reason why we would want to [change the stance of policy] over the next 6-week period" (lines 916-917)
- Symmetric directive adopted unanimously
- MR. ANGELL: "I do want to call the Committee's attention to what I think is a possibility of our getting behind the curve by maintaining the fed funds rate at 3 percent for too long" (lines 976-978) — minority forward-looking hawkish signal

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is neutral — symmetric directive, no indication of imminent action in either direction. The consensus is to hold and observe. Angell and Melzer provide a faint hawkish forward lean that keeps the score from dropping below 50.

**Minority views:** Angell's "getting behind the curve" comment is the most notable forward-looking signal, but he explicitly did not push for action.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ANGELL: "there's just too much uncertainty here" (line 38-39)
- MR. KEEHN: "The uncertainties are very, very significant" (lines 507-508)
- MR. KELLEY: "We're dealing with large uncertainties, vitally important unknowables" (line 750)
- MR. MELZER: "if our staff forecast is right, 3 percent is probably not a problem; if the Blue Chip forecast is right, it's probably a big problem" (lines 1081-1082)

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about the M2/velocity relationship and its implications, but also about the economic outlook more broadly. The staff vs. Blue Chip forecast divergence on inflation adds to uncertainty. However, this is not deep Knightian uncertainty — most participants feel reasonably confident about the broad economic picture.

**Minority views:** Melzer's framing of staff-vs-Blue-Chip as a binary suggests meaningful uncertainty about the inflation path.
