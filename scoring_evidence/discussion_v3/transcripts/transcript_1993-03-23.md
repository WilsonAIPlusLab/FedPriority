# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1993-03-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions. It contains a full go-around of district reports followed by a policy discussion. Speakers include Presidents Boehne, Forrestal, Keehn, Parry, Broaddus, McTeer, Hoenig, Syron, Stern, Jordan, Melzer, and Governors Mullins, Angell, LaWare, Kelley, Phillips, Lindsey, plus Chairman Greenspan and Vice Chairman Corrigan. Approximately 18 speakers.

### Era Context
March 1993: The U.S. is in the early stages of recovery from the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate is at 3% (historically low). Unemployment is around 7%. The Clinton fiscal package is being debated. Long-term interest rates have declined. Inflation has shown recent firming in CPI data but the underlying fundamentals (labor costs, slack) suggest subdued pressures.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as mildly below the historical norm but with recent firming that generates concern. The center of gravity is that underlying fundamentals (labor costs, slack, weak credit) do not support accelerating inflation, but measured CPI has been running at ~4% over 6 months. Most participants (Boehne, Forrestal, Parry, Stern, Syron, Hoenig, Phillips, Greenspan) view recent CPI as likely aberrant or not yet signaling a fundamental shift. A vocal minority (Mullins, Angell, Lindsey, Melzer, Broaddus) are more concerned about the CPI trend. The consensus view is that inflation is "around 3%" with some firming risk but not yet alarming. Greenspan: "labor costs...are clearly under control" and "unit labor costs clearly are under very significant restraint." Mullins: "core CPI has been 4 percent" over 6 months. The center of gravity acknowledges firming but sees it as likely temporary with good fundamentals. Score should be somewhat above the midpoint -- participants see some inflationary pressure but mostly contained.
- Direction: Slightly above neutral/target, around 3% with upside risk
- Magnitude: "somewhat" above comfort = 5-10 points from midpoint
- Score: 53 (modest firming acknowledged, but majority sees fundamentals as restraining)

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants describe the economy as in "moderate" recovery, "below potential," with "ample slack." Parry: "real GDP remaining below potential." Mullins: "ample slack in the economy both on the labor side and in terms of capacity utilization." Greenspan: "a still severely impaired financial system." Multiple participants note the expansion is still "young" or "moderate." However, the economy is growing -- roughly 3% -- so the gap is narrowing. Several districts report improvement.
- Bright-line rule: "below potential" language -> must be < 50
- Magnitude: "moderate" recovery with "slack remains" = 36-42 range
- Score: 40 (recovering but still clearly below potential; moderate growth)

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Labor markets are described as having significant slack. Forrestal: "Labor supplies are still plentiful." Multiple participants note reluctance to hire, restructuring continuing. Boehne: "Hiring is still sluggish." However, there are "spot labor shortages" (Stern) and some firms beginning to add workers. Unemployment is ~7%, with Lindsey noting that ex-California it's 6.7% (closer to NAIRU but still above). The dominant view is slack with early signs of tightening.
- Score: 35 (below full employment, still significant slack, but early signs of improvement)

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Consumer spending is "continuing" but not surging. Phillips: "it's not surging, but it's at least continuing at a reputable pace." Retail sales mixed -- some districts up, some hit by weather and Clinton uncertainty. Auto production schedules are optimistic (Keehn: 12% over prior year). Business investment showing improvement -- capital goods strong, residential construction doing well. The Greenbook growth forecast of ~3% is broadly accepted. Several note that "interest-rate-sensitive sectors" are the main growth driver (Parry).
- Score: 48 (roughly at trend -- moderate pace of expansion, not particularly strong or weak)

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Financial conditions are described as mixed. Long-term interest rates are at their lowest in decades -- a very accommodative signal. Stock market near all-time highs. Bond rally significant. However, credit growth is extremely weak. Greenspan: "an extraordinarily restrained credit and financial system." Syron notes credit flows "not very expansive in virtually any sector." Banks are looking for borrowers but demand is modest. M2 is extremely weak. So conditions are loose in terms of rates/asset prices but tight in terms of credit availability and money growth.
- The net picture: rates are very low (loose), asset prices high (loose), but credit/money growth very weak (tight). The overall financial conditions are somewhat loose to neutral.
- Score: 38 (somewhat loose -- low rates and strong asset markets dominate, but credit weakness pulls it toward neutral)

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The vote is for alternative B (no change) with symmetric language. The clear majority supports holding steady. However, there is a meaningful hawkish minority: Angell and Lindsey explicitly favor alternative C (tightening). Mullins, Broaddus, Melzer express hawkish concerns but vote for B. No one advocates easing. Greenspan frames the discussion as "this is not the time" but acknowledges "we are going to have to move" at some point, possibly soon. The lean is clearly toward the hawkish side of hold -- everyone agrees the next move will be up, the question is when.
- Score: 52 (hold steady but with clear tightening bias in the discussion; 2 explicit dissents for C, many others hawkish-leaning but voting B)

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: Two participants (Angell, Lindsey) explicitly choose alternative C (tightening). Several others (Mullins, Broaddus, Melzer) express significant hawkish concerns but vote with the majority. The policy discussion has genuine tension -- the inflation hawks make forceful arguments. However, the final vote is overwhelmingly for B symmetric. There is real debate but it converges.
- Score: 40 (moderate differences; 2 dissents/alternative preferences, several with reservations, but broad agreement on hold)

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Financial stability per se is not a major topic. There are mentions of the bond rally's vulnerability (Corrigan, Greenspan), stock market being "high" (Greenspan), and mutual fund managers being "nervous" about "reach for yields" (Syron). But these are passing references, not sustained discussion of systemic risks. The focus is overwhelmingly on the real economy and inflation.
- Score: 15 (brief mentions of bond market vulnerability and asset prices, but not a focused topic)

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward eventual tightening. Greenspan: "at a 3 percent funds rate and effectively zero or slightly negative real rates, there is no way that we can stay at this level for any extended period." Multiple participants echo this. Broaddus: "the next several months are going to be a really critical period." But the timing is uncertain and data-dependent. The directive is symmetric, meaning no formal forward lean.
- Score: 57 (mild tightening bias; broad agreement next move is up but timing uncertain; symmetric directive tempers the forward signal)

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: Participants express meaningful uncertainty. LaWare: "forecasting in this environment is like trying to solve an equation with 23 unknowns." Clinton program effects are uncertain. Corrigan: "nagging sense of unease that things are either going to be better or worse." Greenspan: "this business cycle is different from anything any of us has seen." Multiple sources of uncertainty: fiscal policy, health care, trade policy, Russia, inflation dynamics.
- Score: 50 (elevated uncertainty; many participants emphasize unusual difficulty of forecasting, multiple unknowns, but not "uncharted territory")

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack. Makes sense.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Both lean mildly hawkish. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because the discussion clearly signals future tightening even though the current action is hold. This divergence is small and explainable.

### Speaker Count by Policy View
- Hold (alternative B): Boehne, Forrestal, Keehn, Parry, McTeer, Hoenig, Syron, Stern, Kelley, Phillips, LaWare, Mullins, Melzer, Broaddus, Corrigan, Greenspan (~16)
- Tighten (alternative C): Angell, Lindsey (~2)
- Ease: None

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. BOEHNE: "The recent price numbers strike me more as an aberration than as signalling a new round of inflationary pressures...subdued rather than increasing inflationary pressures."
- MR. MULLINS: "in three out of the past five months core CPI has been 1/2 percent and over the past six months core CPI has been 4 percent...it's hard to convince oneself that we're safely gliding on the Greenbook path toward continued gradual disinflation."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "Everything we can see is exceptionally well behaved in the crucial area of labor costs...wage rates are clearly under control."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The majority views inflation fundamentals as well-behaved (labor costs restrained, slack remaining) but acknowledges that measured CPI has been running above comfort levels for several months, creating a modest upward pressure signal.

**Minority views:** Mullins, Angell, and Lindsey see inflation as a more immediate concern, with Angell citing commodity price acceleration and Mullins noting 4% core CPI over 6 months. This widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. PARRY: "the outlook for inflation in our view is reasonably favorable with real GDP remaining below potential."
- MR. MULLINS: "There is ample slack in the economy both on the labor side and in terms of capacity utilization."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we have at this stage a still severely impaired financial system."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is in a moderate recovery (~3% growth) but remains below potential. Slack persists in labor markets and capacity utilization. Multiple structural headwinds (defense cuts, restructuring, fiscal uncertainty) restrain the pace.

**Minority views:** Broaddus and Angell see somewhat stronger growth than the Greenbook, but even they don't describe the economy as above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. FORRESTAL: "Labor supplies are still plentiful. Factory activity is rising but firms are reporting little change in current or future employment levels."
- MR. STERN: "there are a couple of areas of spot labor shortages...it doesn't seem to be very pervasive at the moment."
- MR. JORDAN: "there is a great reluctance to add workers, especially low skilled."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets have significant remaining slack with ~7% unemployment. Firms are actively resisting hiring, preferring to restructure and automate. Some early, isolated signs of tightening (spot shortages, longer workweeks) but these are not pervasive.

**Minority views:** Lindsey notes that ex-California unemployment is 6.7%, closer to NAIRU, suggesting tightness may be underestimated in aggregate data.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MS. PHILLIPS: "We are seeing consumer purchasing continue; it's not surging, but it's at least continuing at a reputable pace."
- MR. KEEHN: Auto industry "second-quarter domestic production schedules...set at about 12 percent over the second quarter of last year." Heavy trucks up 17%, medium trucks up 13%.
- MR. BOEHNE: "I hear very little talk these days that the expansion will grind to a halt...But there still is a lot of caution about just how fast the expansion will proceed."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Spending and investment are at a moderate pace, roughly at trend. Some sectors show strength (autos, housing, capital goods) but offset by restructuring, defense cuts, and consumer caution. The overall picture is moderate, sustained growth rather than a strong surge.

**Minority views:** Jordan notes some recent softening in orders ("the phones stopped ringing" in February for some firms), while Broaddus sees upside risks to growth.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "We are not getting an aggregate level of finance that is anywhere near being consistent with very strong nominal GDP growth...we are getting a significant profit margin opening up which is financing a good deal of the activity. [Businesses] are financing essentially from cash flow, not from credit."
- MR. MULLINS: "We have the lowest interest rates in several decades and the stock averages are within whispering distance of their all-time highs."
- MR. SYRON: Mutual fund managers see "very, very strong inflows" but also "nervousness...because of the inevitable reach for yields."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall -- interest rates are at multi-decade lows, the bond market has rallied strongly, and the stock market is near record highs. However, credit growth is exceptionally weak and M2 is declining, suggesting the transmission to the real economy is impaired.

**Minority views:** Greenspan sees the financial system as "still severely impaired." The tension between loose rate conditions and tight credit availability creates the wider uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "at a 3 percent funds rate and effectively zero or slightly negative real rates, there is no way that we can stay at this level for any extended period...In my judgment this is not the time to be immediately concerned about this problem."
- MR. ANGELL: "I choose alternative C...now is the time to say that monetary policy is working and we need to [begin to adjust] it before the time comes to do something drastic."
- MR. FORRESTAL: "it is premature to make any move at this time. I think it would be a serious mistake for the Committee to take action."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The overwhelming majority supports holding the fed funds rate at 3% with symmetric language. However, the discussion is dominated by inflation concerns and the recognition that tightening will come -- the question is when, not if. Two members (Angell, Lindsey) explicitly prefer tightening now. The lean is mildly hawkish hold.

**Minority views:** Angell and Lindsey dissent for alternative C (tightening). Several others (Mullins, Broaddus, Melzer) are sympathetic but vote to hold.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. ANGELL: "I choose alternative C...I am only the fool who sees it correctly!"
- MR. LINDSEY: "I have managed to convince myself that at this point long-term economic growth without inflation can best be had with a small rise, maybe 1/8 point."
- MR. MULLINS: "at this stage I would support the Chairman's judgment to ride this out a bit longer" (despite extensive hawkish analysis).

**Center-of-gravity summary:** There is genuine disagreement, with 2 explicit dissenters for tightening and several more expressing hawkish reservations. The debate is substantive and extended, but ultimately the vast majority converges on holding steady. The disagreement is about assessment of inflation risks more than fundamental framework differences.

**Minority views:** The hawkish camp (Angell, Lindsey, with Mullins, Broaddus, Melzer sympathetic) is vocal and well-argued but ultimately a minority.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- VICE CHAIRMAN CORRIGAN: "I think this long bond rally is vulnerable. I think we could see a turnaround in the long bond market."
- MR. SYRON: Mutual fund managers have "a lot of nervousness...because of the inevitable reach for yields and some concerns about credit quality spreads."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "if I were in the private sector at this stage, I would be having fits on the bonds in my portfolio."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is mentioned in passing -- bond market vulnerability, stock market being "high," and reach for yield -- but it is not a sustained or prominent topic. The meeting's focus is overwhelmingly on inflation dynamics and real economic conditions.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "we are going to have to move...in a somewhat aggressive manner. It's just that I cannot get myself to believe that this is the particular point."
- MR. BROADDUS: "the next several months are going to be a really critical period for monetary policy...if we get another month or two of crummy inflation data, I think we need to think about a change."
- MR. PARRY: "even with symmetry, we certainly have the capability of taking action in the period between now and the next meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is clearly tilted toward eventual tightening. There is near-universal agreement that the 3% funds rate is unsustainably low and the next move will be up. The timing is data-dependent, especially on upcoming CPI reports. The directive is symmetric but the discussion strongly signals a tightening bias.

**Minority views:** Forrestal and Syron emphasize that the recovery is still young and caution against premature tightening, but even they agree the next move will eventually be up.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- MR. LAWARE: "forecasting in this environment is like trying to solve an equation with 23 unknowns."
- VICE CHAIRMAN CORRIGAN: "I still have this nagging sense of unease that things are either going to be better or worse than that rather benign forecast, and I can't decide which way it's going to be."
- CHAIRMAN GREENSPAN: "this business cycle is different from anything any of us has seen. It has different dynamics; it has different characteristics."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is elevated. Participants cite multiple major unknowns: the Clinton fiscal/health care program effects, trade policy (NAFTA, GATT), international weakness (Japan, Europe, Russia), the meaning of weak M2, and whether recent CPI firming is aberrant or fundamental. The business cycle itself is described as unusual.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 55**
Multiple participants discuss goods price firming: steel prices up (Keehn: three $20/ton increases), lumber prices surging, scrap steel up sharply. Angell: commodity index "up 26 percent at an annual rate in the first quarter." However, many view these as supply-driven or temporary.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: N/A**
No specific discussion of services inflation distinct from overall CPI.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: N/A**
Housing construction is discussed but not housing/shelter inflation specifically.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 52**
Mullins: "Expectations survey data remain high." Mullins also notes implied forward rates "remain 200 to 300 basis points above those in Germany and Japan." Syron notes financial sector people are "much more optimistic...on the inflation front longer term, but not in the short run." Net: expectations roughly anchored but with some upward risk.

**CREDIT_STRESS: N/A**
Credit conditions discussed but more in terms of weak demand than stress/dysfunction.

**EQUITY_STRESS: N/A**
Stock market mentioned as being near highs but no valuation stress discussion per se.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
Banking system discussed as looking for borrowers; no stress discussion.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
No discussion of Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: 55**
Angell: "Why isn't the dollar stronger? That seems to me an indicator that we may have more dollars out there than we realize." Lindsey: "the transmission mechanism from international weakness should be a strengthening dollar...it is not happening. And I am very concerned." Dollar weakness despite relative US strength is noted by multiple hawkish participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 58**
Greenspan: "Productivity is picking up in a fairly substantial way, and I suspect it is basically real." Mullins: "wage increases are unlikely to swamp productivity gains." Multiple participants note firms producing more with fewer workers. Above-trend productivity is perceived.
