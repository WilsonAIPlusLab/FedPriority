# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-05-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **transcript** with individual speaker attributions from a go-around discussion and policy round. Speakers include: Broaddus, Moskow, Parry, Jordan, Hoenig, McTeer, Poole, Stern, Guynn, Minehan, McDonough, Kelley, Gramlich, Ferguson, Meyer, Greenspan, plus Rivlin (noted but statements not included in text). Approximately 16-17 participants.

### Era Context
May 1999: The late-1990s boom. The economy is in the midst of a long expansion, with very low unemployment (~4.2%), strong GDP growth, a productivity surge, and the aftermath of the 1998 LTCM/Russia crisis easing that injected extra stimulus into an already strong economy. The April 1999 CPI report just showed a notable jump. The Committee is debating whether/when to begin tightening after the fall 1998 easings.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**
Direction: Participants see inflation as modestly below alarming levels but with clearly rising risks. The center of gravity is that current inflation remains low (Greenspan: "three-month average for the core PCE is a rise at an annual rate of 1.1 percent"; "we do not have strong evidence of rising inflation") but participants nearly universally flag upside risks and early signs of acceleration. The April CPI was a "jolt" (Kelley), "worrisome" (Broaddus), and broadly based. Multiple participants report anecdotal price pressures accelerating. Meyer: "Inflation... is stabilizing in the near term... and is poised to move higher going forward." However, Greenspan notes actual inflation data are still quite subdued. The center of gravity sees inflation at or slightly above a comfortable level with building pressure -- not yet elevated but the risk profile has clearly shifted upward. Score: ~52. Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows inflation is near target but with nascent upward pressures perceived by most participants. This is slightly above midpoint.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**
Direction: Participants see the economy as operating above potential. Broaddus: "Private domestic final purchases rose at a 7.5% rate in the first quarter... arguably at risk of overheating." Hoenig: "growth... well above its longer-term trend." Greenspan: "growth in aggregate demand still exceeds the rate of increase in productivity and is continuing to put pressure on the system." Meyer: "growth will be above trend." Multiple participants describe their regional economies as "very strong," construction booming, etc. The bright-line rule requires >50 for "above potential/above trend" language, which is pervasive here. The economy is clearly running above potential but with the productivity surge providing some cushion. Score: ~60. This is the late 90s boom, anchors suggest 57-63.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants see labor markets as very tight. Unemployment is ~4.2%, well below most NAIRU estimates. Hoenig: "Our unemployment rate is about 3.4 percent and we have a very high participation rate, so we have very strong pressure on our labor markets." Multiple participants cite worker shortages in construction, manufacturing, trucking, casinos. Jordan reports a job fair was cancelled because all displaced workers found jobs before it occurred. Guynn: casino operators "bought or leased a complete apartment complex to house workers they are now bringing in from other less tight labor markets." Minehan: "The supply of labor continues to be a concern. Jobs would grow faster if there were people to fill them." Greenspan: "erosion in the pool of people seeking work... at or below its previous low in the history of this series." The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score: ~70.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**
Direction: Participants see spending as well above trend. Broaddus: "Consumer spending is very strong... its recent strength has exceeded even those expectations." "Private domestic final purchases rose at a 7.5% rate." Multiple presidents report robust consumer spending, strong construction (both residential and commercial), strong auto sales. Moskow: "Motor vehicle producers continue to experience strong sales. April sales of light vehicles again surprised us on the upside." McTeer: construction "robust," residential market "heated up." The only areas of weakness are agriculture and energy. The overwhelming weight of discussion is on above-trend spending. Score: ~67. Well above trend but not at "unsustainably rapid" extreme levels.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**
Direction: Participants see financial conditions as quite loose/accommodative. Broaddus: "There is plenty of money around... both commercial and consumer credit are readily available." "competition for loans is very, very strong -- probably excessive." Minehan: "credit conditions are now coming back to the narrower spreads and are reminiscent of the ease we had last summer." Poole: "I don't think there is any question that financial conditions are accommodative as measured by money growth, the ease of financing in markets, and the behavior of the equity markets." Greenspan notes money supply has been "overly expansive." Multiple participants describe easy credit. Higher = tighter, so low score. Score: ~20. Conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, consistent with 16-30 range.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**
Direction: The center of gravity is clearly hawkish -- the debate is between holding rates steady with an asymmetric tilt toward tightening (the majority/consensus) versus actually raising rates now (a significant minority). NO ONE argues for easing or even a symmetric stance. Greenspan proposes holding rates but moving to an announced asymmetric (tightening) directive. McDonough, Kelley, Stern, McTeer, Moskow, Parry, Ferguson, Guynn support this. Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan, Hoenig prefer an immediate 25bp hike. Gramlich says he's "four-fifths" in agreement with the rate-hikers but will support the tilt. Meyer "enthusiastically" supports the tilt. So the debate spans from "tilt toward tightening" to "raise now." Score: ~65. This is a moderate hawkish lean -- consensus for tightening bias with a substantial minority wanting immediate action. Anchors for 2017-18 hiking are 70-75, but this is pre-liftoff positioning, so slightly below that.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**
Direction: There is notable disagreement about pace/timing but not direction. Everyone agrees the risks are to the upside and policy should lean hawkish. The split is between "tilt now, maybe hike later" (majority) and "hike now" (Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan, Hoenig -- about 5 participants). No one dissents from the opposite direction. This is a moderate level of disagreement -- same direction but different magnitudes. Jordan also expresses procedural concerns about announcing the tilt. Score: ~38. Moderate differences about pace, 1 direction of disagreement, 0 formal dissents ultimately (Gramlich says he won't dissent despite wanting to hike).

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**
Direction: Several participants discuss financial conditions meaningfully. Minehan: "Stock markets, real estate markets, and corporate and personal debt may not all be similarly extended, but they are getting there." "signs of excess are beginning to show." Broaddus: "competition for loans is very, very strong -- probably excessive -- which may be laying the foundation for problems down the road." Minehan mentions her Academic Advisory Council discussing "asset market bubbles" and potential margin requirement increases. Guynn: bank examiners see "some evidence of deterioration in credit quality." Ferguson mentions equity and fixed income market reactions. But this is not the dominant topic -- it's secondary to the inflation/growth discussion. Score: ~35. Limited but present discussion, several participants mention it meaningfully.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**
Direction: The forward signal is clearly toward tightening. The entire meeting is about establishing a tightening bias. Greenspan: "we will be in a position where we can move if necessary at the next meeting or the meeting after that." Meyer: "the balance of risks in my view is toward... ultimately higher inflation." Multiple participants expect rate hikes will follow. The announced asymmetric directive IS the forward signal -- it points toward tightening. Score: ~68. Mild to moderate tightening signal -- not an explicit commitment to hike, but a clear directional signal.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**
Direction: There is moderate uncertainty, primarily about the productivity trend and its implications. Parry: "recent forecast errors suggest that there is considerable uncertainty associated with this outlook." Poole: "there is no question that the outlook for productivity is subject to a great deal of uncertainty going forward." Greenspan discusses measurement uncertainties in wages and inflation data. However, the overall tone is more confident than uncertain -- participants have a fairly clear view that risks are to the upside. The uncertainty is about magnitude and timing, not direction. Score: ~38. Somewhat elevated uncertainty, mainly about productivity/supply side.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with boom conditions. Labor tightness is higher because unemployment is extremely low while GDP may not be as far above potential due to the productivity surge -- this makes sense.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned -- both lean hawkish. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because the entire forward signal points to tightening, while the current action is just a tilt (no rate change).

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows nascent/building pressure, slightly above neutral. 52 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence shows loose/accommodative conditions. 20 matches (low score = loose).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Consensus for tightening bias. 65 matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Greenspan: "the three-month average for the core PCE is a rise at an annual rate of 1.1 percent. This is not evidence that somehow we are far behind the curve." Meyer: "Inflation, after falling throughout the last few years, is stabilizing in the near term... and is poised to move higher going forward." Broaddus: April CPI "offers the first direct evidence in some time that inflation pressures may be building."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Participants see actual inflation as still low but perceive the tide turning -- risks have shifted clearly to the upside with multiple early signs of building pressure.
- **Minority views**: McTeer is more sanguine ("less and less discussion of upward pressure on wages" in his district). Broaddus/Jordan see more urgency. The uncertainty band reflects these views.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Broaddus: "Private domestic final purchases rose at a 7.5% rate in the first quarter... arguably at risk of overheating." Hoenig: "growth... well above its longer-term trend." Greenspan: "growth in aggregate demand still exceeds the rate of increase in productivity."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating above potential, with the productivity surge providing some offset but not enough to close the excess demand gap.
- **Minority views**: Some participants (Stern) are more optimistic about supply capacity, slightly narrowing the gap.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Hoenig: "Our unemployment rate is about 3.4 percent... very strong pressure on our labor markets." Guynn: casino operators "bought or leased a complete apartment complex to house workers they are now bringing in from other less tight labor markets." Jordan: job fair "cancelled because the workers had all found jobs before the fair took place."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are very tight by any measure, with widespread shortages across regions and industries, unemployment well below NAIRU estimates.
- **Minority views**: The puzzle is that wage growth remains subdued despite tightness, which some see as tempering the signal. Minehan: "Wages, however, still don't reflect this lack of supply."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 67 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Broaddus: "Consumer spending is very strong... its recent strength has exceeded even those expectations." "Private domestic final purchases rose at a 7.5% rate in the first quarter after growing at a rate of 6.5% last year." Moskow: "Motor vehicle producers continue to experience strong sales. April sales of light vehicles again surprised us on the upside."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and investment are well above trend across nearly all regions, with only agriculture and energy as weak spots.
- **Minority views**: Moskow notes "a few signs of moderation" in consumer spending and housing in the Seventh District.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Poole: "I don't think there is any question that financial conditions are accommodative as measured by money growth, the ease of financing in markets, and the behavior of the equity markets." Broaddus: "both commercial and consumer credit are readily available... competition for loans is very, very strong -- probably excessive." Minehan: "credit conditions are now coming back to the narrower spreads."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative -- easy credit, narrow spreads, strong equity markets, ample liquidity.
- **Minority views**: None dissent from this characterization; it is essentially unanimous.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Greenspan: "those of you who have raised the issue of moving to a tilt toward restraint have the arguments strongly on your side... it is definitely premature to move rates... it is by no means premature to move toward a tilt." Broaddus: "my preference would be to go ahead and move the funds rate up 1/4 percentage point today." Gramlich: "I am sitting four-fifths of the way from you to Al Broaddus... I have something like four-fifths agreement with him."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consensus for tightening bias (asymmetric directive toward firming), with a substantial minority (~5 participants) wanting an immediate 25bp hike.
- **Minority views**: Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan, Hoenig favor immediate tightening. McTeer hopes "we won't have to follow through anytime soon" -- a slightly less hawkish note within the consensus.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Roughly 5 participants (Broaddus, Minehan, Poole, Jordan, Hoenig) prefer immediate rate increase vs. majority supporting tilt only. Gramlich: "four-fifths is not 100 percent so I won't dissent, but that's where I am." No formal dissents; no opposing-direction disagreement.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Moderate differences about pace/timing, but no directional disagreement. All agree risks are to the upside.
- **Minority views**: The spread is between "act now" and "signal now, act soon" -- a meaningful but not deep split.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Minehan: "Stock markets, real estate markets, and corporate and personal debt may not all be similarly extended, but they are getting there... signs of excess are beginning to show." Broaddus: "competition for loans is very, very strong -- probably excessive -- which may be laying the foundation for problems down the road." Minehan also notes Academic Advisory Council discussed "asset market bubbles."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a secondary topic -- several participants mention credit excess, asset valuations, or debt buildup, but it is not the primary focus of the meeting.
- **Minority views**: Most participants focus on the macro inflation/growth picture rather than financial stability per se.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Greenspan: "we will be in a position where we can move if necessary at the next meeting or the meeting after that." Meyer: "I believe an asymmetric directive is appropriate at this time." Poole: "I anticipate a tightening trend."
- **Center-of-gravity**: The announced asymmetric directive is a clear forward signal toward tightening. Most participants expect rate hikes will follow if data continue on current path.
- **Minority views**: McTeer "still hope we won't have to follow through anytime soon." Kelley: "the Committee should not feel that it locks us in to an early move."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Parry: "recent forecast errors suggest that there is considerable uncertainty associated with this outlook." Poole: "the outlook for productivity is subject to a great deal of uncertainty going forward." Greenspan discusses measurement problems in wages and inflation data.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty centered on the productivity trend and its inflation implications, but participants are fairly confident about the directional risk.
- **Minority views**: Some participants express more certainty about inflation risks (Broaddus, Jordan) while others emphasize the genuine uncertainty about whether the "new economy" thesis holds.
